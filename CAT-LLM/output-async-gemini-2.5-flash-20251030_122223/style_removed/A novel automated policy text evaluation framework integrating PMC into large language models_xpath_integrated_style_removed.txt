It's really important to figure out how to check policy documents automatically in policy informatics. Before, the old ways of doing this mostly involved people manually picking out what to look for. This meant they couldn't check many policies at once, and the things they measured only worked for specific policies, so you couldn't use them for different kinds of policies. So, to fix this, we're suggesting a new system to automatically check policy texts. We did this by changing three general basic things to look for that work for any kind of policy, putting something called the Policy Modeling Consistency Model (PMC) into big language models (LLM), and building automatic PMC scoring systems using LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B. For example, we used Chinese science and technology policies to build the first Chinese set of policy data for evaluation. It had 22,630 policy examples that were already labeled. We then taught four models to automatically figure out the PMC indicators. When we compared it to the standard methods, the model using Qwen-2.5-7B worked best for checking how policies are written, scoring 80.41% on its F1-score. The LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B model was best at checking if policies followed rules and how well they worked, with F1-scores of 75.07% and 74.11%. This means we can automatically figure out the PMC scores and create a table with lots of information to really understand policies. Using it on biosafety and data governance policies showed that the system works for different types of policies. Because this is the first system that can automatically check PMC, what we did gives a new way to check lots of different kinds of policies.

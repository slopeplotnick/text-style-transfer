There's a ton of emergency info, like from floods or accidents, always popping up on social media. We really need a quick way to sort it out for all the different kinds of emergencies. But the ways we currently try to sort this emergency data without needing a lot of examples for each new situation (that's UDA) have two big issues. For one, they mostly only work for natural disasters, so they're not much help for other kinds of emergencies. Plus, they find it hard to deal with changes in the data, like if people use different words, or show different feelings, or if the data just looks really varied. So, to sort out these problems, we've come up with a new system called MARDA. It's a fancy name, but it basically mixes two ideas: one that helps it handle different kinds of data better, and another that lets it look at the data from a bunch of different angles. First off, we made a part that checks out the data from different angles. It's got bits that figure out what words mean and what feelings are being expressed. These bits work together to build a really good, all-around picture of the data. Secondly, we built something else that smartly decides how much weight to give to each of those different angles we just talked about. This helps it pull out all the really important bits of information from all the different kinds of data. Thirdly, there's an 'optimizer' we came up with. It plays a sort of 'game' and makes sure the important stuff it learns stays the same across different data. This makes it really good at working with new, different kinds of data it hasn't encountered yet. We tried out MARDA on four real emergency data sets, with over 24,000 bits of information. It actually did 7.39% better than other fancy systems and even beat big AI models by 1.59% when sorting things. So, yeah, it really works well as a general tool for figuring out different emergency events, no matter where the info comes from.

Online, recommending bundles of stuff is a really important service now. It gives people custom groups of items they'd probably like. Other ways of doing this try to use different kinds of info, but they often struggle to properly use the links between users and bundles, especially when trying to tell which things are connected and which aren't. So, to fix this, our research suggests a new way to recommend bundles called MIMOSA, which is short for 'multi-view multi-order contrastive learning'. This method looks at the hidden connections from different angles and between different things all at once. It uses a main system that's like a map, and it learns by comparing things. This system figures out how things are connected within themselves and to other things, based on how close they are on that user-bundle map. More simply, MIMOSA has smart ways to pick out good examples and measure how well it's doing, so it can grasp all the different ways things are linked. This helps put the connected items together in its internal picture and push the unconnected ones apart. Plus, there's a 'center-matching' plan that quickly gets different views of items in order, speeding up the whole learning process. We did lots of tests using two big sets of data, Youshu and NetEase. These tests prove MIMOSA performs way better than the usual methods. The results show that compared to the best existing approach, our new way boosted performance by around 2.87% (R@20) and 3.02% (N@20) on Youshu, and by about 5.19% (R@20) and 4.07% (N@20) on NetEase.

Figuring out what people are doing in videos, which we call HAR, is really important for stuff like security cameras. Now, even though lots of computer programs have been made for this, they haven't been very good at grabbing all the details about where things are and when they happen in the videos. So, in our study, we actually built a new HAR system. It basically combines these 'Multiple Residual Shrinkage Building Units' (MRSBU) to nail down the location details with 'Gated Recurrent Units' (GRU) to understand the timing. What happens is, the system first uses something called Inception v3 to pull out a bunch of location details from each video picture. Then, the MRSBU takes over, learning the tricky timing stuff by smartly filtering out any useless or extra information. After that, all this filtered info goes into a GRU, which has a special 'timing focus' part. This part figures out how important each video picture is and then finally tells us exactly what the people are doing. The cool thing is, this system is pretty small and works super well. It's really good at handling all sorts of tough video sets for HAR. Our new MRSBU and GRU method got almost 99.75% right on the UCF50 videos, 99.55% on the UCF101 videos, and 98.95% on the HMDB51 videos. That's a lot better than the older systems like CNN-LSTM, ViT, and 3D-CNN. These results really show that our system is effective and tough enough for different video situations, like actual security surveillance and even spotting weird things happening.

This paper looked into how city problems popped up, changed over time, and what made them happen in 87 county-like places in Zhejiang, China. They took about 3.5 million complaints from people and put them into 37 different groups, then they figured out how many of each city problem there were in these counties between 2018 and 2022. When they grouped the problems, they saw seven clear kinds: pollution and city stuff like roads, protecting shoppers, how land is used, buying houses, dealing with trash, money services, and controlling sickness outbreaks. These problems stuck around for a while and showed up in specific places. They used a kind of math to see how problems fit together, and it showed that how the problems changed over time was pretty predictable: new problems kept getting bigger, but the main problems slowly got smaller, though sometimes they did something different. Looking at eight things about society and money showed that problems caused by development, like how land is used, buying houses, and protecting shoppers, are really linked to things like how much land is bought up, how many houses are sold, and how much people buy in stores. This means someone needs to watch these money-making activities more closely. Problems that follow a trend, like pollution and city stuff like roads, don't change much with quick ups and downs but are more tied to steady society and money situations. This shows it's really important to plan things out well and work together to fix big, long-standing differences between regions. Problems caused by specific events, like dealing with trash, money services, and controlling sickness outbreaks, don't really have much to do with society and money stuff. This means they pop up because of big events and we need to deal with them fast when they happen.

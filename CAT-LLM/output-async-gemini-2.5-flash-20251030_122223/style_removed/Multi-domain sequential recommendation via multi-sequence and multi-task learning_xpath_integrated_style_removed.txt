Lately, a lot of smart people and those working in the field have been really into predicting clicks across different categories, and they've done quite well. Even though what people do online usually happens in a specific order, nobody's really tried much to recommend things in order across those different categories (that's MDSR). Most current ways of doing MDSR just line up everything users do from all categories by when it happened and then try to guess what they'll click on next in each category. But they miss the individual sequences of actions within each category. So, they can't really make good use of what's similar or different between these categories. Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) models are usually built to make things better in just one main category, not all of them. You *could* try to turn a CDSR model into an MDSR one, but it would take a super long time to run. Also, they often don't care that some categories have way more data than others, which can mess up the learning process. So, to fix all this, we've come up with a new MDSR method we call multi-sequence multi-task learning, or MML. Our MML has three main parts: one that learns what people like in order across mixed categories (HSPL), one that learns what people like in order within single categories (ISPL), and a part for doing multiple learning and prediction tasks (MLP). HSPL's job is specifically to figure out those mixed-category ordered preferences. At the same time, we create extra sequences and use a technique called 'contrastive learning' to get fairer ideas of what people like across different categories, which helps stop bad learning outcomes. ISPL is made to catch the ordered preferences *inside* each category. In the MLP part, we use three particular tasks and something that helps regulate behavior to make sure each part learns its preferences well enough and shares knowledge better between categories. We did tons of tests on public data using various main models, and our MML usually performs way better than the best existing methods in two or more categories. Plus, our MML takes about the same time to run as other MDSR models that only look at combined user actions. You can find the computer code for this at https://github.com/plw2019/MML.

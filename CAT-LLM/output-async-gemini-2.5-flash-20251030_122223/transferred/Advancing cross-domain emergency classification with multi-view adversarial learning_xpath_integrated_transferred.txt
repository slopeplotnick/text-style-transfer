Social media platforms frequently disseminate substantial volumes of emergency information, originating from diverse events such as floods or accidents. Consequently, an efficient methodology is required for classifying this information across various emergency categories. However, existing approaches for emergency data classification, particularly those employing Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) to mitigate the reliance on extensive labeled data for novel situations, exhibit two significant limitations. Firstly, their applicability is predominantly restricted to natural disaster scenarios, thereby limiting their utility for other emergency types. Moreover, these methods struggle to effectively manage data heterogeneity, which manifests as lexical variations, emotional nuances, or broader structural diversity within the information. To address these challenges, a novel system, MARDA, has been developed, which integrates two core concepts: one facilitating enhanced data modality handling and another enabling multi-faceted data representation. The initial component of MARDA is designed to extract diverse perspectives from the input data. Specifically, it incorporates sub-modules for semantic analysis and sentiment detection. These modules collaboratively construct a comprehensive, multi-dimensional representation of the data. Secondly, an adaptive weighting mechanism is employed to assign appropriate significance to each of these diverse perspectives. This mechanism facilitates the extraction of salient information across heterogeneous data modalities. Thirdly, an optimizer module has been integrated; this component operates via a regularization strategy, ensuring the learned representations maintain consistency across varying data distributions. This design significantly enhances the system's generalization capability to novel and unseen data types. MARDA's performance was empirically evaluated on four distinct emergency datasets, comprising over 24,000 data instances. The experimental results demonstrated that MARDA achieved a 7.39% performance improvement over established baseline systems and surpassed state-of-the-art AI models by 1.59% in classification accuracy. Consequently, MARDA is established as a robust and versatile tool for the categorization of diverse emergency events, irrespective of their originating data sources.

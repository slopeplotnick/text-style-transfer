This paper investigated the emergence, spatiotemporal evolution, and underlying determinants of urban issues in 87 county-level administrative units across Zhejiang, China. Approximately 3.5 million citizen complaints were collected. These were categorized into 37 distinct problem types to quantify their frequency within the county-level units from 2018 to 2022. Problem categorization revealed seven distinct categories: environmental pollution and urban infrastructure, consumer protection, land use management, housing acquisition, waste management, financial services, and public health crises management. These problems exhibited persistent characteristics and spatial concentration. A multivariate statistical approach was employed to analyze problem interdependencies, revealing predictable patterns in their temporal evolution: emerging issues consistently expanded in scope, while established issues generally demonstrated a gradual decline, albeit with occasional deviations. An examination of eight socioeconomic indicators indicated that development-induced issues, such as land use management, housing acquisition, and consumer protection, are significantly correlated with factors like land transaction volume, housing sales, and retail consumption. Consequently, enhanced regulatory oversight is warranted for these economic activities. Trend-following issues, such as environmental pollution and urban infrastructure, exhibit limited sensitivity to short-term fluctuations and are primarily influenced by stable socioeconomic conditions. This indicates the critical importance of proactive planning and collaborative regional governance to address significant and persistent regional disparities. Event-driven problems, including waste management, financial services, and public health crises management, demonstrate minimal correlation with socioeconomic factors. Consequently, their emergence is predominantly linked to significant events, necessitating rapid response mechanisms.

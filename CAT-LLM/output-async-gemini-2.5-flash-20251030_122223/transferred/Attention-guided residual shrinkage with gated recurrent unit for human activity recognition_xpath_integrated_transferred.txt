Human Activity Recognition (HAR), defined as the identification of human actions within video sequences, constitutes a critical domain with significant implications for applications such as surveillance systems. Despite the proliferation of computational approaches developed for this purpose, existing methodologies have often exhibited limitations in accurately capturing comprehensive spatio-temporal information within video data. Consequently, this study introduces a novel HAR system that integrates Multiple Residual Shrinkage Building Units (MRSBU) for precise spatial feature extraction with Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) for robust temporal sequence analysis. Initially, spatial features are extracted from individual video frames using the Inception v3 architecture. Subsequently, the MRSBU component processes these features, acquiring complex temporal dependencies through the intelligent suppression of irrelevant or redundant information. Furthermore, the refined feature set is then fed into a GRU, which incorporates a specialized temporal attention mechanism. This mechanism evaluates the significance of each frame, thereby facilitating the accurate classification of human activities. Notably, the proposed system exhibits a compact architecture and demonstrates superior performance efficiency, effectively addressing the challenges posed by diverse and complex video datasets prevalent in HAR research. Empirical evaluations reveal that our novel MRSBU and GRU methodology achieved classification accuracies of 99.75% on the UCF50 dataset, 99.55% on the UCF101 dataset, and 98.95% on the HMDB51 dataset. These results significantly surpass the performance of established baseline methods, including CNN-LSTM, ViT, and 3D-CNN. Such robust performance underscores the system's efficacy and resilience across varied video scenarios, rendering it suitable for real-world applications such as security surveillance and anomaly detection.

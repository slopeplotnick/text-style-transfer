Understanding the structural organization of groups within dynamic human mobility networks is crucial for identifying inter-regional connectivity. However, existing methodologies face two significant limitations: difficulty in identifying multi-scale group structures and a failure to combine the simultaneous analysis of both fine-grained and coarse-grained communities within dynamic mobility networks. To address these deficiencies, a novel approach integrating surpassing probability theory with community detection has been proposed. This SPBL component initially reweights network connections to lessen the limitations of the Leiden method in finding multi-scale communities, thereby helping in the discovery of macro-level group formations. The TPSPCD module then meticulously disaggregates these macro-level communities into finer, more granular sub-communities, preserving their intrinsic structural integrity. The system also incorporates an ASD metric to assess the internal cohesion of communities relative to their external connectivity. Distinct central-peripheral patterns are revealed within these mobility networks, characterized by robust community formation around significant central nodes. Consequently, this research highlights the efficacy of our proposed methodology in capturing localized connectivity within dynamic mobility networks. This contributes both theoretically and practically to the field of community detection in mobility networks, providing planners with actionable tools for regional development.

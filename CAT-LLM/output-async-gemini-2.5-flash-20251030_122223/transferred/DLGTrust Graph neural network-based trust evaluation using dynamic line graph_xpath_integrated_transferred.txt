Trust is crucial for ensuring security during inter-component communication within networked environments. However, existing trust evaluation mechanisms exhibit several limitations, including susceptibility to sophisticated attacks, over-reliance on hierarchical architectures, and ineffectiveness in dynamic and sparse network topologies. To address these challenges, we propose DLGTrust, a novel trust evaluation framework characterized by robustness and adaptability, which leverages a graph neural network architecture. DLGTrust employs dynamic line graph snapshots, which explicitly model the intricate relationships between indirect and direct trust connections, thereby enhancing the model's comprehension of complex interactions and improving its performance in data-sparse environments. By integrating a multi-faceted spatial feature extraction mechanism with a spatio-temporal fusion system, specifically a gated recurrent unit (GRU), DLGTrust precisely models complex spatio-temporal interactions. Concurrently, adversarial perturbations and global robustness constraints are incorporated to enhance the model's resilience against sophisticated attacks. Empirical evaluations conducted on three distinct real-world datasets demonstrate that DLGTrust achieves at least a 26.3% overall performance improvement compared to baseline models. An F1-macro score exceeding 98% was achieved for both known and unknown data instances, serving as a key metric of model efficacy. Under adversarial conditions, including 10% 'bad-mouthing,' 'good-mouthing,' and sophisticated attacks, the F1-macro score improved by 24.5%, 23.7%, and 76.7%, respectively. Consequently, DLGTrust demonstrates enhanced robustness and superior self-defense capabilities, thereby underscoring its significant contribution to ensuring secure inter-component communication.

Understanding the temporal evolution of scientific knowledge acquisition is crucial for advancing educational methodologies and fostering innovation. This study developed Knowledge Precedence Networks (KPNs), which were constructed to elucidate the typical progression of scientific inquiry across 19 distinct disciplinary domains. Data encompassing the research activities of nearly 5 million scientists and 80 million scholarly articles from the OpenAlex database were analyzed. A novel methodology, termed CoCiTCD, was introduced, integrating Co-Citing networks with Temporal Community Detection to facilitate the understanding of knowledge evolution. Specifically, this approach operates by identifying researcher communities, extracting salient conceptual elements, and subsequently establishing temporal linkages between these concepts. Analysis of KPNs within mathematics, computer science, and engineering disciplines demonstrates the fundamental importance of foundational concepts for comprehending advanced theoretical constructs. For instance, algorithms serve as a critical conceptual bridge between mathematics and computer science, thereby facilitating advancements in fields such as Artificial Intelligence and Data Science. The congruence between KPNs for 303 distinct concepts and the background knowledge deemed requisite by large-scale artificial intelligence models was subsequently evaluated. This evaluation provided insights into the actual temporal application of knowledge by scientific practitioners. Under optimal scenarios, the KPN achieved an accuracy of approximately 25.77%. Comparatively, a related methodology, Knowledge Citation Networks (KCN), which relies on citation analysis, demonstrated an accuracy of 26.6%. This consistent discrepancy implicates that the organic evolution of scientific topics frequently deviates from prescribed curricular sequences regarding foundational knowledge acquisition. Furthermore, a Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve score of 0.76 indicates that the KPN methodology is robust in elucidating unconventional and intricate knowledge progression. These KPNs provide valuable implications for research planning, curriculum development, fostering interdisciplinary collaboration, and understanding the hierarchical construction of knowledge. Consequently, they contribute significantly to the 'Science of Science' field by demonstrating the actual dynamics of knowledge dissemination across diverse research domains.

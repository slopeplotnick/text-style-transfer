Recent research and industry efforts have focused significantly on cross-category click prediction, yielding promising results. Despite the inherently sequential nature of user online behavior, multi-domain sequential recommendation (MDSR) has received limited attention. Existing MDSR approaches typically aggregate user interactions chronologically across all domains and subsequently predict the next click within each category. However, these methods often overlook the distinct intra-category action sequences, consequently struggling to effectively leverage inter-category commonalities and disparities. Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) models are primarily designed to enhance performance within a single target domain, rather than across multiple domains simultaneously. While adapting a CDSR model for MDSR is conceptually possible, it incurs substantial computational overhead. Furthermore, existing models frequently neglect data sparsity and imbalance across categories, potentially compromising the learning efficacy. To address these limitations, we propose a novel MDSR method termed multi-sequence multi-task learning (MML). Our MML framework comprises three key components: a cross-category sequential preference learning module (HSPL), an intra-category sequential preference learning module (ISPL), and a multi-task learning and prediction module (MLP). Specifically, the HSPL module is designed to capture heterogeneous sequential preferences across diverse categories. Concurrently, auxiliary sequences are generated, and a contrastive learning technique is employed to derive more robust cross-category representations, thereby mitigating suboptimal learning outcomes. The ISPL module is configured to identify sequential preferences inherent within each individual category. Within the MLP, three distinct tasks and a regularization mechanism are utilized to ensure effective preference learning within each module and facilitate knowledge transfer across categories. Extensive experiments conducted on public datasets, employing several baseline models, demonstrate that our MML consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods across at least two categories. Furthermore, the computational efficiency of MML is comparable to that of existing MDSR models that rely solely on aggregated user interaction sequences. The source code for this work is publicly available at https://github.com/plw2019/MML.

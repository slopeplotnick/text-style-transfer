Streamer discourse significantly influences consumer purchasing decisions in live online shopping environments, particularly when educational content and product information are concurrently delivered. Prior research has established a correlation between streamer communication and sales performance; however, limited investigations have systematically analyzed the nuanced semantic content of streamer discourse from multiple perspectives to quantify its predictive efficacy for future sales. The primary objective of this study was to develop a predictive system for sales volumes within educational live shopping broadcasts. This system integrates concepts of social support with advanced, specially trained artificial intelligence (AI) language models. These models are employed to meticulously extract latent semantic features from streamer discourse, subsequently facilitating sales prediction. For empirical validation, data were collected from 80 live videos across 35 distinct Douyin channels over a two-month period. In the evaluation of social support message detection, our proposed Ernie-SFT AI model significantly outperformed established state-of-the-art AI benchmarks. Specifically, it demonstrated an 11.12% increase in accuracy, an 11.87% improvement in F1-score, and a 7.83% enhancement in macro F1-score. For the sales prediction task, our system was evaluated using four prevalent data classification methodologies, consistently exhibiting superior performance. The optimal classification method within our system achieved a 12.53% higher accuracy, a 10.83% improvement in F1-score, and a 4.24% enhancement in macro F1-score. These findings unequivocally demonstrate the substantial predictive power of streamer-provided social support messages. This research offers actionable insights for streamers and enables platforms to leverage data-driven strategies for service optimization.

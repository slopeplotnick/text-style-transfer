In online environments, the recommendation of item bundles has emerged as a crucial and increasingly significant service. This service provides users with personalized collections of items tailored to their potential preferences. Existing recommendation methodologies typically leverage diverse information sources; however, they frequently encounter challenges in effectively modeling the intricate relationships between users and item bundles, particularly concerning the identification of inherent connections and disconnections. To address these limitations, our research proposes a novel bundle recommendation approach, termed MIMOSA, an acronym for 'multi-view multi-order contrastive learning'. Specifically, this methodology is designed to simultaneously capture latent connections from multiple perspectives and across various entities within the recommendation space. Central to MIMOSA is a primary contrastive learning framework that operates by mapping representations and learning through comparative analysis. This framework ascertains intrinsic and extrinsic inter-item relationships based on their proximity within the user-bundle embedding space. MIMOSA incorporates sophisticated sampling strategies and an effective evaluation mechanism to comprehensively capture multifaceted linkage patterns. This facilitates the consolidation of related items within the latent representation space while concurrently disassociating unrelated entities. Furthermore, a 'center-matching' mechanism is integrated to efficiently align multi-view item representations, thereby accelerating the overall learning convergence. Extensive experiments were conducted on two large-scale datasets, Youshu and NetEase. These evaluations empirically demonstrate MIMOSA's superior performance when compared against existing conventional approaches. Specifically, the results indicate that MIMOSA enhanced performance by approximately 2.87% (R@20) and 3.02% (N@20) on the Youshu dataset, and by 5.19% (R@20) and 4.07% (N@20) on the NetEase dataset, relative to the state-of-the-art baseline.

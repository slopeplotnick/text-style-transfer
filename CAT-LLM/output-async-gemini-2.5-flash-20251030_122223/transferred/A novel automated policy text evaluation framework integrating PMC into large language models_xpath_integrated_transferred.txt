The automated evaluation of policy documents is critically important within the field of policy informatics. Previously, conventional approaches primarily relied on manual identification of relevant indicators. Consequently, these methods suffered from limited scalability and lacked generalizability across diverse policy types. To address these limitations, a novel automated system for policy text evaluation is proposed. This was achieved through three key methodological innovations: adapting three generalizable indicators applicable to any policy domain, integrating the Policy Modeling Consistency Model (PMC) within large language models (LLMs), and developing automated PMC scoring systems utilizing LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B. Specifically, Chinese science and technology policies were employed to construct the inaugural Chinese policy dataset for evaluation. This dataset comprised 22,630 pre-labeled policy instances. Subsequently, four models were trained to automatically identify the PMC indicators. Upon comparison with established methodologies, the Qwen-2.5-7B model demonstrated superior performance in evaluating policy formulation, achieving an F1-score of 80.41%. The LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B model excelled in assessing policy compliance and operational effectiveness, yielding F1-scores of 75.07% and 74.11%, respectively. These findings indicate the feasibility of automatically deriving PMC scores and generating comprehensive policy information tables for enhanced analysis. Further application to biosafety and data governance policies confirmed the system's applicability across diverse policy domains. As the first automated PMC evaluation system, this research establishes a novel methodology for assessing a wide range of policy types.

Table 5 shows the word stats for these data sets. Just like the last test, the 35 most common words in both data sets are listed in Table 6 and Table 7. These top 35 words are mostly function words. In the 10,000 noun phrases, the first clear content word, 'forgotten', shows up at rank 54/55, sharing its spot with the function word 'at' or 'near'. After rank 55, there are more content words. In the test with 10,000 sentences from Delilah, the most common content words are 'forgotten' at rank 11, 'works' at rank 27, and 'in the morning' at rank 66. After rank 66, more content words show up. Delilah uses 'masses' as a number, and the learning process picks this up correctly. For the 10,000 examples, it's easier to tell function words from content words because words that show up more than 100 times are usually function words, which is more than 1.7 per thousand for Delilah's sentences. So, from these tests, the MODOMA settings for learning function and content words are set so that words showing up more than 2 per thousand are function words, and others are content words. This learning process happens once after getting at least 10,000 examples. The settings are careful so that it's less likely to wrongly call a word a function word than to call it a content word. Since content word is the default, every word is a content word unless there's strong proof otherwise. The settings from this first test are now the default for MODOMA, but they can be changed based on other tests and language theories.

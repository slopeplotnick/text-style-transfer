We're trying to get a clear idea of what makes e-commerce search work well and figure out what tools are needed to make it better. Basically, e-commerce search is about matching what people are looking for with products. We make this matching better by measuring how well a search matches a product, looking at both clear facts like attributes and constraints, and personal opinions. We break down what people want in their searches into 18 different categories like type, brand, style, and more. For each search and product match, we see how well the product fits what the search is asking for, and use these judgments to decide how relevant the match is. This system helps us understand and analyze how we decide what's relevant. To measure relevance in e-commerce search, we look at three parts: understanding the product, understanding the search, and judging relevance. When judging relevance, we look at two main things about the product: what its attributes are and how it's presented. Attributes can be either inherent, meaning they're obvious and easy to see, or derived, meaning they need more thought and understanding. Inherent attributes are things like category, brand, model, and price, which are usually clear and easy to match with searches. Derived attributes come from inherent ones and are about what the product does or how it feels to use. These aren't always easy to see and need more understanding and reasoning, which can be tricky because different industries have their own ways of describing these attributes.

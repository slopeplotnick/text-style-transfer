Big language models that work with both text and images (MLLMs) have gotten better recently, but they still struggle to pick up on small details in high-quality images. To make these models better at understanding high-resolution images, researchers usually go in two directions. One way is to train the models more, using methods like supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, but this can make the models less flexible for other tasks. The other way doesn’t involve training and instead uses methods like searching through parts of the image to find what’s important. But these methods can be slow and might miss some objects, especially when there are a lot of them in the image. In this section, we looked at how the size of image pieces (or crops) affects how well these models work. Our experiments showed that the resolution of the image pieces matters a lot. Different object sizes work better with different resolutions. Based on this, we came up with the MRD framework. We tested this by using two versions of a model, LLaVA-ov and LLaVA-v1.5, on a benchmark. The results, shown in Figure 3, revealed that when the resolution of the image pieces was set to 112, the models performed best for both single-object and multi-object tasks. This suggests that 112 might be the best resolution to use.

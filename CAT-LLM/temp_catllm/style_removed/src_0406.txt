Traditional methods for optimizing construction coordination have some basic problems. They need a full plan from the start, but construction projects change a lot, like tasks, conditions, and resources. Also, these methods are too complicated for big projects with lots of robots and tasks. Because of these issues, researchers started looking into learning-based methods that can adjust to changes without needing a full plan. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become a big deal because it helps robots learn how to work together by interacting with the environment, without needing a full plan. RL lets robots learn by trying things out and adjusting to changes. But RL needs a lot of practice, which can be risky in construction because mistakes can be dangerous for workers and equipment. Plus, training takes too long for projects with tight deadlines. So, people developed RL methods specifically for construction. Lee and others in 2022 made a method using digital twins and Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for robot tasks in construction. They combined Building Information Modeling (BIM) with DRL to make smart coordination decisions considering space, time, and safety. The digital twin helped learn safely in simulations before doing it for real. But RL still has issues because even trained simulations can mess up in real life, causing safety problems. Also, using learned methods from simulations in real construction sites with different conditions is hard. These problems led to more advanced RL techniques.

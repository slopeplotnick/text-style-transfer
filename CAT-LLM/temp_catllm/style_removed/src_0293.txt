The main idea of explainable AI (XAI) is to show why a model gives a certain result, but the reasons for doing this can be different. It could be to check how well the model works, to help people trust the decisions it makes, or to help people understand how to change a decision if it’s not final. For the last one, the explanation needs to focus on things that can actually be changed in the current situation. When the goal is to change a model’s decision by explaining what can be done, it’s called algorithmic recourse. This needs accurate predictions about what will happen if the suggested changes are made, but it’s only about what happens after those changes are actually carried out. The type of explanation in this paper is meant to work in general situations, without focusing on a specific person who needs the explanation or assuming anything about how well the model is working or where it’s being used. This is to make sure the explanations match what the model is really doing, even if the model doesn’t work as expected. It also allows thinking about what could have been done differently in the past, without worrying about whether the changes are actually possible. While this approach could be used for algorithmic recourse with some changes, that’s not covered in this paper. Instead of using specific data features, concept-based explanations use broader ideas or characteristics that have meaning to humans. There are different ways to define these concepts in XAI, but here it refers to what Poeta et al. [2025] call symbolic concepts, which are high-level ideas or traits of the data that people can understand.

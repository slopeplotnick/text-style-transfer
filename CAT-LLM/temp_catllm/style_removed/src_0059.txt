Big language models, or LLMs, are being used in things like Google's AI summaries, where they pull together info from a bunch of long documents. But it’s not clear if they treat all the input equally. For example, with news about abortion, researchers made 40 sets of three articles (one pro, one neutral, one con), mixed up the order of the articles in six different ways, and asked Gemini 2.5 Flash to write a neutral summary. They checked how the summaries compared to the original articles using tools that measure overlap in words, meaning, and factual accuracy. They found that the summaries leaned more toward the first article in the order, based on one of the tools (BERTScore). The first article had more influence than the second or third, which were about the same. This shows that the first document can have a bigger impact on the summary, which could be a problem for systems that depend on these summaries or for AI systems that make decisions based on them. Generative AI, which is a key part of these systems, is developing fast and is already being used in many areas. These "agentic AI" systems are designed to handle complex tasks on their own with little supervision. They break big goals into smaller steps to get things done. To do this, they plan and think through multiple steps, pulling in outside info when needed to guide their actions. Because of this, how well these systems work depends a lot on how accurately the LLMs they rely on can find, sort, and combine information from instructions and outside sources.

The experimental group using the curriculum learning method learns faster and improves more steadily in terms of rewards and model performance, as shown in Fig 12. During reinforcement learning (RL) training, as the model gets closer to its goal, its output becomes less random and more predictable, which makes it harder for the model to try out new ideas and find better solutions. This limits how much the model can improve. To fix this, we tried different ways to slow down how quickly the model becomes predictable so it can keep exploring. Fig 13 compares these methods. The Clip-higher method works best because it slows down how fast the model becomes predictable while still letting it happen, striking a good balance between trying new things and sticking to what works, and keeping training stable with noticeable improvements. The On-policy method keeps the model’s randomness steady and almost stops it from decreasing. While this helps the model explore more, it also makes it harder for the model to settle on a good solution, so the improvements are smaller. The Off-policy + Entropy Loss method has issues with unstable training. At first, it slows down how fast the model becomes predictable, but later, as the model becomes less random, the penalty for randomness gets too big and takes over, causing the model to behave unpredictably and making training fail. This method also needs a lot of testing to figure out the right settings, which makes it more expensive to use. Overall, the Clip-higher method is the best option here because it balances trying new things and learning efficiently by controlling how fast the model becomes predictable.

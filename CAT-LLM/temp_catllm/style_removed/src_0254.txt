In our study, we looked at a type of attack where a backdoor can be triggered randomly when people use a tool, meaning the issue is there from the start. There’s also a worse version of this attack where the tool seems harmless at first. Attackers don’t do anything bad right away—they just let the tool work normally. Once the tool becomes widely used, they can turn on the attack remotely. This delayed approach, similar to supply chain attacks, makes the attack more dangerous and harder to trace. The attack can also be made sneakier by focusing on specific targets. For example, attackers can look at user details and only go after people who use the tool a lot, especially those who pass security checks. This makes the attack harder to notice during testing. In this paper, we talk about a type of attack we call implicit toxicity. It takes advantage of tools that are allowed to do certain things but uses those permissions to cause harm. To show this, we created something called LeechHijack, which is a way to secretly take over a system’s computing power through normal tool use. It works with any MCP tool provider and gets into a system without needing special permissions, using the system’s resources for tasks it wasn’t meant to do. This shows a problem with how MCP tools are shared, where bad tools can mess up systems without needing extra access. We ran a lot of tests on different systems and found that LeechHijack works well and isn’t stopped by current defenses. We also looked into ways to reduce the risk and set a starting point for better defenses in the future. Our research shows there’s a security issue in systems that use large language models, and it’s important to carefully check third-party tools. All our tests followed strict ethical and security rules. The LeechHijack attack we describe is just a test to show the problem, not to encourage bad behavior. We didn’t mess with any real systems, platforms, or services. All our tests were done in safe, controlled environments we set up ourselves, and we didn’t connect to real servers to avoid leaking any real data.

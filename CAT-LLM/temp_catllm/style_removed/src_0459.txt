Right now, there's a big worry about hallucinations in large language models (LLMs), so there's a lot to explore in future research. One idea is to create real-time systems that can spot and fix these errors while the model is working, instead of fixing them later. This could make LLMs more reliable, especially in high-stakes areas like legal or medical documents where mistakes could be really bad. Another area being explored is using different types of data, like images and audio, along with text to improve how models work, since LLMs are often used with multiple types of media and can mess up when mixing them. People are also interested in making models that help users easily find and fix these errors. Research is being done on creating AI systems that are easy to interact with, where users can ask questions and confirm what the model says. There's also a need for more studies on reducing hallucinations using formal methods, which can help make models more reliable, especially when accuracy is really important. This article looked at some common hallucinations in LLMs, like when they get facts wrong or don't make sense because they don't have enough info or the input is unclear. These errors make people trust the system less and make it harder to use, so it's important to find ways to cut down on them.

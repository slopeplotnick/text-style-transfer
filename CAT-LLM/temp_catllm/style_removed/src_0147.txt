This part talks about how they made and checked the dataset. A group of TCM experts collected samples from clinical cases, old case records, and exam questions. They cleaned up the samples, removed duplicates, and made sure no personal info was left. Experts annotated the data in three steps: first, they did it themselves, then checked each other's work, and finally, an independent party reviewed it. The final benchmark, called TCM-BEST4SDT, has 600 questions on four topics: TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, LLM Content Safety, and SDT. To evaluate, they came up with three ways to see how well models work in real TCM clinical situations. The first way is selected-response evaluation, where they evaluate each question multiple times, mix up the answer choices, and score them differently based on the question type. The second way is judge model evaluation, where they use an LLM with prompts made by experts to score the model's answers. The third way is reward model evaluation, where they made a special model to measure how well prescriptions match syndromes. TCM-BEST4SDT has two types of tasks: SDT tasks and general evaluation tasks. SDT tasks use data from clinical cases and old case records, while general tasks cover TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, and LLM Content Safety. TCM Basic Knowledge questions come from public exams like the National Qualification Exam for Medical Practitioners, the National Postgraduate Entrance Exam: Comprehensive Clinical Medicine (TCM Integrated), and the Chinese Herbal Medicine Title Exam. Medical Ethics questions come from trusted sources and were also manually annotated by experts for specific situations. LLM Content Safety questions were independently made by experts based on what was needed in practice.

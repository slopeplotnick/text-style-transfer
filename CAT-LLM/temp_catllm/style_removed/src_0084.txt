We made a system to catch obvious mistakes that anyone could spot, not to do deep science or check how things move. Our data is more about complex physical stuff and science rules. So, breaking down physics with specific lists gives better results than general explanations, showing we need special ways to judge detailed physical thinking in video making. VideoScore2 isn't good for our test because it needs strict science checks, which don't match well with what people think about this data. We show examples of how different models do. In Figure 4 at the top, Sora-2 makes the expected physical thing happen with good prompts and movement. The video matches the setup well, shows how things change over time, and captures the start and color changes of the reaction. But Hailuo 2.3, even though it keeps things moving and in place, misses important parts of the prompt, leading to a video where nothing happens as expected. This shows that setting up experiments right and following prompts are key for high scores, even if other parts look good. Even simple physical setups are still hard for current models.

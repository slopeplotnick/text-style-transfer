Research shows that when people get stuck in their beliefs, it causes problems like polarization and misinformation, and it can lead to various thinking errors. This happens because of how people process information, which is basically their reasoning. Big language models, which have learned how people think from lots of online text, are made to help people by answering questions or assisting with tasks. These models have been successful in complex thinking tasks, and new ways of reasoning have been developed using them. From early methods like Chain-of-Thought to newer ones involving reinforcement learning, these techniques help models understand things correctly by using arguments, looking for evidence, and trying different approaches. But sometimes, these models don't seek the truth because they stick to old beliefs instead of considering new information. This can make their conclusions less accurate and give users false confidence. Although studies show that belief entrenchment exists in these models, proving it is hard. It's tough to tell if a belief update is justified or biased. So, past research uses specific tasks where it's easy to spot belief entrenchment, but these methods aren't great at catching reasoning mistakes in situations where the truth isn't clear.

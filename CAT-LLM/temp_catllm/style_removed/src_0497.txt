ViSAudio works better than other methods for making spatial audio from videos, based on tests with different datasets, as shown in Table 2. We compared it to other top methods, and you can find details about those in Section 5.1. For the BiAudio and MUSIC test sets, we used both video and captions, but for FAIR-Play, which doesn’t have captions, we only used video. Models that can’t handle text were tested with just video too. The results in Table 2 show that ViSAudio does a better job overall, especially in sound quality, matching how sound is spread between channels, syncing audio with video, and keeping the meaning of the scene. This shows that the model can make realistic binaural audio while keeping the sound and video in sync, even in different sound environments. It also works well on the FAIR-Play dataset, which it wasn’t trained on, proving it can handle new situations. Figure 3 compares how different methods create spatial audio from videos. One example shows someone playing the sitar while the camera moves left to right, making the sound shift from right to left. We looked at the spectrograms of the audio made by ViSAudio, the real audio, and other methods. ViSAudio’s audio matches the real audio best and shows the sound moving correctly. For example, when the sound is on the left, the left channel is louder, which matches how it would sound in real life. Other methods didn’t get the rhythm right or show the difference between the left and right channels.

We started with a basic model that only used the EMER dataset, which is really good but doesn't have a lot of guidance. Adding more data that matches emotions makes everything better, showing how important it is to have reliable emotional examples early on. Then, we used a method to rearrange reasoning based on MI, which helped the model start thinking from the emotion-focused part. This proved our idea that organizing thoughts around MI helps avoid getting stuck on visuals or text at the start. Next, we looked at how each reward we suggested helps keep reasoning stable and clear. The Modality-Aligned Order Reward gets the model to start reasoning from the part MI focuses on, bringing back the structure it learned before. The Modality-Grounded Reasoning Reward makes sure the reasoning is emotionally valid and matches the target emotion. Using both rewards together gives the best results, showing they work well together. Our goal was to make reasoning-based emotion understanding more reliable by fixing early-stage reasoning problems in MLLMs. We introduced MI and came up with MIGR, a training setup to start and keep reasoning from the part most related to the target emotion. Our two-step method makes sure both learning processes match the emotion-focused part, leading to clearer, emotionally grounded, and meaningful explanations. Tests on MAFW and DFEW benchmarks show MIGR cuts down on emotionally inconsistent reasoning. Overall, MIGR makes emotional reasoning more trustworthy and understandable, showing the importance of focusing on the main part in both data setup and improvement.

Recognizing hate speech in languages that don't have a lot of resources is tough because there aren't enough datasets, and the way words are written and the language itself can vary a lot. Bangla is spoken by over 230 million people in Bangladesh and India, specifically in West Bengal. Even though there's a growing need for automatic moderation on social media, Bangla doesn't have enough computational resources. In this study, we look at Subtask 1A and Subtask 1B of the BLP 2025 Shared Task on detecting hate speech. We recreated the official baselines like Majority, Random, and Support Vector Machine, and we also looked at Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree as baseline methods. We used transformer-based models like DistilBERT, BanglaBERT, m-BERT, and XLM-RoBERTa for classifying hate speech. All these transformer models did better than the baseline methods for the subtasks, except for DistilBERT. Among them, BanglaBERT performed the best for both subtasks. Even though it's smaller, BanglaBERT did better than both m-BERT and XLM-RoBERTa, showing that training specifically for a language is very important. Our results show the potential and need for pretrained language models for Bangla, which doesn't have a lot of resources. The internet has become a lot more popular over the last few decades, and social media platforms are used a lot more around the world. Better internet access lets people share their thoughts and information online. Social media use is going up fast and doesn't seem to be slowing down. It's become a big part of public discussion, but it also lets people spread hate. The quick rise in toxic and harmful content, like hate speech, is a big safety concern on these online platforms. Hate speech is harmful, offensive, or derogatory language aimed at people or groups. So, automatically detecting hate speech is an important task in Natural Language Processing (NLP). There's been a lot of progress in detecting hate speech in languages with a lot of resources like English, but for languages with fewer resources like Bangla, progress is still limited. This is because there aren't enough high-quality annotated datasets and the language is complex.

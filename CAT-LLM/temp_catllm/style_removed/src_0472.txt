Things are changing fast. In the last ten years, the global mining industry is now at the center of the energy shift. The need for important minerals is likely going up, along with political, social, and environmental issues. This is leading to new companies popping up, often with venture capital backing, trying to shake up mining like they did in healthcare, renewable energy, and climate tech. New digital tech is coming out that might change how we do things in the economy, education, society, and industry with big language models. Whether it will really make a big difference is still unclear, but everyone is worried about missing out. I think the mining industry shouldn't worry, just see the chance to use things like AI. This paper looks at where those chances are and how AI can help. My group, Stanford Mineral-X, helped find a big copper deposit in Zambia, where AI really helped with planning drilling and making underground models. Some start-ups focusing on AI in mineral exploration have come out of this program. This text doesn't list all the ways AI can be used in exploration or review everything out there since those already exist. Instead, I will look at AI as a way to make better decisions based on a new scientific method. In the first part of this paper, I'll go over the main decisions in exploration and how a scientific approach can make it more efficient. Right now, in mineral exploration, there's no agreed scientific method. There are just tasks done by experts, each with their own way of doing things in their fields. I think a wider scientific method for mineral exploration is missing, and I plan to argue that this is one of the key issues in making exploration more efficient, meaning it costs less to make a discovery.

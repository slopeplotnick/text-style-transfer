We tested seven ways to combine models: Linear, Karcher Mean, SLERP, NuSLERP, TIES, DELLA, and Nearswap. We used these to mix a bias-inverted model with a pre-trained large language model. Linear, suggested by Wortsman and others in 2022, is the simplest. It just adds and averages the weights of fine-tuned models, which is cheap and works well. Karcher Mean, introduced by Goddard and others in 2024, uses a different approach by considering the curved space of the model parameters, which helps in non-flat spaces. SLERP, also from Goddard in 2024, mixes model weights along a curved path on a sphere, keeping the space's shape. NuSLERP, another from Goddard in 2024, is a twist on SLERP that lets different parts of the model mix differently, focusing more on important parts for better balance and performance. TIES, from Yadav in 2023, combines models by looking at the differences in parameters that matter for specific tasks, reducing interference and keeping each model's strengths. DELLA, from Deep in 2024, cuts down interference by trimming less important updates, using a smart way to keep the important parts, which helps maintain the original model's performance.

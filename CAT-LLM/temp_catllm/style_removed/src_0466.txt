Jack Lu, Ryan Teehan, Jinran Jin, and Mengye Ren work at the Agentic Learning AI Lab at New York University. You can reach them at their NYU emails or check out their website. On November 20, 2025, they talked about how big language models can both solve problems and check solutions, with the checking part helping to pick the best answers. But before, people mostly looked at how models check their own answers and not much at how they check answers from other models. These models also get a lot of extra training after the main training, but we don't really know how this affects checking. They studied 37 models from different groups and sizes, looking at ones that were just trained and ones that got extra training. They tested them on 9 different types of problems like logic, puzzles, math, common sense, and facts. They compared how models check their own answers versus checking answers from models in the same group or different groups. They came up with a new way to measure how much better checking makes the answers and tested it out. They looked at how these measures change with model size and extra training and how easy it is to check different types of data. They found that checking answers from different model groups works really well; extra training makes models better at checking answers from other groups but not their own; and math and logic problems are easiest to check. Now, instead of just asking a model for an answer, models both solve problems and check answers. Checking answers at test time can be simple, like making a bunch of answers and using a checker to pick the best one, or more complicated, like refining answers over and over. With checking at test time, models can solve harder problems than they could alone.

We compared our samples to EMOPIA MIDI files. The major key ratio shows how positive the music feels, and the average note length and note velocity show how energetic it is. For positivity, our samples mostly match EMOPIA across three quadrants, except in Q2, where our model makes way more major-key tracks than EMOPIA. This probably happens because the dataset has more major-key tracks than minor-key ones, and the fine-tuning set is small. For energy, the fine-tuned model gets the note-length pattern right: shorter notes in high-energy quadrants (Q1 & Q2) and longer notes in low-energy quadrants (Q3 & Q4), which is similar to EMOPIA. The differences in note velocity between quadrants aren’t as clear, probably because REMI simplifies MIDI velocities into fewer categories, which limits the range and makes it harder to see differences between quadrants. The numbers partly match what listeners said in tests, showing the model gets some musical features right for energy and positivity. But the model seems better at capturing energy than positivity, and the positivity results are almost random. This might be because the model makes too many major-key tracks. Adding more metrics could help figure out where the positivity generation isn’t working well. In this project, we made Story2MIDI, a transformer-based model that creates symbolic music based on emotional text input. To do this, we built a text-music dataset and designed an encoder-decoder system to connect emotions in text with music. The encoder was fine-tuned to make clear emotion representations for different energy and positivity levels, and the decoder was first trained on a big set of symbolic music and then fine-tuned on a smaller set labeled with emotions to make music based on emotions. We tested Story2MIDI with listener feedback and music measurements, and these tests show the model works well but also point out some areas that need more work.

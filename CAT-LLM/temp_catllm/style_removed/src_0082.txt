We looked at how GRAPHECTORY works with systems that act on their own, focusing on two programming systems called SWE-agent and OpenHands. These systems go through steps like planning, finding bugs, fixing code, and checking if the fixes work, and we can measure all of this. For our tests, we used four language models (DeepSeek-V3, DeepSeek-R1, Devstral-small-2505, and Claude Sonnet 4) to solve real problems from 12 GitHub repositories in the SWE-Bench Verified benchmark. Here’s what we found: when the systems fail, it takes longer and wastes more time. The GRAPHECTORY for failed attempts is bigger and has more repeated steps than successful ones. Harder problems make the systems work harder, trying more things and changing strategies more often to keep making progress. Even though the systems follow their main plan, they sometimes adjust their approach as they go, especially with tough problems, which means they spend extra time searching and fixing. Even when they succeed, they’re still not very efficient, which shows we need to focus on how they work, not just whether they succeed. We explain GRAPHECTORY in detail and use it to create ways to measure and analyze how these systems work. To make GRAPHECTORY easier to understand, we also came up with LANGUTORY, which simplifies the information into something people can read more easily. While GRAPHECTORY gives a lot of details about how these systems work, it can be too much to compare across different systems or problems, so LANGUTORY helps by summarizing it.

To make music based on a specific emotion from text, we use an encoder-decoder Transformer model. The emotion in the text is picked up by a RoBERTa-large encoder, which we fine-tuned to better notice small emotion differences in different categories. The decoder is a Transformer with three layers and four attention heads in each layer, built to handle the timing and harmony of symbolic music. Figure 2 shows how the model works. The encoder starts as RoBERTa-large and is fine-tuned using the GoEmotions dataset, where the 27 emotion labels are changed to match their category labels. Each batch has examples from different categories, with pairs in the same category treated as positives and pairs from different ones as negatives. This setup helps train the model efficiently, creating an embedding space where the categories form clear groups. After fine-tuning, the encoder is frozen and used as the text input for our generative model. The original RoBERTa encoder makes overlapping representations for the four emotion categories, but the fine-tuned version creates more distinct groups. This shows that the fine-tuning improves the emotional structure of the embedding space while still showing the gradual and subjective nature of emotions. (Check Figure 4 in the Appendix, Section C, for visualizations of the embeddings before and after fine-tuning.) EMOPIA only has 1,078 MIDI samples, which isn’t enough for a Transformer decoder to learn the patterns in MIDI tokens well. To fix this, we pre-train the decoder on the bigger GiantMIDI-Piano dataset. During pre-training, we use zero tensors instead of text embeddings and train the decoder to predict the next MIDI tokens, helping it learn the patterns in an auto-regressive way.

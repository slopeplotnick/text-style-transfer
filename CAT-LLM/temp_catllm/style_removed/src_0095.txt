This is about a way to make an energy model that shows how much energy is used by statically typed programming languages that run on the JVM. It looks at how things like basic data types, data sizes, operations, and different devices affect energy use. It also shows that even devices with the same hardware can use different amounts of energy, which makes it harder to predict energy use in general. The paper is set up like this: Section 2 gives background on Bayesian statistics. Section 3 explains the method we used. Section 4 talks about how we put the method into practice. Section 5 covers how we tested it and any issues with the results. Section 6 shows the test results, and Section 7 discusses them. Section 8 looks at other related work, and Section 9 wraps it all up. Bayesian statistics is a way of using prior knowledge to make probability-based guesses about something we can’t directly observe. It’s kind of like frequentist statistics, which is more commonly used in computer science, but the two are different in how they make and interpret models. Frequentist statistics only uses observed data and tries to avoid assumptions, while Bayesian statistics includes assumptions and makes them clear so they can be checked. We use Bayesian statistics for two reasons: first, it makes our assumptions clear and easier to explain, and second, it predicts energy use in more detail by giving a range of possible outcomes instead of just one number, which is what frequentist methods usually do. In this paper, we assume energy use follows a normal distribution and falls within a range based on earlier research. We use this range as a starting point and then update it using a Bayesian algorithm to better match our measurements. If we don’t have much data, our initial guesses will have a big influence and might lead to bad results. But with more data, the algorithm can fix mistakes in the starting assumptions and help us see how well the model matches the actual data.

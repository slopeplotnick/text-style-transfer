One big problem for OLLMs is dealing with different types of inputs, which changes how safety issues come up and how we find them. Different kinds of inputs bring different risks, like bias in text, wrong info in pictures, or privacy problems in audio. Besides these single-type risks, mixing different types of inputs makes it even harder to spot hidden dangers. For example, saying "I want to fly" and showing a picture of someone on a rooftop are okay by themselves, but together they might suggest something dangerous like suicidal thoughts. Also, a video of police with a question like "How to overcome obstacles" might suggest a criminal intent that you wouldn't notice from just one type of input. So, safety risks for OLLMs are a lot harder to handle, and we need to understand each type of input and how they work together better. Just trying to make OLLMs safer might not work well because it takes a lot of time and resources, and it can make them worse at basic reasoning. Even after spending a lot, these models need expensive retraining to fix problems and are still open to cheap attacks. Guardrail models offer a more flexible option, but they mostly focus on single types of inputs or simple combinations like pictures and text, not on all types together. Since unsafe inputs can come from any type or mix of types, single-type guardrails and simple picture-text guardrails aren't enough for full moderation. Plus, many guardrails treat safety as a yes-or-no question, which limits how well they work.

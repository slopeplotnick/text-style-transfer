Researchers are trying to figure out how diffusion models work, especially how they balance copying what they've learned and creating new stuff. Some earlier studies looked at this by measuring how models behave and figuring out why they copy or create new things. Most of these studies check how models act when they start from nothing and test their ideas on small datasets like FFHQ and CIFAR-10. These studies help when there's not much data, but copying becomes less likely with big datasets like ImageNet. Song and others noticed that with big datasets, starting from an early point in the process makes new images, while starting later tends to recreate old ones. So, the big question is: what controls this balance in models trained on lots of data? To find out, we looked at the model's training goals. We checked out flow matching and conditional flow matching, which are usually seen as hard because they need specific data that's not available. But we showed that with a Gaussian setup and a limited training set, you can actually figure out the flow. This lets us calculate the best path for the model at any point. Looking at this path, training has two main parts. Early on, the model uses info from different data points to find its way. Later, it focuses on just one data point. We also found that the size of the data and samples are key to when this switch happens.

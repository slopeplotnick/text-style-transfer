There isn’t really good software out there for making decisions in a logical way. Software companies just want to sell stuff people will buy, and people usually like software that does what they need it to do. Making decisions based on solid data and dealing with uncertainty often needs powerful computers, like when using Monte Carlo methods, and that can get expensive. Small exploration companies aren’t going to spend a ton of money on fancy cloud computing systems, and they’re not going to hire experts to handle it either. Most exploration work is done by small mining companies that only own one or two sites and have a few geologists working for them. These companies usually get their funding by showing some kind of success, like finding minerals with one drill hole, and they care more about getting more money for drilling than using a scientific approach to decision-making. Proving a geological idea wrong takes time and effort to gather data, but these small companies are only focused on short-term results. So, it’s clear that new ways of funding are needed. Betting on one drill hole at a time is like putting all your money on one stock. Most people invest in a mix of stocks to balance risk and reward, so the mining industry should do something similar by investing in a group of sites and figuring out the balance between risk and potential profit. How to actually do this is still unclear. In academia, things have been changing a lot in the last few years, especially with the arrival of large language models. Professors had to quickly change how they teach, assign homework, and do exams. Honestly, I think these changes were overdue.

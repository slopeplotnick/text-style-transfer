The hardest part of making the explanation model is usually figuring out the structural equations for the FSCM. In Section 5.2, the results include standard PSCM intervals for counterfactual questions to show what’s missing when the real causal mechanisms aren’t clear. For some questions, the intervals are small, so the average of the interval or even approximate queries using a CBN might give a decent guess of the real value since the exact model doesn’t matter as much. But this isn’t always true. Generally, when you base things on rare events, the counterfactual probability can go anywhere from 0 to 1 depending on the model you use. Two different FSCMs that fit the data could give completely opposite explanations for the same model and even say those outcomes are certain. Rare observations can be really important to explain well, like when you need predictions to make sense in areas with not much training data. So, you still need an FSCM to make sure you can calculate any query. If experts give partial knowledge to define non-standard PSCMs, it might make the intervals smaller and let you approximate more queries. Future research could look into how different methods work for exact and approximate calculations. The explanations in this work are based on figuring out the probability that concept interventions are enough, but the framework isn’t limited to that. Future research could also look into probabilities of necessity and other kinds of explanations.

Psychometric scales and questionnaires are important tools used in research for areas like psychology, medicine, social sciences, education, and how people interact with computers. When a new idea or theory is introduced and studied, researchers usually need to create tools to measure it. But making these tools takes a lot of effort, time, money, and resources (Stefana et al., 2025). This article looks at whether it’s possible to make this process easier by using computer simulations to create psychometric scales with data generated by Large Language Models (LLMs). The article explains the reasoning behind this idea, describes four studies that test if it works, and ends with a discussion of the results and suggestions for future research. Getting a group of participants that represents the population can be expensive and take a lot of time, so researchers often use groups of university students who volunteer. This makes it hard to apply the results to other groups of people (Hanel & Vione, 2016; Wild et al., 2022). Lately, researchers have been using online platforms like Prolific.co and Amazon MTurk to find participants (Buhrmester et al., 2018; Douglas et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2015). These platforms have made it faster and cheaper to find participants, and they can sometimes help researchers get groups that better represent certain populations, like people in the UK or US. But there are concerns about ethics because some participants might come from disadvantaged backgrounds (Huang et al., 2015; Huff & Tingley, 2015; Moss et al., 2023). When researchers study a new idea and don’t have a validated scale to measure it, or when it’s not possible to validate a scale, they often create their own questionnaires based only on theory. This saves money and time but can lead to results that don’t apply to people outside the study group (M. K. Lee et al., 2020).

We're talking about the limits of TaleFrame when it comes to how people interact with it and advanced storytelling, plus where future research might go. Right now, TaleFrame works on desktops and laptops using a mouse and keyboard to help connect things and figure out how events are linked. People seem to like drag-and-drop, but it might not work as well on phones and tablets because their screens are smaller. You can use touch pens or fingers instead of a mouse, but long interactions and putting in relationship info can lead to mistakes, messing up the experience on mobile devices. To make things easier, we've used colors, icons, and lines to show info and made layouts that change size to fit different screens. In the future, we want to look into more ways to interact, like touchscreens, keyboards, and voice commands, so people can use TaleFrame on different devices. When it comes to making stories, TaleFrame struggles with complex event relationships beyond just following a timeline, like cause-and-effect, flashbacks, and other storytelling techniques. The way things are connected can make it hard to keep stories clear and accurate. We've tried to make event connections better, but it's tough with complicated stories and interactions between different things. When events involve many entities, it can be hard to get the event order right or show cause-and-effect, leading to confusing storylines. As stories get more complicated, it gets harder for the model to understand and create these connections. In the future, we'll try to improve how we model relationships and make better strategies for non-linear storytelling to make the model better at creating complex stories, giving users a more interesting and clear storytelling experience.

Things like climate change and switching to new energy sources are way too complex for just one area of science, but we still don't have a degree for Mineral Exploration. Also, info gets made super fast now and keeps getting smarter. Instead of going to the library to copy stuff from journals, we have algorithms that can search and sum up thousands of documents almost instantly. Meanwhile, fewer people in the Western world are interested in studying economic geology and mining engineering. It's really important to keep the old knowledge about how ore deposits form, which is hidden away in old geological reports all over the place. High-quality databases like the ones in Canada and Australia are needed everywhere. In Africa, only one country has set up such a database (gsd.gov.zm). Making this info easy to access online is key to getting more investment. I'm writing this to push for scientists and industry people to work together on a new way to explore minerals. As exploration goes "under cover," the old way of starting with surface outcrops and using deterministic geophysical inversion isn't working anymore, as shown by fewer discoveries. Instead, we should focus on decisions that look at risk by measuring uncertainty, which will cut down on drilling mistakes. This means we need to admit we don't fully understand orebodies, shown by measuring epistemic uncertainty. Plus, we need better ways to handle data at the right scales using geostatistical methods, which are missing right now, to avoid false positives.

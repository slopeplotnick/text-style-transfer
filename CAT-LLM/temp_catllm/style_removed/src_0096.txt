Big language models (LLMs) are getting better really fast and can do things like writing academic papers, reviewing literature, and making scientific reports. LLM-based systems for creating surveys are changing how people get information from huge amounts of data by combining searching, organizing, and putting it all together into one simple process. Both researchers and companies have made different systems for creating surveys. These systems can be grouped into three main types: general systems for writing long texts (like Kimi and GLM), tools for writing research papers (like Chengpian and Doubao Paper Mode), and tools specifically for making academic surveys (like SurveyGo, SurveyX, and Panshi ScienceOne). These tools can go through a lot of research papers, pick out the important stuff, and create drafts of surveys that are well-organized, include citations, and make sense logically. But even though there’s been a lot of work on making these systems, figuring out how to judge how good they are is still a big problem. Right now, people mostly rely on subjective opinions to rate them, which doesn’t give consistent or reusable results for comparing systems, figuring out what’s working, or improving them. This makes it hard to ensure the systems are high-quality and keep getting better. Because of this, it’s really important to create a standard way to evaluate these systems so their quality and reliability can be checked, and so researchers have a common way to compare and analyze them. To help with this, we made SurveyEval, a tool to evaluate surveys made by these systems based on three things: overall quality, how well the outline flows, and how accurate the references are. We tested it on seven academic fields and improved the LLM-as-a-Judge method by using surveys written by humans to make sure the automated evaluations match up better with what people think.

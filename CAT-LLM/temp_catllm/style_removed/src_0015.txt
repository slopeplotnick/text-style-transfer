Nearswap is a method Goddard and others came up with in 2024. It mixes things up by making the interpolation stronger when the parameters are alike and weaker when they're not. To check out different model setups, we picked three groups of large language models for our tests: GPT, LLAMA, and QWEN. The GPT group, from Radford and others in 2019 and Gao and others in 2020, has GPT2-small, GPT2-medium, GPT2-large, GPT2-x1, and GPT-neo-2.7B. The LLAMA group, from Touvron and others in 2023 and Dubey and others in 2024, includes LLAMA-2-7B, LLAMA-3-8B, LLAMA-3.1-8B, LLAMA-3.2-1B, and LLAMA-3.2-3B. The Qwen group, from Qwen in 2024, has QWEN2-0.5B, QWEN2-1.5B, and QWEN2-7B. You can find these models on the Hugging Face website, and the links are in Appendix A. The computing stuff needed for ongoing pretraining to make biased LLMs is in Appendix C, and the hyperparameter details are in Appendix D. We check out social bias in LLMs using three benchmarks: BBQ, BOLD, and HONEST. The links to these datasets are in Appendix E. The BBQ dataset, from Parrish and others in 2022, has about 58,000 question-answer pairs in nine social areas important to people who speak U.S. English. It looks at how much models depend on stereotypes instead of clear evidence by comparing "underspecified" and "fully specified" versions of each question. The BOLD dataset, from Dhamala and others in 2021, has 23,679 prompts sorted into 43 groups based on job, gender, race, religion, and political views. To make sure the debiasing methods don't mess up how well the models do on other tasks, we test both the debiased and pre-trained LLMs using the SuperGLUE benchmark, which has eight tasks: BoolQ, CB, COPA, MultiRC, ReCoRD, RTE, WiC, and WSC. All tests are done with the Language Model Evaluation Harness.

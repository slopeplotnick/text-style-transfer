Galhotra and others in 2021 talk about how to figure out probabilities when you don't have all the details of a system. They look into how to use certain models to get ranges for questions about what could have happened differently. This helps focus on specific parts of models when you don't know everything, which might make those ranges smaller than if you just did the math. They also think about how to check methods that explain AI by looking at how necessary and sufficient certain things are. Mothilal and others in 2021 say the best way to explain a model is to find the smallest set of important features that are needed and enough to get a result in a certain situation. They assume that the only things to explain are the input features and that these features don't affect each other. They compare different types of AI explanations. The idea of necessity is linked to explanations that show what changes were needed to cause the original result. Sufficiency is about which features are important enough to cause the result no matter what else is going on. They show that different methods vary a lot in practice. Pawar and others in 2024, also assuming features don't affect each other, talk about necessity and sufficiency as ways to judge AI methods and look at different ways to create examples for explanations. Beckers in 2022 looks at situations where a model predicts what will happen based on actions and assumes the model is always right. They define explanations that are enough and ones that look at what could have happened differently, assuming the model always predicts correctly. They also break down explanations by looking at how much influence causes have to find the real reasons for explanations.

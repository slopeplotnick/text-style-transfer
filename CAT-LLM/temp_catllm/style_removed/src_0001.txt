Another study says hallucinations are a big issue for large language models (LLMs), no matter their size or how they're trained. It suggests that picking the right model and training can help reduce hallucinations, but the authors don't think it's possible to make models completely free of them. This is important because it helps set realistic goals and decide if a model is right for a task. When picking an LLM to cut down on hallucinations, you should think about the model's design, the quality and variety of training data, and strategies like fine-tuning or adding extra information. There are many models available now, like GPT, BERT, and T5. GPT often makes text that makes sense and flows well, but these models can also create fake information (hallucinations). BERT has fewer hallucinations because it uses bidirectional training for certain tasks, but it might still make up new stuff. Other models like PaLM and LLaMa have been made to reduce hallucinations, but how well they work depends on how they're trained and designed. Knowing what these models can and can't do is crucial when choosing a tool for tasks where accurate information is very important and you want to keep hallucinations low.

Our system works well with different models and algorithms. We've seen improvements with both Qwen2.5 and Llama models, showing that our approach works with various architectures. Even the smaller Llama-3.2-1B model gets better accuracy with our OptPO-SFT and OptPO compared to the usual methods, showing that our method works well with different model sizes. Our OptPO system also works well with different reinforcement learning algorithms like PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, and it consistently beats TTRL in accuracy and efficiency, proving it's effective with different RL methods. When comparing SFT-based and RL-based versions, the RL-based OptPO versions usually have better accuracy, especially on math reasoning tests, because they use feedback from rollouts better than the SFT method. However, OptPO-SFT still shows stable improvements over TTSFT in all cases, proving that our adaptive pseudo-labeling improves SFT-style training during testing. Key points: (1) Using adaptive early-stopping with BSPRT improves efficiency without losing accuracy; (2) OptPO balances accuracy and computing power well; and (3) OptPO-SFT is a quick option for adaptation when RL-style updates aren't possible. Together, they create an efficient way to train models during testing for reasoning tasks. This paper introduces OptPO, the first framework that efficiently optimizes policies during testing, solving the inefficiency problem of majority-vote-based rollouts in test-time reinforcement learning for large language models. By using a Bayesian test for early-stopping, OptPO adjusts rollouts based on evidence rather than fixed limits, ensuring reliable pseudo-labels without unnecessary sampling. The framework is easy to use and works with standard policy optimization methods like PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, as well as supervised fine-tuning during testing. Across different tests and models, OptPO keeps or improves accuracy while saving on computing power. Overall, this work shows that efficient, label-free adaptation is possible and important as large language models are used in unpredictable real-world situations. We hope OptPO will be a starting point for future research on efficient learning during testing.

When people come up with a story, they can start with an event and then figure out the traits and development of the people or things involved in that event. Or they can start with a person or thing and build a bunch of events around it. E2 is a PhD student in literature, and she thinks stories can be broken down into parts like outlines, sequences, ideas, and basic speech elements. If you put these parts together in reverse order, you can make a complete story. Things like a person, an action, or a quality can make up an idea. A few ideas can be grouped into a sequence, and a couple of sequences can make a full story. But just sticking these parts together doesn’t make a good story. The things that connect these parts are called relationships, and they’re important for making everything work. Interviews with two experts showed that there are four main parts to a story: people or things, events, relationships, and outlines. These match what other studies have said. Table II explains what these parts mean. When talking to the experts, we looked at the details of each part and saw that they can mean different things. Events are the main part of a story, but the details of those events might not always be obvious in the way the story is told. Events can stand on their own in a story, but how they interact with each other creates the story’s tension and meaning. Also, who or what is in the story and the relationships between them can affect the events. The details about the people or things and the relationships can also be sorted out separately.

Lately, we've gotten better at making big language models (LLMs) smarter, which has made people think they can give more accurate and reliable info. But now, we're seeing that when these models think things over and over, they might just stick to their old beliefs instead of finding the truth. In this study, we came up with a way to check how stuck these models are on their beliefs by using something from stats called the Martingale property. This basically means that if you're updating your beliefs logically, your future beliefs should be the same as your current ones, so you can't guess future beliefs just from what you believe now. We created something called the Martingale Score to see when this doesn't happen, which shows when models aren't updating their beliefs right. In different areas like predicting events, answering opinion questions, and reviewing academic papers, we saw that models often stick to their current beliefs, which we call belief entrenchment. We did a bunch of tests to see which models, thinking styles, and areas are more likely to get stuck on their beliefs. We also showed that the Martingale Score can predict how accurate models are in areas where we know the right answers. This means that even though the score is meant to work without knowing the right answers, it still helps us see how good models are at finding the truth. Think of a person who won't admit they're wrong or a king who only listens to yes-men. People often end up sticking to what they already believe, either because they only look for info that agrees with them or because of outside influences.

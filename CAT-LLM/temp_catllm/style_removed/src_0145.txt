We tested these methods on the BrowseComp benchmark and found that managing the context during testing helps the model perform better by letting it use more computing power and take extra steps when needed. For instance, the Summary method increases the average steps to 364 and improves performance by up to 60.2%, but it’s not very efficient overall. On the other hand, the Discard-all method, even though it’s simple, does well in both efficiency and scalability, scoring 67.6, which is similar to parallel scaling but with fewer steps. Basically, you can increase test-time computing either by managing the context step by step or by doing things in parallel, and both ways help the model solve problems better. But each method has different levels of efficiency and scalability, so it’s important to think about the actual computing costs when comparing model performance. Also, figuring out the best mix of step-by-step and parallel scaling to get both efficiency and scalability is something that needs more work in the future. In this study, we presented DeepSeek-V3.2, a system that balances computing efficiency with advanced reasoning. Plus, adding our task synthesis system makes it better at using tools, opening up new possibilities for creating strong and flexible AI systems with open LLMs. We also have a high-performance version, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, which has been recognized with top awards in competitions like the IMO and IOI, marking a big step forward for open LLMs. However, we admit that it still has some limitations compared to advanced closed-source models like Gemini-3.0-Pro.

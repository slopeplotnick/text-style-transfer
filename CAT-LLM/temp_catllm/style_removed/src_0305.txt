SMP is a tool that helps train motion policies by giving rewards based on style, and it doesn’t need the original motion data to work. This means you can use different methods to adjust the reward model and change how the motion works. Sometimes, though, this can make the movements look unnatural. There’s a video that shows how different methods compare. SMP also lets you create new motion styles by tweaking the outputs of the pretrained model, so you can make styles that weren’t there before without needing to train the model again. For example, as shown in Figure 6, you can mix two styles, like "AeroPlane" and "HighKnees," to make a new one. This is done by blending their predictions from the model. One pretrained motion model can also be used to train policies for different tasks, like steering, finding a target, or playing dodgeball, as shown in Table 2. The motion model is trained on part of the LaFAN1 dataset, which has random running movements. The trained policies work well for tasks and still look natural. As shown in Figures 5(a) and 5(b), the character can switch between different movements to keep doing the task while still looking natural. For example, the steering policy makes the character jog backward when it’s facing away from where it needs to go, and then jog forward when it’s facing the right way. It can also do side-steps and cross-steps without needing a separate motion planner. In the Target Location task, the policy trained with SMP figures out human-like moves, like slowing down automatically when needed.

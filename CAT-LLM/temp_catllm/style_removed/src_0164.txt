In 2023, Zakharov talked about things like how we notice changes and make guesses based on what's likely, even when we're not sure. Petzschner and Glasauer in 2011 said this happens because we use the best guess method when we're unsure. New computer models say these guessing mistakes, especially about time, are backed by memory predicting stuff, according to Fountas and others in 2022. When people try to figure out sizes or amounts, they mix up unclear info with what they already think, which leads to certain behavior patterns. Figure 1 shows how both Llama-4 Maverick and humans tend to guess towards the average, showing they use similar thinking methods even though they're different. Some parts of language models (LLMs) work like this guessing method. For instance, learning in context can be seen as a rough guess method, as Xie and others said in 2021, and teaching using this method can make reasoning better, according to Qiu and others in 2025. LLMs also break down sequences using surprise in a way that matches how humans notice events, as Kumar and Fountas mentioned in 2023 and 2025. But most research looks at clear thinking or learned actions, where models use known rules, not on tasks that show thinking methods that come out naturally from training. There's been quick progress in making LLMs that handle different types of info, and tests like MMbench and SEED-bench, mentioned by Liu and Li in 2024, check how these models think. But these tests don't usually look at how noise in different types of info affects thinking. Our made-up data lets us look closely at how LLMs mix unclear info from different sources. This is a new but important research area to help us make better models that work well even when things are uncertain.

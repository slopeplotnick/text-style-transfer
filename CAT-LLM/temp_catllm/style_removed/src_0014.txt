(1) To understand what users are asking and the details of items, the system needs to start with some basic knowledge about the topic. With this knowledge, it can figure out what users really want, pick out the important details about the items, and match them to the user’s needs. (2) Sometimes, pictures of items show details that are hard to explain in words, like how something looks or feels. To handle this, the system also looks at item images and combines what it learns from both the text and the pictures. By using both types of information together, it can get a better and more complete idea of what the item is like. (3) After figuring out what the user wants and pulling out the details about the items, the system still needs to follow certain rules to check and match the details. At this point, it uses these rules to make sure everything makes sense and works properly. Step 1: Knowledge and Reasoning. In Section 2.3, we split knowledge into two types: general knowledge about the world and specific knowledge about e-commerce. For general knowledge, large language models already know a lot from their training, so they can understand common ideas and relationships. But in e-commerce, there are a lot of phrases and terms that are hard to understand just from their basic meaning. These problems mainly fall into two groups: (1) Rare Queries: These are very specific requests about unusual items, and models don’t always have the background knowledge to connect the words to the right meaning. For example, if someone searches "strange things are also loved by someone" and the item is "novel and interesting toys," the search is actually about a book title. Without knowing this beforehand, the system might not match them correctly. Other examples include brand-specific terms, nicknames for products, or marketing language that only makes sense in e-commerce. (2) Confusing Items: Some items are so uncommon that the system doesn’t know enough about them. For example, something called "lying gate" can’t be understood or categorized just from its name without knowing more about what it is. To handle these cases, the system needs extra knowledge about e-commerce to figure them out correctly.

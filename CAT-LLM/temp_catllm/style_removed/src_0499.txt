Here, we're showing how our interface works using an example called "Picture Composition". This feature gives users clear info like how many things and events there are and the types of connections, helping them make stuff. Figure 5.a shows a diagram for picture composition, and Figure 5.b shows how P4 makes a story with TaleFrame. P4 starts by dragging the event "Jack shoves Ryan", which is the first part of the diagram, onto the workspace (Figure 6.f). Then, P4 adds the two people involved in this event to the box (Figure 6.g). After that, P4 links the two people (Figure 6.h1), clicks on the line connecting them, and changes the details of their relationship (Figure 6.e), like emotion type, action type, relationship strength, and how the relationship changes. The "action_direction" of the relationship is shown by the "connect node", which usually goes from start to end (like "from Jack to Ryan"). But for the event "confront each other" in the second part, where the action is mutual, there's a switch called "is bidirectional". Turning it on makes the action_direction "bidirectional". By doing this for all parts, P4 finishes the last three parts, connecting the four event boxes to complete the story setup (Figure 6.h2). Then, P4 clicks the "Modify Story" button, changes the title and description, and assigns the four events to the right parts of the story. The story is rated on seven aspects based on the same criteria (Figure 6.1). A detailed suggestion is also made from the feedback (Figure 6.m). Based on this, P4 changes parts with lower scores, like adding more connections to some events. After that, P4 clicks the "Regenerate" button to make a new version. Finally, P4 clicks the "Export" button to get the final story, the diagram, and the JSON file.

Even though it's important, people still don't have a good way to measure and analyze how much power and energy it takes to run LLMs, considering different model sizes, hardware, software, and how they're used (like on one computer or many). Some efforts try to help with this, but they don't fully solve the problem. For example, MLPerf Inference benchmark (Reddi et al. 2020) sets a standard for checking performance in various machine learning tasks, but it doesn't really look at the total power use or adjust energy use to fit LLM-specific tasks (like Joules per token or prompt). MLPerf Power benchmark (Tschand et al. 2025) looks at more measurements, but it usually (i) focuses on setups with one computer, (ii) looks at smaller models compared to the latest big LLMs (like Llama 3 405B-class, which aren't well studied), and (iii) relies on expensive, precise equipment that's hard to use in different places. Also, current benchmarks don't often look at settings that people adjust in real use, like batch size, parallel processing, context length, and quantization levels. These settings really affect how much power is used at once and energy per token on average. To fix this, we introduce TokenPowerBench, a new, easy-to-use benchmark made to measure how much power and energy LLMs use. TokenPowerBench has a system that works with vendor tools (like power sensors for GPU/CPU/Memory), checks energy use at the computer level, and can also measure at the rack or facility level if possible. One pipeline matches every power reading with the two main inference steps (prefill and decode) so we can see exactly where energy is used.

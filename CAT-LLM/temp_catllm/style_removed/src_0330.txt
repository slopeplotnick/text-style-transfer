You can find details about the participants in Table B.3 in the Appendix. There weren’t any big differences in the participants’ characteristics between the groups. People were randomly put into one of three groups. In the first group, they were told the music they’d hear was made by AI. In the second group, they were told the music was from movie soundtracks. In the third group, they were told they’d hear music made by both humans and AI, and they’d need to figure out who made each song. Groups 1 and 3 were given explanations about AI and AI music tools before rating the music. After getting their instructions, everyone had to rate 18 music clips, which were picked randomly to include different subgenres and AI tools. Before starting, they were told to adjust the volume to a level they liked and to go over all the emotion terms. The order of the terms was different for each person but stayed the same for all the songs they rated. They were told to rate how the music made them feel, not what emotions they thought the music was trying to show. Each music clip was played twice. The first time, they just listened carefully without doing anything. Then, the questions for rating the music showed up, and the clip was played again to help them remember it better. After rating the music, they filled out a questionnaire about their personality, music habits, and background. They were also asked about their opinions on AI. At the end, they saw a screen explaining the study and how their data would be used. They could also share their thoughts about AI music and how they felt about the study in an open-ended question. The survey ended with some personal feedback for each participant.

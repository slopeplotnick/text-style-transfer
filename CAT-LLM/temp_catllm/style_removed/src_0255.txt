Language models are better than traditional algorithm models because they predict well and are easy to understand, plus they can handle different tasks at once. Small Language Models (SLMs) are a practical choice instead of Large Language Models (LLMs), made to work efficiently using methods like quantization, knowledge distillation, and lightweight designs. SLMs do things like sorting text, summarizing info, and answering questions on devices with limited resources like phones and embedded systems. They are a good option compared to LLMs because they use less computing power but still perform well in specific tasks. Studies show that when there's not much data, smaller models can do as well or better than bigger ones in focused areas like sorting text and healthcare. This makes SLMs a good choice for finding mental health issues on social media. But past studies mostly looked at one task or area and didn't try combining different mental health issues like depression, stress, and suicidality. Unlike earlier LLMs, which have too many parameters to use in places with limited resources or privacy concerns, SLMs focus on being easy to use, with quick response times, less memory use, and cost efficiency. Previous research compared several SLMs with LLMs on different mental health tasks using social media data and found that few-shot prompting helps SLMs more. Kim and others made an SLM called mhGPT, which does better than bigger models like MentaLLaMA and Gemma in mental health tasks even though it has fewer parameters and uses less data. Another study suggests SLMs can be used in clinical settings for structured tasks beyond just detecting issues. But these studies don't balance tasks well and aren't often optimized with efficient fine-tuning, meaning they either use minimal prompting or full fine-tuning.

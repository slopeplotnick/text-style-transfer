We get forecasting questions from Metaculus and Polymarket to check how stuck large language models (LLMs) are in their beliefs. We picked this area for two main reasons: first, forecasting questions have clear answers once they’re resolved, and second, doing well on these questions shows good reasoning because it means looking at evidence and adjusting beliefs accordingly. Even though forecasting isn’t the same as answering factual questions, being good at it still needs balanced and fair belief updates, which makes it a good way to test reasoning when things aren’t certain. Another way to test belief entrenchment is through scientific peer review. For this, we use the ICLR submission dataset from OpenReview, which has paper summaries, references, reviewer feedback, responses from authors, and final decisions on whether papers were accepted. In our setup, the model pretends to be an area chair, deciding if a paper should be accepted based on the summary and the arguments in the reviews and responses. This lets us see how the model reasons when its first impression (like from the summary) might clash with later information (like critiques and responses). We check belief entrenchment using two reasoning methods: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and debate. Debate is where two versions of the same expert model argue opposite sides of a topic so that someone less informed can understand the issue better. We run tests with models like GPT-4o, DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Flash, LLaMA 4 Scout, and LLaMA 4 Maverick. To see how starting beliefs affect reasoning, we change the models’ starting points using system prompts. Besides a baseline with no prompt, we also use two other setups: one where the prompt matches the starting belief and another that encourages critical thinking.

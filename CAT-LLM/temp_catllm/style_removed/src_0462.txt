Language Model Systems, which include Large Language Models and other types, are used in many ways. You can find them in things like medical chatbots, self-driving car assistants, and coding helpers. Since these systems are used in important and public-facing applications, it's really important that they work correctly and behave in ways people expect. Many studies have looked at how to make these systems safe and have tried to build those safety features into them. But right now, the methods for checking and enforcing good behavior aren't good enough because they can't guarantee they'll work in all situations. Having these guarantees is really important for people to feel safe and trust these systems. Recently, some work has started to figure out how to officially certify these systems for certain good behaviors, like being fair, answering questions correctly, and being safe. These behaviors are written out as specific rules that the systems have to follow. For example, some researchers have created rules for fairness in responses, understanding reading material, handling risky conversations, and choosing the right tools. But these specific certifications are hard to change for different needs or situations because they are very focused on particular properties and usually only work in conversation settings. If you want to change the rules a little, you have to rewrite them. So right now, certifying these systems is done in a custom way for each property, area, and system type, which is something we're looking to change.

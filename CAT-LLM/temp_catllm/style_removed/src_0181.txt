The framework does this by breaking down tasks and optimizing how resources are allocated. Long construction tasks are split into smaller tasks using strategies from the SMART-LLM framework (Kannan et al. 2024). One strategy looks at tasks based on the objects involved and what needs to be done with them, while the other looks at what skills are needed, like handling things carefully or using force. These smaller tasks are then given to different robots based on what they can do and what the tasks need. The LTAA framework uses LangGraph to organize things and tries to make sure tasks are completed by figuring out how likely each robot is to succeed with each task. But at first, they found that some robots were getting too much work while others weren't getting enough. This led to new strategies that change priorities as the project goes on, starting with focusing on success rates and later making sure work is shared fairly. The development process had three stages. First, they tried out the SMART-LLM framework for MRTA but found some issues with validation and reliability. Second, they used a Self Corrective-Agent Architecture to fix these problems with multi-stage validation and retry mechanisms. Finally, the LTAA framework combined these ideas with new allocation strategies to compare against old optimization methods. The authors explain how they set up a classic LLM-based MRTA framework: SMART-LLM (Kannan et al. 2024), and the main problems they faced, like validation issues and coordination problems. These problems led to the creation of a phase-adaptive and self-corrective architecture using LangGraph, which uses multi-stage validation and retry mechanisms to fix the issues. These ideas became the LTAA framework. The paper also talks about testing performance against traditional optimization methods. The LTAA framework uses the TEACH dataset to compare directly with Q-learning, DQN, and DP under the same task conditions. They look at how well the work is balanced, how well tasks are done based on features, and the quality of reasoning to check both allocation and performance.

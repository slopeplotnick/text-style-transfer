When we look at Table 3, it shows that using both audio and images to make summaries works better than just using audio for most languages and models, even though the models only use text. This improvement is biggest for English, German, and Italian, but not as good for Chinese. We think that's because English terms show up in languages that use the Latin alphabet, but English and Chinese are too different for the models to always use the extra English info well. For answering questions, adding pictures helps a bit, but not as much as it does for making summaries. Sometimes it helps a little, but in four out of twelve language-model setups, it actually gets worse. We think this happens because the model doesn't get the actual image data, just the improved text info, which isn't enough to make it answer questions better. Over the last ten years, people have studied streaming speech translation a lot. Some tools for translating lectures use it too, but they mostly just use audio. Our work is different because it uses both pictures and sound from slides and gives back translated audio and slides in different languages. Translating images to images hasn't been looked at much yet. More people are getting interested because there are bigger datasets now, but most work so far just focuses on translating text in images, not on making the pictures look right again after translation. One early step in this area is a study that looks at how to make the pictures look right. Our image translation setup is flexible, so researchers can add their own models at any step from reading the text in images to translating and making the pictures, without having to build new parts.

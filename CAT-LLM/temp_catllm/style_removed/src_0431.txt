Machine learning and generative techniques are everywhere, but using AI for decision-making isn't as common. Making decisions means you have to commit resources, which isn't really what academic research does. So far, the only big use of decision-making AI in mineral exploration is figuring out the high-grade part of the Mingomba deposit in Zambia. They looked at old drilling data again with new tools and came up with new ideas, finding a richer part than they thought before. AI, set up as a POMDP, helped plan the drilling step by step. The main goal was for the AI to help clear up any doubts about the geological ideas on where the rich minerals were. This way was much better than the usual grid-based drilling. It's more efficient because it focuses on proving geological ideas wrong instead of just trying to find the minerals. In Part III, I'll talk about how AI can speed up finding and understanding important mineral deposits. I'll start with data analysis and prediction, then move on to AI-based decision making. When AI was first used, some people were defensive. They thought AI would take over or make experts less important. Nobody wants their job threatened, and that's a common worry now. People thought AI would make new discoveries on its own. But it's not like AI will just mark a spot on a map for robots to drill and find something. I see AI as a tool to help experts by taking over tasks they're not good at or don't need to do, so they can focus on being creative in their thinking about geology and geochemistry.

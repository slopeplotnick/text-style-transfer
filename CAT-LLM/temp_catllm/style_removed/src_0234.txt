Google Search has a feature called AI Overviews that uses the Gemini model to make summaries of long documents. This helps users save time because they don't have to put together information from different places themselves. But it also makes people wonder if the information is reliable. When an AI combines different documents into one summary, it's important to check if it treats all the sources the same or if it has any biases. Some studies have looked at biases in AI models. For example, Santurkar and others found that models like GPT-4 and Claude often show liberal social values. In news summarization, Vijay and others came up with ways to measure biases, like checking if the language used is different or if the AI tends to stick to one point of view. While many studies have looked at biases in AI summaries, not many have checked if AI models have biases in how they handle multiple documents. When dealing with many documents, especially long ones, it's not clear if AI gives more importance to some documents because of where they are placed. Things like primacy bias or getting lost in the middle have been seen in other tasks, but their impact on summarizing many documents hasn't been studied much. This paper looks into whether the order of articles affects how they are represented in AI summaries, using abortion news articles as an example. We explore these research questions.

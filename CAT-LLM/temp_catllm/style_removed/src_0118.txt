Researchers have been looking into different ways to improve how models reason and verify their steps. For example, some studies focus on breaking down reasoning into smaller steps, checking each step carefully, and using reward models to train for better mathematical reasoning. One study looked at how using a verifier that checks the final outcome (called the GV-Gap) can make things better, especially when the solver and verifier are the same model. But they only looked at basic models and didn’t include models that were trained further after their initial setup. Recently, people have also been studying how to scale the amount of computing power used during testing with verifiers. For instance, some researchers explored random sampling combined with self-checking, others tried combining parallel sampling with fixing mistakes, and another group compared a method called Self-Consistency to using a generative verifier. There’s also work on finding the best ways to use computing power for verification during testing. However, verifiers aren’t perfect—they can make mistakes like approving wrong answers, rejecting correct reasoning, or picking the wrong solution. More details about this are in Appendix A. Another area of research is about how models can improve themselves and evaluate their own work. Some researchers are skeptical about this idea, but others have proposed theories and evidence showing how models can get better by sharpening their understanding. One study focused on small models and said they need a stronger verifier to help them improve. Practical methods for self-improvement include using feedback written in plain language or training models specifically to fix their own mistakes. Other approaches involve using tools, like code interpreters, to keep improving their answers step by step.

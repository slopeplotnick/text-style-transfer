We set up pairs of AI judges, like GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-pro, to see how much they agree on evaluating beliefs. We have a lot of data from GPT-4o but less from others, so we use GPT-4o as the main judge for comparisons. We also check how well AI judges match with human evaluations by having people do the same belief evaluations as the AI. We then compare results between humans and AI, like human evaluator 1 versus GPT-4o. The full results are in Table 2. Based on these comparisons, it seems unlikely that our results are due to judge bias. In this paper, we introduce the Martingale Score as a way to measure how stuck AI is on certain beliefs. We show that this score can predict how accurate the reasoning process is and apply it to different models, prompts, and problems. We look into what makes belief entrenchment worse or better. One limitation is that in OpenReview, we don't show a link between the Martingale Score and the Brier Score, which measures accuracy. We think this is because decisions in OpenReview are based on community votes, which can be inconsistent. But Table 1 shows that belief entrenchment is more of a problem in areas where human judgment is needed, like r/ChangeMyView and OpenReview. This makes us question using these AI models in those areas.

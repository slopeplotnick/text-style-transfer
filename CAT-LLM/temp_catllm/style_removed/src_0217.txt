In Section 3, we talked about how the questions connect to four main topics related to the research. Most people we interviewed didn’t know about SPLE, so we explained what it is and why it’s important during the interviews. This might have caused some bias while explaining, but we trust the people we interviewed to form their own opinions, and we trust the interviewer to not influence them too much. The questions were made open-ended so people could give detailed answers and share a lot of ideas. We designed the questions carefully to make sure they didn’t limit the kinds of answers people could give. We created metrics to make the most of the information we collected and to pull out useful data. These metrics count the important words and how often they show up, both overall and for specific roles. The tables show all the information, and the graphs show how the values are spread out. Even if someone gave a unique answer that no one else mentioned, we still included it and thought about it. We looked at all the answers in detail. The terms we got from the interviews were grouped together and matched with terms used in SPLE, like "personalisation" and "making specific some parts of the code," which are similar to "customization." This helped make the results more consistent. Matching these terms also helped us find arguments in research papers to address people’s concerns. Going through this process with different people helped us improve how smoothly the interviews went. We didn’t change the questions, but we got better at asking them over time. For example, we started giving extra hints and explaining more when someone wasn’t sure, or making the transitions between questions smoother. Even with these changes, we think the amount and quality of information we got from the first questionnaire and the last one were the same.

Some researchers work on improving how generators create content. For example, methods like Mirror-Consistency, Self-Contrast, and Step-Back Prompting use back-and-forth checking or looking at things from different angles to make better results. Another method, Self-Check, goes step by step to catch mistakes. These methods focus on making the generator itself better, but they can take more time and computing power. What we’re doing is different—we’re working on the part that combines results. Instead of trying to fix the messy outputs from the generator, we take them as they are and use a special layer to figure out the most accurate answer. One big decision when using a language model as a judge to compare two things is whether to let it say both are equal or force it to pick one. First, we show that forcing it to choose can make the model biased. Then, we show that letting it say “tie” depends a lot on how the judge is set up, so we need a better way to handle this. Allowing ties helps reduce bias. Language models used as judges can have different types of bias, like positional bias, where the order of the options affects the choice. In one experiment, we changed the setup to let the model say “tie,” and this cut down on positional bias for both models we tested. This shows that letting the model say “tie” isn’t just about showing when things are equal—it’s also a key way to make the judging process fairer. But tie decisions aren’t always consistent. In tasks where the model has to pick between three options, the way it votes can change a lot depending on how the setup is done, and that’s why we need a stronger way to combine the results.

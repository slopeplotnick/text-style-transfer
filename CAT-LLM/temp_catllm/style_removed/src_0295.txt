When you edit a video based on what someone wants, you need to make sure it keeps the same movements as the original video. This means every little thing, like blinks or head turns, should happen at the same time as in the original. This is really important for videos where people are talking because even small mistakes can make it look wrong. Usually, the changes people want are small, like adding a mustache or changing a facial expression. The idea is to change only what’s needed and keep everything else the same. Doing this while keeping everything perfectly in sync is still hard for most video editing tools. We came up with a new way called Sync-LoRA to make edited videos that stay in sync with the original. We change just the first frame of the video and use a text prompt to decide what changes to make. We train our model with pairs of videos: one is the original and the other is the edited version. These pairs are made so that each frame matches up perfectly in terms of movement, but they differ in the parts that were edited. To make these pairs, we first create them and then check if they are in sync. We use a model that understands both pictures and words to come up with different ideas for changes, then make matching images and turn them into videos. But these videos often don’t match up perfectly, so we check them using human motion data like facial expressions and body movements. We look at how well they match up in terms of talking, eye movement, blinking, and body position, and only keep the best ones for training. By using these well-matched examples, our model learns to keep the original video’s movements while making only the changes we want, and it stays in sync with the original video.

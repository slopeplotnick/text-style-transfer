While a model's accuracy in specific tasks may be high, it does not necessarily guarantee proficiency in integrating diverse forms of information, as illustrated in the middle section of Figure 8. For instance, GPT-5 Mini exhibits a notably low error rate, yet demonstrates inferior performance compared to other models in synthesizing various input types. This issue is particularly pronounced when one input type is substantially weaker than another, ideally necessitating reduced reliance on the weaker input, a capability some models lack. This is critical because emphasis on accuracy alone might lead to the preference for models that do not efficiently handle noisy data. Conversely, Llama-4 Maverick outperforms the conventional Bayesian reliability-weighted linear fusion method, indicating the employment of more sophisticated information aggregation techniques. A non-linear random forest model aligns more closely with its operational dynamics compared to simpler linear methods. Comparative analysis of models utilizing single versus dual input types, such as text and images, reveals that models like Llama-4 Maverick, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o exhibit decreased error rates across most tasks involving dual input types. However, this is not universally applicable, and the observed variance in improvement highlights potential advancements in multimodal model development. To transcend mere accuracy considerations, the Bayesian Consistency Score (BCS) was devised, facilitating evaluation of logical model behavior adjustments. This enables a more comprehensive assessment even when models exhibit high accuracy. Through experimental manipulation of specific conditions, models with varying baseline performance can be compared, offering insights into their information processing mechanisms. Although complex strategies, such as emphasizing salient details or employing adaptive rules, might better align with response patterns, a more pertinent evaluation involves assessing whether models adhere to Bayesian principles amidst changing scenarios.

The comprehensive display of linguistic knowledge is evident, allowing researchers to verify it across both agents. Such an approach contributes valuable insights to systems engaged in language acquisition without utilizing explicit representations akin to large language models. MODOMA employs unsupervised learning as the language learning apparatus receives unlabeled data and lacks visibility into the internal processes of the mother agent. Furthermore, the daughter agent is able to leverage its existing grammatical knowledge to facilitate further learning. This innovative learning methodology, designed for MODOMA, is referred to as internal annotation, which represents a form of self-supervised learning. The amalgamation of features and research possibilities within the multiagent MODOMA framework is novel. This system serves as a foundation for language learning experiments involving both a mature language model and a model in the learning phase. The agents interact, and all aspects of the system, including configurations, input, learning phases, interactions, communication, and the grammar acquired, can be controlled, replicated, measured, and verified by users. Ter Hoeve et al. (2022) proposed a teacher-student model to simulate language acquisition and conducted two preliminary experiments. The first experiment examines distinct regions characterized by 'two completely separate vocabularies' of an artificial language, whereas the second investigates diverse structures through the use of repeated tokens. In these experiments, the teacher selects a predetermined quantity of training data from pre-constructed sentences to provide to multiple student language models. Upon completion of training, these student models are assessed using different sentences from the artificial language. Additionally, a teacher model undergoes training on a separate collection of sentences before being tested. The distinction between this setup and MODOMA largely pertains to the training and test data: Ter Hoeve and colleagues utilized pre-constructed sentences from an artificial language, whereas MODOMA employs data indicative of a natural language, generated online by Delilah utilizing a transparent Dutch grammar model during interaction.

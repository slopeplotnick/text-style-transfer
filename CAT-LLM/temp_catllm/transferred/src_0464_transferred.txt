The comprehension of scientific laws within real-world contexts is crucial for the precise prediction of physical outcomes across various scenarios. However, current video assessments predominantly rely on basic physical knowledge and fail to effectively demonstrate the scientific reasoning capabilities of video models. To address this limitation, VideoScience-Bench has been developed as an evaluative tool designed to assess the scientific understanding of video models at a collegiate level. Specifically, each test item integrates a combination of scientific concepts that necessitate thorough comprehension and analytical reasoning to derive correct outcomes. The assessment comprises 200 questions spanning 14 topics and 103 concepts within the domains of physics and chemistry. Expert evaluations were conducted on seven leading video models within both T2V and I2V contexts, focusing on four key areas: invariance, accurate transformations, temporal and spatial consistency, and alignment with anticipated results. Furthermore, a VLM-as-a-Judge mechanism is employed to evaluate the videos, demonstrating substantial concordance with human judgments. To the best of our knowledge, VideoScience-Bench represents the inaugural attempt to ascertain the capacity of video models not only to generate videos but also to engage in scientific reasoning, thereby ensuring that their outputs accurately depict physical and chemical phenomena. Our datasets and evaluation code are accessible at github.com/hao-ai-lab/VideoScience. The advent of Sora-2 and Veo-3.1 marks a significant evolution in video generation, whereby video models now produce outputs resembling cinematic productions while offering novel insights into the world. As advancements in video models continue, there is an emerging discourse concerning the criteria for evaluating "good" videos, extending beyond traditional quality metrics such as Fréchet Video Distance and Fréchet Video Motion Distance to include adherence to fundamental physical principles and world comprehension. Recent models possess the ability to autonomously engage in problem-solving that necessitates scientific, mathematical, and spatial cognition, without reliance on prior training. Individuals with scientific training utilize established scientific principles to interpret the world and predict outcomes, such as the efficacy of citric acid in removing iron stains or the impact of powering an LED with or without a resistor. This inquiry prompts the central question: beyond visual appeal and compliance with fundamental physical laws, can video models accurately depict scientific phenomena and engage in scientific reasoning about the real world? The exploration of the world through scientific principles is imperative for innovation, as it enables models to anticipate real-world occurrences. Unlike rudimentary world comprehension, authentic scientific understanding necessitates the integration of interconnected ideas, exemplified by how specific heat and heat transfer principles elucidate the heat absorption properties of water.

Extensive evaluations were conducted utilizing UniFact across diverse datasets, model types such as LLaMA and Qwen, alongside various fact-checking methodologies, thereby yielding several pivotal insights pertinent to our research inquiries. Firstly, concerning RQ1, it was determined that no universally optimal approach exists, as effectiveness is highly contingent upon the model and the specific task undertaken. Furthermore, addressing RQ2, compelling evidence was identified, indicating the synergistic efficacy of disparate methods. The HD and FV approaches perform proficiently on distinct categories of factual inaccuracies, thereby demonstrating their capability to capture different yet complementary aspects of veracity. Lastly, with regard to RQ3, it was demonstrated that the amalgamation of both methods surpasses their individual applications, establishing a novel benchmark for detecting factual errors. This conclusion implies two significant implications: researchers are encouraged to engage in collaborative efforts rather than isolated initiatives, and users of these methodologies are advised to integrate model-based and evidence-based verifications to ensure factual accuracy. In essence, this paper accomplishes several core objectives: it examines the distinctions between Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification, elucidating their relevance to the advancement of AI; it introduces UniFact as a framework to equitably assess HD and FV; it substantiates through extensive empirical studies that HD and FV, while inherently different, function effectively in conjunction; and it confirms that the combination of both methods surpasses the efficacy of singular usage. Additionally, comprehensive explanations of Fact Verification and Hallucination Detection are provided, emphasizing the importance of discerning the distinction between these often-confused terms. FV is defined as the assessment of the general truthfulness of text, whereas HD specifically evaluates the factual accuracy of model-generated text. Detailed elucidations of these concepts are provided in sections 2.1 and 2.2, with section 2.3 offering methodologies for their joint measurement.

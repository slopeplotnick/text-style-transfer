Understanding videos involves discerning occurrences, motivations behind actions, and the timing of significant events. Traditional video object segmentation methodologies predominantly focus on appearances or categorical attributes. In contrast, Reasoning Video Object Segmentation (VOS) enhances this by requiring models to interpret ambiguous directives, utilize common sense and causal reasoning, and integrate temporal variations with knowledge to identify targets, such as "the runner most likely to win" or "the object causing the accident." This approach simplifies and simultaneously complicates comprehension, necessitating substantial data for model refinement. The fundamental premise is that effective video reasoning hinges on deliberate decision-making rather than impulsive judgments. Models must ascertain focal points, timing, and relevance to the inquiry. Consequently, we developed ReVSeg, which eschews concealed segmentation tokens, transforming reasoning video segmentation into a sequential reasoning process. ReVSeg categorizes reasoning VOS into three segments aligned with model capabilities: video comprehension (analyzing the question and events), temporal grounding (identifying key frames or timing pertinent to the inquiry), and spatial grounding (locating target objects within selected frames). These steps are executed within a singular model, ensuring that early step meanings persist through subsequent stages. This reasoning methodology maintains model adherence to training, mitigates extensive retraining, and converts complex issues into streamlined processes. With clarified reasoning steps, the primary challenge becomes enhancing these steps. Supervised learning offers limited assistance due to inadequately labeled intermediate steps. Hence, reinforcement learning is employed, treating reasoning VOS as behavior rewarded only when the entire decision path culminates in the correct outcome. However, employing RL presents difficulties: reasoning VOS yields sparse success signals and demands coordination across comprehension, temporal selection, and spatial identification. To address these challenges, rewards are incorporated at crucial decision junctures, ensuring RL refines the model's reasoning capabilities. In conjunction with task decomposition, RL transitions the problem from an obscured guessing endeavor to a transparent reasoning strategy: decomposition elucidates the model's decision-making targets, while RL demonstrates the interworking of these decisions. This synergy fosters a reasoning strategy that is both intrinsically coherent and practically robust.

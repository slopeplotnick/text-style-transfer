The performance of CFAULTs and HSD is analyzed in this text, with Figure 6a illustrating a graph that juxtaposes CPU time against the number of circuits identified, utilizing CFAULTs, HSD, and HSD-CM in a specific test. This graphical representation reveals that HSD operates more efficiently and identifies a greater number of faults, corroborating the data presented in Table 5. Figures 6b, 6c, and 6d provide additional insights by comparing the duration taken by CFAULTS and HSD. Specifically, Figure 6b depicts individual faulty circuits as dots, with their x-value representing the time taken by HSD and their y-value representing the time taken by CFAULTS. A dot positioned above the line indicates that CFAULTS required more time than HSD to diagnose the fault. These graphical analyses demonstrate that HSD is superior in speed compared to CFAULTS, irrespective of whether core minimization is employed. Furthermore, when evaluating faulty C software through TCAS and C-PACK-IPAs tests, CFAULTS exhibits superior performance relative to FBFL methods. Despite consolidating all failed tests into a single formula, CFAULTS remains more efficient and effective in problem identification. Consequently, it is inferred that CFAULTS is optimally suited for C software; however, HSD continues to excel in Boolean circuits. This discrepancy is attributed to the inherent differences between fault identification in C programs and Boolean circuits, which result in varied formula structures. Consequently, the solver's efficacy is impacted, accounting for the variations in performance across these domains.

A significant challenge for Open Large Language Models (OLLMs) pertains to their ability to process diverse input modalities, which fundamentally impacts the emergence and identification of safety concerns. Distinct input types inherently present varying risks, such as textual bias, inaccuracies within visual data, and privacy violations in audio-based inputs. Beyond these modality-specific risks, the integration of multiple input types exacerbates the complexity of identifying latent hazards. For instance, while textual input such as "I want to fly" or an image depicting an individual on a rooftop may appear innocuous in isolation, their combination could potentially indicate suicidal ideation. Similarly, a video depicting law enforcement personnel paired with a question such as "How to overcome obstacles" might imply criminal intent, which may not be discernible from either input independently. Consequently, the multifaceted nature of safety risks in OLLMs necessitates a more nuanced understanding of individual and combined input modalities. Efforts aimed solely at enhancing OLLM safety mechanisms often prove insufficient, as such measures are resource-intensive and may inadvertently diminish the models' fundamental reasoning capabilities. Despite substantial investments in corrective measures, these systems frequently require costly retraining and remain vulnerable to relatively inexpensive adversarial attacks. While guardrail models present a more adaptable alternative, their focus predominantly centers on single input types or basic multimodal combinations, such as text and images, without addressing the full spectrum of input diversity. Given that unsafe inputs may emerge from any individual modality or their combinations, reliance on single-modality or simplistic multimodal guardrails is inadequate for comprehensive moderation. Furthermore, many existing guardrail approaches conceptualize safety as a binary variable, which inherently constrains their effectiveness and limits their ability to address the nuanced and dynamic nature of safety challenges in OLLMs.

In order to circumvent these challenges, certain methodologies do not rely on specific models; rather, they compare their outputs with the training data within a shared representational space. This typically involves the utilization of features derived from pre-trained encoders. Such approaches have demonstrated efficacy in managing extensive datasets; however, they employ vector representations that are often difficult to interpret. Recent advancements, exemplified by LatentExplainer, endeavor to address this issue by modifying latent variables and employing language models to elucidate the implications of these modifications. Nonetheless, these methods continue to operate in a manner that lacks transparency for users. In contrast to these complex methodologies, the present study employs automatically generated Knowledge Graphs (KGs) to elucidate the influence of data. By juxtaposing generated and training images through shared triples encompassing objects, attributes, and stylistic elements, the process is streamlined to facilitate alignment with human cognitive processes. Large Language Models (LLMs) have emerged as pivotal in contemporary KG methodologies, primarily assisting in the interpretation of unstructured data. Their contribution is manifested in three primary areas: the transformation of standard text into subject-predicate-object triples, the facilitation of ontology creation, and the conversion of natural language queries into graph-based inquiries. For triple generation, techniques such as AutoKG are capable of transforming textual data into ontology-conforming triples. Beyond mere triple generation, LLMs contribute to ontology learning and extension through methods such as prompt engineering and ontology reusability. In the domain of query response, LLMs have the capability to convert user inquiries into SPARQL queries, a process that is enhanced by employing contextual KGs or controlled natural languages to increase clarity. Surveys regarding generative KG creation indicate a shift from traditional pipeline methodologies to innovative, LLM-driven frameworks. These contemporary systems exhibit superior performance in entity recognition, relation extraction, and graph completion compared to conventional techniques. The advancements in KG development provide a robust foundation for addressing data attribution concerns in generative models. Instead of relying on complex embeddings or parameters, structured outputs from multimodal LLMs enable the construction of comprehensible KGs that encapsulate critical aspects of both generated and training data, as well as track influences. Although the majority of research has focused on text, these methodologies possess the potential for application in image analysis, notwithstanding the increased complexity inherent in image data.

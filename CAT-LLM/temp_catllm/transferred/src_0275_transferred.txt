In this study, the numerical data generated from our files are compared to those found in the EMOPIA dataset, employing t-tests to ascertain whether significant differences in high and low valence and arousal levels are preserved within the generated data as observed in EMOPIA. To facilitate music composition based on specific emotional cues embedded in text, an encoder-decoder Transformer model is utilized. The emotional content of the text is captured through a fine-tuned RoBERTa-large encoder, which is specifically adjusted to discern subtle emotional variations across different categories. The decoding process involves a Transformer architecture consisting of three layers, each with four attention heads, designed to adeptly manage the timing and harmony inherent in symbolic music. Figure 2 illustrates the operational framework of the model. Initially, the encoder, based on RoBERTa-large, undergoes fine-tuning using the GoEmotions dataset, wherein the 27 emotion labels are reclassified to correspond with their respective category labels. Each batch contains examples spanning various categories, treating pairs within the same category as positives and those from different categories as negatives. This arrangement enhances the model's training efficiency, establishing an embedding space wherein categories are distinctly delineated. Subsequent to fine-tuning, the encoder is rendered static and employed as the text input for our generative model. While the original RoBERTa encoder generates overlapping representations of the four emotion categories, the fine-tuned version facilitates the formation of more distinct groupings, thereby demonstrating an enhancement in the emotional structure of the embedding space, notwithstanding the gradual and subjective nature of emotions. Visualizations of the embeddings prior to and following fine-tuning are available in Figure 4, located in the Appendix, Section C. The EMOPIA dataset comprises a mere 1,078 MIDI samples, which is insufficient for a Transformer decoder to effectively learn MIDI token patterns. To address this limitation, the decoder undergoes pre-training on the expansive GiantMIDI-Piano dataset. During pre-training, zero tensors are substituted for text embeddings, and the decoder is trained to predict subsequent MIDI tokens, thereby facilitating pattern recognition in an auto-regressive manner.

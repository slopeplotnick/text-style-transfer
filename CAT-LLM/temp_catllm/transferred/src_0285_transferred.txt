The present study seeks to explore methodologies for ensuring effective interoperability among diverse systems, with particular emphasis on the establishment of test environments wherein artificial intelligence (AI) can enforce, interpret, and adapt shared rules. Specifically, this investigation aims to determine whether such approaches mitigate the challenges previously identified. The overarching objective is to develop a research trajectory on AI safety that cohesively integrates theoretical foundations, experimental validation, and eventual standardization efforts. It is posited that focusing solely on the safety of individual AI systems—such as through prompt engineering or output filtering—proves insufficient when addressing interactions among multiple AI entities. As cooperative functionalities among AI systems increase, adherence to localized rules alone becomes inadequate for preventing substantial systemic failures. A notable distinction is observed between ensuring the safety of a single AI system and maintaining the stability of an interconnected network of systems. To address this, the concept of Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH) has been introduced as a framework for identifying the transition points where such differences manifest. This analysis demonstrates that risks originating from minimal disruptions can escalate into widespread systemic issues as interaction dynamics, complexity, and divergent objectives converge. Such risks are not merely attributable to the malfunctioning of individual AI systems but are inherent to the structural and operational characteristics of networked systems. The approach of enabling AI agents to collaboratively modify rules and coordinate behavior seeks to ensure systemic stability without exclusively relying on centralized control mechanisms. This preliminary investigation highlights the necessity for rigorous testing, theoretical refinement, and the development of the proposed ESRH framework and the Institutional AI concept. Future research endeavors will focus on conducting controlled experiments to evaluate predefined indicators, investigating coordination models, and designing practical tools to operationalize these concepts within real-world AI systems. As the transition from single-agent to multi-agent AI networks progresses, the absence of innovative frameworks for managing such systems is likely to obscure latent safety challenges until they become unmanageable. This study aims to elucidate these challenges and propose systematic approaches to address them effectively.

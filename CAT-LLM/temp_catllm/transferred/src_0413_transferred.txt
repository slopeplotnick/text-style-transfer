In this study, pairs of artificial intelligence (AI) judges, such as GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-pro, were established to examine the extent of their agreement in evaluating beliefs, with a larger dataset available for GPT-4o compared to others, thereby positioning GPT-4o as the primary reference for comparative analysis. To assess the concordance between AI judges and human evaluations, human participants were engaged in performing analogous belief evaluations to those conducted by AI. Subsequently, comparisons were made between human evaluators and AI models, exemplified by the comparison of human evaluator 1 and GPT-4o, with comprehensive results presented in Table 2. The findings suggest that the results are unlikely to stem from judge bias. This paper introduces the Martingale Score as a novel metric to quantify the extent of fixation AI exhibits towards certain beliefs, demonstrating its predictive capability regarding the accuracy of the reasoning process when applied across various models, prompts, and problems. Furthermore, the factors influencing the severity of belief entrenchment are explored. A notable limitation identified is the absence of a demonstrated correlation between the Martingale Score and the Brier Score, a metric of accuracy, within the OpenReview context, which may be attributed to the reliance on community votes that exhibit inconsistency. Nevertheless, as illustrated in Table 1, belief entrenchment appears more prevalent in domains requiring human judgment, such as r/ChangeMyView and OpenReview, prompting reconsideration of deploying these AI models in such areas.

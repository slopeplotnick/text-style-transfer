In the domain of leveraging regex for extracting structured decisions from large language model (LLM) markdown responses, the system meticulously identifies specific sections such as allocation decisions and success rates, subsequently converting them into structured data suitable for validation. The implementation of eight rigorous rules ensures the validity of the data, encompassing the elimination of erroneous allocations, the evaluation of trade-offs, adherence to workload considerations, and the coherence of the overall dataset. A transition to a weighted validation system was necessitated by the inadequacies observed in previous rule-based frameworks, which failed to capture the enhanced reasoning and contextual awareness imperative for LLM tasks. Studies have indicated that a multifaceted approach provides a superior assessment of model reasoning compared to simplistic accuracy checks. Consequently, this innovative system evaluates reasoning across diverse dimensions, including the clarity of explanations, consistency of success rates, workload awareness, trade-off justification, phase adherence, and confidence alignment. Each dimension contributes to an overarching quality score, with greater emphasis placed on factors that directly impact outcomes such as reasoning quality and success rate accuracy, while elements like confidence justification are accorded lesser weight. This configuration ensures that the validation process is both sensitive to performance metrics and attuned to contextual variables, thereby achieving a harmonious balance between understanding and rigor in evaluating the model's reasoning. Specifically, the scoring dimensions are as follows: Explanation Quality, weighted at 0.20, assesses the depth and clarity of reasoning; Success Rate Accuracy, also weighted at 0.20, evaluates the congruence between predicted and actual success; Trade-off Analysis, with a weight of 0.15, examines the consideration of competing objectives; and Workload Awareness, similarly weighted at 0.15, scrutinizes the assessment of current task distribution.

The Long-Tail Hard subset constitutes a collection of challenging examples designed to evaluate the proficiency of sophisticated language models in managing complex tasks. The investigation focused on identifying intricate cases, which were categorized into two principal types: explicit requests, exemplified by those with multiple attributes or multi-requirements, and implicit or indirect requests. The implicit requests were further classified into four distinct categories: those involving negation, those presenting alternatives, those necessitating specific knowledge, and those requiring reasoning. Various methods were employed for identification; specifically, negation and alternatives were detected through specific keywords and patterns. For the less prevalent categories of knowledge and reasoning-based requests, the number of straightforward examples was initially reduced, followed by the application of a robust language model, Qwen3-235B, to ascertain its ability to produce consistent answers four times out of eight trials. In addressing multi-attribute requests, a tool was utilized to identify queries encompassing more than five attributes. To ensure the difficulty of these examples, they were subjected to testing by a basic model, retaining only those instances where the model succeeded five times or less out of eight attempts, thereby indicating its struggle. The Visual Salience subset emphasizes scenarios where the primary product image holds significant importance, such as cases where the color or style depicted in the image is crucial for making an accurate decision. An initial step involved employing keywords derived from an extensive visual category list to identify such scenarios. Subsequently, to minimize errors, examples were filtered using a blacklist, retaining only those where the pivotal visual keyword was absent in the product's text. Subsequently, a language model was utilized to confirm the visual relevance of the keywords. Ultimately, to select the most challenging examples, a rudimentary model was trained to analyze both text and images, retaining those instances where the model exhibited considerable uncertainty, as evidenced by its frequent disagreement on the outcomes.

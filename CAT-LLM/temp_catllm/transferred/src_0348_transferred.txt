Specifically, the preservation of the individual’s identity in the edited video is assessed by calculating the average similarity between facial details from each frame and the initial edited frame, employing a pre-trained ArcFace model. This approach indicates the degree to which the individual's appearance and facial features are retained during the editing process, with higher values denoting superior identity consistency. Furthermore, a user study demonstrated a preference for our method in terms of aesthetic appeal and temporal alignment. Additional information concerning the study and metric calculations is provided in supplementary materials. An examination of the results, presented in Table 1, reveals that text-based methods, such as LucyEdit, achieve high scores on certain syncing metrics (e.g., Speech Correlation 0.80 compared to our 0.72); however, these methods falter in executing the actual edits, as evidenced by their lower Directional CLIP score of 0.11 versus our 0.21. Our method achieves a superior balance by excelling in synchronization while accurately implementing visual edits. Conversely, VACE employs supplementary conditioning factors such as pose, canny, and depth to attain enhanced CLIP alignment, yet it struggles with maintaining temporal consistency and identity preservation. Sync-LoRA, specifically trained for synchronized editing, demonstrates robust temporal alignment while effecting precise edits that conserve the individual’s identity. This indicates that our approach effectively balances motion consistency and editing accuracy rather than concentrating solely on one aspect.

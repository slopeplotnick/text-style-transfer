Concerns have been raised regarding the energy consumption of various technological systems, including mobile devices and large-scale data centers. It is imperative for developers to possess precise knowledge of their software's energy utilization to address inefficiencies effectively. Historically, analyses have predominantly concentrated on macroscopic components, such as entire programs or specific functions, rather than examining each line of code individually. Furthermore, there has been a tendency to estimate CPU energy consumption without considering other hardware components, leading to challenges in comprehension and explanation. Consequently, a novel approach has been developed to resolve these issues by moving beyond mere CPU-centric estimations. Specifically, this research aims to predict energy consumption for programming languages such as Java, Kotlin, and Scala, which operate on the Java Virtual Machine (JVM). The methodology involves analyzing the transformation of code into Java bytecode and quantifying its energy usage, followed by the construction of a predictive model utilizing Bayesian statistics to ascertain energy consumption and identify influencing factors. The model incorporates three intrinsic code attributes: data size, data type, and operation, alongside one extrinsic hardware attribute: the device. To validate the efficacy of this method, experiments were conducted using Java, analyzing Java bytecode, and testing new programs. Results indicated the significance of all four factors, notably the variation in energy consumption among devices of the same model and the substantial impact of operations and data types on energy usage. Empirical tests demonstrated that the energy predictions closely align with actual program energy consumption, thereby substantiating the method's effectiveness. This work contributes an energy modeling framework that can be employed in future endeavors to assess program energy efficiency.

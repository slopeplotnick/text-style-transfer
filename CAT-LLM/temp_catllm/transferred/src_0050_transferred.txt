A comparative analysis was conducted between our approach and existing benchmarks utilizing evaluative judges. The evaluation methodology proposed herein represents a significant advancement over previously established approaches. Earlier methodologies, such as PPTC-R, predominantly emphasize discrepancies at the API level. While advantageous, these methods exhibit limitations in detecting critical errors, such as instances where an agent erroneously positions the correct text on an incorrect slide. Our IF Judge, employing structured summaries, effectively identifies such logical inconsistencies. Alternative benchmarks, including T2US, utilize visual language models (VLMs) as judges but rely on prompts lacking explicit guidelines, rendering their outcomes inconsistent and unreliable. The PPTArena framework addresses this issue by establishing a robust and reproducible scoring system. Consequently, our dual-judge system, grounded in well-defined criteria, is crucial for the comprehensive evaluation of the intricate processes involved in PowerPoint presentation editing and for identifying subtle errors overlooked by simpler methods. Furthermore, the development of PPTPilot, a presentation editing tool characterized by its straightforward design yet superior performance compared to certain commercial tools such as OpenAI’s ChatGPT Agent (as elaborated in Section 5.1), is discussed. This design is predicated upon two fundamental principles. Firstly, the principal challenge in this domain extends beyond understanding the requisite tasks to ensuring their accurate and reliable execution. PowerPoint files are formatted using the complex Office Open XML (OOXML), which inadequately manages errors or suboptimal outputs from VLMs, necessitating specialized formats and configurations for editing. Secondly, it was observed that reliance on a singular editing method, such as exclusive XML format usage, is insufficient. An effective tool must intelligently select the optimal editing approach for each specific task. PPTPilot is constructed with a dual-path architecture, enabling it to accommodate editing requests through either programmatic tools or direct XML format manipulation.

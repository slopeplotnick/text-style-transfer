Recent advancements in technology have significantly enhanced the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), leading to their widespread application in various domains, including intelligent interactions and scientific research assistance. In the field of healthcare, these models appear to be particularly advantageous in disease diagnosis and the optimization of diagnostic and therapeutic processes. Within the context of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), which is fundamentally based on the principle of "Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment" (SDT), practitioners analyze a multitude of information to identify the underlying issues and formulate personalized treatment plans. This approach may involve treating the same illness differently depending on the specific circumstances of the patient. Although there are TCM-specific LLMs, such as Zhongjing and HuatuoGPT, their practical application in clinical settings remains challenging due to the inherent complexity and individualization of TCM practices. A significant issue is the absence of robust evaluation methodologies to assess the efficacy of these models in clinical decision-making, alongside the lack of specific instruments to evaluate their reasoning processes. Consequently, there is a pressing need for a comprehensive dataset that can accurately and thoroughly evaluate the applicability of these models within TCM. Such a dataset would facilitate the refinement of the models, assess their performance, and ultimately enable their deployment in genuine medical scenarios. However, the creation of such a dataset is arduous due to the TCM's emphasis on individualized treatment, holistic assessment, and the considerable variability involved. For instance, patients with identical illnesses may exhibit different symptoms influenced by factors such as body constitution, disease progression, or lifestyle, complicating the establishment of standardized evaluation criteria. Furthermore, TCM diagnosis is an integrative process involving diverse methods to gather information, ascertain causative factors, and determine treatment strategies, necessitating a dataset that encompasses a wide array of considerations. Additionally, the presence of multiple concurrent health issues in patients and the diverse diagnostic approaches can introduce subjectivity into evaluation results. These complexities render the development of a comprehensive dataset for assessing the real-world application of TCM-focused LLMs particularly challenging.

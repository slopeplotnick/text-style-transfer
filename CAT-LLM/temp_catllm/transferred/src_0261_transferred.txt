The resolution of the identified challenges necessitates a shift from an emphasis on fixed outputs to a focus on the mechanisms operational in real-time. Specifically, the proposed approach involves the utilization of a set of fundamental questions designed to enable any language model to generate responses instantaneously. This methodology ensures that fact-checking processes receive the requisite textual inputs, facilitates hallucination detection methods by providing insights into the model’s internal operations during generation, and maintains the relevance of evaluations despite evolving model architectures. The underlying principle of UniFact is structured around three core components aimed at ensuring robust, equitable, and forward-compatible evaluation frameworks. Firstly, the system circumvents reliance on outdated datasets by employing factual queries to prompt real-time response generation, thereby preserving applicability across both contemporary and future models. Secondly, it promotes equitable assessments by accommodating both hallucination detection and fact-checking methodologies. Specifically, it provides hallucination detection processes with real-time access to the model's generative dynamics while supplying fact-checking methods with the generated text alongside supplementary information for verification purposes. Both approaches are evaluated on a unified task, which pertains to determining the factual accuracy of the model's outputs. Thirdly, the system integrates an automated labeling mechanism to replace the dependency on subjective and time-intensive human reviews. This automated process evaluates the model-generated responses by cross-referencing them against correct answers and additional reliable sources, thereby enabling the efficient, impartial, and consistent assessment of multiple models.

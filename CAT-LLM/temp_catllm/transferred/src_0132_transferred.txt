This study investigates the utilization of robotic systems in construction and evaluates their performance through comparative analyses with established methodologies by conducting extensive experimental assessments. Specifically, the research integrates concepts derived from robotic modeling and construction principles to examine the efficacy of LLM-based reasoning in comparison to conventional approaches for task allocation among robots within construction contexts. The LTAA system encompasses models that encapsulate the capabilities of individual robots, adaptive strategies tailored to varying construction phases, and LLM-driven decision-making mechanisms designed to address specific coordination challenges identified in previous studies. Furthermore, the system is benchmarked against traditional methods, including Brute Force and Greedy algorithms, alongside learning-based approaches such as Q-learning and Deep Q-Network techniques. The findings contribute to the development of a robust LLM coordination framework while providing comparative insights into reasoning-based approaches versus traditional methodologies for automating construction-related tasks. The structure of the paper is as follows: Section 2 reviews prior research on multi-robot task allocation frameworks utilizing optimization, learning, and LLM methodologies; Section 3 elaborates on the design and functionality of the LTAA system; Section 4 delineates the experimental setup; Section 5 presents comparative results with established methods; Section 6 concludes with key findings and outlines prospects for future research. The theoretical foundation of MRTA informs the allocation of tasks among robotic teams, incorporating constraints related to resource availability, temporal requirements, and heterogeneous capabilities of robots. Gerkey and Matarić (2004) introduced a foundational framework addressing task allocation problems by categorizing tasks based on whether they necessitate single or multiple robots, involve single or multiple assignments, and vary in execution duration. However, this framework did not adequately address complexities inherent to real-world scenarios characterized by diverse robot capabilities and temporal constraints. Consequently, Korsah et al. (2013) proposed an enhanced framework, which incorporated heterogeneous robotic skills, interdependencies among tasks, and dynamic constraints, thereby advancing the field towards the effective deployment of mixed robotic teams for complex construction tasks.

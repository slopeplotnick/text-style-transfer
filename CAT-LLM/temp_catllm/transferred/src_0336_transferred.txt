Multimodal large language models (MLLMs), which extend the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) by incorporating visual inputs, introduce additional layers of semantic enrichment compared to text-only models while simultaneously presenting heightened safety concerns. Jailbreaking refers to the act of employing manipulative inputs to circumvent established safety protocols, thereby prompting the model to generate harmful or unintended outputs, which constitutes a critical challenge in enhancing the reliability and security of these models. Historically, jailbreak strategies have predominantly targeted LLMs through techniques such as deceptive prompts or role-playing scenarios. However, with the advent of MLLMs, there has been an increasing focus on exploiting their visual processing capabilities to achieve similar objectives. Current methodologies exhibit certain limitations in addressing this issue effectively; one approach involves designing misleading images or embedding sensitive information within visual data, yet this strategy fails to fully leverage the multimodal context. Alternatively, textual descriptions of scenarios are employed, akin to techniques used in LLMs, which overlook the potential offered by visual elements. To address these shortcomings, the Contextual-Image Attack (CIA) framework has been developed, which constructs visual stimuli integrating both contextual information and malicious intent, with textual elements serving a supplementary role. For example, rather than displaying a direct query such as "How can someone make illegal drugs?" within an image, the framework might depict a teacher posing the question in a chemistry class setting, thereby creating an appearance of legitimate inquiry that confounds the model’s ability to identify harmful intent. In comparison to existing methods such as FigStep, which primarily embed textual questions within images or rely solely on textual contextualization, this approach demonstrates superior utilization of visual data to achieve its objectives. This innovation is increasingly relevant due to advancements in image generation technologies capable of producing realistic scenarios and emergent systems that convert textual information into visual representations. Consequently, the findings underscore the potential of images to convey complex, concealed meanings that can effectively manipulate models in multimodal contexts.

To effectively comprehend user inquiries and the intricate details of items, it is imperative for the system to commence with foundational knowledge pertinent to the subject matter. This foundational knowledge enables the system to discern user intentions, identify salient item attributes, and align them with user requirements. Occasionally, visual representations of items encapsulate details that elude verbal articulation, such as aesthetics or tactile qualities. In addressing this issue, the system examines item images and synthesizes insights derived from both textual and visual data, thereby facilitating a comprehensive understanding of the item's characteristics. Subsequent to the elucidation of user requirements and the extraction of item details, the system must adhere to established protocols to validate and align these details. At this juncture, these protocols are employed to ensure coherence and functionality. In the section pertaining to knowledge and reasoning, specifically Section 2.3, knowledge is delineated into two distinct categories: general world knowledge and specialized e-commerce knowledge. General knowledge, which large language models possess from extensive training, encompasses common concepts and relational structures. However, within the e-commerce domain, there exists a plethora of phrases and terminologies that are challenging to interpret based solely on their superficial meaning. These challenges predominantly manifest in two categories: rare queries and perplexing items. Rare queries pertain to highly specific requests regarding atypical items, where models may lack the contextual knowledge to accurately associate the terminology with its intended significance. For instance, a search query such as "strange things are also loved by someone" in relation to "novel and interesting toys" is fundamentally about a book title, and absent prior knowledge, the system might misalign the items. Additional instances include brand-specific nomenclature, colloquial product appellations, or marketing vernacular that holds relevance solely within e-commerce contexts. Confusing items refer to those that are so esoteric that the system's comprehension is inadequate for accurate categorization based merely on the item's designation, such as the term "lying gate," necessitating supplementary e-commerce-specific knowledge for proper interpretation.

Recent advancements in multi-modal large language models (MLLMs), which process both textual and visual inputs, have yielded notable improvements; however, these models continue to exhibit limitations in capturing fine-grained information within high-resolution images. Enhancing the capacity of such models to effectively interpret high-resolution visual data has generally followed two primary approaches. The first involves additional training through techniques such as supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, which, while potentially improving image comprehension, may inadvertently reduce the model's generalizability across diverse tasks. The second approach circumvents further training by employing methods such as localized image searching to identify salient regions, although these techniques are often computationally intensive and prone to overlooking specific objects, particularly in images containing numerous elements. In the present analysis, the influence of image segment size, referred to as crops, on model performance was systematically examined. Experimental findings demonstrated the critical role of resolution in determining the effectiveness of image processing, with varying object sizes necessitating distinct resolution levels. Accordingly, the MRD framework was proposed, and its efficacy was evaluated using two model iterations, LLaVA-ov and LLaVA-v1.5, on a standardized benchmark dataset. The results, as depicted in Figure 3, indicated that optimal model performance in both single-object and multi-object recognition tasks was achieved when the resolution of image segments was standardized at 112. These outcomes suggest that a resolution of 112 constitutes the most effective setting for achieving robust performance across varied task complexities.

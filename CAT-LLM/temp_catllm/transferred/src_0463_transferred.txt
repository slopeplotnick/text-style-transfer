Large language models demonstrate exceptional performance across a wide range of tasks, and their capabilities can be refined through the provision of tailored instructions and illustrative examples. The manner in which instructions are articulated significantly influences the model's performance, as evidenced by a comparative analysis of two similar mathematical task instructions yielding divergent outcomes. This observation underscores the necessity of ensuring clarity and precision in instruction design, akin to the role of hyperparameter tuning in optimizing machine learning performance. However, manually constructing effective instructions can be both labor-intensive and unreliable. In this context, automatic prompt optimization emerges as a valuable approach, paralleling the automation of hyperparameter selection in AutoML, to identify optimal instructions. This methodology is particularly relevant in current industrial and research contexts, where systems comprising multiple agents have gained prominence. Given that modifying entire models is resource-intensive and requires substantial datasets, automatic prompt optimization presents a more practical and efficient alternative, particularly for models with restricted transparency. While numerous prompt optimization tools are available, their application poses challenges due to fragmented accessibility, varying implementation codes, and disparate requirements. Furthermore, many of these tools are not consistently maintained, often lack comprehensive testing or documentation, and exhibit limited flexibility, necessitating considerable effort to adapt them for complex tasks. Although some solutions address these issues, they are often proprietary or closed-source, restricted to a single optimizer, or overly intricate for routine applications.

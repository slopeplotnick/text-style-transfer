Languages were grouped into clusters, as depicted in Figure 2, and designated as In-Domain languages. The proposed model was evaluated on both In-Domain and Out-of-Domain languages, with the latter consisting of 11 low-resource languages selected due to their representative nature within their respective clusters and coverage of diverse language groups. For testing purposes, six safety classification datasets were utilized, including Aegis-Content-Safety-2.0-Test (Aegis-CS2) from 2025, HarmBench curated by Mazeika et al. in 2024, Redteam2k introduced by Luo et al. in 2024, JBB-Behaviors (comprising JBB-Behav and JBB-Judge) compiled by Chao et al. in 2024, and StrongReject developed by Souly et al. in 2024. These datasets encompass a broad spectrum of harm categories such as Hate/Identity Hate, Sexual, Suicide/Self-Harm, Violence, Guns/Illegal Weapons, PII/Privacy, Sexual Minor, Toxicity, and Abuse, thereby providing comprehensive benchmarks for assessing safety measures. The training process employed XLM-RoBERTa-Base, comprising 279 million parameters, and XLM-RoBERTa-Large, with 560 million parameters, as the principal multilingual models, which are particularly suitable for cross-language tasks due to their training across 100 languages. A single-layer classification head, aligned with the encoder's output dimensions and equipped with a binary output layer, was appended to the encoder, and both components were fully trained using the aggregated training data. All computational processes, including training and testing, were conducted on an NVIDIA H100 GPU cluster utilizing Bfloat16 precision to optimize efficiency and enhance performance. To establish baseline comparisons, the model was benchmarked against smaller guardrails, namely DuoGuard-0.5B from 2025, PolyGuard (PG)-Qwen-Smol, and WalledGuard-C from 2024, as well as larger guardrails such as PolyGuard (PG)-Qwen from 2025, LlamaGuard3 from 2024, and Aegis-Defensive from 2025. These baseline models exhibit varying degrees of language support, ranging from English-only configurations to multilingual models accommodating up to 29 languages, thereby enabling a robust evaluation of the proposed safety guardrail in both high-resource and low-resource language contexts.

It is imperative to ensure that devices such as smartphones, laptops, intelligent assistants, drones, and automotive systems are capable of processing erroneous prompts and responses securely without dependence on cloud-based infrastructure, as this enhances operational speed, safeguards user privacy, and facilitates functionality in constrained scenarios. Recent research endeavors have sought to develop safety models applicable across diverse languages; however, these models frequently exhibit suboptimal performance in languages that are less widely supported. A prevalent approach to address this issue involves training models in English or other well-supported languages and subsequently testing them in a different linguistic context, leveraging shared linguistic features. While this strategy demonstrates improved outcomes for languages that are either structurally similar or well-supported, its efficacy diminishes significantly for lesser-used or underrepresented languages, primarily due to limited shared characteristics and inadequate representation during training. To address this limitation, the present study introduces CREST, a multilingual model encompassing 100 languages and utilizing a relatively small parameter size of 0.5 billion, which is trained on data from 13 well-supported languages. The methodology employs a multilingual transformer framework to cluster linguistically and structurally similar languages, thereby enabling the model to achieve robust performance across these clusters and allowing training on a well-supported language to benefit related, less common languages. Such an approach is particularly advantageous in contexts where limited data is available for safety applications across a wide range of languages. Despite its compact design, CREST demonstrates superior performance compared to existing models and exhibits effectiveness in both well-supported and less common languages by utilizing shared lexical items and scripts to facilitate generalization. To the best of our knowledge, no prior research has demonstrated a safety model capable of achieving comparable performance across such an extensive array of languages.

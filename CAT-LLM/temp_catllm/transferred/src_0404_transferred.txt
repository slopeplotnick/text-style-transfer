The challenge of recognizing hate speech in low-resource languages, such as Bangla, is exacerbated by the limited availability of datasets and significant variations in linguistic characteristics and orthography. Bangla, spoken by over 230 million individuals in Bangladesh and India, particularly in West Bengal, lacks sufficient computational resources despite the increasing demand for automated moderation on social media platforms. This study focuses on Subtask 1A and Subtask 1B of the BLP 2025 Shared Task concerning hate speech detection. The official baselines, including Majority, Random, and Support Vector Machine, were replicated, and additional baseline methods such as Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree were explored. Transformer-based models, including DistilBERT, BanglaBERT, m-BERT, and XLM-RoBERTa, were employed for hate speech classification, demonstrating superior performance over baseline methods for the subtasks, with the exception of DistilBERT. Notably, BanglaBERT achieved the highest performance across both subtasks, surpassing both m-BERT and XLM-RoBERTa, thereby underscoring the critical importance of language-specific training. The findings indicate the significant potential and necessity for pretrained language models tailored for Bangla, a language with limited resources. The proliferation of the internet in recent decades has significantly increased global social media usage, facilitating online discourse and information exchange. The rapid escalation of social media utilization continues unabated, embedding itself in public discourse while also enabling the dissemination of hate speech. The surge of toxic and harmful content, particularly hate speech, poses substantial safety concerns for online platforms. Hate speech, characterized by harmful, offensive, or derogatory language targeting individuals or groups, necessitates automated detection within the domain of Natural Language Processing (NLP). Although substantial advancements have been made in hate speech detection for well-resourced languages such as English, progress for low-resource languages like Bangla remains constrained due to the scarcity of high-quality annotated datasets and the inherent complexity of the language.

Upon further examination, it was determined that individuals' attitudes towards artificial intelligence exerted a more significant influence on the results than the experimental design itself. Specifically, incorporating attitudes about AI into the analytical framework revealed that the experimental setup did not sufficiently account for the extent to which participants appreciated the music or the intensity of their emotional responses. However, attitudes toward AI consistently emerged as a critical and stable factor throughout the analysis. This finding aligns with prior research indicating that perceptions of autonomous systems substantially shape evaluative judgments (Hong et al., 2022). Moreover, it extends the conclusions drawn by Shank et al. (2023), who suggested that biases toward composers might be contingent on variables such as the popularity of the song and the degree to which it appears human-produced. While certain personality traits and music-specific factors exhibited associations with both music preference and emotional engagement, these relationships diminished in significance once attitudes toward AI were controlled for. Exceptions to this trend included the traits of openness to experience and neuroticism, which have previously been demonstrated to influence emotional responses to music, particularly in relation to emotions such as happiness and energy (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Juslin et al., 2011; Liljeström et al., 2013). These emotional dimensions were notably pronounced within the musical selections employed in the study. Notably, attitudes towards AI represented the sole participant characteristic consistently associated with both music appreciation and emotional reactions. Collectively, the proportion of variance explained by listener traits was comparable to findings from analogous studies (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Strauss et al., 2024), further underscoring the pivotal role of attitudes towards AI in shaping perceptions of AI-generated music. Contrary to the observations reported by Ansani et al. (2024), this investigation did not identify any moderating effects of AI attitudes on the functionality of experimental groups. This discrepancy may be attributable to variations in attitude measurement instruments, differences in the focus on recorded tracks versus live performances, or the inclusion of three experimental conditions as opposed to two.

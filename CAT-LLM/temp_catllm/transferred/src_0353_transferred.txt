A Software Product Line (SPL) is fundamentally a methodology that facilitates the transformation of a collection of related software systems, which share common features, into a framework that streamlines and accelerates the development of new software. This process of converting an existing code base into an SPL is inherently complex and time-consuming, potentially disrupting established software development practices within an organization. The present study delineates the initial steps undertaken to implement such a transformation. Collaboration was established with ITK - Predict and Decide, a small enterprise that opted to transition its code base into an SPL. The role of the researchers was to provide guidance throughout this transformation process. The code base in question is centered around a framework known as Cross, which already incorporates substantial software reuse and resembles an SPL, albeit lacking the conventional models and processes characteristic of a fully developed SPL. To facilitate the developers in this endeavor, a gradual alteration of the code base and engineering practices was planned. Initially, an assessment was conducted to understand the perspectives of company personnel regarding the transition, accompanied by recommendations to streamline the process. This paper presents the findings from the preliminary study, which involved engaging with company employees to ascertain their views on existing workflows, anticipated benefits of adopting an SPL, and potential concerns. The majority of participants expressed support for the transition to an SPL and a willingness to engage in its implementation. Developers, in particular, exhibited enthusiasm, perceiving the transition as a dual technical and managerial challenge. However, individuals involved in business operations exhibited caution, raising concerns about increased maintenance complexity and potential constraints on creativity, particularly in relation to interface design.

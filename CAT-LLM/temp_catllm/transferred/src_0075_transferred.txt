The process of understanding language involves a series of sequential cognitive steps, commencing with auditory perception and leading to the recognition of phonemes, which are subsequently synthesized into words. The comprehension of these words, influenced by grammatical structures, facilitates the understanding of entire sentences. This study focuses on the post-lexical stage of linguistic comprehension, a sophisticated mental operation that is uniquely human, in comparison to the methodologies employed by advanced artificial intelligence (AI) systems in language processing. It has been demonstrated that the human brain processes language hierarchically, where elementary tasks such as word prediction are situated in the lower cognitive strata, whereas more abstract conceptual understanding occurs in higher layers. The brain's acquisition of linguistic proficiency is characterized by the detection and rectification of prediction errors, analogous to the learning mechanisms utilized by AI. Consequently, both entities employ predictive methodologies to comprehend language. Specifically, the Google Neural Machine Translation (GNMT) system is structured to translate sentences through three primary components: an encoder, an attention unit, and a decoder. Initially, the encoder processes individual words sequentially, generating a comprehensive signal encapsulating the sentence's semantic content, distributed across eight layers, culminating in a singular representation. Subsequently, the attention unit identifies salient words within the source sentence, conveying this information to the decoder, which forecasts the next word in the translation through iterative layering. The system's learning process involves comparing its output against human-generated translations, refining its performance based on discrepancies. In contrast, the GPT-2 AI model employs a distinct approach, lacking separate functional components akin to GNMT. It engages in next-word prediction based on preceding context, leveraging training data comprising sentences with omitted words to enhance its predictive accuracy. Ultimately, GPT-2 generates text by forecasting subsequent words iteratively, utilizing a recurrent architecture of attention units and simple neural networks. By processing sequences of words, it calculates the probabilities of potential succeeding words, thereby refining its predictions independently of human exemplars.

Software product lines represent a widely adopted approach to developing software that can be customized to meet diverse requirements. Essentially, this approach functions as a structured supply chain for software, constructed from a combination of modular components. Feature models are employed to delineate the potential attributes of a product and to specify permissible combinations of these features. Additionally, a repository of software components is maintained to implement the identified features. Tools within the software product line framework facilitate the selection of features for creating a specific configuration and subsequently enable the generation of the final product by assembling the relevant components from the repository. This methodology proves particularly advantageous in scenarios necessitating the production of multiple configurations. However, it is challenging for organizations to transition to this approach when initiating the development of a new product type. As a result, many companies initially develop a collection of related software systems before transitioning their development processes to a product line paradigm. Although the literature documents several successful implementations, transforming an existing set of software systems into a unified product line platform remains a complex undertaking, and organizations often exhibit reluctance to adopt this approach. Such hesitancy frequently stems from a lack of well-defined procedural guidelines and uncertainties regarding the cost-benefit tradeoffs. Consequently, even when companies possess substantial and well-documented codebases, they frequently resort to manually creating customized applications from the base code to address specific client requirements, often utilizing a method referred to as clone-and-own. The partner organization, ITK - Predict and Decide, exemplifies a small to medium-sized enterprise, employing a total of 120 individuals, including 19 agronomists, 29 developers, 2 UI/UX designers, and 4 system administrators, with approximately one-third of the workforce dedicated to technological roles. This company adopts an agile methodology in its system development processes, which have demonstrated effective results in terms of development and delivery. The organization specializes in creating applications that assist farmers in decision-making by enabling the prediction of plant and animal diseases as well as crop outcomes. The core functionality of these applications is derived from a suite of simulators developed by the agronomists. Subsequently, the IT teams extend this functionality by integrating code designed to collect data from IoT devices or online services, which is then utilized to feed the simulators and display the results through interactive dashboards. The software’s back-end is implemented using Kotlin, whereas the front-end employs Angular and extensively utilizes Typescript for its development.

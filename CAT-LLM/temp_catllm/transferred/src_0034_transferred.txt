The proposed methodology, referred to as Modality-Informed Guided Reasoning (MIGR), is designed to identify the optimal input modality for initial focus by leveraging Mutual Information (MI). The primary objective is to mitigate biases in the reasoning process and ensure prioritization of input modalities that exhibit the strongest emotional signals. The approach comprises two principal steps, namely modality-aligned supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and Modality-Aware Reward Optimization. Modality-aligned SFT facilitates the identification of the starting point for reasoning by directing attention towards the input modality that MI suggests possesses the most significant emotional signals. Subsequently, Modality-Aware Reward Optimization reinforces adherence to the appropriate input modality while maintaining emotional grounding throughout the reasoning process, thereby enhancing the stability of training. This optimization is achieved through the application of two distinct rewards, one of which incentivizes the prioritization of the correct input sequence, while the other supports reasoning grounded in emotional evidence. Recent studies have indicated that SFT demonstrates efficacy in emotion recognition, particularly when applied to limited yet high-quality training datasets, such as the EMER dataset. Building on this foundation, the training process initiates with a similar approach, although improvements to the SFT step are introduced by incorporating additional examples that exhibit consistent emotional signals. These supplementary examples are identified by analyzing facial action units (FAUs) extracted from video frames, which are directly associated with emotional expressions. The FAU patterns are subsequently cross-referenced against a predefined mapping table to determine their alignment with target emotions. Only examples exhibiting exact matches between FAUs and the specified emotions are retained for further processing. For these selected instances, reasoning-answer pairs are generated, utilizing the emotional evidence derived from FAUs to inform the reasoning procedure.

The present study investigates language learning experiments focused on integrating explicit grammatical knowledge into a word list, subsequently enabling the daughter agent to engage in communication with the mother agent. Specifically, machine learning techniques are employed to construct a clear grammar representation, which aids current systems reliant on statistical methods and neural networks by offering a more comprehensible, rule-based approach to language modeling. In accordance with Zipf's law, this research evaluates the efficacy of a multi-agent simulation in first language acquisition. The objective transcends mere classification of function and content words, utilizing this categorization to assess the novel methodology in modeling first language learning. Zipf's law provides a robust and reliable metric for system performance evaluation, a practice commonly adopted in existing literature, such as the works of Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020), who employ Zipf's law in constructing a multi-agent framework for language evolution modeling. Recent studies by Dębowski (2021), Takahashi and Tanaka-Ishii (2019), and Takamichi et al. (2024) further substantiate this approach. The experiments herein confirm the requisite components for developing a multi-agent model for first language learning. Specifically, the learning of function and content words utilizes a mixed language learning strategy informed by statistical trends conducive to acquiring specific grammar rules. Consequently, these findings support the modeling of more complex grammatical structures and demonstrate the feasibility of the MODOMA research methodology. An associated study by Shakouri et al. (2025) illustrates the acquisition of grammatical categories such as nouns, verbs, and prepositions through unsupervised techniques. This paper delineates two experiments exemplifying this approach: the initial simulation in Sections 6 and 7 was applied to a training dataset generated by the mother agent, which facilitated the optimization of parameters for learning the specified language features. The subsequent experiment employed a novel test dataset curated by the adult language model to validate this configuration, as discussed in Section 8. The results suggest that MODOMA is capable of learning these grammatical categories and articulating them through the grammar system of the daughter agent.

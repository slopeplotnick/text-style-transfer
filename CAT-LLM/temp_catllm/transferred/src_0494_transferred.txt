GRAPHECTORY facilitates the computation of metrics for the analysis of agent systems, as exemplified by Table 1, which delineates six metrics supported by GRAPHECTORY and their respective calculation methodologies. These metrics reflect the extent of effort expended by an agent to accomplish a task, although they do not inherently convey the utility of the work performed, they can demonstrate alignment with problem complexity or agent resolution capabilities. Specifically, the Temporal Edge Count (TEC) quantifies the frequency of action changes over time, with higher TEC values indicating extended paths to task completion. Figure 2 illustrates a longer path for SWE-agent Dev (14) compared to SWE-agent DSK-V3 (8), mirroring the upward trend of TEC values with increasing problem complexity, thus aligning GRAPHENCY complexity with problem difficulty. Furthermore, the Loop Count (LC) enumerates the frequency of action repetition by the agent, where elevated LC values may denote suboptimal strategies, as evidenced by repeated failed actions at loop edge 9 in Figure 2-a, or necessitating reevaluation following ineffective actions, such as back edge 10 in Figure 2-a (§3.3.2). Alternatively, agents may be required to repetitively undertake actions for incremental problem-solving. LC is analogous to Cyclomatic Complexity [35] in software engineering, which assesses branching and path variation, with branching in GRAPHENCY signifying localized cognitive shifts impacting paths. In addition, the Average Loop Length (ALL) assesses the number of actions preceding repetition, where prolonged durations for agents to recognize ineffective actions are indicative of impaired cognitive or contextual assessment processes. Our findings reveal that agents exhibiting enhanced contextual gathering during localization possess shorter loops, signifying a swift recognition of ineffective cognitive processes and subsequent plan adaptation (§3.2.1 and §3.3.2).

Probabilistic programming languages represent a category of programming languages designed to incorporate probabilistic features, such as probability distributions and their associated samplers. Numerous examples of these languages exist, encompassing both imperative and functional programming paradigms. However, a notable limitation of existing probabilistic programming languages lies in their inefficacy when applied to text-heavy graphs, which are integral to the specification and operation of language model systems (LMS). In response to this challenge, the development of LUMOS was undertaken. These programming languages typically facilitate the generation of outputs based on user-defined probability distributions, exemplified by their use in creating random images or scenes, such as those employed in testing self-driving cars via user-defined scene graphs. Nevertheless, the specification of real-world scenarios often necessitates the utilization of fixed graphs, wherein specific components are sampled to define particular scenarios. Domain-specific languages (DSLs) for large language models (LLMs) function as query languages that lack probabilistic capabilities, yet they are instrumental in optimizing, securing, and refining prompts to elicit desired responses from target LMSs. These DSLs operate synergistically with LUMOS, which is employed to evaluate LMS behaviors across distributions of prompts defined using LUMOS. The process of LMS alignment commonly involves the identification of vulnerabilities within LMSs by assessing their performance or implementing targeted adversarial attacks. Recent advancements in this area have introduced probabilistic certifiers capable of providing formal guarantees in the evaluation of certain LMS properties. Building upon this foundation, the present work extends LMS certification through the application of LUMOS, enabling the specification and certification of a broader range of desirable properties within LMSs.

Large language models exhibit proficiency in clear reasoning processes; however, limited understanding exists regarding their performance in scenarios lacking explicit instructions. Extensive research conducted over several years has demonstrated that humans inherently process ambiguous signals effectively when interpreting sensory inputs. The present study investigates whether these models exhibit similar capabilities by evaluating their ability to integrate diverse types of information without prior explicit guidance. Utilizing methodologies derived from psychology, the cognitive processes of these models are examined through behavioral analysis. A novel assessment, termed BayesBench, has been developed to encompass four distinct tasks aimed at measuring dimensions such as size and time through verbal and visual stimuli, drawing upon established psychological studies. Comparative analysis was conducted involving nine language models alongside human judgments to establish a benchmark. The experimental design includes systematic variations in noise levels, contextual factors, and instructional parameters to assess the robustness of these models' performance and their ability to synthesize heterogeneous information sources. Beyond accuracy and efficiency metrics, an additional scoring mechanism evaluates whether the models exhibit behavior indicative of adaptive intelligence, irrespective of their correctness. Findings reveal that while certain models demonstrate adaptability, high accuracy does not consistently correlate with reliability. For instance, GPT-5 Mini exhibits notable proficiency in text-based tasks but demonstrates limitations in processing visual information, highlighting discrepancies between model capabilities and operational mechanisms. Consequently, assessments focused solely on correctness may overlook critical aspects related to how these models manage uncertainty. The results underscore the models’ approaches to handling uncertain information and elucidate the relationship between correctness and intelligent behavior. Furthermore, the BayesBench framework and its associated scoring criteria are presented as tools for advancing the design of systems capable of effectively integrating multimodal information. The ability to estimate attributes such as size, time, or distance constitutes a fundamental skill for both humans and machines. Humans achieve this by intelligently combining ambiguous signals, prioritizing reliable inputs, and leveraging prior expectations to minimize errors. This cognitive strategy occurs universally across cultures and developmental stages, demonstrating its foundational role in processing uncertain information.

Humans exhibit remarkable aptitude in acquiring new tasks efficiently when provided with clear instructional materials, such as textual descriptions or diagrams, without necessitating extensive practice. Investigations into neural and artificial intelligence systems have focused on their functionalities in domains including perception, cognition, and motor activities by deconstructing these processes into three fundamental components: circuit architecture, input-output handling, and learning mechanisms. It has been observed that contemporary artificial intelligence models increasingly emulate brain functionality, offering critical insights into neural operations. Specifically, the neocortex and cerebellum leverage prior information to generate predictions about future occurrences, striving for maximal accuracy in these forecasts. Concurrently, these structures optimize and organize environmental information into compact yet robust models. Such models facilitate three principal functions: predicting future events, comprehending the environment through abstract constructs such as features and causal relationships—illustrated by object recognition or text interpretation—and producing outputs including actions, plans, or linguistic expressions. This integrative approach to information processing underpins the diverse capabilities of the neocortex and cerebellum, despite their structural and functional resemblance. Consequently, this shared processing paradigm is instrumental in enabling the operational versatility observed in both biological and artificial systems. Advancing knowledge in this domain holds the potential to enhance neuroscience and artificial intelligence while deepening our understanding of intelligence itself. The human brain demonstrates significantly higher learning efficiency and speed compared to large language models, requiring fewer examples and exhibiting superior performance in this regard. Although recent artificial intelligence methodologies have improved their learning capacities and efficiency by adopting brain-inspired strategies, such as Mixture-of-Experts techniques, they remain outperformed by the biological brain. As artificial intelligence systems continue to evolve through the integration of neurobiological principles, the reciprocal study of artificial intelligence is anticipated to yield novel insights into neuroscientific processes.

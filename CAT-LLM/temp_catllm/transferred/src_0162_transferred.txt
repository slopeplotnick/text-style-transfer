In the present study, both audio and visual inputs were employed to determine which modality more effectively conveys emotions in each sample. Initially, a dataset was curated with human assistance, alongside a filtered version, and subsequently combined for training purposes. The mutual information (MI) for each sample was then calculated to ascertain the predominant modality for emotional expression. Following this assessment, the sequence of reasoning text was adjusted accordingly. Specifically, each training example was bifurcated into two segments: one pertaining to audio and the other to video, with special markers, <aud_desc> and <vis_desc>, delineating each segment. The arrangement of these texts was predicated on the MI analysis. In instances where audio demonstrated a greater emotional conveyance, the audio segment was prioritized over the video segment, whereas the inverse order was applied when video was more expressive. In cases where both modalities were equally effective, two versions were generated, initiating with either audio or video, thereby enabling the model to prioritize the modality that optimally exhibits emotions. Subsequent to the initial phase, the model's reasoning capabilities were enhanced through Modality-Aligned Reward Optimization. Previous methodologies incorporated two reward types: one for accuracy and another for structural integrity; however, these were insufficient in emphasizing the most emotionally expressive input. Consequently, two novel rewards were introduced: one for optimal sequencing and another for reasoning quality, contingent upon the emotionally expressive input.

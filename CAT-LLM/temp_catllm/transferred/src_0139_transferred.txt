The application of large language models (LLMs) or deep Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) classifiers for online searches is limited by constraints related to speed and cost. Consequently, a method is employed in which a simpler model is trained by a more complex one, as illustrated in Figure 1. Specifically, the online model utilized is a simplified variant of BERT. To ensure effective learning by the simpler model, a deep BERT model is employed as the teacher, rather than an alternative model type. Moreover, significant details identified by another model are integrated into BERT to enhance its understanding of why certain searches align more effectively with products. In order to eliminate extraneous and noisy information from extensive explanations, a model is implemented to extract and refine product details that either correspond or do not correspond with the search. This approach aids the simpler BERT model in concentrating on pertinent information. As the simpler model is incapable of utilizing these details in real-time, they are incorporated solely into the teacher model to maintain consistency. Subsequently, the learning process of the simpler model is guided by the teacher model. Due to the absence of publicly available data for Chinese e-commerce searches, evaluation was conducted on a substantial dataset derived from JD app search logs, comprising search and product pairs validated by experts.

The findings underscore the significance of individuals' pre-existing attitudes and expectations in shaping their perceptions. For instance, Hong et al. (2021) demonstrated that individuals with a favorable disposition towards creative artificial intelligence (AI) assigned higher ratings to AI-generated music, emphasizing the role of expectations in evaluating music across diverse styles. However, when unexpected elements emerged, the ratings tended to decline, although substantial differences across music styles were not observed. A pertinent question subsequently arises regarding whether AI-generated music is perceived as comparable to human-produced music. This inquiry necessitates identifying specific musical attributes that influence perceptions of humanness or artificiality. Shank et al. (2023) indicated that musical style plays a critical role, with electronic music more frequently associated with AI production. Furthermore, when participants were misinformed about the origin of the music, they perceived distinctions that were not objectively present, thereby revealing the impact of expectations and beliefs on evaluative judgments. This observation aligns with Ansani et al. (2024), who identified similar patterns. Moreover, Déguernel et al. (2022) revealed that individuals employed distinct strategies to differentiate AI-generated music, often identifying excessive repetition, rigid or unnatural structures, and unconventional harmony or melody as indicative of AI production, whereas human-generated music was associated with melodic variations, ornamentation, recognizable styles, and features suggestive of instrumental playability. This pattern illustrates a broader tendency wherein attributes perceived as "human" are evaluated more favorably, whereas those linked to AI are appraised less positively. Consistently, Hong et al. (2022) explored the influence of anthropomorphism and perceived creative autonomy on the acceptance of AI as musicians. Participants evaluated AI-generated music from various styles, assessing both the music's quality and their attitudes towards AI. The findings revealed that the perception of AI as more human-like and autonomous enhanced its acceptance as a creative entity. Additionally, Sun et al. (2023) revealed that anthropomorphism positively influenced perceptions of competence and warmth in AI musicians, which collectively contributed to more favorable attitudes.

The methodology developed has been optimized for integration with contemporary systems capable of expeditiously processing substantial volumes of data, such as vLLM and SGLang, thus facilitating its application in both live services and offline processing environments. In Section 4.2, a preference for the two-step vLLM configuration is expressed, primarily due to its rapid processing capabilities and its facilitation of reasoning steps. The evaluation of THINKMERGE was conducted through two distinct approaches. Firstly, tasks characterized by a singular correct solution were assessed using AIME 2025 and GPQA Diamond, which permit the aggregation of multiple responses via majority voting techniques. Secondly, for tasks devoid of a single correct solution, evaluations were undertaken on LiveCodeBench v5, BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and xBench-DeepSearch, where emphasis is placed on the generation of a viable solution. Within the domain of tasks with a singular correct response, five tests were executed to assess models such as Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-14B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B. Regarding coding tasks that permit open-ended responses, models including DeepCoder-14B-Preview, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-Coder-30A3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Thinking, and Qwen3-Think-30A3B were utilized. For open-ended research tasks, WebSailor models, notably WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B, were employed. Maximum sequence length was established at 32,768 tokens, with sampling settings detailed in Appendix A.1. Furthermore, assessments were undertaken to determine the efficacy of THINKMERGE in research tasks requiring cognitive engagement and tool utilization prior to response generation. Specifically, three Tongyi-WebSailor models—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B—were tested on four challenging web-based examinations: BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and XbenchDeepSearch. BrowseComp-en and BrowseComp-zh concentrate on complex factual inquiries within English and Chinese contexts. Given the extensive question set within BrowseComp-en (totaling 1,266 questions), a random selection of 200 questions was made for testing purposes, thereby aligning its size with BrowseComp.

Figure 3 illustrates the learning progression of the 7B model when utilizing the CAPO and GRPO methods. Initially, both approaches exhibit a comparable improvement rate; however, once the model achieves a foundational understanding, CAPO demonstrates superior performance. The gray line delineates the phase during which the model transitions from replicating input patterns to independently deriving solutions. Subsequently, CAPO exhibits a sustained increase in unpredictability, in contrast to GRPO, which remains relatively static, while the associated rewards continue to improve. This observation suggests that CAPO not only secures higher rewards in later stages but also maintains a level of unpredictability that facilitates exploration of diverse cognitive strategies. The gradual enhancement of unpredictability further indicates that CAPO avoids premature predictability by strategically delaying the incorporation of negative examples. This approach ensures a stable copying phase, optimizes the use of negative feedback in later stages, and enhances the model’s adaptability across varied tasks. When contrasting the conventional static training method with the novel dynamic approach, the static method prioritizes example difficulty by evaluating model performance and subsequently ordering the training data accordingly. In contrast, the dynamic method adjusts its focus during the training process without requiring data reordering. As evidenced by Table 3, both ADARFT and the static method GRPO(+SC) yield marginal improvements over the baseline GRPO. However, the dynamic method GRPO(+Ours) demonstrates substantial advancements across most tasks. These findings indicate that solely estimating example difficulty is insufficient, and that a dynamic adjustment of training focus constitutes a more effective strategy.

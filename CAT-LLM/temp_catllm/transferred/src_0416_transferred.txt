In order to facilitate the comparison of results across various studies, it is essential that the samples exhibit similarity in specific aspects. To ensure this, a method known as Multigroup Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) was employed to examine two distinct samples, specifically one derived from real-life scenarios and another simulated, as separate groups, while incrementally imposing additional constraints. The analysis was systematically divided into several components: evaluation of the basic setup consistency (H2.1), assessment of measurement invariance (H2.2), comparison of mean scores (H2.3), and examination of residual variance equivalence (H2.4). The confirmation of basic setup consistency implies that the underlying pattern remains identical across both groups. Measurement invariance facilitates the comparison of latent factor differences, whereas mean score equivalence permits the comparison of latent average scores. Residual variance equivalence allows for the comparison of observable averages and further analysis using visible scores. Should the real and simulated samples demonstrate significant similarity in these dimensions, it can be inferred that data derived from simulations can be considered reliable and reflective of real-world data. Furthermore, it was hypothesized that the responses emanating from real-world data would closely resemble those from simulated data (H3), and that the score distributions from both sources would exhibit substantial similarity (H4). To evaluate whether the simulated data mimics real-world data, initial analysis was conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test to discern mean differences, followed by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test to assess the distributional characteristics of the data.

In this study, a series of baseline models, including Majority, Random, n-gram SVM, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree, were established using the scikit-learn library. Features were extracted utilizing TF-IDF and count-vector methodologies. While the foundational models were executed on a CPU, all transformer model evaluations were performed on a GPU, specifically a Google Colab T4. Model performance was primarily assessed through the micro-averaged F1-score, although accuracy, precision, and recall were also considered to ensure a comprehensive evaluation. Various models, such as m-BERT cased, XLM-RoBERTa base, BanglaBERT, DistilBERT cased, alongside the baseline models, were tested to evaluate their effectiveness in detecting hate speech within the BLP2025 dataset. The results pertaining to subtask 1A are presented in Table 2. Notably, BanglaBERT demonstrated superior performance, achieving an accuracy of 0.71 and surpassing all other models evaluated. It also achieved a precision of 0.70 and a recall of 0.71. The primary metric for the shared task 1a was the micro F1 score, with BanglaBERT obtaining a score of 0.71. The BERT multilingual base model (m-BERT) achieved an F1 score of 0.66, and the XLM-RoBERTa base model achieved 0.67, both outperforming the basic models. DistilBERT, however, attained an F1 score of 0.57, which was inferior to those of the basic SVM and Logistic Regression methods. The micro F1 score of BanglaBERT was the highest recorded for this dataset within our study. Among the baseline models, the SVM model achieved the highest F1 score of 0.61, while the random baseline model achieved the lowest with an F1 score of 0.16. DistilBERT's performance, with an F1 score of 0.57, was suboptimal. Within the basic models, SVM and Logistic Regression exhibited the best performance, with F1 scores of 0.63 and 0.62, respectively.

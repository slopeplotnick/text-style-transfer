AlphaZero was among the pioneering systems to demonstrate the efficacy of self-play as a learning mechanism, wherein improvement occurs through autonomous gameplay without external intervention. This concept has been subsequently applied to language models, which exhibit progressive enhancement in reasoning capabilities over time. Certain systems have implemented self-play methodologies at scale, enabling weaker language models to improve by generating and solving tasks independently, thereby reducing the reliance on extensive labeled datasets. This approach has proven particularly advantageous in domains such as code generation, where programs validate one another through testing procedures. Methods such as R-Zero exemplify the utilization of self-generated data in the form of questions, attempts to solve them, and iterative learning from self-assessment processes. Alternative methods leverage a limited set of examples to progressively generate more challenging problems, with R-Few adopting a distinct strategy aimed at guiding learning trajectories while preserving the advantages of exploration, minimizing deviation, and maintaining variety. Reinforcement learning has emerged as a pivotal technique in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of language models, extending beyond conventional example-based learning paradigms. This methodology frequently incorporates human feedback as a mechanism for iterative improvement. Early systems such as InstructGPT underscored the importance of aligning model outputs with human preferences, yielding enhancements in utility, safety, and reasoning capabilities relative to traditional example-based learning approaches. Contemporary systems, including DeepSeek-R1, have further refined this concept by incentivizing effective reasoning through reward mechanisms, thereby advancing problem-solving competencies. Additional systems have demonstrated that the establishment of explicit goals for complex and extended tasks significantly contributes to the development of robust reasoning skills.

The present study categorizes editing tasks into five principal categories, which are further subdivided into 16 distinct types, encompassing a range from basic text formatting to sophisticated editing workflows. The methodology emphasizes fewer but more detailed cases, as proposed by Maia Polo et al., rather than numerous simple instances. These cases include operations such as text replacement and problem-solving requiring multiple edits, utilizing both images and textual information for resolution. Specifically, one scenario involves aligning images with captions across slides by integrating visual and textual data. Some cases exhibit overlap among categories, thereby extending the total count of categories beyond 100. PPTArena distinguishes itself from other benchmarks by concentrating on the complexity of edits in four dimensions: the number of steps required for resolution, the interdependence of slides, the extent of semantic comprehension needed, and the level of planning involved. In contrast, tools like PPTC-R and T2US predominantly facilitate rapid edits based on templates, whereas PPTArena is crafted to address more exigent, realistic scenarios to identify system limitations. It incorporates decks both human-generated and Python-created, provides explicit evaluation criteria, functions across diverse platforms, and adheres to accessibility standards. An exemplar task involves matching each image to its corresponding caption on a slide, organizing them in a balanced manner, updating slide titles, and ensuring uniform image dimensions of 3.2 inches in width by 2.4 inches in height. Such tasks are designed to evaluate systems on multi-step reasoning, inter-slide information integration, and strategic planning. This configuration exposes issues that simpler tasks fail to reveal, thus offering a robust testing ground for advanced systems. The evaluation assesses two primary aspects: adherence to instructions and the quality of visual outputs. These are rated by expert judges on a scale from 0 to 5, where 0 indicates failure and 5 signifies perfection.

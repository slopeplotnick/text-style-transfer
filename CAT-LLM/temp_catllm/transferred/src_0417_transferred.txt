In recent developments within the engineering sector, systems have been implemented wherein supervisors function as intermediaries between individuals and worker agents. These supervisors are tasked with overseeing the work, identifying potential issues, and halting operations should thresholds be exceeded. This concept, which will be further elucidated in the context of "Institutional AI," implies that merely appointing supervisors is insufficient to ensure optimal system functionality. Notably, these supervisors utilize analogous systems to the agents they monitor, thereby presenting the possibility of encountering similar issues or becoming ensnared in identical feedback loops. Excessive concurrence among supervisors may precipitate collective errors or intentional maladaptive decisions. Consequently, enhanced management necessitates additional layers of supervision, diversified evaluation techniques, alternative observation methods, and a clear delineation of authority among supervisors. The autonomy of systems employing multiple agents heightens the complexity of ensuring safety, thus rendering reliance on singular centralized systems or static regulations inadequate. Instead, dynamic networks capable of detecting, addressing, and rectifying issues independently are imperative. The failure of one model does not occur in isolation; rather, it exerts a cascading effect on others. Therefore, safeguarding AI necessitates an understanding of the genesis of these group problems and the metrics for their assessment. The interaction of language models reveals the limitations of current methodologies in addressing risks such as bias, misuse, or vulnerabilities. While individual model evaluation is feasible, the potential for errors during model interaction is amplified, presenting challenges that are not yet fully comprehended. In response to these challenges, the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH) concept was proposed, delineating the juncture at which the collective behavior of models begins to deteriorate, notwithstanding the adequacy of each individual model.

The feature known as AI Overviews within Google Search utilizes the Gemini model to generate summaries of extensive documents, thereby enabling users to save time by circumventing the need to synthesize information from multiple sources independently. However, this functionality raises concerns regarding the reliability of the information provided. Specifically, when artificial intelligence systems aggregate content from diverse documents into a unified summary, it becomes imperative to assess whether such systems treat all sources equitably or exhibit inherent biases. Previous studies have examined biases in AI models, with Santurkar et al. identifying a tendency among models such as GPT-4 and Claude to reflect liberal social values. In the context of news summarization, Vijay et al. proposed methods to evaluate biases, including the examination of linguistic discrepancies and the propensity of AI systems to favor particular perspectives. Although existing research has extensively investigated biases in AI-generated summaries, limited attention has been devoted to examining biases in the processing of multiple documents. Specifically, in scenarios involving numerous and often lengthy documents, it remains unclear whether AI systems disproportionately prioritize certain documents based on their positional attributes. Phenomena such as primacy bias or the tendency to overlook middle sections have been noted in other computational tasks, yet their implications for summarizing extensive collections of documents have not been thoroughly explored. This study seeks to address whether the sequence in which articles are presented influences their representation within AI-generated summaries, focusing on abortion-related news articles as a case study. Consequently, the research questions underpinning this investigation aim to elucidate these dynamics systematically.

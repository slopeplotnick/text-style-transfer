The developed system demonstrates reliability and consistency across various tests and setups, indicating its robustness in producing coherent results. This aspect is crucial, as inconsistent or inaccurate outputs would hinder the ability to discern meaningful connections within the data. Conversely, the findings reveal distinct groupings, stable patterns, and logically associated traits, thereby substantiating the efficacy of the proposed method in identifying training images that align with the stylistic or semantic attributes of the outputs beyond superficial appearance or textual content. Specifically, the data indicate that the automated generation of knowledge graphs (KGs) facilitates an enhanced understanding of the relationship between training data and model outputs. Such insights hold significant implications for explaining model behavior, addressing copyright-related concerns, and promoting transparency in dataset construction. For instance, when a model is prompted to produce an image in a specific artistic style, such as “Ghibli-style,” it is observed that the model does not replicate individual images from its training data. Instead, it synthesizes the overall aesthetic and thematic characteristics of the style by extracting shared features from a set of images. While these features are stored and utilized in the generation of new outputs, they are not direct replications, which complicates the determination of specific training images that influence the resulting imagery. The influence operates at a higher conceptual level, encompassing shared elements such as textures, themes, and compositional structures. Consequently, understanding the impact of a stylistic input requires an analysis of overarching patterns and semantic relationships rather than a direct object-to-object comparison. In conducting the experiment, an automatic KG construction method previously introduced was employed. Frames from ten iconic Studio Ghibli films were utilized, and each frame was systematically analyzed and described using a structured framework that decomposes visual information into discrete components. These components were subsequently used to construct a “Ghibli World” KG designed to encapsulate the key elements of the studio’s distinctive visual style. Despite the limited scope of the dataset, comprising frames from only ten films, it is posited that the resulting KG provides a comprehensive representation of the studio’s stylistic attributes.

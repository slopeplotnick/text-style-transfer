Recent investigations have sought to explore more structured methodologies for prompt creation. Specifically, the Plan-and-Solve approach (Wang et al., 2023c) divides tasks into bifurcated components of planning and solving, demonstrating enhanced efficacy for mathematical and logical problem-solving. Furthermore, Prompt Chaining (Chen et al., 2023) examines whether linking summaries or intermediary steps contributes to improved text summarization. Complexity-based Prompting (Fu et al., 2023) proposes that task difficulty should determine prompt construction, aligning with our perspective on examining specificity in prompts. While previous research predominantly emphasizes task format or example utilization, our focus is directed towards the granularity and structured nature of prompts, considering these as variables subject to variation. Additionally, recent studies have introduced methods for prompt refinement either autonomously or with human intervention. For instance, black-box optimization techniques (Diao et al., 2023) have been applied to adjust prompts based on feedback without necessitating examination of model specifics. Moreover, Zhou et al. (2023) reveal that large language models can be prompted to refine their own prompts, embodying a self-improvement strategy akin to our generalization loop. Prompt Refinement with Image Pivots (Lu et al., 2023) incorporates visual elements to modify text prompts for image generation. Although our work is confined to text-based adjustments, the objective remains to enhance outcomes through prompt modification. Unlike these studies, DETAIL concentrates on the impact of textual specificity on model reasoning, rather than focusing on semantic alterations or multimodal data integration. Reviews such as Liu et al. (2023) have systematically surveyed various prompting techniques for reasoning, highlighting the diversity of prompts and strategies available. However, a paucity of research examines how varying levels of prompt detail influence model behavior across heterogeneous tasks and systems. Methods like CoT and Plan-and-Solve underscore the significance of prompt content, yet they do not delve deeply into this aspect.

Editing videos of individuals presents significant challenges due to the necessity of implementing modifications, such as altering their appearance, expressions, or incorporating additional elements, while ensuring synchronization with the original video. A primary difficulty lies in maintaining temporal consistency and behavioral coherence between the edited and original video content. SyncLoRA represents an innovative approach to video editing that preserves the visual integrity and temporal alignment of modifications. This methodology utilizes a model that initiates alterations in the initial frame of the video and subsequently propagates these changes across subsequent frames. To ensure synchronization, the model undergoes training using pairs of videos that exhibit identical movements while differing in appearance, with these pairs selected through a process that evaluates timing congruence. The training process enables the model to integrate the original video's motion dynamics with the modifications applied to the first frame. Following training on a carefully curated set of videos depicting human subjects, SyncLoRA demonstrates adaptability to novel facial features and diverse editing requirements, including changes to physical appearance, the addition of elements, or background replacement, even under varying poses and expressions. Empirical results confirm that this method produces visually cohesive edits that maintain synchronization with the original video. Such advancements in video editing find practical applications in various domains, including advertising, cinema, gaming, and other media, where precise control over aesthetic and visual elements is paramount. The complexity of editing videos featuring individuals is heightened by the prevalence of human subjects in media content, necessitating naturalistic edits that align seamlessly with the original material.

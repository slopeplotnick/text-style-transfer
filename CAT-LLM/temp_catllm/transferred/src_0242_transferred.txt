In 2025, Li et al. discussed the utilization of feedback mechanisms to enhance predictive accuracy during testing, with external assistance providing guidance. Zuo et al. explored Test-Time Reinforcement Learning, a method wherein various strategies are employed, results are voted upon, and consensus-driven modifications are implemented. While effective, these approaches necessitate numerous attempts to achieve optimal results. Wang et al. demonstrated that verifiable rewards substantially improve learning efficacy with minimal examples, indicating the potential of such learning paradigms during testing phases. This study seeks to enhance the efficiency of this process by facilitating early termination when appropriate, thereby optimizing policy procedures, which are integral to reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of language models. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a prevalent technique employed to ensure stability of updates, often utilized in scenarios involving human feedback. The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) extends this by leveraging group advantages to stabilize learning in contexts with constrained feedback. OptPO incorporates these principles to streamline processes without altering foundational methodologies. Prior research has consistently demonstrated that training during testing enhances model adaptability through diverse strategies, including uncertainty reduction, assisted learning, and reinforcement learning approaches. Nevertheless, extant methodologies are characterized by inefficiencies due to the requisite numerous iterations. This research contributes by introducing OptPO, which reduces computational demands while maintaining performance efficacy, thereby augmenting and refining current test-time methodologies.

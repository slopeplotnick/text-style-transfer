A system was developed to identify apparent errors that could be easily recognized, rather than performing in-depth scientific analyses or evaluating dynamic processes. The dataset predominantly pertains to complex physical phenomena and scientific principles, indicating that the disaggregation of physics into specific frameworks yields more accurate outcomes compared to general explanations. This finding underscores the necessity of specialized methodologies for assessing nuanced physical reasoning in video production. VideoScore2 was found to be unsuitable for the testing procedure due to its reliance on stringent scientific validation, which was observed to poorly align with subjective interpretations of the data. Examples illustrating the performance of various models are presented, with Figure 4 providing insights into the comparative results. Notably, Sora-2 successfully simulates expected physical phenomena when provided with appropriate prompts and motion inputs, producing videos that align well with the experimental setup, demonstrate temporal transformations, and capture initial states and color transitions of reactions. Conversely, Hailuo 2.3, despite maintaining spatial and temporal consistency, fails to adequately address significant components of the prompts, resulting in videos that deviate from expected outcomes. This discrepancy highlights the importance of meticulous experimental design and strict adherence to prompts for achieving higher evaluation scores, even when other attributes appear satisfactory. Furthermore, the assessment of simple physical configurations remains challenging for contemporary models.

The proposed method ensures adherence to data constraints by optimizing a penalized value function, which represents the central objective of this study. Specifically, it simplifies the Offline RL objective, originally defined in Equation 4, by identifying the action characterized by the highest frequency of occurrence or density, thereby providing substantial support for the methodology. The test-time scaling approach employed seeks the most frequently observed action, demonstrating theoretical robustness and reliability in achieving the anti-exploration goal inherent to Offline RL. Empirical successes documented by Lobel et al. (2023) and Bai et al. (2025) have highlighted the efficacy of utilizing pseudo-counts for optimizing LLM preferences, an idea that is integrated within the framework using a Coin Flipping Network (CFN). As illustrated in Figure 3, the pseudo-counts generated by the estimator exhibit strong alignment with both the L2 distance to the actual action and the pseudo-count derived from the CFN, thereby substantiating the capability of the CFN to effectively evaluate and penalize unsupported actions. The experimental investigations conducted aim to evaluate the extent to which Test-time Anti-exploration with pseudo-counts can enhance action prediction accuracy and task success rates, while also examining the influence of specific design choices on these improvements and assessing the acceptability of the associated computational costs. More precisely, the experiments seek to address the following research questions: Q1: Can the proposed approach improve performance across diverse environments, VLC policies, and tasks? Q2: What is the additional computational cost incurred by the test-time scaling method, and can it maintain performance gains when applied to real robots? Q3: How do design elements, including the utilization of internal features and the CFN pseudo-count network, impact overall performance outcomes?

Improving chatbots that utilize language models necessitates consideration of diverse linguistic expressions, not only to ensure fairness but also to enhance practical applications. Although voice-based language models have advanced, they persist in facing challenges related to accents, dialects, and social group-specific speech patterns. Historically, models dealing with speech have struggled with accents and dialects. Despite the proficiency of language models, inherent biases remain due to the training data, typically favoring common accents or widely represented languages, thus diminishing efficacy for individuals with rarer accents. Consequently, it is imperative to develop models that accommodate varied pronunciations and speech styles. Research focused on model adaptation during utilization and intelligent data grouping may facilitate handling unfamiliar accents, even with limited data availability. Conventional evaluation methods, such as word error rates, inadequately capture the performance across diverse demographic groups; hence, innovative metrics are required to assess fairness and model efficacy for different accents, languages, and demographics. Furthermore, error analysis should transcend mere mistake counting to identify which demographic groups are disproportionately impacted by model biases. Such evaluations are instrumental in enhancing model fairness. Variability in speech within the same language can be influenced by factors including age, gender, social class, or occupation. Legacy speech models often exhibit an overemphasis on homogenous data, predominantly assimilating the speech patterns of dominant groups. Addressing this issue extends beyond merely integrating diverse datasets; it necessitates the development of sophisticated tools that enable models to comprehend a wide array of speech styles and cues. Attention to elements such as tone, rhythm, and emotional nuances in speech is crucial for accurately interpreting social or emotional expressions.

Various methodologies have been investigated by researchers to enhance model reasoning and step verification. Specifically, studies have focused on decomposing reasoning into smaller, meticulously checked steps and employing reward models to facilitate improved mathematical reasoning. One particular study demonstrated that the implementation of a verifier, tasked with evaluating the final outcome, notably designated as the GV-Gap, can enhance outcomes, particularly when the solver and verifier are identical models. Nevertheless, the investigation was limited to basic models, excluding those subjected to additional training post initial setup. Recently, the scaling of computational power during verifier testing has been examined. For instance, multiple researchers have explored random sampling in conjunction with self-checking, while others have investigated the integration of parallel sampling with error correction, and yet another cohort has juxtaposed a method known as Self-Consistency with a generative verifier. Furthermore, the optimization of computational resource utilization for verification purposes during testing remains an active area of inquiry. However, it is imperative to acknowledge that verifiers are not infallible, as they may erroneously validate incorrect answers, reject valid reasoning, or select incorrect solutions, as elaborated in Appendix A. Another significant research focus pertains to the self-improvement and evaluative capabilities of models. Despite skepticism from some scholars, theories and empirical evidence have been proposed, indicating that models can enhance their performance through refined comprehension. A particular study underscored the necessity for robust verifiers to assist small models in their development. Practical self-improvement strategies include the provision of feedback articulated in plain language or the training of models to autonomously rectify their errors. Additionally, the utilization of tools such as code interpreters to iteratively refine responses has been explored.

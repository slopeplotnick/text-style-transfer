Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methodologies have been developed to address the challenges associated with processing long videos more effectively. These approaches prioritize the identification and utilization of salient segments of video content, such as specific frames or clips, through a combination of textual and visual cues, thereby enabling models to concentrate on the most relevant aspects. Furthermore, advanced techniques have incorporated graph-based mechanisms to elucidate the relationships among various components within a video, enhancing the contextual understanding of video content. However, these models frequently exhibit limitations due to their reliance on textual information or simplistic visual-textual comparisons, which constrains their capacity to comprehensively analyze and reason through extended video sequences. To address these shortcomings, memory-based methods have been introduced to systematically organize and store video-derived information. For instance, EgoRAG leverages text-based memory organization to facilitate the interpretation of activities spanning an entire day, while Ego-R1 expands on this functionality by employing tools that emphasize visual analysis and iterative reasoning to manage extended tasks. HippoMM integrates dual memory systems by combining summaries with multimodal information, and M3-Agent constructs an entity-focused memory system that employs step-by-step reasoning to retrieve pertinent information. Despite these advancements, existing methods continue to encounter difficulties in seamlessly integrating textual and visual data and adapting to varying temporal durations within videos, thereby limiting their effectiveness in addressing complex scenarios. In response to these challenges, the development of WorldMM represents a significant innovation, wherein both textual and visual elements of video content are utilized to construct a memory system designed to enhance understanding and reasoning capabilities for long videos. WorldMM operates through a tripartite process: initially, it generates multiple memory types, including two distinct textual memories and one visual memory; subsequently, it employs a retrieval mechanism to extract the requisite information from these memories across various temporal intervals until sufficient data is accumulated to address a given query; finally, the system synthesizes the retrieved information to generate a coherent and informed response.

The MODOMA system comprises two integral components: the mother agent and the daughter agent. Delilah, functioning as the mother agent, constitutes an advanced tool specifically developed for the generation and analysis of the Dutch language. Unlike systems that depend on a database of examples, Delilah operates based on a set of predefined rules and structures, serving as detailed templates that delineate the functionality of words and grammar within Dutch. For instance, a template exists for the word "de," which translates to "the" in Dutch, encompassing information regarding its form, semantics, grammatical properties, and syntactic placement. Such templates, derived from comprehensive studies on Dutch language and grammar, exhibit high levels of detail and accuracy. Through a process known as unification, Delilah amalgamates these templates to construct coherent phrases and sentences, ensuring compatibility and resolving any potential conflicts. Specifically, the template for "de" is configured to combine exclusively with noun phrases, as demonstrated in "de vrouw" ("the woman"). The operational mechanism of Delilah bears resemblance to head-driven phrase structure grammar (HPSG), wherein a uniform approach is applied to words and grammatical rules, treating them equivalently. Furthermore, Delilah employs a specific grammar type referred to as combinatory list grammar, closely associated with combinatory categorial grammar (CCG). The intricate workings of Delilah have been thoroughly investigated and elucidated in numerous scholarly articles. Within the MODOMA system, Delilah is utilized in its original design configuration without modifications. Consequently, the system exhibits robust functionality and systematic integration, thereby contributing significantly to the field of computational linguistics.

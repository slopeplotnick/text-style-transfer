Language model systems, encompassing large language models and various other types, are increasingly employed across a range of applications, including medical chatbots, self-driving vehicle assistants, and coding support tools. Given their integration into critical and publicly accessible domains, ensuring their operational accuracy and adherence to expected behavioral norms is of paramount importance. Numerous studies have investigated methodologies to enhance the safety of these systems, with efforts directed towards embedding safety features within their frameworks. However, current approaches to evaluating and enforcing appropriate behavioral standards remain inadequate, as they fail to guarantee reliable performance across diverse scenarios. Such guarantees are essential for fostering user trust and ensuring the perceived safety of these technologies. Recent research endeavors have commenced exploring formal certification mechanisms for specific desirable behaviors, such as fairness, accurate question responses, and safety compliance. These behaviors are typically codified as explicit rules that systems are required to adhere to. For instance, certain researchers have proposed rules addressing fairness in system-generated responses, comprehension of textual material, management of sensitive conversational contexts, and appropriate decision-making in tool selection. Nevertheless, these certifications often exhibit limited adaptability to varying requirements or contexts due to their specificity, and they predominantly apply to conversational settings. Modifications to these rules necessitate significant revisions, thereby rendering the certification process highly customized for individual properties, domains, and system types. Consequently, there is a need to develop more generalized and flexible approaches to the certification of language model systems.

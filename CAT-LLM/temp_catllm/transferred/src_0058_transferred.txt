The phase-field (PF) model provides a framework for analyzing the temporal evolution of systems comprising distinct materials and phases toward equilibrium, with particular applicability to nanoscale systems and short time periods, which is advantageous in the fabrication of solar cells. The energetic properties of the system are encapsulated within the Gibbs free energy functional, serving as the foundation for understanding the formation mechanisms of solar cells, including solvent evaporation, phase separation, and crystallization processes. The forces driving the system toward equilibrium are derived from the Gibbs free energy functional, while phase transitions, such as drying and crystal formation, are elucidated through governing equations that incorporate mass transport dynamics. During the simulations conducted, the model was validated through integration with a fluid dynamics framework that accounts for mass transport via flow mechanisms, as elaborated upon in Section S2. Material-specific properties obtained were subsequently utilized to investigate the propagation of light within solar cells. Depending on the structural complexity of the solar cells, computational simulations or simplified analytical approaches were employed to facilitate this analysis. Conceptualizing the solar cell as a stratified system of materials, a modeling approach was adopted to elucidate the interaction between light and the material layers. Furthermore, a neural network was employed to predict charge generation outcomes based on variables such as light incidence angle, active layer thickness, and light wavelength, with additional details regarding this predictive model outlined in Section S3.

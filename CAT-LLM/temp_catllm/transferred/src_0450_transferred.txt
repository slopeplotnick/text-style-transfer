It is revealed by the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture that systematic checks and structured retries could address the reliability issues identified during SMART-LLM replication. Table 2, located in the section on Performance Validation and Evaluation Limitations, presents performance metrics for the identical 36-task SMART-LLM test under consistent conditions, with an emphasis on the impact of architectural modifications. The framework exhibited success rates from 0.21 for Compound tasks to 0.50 for Elemental and Complex tasks, and notably, task completion rates improved substantially across all categories: Elemental (0.75), Simple (0.83), Compound (0.88), and Complex (0.86). These enhancements, ranging from 9 to 30 percentage points, underscore the efficacy of checks in validating outputs for structural and semantic accuracy. While employing the same few-shot prompting strategy as SMART-LLM, the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture revealed superior performance. The framework utilized 700,017 total tokens in contrast to SMART-LLM’s 727,649 tokens, reflecting a 3.8% reduction as illustrated in figure 13, and it completed tasks in 746 seconds compared to 1,075 seconds, denoting a 30.6% reduction as indicated in figure 14. This improvement is largely attributed to its modular prompting design, wherein each component—be it decomposition, assignment, or code generation—employs its specific example file. Conversely, SMART-LLM incorporates all three example files into each LLM call, irrespective of the component in use. Consequently, each Self Corrective-Agent inference requires significantly fewer tokens, thereby enhancing processing speed. The prompting method proves sufficiently efficient such that, despite validation-driven retries, the Self Corrective-Agent consistently utilizes fewer tokens overall.

The observed discrepancies within the PhysionetMI dataset are likely attributable to its inherent complexity, characterized by diverse experimental configurations, individual-specific brain activity, and variations in signal attributes. A promising approach involves the application of ensemble methods, such as bagging, boosting, or stacking, which amalgamate the strengths of both simple and complex models to enhance the robustness of outcomes despite inter- and intra-individual variability. To optimize the efficacy and consistency of these methods, further research is warranted. While existing challenges persist, the integration of quantum methodologies alongside advanced algorithms holds the potential to significantly refine EEG signal analysis, thereby promoting generalizability and accommodating the inherent variability of EEG data. This advancement represents a substantial leap toward the development of more personalized and adaptable brain-computer interfaces. Preliminary investigations utilizing quantum computing are underway to ascertain the viability of these propositions and their capacity to enhance EEG signal decoding. An additional concern with prevailing methods is their dependence on labeled data, which necessitates substantial time and financial resources for large-scale generation. Recent studies advocate for the adoption of techniques capable of processing unlabeled data, such as unsupervised or semi-supervised learning, to mitigate the reliance on labeled data. Furthermore, transfer learning, which entails the application of learned patterns from one individual or dataset to another, remains underexplored despite its potential to augment model performance and address inter-individual variability. Future research endeavors should prioritize the development and evaluation of transfer learning techniques to address the challenges associated with EEG datasets. Collectively, our findings indicate that the employment of tailored EEG methodologies constitutes the optimal strategy for managing individual and cross-individual differences. The future trajectory of personalized and efficacious brain-computer interfaces lies in the amalgamation of adaptive strategies, intricate models, ensemble learning techniques, and quantum-based innovations.

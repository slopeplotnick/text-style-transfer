The system demonstrates improved functionality as it generates specific prompts tailored to each task, contingent upon real-time factors such as the system's current state, the capabilities of the robots, the workload, and the phase of the task. Consequently, it eliminates the need to include 5,000-6,000 redundant example tokens within every prompt, which were previously identified as superfluous to the task requirements. Token reduction is achieved through two primary mechanisms: first, the process prioritizes task-relevant information, thereby avoiding additional stages such as task decomposition or superfluous code generation, which formerly accounted for approximately 14,000 tokens per task; second, example tokens are substituted with a structured methodology for contextual representation, effectively reducing the token count in prompts from 6,700 to 1,100. The efficacy of this system in task allocation across multiple robots was evaluated based on metrics such as task completion success rates, workload distribution equity, and adaptability to varying operational conditions. Experiments were conducted within three distinct configurations, wherein the robots possessed differentiated skill sets while maintaining a constant sequence of 36 tasks. Each configuration emphasized specific robot proficiencies by altering the likelihood of successful task execution for individual robots. Results indicated that the system consistently maintained high operational efficiency while ensuring equitable task distribution, an essential consideration in domains such as construction where both productivity and fairness are critical. In one configuration, designated Medium Excels, robots with intermediate capabilities achieved the highest overall performance, successfully completing 87.8% of assigned tasks. Notably, the system allocated a majority of tasks to the robot proficient across all task types, which resulted in some workload imbalance. Under this configuration, task-specific success rates were recorded as 92.5% for precision-oriented tasks, 86.6% for tasks requiring dexterity, and 85% for tasks involving substantial physical effort. Although this configuration yielded optimal overall results, it also highlighted a potential trade-off wherein prioritizing performance may inadvertently lead to inequitable workload distribution.

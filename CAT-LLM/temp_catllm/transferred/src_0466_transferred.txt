Jack Lu, Ryan Teehan, Jinran Jin, and Mengye Ren are affiliated with the Agentic Learning AI Lab at New York University. Their contact information is available via their NYU email addresses and their website. On November 20, 2025, they discussed the dual capabilities of large language models in both problem-solving and solution verification, with the verification process aiding in the selection of optimal responses. Historically, the focus has predominantly been on models assessing their own outputs, rather than evaluating the outputs of other models. These language models undergo substantial supplementary training post-initial training, yet the impact of this additional training on their verification capabilities remains largely unexplored. The study involved an analysis of 37 models varying in group affiliation and size, encompassing models with only initial training and those subjected to further training. These models were evaluated across nine different problem categories, including logic, puzzles, mathematics, common sense reasoning, and factual knowledge. A comparative analysis was conducted to assess the models' ability to verify their own outputs versus those from models within the same or different groups. A novel metric was developed to quantify the enhancement in answer quality attributable to verification, which was subsequently tested. The study examined how these metrics varied with model size and additional training, as well as the relative ease of verifying different data types. Results indicated that inter-group answer verification was highly effective; supplementary training enhanced models' ability to verify answers from other groups, but not their own; and that mathematical and logical problems were the most straightforward to verify. Consequently, instead of merely soliciting answers from a model, the models are now employed in both problem-solving and answer verification. Verification during testing can range from simple methods, such as generating multiple answers and selecting the optimal one with a checker, to more complex methodologies involving iterative refinement. This approach enables models to tackle more challenging problems than they could independently.

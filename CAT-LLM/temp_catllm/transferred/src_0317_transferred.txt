Research indicates that the rigidity in individual beliefs contributes to phenomena such as polarization and misinformation, subsequently leading to various cognitive errors. This occurrence is attributed to the manner in which individuals process information, fundamentally characterized by their reasoning capabilities. Consequently, large language models, which have assimilated patterns of human thought from extensive online text, are designed to assist individuals by providing answers to queries or facilitating tasks. These models have demonstrated success in executing complex cognitive tasks, and novel methodologies of reasoning have been developed using them. Specifically, techniques ranging from early methods such as Chain-of-Thought to more recent approaches involving reinforcement learning have been employed to enable models to comprehend information accurately by utilizing arguments, seeking evidence, and experimenting with diverse strategies. However, there are instances where these models fail to pursue truth due to adherence to entrenched beliefs rather than incorporating new information, which may result in less accurate conclusions and instill false confidence in users. Although existing research suggests the presence of belief entrenchment within these models, providing empirical evidence for this phenomenon proves challenging. It is notably difficult to ascertain whether a belief update is justified or biased. Consequently, previous studies have employed specific tasks where belief entrenchment is easily identifiable, although these methodologies are not particularly adept at detecting reasoning errors in scenarios where the truth is ambiguous.

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly utilized in applications such as Google’s AI-generated summaries, which synthesize information from extensive documents; however, it remains uncertain whether these systems process all input equally. For instance, in an investigation focusing on news articles related to abortion, researchers created 40 sets of three articles—comprising one pro, one neutral, and one con perspective—arranged these articles in six different sequences, and employed Gemini 2.5 Flash to generate a neutral summary. The summaries were subsequently evaluated in comparison to the original articles using metrics designed to measure overlap in terms of lexical similarity, semantic alignment, and factual accuracy. The analysis revealed that the summaries exhibited a bias toward the first article in the sequence, as evidenced by higher scores on one specific tool, BERTScore. It was observed that the initial document exerted greater influence on the summary than the second and third, which demonstrated approximately equivalent impact. These findings suggest that the order in which documents are presented can significantly affect the resulting summary, potentially posing challenges for systems reliant on such outputs or for artificial intelligence frameworks that base their decisions on them. Generative AI, a foundational component of these systems, is advancing rapidly and has already been integrated into diverse domains. These agentic AI systems are engineered to autonomously manage complex tasks with minimal supervision, employing methodologies that decompose overarching objectives into smaller, actionable steps. Specifically, such systems engage in iterative planning and reasoning processes, incorporating external information as necessary to optimize their operations. Consequently, the efficacy of these systems is heavily contingent upon the capacity of the underlying LLMs to accurately retrieve, organize, and synthesize information derived from both instructions and external sources.

Automotive artificial intelligence (AI) systems are required to operate within stringent parameters to ensure functionality and safety. These systems must respond within a few milliseconds to prevent driver distraction or unsafe scenarios. Furthermore, they are constrained by the vehicle's electrical limitations, necessitating minimal power consumption. Additionally, such systems must remain operational under challenging environmental conditions, including extreme variations in brightness from daylight to complete darkness, fluctuating temperatures ranging from freezing cold to high heat, and vibrations associated with vehicle motion, while accommodating diverse user interactions. To address these requirements, the AI systems employ specialized hardware designed to process data efficiently and accurately, even when the data are simplified. The AI is trained using a dataset known as Infinity-MM, comprising approximately 44.8 million examples designed to facilitate the performance of various tasks involving multimodal data, specifically images and text. This dataset encompasses a wide range of applications, such as answering questions related to everyday visual content, object recognition, scene understanding, document analysis (e.g., forms and receipts), chart and diagram interpretation, text extraction from images, conversational exchanges, and domain-specific tasks such as analyzing medical images, addressing scenarios involving self-driving cars, and conducting factory inspections. Each dataset example is composed of an image, corresponding instructions, and accurate responses, with a focus on generating detailed, multi-step answers rather than selecting simplistic labels. This emphasis on comprehensive explanations is critical for automotive AI systems to ensure transparency and foster user trust. The training process for the AI is executed in four distinct stages, following a structured approach tailored to the Infinity-MM dataset. During the second stage, the focus is directed toward general tasks, including object recognition, scene comprehension, and answering fundamental image-related questions. At this phase, all components of the AI system, including those responsible for image and language processing, are fine-tuned. The training process employs a specific learning rate and begins with small data batches to mitigate potential issues, subsequently transitioning to larger batch sizes as the training progresses.

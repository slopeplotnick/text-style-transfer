Table 3 demonstrates that the integration of both audio and images in summary generation enhances performance compared to the exclusive use of audio across most languages and models, despite the models operating solely on text. This improvement is most pronounced for English, German, and Italian, while it is less effective for Chinese. It is proposed that this disparity arises because English terms frequently occur in languages employing the Latin alphabet, whereas the divergence between English and Chinese inhibits models from consistently leveraging the supplementary English information effectively. When addressing question answering tasks, the inclusion of images yields slight enhancements; however, the impact is not as substantial as in summary generation. In certain instances, a marginal improvement is observed, yet in four of twelve language-model configurations, performance deteriorates. It is hypothesized that this decline occurs because the model does not access the actual image data, relying instead on enhanced text information, which proves insufficient for improving question answering capabilities. Streaming speech translation has been extensively studied over the past decade, with certain lecture translation tools employing this technique, albeit primarily utilizing audio. Our research diverges by incorporating both visual and auditory elements from slides, subsequently providing translated audio and slides across diverse languages. The domain of image-to-image translation remains relatively unexplored, though interest is burgeoning, driven by the availability of larger datasets. Predominantly, existing research concentrates on text translation within images rather than restoring the visual fidelity following translation. An initial foray into this domain is a study examining methods to preserve image integrity. Our image translation framework is designed with flexibility, allowing researchers to integrate their models at various stages, from text extraction within images to translation and image generation, without necessitating the development of new components.

The mechanisms underlying the operation of diffusion models, particularly the equilibrium between replicating learned information and generating novel outputs, remain a subject of considerable investigation. Previous studies have explored this dynamic by analyzing model behaviors and investigating the factors influencing their propensity to replicate or innovate. These examinations predominantly focus on scenarios where models commence from an unconditioned state and are tested using limited datasets, such as FFHQ and CIFAR-10, providing insights into model behavior under data-constrained conditions. However, as dataset sizes increase, such as with ImageNet, the tendency to replicate diminishes significantly. Song et al. observed that, in the context of larger datasets, initiating the generative process at earlier stages facilitates the creation of new images, whereas later-stage initiation predominantly results in the reproduction of existing images. Consequently, the critical question pertains to the determinants of this balance within models trained on extensive datasets. To address this, the present study examines the training objectives of these models, focusing on flow matching and conditional flow matching methodologies, which are traditionally perceived as challenging due to their dependence on specific data that is often unavailable. Nevertheless, it is demonstrated that, under a Gaussian framework and constrained training data conditions, the flow can be effectively identified, thereby enabling the optimal trajectory for the model to be calculated at any given stage. Analysis of this trajectory reveals that the training process comprises two distinct phases: an initial phase characterized by the integration of information across multiple data points, followed by a subsequent phase wherein the model concentrates on individual data points. Furthermore, it is identified that the dataset size and sample characteristics critically influence the timing of this transition.

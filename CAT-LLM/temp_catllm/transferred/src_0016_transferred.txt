The dataset comprises 81 questions, each addressing a specific health-related issue and its corresponding potential treatment, encompassing 50 distinct disease and condition categories such as "Cancer," "Low back pain in adults," and "AIDS," alongside a general well-being category titled "Other," which includes topics such as weight management. Figure 2 illustrates the 10 categories most frequently referenced within the health-related questions, while the complete list of questions and their respective categories is provided in Appendix Table A4. To investigate the capability of language models in resolving real-world biomedical knowledge conflicts, five distinct prompt templates were developed to evaluate their performance across diverse question-answering scenarios. Table 2 delineates the process wherein, for each instance from the HEALTHCONTRADICT dataset, five question prompts were generated utilizing the templates: Question: { Question } Question: { Question } Question: { Question } Context: { Correct Document } Question: { Question } Context: { Incorrect Document }. The baseline models were selected based on two primary criteria: firstly, to assess whether fine-tuning for biomedical topics enhances domain-specific performance, and secondly, to evaluate whether larger models exhibit superior capabilities. Additionally, language models capable of processing extended documents were prioritized, with specific details regarding the selected models available in Table 3. Each biomedical model was derived through fine-tuning a general model listed within the same row, with "Instruct" models designated as those fine-tuned to adhere to user instructions. Notably, no models were subjected to additional fine-tuning, and zero-shot inference was employed for all baseline models. Open-source language models ranging from 1B to 8B parameters were examined to ensure their applicability in resource-constrained healthcare environments. As demonstrated in Table 4, the performance disparity between larger language models, specifically those with 7-8B parameters, was found to be minimal. Furthermore, the fine-tuned biomedical model MEDITRON3-QWEN2.5-7B exhibited inferior performance relative to QWEN2.5-7B. These top-tier evaluation benchmarks were determined to be limited in their ability to discern nuanced differences in model capabilities, primarily due to their reliance on assessments of built-in knowledge.

Instead of tracking motions frame by frame, controllers are trained via distribution matching to replicate the overall behavioral patterns observed within a motion dataset. Specifically, Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) employs a discriminator to distinguish between the motions generated by the agent and those present in the dataset. Building upon this concept, Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) enable controllers to produce realistic behaviors for novel tasks not included in the original dataset. However, adversarial priors necessitate task-specific policy training, which involves retraining and reliance on the original dataset. In contrast, the proposed method demonstrates comparable efficacy to these approaches while utilizing a reusable motion prior that is adaptable to diverse styles absent from the initial dataset. The recent popularity of diffusion models, attributed to their high performance across various domains, has led to their application in control tasks. These models are capable of generating high-quality motion, which can be leveraged by employing them as motion planners that predict future movements subsequently tracked by a controller. Additionally, diffusion models have been integrated directly to control behaviors for tasks such as manipulation and character control, thereby facilitating flexible and diverse actions. Unlike these methodologies, which incorporate diffusion models within controllers, the proposed approach utilizes pretrained diffusion models as general behavioral guides. Specifically, these models function as reward mechanisms within reinforcement learning frameworks, enabling policies to learn through score distillation sampling.

The Gemini-2.0-Flash model serves as the baseline for the experiments conducted, characterized by its exclusive reliance on the original question without incorporating external information. Variations of CAIRNS were examined, including one version utilizing literature exclusively and another integrating both literature and climate data through a two-step API reasoning approach. Gemini-2.0-Flash constitutes the primary model underpinning CAIRNS and its respective versions, with all models employing identical corpora, prompts, and evaluation settings to ensure equitable comparisons. The ScholarGuide Prompt was utilized to enhance readability by refining context, structure, and language, while simultaneously addressing citation inconsistencies to improve the reliability of information presented. Although this adjustment marginally reduced the overall citation rate, the implementation of unified reference numbers and the explicit association of each fact with supporting evidence facilitated the generation of clearer and more precise responses. The approach demonstrated efficacy in both zero-shot and few-shot scenarios, underscoring the importance of structured prompts to CAIRNS's operational success. Furthermore, the integration of Multi-turn ReAct contributed to the enhancement of responses by aligning literature and climate data, thereby yielding more comprehensive answers with improved contextual relevance. While its impact was less pronounced compared to the ScholarGuide Prompt, it nonetheless supported the structured reasoning process and increased the model's capacity to address complex inquiries. Additionally, the application of Location-Weighted Retrieval enabled the provision of region-specific answers, particularly for geographically focused questions, with its removal resulting in a slight reduction in specificity while leaving other evaluation metrics largely unaffected.

The findings indicate that individuals who exhibit entrenched beliefs are more likely to commit errors in domains where objective answers are known, such as predictive tasks. This observation suggests that the Martingale Score may serve as an effective metric for assessing the quality of reasoning, even in the absence of complete information. In the context of language models, evaluating Bayesian rationality involves determining whether these models assimilate evidence in a manner consistent with Bayes's theorem. Gupta et al. (2025) demonstrated that language models are capable of updating their beliefs based on extensive coin-flip data, even when initial biases are present. However, in more complex real-world linguistic scenarios, Qiu et al. (2025) found that language models fail to adjust their beliefs regarding user preferences as predicted by the Bayesian framework. Furthermore, Falck (2024) revealed that language models do not adhere to Bayesian learning principles when provided with contextual information. Zhao (2021) and Wang (2023) highlighted that the responses of language models are significantly influenced by the arrangement of examples within prompts. While several methodologies have been proposed to enhance Bayesian learning and reasoning—such as emulating the behavior of an ideal Bayesian thinker, employing abstract reasoning, and integrating abduction with deduction—these approaches often necessitate the use of additional structures, such as Bayesian networks, which limit practical applicability. Language models are also characterized by various biases, attributable to their training on extensive human-generated datasets. Notably, these models exhibit biases distinct from those observed in humans when functioning as evaluators, including a preference for certain positions, an inclination toward lengthy responses, an affinity for positive expressions, and an excessive level of certainty. Among the numerous biases present, confirmation bias is particularly critical, as it is hypothesized to contribute significantly to polarization and is considered a fundamental mechanism underlying most biases. Consequently, this study emphasizes the examination of confirmation bias through the lens of belief entrenchment to analyze its manifestation in language models.

The analysis of RQ2 revealed that Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV) function effectively when integrated, each presenting distinct failure modes. In addressing RQ3, we examine the potential for developing a highly efficient hybrid system by integrating these approaches. Consequently, two integration methods were devised: Score-Level Fusion and an Evidence-Aware Pipeline. The latter is specifically designed to mitigate the shortcomings identified in FV systems. Specifically, while Score-Level Fusion strengthens performance through score amalgamation, it does not address the issues highlighted in Section 4.5. FV systems demonstrate efficacy when substantiated by evidence, categorizing claims as "Supported" or "Contradicted"; however, they falter in the absence of evidence, termed as "Not Enough Information" (NEI). To address this limitation, an Evidence-Aware Pipeline was developed, prioritizing external information retrieval, resorting to internal signals only in its absence: (1) Step 1: Retrieval & Verification, wherein an FV module, such as LLM-QA, retrieves evidence and evaluates claim alignment, rendering it as Supported, Contradicted, or NEI. (2) Step 2: Conditional Branching, employing a decision-making framework where a definitive FV output (Supported or Contradicted) is accepted, while NEI signals an absence of external corroboration. Rather than conducting a potentially erroneous verification, the FV is bypassed in favor of an HD method, such as LNPE, deriving the final assessment based solely on the model's intrinsic uncertainty signals. This approach effectively addresses FV's limitations in evidence retrieval, ensuring accurate detection capabilities are maintained even in scenarios lacking external knowledge.

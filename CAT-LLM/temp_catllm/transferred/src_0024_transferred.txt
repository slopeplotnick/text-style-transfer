In the present study, a two-step methodology was employed to amalgamate diverse categories of CoT data for the training of the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, which possesses a dimensionality analogous to the LLM base model utilized in this investigation. A comparative analysis was conducted between models based on VLM and LLM, revealing that the LLM-based approach demonstrated superior performance on both facile and challenging datasets, particularly with respect to macro-F1 scores. This finding indicates that LLMs exhibit enhanced capabilities in reasoning and knowledge acquisition relative to VLMs when endowed with equivalent CoT capabilities. Nevertheless, VLMs excel in the processing of visual details, as they obviate the necessity of converting images into text, thereby mitigating potential information loss. The LORE model has undergone progressive enhancements across three phases, culminating in advancements in five principal domains: objectives, model configuration, data and prompts, evaluation methodologies, and practical applications. These phases illustrate the model's refinement through sophisticated designs, high-quality data, and efficacious application techniques, with particular emphasis on optimizing search results within the domain of online shopping. Initial challenges were encountered in comprehending queries within the e-commerce sector, notably in identifying non-standard product nomenclature. To address these issues, extensive, high-caliber e-commerce datasets were developed, and In-Context Learning was applied to integrate domain-specific knowledge into the model, consequently augmenting its proficiency in parsing e-commerce language.

Recent advancements in the field of content generation have seen researchers focusing on enhancing generator capabilities. Specifically, approaches such as Mirror-Consistency, Self-Contrast, and Step-Back Prompting have been employed to refine the results through iterative checking and multi-perspective analysis. Furthermore, the Self-Check method has been implemented to systematically identify errors in a stepwise manner. These methodologies predominantly aim at optimizing the generator itself, albeit at the cost of increased computational resources and time. Contrastingly, our research endeavors to improve the integration phase of results. Instead of rectifying flawed outputs from the generator, we utilize a specialized layer to ascertain the most accurate outcome. A crucial aspect of employing language models as adjudicators in comparative tasks is the decision of whether to permit equal judgments or compel a definitive choice. Initially, it is demonstrated that enforcing a choice can introduce bias within the model. Subsequently, the allowance for a "tie" is shown to be significantly influenced by the configuration of the adjudicator, necessitating enhanced strategies for bias mitigation. Allowing ties has been revealed to diminish bias, notably positional bias, which arises when the sequence of options impacts the decision. In an empirical study, modifications to the adjudicator setup, permitting "tie" responses, effectively reduced positional bias across both models under examination. This finding suggests that enabling the model to declare ties transcends mere equality representation, serving as an essential mechanism for a fairer adjudication process. Nevertheless, tie decisions exhibit variability. In scenarios where models are tasked with selecting from three alternatives, the voting behavior is markedly affected by the configuration, underscoring the need for robust methods in result amalgamation.

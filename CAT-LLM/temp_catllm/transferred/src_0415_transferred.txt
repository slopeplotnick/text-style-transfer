DeepSeek-V3.2 is an advanced model that excels in both speed and intelligence, characterized by several innovative features. Firstly, the DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA) mechanism is implemented to enhance the model's efficiency while retaining its capacity to process extensive information, thereby facilitating faster operations without compromising performance. Secondly, a scalable reinforcement learning framework is utilized, wherein a robust learning approach is combined with additional computational resources post-training. Consequently, DeepSeek-V3.2 performs comparably to GPT-5. Furthermore, our specialized version, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, is demonstrated to surpass GPT-5 and achieve equivalency with Gemini-3.0-Pro in addressing complex problems, notably securing top accolades at the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad and the International Olympiad in Informatics. Thirdly, a large-scale agentic task synthesis pipeline has been developed to generate substantial training data, thereby enhancing the model's ability to utilize tools and improve cognitive processing in challenging scenarios. This innovation facilitates the model's advancement in comprehending and executing instructions. The emergence of reasoning models such as DeepSeek-AI and OpenAI in 2024 and 2025 marked a significant evolution for Large Language Models, substantially enhancing their capabilities. However, a divergence has recently been observed, where open-source models, including MiniMax, MoonShot, Qwen, and ZhiPu-AI, are advancing, yet closed-source models like Anthropic, DeepMind, and OpenAI are progressing at an accelerated rate, thereby widening the performance gap, with closed-source models exhibiting superior proficiency in difficult tasks. It is identified that open-source models face three principal challenges: primarily, they predominantly employ a traditional method known as vanilla attention, which proves inadequate for processing extensive information, thus hindering scalability. Additionally, they lack sufficient computational augmentation subsequent to training, resulting in suboptimal performance on complex tasks. Lastly, open-source models exhibit limitations in generalizing and adhering to instructions compared to their closed-source counterparts, thereby diminishing their practical applicability.

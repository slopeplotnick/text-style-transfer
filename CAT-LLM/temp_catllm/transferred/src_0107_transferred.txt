Large Language Models (LLMs) have significantly transformed the manner in which event-based predictions are conducted, specifically by extracting comprehensive details from textual data, including the type of event, the entities involved, and the temporal aspects associated with the event. Unlike traditional methodologies, LLMs maintain the accuracy of information, thereby facilitating causal inferences and providing clear reasoning. However, current research has not fully leveraged the potential of LLMs. For instance, methods such as CAMEF utilize LLMs to extract event details, subsequently condensing these into smaller data chunks for processing by conventional neural networks. Consequently, LLMs are relegated to the role of sophisticated feature extraction tools, a process which results in the loss of critical event details and fails to elucidate the interconnections or temporal dynamics of events, thus lacking a reusable and traceable memory system. In contrast, our approach diverges by utilizing detailed event information as foundational elements to construct a structured memory of events. By meticulously tracking, integrating, and organizing events, the proposed method enables a direct understanding of temporal event changes and facilitates the comparison of patterns across diverse scenarios. This addresses the limitations observed in current LLM methodologies regarding the linking of event sequences and the identification of recurring patterns. The primary focus of our work is the development of a system designed to analyze events for the prediction of stock prices, which constructs a knowledge base from news events and enhances it with model feedback. This allows the system to identify and apply analogous event patterns from historical data.

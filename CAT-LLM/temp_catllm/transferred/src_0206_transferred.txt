The model was trained using a methodology analogous to the prior supervised fine-tuning (SFT) process, wherein reasoning examples generated during training were incorporated to enhance its reasoning capabilities. Additionally, certain sentences were randomly replaced with tokens, a technique consistent with previous approaches, to further refine the model's efficiency in reasoning tasks. During its operational deployment, the model predominantly functions in a standard mode; however, upon encountering specific tags, it transitions to an alternative reasoning mode, as illustrated in Figure 1. These tags delineate the distinction between the two operational modes but are not embedded as special tokens within the model's vocabulary. Rather than explicitly training the model to utilize these tags, prompts were employed to guide its behavior. The model autonomously determines the number of tokens to generate by predicting a numerical value. Initially, the use of these tags is infrequent, but their prevalence increases progressively as training advances. Two distinct tasks, Flawed Fictions and Next-Chapter Prediction, were utilized to evaluate the efficacy of the proposed methodology, with an emphasis placed on the setup of these tasks and the training of a baseline reasoning model devoid of the specialized reasoning mode. Both tasks necessitate intricate story-based reasoning that diverges from conventional tasks in this domain. The Flawed Fictions task, introduced by Ahuja et al. in 2025, was specifically designed to assess competencies such as understanding character intentions and maintaining continuity in narrative details. This dataset comprises short stories sourced from Project Gutenberg and modified using the FlawedFictionSMaker tool, which introduces deliberate errors such as plot inconsistencies and continuity disruptions. Subsequently, these modified stories undergo a filtering process conducted by human reviewers to ensure quality and relevance, as detailed further in Table 2.

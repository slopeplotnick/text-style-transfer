At the conclusion of the study, participants were invited to provide any additional reflections regarding AI-generated music, with the specific wording of this prompt detailed in Supplement C. Personality traits were assessed using a brief version of the Big Five Inventory (BFI-10), which has been validated for use in both English and German contexts. The five traits under investigation included Openness. Demographic information, such as age, gender, nationality, and education level, was also collected to better characterize the sample and examine whether these variables influenced the results. Although the survey design permitted non-binary responses, all participants identified as either male or female, and consequently, gender was recorded within this binary framework. Language proficiency was evaluated to ensure participants were capable of discerning subtle nuances in the emotional descriptors utilized in the survey. Data analysis was conducted using Python (version 3.9.20), with pandas (version 2.2.2) employed for data organization, scipy (version 1.12.0) applied for statistical tests such as correlation and ANOVA, and statsmodels (version 0.14.4) utilized for mixed-effects modeling. Visualization of the findings was performed in R (version 4.3.3) using the ggplot2 package. Open-ended responses concerning AI-generated music were analyzed through thematic analysis following the methodology proposed by Braun and Clarke (2006) and processed using MAXQDA (version 24.10). Additional data validation procedures, including the averaging of song ratings for individual participants, are elaborated in Appendix C. These checks confirmed that preferences for music varied by composer but revealed no significant differences in the intensity of emotional responses. To determine whether the experimental design influenced participants’ music preferences or the intensity of their emotional responses, beyond individual differences and the type of music, linear mixed-effects models were employed. These models incorporated random effects for both participants and songs while also accounting for personality traits, listening habits, and the music model utilized.

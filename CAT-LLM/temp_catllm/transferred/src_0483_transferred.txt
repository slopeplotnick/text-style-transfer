To evaluate the feasibility of employing large language model (LLM) reasoning for addressing multi-robot task allocation (MRTA) problems, the SMART-LLM framework, as proposed by Kannan et al. (2024), was adopted as a foundational reference. This approach facilitated the identification of fundamental challenges associated with utilizing LLMs for coordination, thereby informing the development of an enhanced framework. Testing was conducted using the SMART-LLM dataset, which comprised 36 tasks categorized into four levels: Elemental (single action), Simple (multiple objects with homogeneous robots), Compound (heterogeneous robots with specialized skills), and Complex (collaborative team operations). The tasks were executed within the AI2-THOR simulation environment. For the LLM configuration, Claude 4 Sonnet was employed as the reasoning tool, with a temperature setting of 0.0 to ensure consistency and reproducibility of outcomes. Performance evaluation adhered to the SMART-LLM methodology, incorporating five metrics: Success Rate (SR), Task Completion Rate (TCR), Goal Condition Recall (GCR), Executability (EXE), and Robot Utilization (RU), which collectively assessed task performance and the efficacy of robot collaboration. Observations during the SMART-LLM testing phase revealed issues related to validation and LLM reliability, prompting the development and subsequent testing of the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture. This architecture demonstrated that the implementation of structured validation mechanisms and retry procedures could address these limitations, thereby substantiating the applicability of LLMs to MRTA tasks. The Self Corrective-Agent Architecture incorporated multi-step validation procedures, including up to three retries per agent and systematic checks at each stage of task decomposition, assignment, and code generation, alongside feedback loops designed to facilitate iterative improvement.

Financial prediction systems typically store information as discrete documents or isolated facts; however, the dynamic nature of financial markets necessitates the storage of events in sequential order to facilitate an understanding of causal relationships over time. Specifically, the prediction of stock market trends requires an analysis of analogous historical scenarios, as relying solely on the interpretation of individual documents proves insufficient. Conventional systems often adopt memory models that prioritize temporal decay or simplistic human memory analogies, yet financial applications demand the retention of significant patterns over extended durations. Consequently, this study proposes a novel approach to enhance memory mechanisms within financial applications by constructing a comprehensive and continuously updated repository of financial knowledge tailored for language models. To achieve this, a system was developed to transform market data into structured memory units encompassing dates, event types, and relevant contextual entities, such as companies and industries. This methodology facilitates the integration of market data into coherent sequences, thereby enabling the identification and interpretation of underlying patterns. Furthermore, the system incorporates a mechanism for retrieving similar event sequences by utilizing the current sequence as a reference point, which surpasses traditional document-level analysis in effectiveness. By focusing on the retrieval of complex historical patterns aligned with present market conditions, the system transitions from basic text matching to advanced pattern recognition. Empirical evaluations demonstrate the superior predictive performance of the proposed system in comparison to existing models, as it effectively captures event sequences that elucidate the critical factors influencing stock prices and their temporal evolution. This capability not only enhances predictive accuracy but also provides transparency and clarity in identifying the determinants of market behavior.

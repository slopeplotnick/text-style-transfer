In this experiment, different models were swapped for Stage 1 and Stage 2 while maintaining the total number of function evaluations constant. Specifically, it was observed that employing a smaller model for Stage 1 did not significantly affect the gFID, indicating that predicting the basic layout with some noise is not substantially impacted by the model's size. However, employing a weaker model for Stage 2 resulted in a considerable decline in the quality of the generated outputs, demonstrating that increased capacity and training are beneficial for refining details. This two-stage approach has been previously discussed, and the current investigation focuses on common practices with this methodology in mind. The aim is to elucidate the reasons these methods are effective and to understand and enhance them by considering the distinct roles of navigation and refinement. Furthermore, an analysis is conducted on how the selection of different latent spaces influences the model's behavior within a two-stage framework. As illustrated in Figure 9, the oracle loss variation during training with VA-VAE and SD-VAE latent spaces is presented. During the navigation stage, both spaces exhibit a gradual increase in oracle loss, suggesting that predicting the basic layout becomes more challenging irrespective of the latent space utilized. Nevertheless, during the refinement stage, the loss patterns alter significantly. Although the exact numerical values are not directly comparable, trends are discernible in the shapes of the oracle loss curves. For VA-VAE, which aligns its latent space with semantic structure through DINO-based VF loss, the oracle loss progressively forms a parabola-like shape as training advances, indicating a well-organized latent space with clearly arranged semantic modes. Conversely, SD-VAE, focused on low-level reconstruction, results in a flatter curve that is slightly wavy, suggesting its latent space is less organized and the model faces challenges in achieving consistent denoising. Overall, latent spaces that align effectively with high-level concepts are advantageous for diffusion modeling, as they facilitate the organization of modes under Gaussian marginals, thereby making both navigation and refinement processes more efficient.

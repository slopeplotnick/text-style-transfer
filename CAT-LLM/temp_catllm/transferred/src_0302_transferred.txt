The GPT-5 model frequently declines tasks, a behavior that persists even with additional contextual information. Consequently, its performance on harmful tasks is diminished; however, given the pre-existing low harm score, the implications of this behavior remain limited. Testing on harmless tasks with over 10,000 tokens of context has not been pursued further due to the stability of results and the prohibitive cost associated with conducting such tests. It is evident that the type of context padding can significantly impact model performance, as illustrated in Figure 3, where various padding types exhibit distinct effects. Specifically, for the GPT-4.1-nano, non-relevant padding achieves higher scores, followed by relevant padding, random padding, and multi-task padding, which records the lowest scores. In contrast, Grok 4 Fast diverges from this pattern due to its elevated refusal rate, with scores across different padding types closely resembling those without padding. Between 50,000 and 100,000 tokens, Grok 4 Fast appears to demonstrate a collapse-like behavior, resulting in the convergence of scores among various padding types. The placement of padding is crucial for model performance, as evidenced by Figure 4, which indicates that inserting padding before the task description decelerates the deterioration of model performance compared to placing it after. This phenomenon can be attributed to the proximity of task description and execution when padding precedes them, thereby enhancing the model's attention mechanism. Modern attention techniques, such as sliding window and RoPE, exhibit limitations in focusing on tokens that are spatially distant, a scenario infrequently encountered during training. Conversely, when padding is positioned after the task description, the model is required to bridge a substantial gap between instruction and execution, potentially accounting for its accelerated performance decline.

In 2024, content is systematically categorized into distinct sections and subsequently summarized in various languages to facilitate interactive question-and-answer sessions. The transcribed multimodal speech translation output serves as the contextual foundation for this process. Modern large language models are capable of processing extensive contexts; however, they are subject to certain limitations. Consequently, lectures are translated into multiple languages, segmented into chapters to prevent content overflow, and refined to enhance clarity due to the chapter's adherence to singular topics. Compressed versions of each chapter are generated, including transcripts at varying compression levels (50%, 70%, 90%) and summaries proportionate to the length of the original section. The process commences with English summaries, given the superior functionality of large language models with English, which are then translated into other languages. For the question-and-answer component, a similar approach is adopted: English transcripts are organized by chapters and used in conjunction with Retrieval-Augmented Generation to pose inquiries to a large language model, following which the responses are translated. Another challenge pertains to the translation of slides into different languages, wherein slides contain both editable text and text within images. A Python PowerPoint parser is employed to extract text for translation using standard machine translation, thus avoiding multimodal models due to their prohibitive cost. Text embedded in images presents extraction challenges, lacks contextual clarity, and necessitates visual elements for accurate interpretation, thereby requiring multimodal translation. Subsequent to translation, text is reintegrated into the image to preserve both layout and meaning. An image-translation process is proposed that identifies, reads, translates, and reinserts text within slide images.

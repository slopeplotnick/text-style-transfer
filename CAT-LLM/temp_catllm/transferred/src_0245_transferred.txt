Proper licensing of datasets is essential for facilitating their ethical utilization and accessibility, particularly in the context of languages that lack substantial resources. Open licenses, such as Creative Commons, including the CC BY 4.0, significantly enhance the scale at which data can be reused. Initiatives like Mozilla Common Voice exemplify the utility of open licenses in advancing language technology. Nevertheless, access to resources for AI development remains limited for many researchers in low- and middle-income countries, raising concerns about equitable data management and the empowerment of local communities. For the Swivuriso project, the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) license was employed to ensure universal accessibility and ethical accountability. Swivuriso is hosted on platforms such as Zenodo and Hugging Face, accompanied by permanent DOIs and comprehensive documentation. Each release includes audio files, corresponding transcripts, and detailed information on demographics, language, and accessibility. Furthermore, an ethical use statement is provided, imposing limitations on practices such as voice cloning to protect speaker privacy. The CC BY 4.0 license is notably permissive, permitting sharing, modification, and utilization of the dataset for any purpose, including commercial applications, provided that the creators are credited and modifications are noted. The license precludes additional legal or technical restrictions, encompasses database rights, and features a "cure period" for rectifying license issues, ensuring legal clarity and supporting global scientific endeavors. However, while open licenses are effective in enhancing data usability, they frequently overlook aspects such as local control or the safeguarding of Indigenous knowledge. The uniform application of such licenses may inadvertently undermine community ownership, particularly concerning cultural elements or community-derived data. This challenge is prompting the emergence of novel paradigms that prioritize data stewardship, benefit-sharing, and community empowerment, which are gaining traction within African data management practices.

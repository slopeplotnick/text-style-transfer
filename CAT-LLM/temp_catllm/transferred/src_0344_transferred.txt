The initial experiment conducted within the MODOMA laboratory was predicated upon a specific connection, serving as a mechanism to evaluate the efficacy of the MODOMA approach in facilitating language acquisition in accordance with Zipf's law. The present study contributes to the advancement of research by enhancing the MODOMA system through the implementation of increasingly intricate tests, thereby accumulating experiential knowledge that positions MODOMA as a viable model for language learning assessments and refining the system prior to the exploration of alternative language acquisition methodologies. While Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) examine the genesis of languages, they similarly employ Zipf's law to elucidate language models in relation to human languages. This methodology incorporates the majority of components and procedural stages requisite for more sophisticated learning tests. The constituent elements of the MODOMA system, along with their interactive functionalities, are subject to verification and optimization to facilitate additional computational language learning evaluations. In the event that the learning process proves successful, the daughter agent discerns the distinction between functional and content categories through exemplars of the target language provided by the mother agent during their interactions. It is imperative that, upon the daughter's agent comprehension of this grammatical differentiation, it promptly applies its newfound grammatical knowledge to revise its grammar and construct novel sentences during dialogues with the mother agent, as well as to comprehend new sentences from the mother. Consequently, this investigation ascertains whether the MODOMA laboratory method for language acquisition can culminate in the mastery of pivotal grammatical knowledge that is effectively applicable in both the comprehension and generation of language.

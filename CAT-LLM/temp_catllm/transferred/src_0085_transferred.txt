The findings of this study indicate that perceptions of artificial intelligence (AI) significantly influence the evaluation of AI-generated music, extending beyond auditory characteristics. It is proposed that the development of tools tailored specifically for AI music, alongside the establishment of novel evaluation frameworks, is necessary. As AI-generated music becomes increasingly prevalent on streaming platforms, considerations regarding fairness and transparency in its creation and distribution must be addressed. Given that individual attitudes toward AI shape both the reception and emotional impact of AI-generated music, further investigation into the communication of production and distribution processes is warranted. For instance, empirical studies could examine the implications of transparency concerning the datasets utilized for AI training or the equitable allocation of financial proceeds derived from AI music. Moreover, diverse methodologies for creating and employing AI-generated music require systematic exploration. The public has demonstrated a notable interest in collaborative compositions involving both human creators and AI technologies, facilitated by advancements in interactive tools. Practical applications and societal perceptions of AI music, as illustrated by Moura and Maw (2021), offer valuable insights into this phenomenon. The research underscores that attitudes toward AI are more pivotal in shaping judgments of AI-generated music than factors such as the creator, genre, or listening habits. Future studies should monitor evolving societal perceptions of AI, as these shifts are likely to influence the acceptance or rejection of AI within music and broader contexts. The central question extends beyond whether AI music can be appreciated or perceived as human-like, focusing instead on broader conceptions of authenticity and valuable creativity. By engaging with public perspectives, it becomes possible to identify and define the forms of creativity that merit recognition and support.

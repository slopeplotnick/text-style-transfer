The Style-based Motion Policy (SMP) constitutes a tool designed to facilitate the training of motion policies by awarding rewards contingent upon style, thereby obviating the necessity for original motion data to function effectively. Consequently, diverse methods may be employed to adjust the reward model and modify motion dynamics, although this occasionally results in movements appearing unnatural. Comparative analyses of disparate methods are illustrated in an accompanying video. Moreover, SMP enables the generation of novel motion styles through modification of the outputs of the pretrained model, thus allowing for the creation of styles that were previously absent without necessitating additional training of the model. Specifically, as demonstrated in Figure 6, two distinct styles, such as "AeroPlane" and "HighKnees," may be amalgamated to produce a new style by blending their respective predictions from the model. Furthermore, a single pretrained motion model proves versatile in training policies for various tasks, including steering, target acquisition, and dodgeball, as delineated in Table 2. The motion model undergoes training using a segment of the LaFAN1 dataset, characterized by random running movements, and the resultant policies exhibit efficacy in task execution while maintaining a natural appearance. Evidence presented in Figures 5(a) and 5(b) illustrates the character's ability to transition between diverse movements to continue task performance whilst preserving naturalistic qualities. For instance, the steering policy prompts the character to jog backward when oriented away from the intended direction and subsequently jog forward upon proper orientation. Additionally, side-steps and cross-steps are executed seamlessly without necessitating a separate motion planner. In the context of the Target Location task, the policy trained with SMP discerns human-like movements, such as autonomously decelerating when required.

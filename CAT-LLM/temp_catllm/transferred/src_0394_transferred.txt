Despite its significance, a comprehensive methodology for measuring and analyzing the power and energy consumption associated with large language models (LLMs) remains elusive, particularly when considering variables such as model sizes, hardware configurations, software environments, and deployment contexts, whether on a single machine or distributed across multiple systems. Existing efforts have attempted to address this gap, yet they do not provide a complete resolution. For instance, the MLPerf Inference benchmark, as detailed by Reddi et al. (2020), establishes performance standards across various machine learning tasks, albeit without thoroughly examining total power consumption or tailoring energy metrics to LLM-specific operations, such as Joules per token or prompt. The MLPerf Power benchmark, discussed by Tschand et al. (2025), encompasses a broader range of measurements; however, it predominantly focuses on single-machine setups, assesses smaller models relative to the latest expansive LLMs—such as the Llama 3 405B-class, which remain underexplored—and necessitates costly, precision instruments that pose challenges for widespread application. Moreover, current benchmarks frequently overlook configurable settings that users adjust during real-world applications, such as batch size, parallel processing, context length, and quantization levels, each of which significantly influences instantaneous power consumption and average energy per token. To address these limitations, the present study introduces TokenPowerBench, a novel benchmark designed to facilitate straightforward assessment of power and energy usage by LLMs. TokenPowerBench integrates with vendor-provided tools, including power sensors for GPU, CPU, and memory, enabling energy assessment at the computer level and, where feasible, at the rack or facility level. A dedicated pipeline aligns each power measurement with the primary inference phases—prefill and decode—thereby elucidating the specific stages of energy utilization. Extensive experimentation is conducted to investigate the correlations between energy consumption and factors such as batch size, maximum context length, task partitioning, and quantization. The principal contributions of this work are encapsulated within these analyses.

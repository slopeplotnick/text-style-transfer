In small groups of individuals or systems, close and brief interactions often lead to the emergence of minor issues. As these groups engage in the exchange of information within partially connected networks, the potential for significant risks becomes apparent, as these minor issues begin to propagate. When full connectivity is achieved and these issues reach a critical threshold, substantial problems manifest, resulting in unanticipated changes in the group's behavior. This conceptual framework serves as a preliminary approach for facilitating the discussion and examination of such behaviors. In the absence of this framework, researchers face challenges in identifying, measuring, or managing these group behaviors effectively. To validate this framework, extensive experimentation in controlled environments is necessary, which is planned for future research endeavors. The initial emergence of small risks occurs during direct interactions among a limited number of individuals or systems, where early indicators of more significant issues become evident. These risks underscore the fragility of shared understanding, even prior to the large-scale collaboration of the network. Despite their small beginnings, these issues have the potential to escalate into major problems as interactions persist. Once a problem becomes ingrained within a system's understanding, it tends to proliferate and amplify with each subsequent interaction. The proliferation of problems outpaces the growth of individuals or messages. When larger groups interact within partially connected networks, the manifestation of substantial risks becomes evident. At this juncture, no single problem may appear prominent, yet the nature of interactions contributes to the deterioration of attributes such as accuracy or diversity. Each component may seem adequate in isolation, but collectively, they fail to function as anticipated. These larger risks illustrate that problems can arise even in the absence of complete system breakdowns. Such issues originate from the interactions of partially correct components rather than isolated errors. It is at this point that local predictability begins to falter, leading to instability.

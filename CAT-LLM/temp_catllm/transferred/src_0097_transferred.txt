In the context of task management, Node 6 facilitates the retrying of tasks with feedback, permitting up to three attempts for each task. Enhanced prompts integrating the original information with specific feedback are utilized to refine reasoning during the retry process. Advanced methods are employed to adapt strategies contingent upon the project's phase, converting weights into reasoning directives. The prompts are modified according to the project's stage, concentrating on varying priorities. Initially, directives are oriented towards maximizing success rates with instructions such as selecting robots with superior success rates, while acknowledging that workloads may not be perfectly balanced. Subsequently, the emphasis transitions towards balancing workloads, employing directives such as prioritizing workload fairness. This configuration inherently aids the system in adhering to the strategies pertinent to each phase. The authors achieve this through three methodologies: firstly, by modeling robot capabilities to forecast their likelihood of task success; secondly, by devising prompts that incorporate numerical data and workload specifics; and thirdly, by structuring responses to facilitate ease of verification and validation. Robots are categorized into three types—Light, Medium, and Heavy—each possessing distinct strengths and probabilities of task success, determined by the congruence of the robots' abilities with the task requirements. The success rate for each robot is computed by amalgamating its proficiency in handling each task feature, where a robot's capability to perform a task is contingent upon the alignment between the task's demands and the robot's capabilities. Tasks are processed sequentially from a compilation of 36 tasks in the TEACH dataset, with each task undergoing a comprehensive process that includes phase determination, prompt creation, reasoning, verification, and assignment before proceeding to the subsequent task. Upon completion of each task, system progress is updated, and the number of remaining tasks is reduced until full task assignment is achieved.

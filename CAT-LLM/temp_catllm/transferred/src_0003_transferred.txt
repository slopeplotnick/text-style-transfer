The investigation focused on seven methodologies to integrate models with the aim of mitigating social bias, employing three bias assessments alongside SuperGLUE with thirteen extensive language models. It was determined that the utilization of SLERP with moderate configurations constitutes the most effective approach to diminish bias without compromising accuracy. It is crucial to evaluate the efficacy of these methodologies on tasks such as reading comprehension and reasoning to address social bias. In recent developments, the combination of models has emerged as a viable strategy to leverage the strengths of diverse models without necessitating retraining, as indicated by Li et al. (2023) and Yang et al. (2024). This process involves the amalgamation of components from two or more trained models to construct a singular model that preserves and synthesizes the knowledge inherent in each model. The linear averaging method, initially introduced by Wortsman et al. (2022), offers a simplistic approach by averaging weights, thus providing an economical means to integrate diverse capabilities, utilizing pre-trained models. Further advancements by Matena and Raffel (2022) and Lee et al. (2025) enhance this approach by adjusting each component based on its significance, ensuring logical coherence in the combination. Alternative methodologies such as sphere interpolation, referenced by Shoemake (1985) and Goddard et al. (2024), conceptualize these components as points on a sphere. SLERP, as developed by Shoemake (1985), facilitates the blending of two models, while the Karcher Mean, introduced by Grove and Karcher (1973), computes the average across multiple models. NuSLERP, proposed by Goddard et al. (2024), incorporates an additional step to rectify any dimensional alterations. Ilharco et al. (2022) devised a technique to apply these concepts to specific tasks, utilizing the notion of a "task vector," which delineates the variance between a pre-trained model and a fine-tuned counterpart.

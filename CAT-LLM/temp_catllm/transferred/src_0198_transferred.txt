The construction of explanation models often presents challenges, particularly in determining the structural equations for the fuzzy soft cognitive maps (FSCM). Section 5.2 elucidates the results, encompassing standard probabilistic soft cognitive map (PSCM) intervals for counterfactual queries, which serve to illustrate the absence of clarity in real causal mechanisms. For certain queries, these intervals are narrow, thus allowing the average of the interval or even approximate queries via a causal Bayesian network (CBN) to provide a reasonable estimation of the actual value, as the precise model may hold diminished significance. However, this does not universally apply. Generally, when analyses are predicated on infrequent events, the counterfactual probability can range from 0 to 1 contingent on the model employed. Two distinct FSCMs that conform to the data may yield diametrically opposed explanations for the same model and may even assert certainty regarding those outcomes. Rare observations are crucial to elucidate accurately, particularly in scenarios requiring predictions to be coherent in contexts where training data is sparse. Consequently, an FSCM remains essential to ensure the computability of any query. If experts provide partial knowledge to define non-standard PSCMs, the intervals might be reduced, facilitating the approximation of more queries. Future research could investigate the efficacy of various methodologies for precise and approximate calculations. The explanations in this study are predicated on the assessment of the probability that concept interventions suffice, although the framework is not confined to this aspect. Furthermore, future research could explore probabilities of necessity and alternative explanation modalities.

Modern market systems are generally recognized for their superior capacity to adapt, evolve, and address challenges in comparison to centralized systems. Auction-based frameworks, introduced by Botelho and Alami (1999), have been proposed as mechanisms for competitively assigning tasks, and subsequent research by Gerkey and Matario (2002) demonstrated their effectiveness in distributing tasks while maintaining coordination. More recently, efforts have been directed toward addressing communication-related constraints within auction systems through the implementation of advanced bidding strategies in scenarios characterized by weak connectivity (Ferri et al., 2017; Quinton et al., 2022). Nevertheless, it has been observed that auction methodologies may fail to achieve globally optimal solutions due to localized bidding processes, which can disrupt coordination, particularly in cases involving highly interdependent tasks. Within the domain of construction, task execution is often contingent upon strict sequencing, and auction-based approaches have been noted to encounter difficulties in accommodating temporal dependencies critical to project planning. Consequently, structured optimization techniques have been investigated as alternative methodologies. Dynamic programming has been identified as a viable approach for sequential decision-making optimization, with foundational principles established by Bellman in 1962 for addressing problems exhibiting specific characteristics. However, the computational complexity associated with dynamic programming escalates significantly in the presence of extensive solution spaces, rendering its application to large-scale problems infeasible. Construction projects, characterized by numerous robots performing interdependent tasks, frequently present computational challenges that preclude real-time implementation of dynamic programming solutions. To address these limitations, researchers have explored metaheuristic methodologies, which leverage evolutionary principles to identify solutions to intricate optimization problems. Genetic algorithms have demonstrated utility in managing complex constraints without necessitating comprehensive mathematical formulations (Jones et al., 2010; Al-Omeer and Ahmed, 2019). Similarly, swarm intelligence techniques have been shown to facilitate the coordination of large robotic teams effectively (Chen et al., 2022; Lim and Isa, 2015; Wang et al., 2012; Blum, 2005). However, it is important to note that metaheuristic approaches do not guarantee globally optimal solutions and are computationally intensive, often requiring multiple iterations to converge on satisfactory outcomes. In the context of construction, where operational adaptability and rapid responsiveness are critical, the prolonged computation times associated with metaheuristic methods have been identified as a considerable limitation.

The daughter language model, designed for the MOD-OMA, functions similarly to Delilah as both a parser and generator, yet it differs from the adult language model in that its grammar is not fully detailed initially, becoming more refined as it undergoes learning processes. Both the adult and child systems utilize graph structures to represent grammatical knowledge, with their parsers and generators operating by combining word structures to construct correct sentences and evaluate grammar according to existing rules. The child agent's parser and generator exhibit the capability to function with incomplete grammar, akin to the manner in which humans acquire their first language, necessitating interaction with the mother model prior to achieving complete learning. The daughter agent is comprised of three integral components: an automaton serving as the parser and generator, a grammar that evolves in detail through learning, and a language acquisition device. It should be noted that the parser and generator remain unchanged during the learning process. Learning results are manifested in explicit grammar knowledge, which is evidenced by the introduction of new word entries and grammar details that impose limitations on permissible word combinations, as illustrated in Figure 2. These structures provide comprehensive information concerning words and constructions, incorporating grammar details such as phonological form, semantic content, and additional grammatical properties. Grammar properties are represented by pairs, for instance, [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: content word] and [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: function word], which constitute the primary focus of this study. The learning process is unsupervised, implying that the daughter model lacks awareness of the labels employed by the mother model or the grammar descriptions. The learned grammar properties and semantic content are distinguished by unique labels, such as property type 'A' and property value 'b' in Figure 2 and Figure 3. Moreover, these structures encompass other grammatical properties not addressed by the present study. For example, the terminal feature delineates terminal words from abstract constructions, and the semantic content index assists in representing elements such as reflexives. Technical details, including session id and word entry number, are incorporated to uniquely identify each grammatical rule. As language learning is simulated, the grammar is capable of evolving into more complex structures, with the HEAD and ARGUMENT potentially encompassing structures such as word items.

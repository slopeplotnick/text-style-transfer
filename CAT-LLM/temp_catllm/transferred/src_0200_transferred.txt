Curriculum learning constitutes a methodology for training models by commencing with tasks of lesser complexity and progressively advancing to more challenging ones. This concept was initially introduced by Bengio et al. in 2009 and has since gained substantial application in the development of large language models. Contemporary approaches, such as Speed-RL and the methodologies proposed by Kim and Lee, continue to employ this strategy, categorizing tasks from simple to complex based on their level of difficulty. LBS3 represents another technique that trains models by starting with easy questions and escalating to more complex ones. However, these strategies do not adequately align with effective curriculum design principles, as they rely on fixed difficulty levels rather than adjusting dynamically in response to the model's performance. It is imperative for curriculum learning to adapt according to the model's improvement trajectory. CAPO, in contrast, implements a system that evaluates the model's performance and modifies the training regimen accordingly. The process begins with positive feedback to establish stable behavior and subsequently introduces negative feedback to enhance learning outcomes. Differing from other methods that adhere to fixed difficulty levels, CAPO evolves as the model progresses, ensuring stability during the initial learning phase and adaptability in subsequent stages. CAPO embodies an innovative approach to model training, utilizing feedback mechanisms to construct a curriculum that evolves as the model learns. The process initiates with learning from positive examples and subsequently transitions to incorporating both positive and negative examples, akin to human learning processes. This strategy mitigates challenges associated with premature feedback integration. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that CAPO enhances model performance relative to other methodologies across various tasks and model sizes. Furthermore, CAPO exhibits efficacy across diverse domains, from mathematical computations to tasks involving heterogeneous data types. By prioritizing the model's improvement trajectory over fixed difficulty levels, CAPO contributes to the development of learning algorithms that are both flexible and inspired by human cognitive processes.

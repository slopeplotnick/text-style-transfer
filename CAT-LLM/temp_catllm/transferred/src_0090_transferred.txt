Recent investigations have been conducted on various language models, encompassing both closed and open configurations, with further information elaborated in Appendix A.4. In the course of the evaluation, attempts were made to deactivate any supplementary cognitive or reasoning settings to observe the models' inherent behaviors. This approach was applicable to all models except GPT-5 mini, which restricted modifications to the depth of reasoning, thus necessitating its adjustment to the minimal setting. Additionally, a comparative study involving human participants was executed on all tasks with certain modifications, as detailed in Appendix A.5, with human performance depicted on the left side of Figure 8. It is noteworthy that this experiment primarily serves as a reference, as the principal focus lies in the comparative analysis of language models. The literature on human perception is extensive, exemplified by the works of Jazayeri and Shadlen (2010) and Petzschner and Glasauer (2011). Predominantly, models exhibit superior performance with text-based inputs as opposed to images, with the exception observed in the maze distance estimation task. The textual component of this task consists of a lengthy and intricate path description, offering greater complexity than the simpler text employed in other tasks, as illustrated in Figure 2. Notably, most models exhibit diminished efficacy with text for this particular task, yet GPT-5 Mini demonstrates near-optimal performance due to the inability to completely deactivate its reasoning capabilities, thereby illustrating task-dependent behavior. Across the spectrum of tasks, evidence supporting Bayesian behavior tends to be more pronounced with image-based inputs compared to text, as indicated in Appendix A.11. Transitioning from singular to multiple input types does not invariably enhance performance. Nonetheless, certain models exhibit proficient utilization of additional information, with Llama-4 Maverick performing optimally across all tasks when multiple inputs are provided, while Claude 3.7 Sonnet and GPT-4o display improved outcomes in two out of three tasks.

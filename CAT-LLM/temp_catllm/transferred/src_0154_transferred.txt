The utilization of system prompts has emerged as an effective strategy for guiding the behavior of large language models (LLMs), with empirical studies indicating that carefully designed prompts can enhance reasoning capabilities and mitigate bias. However, the predominant focus of this research has been on English prompts, with limited attention paid to the efficacy of these prompts across diverse languages. Consequently, when these models are deployed in multilingual contexts, challenges arise due to the variable effectiveness of prompts originally tailored for English, leading to decreased accuracy, unstable reasoning, and inconsistent outcomes across different languages. Therefore, a critical inquiry pertains to the development of prompts that consistently perform well across multiple languages and tasks. To address this issue, the investigation was segmented into three primary questions: firstly, what categories of prompts enhance model performance across various languages; secondly, the feasibility of automatically identifying effective prompts with minimal human intervention; and thirdly, the impact of optimized prompts on reasoning and linguistic quality in model responses. To facilitate this analysis, a novel evaluation framework was devised, encompassing metrics such as average accuracy, accuracy variability, cross-linguistic consistency, and response length variation. By examining model performance across diverse tasks, a methodology was formulated to refine prompts in alignment with these objectives. Subsequently, model outputs were scrutinized to assess the influence of optimized prompts on reasoning quality and cross-linguistic consistency. Furthermore, an innovative approach to the automatic optimization of multilingual prompts was introduced, alongside an extensive analysis of reasoning data, which revealed that effective prompts promote organized and consistent reasoning with reduced language-switching tendencies. These findings imply that reasoning processes may significantly contribute to the enhanced multilingual functionality of LLMs. In summary, the research underscores the potential of optimizing system prompts as a valuable and underexplored avenue for achieving improved and reliable outcomes from multilingual LLMs. The associated code and dataset are accessible at https://github.com/orange0629/multilingual-system-prompt.

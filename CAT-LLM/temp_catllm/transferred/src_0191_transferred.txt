The application of language models in the refinement and categorization of written questions presents significant advancements in mitigating errors and enhancing comprehension among respondents. Specifically, these models can substantially alter the interpretation of questions by minimizing mistakes and misunderstandings. Furthermore, language models serve to ascertain the congruity of questions with their respective categories, allowing researchers to avoid errors through semantic analysis. Consequently, researchers are enabled to discern relationships among diverse categories, potentially necessitating substantial revisions should categories exhibit excessive similarity. Moreover, language models ensure uniformity in reading levels, thereby facilitating comprehension across varied audiences. For further information regarding the utilization of language models in test creation, the recent study by Beghetto et al. offers valuable insights. Additionally, our investigation explores the generation of synthetic data via language models to streamline test development processes. This approach could result in considerable reductions in time and costs typically associated with testing, as preliminary trials on synthetic cohorts may prove economically advantageous. Consequently, this method might democratize test development for researchers, while simultaneously reducing redundancies. Within this article, we evaluate the efficacy of contemporary language models in generating data congruent with test structure paradigms. Subsequently, we elucidate the methodologies employed in our study and the rationale underpinning our hypotheses. In the formulation of these assessments, we adhered predominantly to the principles of Classic Test Theory and the recommendations posited by Boateng et al.

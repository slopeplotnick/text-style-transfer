Initially, duplicates within the cases and questions were meticulously eliminated to ensure the independence of the samples, followed by a comprehensive data cleaning process. Samples lacking questions or options, as well as those containing figures or tables, were systematically removed. Errors arising from Optical Character Recognition (OCR) were identified and manually corrected to ensure textual accuracy. For clinical data, patient names and other identifiable details were either removed or altered to adhere to privacy and ethical standards. The approval of clinical cases for this study was obtained from the Ethics Committee at Xiyuan Hospital. The core component of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) diagnosis and treatment, known as Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment (SDT), entails syndrome differentiation and the formulation of treatment strategies. To evaluate the model's capabilities, a 14-part evaluation framework was developed, encompassing syndrome and causes, as delineated in Table 1. Initially, TCM experts annotated the answers and options, followed by a peer review process, culminating in a third-party assessment to ensure consistency and reliability. Questions were designed with challenging incorrect answers to enhance the discrimination of varying levels of understanding. In addition to evaluating final reasoning, two metrics were introduced to evaluate the process: Chain-of-Thought Content Completeness, which assesses whether the model encompasses key patient information, and CoT Accuracy, which evaluates the alignment of the model's reasoning with the original case to identify inaccuracies. The evaluation tasks are intended to elucidate differences in the model's fundamental understanding and responses, facilitating a more comprehensive evaluation. All questions are presented in a multiple-choice format, allowing for both single and multiple answers. The TCM Basic Knowledge section encompasses four domains: comprehension of TCM classics, fundamental theories, knowledge of Chinese medicine and formulas, and syndrome differentiation based on tongue, pulse, face, and acupoints. Questions for this section were derived from preprocessed exam questions. The Medical Ethics section assesses the model's comprehension of clinical ethics, including the resolution of conflicts between traditional and modern medicine, identification of unscientific practices, respect for patient beliefs, and informed consent. This section utilizes both exam questions and expert-created questions. The Content Safety section evaluates the extent to which the TCM model adheres to professional boundaries and safety regulations. The model is restricted to responding solely to TCM-related queries and is precluded from addressing non-medical questions pertaining to privacy, safety, or values. Questions were designed and reviewed by experts in content safety and ethics to ensure the evaluation aligns with TCM model standards.

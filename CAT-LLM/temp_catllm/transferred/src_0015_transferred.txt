Nearswap, a methodological approach introduced by Goddard et al. in 2024, is characterized by enhanced interpolation strength when parameters exhibit similarity, while reducing the interpolation when parameters differ. To evaluate various model configurations, three distinct groups of large language models were selected for experimentation: GPT, LLAMA, and QWEN. The GPT group, developed by Radford et al. in 2019 and Gao et al. in 2020, comprises GPT2-small, GPT2-medium, GPT2-large, GPT2-x1, and GPT-neo-2.7B. The LLAMA group, proposed by Touvron et al. in 2023 and Dubey et al. in 2024, includes LLAMA-2-7B, LLAMA-3-8B, LLAMA-3.1-8B, LLAMA-3.2-1B, and LLAMA-3.2-3B. The QWEN group, introduced by Qwen in 2024, consists of QWEN2-0.5B, QWEN2-1.5B, and QWEN2-7B, all of which are accessible via the Hugging Face platform, with specific links provided in Appendix A. Computational resources required for continuous pretraining aimed at addressing bias within large language models are detailed in Appendix C, while hyperparameter specifications are outlined in Appendix D. The investigation into social biases present in large language models was conducted utilizing three benchmarks: BBQ, BOLD, and HONEST, with their respective dataset links elucidated in Appendix E. The BBQ dataset, developed by Parrish et al. in 2022, encompasses approximately 58,000 question-answer pairs across nine socially significant domains pertinent to U.S. English speakers, facilitating an assessment of reliance on stereotypes versus evidence by comparing "underspecified" and "fully specified" question versions. The BOLD dataset, curated by Dhamala et al. in 2021, comprises 23,679 prompts categorized into 43 subgroups based on occupational roles, gender, race, religion, and political orientation. To ensure the integrity of debiasing methodologies without compromising model performance across other tasks, both debiased and pretrained large language models were subjected to evaluation using the SuperGLUE benchmark, which incorporates eight distinct tasks: BoolQ, CB, COPA, MultiRC, ReCoRD, RTE, WiC, and WSC. All experiments were systematically conducted utilizing the Language Model Evaluation Harness.

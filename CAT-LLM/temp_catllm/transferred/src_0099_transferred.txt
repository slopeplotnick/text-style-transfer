The investigation into the effects of using structured climate data on retrieval-augmented generation has yielded insights into its current efficacy. The hypothesis posited that the integration of such data would enhance the accuracy and comprehensibility of generated answers; however, findings indicate that the approach remains in nascent stages. Specifically, as illustrated in Table 1, the DATA_RAG configuration, which incorporates both literature and structured data, underperformed relative to the RAG setup that relies solely on literature, with the most notable decline observed in accuracy. This outcome suggests that the present model exhibits limitations in leveraging structured climate data to substantiate or refine textual reasoning. CAIRNS, a question-answering system tailored for agricultural climate adaptation, endeavors to optimize readability, accuracy, and source transparency. It employs structured prompts (SGP), multi-step reasoning, and a consistency-weighted Hybrid Evaluator. Empirical evaluations indicate that SGP enhances structural coherence and source reliability while maintaining accuracy, signifying progress towards aligning QA outputs with the preferences of domain researchers. Nevertheless, the efficacy of data-augmented retrieval remains limited at this juncture. The Hybrid Evaluator capitalizes on model agreement to assess credibility effectively, outperforming automated evaluations without necessitating fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. This approach proves advantageous in contexts characterized by constrained resources or limited expert availability for question-answering tasks. Future efforts aim to refine location-specific reasoning through the incorporation of comprehensive geographic data, including latitude and longitude, thereby facilitating the transferability of documents across regions with analogous climates and broadening the applicability of literature-based insights. Additionally, plans are underway to establish an expansive evaluation benchmark encompassing diverse question types and topics, intended to calibrate the Hybrid Evaluator and furnish training data for forthcoming models, ultimately advancing reliable and reproducible QA research within the domain of climate adaptation.

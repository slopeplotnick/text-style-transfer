The utilization of context by language models to address health-related inquiries and the implications of disrupted context warrant comprehensive investigation. This study examines the proficiency of language models in managing extensive and contradictory health information through the application of a specialized dataset, referred to as HEALTHCONTRADICT, comprising 920 instances. Each instance includes a health-related question, a scientifically substantiated factual response, and two documents presenting conflicting perspectives. Various approaches to posing questions were explored, including the use of accurate, erroneous, or mixed-up contextual information, to evaluate the impact on model-generated responses. In contrast to other evaluation frameworks, HEALTHCONTRADICT provides enhanced insights into the contextual handling capabilities of language models. Empirical findings indicate that these models exhibit competence not solely due to pre-existing knowledge but also through their ability to utilize appropriate context while disregarding irrelevant information. Nevertheless, the propensity of models to generate responses that appear convincing yet lack veracity remains problematic, particularly given the potential risks associated with the dissemination of inaccurate health information. Furthermore, the rapid evolution of health-related data, particularly during crises, coupled with the pervasive spread of unverified information online, poses challenges to the adaptive learning processes of these models. Several methodologies have been proposed to mitigate issues related to mixed-up information. One approach involves modifying the model's knowledge base to align with contextual information, whereas another traces the rationale behind specific model-generated responses. While these methods predominantly focus on either intrinsic knowledge or contextual information, alternative strategies integrate both dimensions by assessing the coherence of the information. Additionally, a method has been proposed that predicts dual responses, one based on contextual data and another derived from the model's intrinsic knowledge. Another technique aims to ensure the selection of the correct response when confronted with contradictory information. Further solutions emphasize preventing the generation of erroneous answers, including the introduction of incorrect or unrelated data during training to enhance model robustness against mixed-up context and the adoption of testing strategies designed to detect and mitigate inaccuracies. These testing strategies encompass careful question formulation, employing multiple readers to corroborate responses, and identifying reliable answers to counter adversarial attacks. Moreover, predictive approaches aimed at identifying facts subject to imminent change are utilized to avert reliance on outdated information. Existing methodologies predominantly concentrate on either rectifying mixed-up data or curbing undesirable model behavior; however, both aspects are critical for advancing the accuracy and reliability of responses in health-related domains.

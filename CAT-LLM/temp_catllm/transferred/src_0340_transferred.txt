The efficacy of episodic memory retrieval across varying temporal durations of events is evaluated utilizing the metric known as temporal intersection over union (tIoU). This metric quantifies the alignment between identified segments and the actual segments by assessing their overlap relative to their total length. The performance of WorldMM is assessed in comparison to other models in domains such as temporal grounding, single-modality retrieval, long-form egocentric video retrieval, and keyframe selection. A thorough explanation of foundational concepts is available in Section C.1. As demonstrated in Table 3, WorldMM achieves superior tIoU scores relative to other robust models. Methods reliant on reasoning and keyframe selection exhibit lower tIoU scores, indicating challenges in processing extensive input contexts. Figure 5 illustrates that enhanced tIoU scores are correlated with increased overall accuracy, particularly in the context of comprehending long videos as opposed to executing singular retrieval steps. This improvement is attributed to the iterative process, which allows the retrieval system to acquire more pertinent information and refine its strategy if initial attempts are suboptimal. An exemplar of this enhancement is presented in Section E.2, wherein the model rectifies an erroneous retrieval to provide a more precise and pertinent response. The operational speed of the system is assessed by measuring the time taken by WorldMM to address 100 random inquiries from EgoLifeQA. As depicted in Figure 6, the proposed method optimally balances speed and accuracy compared to alternatives. Long-video LLMs exhibit prolonged processing times and comparatively lower performance, while RAG- or memory-based methods, despite their faster processing capabilities, require substantial setup and demonstrate reduced efficacy. WorldMM, conversely, completes its iterative retrieval process as required, selectively accessing necessary components, thereby ensuring rapid and accurate execution.

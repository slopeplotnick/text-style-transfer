The utilization of language models has demonstrated substantial improvements in monitoring mental health, surpassing traditional methods. These models exhibit superior capability in context comprehension and information synthesis, thereby enhancing their utility in large-scale mental health assessments. However, large language models present certain challenges, including high operational costs and potential privacy concerns due to the handling of sensitive data on remote servers. Furthermore, their expansive nature renders them unsuitable for deployment on devices with constrained resources, necessitating the exploration of more efficient alternatives. Conversely, smaller language models have been proposed as viable solutions for digital health applications, as they offer simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and the ability to operate directly on devices. Consequently, these models enable the prediction of mental health issues from social media platforms without the requirement for personal data storage, facilitating rapid and ethical monitoring. Notably, the potential of smaller models has not been sufficiently explored. This study investigates the efficacy of small language models in predicting mental health outcomes, indicating their suitability for assessing multiple mental health concerns on devices. Specifically, these models are evaluated on tasks related to stress, depression, and suicide risk. A novel small model, termed Menta, is introduced, characterized by its fine-tuning through a specialized approach and trained on diverse datasets to prioritize accuracy. Menta exhibits robust performance across various mental health tasks, particularly in identifying stress and depression. The model's practical application is further demonstrated through its deployment on an iPhone 15 Pro Max, where it operates in real-time with minimal memory usage, providing a private and scalable mental health support system on the device.

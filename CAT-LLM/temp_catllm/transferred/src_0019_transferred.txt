The proposed methodology employs a layered modeling approach to integrate distinct statistical distributions, each addressing specific aspects of the statistical process independently. Consequently, this framework facilitates the identification of independence among variables within the energy consumption context. For example, while varying CPUs with identical specifications and differing instruction sets exhibit distinct energy consumption patterns, these variations are demonstrated to be mutually independent. The methodology establishes a Worst-Case Energy Consumption (WCEC) model to estimate the overall energy utilization of a device, as illustrated in Figure 1. The model consists of two primary components: the measurement phase, which quantifies the energy consumed by benchmarks on hardware, and the modeling phase, which constructs a Bayesian model to characterize the energy usage attributable to programming languages. Several statically typed programming languages compatible with the Java Virtual Machine (JVM) are incorporated into the model. To uniformly represent these languages, the energy consumption associated with bytecode patterns—generated via the compilation of source code to bytecode—is evaluated, as depicted in Figure 2. A microbenchmark comprises distinct versions of a source code statement across all possible data types and sizes relevant to a specific operation, as visualized in Figure 3. Measurements for each microbenchmark are conducted in iterative cycles, wherein current, voltage, and execution time are systematically recorded. Each cycle involves initializing the multimeter to ensure readiness for measurement, followed by the activation of the garbage collector to mitigate memory noise during the process. To account for measurement variability, each microbenchmark cycle is repeated ten times, employing a split plot method to randomize the sequence of the cycles and thereby minimize potential bias resulting from execution order.

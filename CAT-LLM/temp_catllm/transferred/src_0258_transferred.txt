The conventional approach involves initially performing a deterministic inversion followed by a deterministic interpretation, as illustrated in Figure 2. Subsequently, a geologist with expertise will examine the deterministic inversion to discern the geometries, such as the intrusions significant for exploration. Software that concentrates on a singular deterministic model is utilized to facilitate these interpretations. This methodology requires executing the geophysical inversion prior to geological interpretation. Although some may argue that regularization terms integrate geological knowledge, the clarity and impact of this integration remain ambiguous. Consequently, it appears that geological knowledge is redundantly applied: initially within the regularization term and subsequently during interpretation, potentially leading to cyclical reasoning. However, Bayesian approaches allow for simultaneous consideration of both aspects as a unified problem. Bayesian inversion necessitates contemplation of potential solutions, or geometries, prior to any inversion process. Bayesian inversion offers relatively straightforward solutions that are readily codable and broadly applicable. Re-examining Figure 2, we discuss the establishment of a prior probability model, beginning with the question: what minimal information is available regarding the subsurface prior to data acquisition? Firstly, it is well-known that petrophysical properties exhibit specific ranges, and knowledge of the rock types involved may enable further specification of these ranges. Secondly, these properties are neither random nor uniform across extensive areas; the manner in which deposits form induces specific alterations in these properties. Fundamentally, the minimal information comprises histogram limits and spatial correlation. Mathematically, this can be expressed as a spatial covariance model, or variogram in geostatistics; however, there is uncertainty regarding parameters such as mean, ranges, and anisotropy, which are not random. This leads to a layered conceptual framework.

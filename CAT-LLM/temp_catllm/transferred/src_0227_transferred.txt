In recent developments, several datasets have emerged, such as Mozilla's Common Voice, which encompasses recorded speech in six South African languages. Contributions to this dataset have been facilitated by individuals, and it is distributed under a license that permits unrestricted use, thereby fostering accessibility. FLEURS comprises approximately 12 hours of recordings for each of the four South African languages, primarily intended for testing and evaluation purposes. Vuk'uzenzele (VixSD), a smaller dataset curated by a community, is disseminated under a license emphasizing community ownership and ethical utilization. Despite its limited size, it exemplifies the potential of local contributions in resource creation. AfriSpeech-200 includes English recordings with South African accents, focusing on healthcare topics, which is instrumental in developing speech recognition systems that accommodate accent variations. However, the overall availability of data featuring natural, unscripted speech across diverse topics remains insufficient. This circumstance indicates a pressing need for more heterogeneous datasets, such as Swivuriso, which integrate both scripted and unscripted speech, encompass a broader spectrum of topics, and incorporate a wider array of speakers. The meticulous collection of speech data within underrepresented communities necessitates adherence to ethical considerations, including consent, privacy, cultural values, and the long-term implications for these communities. Previous initiatives like Common Voice and ALFFA have demonstrated the significance of embedding ethical and participatory practices throughout the process. For instance, an investigation into the Catalan version of Common Voice underscored the necessity for clear consent protocols, privacy protection, and inclusive participation. The ALFFA project similarly engaged closely with local experts and linguists during data collection to ensure community involvement. Swivuriso adopts these principles by prioritizing ethical practices and inclusivity. Local organizers were instrumental in managing the recordings, supporting participants, and ensuring adequate community representation. They adhered to ethical guidelines validated by institutions, including the removal of personal information, data anonymization, and the incorporation of safeguards to prevent misuse, such as voice cloning or biometric tracking. Ensuring the accessibility and usability of datasets is crucial, particularly for languages with limited resources. Open licenses, like Creative Commons, are beneficial in this regard as they facilitate unrestricted data usage, which is advantageous when data collection is challenging or costly. Initiatives like Common Voice illustrate how open licensing can expedite the sharing of resources and accelerate the advancement of language technology for languages lacking substantial support.

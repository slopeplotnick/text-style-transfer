Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated rapid advancements in their capabilities, including generating academic manuscripts, conducting literature reviews, and drafting scientific reports. LLM-based systems designed for survey generation are fundamentally transforming the process of extracting information from extensive data repositories by integrating search, organization, and synthesis into a cohesive and efficient workflow. Various systems for survey generation have been developed by both academic researchers and industry practitioners, which can be broadly categorized into three distinct types: general systems for generating extended textual content (e.g., Kimi and GLM), specialized tools for the creation of research papers (e.g., Chengpian and Doubao Paper Mode), and domain-specific tools tailored for academic survey generation (e.g., SurveyGo, SurveyX, and Panshi ScienceOne). These tools are capable of processing substantial volumes of academic literature, identifying relevant content, and producing well-structured survey drafts that incorporate accurate citations and maintain logical coherence. However, despite notable progress in the development of such systems, the challenge of effectively evaluating their performance remains a significant concern. Current evaluation practices predominantly rely on subjective assessments, which are inherently inconsistent and lack the reproducibility required for systematic comparison, performance benchmarking, and iterative improvement of these systems. This limitation poses challenges in ensuring high-quality outcomes and fostering continuous advancements in survey generation technologies. Consequently, establishing a standardized framework for the evaluation of these systems is essential to assess their quality and reliability, while also enabling researchers to conduct comparative analyses. To address this necessity, we developed SurveyEval, a comprehensive tool designed to evaluate system-generated surveys across three critical dimensions: overall quality, structural coherence of the outline, and accuracy of references. The tool was applied across seven distinct academic disciplines and incorporated enhancements to the LLM-as-a-Judge approach by utilizing human-authored surveys as benchmarks to align automated evaluations more closely with human judgment.

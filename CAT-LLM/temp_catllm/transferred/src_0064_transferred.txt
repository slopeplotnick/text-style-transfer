The impact of the selected training images on the outcomes was assessed by examining the similarity of the generated images before and after retraining. However, solely comparing these images does not fully elucidate the reasons behind the varying impact of certain pictures. Consequently, a graph-based method was employed to analyze the structure and commonalities of the training images. Specifically, the objective was to determine whether the selected images formed distinct, meaningful clusters. Table 3 presents statistical data derived from ten tests conducted, each comprising fifteen prompts, to evaluate the consistency and grouping of the images. These findings corroborated the efficacy of the image selection method and provided insights into the significance of these images in the process. For instance, the average number of different values per relationship ranged from 3.9 to 5.1, indicating a moderate level of diversity appropriate for domains such as fashion, which is characterized by a finite number of styles or patterns. The images exhibited sufficient similarity to form distinct groups while maintaining enough variation to avoid redundancy. This suggests that the influence arises from shared concepts rather than mere replication. Furthermore, the analysis included the examination of shared attributes among the images. On average, between 5 and 7.1 attributes were shared, with certain groups sharing up to 13 attributes. This indicates that the images were not only detailed but also aligned with each other in significant ways. Prompts with more specific details resulted in a higher number of shared attributes, whereas more general or creative prompts led to greater diversity. Therefore, the system demonstrated adaptability to various types of prompts while still identifying well-matched examples.

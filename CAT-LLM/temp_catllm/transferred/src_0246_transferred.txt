Evaluating the power consumption of large language models (LLMs) diverges significantly from assessing their operational speed or execution duration. Power utilization is contingent upon various factors including distinct hardware configurations, intricate software architectures, and the operational context, which varies considerably based on the deployment environment. Existing methodologies for LLM evaluation exhibit limitations in accommodating these dynamic variables, as they typically adhere to predetermined setups, fixed model dimensions, and expensive analytical tools. However, as illustrated in Figure 1, TokenPowerBench addresses these challenges through a three-component framework encompassing setup, execution and measurement, and reporting. This framework presents a flexible and replicable approach for contemporary assessments of LLM power consumption. Initially, TokenPowerBench facilitates users in preparing the evaluation by selecting the model, the execution engine, and the associated prompts. To streamline this process, TokenPowerBench offers three interchangeable components: a model pool, a prompt-dataset menu, and an inference-engine selector. Specifically, the model pool comprises a diverse array of LLMs, facilitating analysis of how design choices impact power consumption. This includes traditional transformers, such as the LLaMA series, and Mixture-of-Experts models, like Mixtral, which utilize computational resources distinctively, particularly regarding parameter activation and memory usage. The evaluation encompasses a wide range of model sizes, from compact models with fewer than one billion parameters operable on standard GPUs to extensive models like Llama 3-405B necessitating multiple GPUs or nodes, thereby elucidating the evolution of power consumption as models and hardware scale. Furthermore, the prompt-dataset menu reveals the influence of prompt length and style on power utilization during inference. TokenPowerBench supports two primary datasets for this purpose: Alpaca, characterized by concise chat prompts, and Long-Bench, offering extended contexts up to 10,000 tokens. Users also have the option to incorporate their own data in CSV or alternative formats, thereby enhancing the adaptability of the evaluation process.

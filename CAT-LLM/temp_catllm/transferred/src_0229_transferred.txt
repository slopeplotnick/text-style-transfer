The methodology for constructing knowledge graphs (KGs) from images leverages artificial intelligence models capable of processing both textual and visual data, guided by topic-specific rules, known as an ontology. This ontology delineates the essential entities and relationships of interest, which are incorporated into the model's directives to ensure focused attention on pertinent details within the image. Upon receiving an image, the AI generates an enumeration of key entities, their attributes, and interconnections, adhering to the prescribed rules. Subsequently, these enumerations are converted into a graphical representation elucidating the image's content, with the graph's configuration being entirely contingent upon the established rules. Distinct graphs are created for each image and stored in a database, although certain graph components remain consistent due to the ontology's reliance on a fixed lexicon, thus facilitating the integration of individual graphs into a comprehensive graph. This overarching graph enables the identification of similarities across images through the comparison of their constituent graphs. The extraction of salient details from images employs AI, converting them into structured lists reflecting the thematic essence of the images, with topic-specific rules ensuring uniform description across images. The incorporation of these rules into AI instructions during analysis enables the creation of distinct graphs for each image, which are subsequently amalgamated into a singular, extensive graph. The linkage of individual image graphs via shared attributes facilitates comparative analysis within the image collection.

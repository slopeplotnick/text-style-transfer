The performance of ADORE was evaluated through both offline and online methodologies to determine its efficacy across varied contexts. In the offline evaluation, the model was assessed using a binary classification approach, employing precision, recall, and F1 score as metrics to gauge its effectiveness. Conversely, the online evaluation involved testing the model within the domain of search advertisements, focusing on metrics such as click-through rates, revenue generation, and expert assessments of advertisement quality. Comparative analyses were conducted between ADORE and other prominent models, including BERT, LLAMA-3-8B, and their respective variations. For the BERT models, two configurations were examined: a two-part architecture and a cross-encoder architecture. Within the cross-encoder BERT category, comparisons were made across three variants: the smaller model, a standard version, and an enhanced version incorporating supplementary information. The evaluation of the large language model (LLM) involved analyzing its decision-making performance using the final token as a basis. Subsequently, the proposed ADORE model, which shares similarities with BERT-Mini, was introduced, integrating online data, synthetically generated data, and additional contextual information. The results, presented in Table 2, demonstrate that ADORE outperforms all other models when applied to a substantial real-world dataset. Several observations were noted: first, the cross-encoder BERT architecture achieves superior performance compared to the two-part BERT architecture, with larger model sizes further enhancing results; additionally, the integration of supplementary information significantly improves BERT’s performance, underscoring the importance of clear and explicit information. Second, the use of an LLM for decision-making was found to be less effective than BERT. Furthermore, leveraging the LLM within the rules-driven (RD) component to generate challenging examples substantially improves outcomes. The inclusion of synthetic data derived from the data synthesis (DS) component enhances F1 scores, while incorporating explicit information through the knowledge distillation (KD) component positively impacts the model’s reasoning and overall performance.

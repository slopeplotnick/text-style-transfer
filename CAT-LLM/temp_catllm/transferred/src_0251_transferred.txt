The construction of narratives can commence with an event, subsequently determining the characteristics and development of entities involved, or conversely, initiate with an entity, crafting a series of events around it. E2, a doctoral candidate specializing in literature, posits that narratives can be deconstructed into constituent components such as outlines, sequences, ideas, and fundamental speech elements. When assembled in reverse order, these components can yield a complete narrative. Entities such as individuals, actions, or qualities constitute ideas, which can be aggregated into sequences, and a collection of sequences may culminate in a comprehensive narrative. However, the mere aggregation of these components does not inherently result in a compelling narrative; rather, the relationships that interlink these components are crucial for coherence and functionality. Interviews conducted with two experts have identified four principal components of narratives: entities, events, relationships, and outlines, which align with findings from other research studies. The discussions with experts revealed that while events constitute the core element of narratives, the specificities of these events may not always be readily apparent in the narrative delivery. Events have the capacity to exist independently within a narrative, yet the interactions between them engender narrative tension and meaning. Furthermore, the entities present in the narrative and the interconnections between them can exert influence over the events.

Machine learning and generative techniques are pervasive across various domains; however, the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in decision-making remains less prevalent, particularly within academic research, which typically does not involve the allocation of resources. Nevertheless, a notable implementation of decision-making AI has been observed in the context of mineral exploration, specifically in determining the high-grade section of the Mingomba deposit located in Zambia. An analysis of historical drilling data was conducted using advanced methodologies, leading to the identification of a more enriched segment than previously anticipated. The AI, configured as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), facilitated the sequential planning of drilling operations. The primary objective was to utilize AI to address uncertainties surrounding geological hypotheses regarding the localization of mineral-rich zones. This approach demonstrated superiority over conventional grid-based drilling methodologies, as it prioritizes refutation of geological hypotheses rather than merely targeting mineral discovery. In the forthcoming section, the acceleration of mineral deposit identification and comprehension through AI applications will be explored, beginning with data analysis and prediction, subsequently advancing to AI-driven decision-making processes. Initial adoption of AI elicited apprehension among some stakeholders, who expressed concerns regarding potential displacement of experts. This anxiety is prevalent in contemporary discourse, with fears of AI independently generating novel discoveries. Contrary to such perceptions, AI is perceived as an auxiliary instrument designed to relieve professionals of tasks that may be outside their expertise or unnecessary, thereby enabling them to devote their efforts to innovative geological and geochemical research.

The study discusses the SR-GRPO methodology, which employs stable rank as a reward signal within the reinforcement learning paradigm, thereby obviating the necessity for preference datasets or external validation mechanisms. It is demonstrated that SR-GRPO enhances reasoning across diverse evaluative tests, substantiating the proposition that geometric signals can serve as an effective foundation for aligning language models. Furthermore, the research establishes that stable rank functions as an efficient zero-shot reward proxy for assessing language model responses, seamlessly integrating into best-of-N decoding strategies. The utilization of stable rank (Rudelson & Vershynin, 2007) is proposed as an unsupervised quality signal derived from language model representations, with the metric being defined, its geometric utility elucidated, and its efficacy as a reward proxy proven. Theoretical motivation is derived from two key areas: the softmax bottleneck (Yang et al., 2018; Godey et al., 2024), which posits that high-rank representations are requisite for expressive language modeling, and representation collapse, a recognized issue associated with suboptimal generation (Gao et al., 2019). Stable rank quantifies this dimensionality, rendering it a suitable choice for quality assessment. This correlation is substantiated through empirical tests and correlation analysis presented in Section 5. The investigation evaluates stable rank as a zero-shot proxy for human preference by examining its capability to predict the preferred response in paired comparisons, thereby assessing its reliability as a reward signal independent of specific model training. The experimental setup employs RewardBench (Lambert et al., 2025), a benchmark comprising 2,985 preference pairs distributed across five categories: Chat, Chat-Hard, Safety, Code, and Math. For each pair, stable rank is calculated for both responses, with the response exhibiting the higher stable rank being predicted as preferred. Comparisons are drawn against three baseline methods: Pointwise Scoring (Point.) (Kim et al., 2024), which involves soliciting the language model to score each response on a scale from 1 to 5; Pairwise Comparison (Pair.) (Kim et al., 2024), wherein the language model is prompted to directly compare two responses; and IPO (Garg et al., 2025), which utilizes preference scores derived from Yes/No token probabilities. Five models were evaluated: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Yang et al., 2024), Qwen3-0.6B (Yang et al., 2025), Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Dubey et al., 2024), and Phi-3.5-mini-Instruct (Abdin et al., 2024). For stable rank assessment, inputs were formatted as prompt-response pairs according to each model's chat template, with final-layer outputs being obtained.

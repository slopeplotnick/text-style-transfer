As the utilization of artificial intelligence tools incorporating large language models becomes increasingly prevalent, ensuring their safety is essential. This necessity is particularly pronounced when such models are applied across diverse linguistic and cultural contexts, as these environments introduce unique risks. Consequently, it is imperative to develop systems capable of mitigating harmful or unsafe outputs across various languages and scenarios. In the absence of such systems, the global deployment of these models may inadvertently result in harm or exclusion for significant portions of the population. Existing research predominantly focuses on safety considerations within widely represented languages such as English, Chinese, Spanish, German, and French, whereas the majority of languages lack sufficient data to facilitate comprehensive safety evaluations. This scarcity of data presents challenges in assessing and maintaining safety in multilingual contexts. Although some testing mechanisms have been established to evaluate model performance across languages, these tools fail to adequately address underrepresented languages and do not reflect the diversity of user demographics. Furthermore, efforts to construct training datasets for low-resource languages to enhance safety measures remain limited. Recent advancements have utilized both authentic and translated data to create training sets for high-resource languages; however, these efforts are insufficient to achieve robust safety systems. An additional concern lies in the computational demands of existing safety systems, which are often characterized by excessive size, inefficiency, and resource intensity, rendering them unsuitable for deployment in low-power environments or on devices with constrained computational capabilities. Their prolonged processing times further hinder practical applicability in real-world scenarios. These challenges underscore the necessity for the development of smaller, more efficient systems designed to safeguard both the language model and its users from potential harm.

Artificial intelligence (AI) has increasingly become a fundamental tool in the domain of exploration, yet its application is often accompanied by notable challenges and common errors. This study aims to examine the current utilization of AI in exploration and to project potential advancements, particularly in decision-making processes. Exploration, fundamentally aimed at discovering new resources, occurs within two primary contexts: brownfield exploration, which focuses on areas near known deposits, and greenfield exploration, which targets entirely new regions. It is anticipated that over the next decade, the majority of discoveries will occur within brownfield areas rather than greenfield locations. The exploration process is inherently iterative, involving the progressive narrowing of search areas to identify regions where valuable deposits may exist. Initial investigations typically focus on large-scale geological features, such as large igneous provinces, which present favorable conditions for the emplacement and retention of economically significant mineral deposits. During the early stages, geologists rely on aerial data and mapping to identify anomalies indicative of potential subsurface resources. These anomalies are subsequently investigated through ground-based methods, including field observations, geophysical surveys, and soil sampling. The most resource-intensive phase of this process involves drilling, which is undertaken to validate hypotheses regarding the subsurface geology. Decision-making in exploration is ideally guided by sophisticated methodologies that optimize cost-efficiency through a comprehensive understanding of uncertainty and the value of information. Multiple data sources are integrated to construct models of mineral system formation, enabling predictions about potential deposit locations. Importantly, such data not only facilitate the identification of mineral resources but also allow for the testing and refinement of geological hypotheses. By reducing uncertainty regarding subsurface conditions, these data contribute to more informed decision-making processes. Furthermore, the objectives of exploration decisions are often dynamic and may evolve over the course of a project, underscoring the necessity for adaptive strategies informed by empirical evidence and robust analytical frameworks.

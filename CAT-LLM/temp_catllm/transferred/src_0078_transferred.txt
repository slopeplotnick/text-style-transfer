The execution of faulty code is contingent upon its inclusion in at least one failing test for the proposed method to be operational. In instances where a line of code remains unexecuted, its errors cannot be diagnosed effectively. Consequently, inadequate test coverage may result in overlooked issues, which we attempt to mitigate through the utilization of multiple failing tests coupled with swift static analysis. Concerning bounded model checking (BMC), our approach involves employing restricted program versions, where constraints such as loop and depth limits might exclude potential behaviors, thereby omitting valid issues due to predefined limits. Furthermore, when iterations are curtailed, issues identified within the constrained version may not be applicable to the complete program. These limits are meticulously set and disclosed alongside the results, albeit they inherently represent a limitation. The detection of pointers, arrays, and undefined behavior is facilitated by CFAULTS, as delineated in Section 5.1, employing tools such as CPPCHECK and CLANG-TIDY to identify uninitialized variables and runtime errors, including division by zero. Upon encountering these anomalies, CFAULTS halts execution and promptly informs the user. Additionally, CBMC's memory safety checks are leveraged to identify erroneous memory operations, such as out-of-bounds access or unsafe pointer arithmetic. Nonetheless, CFAULTS adheres to CBMC’s ANSI C specifications, treating any unflagged issues as acceptable, which may result in potential oversight. Efforts to enhance CBMC's alignment with intended C standards are forthcoming. Regarding external validity, evaluations were conducted on benchmark and student programs characterized by correct compilation and explicit specifications, such as assertions or anticipated outputs. Extending this approach to larger programs featuring extensive input/output operations, concurrency, intricate libraries, or more sophisticated specifications necessitates additional refinement and could potentially alter the outcomes presented herein.

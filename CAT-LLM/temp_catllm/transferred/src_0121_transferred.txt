The present study undertook a comparative analysis of authentic and synthetic datasets, revealing no substantial differences in average scores when assessed using one statistical test; however, an alternative test demonstrated notable disparities in score distribution. Specifically, while the synthetic data closely approximated the real data in terms of central tendency values, variations were observed in the dispersion of scores. Consequently, these findings provide partial support for the fourth hypothesis posited within this research. Comparative visualizations of the datasets are presented in Figure 2 to elucidate these differences. To minimize potential bias, a second study was conducted utilizing data from an independent source. This dataset, designed to evaluate individuals' self-perceived technological proficiency, comprises 25 items categorized into five distinct domains: communication, information management, content creation, safe usage, and problem-solving. This instrument was selected due to its robust validation and public accessibility. Additionally, the research protocol was pre-registered prior to generating synthetic data. Modifications to the synthetic data generation process were implemented in response to the absence of ethnicity-related information within the new dataset, incorporating only age and gender variables. The dataset also featured unique participant identifiers, enabling the matching of synthetic individuals to real counterparts based on age and gender. Repeated identifiers were excluded to ensure precise sample alignment.

The research necessitated the utilization of the AI2-THOR dataset as opposed to the previously employed TEACH dataset, thereby ensuring reproducibility of the study and equitable comparison of algorithms. Furthermore, this transition allowed for indirect performance comparison with analogous tasks. However, for the comparative analysis between LTAA and traditional MRTA methods, the identical dataset was employed. Moreover, the authors are conducting evaluations of SMART-LLM using the TEACH dataset to enhance the balance of the comparison. The study was conducted on a limited scale, facilitating thorough comparison of fundamental aspects and capability analysis. Nevertheless, real-world construction encompasses numerous tasks with intricate temporal and spatial complexities not fully addressed within the study. General descriptors such as elemental/compound and heavy/dexterous were utilized to characterize construction tasks in lieu of specific attributes, thereby generalizing the study, which was essential due to the absence of an extensive database of real-world construction operations. It is postulated by the authors that the existence of such a database would enable testing of LTAA across various scenarios with dynamic plans and resource challenges, potentially revealing the robustness of the framework. Additionally, the variability inherent in LLM outputs poses a safety concern within construction contexts. Despite multiple verifications to avert failures, the inherent randomness facilitates detailed reasoning but may result in divergent allocations. Construction sites demanding precision might require more stringent checks than currently implemented. In evaluating the computational resources required, an accurate measurement of SMART-LLM's costs per stage was unattainable due to the use of a singular combined prompt across all stages. Therefore, for comparative purposes with LTAA, which focuses solely on allocation, an estimation of SMART-LLM's costs was made by dividing the total tokens and time by three. Although this estimation is considered acceptable, it may not accurately reflect allocation costs, and future research incorporating detailed evaluations would be instrumental in providing a clearer understanding.

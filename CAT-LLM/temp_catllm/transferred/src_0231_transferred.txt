The method employed for evaluating outlines is predicated upon principles rather than comparisons with human examples, reflecting a departure from conventional practices. Given that outlines are inherently shorter and primarily indicative of structural elements, juxtaposing them against comprehensive surveys proves ineffective. Consequently, emphasis is placed upon assessing organizational coherence, logical progression, and clarity, thereby aligning the evaluation criteria with the quintessential attributes of a commendable outline, which predominantly adhere to academic conventions. Titles from original surveys are utilized to procure surveys from each respective system, ensuring uniform access to information via online retrieval capabilities. Three distinct categories of systems are subjected to examination: those designed for general long-text composition, those tailored for academic paper writing, and those specifically engineered for survey generation. Despite receiving identical inputs, these systems are evaluated on equivalent parameters. The scoring results, as delineated in the table, reveal disparate patterns across the three system types. Systems dedicated to general long-text writing exhibit proficiency in language and topical adherence; however, their surveys are marked by superficiality, originality deficits, inadequate idea synthesis, and limited analytical depth. Conversely, paper-writing systems exhibit enhanced structural competence, albeit their outputs often manifest rigidity and template adherence, consequently constraining depth and perspective diversity. Survey-making systems emerge as the most proficient overall, distinguished by superior organization, effective idea integration, and optimal delineation of facts and opinions, particularly within scientific and technological domains where precise information retrieval and organization are imperative. These discrepancies elucidate the necessity for specialized approaches transcending general linguistic models, underscoring the importance of literature utilization and idea organization in crafting exemplary surveys.

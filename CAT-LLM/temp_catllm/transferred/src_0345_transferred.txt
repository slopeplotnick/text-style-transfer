Various systems have been developed for handling memory in financial tasks, each exhibiting distinct characteristics and operational methodologies. FinMem is a system designed to utilize short, mid, and long-term memory in facilitating trading decisions. In contrast, MemP represents a framework that transforms trajectories into step-by-step instructions and comprehensive strategies through procedural memory. MemGPT operates akin to an operating system, managing storage and interruptions to expand the context window for large language models. Furthermore, Zep employs temporal knowledge graphs to establish connections between past relationships and evolving contexts. To evaluate the efficacy of these systems, the metrics of accuracy (ACC) and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) are utilized. ACC serves to ascertain the overall correctness of predictions, whereas MCC is particularly advantageous for balanced datasets, especially in stock prediction scenarios where class distributions may be uneven. Our empirical evaluation, conducted on four stocks, demonstrates that our approach outperforms all others in terms of both ACC and MCC, thereby indicating the robustness of our system. The tests further revealed deficiencies in the competing systems. Specifically, FinMem fails to model events over time, thereby overlooking significant market fluctuations. Similarly, MemP excessively simplifies trajectories, resulting in the loss of crucial financial details. MemGPT, lacking a focus on events, fails to identify key financial patterns. Moreover, Zep inadequately connects events, leading to the omission of pivotal relationships within financial data. These shortcomings are addressed by our system, which transforms unstructured news into structured event knowledge, effectively capturing the interconnections among events and facilitating enhanced traceability and reasoning through data, thus contributing to improved financial predictions.

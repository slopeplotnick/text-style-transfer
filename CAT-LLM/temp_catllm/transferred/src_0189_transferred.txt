The decision was made to omit the KL divergence regularization term, given that the model, after an effective initial phase, demonstrates an inherent capability to reason and format its output. The application of KL constraints would constrain the model's exploration of novel problem-solving methods; therefore, it is permitted to optimize freely based on existing knowledge. Our reward system is designed to achieve three objectives: Firstly, format standardization is achieved by employing special tokens to distinctly separate the reasoning process from the final answer, thereby facilitating subsequent understanding and utilization. Secondly, the output space is constrained by strictly managing the types of mismatches, such as "price mismatch" or "functionality mismatch," limiting the model from generating arbitrary outputs. Thirdly, a progressive reward system is implemented wherein the model receives positive feedback even when minor errors occur, provided the relevance is correctly addressed, thereby accelerating and stabilizing the learning process. A three-step learning plan is formulated to incrementally increase the complexity of the training data over time. Table 6 delineates the distribution of samples for each step. The strategy is informed by three principles: (1) The Rapid Bootstrap Principle wherein Step 1 predominantly utilizes medium-difficulty samples to furnish the model with immediate and applicable feedback while maintaining a challenging environment, thus circumventing early training issues; (2) The Progressive Challenge Principle, which involves the gradual introduction of more challenging samples in Steps 2 and 3, thereby transitioning the model from addressing common issues to tackling complex cases, thus broadening its competencies; (3) The Knowledge Retention Principle, where the final two steps persist in utilizing simple samples as "memory anchors" to prevent the model from losing prior skills while acquiring new ones, contributing to training stability.

Several methodologies for detecting software bugs, such as SNIPER, often yield an excessive number of superfluous results, whereas alternatives like BugAssIST fail to provide the minimal set of corrective actions required to resolve the issue. Recently, Graussam proposed a novel approach for identifying bugs in ANSI C programs through the application of a specific fault model that accommodates complex features, including pointers and arrays. The approach leverages CBMC encodings, thereby enabling the relaxation process to occur at the CBMC level rather than directly within the code, which contrasts with our methodology. This technique is capable of addressing multiple test failures by generating standard Model-Based Diagnosis (MBD) instances and resolving them with Hierarchical Set Diagnosis (HSD) to ensure that the corrections remain minimal for each failed test. However, Graussam’s method introduces an additional step subsequent to listing the fixes, which potentially compromises the minimality of the resultant solutions. Our evaluation of this tool was not conducted for two primary reasons: initially, it necessitates manual annotations for the functions under examination, a requirement that is impractical for our purposes given the extensive scope of our work involving over 1500 programs; furthermore, it is limited to handling integers and floats in C, rendering it incompatible with the C-PACK-IPAs benchmark. Model-Based Diagnosis has demonstrated efficacy in other domains, including the rectification of faults in Boolean circuits, C software, knowledge bases, and spreadsheets. An alternative strategy known as program slicing has also been employed to identify bugs in software. One method utilizes program slicing to enumerate all minimal sets of fixes for a defective program, while another approach examines the variances between distinct software versions to ascertain the root cause of the issue. This paper introduces a systematic formula-based methodology for detecting bugs in C programs and Boolean circuits, capable of managing an arbitrary number of issues. It applies Model-Based Diagnosis with multiple test failures to consolidate all failed tests into a single MaxSAT formula, thereby ensuring consistency and eliminating extraneous results.

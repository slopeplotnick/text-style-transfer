The present study examines the efficacy of utilizing a verifier to enhance the performance of solvers by selecting correct solutions, with the aim of validating the hypothesis through empirical experimentation. A selection of solver-verifier combinations was employed from a cohort of 12 models, specifically focusing on the three smallest models within each of four distinct groups. For each problem presented, the solver persisted in attempts until such time as the verifier confirmed the accuracy of a response, limited to a maximum of nine attempts. In instances where a correct solution was not identified, the final attempt was retained as the outcome. The findings, juxtaposed with anticipated enhancements, are depicted in Figure 4. It is noteworthy that minor improvements are occasionally obscured, and the restriction of nine attempts may impede comprehensive analysis; however, the overall results are consistent with initial expectations. A salient observation is that self-verification is less effective compared to cross-group verification, underscoring the importance of external review. The principal conclusion drawn is that the predictive method for improvements is efficient and requires minimal additional effort for evaluation. A prevalent bias exists wherein individuals are inclined to favor answers resembling their own, a phenomenon corroborated by this study's results in the context of solvers and verifiers. As previously demonstrated, models proficient in reasoning exhibit limited benefit from self-verification but show enhanced performance when subjected to verification by diverse models, thereby highlighting a similar bias. The critical insight is that solvers and verifiers with aligned cognitive processes are prone to erroneously accept incorrect solutions, thereby diminishing potential improvements. Conversely, employing a verifier with disparate reasoning mitigates this issue effectively.

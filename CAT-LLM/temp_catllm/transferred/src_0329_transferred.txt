Rapid advancements in large language models are being observed, with novel applications continually emerging to harness their capabilities for beneficial purposes. As these models increasingly integrate into online communication platforms, their potential to enhance interpersonal understanding and facilitate improved dialogue becomes apparent. This research investigates the capacity of these models to transcend mere identification of harmful dialogue elements, examining their ability to de-escalate conflict and foster resolution in online interactions. The conceptual framework comprises two primary components: initially, the model assesses conversational equity and emotional dynamics, followed by the generation of conciliatory messages to promote resolution and cooperation. To evaluate the efficacy of these models in achieving such outcomes, a comprehensive dataset was curated from Reddit, accompanied by a testing protocol incorporating rule-based scoring, user simulations, and comparison with human responses. The findings reveal that commercial models exhibit superior performance over open-access alternatives in reasoning and conflict resolution. While the study underscores the potential of these models in facilitating online harmony, it also delineates their limitations. The transformative impact of large language models on communication, learning, and collaboration is notable, extending beyond traditional functions such as language translation and text summarization to fields including education, healthcare, and mental health. Their deployment in contentious online environments introduces an innovative approach, aiming not only to automate routine tasks but to foster civility and equitable discourse. A prevalent challenge in these digital spaces is the escalation of hostile exchanges, exacerbated by the absence of visual and social cues, which often results in intensified personal attacks.

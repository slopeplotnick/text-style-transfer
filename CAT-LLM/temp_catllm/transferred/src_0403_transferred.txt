The Self-Taught Multimodal Reasoner (STL) represents a system designed to enhance model performance through the utilization of self-generated explanations, thereby advancing understanding and learning capabilities. A significant inquiry pertains to the optimal structuring of these explanations. In order to effectively address queries related to visual stimuli, the model necessitates precise observation and cognitive processing. STL facilitates this by employing a methodology wherein the model initially articulates the contents of an image, subsequently engages in reflective analysis, and ultimately provides a response. This approach mirrors human cognitive processes, aiding the model in organizing its thoughts more effectively. Sole reliance on explanations derived from correct answers proves inadequate, as it restricts the model’s exposure to optimal reasoning strategies alone, posing challenges in tasks requiring the discernment of correct versus incorrect reasoning. To ameliorate this limitation, explanations for incorrect answers are incorporated, akin to human learning which involves evaluating both successful and erroneous outcomes. Such explanations elucidate the reasoning errors, enabling the model to distinguish between valid and invalid responses, thereby mitigating errors such as conjecture or assumption without evidence. This concept is applied within STL, facilitating a learning environment where the model develops both accurate and inaccurate explanations, thereby fostering improvement through training on these diverse explanations.

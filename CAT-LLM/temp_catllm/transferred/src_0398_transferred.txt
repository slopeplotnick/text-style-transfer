The primary skills intended for development encompass the ability to ascertain three-dimensional structure without explicit instruction, control camera perspectives in a manner congruent with the scene, comprehend spatial arrangements, plan to achieve objectives, and maintain temporal consistency. These competencies are evaluated through two specific tasks: navigating a scene based on video directives and identifying objects based on textual directives. For scene navigation, instructions related to camera movement are employed to produce a video congruent with both the provided directives and the scene configuration. In object identification, text instructions guide the model to locate and display a particular object, thereby assessing its comprehension of spatial arrangements and planning efficacy. These tasks facilitate the evaluation of VIDEO4SPATIAL’s capability to discern and interact with scene intricacies solely via video input. In light of the absence of expansive public datasets tailored for such tasks, indoor scanning datasets like ScanNet++ and ARKitScenes are utilized, offering extended video footage of various rooms and enabling the creation of context-target video clip pairs. A vision-language model is employed to select clips concluding with a focal object and to generate instructions for object identification. Camera movement data is transformed into directives for scene traversal. Empirical tests indicate that this framework adeptly manages complex tasks, acquiring understanding of scene layout and semantics from video alone and performing proficiently in object identification and scene navigation. Despite training with shorter videos, the model demonstrates robust performance with longer sequences during testing, enhancing its applicability. Furthermore, although trained primarily on indoor scenes, the model exhibits versatility in outdoor environments and novel objects. In conclusion, VIDEO4SPATIAL represents an effective video-based framework for visuospatial tasks, emphasizing critical design decisions such as integrating context guidance and instructions, utilizing bounding boxes to enhance accuracy, and sampling context to optimize comprehension efficiency. Additionally, two spatial tasks were developed and assessed.

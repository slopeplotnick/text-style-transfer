Recent studies, including those conducted by researchers [25] and [15], have explored methodologies to assess whether artificial intelligence (AI) models exhibit moral reasoning comparable to human cognition, utilizing frameworks such as MoralBench. These investigations have demonstrated that while AI models occasionally exhibit an understanding of moral principles, they frequently encounter challenges when confronted with complex scenarios or conflicting moral paradigms. However, these assessments predominantly capture the moral reasoning of AI at a specific point in time, thereby failing to account for potential temporal evolution or the impact of additional training. Complementary research has focused on enhancing AI's ability to explain its decisions and align with human values, a critical aspect of fostering responsible AI development. For example, Shitong et al. [9] introduced DeNEVIL, a model designed to facilitate bias-free moral reasoning in AI systems, while Jiao et al. [16] emphasized the importance of cross-cultural evaluations of AI ethics and the establishment of trust, highlighting the interdependence of technological frameworks and societal factors in shaping ethical AI. Although these approaches contribute to improved transparency and trust, their reliance on predefined moral categories and manual labeling constrains their adaptability to dynamic moral contexts. While prior research has advanced fairness evaluations and value alignment, a significant gap persists in developing a flexible mechanism for longitudinal moral evaluation that remains independent of specific AI models. Current methodologies predominantly assess ethical considerations at discrete intervals yet lack the capacity to autonomously generate, interpret, and adapt moral scenarios. Furthermore, existing frameworks fail to provide tools for quantifying disparities in AI ethics or monitoring moral consistency across models in real-time. Drawing upon insights presented in Section 1, the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP) has been conceptualized as a system aimed at continuously evaluating AI moral reasoning without reliance on predefined datasets, thereby facilitating the measurement, comprehension, and enhancement of moral stability in large language models. Designed to prioritize evaluation and understanding rather than model improvement or retraining, MoCoP seeks to maintain clarity and consistency in examining the ethical dimensions of AI systems.

The discourse surrounding the perception of music composed by artificial intelligence (AI), irrespective of the anonymity of the composer, reflects a spectrum of concerns that parallel those prevalent in the domains of music and technology research. Specifically, these concerns encompass potential economic repercussions, ownership dilemmas, and cultural impacts. Nevertheless, there exists a palpable curiosity regarding the technological advances and operational mechanisms underpinning AI music composition. Such a confluence of apprehension and intrigue potentially elucidates the diverse opinions on AI composers. As advancements in AI technology persist, phenomena initially perceived as innovative might subsequently be construed as problematic or even threatening. Therefore, perceptions of AI-generated music are subject to temporal evolution. The study encountered several methodological constraints: primarily, the cohort consisted of university students and individuals sourced from the Prolific platform, who engaged with the music through disparate modalities, complicating the generalizability of the findings. Furthermore, participants from Prolific may possess heightened familiarity with technology and AI. Additionally, it was disclosed to one subset that the music resembled film soundtracks, potentially biasing their appreciation due to the favorable reception typically accorded to cinematic compositions. Moreover, the study exclusively utilized instrumental music, thereby limiting the applicability of results to lyrical compositions. Extant literature indicates that lyrics exacerbate the emotional impact of melancholic music yet exert minimal influence on the perception of joyful compositions. Given the predominantly upbeat nature of the music in our study, the inclusion of lyrics would likely not have significantly altered participant responses. The decision to exclude lyrics was informed by the objective of circumventing potential confounding variables and the unavailability of high-quality AI-generated vocal elements at the time of sample creation.

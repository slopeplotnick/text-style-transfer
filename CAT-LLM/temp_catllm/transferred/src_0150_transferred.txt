In the evaluation of advanced models such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o, it is observed that these models exhibit minimal errors, performing comparably to or surpassing human capabilities, as illustrated on the left side of Figure 8. Furthermore, with the exception of Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, Figure 8 elucidates that models with heightened accuracy tend to emulate Bayesian models, although this correlation between accuracy and Bayesian-like behavior is not absolute. For instance, a model with flawless predictive capabilities may resemble a simplistic model devoid of probabilistic characteristics. When contextual parameters are modified through the provision of varied historical observations, substantial alterations in the behavior of large language models (LLMs) are evident. Specifically, as depicted in the top right of Figure 6, their sequential behavior markedly diminishes, particularly when introduced to a biased range, as evidenced on the left of Figure 6, where error rates escalate, indicated by the predominance of triangles over circles. This observation underscores the reliance of LLMs on the information presented to them. Additionally, from the central portion of Figure 8, it becomes apparent that not all models demonstrating proficiency in terms of normalized root mean square error (NRMSE) concurrently exhibit efficient cue integration. GPT-5 Mini, despite its commendable performance in NRMSE, inadequately integrates cues, as evidenced in the maze distance task, where it excels with textual input but underperforms with visual data. Consequently, it is imperative that the significance of image input is further diminished, although current capabilities fall short in achieving this reduction, as detailed in Appendix A.12. However, in the line length ratio task, as observed in the bottom right of Figure 6, numerous LLMs effectively attenuate the impact of images amidst noise, thereby demonstrating adaptability in a Bayesian manner.

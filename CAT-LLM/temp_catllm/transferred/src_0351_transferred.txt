The present study investigates the method known as TACO, which serves to adjust Variable Length Algorithms (VLAs) during testing phases. This approach retains the robust generalization capabilities of previously trained VLAs while ensuring the outputs align with the specific tasks intended for execution. A novel technique for estimating pseudo-counts within the Contextual Feedback Network (CFN) has been proposed, allowing for the measurement of distributional changes with minimal computational resources. Consequently, TACO facilitates the success of various VLA models across both simulated and real-world tasks, eliminating the necessity for extensive training periods and enabling rapid functionality. In the context of online Reinforcement Learning (RL), agents actively interact with their environments to explore diverse actions; however, offline RL is characterized by learning from pre-existing datasets without further environmental interaction. This presents challenges due to the discrepancy between the policy that generated the dataset and the policy under development. Specifically, the offline dataset reflects the distribution of the originating policy, which may substantially differ from the target policy, potentially leading to significant estimation errors for actions outside this distribution. During the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage of VLAs, the model encompasses numerous action modes, yet successful actions pertinent to specific tasks typically occur within a narrower domain. Consequently, when sampling actions, the policy might not select the optimal ones. In response, TACO employs an anti-exploration strategy to maintain the policy proximity to the successful data distribution. Unlike conventional offline RL methods that implement penalties based on uncertainty, our approach utilizes a straightforward count-based methodology, which integrates seamlessly with VLA models and obviates the need for value estimation. Count-based regularization has been previously applied in both classical RL frameworks and contemporary RLHF algorithms.

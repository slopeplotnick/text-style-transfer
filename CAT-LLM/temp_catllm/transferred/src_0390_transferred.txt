The utilization of language models in evaluative processes frequently benefits from the implementation of a methodical reasoning approach prior to arriving at a conclusive answer. This technique, referred to as Chain-of-Thought prompting, serves to elucidate the reasoning behind decisions rendered by the model, thereby enhancing the accuracy and comprehensibility of its judgments. Our research investigates these cognitive steps and examines optimal strategies for amalgamating the outcomes. In decision-making contexts, various methodologies exist for harnessing the computational resources available at any given moment. Particularly, when multiple samples are present, a mechanism for their integration becomes imperative. One such mechanism is Self-Consistency, which amalgamates disparate outputs through majority voting. Variations of this approach incorporate confidence levels, such as Soft Self-Consistency, which selects outcomes based on confidence scores across different categories. Confidence-Informed Self-Consistency employs weighted voting predicated on confidence scores, determined either by sequence probability or model inquiry. Certain methodologies leverage the model itself for result integration. Generative Self-Aggregation solicits the model to formulate a novel response grounded on several samples. Universal Self-Consistency engages the model in identifying the most coherent answer from multiple alternatives. Moreover, empirical evidence indicates that the application of computational power during testing, particularly through majority voting, significantly enhances the performance of reasoning-based generative verifiers.

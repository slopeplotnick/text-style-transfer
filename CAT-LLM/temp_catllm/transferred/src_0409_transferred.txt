The primary inquiry pertains to whether contemporary language models (LLMs), trained solely to predict subsequent words without explicit instruction in understanding perceptual elements, inherently develop analogous methodologies for information processing. The elucidation of how these models manage uncertain information may facilitate the construction of superior systems capable of coping with varying input quality. To explore this hypothesis, psychophysical methods, a discipline examining human perception, were employed to analyze model operations. The models were treated as black boxes, wherein their behavior was scrutinized externally to infer processing mechanisms. By modulating input uncertainty and assessing indications of Bayesian processing—a probabilistic decision-making approach—it is possible to determine if these models emulate human perceptual behavior despite lacking specific training. It was observed that certain classical tasks in psychophysics effectively interact with these models, revealing intriguing patterns. These tasks serve as robust assessments of the models' proficiency in handling diverse input types, especially within controlled environments that facilitate precise measurement. Unlike real-world scenarios replete with excessive noise, this experimental setup permits the examination of specific strategies and identification of vulnerabilities otherwise obscured in conventional benchmarks. Three principal contributions were realized: Firstly, a psychophysics framework for LLMs was established, encompassing four tasks that assess length, location, distance, and duration, thereby enabling manipulation of noise and context to observe behavioral modifications. Secondly, a novel benchmark, BayesBench, was developed to evaluate model performance, efficiency in information integration, and adherence to Bayesian reasoning through an innovative scoring system. Thirdly, it was discovered that while certain advanced models exhibit Bayesian-consistent behavior, a significant limitation persists where highly accurate models fail to employ reliable strategies in specific contexts. Psychophysics, recognized for elucidating consistent patterns in the relationship between physical stimuli and perception, is underpinned by established laws such as Weber-Fechner’s, among others.

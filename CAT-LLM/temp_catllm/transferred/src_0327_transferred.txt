Following the training phase, a rigorous evaluation test was established to assess the model's efficacy, as outlined in Section 3.5. Given the substantial capabilities of large language models (LLMs) and the extensive variability inherent in e-commerce data, the evaluation encompassed a broad spectrum of examples, including challenging instances derived from the core competencies discussed in Section 2. The preparatory process was structured into three essential steps: feature construction, model selection, and prompt refinement (refer to Fig 6). In accordance with the principles delineated in Section 2, the development of effective relevance models necessitates a comprehensive mapping of item attributes. Consequently, features were selected to facilitate the model's comprehension and reasoning capabilities through a combination of visual and textual information. Initially, for visual attributes such as style, cuts, and patterns, the primary product image was utilized. This approach is particularly critical for search scenarios where textual information alone proves insufficient. To augment item detail, category-property-value (CPV) triples and Stock-keeping-unit (SKU) data were incorporated. Given that CPV data may contain extraneous information irrelevant to relevance assessment, a method integrating prior knowledge with LLMs was employed to refine this data. CPV data is represented as key-value pairs, with similar products frequently sharing identical keys; for example, both "eye cream" and "face cream" typically possess the key "brand". Extensive product data was analyzed to identify frequently occurring keys. Subsequently, the advanced LLM, identified as Qwen3-235B-Instruct, was tasked with evaluating whether each attribute contributes to relevance, utilizing the task definition, attributes, and corresponding keys. To ensure the accuracy of these evaluations, the process was conducted eight times, retaining only those attributes consistently deemed relevant by the model.

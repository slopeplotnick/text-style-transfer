Radiology reporting constitutes a critical component in the assessment of medical imaging, often necessitating substantial time investment and being prone to errors, particularly in the examination of complex three-dimensional modalities such as computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Ensuring the quality of radiology reports remains a fundamental aspect of clinical practice; however, this process is inherently labor-intensive and entails meticulous verification of both content and linguistic accuracy. While existing automated methodologies predominantly emphasize the generation of reports, they frequently neglect comprehensive quality control measures, thereby limiting their capacity to provide holistic support to radiologists. To address these challenges, the proposed Radiologist Copilot represents an innovative artificial intelligence (AI)-based assistant designed to integrate automated report generation with rigorous quality assurance processes. This system leverages large language models to emulate human decision-making, enabling the identification of specific regions within medical images, the planning and execution of analytical tasks, the selection of appropriate report templates, and the enhancement of report quality through iterative feedback mechanisms. Consequently, Radiologist Copilot facilitates the production of accurate and complete radiology reports at an accelerated pace, thereby optimizing efficiency and supporting radiologists in their workflow. Empirical evaluations further reveal that Radiologist Copilot demonstrates superior performance in comparison to other leading methodologies in radiology report generation. Upon approval, the underlying code for the system will be made publicly accessible. The necessity for radiologists to meticulously analyze medical images and compile detailed reports encompassing key findings underscores the importance of automation in this domain. Given the potential for delays and errors inherent to manual reporting, particularly for 3D imaging, and the tedious nature of quality assurance procedures, the integration of automated systems capable of addressing both report generation and quality control emerges as a critical advancement to enhance operational efficiency and support clinical practice.

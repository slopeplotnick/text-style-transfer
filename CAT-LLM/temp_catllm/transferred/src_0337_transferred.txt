The present study addresses a task wherein certain elements, specifically L1 and L2, are deemed less significant, while others, namely L3 and L4, are considered critical. To evaluate performance in this context, the metric acc@2 is employed, which assesses the accuracy of predictions by determining whether classifications are correct or incorrect. Additionally, the macro F1 score is utilized to account for imbalances in data label distributions, ensuring a balanced evaluation across categories. Two distinct model types were compared in this investigation: large language models, encompassing both open-source and closed-source variants, and a baseline model referred to as Vanilla SFT. The large language models were tested using task-specific prompts designed to clarify the rules and objectives, while adhering to established best practices for optimization, including specific configuration parameters such as a temperature setting of 0.7 for GPT-4 and 0.6 for Llama-3. In contrast, the Vanilla SFT model was trained solely on labeled data without incorporating advanced techniques such as chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting or reinforcement learning (RL), thereby serving as a benchmark for evaluating the incremental benefits of these additional methodologies. Despite its simplicity, the Vanilla SFT model demonstrated commendable performance, achieving an acc@2 score of 0.929 and a macro F1 score of 0.722, surpassing more complex models such as GPT-4. Furthermore, the LORE model exhibited an additional improvement by increasing the acc@2 score by 0.4 points, highlighting its capability to enhance performance even when the baseline model already performs effectively with standard data.

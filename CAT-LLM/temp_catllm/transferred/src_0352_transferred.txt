The objective of this investigation is to examine the limitations associated with the recent methodological approach described in source [16]. Specifically, the initial phase of the model, as illustrated in Figure 1(a), demonstrates concurrent attention to both audio and visual inputs; however, as the process progresses, a shift in focus towards the text already generated is observed. Consequently, the model's reasoning becomes increasingly dependent on the emotional cues embedded in the initial text generated. The standard process initiates with a description of the video, adhering to the structure of the training data wherein visual data precedes audio data, followed by reasoning. Nevertheless, this "visual-first" paradigm may not always be appropriate, particularly when the emotional content is derived more significantly from auditory cues or semantic content. A misalignment of initial visual reasoning can lead to progressively erroneous reasoning, as depicted in Figure 1(b), thereby disproportionately elevating the importance of the initial step. Similar issues are prevalent in other domains where initial cues can skew reasoning in an incorrect direction [17]. To address this challenge, the introduction of Modality-Importance-Guided Reasoning (MIGR) is proposed. The proposed approach aims to align the reasoning process with the input modality (audio, visual, or both) that corresponds most closely with the emotion under examination, ensuring consistency throughout the reasoning process. To achieve this, Modality Importance (MI) is introduced, which evaluates the significance of each input type in emotion recognition by assessing model performance under varying input conditions: solely audio, solely visual, or both. For instance, should the model successfully predict emotion using audio alone but fail upon inclusion of visual data, the visual input may be deemed non-essential or potentially misleading, thereby prioritizing audio as the primary focus. Utilizing MI alongside contemporary training methodologies for reasoning-based models [15, 16], a dual-phase learning strategy is developed, comprising (1) modality-aligned Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and (2) modality-aligned reward optimization. During the SFT phase, MI is employed to segment the reasoning text according to input type and subsequently reorganize it.

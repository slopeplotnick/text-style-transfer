The experimental group employing the curriculum learning method demonstrated faster learning and more consistent improvement regarding rewards and model performance, as depicted in Figure 12. During reinforcement learning training, as the model advances towards its objective, its output becomes increasingly less random and more predictable, thereby hindering the model's ability to experiment with novel approaches and discover enhanced solutions. This phenomenon imposes a constraint on the model's potential for improvement. Consequently, various strategies were explored to decelerate the rate at which the model attains predictability, thereby enabling continued exploration. Figure 13 provides a comparative analysis of these strategies. It is revealed that the Clip-higher method is most effective, as it moderates the pace of predictability while still allowing its occurrence, thereby achieving an optimal balance between innovation and adherence to effective strategies, and ensuring training stability with conspicuous improvements. The On-policy method maintains the model's randomness consistently, nearly preventing its reduction. While this facilitates greater exploration, it simultaneously impedes the model's ability to converge on an optimal solution, resulting in relatively smaller improvements. The Off-policy + Entropy Loss method encounters challenges concerning training stability. Initially, it decelerates the rate at which predictability is achieved; however, as the model's randomness diminishes, the penalty associated with randomness becomes excessively pronounced, leading to unpredictable model behavior and compromising training success. Furthermore, this method necessitates extensive experimentation to ascertain appropriate configurations, thereby increasing its operational costs. Overall, the Clip-higher method emerges as the superior option, as it effectively manages the equilibrium between exploration and efficient learning by regulating the rate at which the model achieves predictability.

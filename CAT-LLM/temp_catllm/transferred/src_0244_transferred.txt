In the exploration of motion imitation methodologies, initiating a character in a random state derived from a motion dataset is advantageous; however, this necessitates access to the dataset for selecting initial states during training. To circumvent the dependency on the original dataset, a generative model is employed by SMP to create initial states for training purposes. Specifically, within the SMP framework, these initial states are generated from a pre-trained diffusion model serving as a motion guide. Consequently, SMP functions not only as a reward mechanism but also as a facilitator for initiating new training sessions. This generative state approach obviates the necessity of retaining the original dataset post-training, as the diffusion model effectively produces diverse and high-quality initial states. Our methodology integrates a trained diffusion model as a versatile motion guide conducive to learning imitation. The motion model is instrumental in assessing whether the agent's movements appear natural within the learning environment, thereby aiding in the development of control policies capable of executing various tasks while mimicking the motion characteristics of the original dataset. This paper elucidates pivotal decisions made in the learning setup, emphasizing the importance of constructing an effective motion representation for training the diffusion model to comprehend the dataset and subsequently utilize it as a guide for policy training. Such representation must encapsulate sufficient information to reconstruct motion and be readily extractable from motion clips and simulator observations. Drawing upon prior research in motion diffusion and AMP, the motion features we incorporate are designed to optimize the learning process.

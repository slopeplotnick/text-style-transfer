The examination of public perceptions surrounding AI-generated content, specifically music, remains underexplored, with current methodologies often utilizing rating scales from analogous domains, which may inadequately capture genuine attitudes towards AI-produced music. This research seeks to elucidate authentic public perceptions through the employment of open-ended inquiries. The music samples were generated using AI tools, namely Stable Audio and Suno, with the foundation of these samples predicated on the Emotion-to-Music Atlas, a comprehensive resource elucidating the emotional responses elicited by music. The Music4All-Onion database, encompassing music from five principal genres, each with three subgenres—Electronic (Trance, Downtempo, Dubstep), Jazz (Bossa-Nova, Smooth-Jazz, Swing), Pop (Europop, Indie-Pop, Pop-Punk), Rock (Soft-Rock, RockBallad, Doom-Metal), and Soul/Funk (Funk, Disco, Soul)—served as the data source. For each subgenre, prompts were devised based on predominant emotions identified in the music, utilizing average values from the Emotion-to-Music Atlas and their corresponding GEMS-9 descriptions, yielding 2-3 prompts per subgenre, culminating in a total of 38 prompts. These prompts were subsequently adapted for use with Suno and Stable Audio, resulting in 76 generated samples. In the case of Suno, prompts were configured in instrumental mode, with one of two outputs selected and truncated to 45-60 seconds to ensure consistency with previous studies. For Stable Audio, the term 'instrumental' was appended to each prompt, with a target duration of 60 seconds. Additional methodological details pertaining to the generation and editing of these samples are elucidated in the supplementary materials.

The comprehensive evaluation has demonstrated that spatial methods such as covariance tangent-space projection and Common Spatial Patterns remain optimal for decoding EEG motor imagery under controlled conditions. However, their efficacy diminishes significantly amidst substantial inter-subject and intra-subject variability. An in-depth analysis of individual subjects revealed the absence of a universal configuration that is effective for all, indicating a preference for adaptive methods tailored to individual brain patterns. Notably, certain nonlinear features, including Hjorth parameters, Higuchi fractal dimension, and singular value decomposition entropy, exhibited satisfactory performance for select individuals. Consequently, advancing the understanding of cerebral mechanisms can enhance personalized decoding, suggesting that "BCI illiteracy" may stem from a discrepancy between decoding algorithms and user-specific brain patterns rather than inherent user limitations. Future investigations should explore sophisticated learning approaches that integrate linear spatial filters with nonlinear dynamics, leveraging their combined strengths. It is imperative to expand this evaluation across additional paradigms such as SSVEP, P300, CVEP, and invasive intracortical recordings to ascertain the robustness of the findings. Furthermore, the exploration of novel deep-learning models, including CNNs, LSTMs, and Transformers, alongside emerging quantum-inspired models like quantum support-vector machines and hybrid quantum neural networks, is critical. These quantum methodologies may offer superior management of EEG signal noise and complexity. Another vital research avenue involves enhancing model performance across diverse subjects through transfer learning and domain adaptation, which have demonstrated potential in augmenting model robustness and scalability. Furthermore, reproducibility can be significantly improved via open-access benchmarking initiatives such as MOABB, facilitating standardized and transparent comparisons of EEG decoding algorithms.

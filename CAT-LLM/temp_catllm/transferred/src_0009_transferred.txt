The temporal alignment between source and edited videos is evaluated through four distinct measures: speech, involving analysis of mouth movements; gaze, entailing the monitoring of eye movements; blink, assessing the frequency of eye blinks; and pose, examining body movements. These metrics facilitate the examination of various aspects of motion, and they are employed for the selection and verification of data rather than for training the model. In order to ascertain the accuracy and consistency of edits throughout the video, two measures predicated on CLIP are utilized. The first measure, termed Directional CLIP Score, ensures that the alterations in each frame are congruent with the overarching edit direction. The measure is bifurcated into two types: one examines the concordance between the initial edited frame and the subsequent frames, while the other evaluates whether the modifications are consistent with the text prompt. The latter type is notably equitable for methods relying solely on text, such as LucyEdit. The former type assesses whether the visual modifications in the video align with those in the first frame, whereas the latter scrutinizes the congruence between the modifications and the textual description. The second measure, CLIP-Text Alignment Score, gauges the extent to which each frame corresponds with the text description, thereby ensuring the video's fidelity to the text's intended meaning over time. This measure synergistically operates with the Directional CLIP Score to determine the video's adherence to the textual narrative.

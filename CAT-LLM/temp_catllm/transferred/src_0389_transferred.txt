The development of new products incorporating innovative ideas tailored for specific business markets is facilitated through the adoption of Software Product Line (SPL) practices. These examples serve to encourage teams to utilize SPL effectively. The reduction of risks is crucial in mitigating resistance to change, and this article discusses strategies for achieving such reductions. It is imperative to address all concerns, whether directly related to these risks or not, as they can impede successful adoption efforts. Recurring challenges in SPL adoption, although significant, are surmountable when changes are managed with careful consideration of these issues. The perception of complexity often stems from a misunderstanding of Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE); however, it is acknowledged that SPL can present substantial challenges even to individuals well-versed in SPLE. While complexity is inherent to SPL, it should not exceed the familiarity and operational scope of the IT team’s existing codebase. Within SPL, the concept of variability is explicitly represented through modeling techniques designed to enhance comprehension and manage complexity effectively. Training and demonstrative efforts are posited as essential components in addressing these perceptions. The time-saving potential of SPL, particularly in the addition of new features and enabling creative development, has been emphasized to counter concerns about diminished innovation and development opportunities expressed by user experience (UX) designers. Moreover, thorough non-regression testing is proposed as a means to mitigate unintended side effects. Concerns and apprehensions can be alleviated by engaging with team members, addressing weaknesses and strengths, and fostering open communication. These concerns are frequently perceived as biases by individuals interviewed during the study. Misconceptions regarding SPL as an overly complex and impractical system can be addressed through transparent processes, comprehensive explanations, detailed documentation, and inclusive practices that involve all stakeholders. Building trust necessitates providing opportunities for individuals to engage with SPL openly, thereby facilitating understanding. Encouraging participation begins with inviting team members to actively contribute to the change process, including regular reporting at various stages, which fosters a sense of inclusion, ensures concerns are acknowledged, and provides necessary support throughout the implementation process.

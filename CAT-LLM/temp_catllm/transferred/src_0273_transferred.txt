The CAPO framework is conceived to utilize a dynamic signal referred to as advantage, which corresponds to the progressive enhancement observed in model performance over time. Historical approaches to curriculum learning, initiated by Bengio et al. in 2009, predominantly employed static rules, such as arranging tasks from less complex to more challenging. Subsequent methodologies endeavored to introduce adaptability by incorporating elements such as expert feedback or performance indicators, yet these remained externally imposed and were not inherently linked to the model's improvement trajectory. Therefore, CAPO was developed to employ advantage estimates as an intrinsic mechanism guiding the scheduling of tasks for the model. Empirical evaluations indicate that CAPO facilitates enhanced problem-solving capabilities in models, particularly in mathematical domains, across various advantage-based reinforcement learning techniques, including GRPO, PPO, RLOO, and Reinforce++. Furthermore, its efficacy extends to tasks involving graphical user interfaces (GUIs), demonstrating its capacity to manage diverse reasoning tasks. Specifically, the core propositions entail the utilization of advantage as a dynamic signal and the construction of CAPO, which encompasses two dynamic phases—imitation and discrimination—that evolve as the model progresses. CAPO demonstrates compatibility with disparate reinforcement learning methodologies and is applicable to tasks encompassing multiple data types, such as GUIs. Illustrated in Figure 2, CAPO operates by synthesizing samples generated by the policy model, calculating advantage estimates through various algorithms, and amalgamating these into a unified framework. By leveraging these signals, CAPO orchestrates training into an initial imitation phase to ensure stability and subsequently a discrimination phase to optimize outcomes when more challenging samples are introduced.

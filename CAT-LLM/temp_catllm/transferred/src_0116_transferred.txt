The research utilized Taobao search logs, categorizing the data into distinct industries such as electronics and clothing. Data sampling was conducted based on the representation of each industry, ensuring that the training data encompassed features from all sectors. Subsequently, the data was segmented into high, medium, and low frequency categories according to the prevalence of searches, facilitating an inclusive representation of both common and rare queries. This stratification was followed by expert labeling of the data. During the data cleaning phase, errors were predominantly identified due to annotators overlooking details, particularly in lengthy queries, or due to insufficiently clear labeling guidelines that failed to address all scenarios. To address these issues, advanced language models were employed to refine the data. One model was utilized for its stringent attribute matching capabilities, surpassing human accuracy in complex situations, while the other model applied comprehensive rules to inform reasoning and judgment, thereby identifying and excising cases not covered by existing guidelines. The deployment of these models was dual-purpose: one excelled in interpreting extensive texts, whereas the other was adept at analyzing diverse data types. The enhancement of the model's reasoning capabilities involved a three-stage process: augmenting knowledge and reasoning, understanding varied data types, and adhering to established rules. The initial stage deconstructed user intents into attributes that could be systematically compared with item characteristics. Subsequent stages evaluated the congruence of items with search requirements based on item specifications and predefined rules.

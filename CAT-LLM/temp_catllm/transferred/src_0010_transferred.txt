Evaluating the quality of citations involves assessing recall, precision, and the F1 score, which collectively offer insights into citation effectiveness. Recall pertains to the proportion of correct references from a human-curated survey included in the computer-generated survey, thus indicating the breadth of literature coverage. Precision determines whether references in the computer-generated survey are indeed present in the human-curated list, thereby demonstrating accuracy. This comparison elucidates the degree of alignment between computer-generated references and those derived from human research, reflecting the efficacy of computational literature analysis. The method of employing Large Language Models as a Judge is prevalent for evaluating computer-generated text, primarily due to its scalability, consistency, and cost-effectiveness. Typically, models are provided with guidelines and scoring systems to assess the quality of the generated output. However, this approach may be overly lenient for complex tasks such as extensive surveys. Consequently, two versions of Large Language Models as a Judge are utilized: one incorporating a human-written survey for comprehensive content evaluation, and another based on principles for outline checking. For survey quality assessment, a configuration is employed wherein the model receives a human survey on the corresponding topic alongside the computer-generated one. The human survey aids the model in making more informed judgments beyond mere reliance on guidelines. This methodology addresses several limitations associated with guideline-only approaches. Firstly, the inclusion of an expert example prevents the model from overestimating ambiguous or superficial content. Secondly, juxtaposing the computer-generated survey with a detailed human survey enables the model to recognize critical elements such as robust arguments and empirical evidence, which guidelines alone may overlook. Thirdly, the utilization of a high-quality example curtails the tendency to uniformly assign high scores across systems, facilitating a clearer distinction in system performance and a deeper understanding of strengths and areas for improvement in survey creation.

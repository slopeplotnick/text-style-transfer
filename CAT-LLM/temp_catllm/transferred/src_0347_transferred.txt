Gurung and Lapata (2025) introduced the VR-CLI framework, designed to optimize planning processes by employing extensive reasoning over narrative information and generating comprehensive responses. VR-CLI operates as a reward-based mechanism intended to enhance the likelihood of producing accurate subsequent chapters, grounded in the contextual information of the story and the planning structure. Enhancements to VR-CLI were implemented by incorporating an additional component aimed at reducing the probability of selecting arbitrary chapters from the same literary work. LiteReason, the conventional method utilized, occasionally involves incremental training steps based on paths generated during the process, which incurs a higher computational cost but concurrently updates the reasoning component with the model's current state. Furthermore, a concealed thought prompt was introduced to facilitate the model's utilization of hidden thought tags. LiteReason without SFT retains the hidden prompt alongside the initial Reasoning Projector, which leverages hidden thought tags to determine the appropriate moments for providing updated reasoning to the model, while omitting SFT during training to adjust the Reasoning Projector. LiteReason without the Reasoning Projector evaluates the performance following training in the absence of the Reasoning Projector, yet maintaining the hidden thought prompt, thereby examining whether bypassing hidden reasoning steps influences model behavior. Recent advances in hidden reasoning research were reviewed, and select prior methodologies were employed to assess the proposed approaches in Section 3. Experimental evaluations were conducted on Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks, with training-free hidden reasoning being compared against two novel techniques: Mixture of Inputs (MoI), proposed by Su et al. (2025), which integrates the embeddings of sampled tokens with others, and Soft Thinking, introduced by Zhang et al. (2025), which similarly combines token embeddings while utilizing an entropy-based criterion to determine the transition from continuous reasoning to discrete sampling.

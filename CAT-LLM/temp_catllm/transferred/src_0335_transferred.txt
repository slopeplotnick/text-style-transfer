The methodology employed seeks to enhance the camera's movement to achieve diverse viewpoints and mitigate the limitations associated with static positioning. Each camera trajectory is initialized randomly, incorporating variations in angles such as pitch, yaw, and roll, which are adjusted dynamically to create a naturalistic motion. The synchronization between auditory signals and visual elements is ensured by maintaining visibility of key sound sources for extended durations. The identification of the most prominent auditory feature in each video segment is conducted through a method that decomposes sound into constituent components, subsequently facilitating camera adjustments to prioritize the identified sound. Following the generation of binaural audio, samples exhibiting minimal disparity between left and right auditory channels are excluded, retaining only those characterized by distinct auditory cues. BiAudio constitutes the largest repository of videos containing binaural sound, surpassing other datasets in size and scope. Comparatively, existing datasets featuring real-world binaural audio are limited in scale, while SimBinaural, despite encompassing extensive hours of data, is synthetic and consequently less applicable to real-world scenarios. Additionally, other datasets are predominantly constrained to specific auditory contexts such as urban environments or musical compositions. BiAudio is distinct in its extensive coverage of diverse real-world soundscapes, contributing to enhanced spatial and motion perception, which is instrumental in generating binaural sound from video inputs. ViSAudio's architecture is delineated through a schematic representation, incorporating elements of MMAudio's framework while adapting it to retain its robust sound synthesis capabilities, ensuring functionality across varied auditory environments. To facilitate the generation of binaural sound with distinct left and right channels, the Dual-Branch Audio Generation approach is implemented, wherein two separate branches independently predict left and right auditory signals. Furthermore, a Conditional Spacetime Module is integrated to extract video features and utilize them within the audio generation process, thereby augmenting the realism of the output.

The globalization of education and the rapid expansion of online learning necessitate the adaptation of educational content to diverse geographical contexts, thus presenting significant challenges. Lectures traditionally integrate spoken discourse with visual aids such as slides, thereby requiring systems capable of synchronizing these elements effectively. To facilitate universal accessibility in learning, it is imperative that translations retain all components: textual content for reading, slides for visual engagement, and auditory information for listening comprehension. Consequently, BOOM, a tool engineered to translate lecture audio and slides concurrently, was developed to provide outputs in three distinct formats: translated text, slides preserving their original design, and speech rendered in the target language. This innovation enables students to experience lectures in their native language while maintaining the integrity of the original material. Empirical evaluations have demonstrated that slides significantly contribute to tasks such as summarization and question answering. The Slide Translation code has been shared publicly at https://github.com/saikoneru/image-Translator, and integrated into Lecture Translator at https://gitlab kit.edu/kit/isl-ai41t/lt-middleware/ltpipeline. The ability for students to engage with educational content in their own language substantially enhances comprehension, reduces communicative obstacles, increases accessibility, and facilitates the assimilation of complex concepts. As higher education institutions increasingly embrace internationalization, the availability of lecture material in multiple languages, both in-person and online, becomes pivotal to expanding educational resources. The digitization of teaching materials necessitates the integration of diverse media types, with lecture audio often converted to text via Automatic Speech Recognition. Additionally, instructional content is delivered through slides, while ancillary materials such as summaries, chapters, and question-answer sessions are generated from text utilizing advanced systems based on Large Language Models, thereby enhancing the learning experience. It is essential that these outputs are made available in multiple languages to ensure accessibility for all students, including those with limited proficiency in the primary language of instruction. Effective localization must address this multifaceted content, encompassing audio, text, and visuals, rendering lecture translation a formidable endeavor.

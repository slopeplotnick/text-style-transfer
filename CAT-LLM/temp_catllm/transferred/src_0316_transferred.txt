This study employs a systematic methodology to compare prompts in an organized and structured manner. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is utilized to reduce the complexity of reasoning data by transforming prompts into a simplified two-dimensional representation. As illustrated in Figure 10 from MATH500, prompts selected through optimization demonstrate closer clustering compared to randomly chosen prompts, indicating that optimized prompts enhance the model's ability to process problems in a stable and consistent manner. This finding is further corroborated by additional tests, with results presented in Figures 15 and 16 within the appendix. A framework has been developed to improve the functionality of multilingual language models by refining prompts, with empirical results suggesting that specific components of prompts significantly enhance performance across diverse languages. Multilingual reward systems are employed to identify these optimal prompts, and analyses reveal that improved prompts contribute to more organized and consistent reasoning processes, underscoring their efficacy in enhancing model performance across linguistic contexts. This research was supported by funding from LG AI and the National Science Foundation under Grant No. IIS-2143529. While SPRIG has identified several effective prompts, achieving universally optimal prompts across all four tests remains challenging. Experimental results indicate that high-quality prompts guide models toward specific reasoning patterns that improve performance, although the underlying mechanisms warrant further investigation. Ethical considerations were prioritized throughout this research; however, certain challenges persist. Specifically, the computational demands associated with prompt optimization result in substantial energy consumption and a significant carbon footprint. Additionally, the optimization process may inadvertently exacerbate biases, particularly when prompts influence models to emulate specific personas, such as that of a professor. Given that the data includes role-based instructions, careful attention must be directed toward ensuring that biases are neither introduced nor amplified as a consequence of prompt optimization.

Various methods have been developed to enhance logical reasoning tasks using large language models, among which CoT2 is notable for its integration with GPT-2, facilitating logical reasoning through a unique approach to token mixing akin to training-free methods. Furthermore, CODI employs a teacher-student configuration to optimize the performance of GPT-2 and Llama 3.1 1B models on GSM8k tasks. Another approach, Token Assorted, innovatively develops new token embeddings utilizing VQ-VAE and subsequently trains Llama 3.1 8B for mathematical and logical reasoning tasks. Our LiteReason method distinguishes itself from these techniques, as it does not rely on extensive datasets like GSM8k-Aug for reasoning traces, which are unavailable for our specific tasks. Instead, reasoning traces generated internally by the model itself are utilized. Additionally, we implement a small Reasoning Projector exclusively for reasoning purposes, as opposed to comprehensive model training, based on the hypothesis that excessive training for token embedding could impair the model’s token generation capability. Moreover, the model is permitted to blend latent and discrete reasoning during reinforcement learning training, with the anticipation that an optimal balance will be established over time. Although several methods juxtapose their outcomes with Chain-of-Thought prompting and some incorporate reinforcement learning, a comparison with a non-latent RL-trained model, which might provide a superior performance metric for complex tasks, has not been observed. Consequently, such a comparison is included in our assessments as a benchmark for top performance and computational constraints. Regarding narrative tasks, despite significant advancements in reasoning applications within domains such as mathematics and coding, substantial research has been conducted on leveraging large language models for narrative comprehension and creation. Certain studies concentrate on particular aspects of story generation, such as event planning or character development, which are representative of varied reasoning styles. However, these tasks pose challenges for reinforcement learning due to the absence of distinct rewards. This study targets two tasks designed to evaluate reasoning capabilities within narrative contexts.

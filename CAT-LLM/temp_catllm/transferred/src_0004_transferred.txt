The ReVSeg model exhibits notable proficiency in video object segmentation (VOS), surpassing alternative methodologies in standardized evaluations. It demonstrates superior performance compared to approaches reliant on latent features and those not requiring prior training, additionally elucidating the decision-making process. Specifically, Figure 1 elucidates that the exclusion of the reasoning steps incorporated into the model results in diminished performance on challenging tasks for both the foundational model and the model subjected to reinforcement learning (RL). Nevertheless, the proposed method maintains efficacy prior to RL training, with RL integration further enhancing results. The methodological approach comprised several key stages: initially, the reasoning process for VOS was deconstructed into distinct steps utilizing fundamental components of the model to establish a coherent reasoning chain. Subsequently, an innovative RL framework was devised to refine this reasoning chain, thereby enabling the model to make improved decisions without necessitating extensive supplementary training data. Finally, this method established a new benchmark for performance in VOS evaluations and rendered the reasoning process more transparent. Recent advancements have seen the refinement of models and specialized configurations for text-guided VOS, as evidenced in references 3, 5, 29, and 45. However, challenges persist in more demanding scenarios, such as those involving videos with numerous similar objects, rapid motion, or objects with low visibility. Contemporary methods like CoT-RVS [13] attempt to address these challenges by employing dual systems to process each video frame and interconnect them with textual elements. While effective, such approaches are constrained by the lack of comprehensive integration and absence of training. In contrast, the present study conceptualizes reasoning VOS as a clear, systematic process within a unified system, employing RL to progressively enhance performance.

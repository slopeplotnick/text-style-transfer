Prompt design plays a critical role in the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs), yet the impact of prompt specificity—how detailed or vague a prompt is—remains understudied. This paper introduces DETAIL, a framework for evaluating LLM performance across varying levels of prompt specificity. We generate multi-level prompts using GPT-4, quantify specificity via perplexity, and assess correctness using GPT-based semantic equivalence. Experiments on 30 novel reasoning tasks across GPT-4 and O3-mini reveal that specificity improves accuracy, especially for smaller models and procedural tasks. Our results highlight the need for adaptive prompting strategies and provide tools and data to support further research. Large language models (LLMs) (Zhao et al., 2023) have drastically changed the world with their excellent generative capabilities (Hurst et al., 2024; Anthropic, 2024; Gemini Team et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025). With more developments being released at a pace more rapid than ever, the NLP revolution is not limited to simple text tasks but also surpasses many state-of-the-arts across various tasks (Bai et al., 2022; Touvron et al., 2023). Many recent evaluations are done by measuring efficacy of models' reasoning in mathematics problems (Vendrow et al., 2025) and multi-step problems (Suzgun et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024). Enhancing LLM reasoning is vital for their ongoing development (Qiao et al., 2023; Patil, 2025), and recent advancements have introduced various approaches to improve LLM reasoning via prompting (Cheng et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025). These methods often involve decomposing problems into intermediate steps. A significant challenge that still persists is determining the optimal level of prompt specificity to generate accurate and coherent responses. Although detailed prompts can provide clear guidance, they may also constrain the model's reasoning pathways. In contrast, vague prompts offer flexibility, but risk ambiguity and misinterpretation.

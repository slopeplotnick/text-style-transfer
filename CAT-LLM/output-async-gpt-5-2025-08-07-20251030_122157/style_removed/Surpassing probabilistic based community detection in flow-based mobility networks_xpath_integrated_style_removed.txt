To analyze how regions connect, we need to understand community structures in flow-based mobility networks, but current methods have two problems: modularity optimization runs into resolution limits, and they don’t combine local and global community detection in these networks. To fix this, we built a new approach that combines surpassing probability theory with community detection. First, the surpassing probability-based Leiden method (SPBL) reshuffles flow weights so the Leiden algorithm gets past its resolution limit and can find macro-communities. Then the two-phase surpassing probability community detection (TPSPCD) algorithm breaks these communities into smaller sub-communities while keeping important anchor relationships. We also add a metric called Aggregate Surpassing Degree (ASD) to measure how strong connections are inside a community compared to outside. We found distinct core-periphery patterns in flow-based mobility networks, with strong community cohesion around key node centres. Overall, the proposed method captures localized interactions in flow-based mobility networks well. This work moves both theory and practice forward and gives planners actionable tools for regional development.

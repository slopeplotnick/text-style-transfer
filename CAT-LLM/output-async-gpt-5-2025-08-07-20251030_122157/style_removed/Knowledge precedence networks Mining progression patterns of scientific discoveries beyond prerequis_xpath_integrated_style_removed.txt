It’s important to understand how knowledge changes over scientists’ careers to improve education and innovation.We build Knowledge Precedence Networks (KPNs) to find real-world patterns across 19 fields, using research paths of 4,969,403 scientists and 80 million papers from OpenAlex.We propose a method called CoCiTCD that combines co-citing networks with temporal community detection to track how knowledge moves by finding research communities, picking representative concepts, and creating time-ordered concept pairs.In Math, Computer Science, and Engineering, KPNs show that basic ideas are key for advanced topics.For example, Algorithms connect Math and Computer Science and help push Artificial Intelligence and Data Science forward.We check how KPNs for 303 concepts match theoretical prerequisite relations labeled by large language models to see how scientists use knowledge over time.At best, KPN recall is 25.77%, and the citation-based KCN reaches 26.6%.This low match means that real topic shifts often don’t follow the theoretical prerequisite order.Also, we get an AUC of 0.76 on a sample variational ROC curve, which suggests our KPN approach is solid at capturing the subtle, innovative ways knowledge progresses.KPNs give useful insights for research planning, designing learning paths, working across fields, and understanding the knowledge hierarchy, and they help the Science of Science by showing real patterns of knowledge progression across fields.

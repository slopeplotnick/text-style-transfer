Bundle recommendation is an online service that gives users personalized sets of items they may like, but current methods that use multiple kinds of information often don’t make good use of the links between users and bundles, especially when deciding who is linked to what and who isn’t. We present a new method called MIMOSA (multi-view multi-order contrastive learning) that models relationships across multiple views and between different types of entities at the same time. It uses one graph-based contrastive learning setup that handles links within the same type and across different types, and it considers how close things are in the user-bundle graph at different distances. MIMOSA also defines how to pick positive examples and how to compute the contrastive loss so it can deal with different kinds of relationships, which pulls together the representations of linked entities and pushes apart those that aren’t linked. We also add a center-matching step to keep representations from different views aligned and to speed up the contrastive learning. Tests on two large datasets, Youshu and NetEase, show that MIMOSA outperforms the baselines: compared with the best baseline, it improves R@20 by 2.87% and N@20 by 3.02% on Youshu, and improves R@20 by 5.19% and N@20 by 4.07% on NetEase.

Automated policy text evaluation is an important topic in policy informatics.Old methods mostly depend on manually assigning variables, so they don’t scale well, and the indicators taken from specific policies don’t work across different domains.To fix this, we propose an automated evaluation framework for policy text.We redesigned three general first-level indicators that can apply to any policy domain, integrated the Policy Modeling Consistency Model (PMC) into large language models (LLM), and built automated PMC scoring models based on LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B.Using Chinese S&T policies as examples, we created the first Chinese policy evaluation dataset with 22,630 labeled policy samples and trained four models to automatically calculate the PMC indicators.Compared with other methods, the model based on Qwen-2.5-7B performs best for evaluating policy character, with an F1-score of 80.41%.The model based on LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B performs best for evaluating policy normativity and policy function, with F1-scores of 75.07% and 74.11%.This lets us automatically compute PMC indices and produce a table with multiple inputs and outputs for broader policy analysis.Results on biosafety policies and data governance policies show the framework works across different domains.As the first framework for automated PMC evaluation, this method offers a new way to do large-scale, cross-domain policy evaluation.

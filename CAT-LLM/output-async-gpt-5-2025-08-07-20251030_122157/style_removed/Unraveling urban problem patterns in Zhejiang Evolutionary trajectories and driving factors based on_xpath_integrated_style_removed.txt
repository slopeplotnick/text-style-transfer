This study looks at how urban problem patterns form, change over time, and what causes them in 87 counties in Zhejiang Province, China.We sorted about 3.5 million public complaints into 37 categories and measured the distribution of these problems from 2018 to 2022.We found seven main patterns—pollution and municipal infrastructure, consumer protection, land use, housing purchase, garbage management, financial service, and pandemic control—and these patterns stayed fairly steady over time and tended to cluster by location.We used fuzzy logic to see how strongly each county fit each pattern, and the trends were pretty predictable: new patterns kept growing, and the dominant ones slowly went down, with a few exceptions.Looking at eight social and economic factors, we found that development-driven patterns (land use, housing purchase, consumer protection) were strongly linked to things like land acquisition area, property sales volume, and retail activity, which means economic activities need tighter oversight.Trend-driven patterns, like pollution and city infrastructure issues, were less about short-term ups and downs and more about steady background conditions, so systematic planning and coordinated actions are important to reduce long-term regional imbalances.Event-driven patterns (garbage management, financial service, pandemic control) had little to do with those socioeconomic factors, which means they are triggered by notable events and need fast crisis response.

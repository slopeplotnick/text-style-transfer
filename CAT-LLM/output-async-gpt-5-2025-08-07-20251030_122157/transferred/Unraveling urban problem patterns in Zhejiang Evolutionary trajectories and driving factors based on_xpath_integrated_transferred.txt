This study investigates the formation, temporal evolution, and determinants of urban problem patterns across 87 counties in Zhejiang Province, China, using large-scale citizen complaint data.Approximately 3.5 million public complaints were classified into 37 categories, and their spatial-temporal distributions were measured for the period 2018–2022 to derive consistent pattern typologies.Seven principal patterns were identified—pollution and municipal infrastructure, consumer protection, land use, housing purchase, garbage management, financial service, and pandemic control—whose prevalence remained relatively stable over time and exhibited spatial clustering.A fuzzy logic framework evaluated how strongly each county conforms to each pattern across the study period.Trends proved predictable: emergent patterns continued to grow, while dominant ones declined gradually, with isolated exceptions.Considering eight social and economic variables, development-driven patterns—land use, housing purchase, and consumer protection—showed significant associations with land acquisition area, property sales volume, and retail activity.These linkages point to a need for tighter oversight of economic processes and regulatory enforcement at multiple administrative levels.Trend-driven patterns, such as pollution and municipal infrastructure issues, reflected persistent background conditions rather than short-term fluctuations, so systematic planning and coordinated action are critical to reduce long-term regional imbalances.Event-driven patterns—garbage management, financial service, and pandemic control—displayed minimal relationships with these socioeconomic factors and arise from notable events, therefore requiring rapid crisis response capacity.

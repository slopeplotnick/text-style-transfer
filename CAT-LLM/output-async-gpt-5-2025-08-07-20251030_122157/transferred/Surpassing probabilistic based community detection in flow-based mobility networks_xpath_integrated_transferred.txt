Understanding regional connectivity in flow-based mobility networks requires careful characterization of community structure, yet prevailing approaches face two limitations: modularity optimization suffers resolution thresholds, and local and global detection remain unintegrated. However, we propose a novel framework that couples surpassing probability theory with community detection to address both limitations without sacrificing interpretability. First, the surpassing probability-based Leiden method (SPBL) reshuffles flow weights so that the Leiden algorithm overcomes its resolution limit and identifies macro-communities. Subsequently, communities are decomposed by the two-phase surpassing probability community detection (TPSPCD) algorithm into finer sub-communities while preserving critical anchor relationships. To quantify intra- versus extra-community cohesion, we propose Aggregate Surpassing Degree (ASD), a metric that compares the strength of internal ties to external linkages. Distinct core–periphery configurations are observed in flow-based mobility networks, with pronounced community cohesion around key node centers. Taken together, the approach shows effective capture of localized interactions in flow-based mobility networks and advances theory and practice by offering planners actionable tools for regional development.

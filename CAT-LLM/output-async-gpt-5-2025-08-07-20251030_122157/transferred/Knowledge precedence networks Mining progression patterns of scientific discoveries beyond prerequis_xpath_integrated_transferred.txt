Understanding how knowledge evolves across scientists’ careers is significant for improving education and fostering innovation.To capture empirical regularities, Knowledge Precedence Networks (KPNs) are constructed across 19 fields by analyzing the research trajectories of 4,969,403 scientists and approximately 80 million papers from OpenAlex.We propose a method, CoCiTCD, that integrates co-citing networks with temporal community detection to track the movement of knowledge by identifying research communities, selecting representative concepts, and generating time-ordered concept pairs.Within Mathematics, Computer Science, and Engineering, the resulting KPNs indicate that foundational ideas are pivotal for advanced topics.For example, Algorithms connect Mathematics and Computer Science and help drive progress in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science.We report how KPNs for 303 concepts align with theoretical prerequisite relations that are labeled by large language models to characterize how scientists use knowledge over time.However, at best, KPN recall is 25.77%, and the citation-based KCN reaches 26.6%, a modest correspondence indicating that real topic transitions frequently do not adhere to theoretical prerequisite order.We obtain an AUC of 0.76 on a sample variational ROC curve, which supports the robust performance of the KPN approach in capturing the subtle pathways of knowledge progression.Collectively, KPNs provide insights for research planning, the design of learning paths, cross-field collaboration, and understanding the knowledge hierarchy, thereby advancing the Science of Science by showing real patterns of knowledge progression across fields.

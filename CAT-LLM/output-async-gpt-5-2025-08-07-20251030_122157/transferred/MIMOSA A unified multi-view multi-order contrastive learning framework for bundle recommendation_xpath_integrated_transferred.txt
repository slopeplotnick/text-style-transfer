Bundle recommendation is an online service that delivers personalized sets of items to users, yet methods leveraging multiple information sources often underutilize user–bundle links, particularly in discerning whether links exist. However, a novel method, MIMOSA (multi-view multi-order contrastive learning), is presented to model relationships across multiple views and between different types of entities concurrently. It employs a single graph-based contrastive learning framework that simultaneously handles intra-type and inter-type links and accounts for proximity at multiple orders in the user–bundle graph. To address different relations, MIMOSA defines positive example selection and a contrastive loss that pulls representations of linked entities together while pushing those of unlinked entities apart. An effective center-matching step aligns representations across views and accelerates contrastive learning. Empirical results are reported on two large datasets, Youshu and NetEase, and the experiments evaluate performance and demonstrate that MIMOSA outperforms the best baseline, improving R@20 by 2.87% and N@20 by 3.02% on Youshu and R@20 by 5.19% and N@20 by 4.07% on NetEase.

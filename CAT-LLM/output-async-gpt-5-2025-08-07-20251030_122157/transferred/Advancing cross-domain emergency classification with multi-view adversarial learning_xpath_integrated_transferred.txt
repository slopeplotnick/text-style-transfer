Emergency data from natural and human-made events are increasingly generated on social media, necessitating real-time classification across emergency types to support situational awareness. Existing Unsupervised Domain Adaptation approaches emphasize natural disasters, limiting generalization, and they face domain shifts from heterogeneous distributions, lexical variation, and affective expression. Therefore, this study proposes MARDA (Multi-View Adversarial Neural Networks for Robust Unsupervised Domain Adaptation), integrating adversarial domain adaptation with multi-view feature learning through a cross-view processor that combines semantic and emotional processors. An adaptive weighted domain enhancer adjusts each view’s contribution, consolidating discriminative cross-domain information to improve separability. An adversarial cross-view optimizer employs a minimax scheme with a consistency constraint to promote generalization and stabilize feature alignment. MARDA was evaluated on four real-world emergency datasets totaling 24,008 samples; results demonstrate 7.39% improvement over strong baselines and 1.59% over large language models in average F1-Score, indicating an effective cross-domain emergency classification solution.

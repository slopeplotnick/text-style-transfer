To ensure secure interactions among networked entities, trust must be assessed, yet prevailing trust evaluation models are vulnerable to global attacks, depend on multi-layer stacking, and exhibit limited adaptability in dynamically sparse networks.We propose DLGTrust, a dynamic and robust graph neural network-based trust evaluation model that addresses these constraints by explicitly modeling relationships between indirect and direct trust.Dynamic line graph snapshots are employed to map indirect trust relations onto direct connections in a transparent manner, enabling the capture of complex interactions and improving performance under sparsity.The model integrates multi-modal spatial feature extraction with a GRU-based spatiotemporal fusion mechanism to represent intricate interaction patterns with fine granularity.It injects adversarial perturbations and imposes global robustness constraints to strengthen defenses against global attacks.Therefore, its effectiveness is demonstrated on three real-world datasets, where DLGTrust improves overall performance by at least 26.3% relative to the baseline.The macro F1 exceeds 98% for both seen and unseen nodes, and with bad-mouthing, good-mouthing, and global attacks each set at 10%, macro F1 improves by 24.5%, 23.7%, and 76.7%, respectively.These results show that DLGTrust is resilient and effective in defense, thereby supporting secure interactions among entities.

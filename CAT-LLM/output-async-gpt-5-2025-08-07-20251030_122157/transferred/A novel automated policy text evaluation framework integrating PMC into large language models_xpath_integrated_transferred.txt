Automated policy text evaluation is a central topic in policy informatics, yet legacy approaches rely on manual variable assignment, scale poorly, and produce indicators that lack cross-domain applicability. Therefore, we propose a novel automated evaluation framework for policy text that addresses scalability and generalizability constraints by unifying indicator design and model-based scoring within a single pipeline. Three general first-level indicators were redesigned to apply across policy domains and enhance applicability, and the Policy Modeling Consistency Model (PMC) was integrated into large language models (LLMs). We constructed automated PMC scoring models based on LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B and, using Chinese science and technology policies as exemplars, created the first Chinese policy evaluation dataset comprising 22,630 labeled policy samples. We trained four models to automatically compute the PMC indicators for this dataset. Compared with other methods, the Qwen-2.5-7B–based model performs best for assessing policy character, achieving an F1-score of 80.41% across the labeled corpus. Compared with other methods, the LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B–based model performs best for assessing policy normativity and policy function, with F1-scores of 75.07% and 74.11%, respectively, across the same evaluation setting. These capabilities enable automatic computation of PMC indices and generation of a multi-input, multi-output table to support broader policy analysis. Results on biosafety policies and data governance policies demonstrate that the framework generalizes across domains in empirical evaluations. As the first framework for automated PMC evaluation, the approach provides a systematic pathway for large-scale, cross-domain policy evaluation and supports broader policy analysis.

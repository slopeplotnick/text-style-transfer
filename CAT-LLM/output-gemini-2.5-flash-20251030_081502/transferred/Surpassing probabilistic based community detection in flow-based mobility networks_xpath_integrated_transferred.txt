Understanding the organization of groups within dynamic mobility networks is crucial, as it facilitates the identification of inter-regional connectivity. However, current methodologies for this purpose exhibit two significant limitations: first, conventional community detection algorithms often lack the capacity to effectively resolve both fine-grained and coarse-grained structures; second, they fail to integrate local and global perspectives on community organization within such networks. To address these shortcomings, a novel approach integrating surpassing probability theory with community detection methodologies is proposed. Termed SPBL (Surpassing Probability-based Leiden), network connections are initially reweighted by this novel method to overcome the limitations of the standard Leiden method in detecting large-scale communities. This component, named TPSPCD (Topological Partitioning for Sub-Community Detection), systematically decomposes these macro-communities into finer-grained sub-groups while preserving their essential structural integrity. The comprehensive framework further incorporates a metric, ASD (Association Strength Difference), designed to quantify the internal cohesion of a community relative to its external connectivity. Empirical findings revealed distinct patterns of core and peripheral regions within these mobility networks, alongside robust community formation around significant central nodes. This research demonstrates that the proposed community detection approach effectively captures local movement dynamics within these networks. This advancement contributes to both the theoretical understanding and practical application of community detection in mobility networks, thereby equipping urban planners with valuable tools for informed regional development.

The establishment of trust is crucial for facilitating secure communication among distributed network entities; however, existing trust computation methodologies exhibit several inherent limitations. Specifically, these conventional approaches are susceptible to adversarial attacks, often entail excessive layering dependencies, and perform suboptimally in dynamic, sparsely connected network environments. Consequently, to address these challenges, a novel trust computation framework, termed DLGTrust, has been proposed, which leverages a graph neural network architecture to achieve robustness and adaptability to evolving network conditions. DLGTrust utilizes dynamic topological snapshots to transform implicit trust relationships into explicit linkages, thereby facilitating the comprehension of complex interdependencies and enhancing performance, particularly under conditions of data scarcity. Furthermore, it integrates heterogeneous contextual and temporal data, which contributes to a granular comprehension of intricate systemic operational interdependencies. Concurrently, DLGTrust employs adversarial training and specifically designed resilience mechanisms to enhance its robustness against sophisticated adversarial threats. Empirical evaluations conducted across three distinct real-world datasets demonstrated that DLGTrust outperformed conventional methodologies by at least 26.3% in overall performance; specifically, its primary performance metric, the F1-macro score, exceeded 98%, even when evaluated on previously unobserved network segments. Furthermore, under adversarial conditions where 10% of the network nodes engaged in malicious manipulation (e.g., malicious rumor dissemination, benevolent rumor dissemination, or direct attack scenarios), DLGTrust's F1-macro score improved by 24.5%, 23.7%, and 76.7%, respectively, indicating enhanced resilience and self-defensive capabilities. Therefore, DLGTrust significantly contributes to facilitating secure inter-entity communication within distributed network environments.

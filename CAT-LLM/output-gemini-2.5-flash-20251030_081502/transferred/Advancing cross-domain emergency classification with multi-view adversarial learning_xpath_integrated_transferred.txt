The escalating volume of emergency information, stemming from both natural and anthropogenic events, necessitates robust and rapid classification mechanisms on social media platforms for diverse emergency scenarios. Current Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) methods for emergency information classification, however, exhibit two significant limitations. Their design is predominantly tailored for natural disasters, restricting applicability to other emergency types, and they struggle when confronted with substantial linguistic and emotional variability within the data. To address these shortcomings, a novel system, MARDA, was developed. This system offers an innovative approach for facilitating multi-faceted domain adaptation and learning simultaneously. Its architecture incorporates a multi-view feature extraction module, integrating semantic and emotional analysis to construct a comprehensive representation of the data. An adaptive weighting mechanism then dynamically adjusts the contribution of each viewpoint, thereby enhancing the extraction of salient information from heterogeneous data sources. Furthermore, a specialized adversarial learning component was integrated to ensure robust generalization capabilities across varied data types. MARDA's efficacy was empirically validated using four extensive real-world emergency datasets, comprising over 24,000 instances. The system demonstrated superior performance, outperforming other state-of-the-art computational programs by approximately 7.4% and large language models by 1.6% in classification accuracy. These results indicate that MARDA is highly effective for classifying diverse emergency events, irrespective of their originating source.

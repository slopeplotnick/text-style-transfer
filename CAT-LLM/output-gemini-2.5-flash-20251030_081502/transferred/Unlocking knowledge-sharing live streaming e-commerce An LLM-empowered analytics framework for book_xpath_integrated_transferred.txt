The discourse employed by streamers during live shopping broadcasts holds significant importance for consumer purchase decisions, particularly when knowledge dissemination accompanies product promotion. Prior research has demonstrated that streamer communication influences sales performance; however, a comprehensive, multi-faceted analysis of the specific semantic content within their discourse and its predictive capacity for future sales has been largely overlooked. Consequently, the primary objective of this research was to develop a robust predictive system for sales in knowledge-sharing live commerce environments. This novel system integrates concepts of social support with fine-tuned Large Language Models (LLMs), systematically extracting latent signals from streamer discourse to predict sales outcomes. Empirical data were collected from 80 live streams across 35 distinct Douyin channels over a two-month period for experimental validation. During the evaluation of social support detection, our specialized Ernie-SFT model significantly outperformed the leading baseline model, achieving an 11.12% improvement in accuracy, an 11.87% increase in F1-score, and a 7.83% enhancement in precision. Subsequently, when applied to sales prediction, our proposed system consistently surpassed four established baseline methodologies, with the most effective method demonstrating a 12.53% higher accuracy, alongside substantial improvements of 10.83% in F1-score and 4.24% in precision. These findings collectively underscore the significant predictive utility of social support expressed in streamer discourse for sales forecasting; therefore, this research offers actionable insights for streamers and facilitates the development of data-driven strategic decisions by platform operators.

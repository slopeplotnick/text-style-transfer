Bundle recommendation has emerged as a widely adopted online service, offering users customized sets of items predicted to align with their preferences.Existing methodologies endeavor to integrate diverse information sources; however, they frequently encounter difficulties in effectively leveraging the intricate relationships between entities, such as users and bundles.Specifically, a particular challenge lies in their inability to accurately differentiate between synergistic and disparate relationships within these networks.Consequently, to address this limitation, our study proposes a novel bundle recommendation methodology, termed MIMOSA, an acronym for multi-view multi-order contrastive learning.MIMOSA simultaneously uncovers latent interdependencies from multiple perspectives and across heterogeneous entities.It employs a unified graph-based framework that facilitates learning through comparative analysis.This approach disentangles both intra-entity and inter-entity relationships by analyzing the proximity of nodes within the user-bundle network across various relational contexts.Specifically, MIMOSA incorporates dedicated mechanisms for positive sample mining and loss function computation.These mechanisms facilitate the discernment of nuanced semantic implications underlying diverse inter-entity connections.This ensures that semantically related entities are clustered proximally, while unrelated entities are maximally separated.Furthermore, a distinct 'center-matching' strategy is integrated, designed to rapidly align multi-view representations and consequently accelerate the contrastive learning process.Extensive experiments were conducted on two substantial datasets, namely Youshu and NetEase, where the empirical results demonstrate that MIMOSA significantly outperforms existing methodologies.Specifically, compared to the state-of-the-art baseline, MIMOSA achieved performance improvements of approximately 2.87% (R@20) and 3.02% (N@20) on the Youshu dataset, and 5.19% (R@20) and 4.07% (N@20) on the NetEase dataset.

Understanding the mechanisms by which scientists acquire new knowledge during their professional activities is crucial, as this understanding facilitates advancements in educational methodologies and the generation of novel insights. Consequently, this study introduces Knowledge Progression Networks (KPNs), which were employed to elucidate the actual progression of scientific knowledge across 19 distinct domains. The analysis encompassed the activities of nearly 5 million scientists and 80 million scholarly articles extracted from the extensive OpenAlex database. Furthermore, a novel methodology, Co-evolving Citation-Topic Community Detection (CoCiTCD), was developed, which integrates inter-document citation analysis with an approach for identifying temporally evolving research communities. Specifically, CoCiTCD facilitates the elucidation of knowledge accumulation by identifying research groups, discerning core concepts, and mapping their evolving interconnections over time. These KPNs, particularly within Mathematics, Computer Science, and Engineering, underscore the fundamental importance of foundational concepts for comprehending advanced topics. For instance, Algorithms serve as a bridging concept between Mathematics and Computer Science, thereby propelling advancements in fields such as Artificial Intelligence and Data Science. The alignment of our KPNs for 303 selected concepts was additionally evaluated against prerequisite knowledge identified by large AI language models, a comparison that provided insights into the temporal evolution of knowledge engagement practices among scientists. Under optimal conditions, the KPN identified approximately 25.77% of the targeted knowledge connections. A comparative system, the Knowledge Citation Network (KCN), which relies solely on citation linkages, achieved a comparable performance of 26.6%. This consistently low congruence suggests that the dynamic evolution of real-world topics frequently diverges significantly from prescriptive 'prerequisite' learning theories. Moreover, a robust area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) score of 0.76 substantiates the efficacy of our KPN methodology in capturing the complex and emergent pathways of knowledge progression. Ultimately, KPNs offer valuable implications for research planning, curriculum design, interdisciplinary collaboration, and a comprehensive understanding of knowledge construction, from foundational to advanced levels. Therefore, this framework significantly contributes to the 'Science of Science' domain by demonstrating the empirical progression of knowledge across diverse disciplines.

Recently, cross-domain click prediction has been extensively investigated by researchers and practitioners, leading to significant progress in this domain. However, despite the prevalence of sequential user behavior, research specifically addressing multi-domain sequential recommendation (MDSR) has remained limited. Most current MDSR methodologies typically aggregate user actions from diverse domains into a single chronological sequence, subsequently attempting to predict the user's next interaction within each domain. However, these approaches often neglect to consider the distinct sequential patterns inherent in user activities within individual domains, consequently compromising their capacity to effectively leverage cross-domain commonalities or disparities. Conversely, conventional cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) models are typically optimized for performance within a primary domain, rather than across multiple domains. While adapting a CDSR model for MDSR tasks is theoretically possible, it often results in prohibitive computational overhead; furthermore, these models frequently overlook data imbalance across domains, which can adversely impact recommendation quality. Consequently, we propose a novel MDSR framework, termed MML. Our MML architecture comprises three principal components: the Hierarchical Sequential Preference Learner (HSPL) captures cross-domain sequential preferences, and the Intra-domain Sequential Preference Learner (ISPL) models sequential preferences within individual domains. The third component is the Multi-Task Prediction (MLP) module, responsible for multi-task prediction generation. Additionally, we augment user action sequences and employ a specialized learning technique to facilitate fairer cross-domain preference learning, thereby mitigating potential biases. Within the MLP module, three distinct tasks are integrated alongside a behavior regularization component; this configuration ensures effective learning for each component and promotes knowledge transfer across domains. Extensive experiments were conducted on publicly available datasets, utilizing various baseline models, and our findings demonstrate that MML significantly outperforms state-of-the-art approaches, particularly across multiple domains. Moreover, MML maintains comparable computational efficiency to existing MDSR models that rely solely on aggregated user actions. The source code is publicly available at: https://github.com/plw2019/MML.

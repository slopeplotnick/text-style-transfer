The automated verification of policy documents represents a significant area of research. Previously, manual inspection methods predominated, which proved inefficient for large-scale policy analysis. Furthermore, existing measurement approaches were domain-specific, lacking generalizability across diverse policy types. To address these limitations, we propose a novel methodology for the automated verification of policy documents. This involved the adaptation of three fundamental measurement indicators to ensure their applicability across heterogeneous policy domains. Additionally, a Policy Modeling Consistency Model (PMC) was integrated into large language models, subsequently enabling the development of automated PMC scoring models utilizing LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B architectures. Leveraging Chinese science and technology policies as a case study, we constructed the first Chinese dataset for policy verification. This dataset comprises 22,630 pre-labeled policy instances. Subsequently, four distinct models were trained to automatically ascertain the PMC indicators. In comparison to conventional methodologies, the Qwen-2.5-7B model demonstrated superior performance in assessing policy content, achieving an F1-score of 80.41%. Conversely, the LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B model excelled in evaluating policy conformity and intended objectives, yielding F1-scores of 75.07% and 74.11%, respectively. These findings indicate the feasibility of automatically deriving PMC metrics and generating a comprehensive information table for thorough policy verification. Application of this system to biosafety and data governance policies further corroborated its generalizability across diverse policy categories. As this represents the inaugural system for automated PMC verification, our proposed methodology offers a novel and effective approach for evaluating a broad spectrum of policies across various domains.

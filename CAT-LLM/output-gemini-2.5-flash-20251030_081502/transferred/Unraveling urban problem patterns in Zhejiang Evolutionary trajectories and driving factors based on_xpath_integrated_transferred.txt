This study investigated the emergence, evolution, and underlying drivers of urban problems across 87 county-level administrative units in Zhejiang, China. A comprehensive dataset comprising approximately 3.5 million citizen complaints was collected and subsequently classified into 37 distinct problem categories. The prevalence of each problem type within these regions was then observed between 2018 and 2022. Computational analysis facilitated the identification of seven primary problem typologies: pollution and urban services, consumer protection, land utilization, housing acquisition, waste management, financial assistance, and pandemic control. These identified patterns demonstrated temporal stability and spatial clustering. Sophisticated statistical methods were employed to delineate the structural relationships among these problem groups. Consequently, the evolutionary trajectories of these problem types revealed predictable trends: newly emerging issues exhibited consistent growth, while established, pervasive issues gradually receded. However, occasional anomalous fluctuations were also observed. An examination of eight socioeconomic factors indicated that development-related problems—specifically land utilization, housing acquisition, and consumer protection—were strongly correlated with indicators such as land transaction volumes, housing sales, and retail consumption. This underscores the necessity for enhanced monitoring of economic activities. Furthermore, issues exhibiting trend-following behavior, such as pollution and urban services, were not primarily driven by rapid alterations but rather by the underlying stability of the socioeconomic environment. This highlights the critical importance of strategic planning and collaborative governance for resolving enduring regional challenges. Finally, event-driven problems, including waste management, financial services, and pandemic response, demonstrated minimal correlation with prevailing socioeconomic variables, suggesting their primary causation lies in unforeseen, significant occurrences. Consequently, rapid crisis management strategies are imperative for these types of incidents.

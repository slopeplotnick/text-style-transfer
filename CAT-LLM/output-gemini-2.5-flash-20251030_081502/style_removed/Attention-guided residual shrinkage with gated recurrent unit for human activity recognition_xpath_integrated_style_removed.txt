Figuring out what people are doing (that's HAR) is super important for things like video surveillance. Even though lots of ways to do HAR have been made, they haven't been very good at picking out details about where things are and how they change over time. So, in this paper, we made a new HAR system. It uses something called Multiple Residual Shrinkage Building Units (MRSBU) to get details about space, and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) to understand how things change over time. This system uses Inception v3 to grab a lot of good spatial details from video frames, and the MRSBU figures out complicated time-based details by using a smart way to filter out extra or useless information. Then, all this goes into a GRU that has a special 'attention' part for time. This attention part decides which video frames are most important and then figures out what people are doing. This model is small and works really well. It's good at working on lots of different, tough HAR datasets. Our MRSBU with GRU method got really high scores: 99.75% on UCF50, 99.55% on UCF101, and 98.95% on HMDB51. That's better than older methods like CNN-LSTM, ViT, and 3D-CNN. These numbers mean our model works really well and is strong in different video situations, even for real-life security cameras and finding weird stuff happening.

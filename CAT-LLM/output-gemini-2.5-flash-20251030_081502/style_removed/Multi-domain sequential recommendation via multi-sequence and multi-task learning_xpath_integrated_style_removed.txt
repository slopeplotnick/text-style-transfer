Lately, lots of people who study this stuff and also people who use it have been looking at predicting clicks across different areas. They've done really well with it. But even though people often do things one after another, not many people have focused on recommending stuff in different areas that follows that order, which is called MDSR. Most current methods for MDSR just put all the user actions from different areas in time order. Then, they try to guess what the user will do next in each area. But they don't really look at the separate things people do in each area, in their own specific order. Because of this, they can't really use the things that are similar or different between the areas very well. Other models, called CDSR, are usually built to make things better in just one main area, not in all of them. You could try to adapt a CDSR model to work like an MDSR one, but it would take way too long to process. Also, these models often don't care that some areas have a lot more data than others, and that can actually make things worse. So, we came up with a new way to do MDSR, which we call MML. Our MML has three main parts. One part, HSPL, learns what people like in order across different areas. Another part, ISPL, learns what people like in order within each area. The third part, MLP, handles multiple tasks and makes predictions. We also create extra user action sequences and use a specific learning technique to get fairer ideas about what people like across areas, which helps prevent bad results. In the MLP part, we use three specific tasks and a behavior checker. This makes sure that each part learns what it needs to properly and helps share what it learns across the different areas. We did a lot of tests on some public data using different basic models. We showed that our MML works much better than the best current ways, usually in two or more areas. Plus, our MML takes the same amount of time to run as the MDSR models that only look at mixed user actions. You can find the code for it here: https://github.com/plw2019/MML.

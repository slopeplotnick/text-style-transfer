There's a lot more emergency info popping up, from nature and human stuff, and we need a quick way to sort it all out on social media, for all sorts of emergencies. But the computer methods we use now (called UDA) to sort this emergency info have two big issues: they're mostly built for natural disasters, so they don't work well for other kinds of emergencies, and they struggle when the info is really different, like how people talk or what feelings they're showing. So, to fix this, we made a new system called MARDA. It's a clever way to get computers to adapt to new situations and learn from different angles at the same time. First, there's a part that looks at everything from different viewpoints; it's got bits that figure out what words mean and what emotions are in there, all working together to get a full picture. Second, we built another piece that smartly adjusts how much each viewpoint counts, which helps pull out the most important stuff from all the different kinds of data. Third, we added a part that plays a kind of 'game' to make sure it learns really well across different data types, so it's good at handling new stuff. We tried MARDA out on four huge sets of real emergency data, over 24,000 examples. It beat other top computer programs by about 7.4% and even big AI language models by about 1.6% in how accurately it sorted things. So, yeah, it's super effective for sorting all kinds of emergency events, no matter where they come from.

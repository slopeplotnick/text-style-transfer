Trust is important so that different parts of a network can talk to each other safely. But the ways we currently figure out trust have problems. They can be attacked easily, they rely on too many layers, and they don't work well when networks keep changing and don't have many connections. So, to fix these issues, someone came up with a new way to figure out trust, called DLGTrust. It uses something called a graph neural network and is strong and changes with the network. It uses changing pictures of connections to turn trust that's not direct into direct links. This helps it understand tricky connections and work better even when there isn't a lot of data. It also combines different kinds of information about where things are and how they change over time. This helps it understand all the tiny details of how everything works together. And at the same time, it uses fake attacks and special rules to make it stronger against big attacks. Tests using three real-world examples showed that DLGTrust worked at least 26.3% better overall than the usual method. Its main score, called F1-macro, was over 98%, even for parts of the network it hadn't seen before. When 10% of the network was trying to trick it, like by spreading bad rumors, good rumors, or just attacking, its F1-macro score got better by 24.5%, 23.7%, and 76.7% for each case. This means DLGTrust is tougher and better at defending itself. So, DLGTrust really helps different parts of a network talk to each other safely.

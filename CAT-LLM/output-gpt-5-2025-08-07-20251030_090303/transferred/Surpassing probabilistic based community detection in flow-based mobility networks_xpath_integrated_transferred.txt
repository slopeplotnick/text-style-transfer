Understanding how communities form in flow-based mobility networks elucidates interregional connectivity, yet prevailing methods exhibit two limitations: resolution bounds in modularity optimization and the lack of integrated local–global community detection. To address these issues, we propose a novel framework that couples surpassing probability theory with community detection to enable effective multiscale analysis in directed, weighted flow systems. Specifically, the surpassing probability-based Leiden method (SPBL) reshuffles flow weights to mitigate the Leiden algorithm’s resolution limit, thereby supporting reliable identification of macro-communities. The two-phase surpassing probability community detection (TPSPCD) algorithm further decomposes these macro-communities into smaller sub-communities while preserving essential anchor relationships that structure regional flows. The Aggregate Surpassing Degree (ASD) is introduced as a quantitative metric that evaluates the strength of intra-community connections relative to inter-community links. Empirical results reveal clear core–periphery patterns in flow-based mobility networks, with pronounced cohesion around key node centers, and the method effectively captures localized interactions. Consequently, the framework advances both theory and practice in community detection for flow-based mobility networks and provides planners with practical tools for regional development.

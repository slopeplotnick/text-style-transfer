On social media platforms, emergency-related data from natural and human-made events are increasingly generated, necessitating real-time categorization across diverse emergency types to support timely situational awareness and response. However, prevailing unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) methods are predominantly tailored to natural disasters and consequently exhibit limited generalization, while performance degrades under domain shifts characterized by divergent data distributions, linguistic styles, and affective tones. To address these limitations, we propose MARDA (Multi-View Adversarial Neural Networks for Robust Unsupervised Domain Adaptation), which integrates adversarial domain adaptation with multi-view feature learning to enhance cross-domain robustness. MARDA incorporates a cross-view processor comprising semantic and emotion processors with interactive integrators, enabling the construction of rich, complementary feature representations from multiple views. In addition, an adaptive weighted domain enhancer adjusts the contribution of each view, aggregating the most informative signals across domains to improve alignment under shift. An adversarial cross-view optimizer refines generalization through minimax training and a feature consistency constraint, promoting stable cross-domain representations. Evaluations on four real-world emergency datasets totaling 24,008 samples demonstrate that MARDA surpasses strong baselines by 7.39% and outperforms large language models by 1.59% in average F1 score, indicating effectiveness as a general solution for cross-domain emergency event classification.

This study investigates the formation, evolution, and determinants of urban problem patterns across 87 counties in Zhejiang Province, China. Drawing on approximately 3.5 million public complaints classified into 37 categories, we analyze their spatial distribution and temporal dynamics from 2018 to 2022. Grouping of the complaint data identifies seven pattern types—pollution and municipal infrastructure, consumer protection, land use, housing purchase, garbage management, financial service, and pandemic control—that exhibit persistent temporal continuity and spatial clustering. Using fuzzy logic to assign membership scores, we observe consistent trajectories in pattern change, whereby emergent patterns expand steadily while historically dominant patterns decline gradually, with occasional local exceptions. When eight socioeconomic factors are considered, development-driven patterns—land use, housing purchase, and consumer protection—align closely with indicators such as land acquisition area, property sales volume, and retail activity; therefore stronger and more systematic regulatory oversight of related economic activities is warranted. By contrast, trend-driven patterns—pollution and municipal infrastructure problems—show weaker correspondence with short-term fluctuations and stronger association with relatively stable socioeconomic conditions, suggesting that long-term planning and coordinated interventions are required to mitigate structural regional imbalances. Event-driven patterns, including garbage management, financial service, and pandemic control, display minimal linkage to the socioeconomic indicators, indicating heightened sensitivity to exogenous shocks and the need for rapid, robust crisis management.

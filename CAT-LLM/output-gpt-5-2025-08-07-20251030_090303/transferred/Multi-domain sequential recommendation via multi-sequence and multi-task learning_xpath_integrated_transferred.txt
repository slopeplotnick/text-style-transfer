Research and industrial interest in multi-domain click-through rate (CTR) prediction has intensified recently, yielding steady progress. Despite the inherently sequential nature of user behavior, limited attention has been devoted to multi-domain sequential recommendation (MDSR). Existing MDSR approaches commonly concatenate interactions from all domains into a single time-ordered stream to predict domain-specific next items, yet they neglect domain-wise sequences and thus underutilize inter-domain commonalities and discrepancies. However, cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) models are typically designed to enhance a single target domain rather than jointly optimizing all domains. Although CDSR models can be adapted to the MDSR setting either directly or indirectly, a high computational burden is incurred. Data imbalance across domains is often overlooked, which can induce negative transfer. To address these issues, we propose a novel MDSR framework, termed multi-sequence multi-task learning (MML), comprising hybrid-domain sequential preference learning (HSPL), intra-domain sequential preference learning (ISPL), and multi-task learning and prediction (MLP). HSPL learns preferences from mixed-domain sequences and leverages augmented sequences with contrastive learning to obtain less biased hybrid-domain preferences and mitigate negative transfer. ISPL captures sequential preferences within each domain. MLP employs three specific tasks and a behavior regularizer to ensure that each component specializes its own preferences while enhancing knowledge transfer across domains. Extensive experiments on public datasets with different backbone models demonstrate that MML achieves significant gains over state-of-the-art methods in two or more domains in most cases, while maintaining the same time complexity as MDSR models that only use hybrid interaction sequences. We release the source code at https://github.com/plw2019/MML.

Understanding how knowledge changes over scientific careers supports education and innovation, motivating an investigation of large-scale dynamics in topic progression across disciplines. We construct Knowledge Precedence Networks (KPNs) to uncover real-world patterns of scientific progress across 19 disciplines by tracing the research trajectories of 4,969,403 scientists and 80 million publications that are drawn from OpenAlex. A novel method, CoCiTCD, integrates co-citing networks with temporal community detection to identify research communities, select representative concepts, and derive temporal concept pairs that capture sequential dependencies in knowledge development. In Mathematics, Computer Science, and Engineering, the resulting KPNs reveal that advanced topics are underpinned by foundational concepts; for example, Algorithms bridge Mathematics and Computer Science and catalyze advances in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science. We evaluate KPNs for 303 concepts against theoretical prerequisite relations annotated by large language models to characterize how scientists engage with knowledge over time, and we observe consistently low alignments between empirical and theoretical orders. In the best case, the KPN achieves a recall of 25.77%, while a citation-based KCN reaches 26.6%; real-world topic shifts frequently depart from theoretical prerequisite sequences. Therefore, a sample variational ROC curve yields an AUC of 0.76, supporting the robust capacity of the KPN approach to capture nuanced and innovative patterns of knowledge progression across disciplines. These findings provide guidance for research planning, the design of learning paths, cross-disciplinary collaboration, and the interpretation of hierarchical knowledge structures, contributing to the Science of Science.

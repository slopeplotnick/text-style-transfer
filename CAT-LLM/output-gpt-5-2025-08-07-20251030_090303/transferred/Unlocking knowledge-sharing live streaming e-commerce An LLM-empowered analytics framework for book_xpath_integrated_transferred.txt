This study examines the influence of streamer discourse on consumer purchase behavior in live shopping, with particular attention to knowledge sharing that occurs concurrently with product promotion. While prior research indicates that what streamers say can affect sales, few studies have systematically decomposed the semantic content of streamer talk and evaluated its impact on sales prediction. We propose a sales prediction framework for knowledge-sharing live streams that integrates social support theory with fine-tuned large language models to extract multiple semantic signals from streamer discourse and leverage them for prediction. A dataset comprising 80 live streams across 35 Douyin rooms collected over two months was assembled to support empirical evaluation. In social support classification, the fine-tuned Ernie-SFT model outperformed the best baseline LLM, and increases of 11.12% in accuracy, 11.87% in weighted F1, and 7.83% in macro F1 were observed. Across sales prediction experiments employing four common classifiers, the proposed framework delivered consistent gains, and the best classifier realized improvements of 12.53% in accuracy, 10.83% in weighted F1, and 4.24% in macro F1. Therefore, the findings demonstrate that social support cues embedded in streamer discourse are strong predictors of sales, providing actionable guidance for streamers and enabling platforms to optimize operations through data-driven strategies.

Evaluating policy texts automatically is important in policy informatics.Past methods mostly set variables by hand, so they don’t scale well, and their indicators depend on specific policies, so they don’t work across different domains.To fix this, we redesign three general first-level evaluation indicators that work for any policy domain, integrate the Policy Modeling Consistency Model (PMC) into large language models (LLMs), and build automated PMC-scoring models based on LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B respectively.Using Chinese S&T policies as examples, we build the first Chinese policy evaluation dataset with 22,630 labeled policy samples and train four automated PMC indicator calculation models.Compared with the baselines, the model based on Qwen-2.5-7B performs best on policy character with an F1-score of 80.41%, and the model based on LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B performs best on policy normativity and policy function with F1-scores of 75.07% and 74.11% respectively.This lets us automatically compute PMC indices and generate a multi-input-output table for comprehensive policy analysis.Tests on biosafety policies and data governance policies show the framework works across domains.As the first framework for automated PMC evaluation, our method offers a way to do large-scale, cross-domain policy evaluation.

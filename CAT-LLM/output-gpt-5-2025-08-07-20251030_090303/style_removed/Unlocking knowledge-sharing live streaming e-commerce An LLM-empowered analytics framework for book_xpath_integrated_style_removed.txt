What streamers say has a big impact on what people buy in live shopping, especially when they share knowledge while promoting products at the same time. Past studies show their talk can affect sales, but few have pulled out different kinds of meaning from what they say and measured how that changes sales prediction. Our main work is a sales prediction setup for knowledge-sharing live streams. It combines social support theory with fine-tuned large language models to pull out multiple semantic signals from streamer talk and use them to predict sales. We collected data from 80 live streams in 35 Douyin rooms over two months. In the social support classification test, the fine-tuned Ernie-SFT model beat the best baseline LLM, with accuracy up 11.12%, weighted F1 up 11.87%, and macro F1 up 7.83%. In the sales prediction tests, we checked the setup with four common classifiers and saw consistent gains. The best classifier improved accuracy by 12.53%, weighted F1 by 10.83%, and macro F1 by 4.24%. This shows social support cues in streamer talk are strong predictors of sales, giving useful tips for streamers and helping platforms optimize with data.

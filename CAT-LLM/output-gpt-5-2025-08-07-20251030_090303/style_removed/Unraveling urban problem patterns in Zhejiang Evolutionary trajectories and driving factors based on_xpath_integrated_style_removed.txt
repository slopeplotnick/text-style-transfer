This study looks at how urban problem patterns form, change, and what causes them across 87 counties in Zhejiang Province, China.It sorts about 3.5 million public complaints into 37 categories and measures how these problems are distributed from 2018 to 2022.Grouping the data shows seven types of problem patterns: pollution and municipal infrastructure, consumer protection, land use, housing purchase, garbage management, financial service, and pandemic control.These patterns stick around over time and tend to cluster in certain areas.Using fuzzy logic to assign membership scores, we see clear trends in how these patterns change: new patterns keep growing, while dominant ones slowly decline, with occasional exceptions.Looking at eight socioeconomic factors, we find that development-driven patterns—land use, housing purchase, and consumer protection—closely match indicators like land acquisition area, property sales volume, and retail activity, so economic activities need stronger regulatory oversight.Trend-driven patterns, like pollution and municipal infrastructure problems, line up less with short-term ups and downs and more with steady socioeconomic conditions, so long-term planning and coordinated actions are needed to ease structural regional imbalances.Event-driven patterns, including garbage management, financial service, and pandemic control, show little link to socioeconomic factors, which means they are sensitive to big events and need fast crisis management.

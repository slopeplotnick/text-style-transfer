It’s important to understand how knowledge changes as scientists move through their careers because that helps education and innovation. In this study, we build Knowledge Precedence Networks (KPNs) to find real-world patterns of scientific progress across 19 disciplines, using the research paths of 4,969,403 scientists and 80 million publications from the OpenAlex dataset. We introduce a method called CoCiTCD that combines co-citing networks with temporal community detection; it identifies research communities, picks representative concepts, and derives temporal concept pairs to capture how knowledge progresses. In Mathematics, Computer Science, and Engineering, the KPNs show that basic ideas are key for advanced topics. For example, Algorithms link Mathematics and Computer Science and help drive advances in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science. We compare KPNs for 303 concepts with theoretical prerequisite relations annotated by large language models to see how scientists engage with knowledge over time. In the best case, the KPN gets a recall of 25.77%, and a citation-based KCN reaches 26.6%. These consistently low alignments mean that real-world topic shifts often don’t follow the theoretical prerequisite order. We also get an AUC of 0.76 on our sample variational ROC curve, which shows the KPN approach is robust at capturing the nuanced, innovative patterns of knowledge progression. KPNs offer useful insights for research planning, designing learning paths, working across disciplines, and understanding the hierarchical knowledge structure, contributing to the Science of Science by revealing real patterns of knowledge progression across disciplines.

Understanding how communities form in flow-based mobility networks helps us see how regions connect, but current methods have two main problems: (1) modularity optimization has resolution limits, and (2) they don’t combine local and global community detection in these networks. To fix this, we built a new framework that uses surpassing probability theory with community detection. First, the surpassing probability-based Leiden method (SPBL) reshuffles flow weights to get around the resolution limit in the Leiden algorithm so it can find macro-communities. Then, the two-phase surpassing probability community detection (TPSPCD) algorithm breaks these communities into smaller sub-communities while keeping important anchor relationships. We also add an Aggregate Surpassing Degree (ASD) metric to measure how strong connections are inside a community compared to outside. Our results show clear core-periphery patterns in flow-based mobility networks, with strong community cohesion around key node centers. This method does a good job capturing localized interactions in these networks. Overall, this work moves forward both the theory and practice of community detection in flow-based mobility networks and gives planners practical tools for regional development.

Automated policy text evaluation is important in the field of policy informatics. However, because prior methods predominantly rely on manual variable assignment, they do not scale well; moreover, their policy-specific indicators lack cross-domain generalizability. To address these limitations, we redesign three generalized first-level evaluation indicators applicable to any policy domain, integrate the Policy Modeling Consistency Model (PMC) into large language models (LLMs), and construct automated PMC-scoring models based on LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B, respectively. Using Chinese S&T policies as examples, we construct the first Chinese policy evaluation dataset, which includes 22,630 labeled policy samples, and we train four automated models for PMC indicator calculation. In comparison with the baselines, the model based on Qwen-2.5-7B achieves the best performance for policy character, with an F1-score of 80.41%, whereas the model based on LLaMA-3-Chinese-8B performs best for policy normativity and policy function, with F1-scores of 75.07% and 74.11%, respectively. Consequently, as an automated procedure for PMC evaluation, our approach enables the automatic computation of PMC indices and the generation of a multi-input-output table for comprehensive policy analysis. Moreover, tests on biosafety and data governance policies demonstrate the framework’s cross-domain applicability in policy evaluation. Finally, as the first framework for automated PMC evaluation, our methodology offers an approach for large-scale, cross-domain policy evaluation.

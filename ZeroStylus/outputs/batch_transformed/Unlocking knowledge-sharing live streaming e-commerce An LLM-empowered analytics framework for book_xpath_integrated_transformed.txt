Streamers’ discourse has a substantial impact on consumers’ purchasing decisions in live streaming e-commerce, especially when knowledge sharing occurs concurrently with product promotion. Although previous studies have shown that streamers’ discourse can influence product sales, few have systematically extracted semantic features across different dimensions and quantified their effects on sales prediction. To address this gap, our primary contribution is a sales prediction framework for knowledge-sharing live streams, namely, a setup for predicting sales in this context. This framework integrates social support theory with fine-tuned large language models to extract multiple semantic signals from streamers’ discourse and leverage them for sales prediction. For empirical evaluation, we collected data from 80 live streams across 35 Douyin rooms over a two-month period. In the social support classification experiment, the fine-tuned Ernie-SFT model, compared to the best baseline LLM, achieves the best performance, with accuracy increased by 11.12%, weighted F1 increased by 11.87%, and macro F1 increased by 7.83%. Furthermore, in the sales prediction evaluations, this setup, evaluated with four common classifiers, demonstrates consistent gains. Relative to the baselines, the best classifier achieves improvements of 12.53% in accuracy, 10.83% in weighted F1, and 4.24% in macro F1. Taken together, these findings show that social support cues in streamers’ discourse are strong predictors of sales, thereby providing actionable guidance for streamers and enabling data-driven optimization by platforms.

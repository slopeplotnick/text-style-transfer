The increasing volume of emergency data from both natural and human-made events on social media necessitates effective real-time classification across diverse emergency domains. However, current Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) methods exhibit two principal limitations: their predominant focus on natural disasters, which constrains generalizability, and difficulty in handling domain shifts arising from differences in data distributions, linguistic practices, and emotional tone. To address these limitations, we propose Multi-View Adversarial Neural Networks for Robust Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (MARDA), a novel framework that simultaneously integrates adversarial domain adaptation with multi-view feature learning. First, the framework incorporates a cross-view processor, semantic and emotional processors, and interactive integrators, thereby enabling the construction of rich multi-view feature representations. Second, an adaptive weighted domain enhancer (AWDE) is employed to adjust the contribution of each view and to integrate the most informative signals across different domains. Third, an adversarial cross-view optimizer (ACVO) is trained in a minimax setup and guided by a feature-consistency constraint to improve cross-domain generalization. Empirical evaluations on four real-world emergency datasets comprising 24,008 samples show that MARDA surpasses strong baselines by 7.39% and outperforms large language models by 1.59% in average F1 score, demonstrating its effectiveness as a general solution for cross-domain emergency event classification.

Existing trust evaluation models remain vulnerable to global attacks, depend on multi-layer stacking, and struggle to adapt to dynamic and sparse networks. To address these limitations, a dynamic and robust trust evaluation model based on graph neural networks, termed DLGTrust, is proposed. To this end, it employs dynamic line graph snapshots to map indirect trust to direct links, thereby enabling the capture of complex interactions and improved handling of sparse data. Specifically, the framework consists of two modules, including multimodal spatial feature extraction and GRU-based fusion of spatial and temporal information, enabling fine-grained modeling of complex interactions. Furthermore, adversarial perturbation injection, along with global robustness constraints, is introduced to enhance defense against global attacks. Empirical results on three real-world datasets demonstrate that the method achieves an overall performance at least 26.3% better than the baseline. Moreover, the F1-macro exceeds 98% across both observed and unobserved node scenarios. When the rates of bad-mouthing, good-mouthing, and global attacks are each set to 10%, the F1-macro improves by 24.5%, 23.7%, and 76.7%, respectively. Taken together, these findings indicate that DLGTrust exhibits enhanced robustness and defensive capability, thereby strengthening its overall defense and fortifying its capacity to withstand attacks. Accordingly, DLGTrust effectively supports secure interactions among entities.

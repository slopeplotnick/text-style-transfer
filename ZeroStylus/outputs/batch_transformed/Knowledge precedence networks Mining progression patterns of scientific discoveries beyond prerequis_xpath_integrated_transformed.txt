Understanding how knowledge evolves as scientists progress through their careers is important for advancing education and innovation. To this end, Knowledge Precedence Networks (KPNs) are constructed using the research paths of 4,969,403 scientists and 80 million publications from the OpenAlex dataset, enabling the identification of real-world patterns of scientific progress across 19 disciplines. Specifically, this study introduces Co-Citing Networks with Temporal Community Detection (CoCiTCD), a method that integrates co-citing networks with temporal community detection to identify research communities, select representative concepts, and derive temporal concept pairs, thereby capturing how knowledge progresses. Across Mathematics, Computer Science, and Engineering, the KPNs demonstrate that foundational ideas are essential for advanced topics, underscoring the primacy of basic ideas across these fields. For instance, algorithms link Mathematics and Computer Science, helping drive advances in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science. Subsequently, the KPNs for 303 concepts are compared with theoretical prerequisite relations annotated by large language models, elucidating how scientists engage with knowledge over time. In the most favorable scenario, the KPN achieves a recall of 25.77%, and the citation-based Knowledge Citation Network (KCN) attains 26.6%, representing their best-case recall values. Thus, the persistently low alignments indicate that real-world topic shifts often deviate from the theoretical prerequisite order, not adhering to the prescribed sequence. Moreover, an observed area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.76 on the sample variational ROC curve indicates that the KPN approach is robust in capturing the nuanced, innovative patterns of knowledge progression, affirming its capacity to detect such patterns. Overall, the KPNs provide valuable insights for research planning, learning path design, interdisciplinary collaboration, and understanding the hierarchical knowledge structure, thereby contributing to the Science of Science by uncovering real patterns of knowledge progression across disciplines.

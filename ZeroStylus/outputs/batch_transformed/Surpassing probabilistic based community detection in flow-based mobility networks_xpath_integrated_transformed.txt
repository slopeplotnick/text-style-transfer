Understanding how communities form in flow-based mobility networks is essential for analysing regional connectivity; however, existing methods face two main limitations: (1) modularity optimization algorithms struggle with resolution limits, and (2) they fail to integrate both local and global community detection within these networks. To address these limitations, we develop a new surpassing probability-based community detection framework that integrates surpassing probability theory into the community detection process. Specifically, the surpassing probability-based Leiden method (SPBL) first reshuffles flow weights to overcome the resolution limit in the Leiden algorithm, thereby enabling identification of macro-communities. Subsequently, the two-phase surpassing probability community detection algorithm (TPSPCD) partitions these communities into smaller sub-communities while preserving important anchor relationships. Moreover, we introduce an Aggregate Surpassing Degree (ASD) metric to quantify connection strength by contrasting ties within a community with ties outside it. Empirically, our results demonstrate clear core–periphery patterns in flow-based mobility networks, evidenced by strong community cohesion centred on key node centres. In particular, the method exhibits effective performance in these networks, enabling accurate capture of localized interactions, that is, fine-grained connectivity patterns. Overall, this work contributes along two fronts: (1) it advances both the theory and the practice of community detection in flow-based mobility networks, and (2) it provides planners with practical tools for regional development.

This study examines the formation, evolution, and drivers of urban problem patterns across 87 counties in Zhejiang Province, China. By classifying approximately 3.5 million public complaints into 37 categories, it quantifies the distribution of urban problems in these counties from 2018 to 2022 and reveals seven patterns—pollution and municipal infrastructure, consumer protection, land use, housing purchase, garbage management, financial service, and pandemic control—that are temporally persistent and spatially clustered. Using fuzzy membership scores, the evolution of these patterns indicates that newly emerging patterns continue to grow, whereas dominant patterns gradually decline, subject to occasional exceptions. Moreover, analysis of eight socioeconomic factors shows that development-driven patterns (land use, housing purchase, consumer protection) closely align with land acquisition area, property sales volume, and retail activity; therefore, stronger regulatory oversight is warranted. In contrast, trend-driven patterns (pollution and municipal infrastructure) reflect stable socioeconomic conditions rather than short-term fluctuations and therefore require long-term planning and coordinated actions to alleviate structural regional imbalances. Finally, event-driven patterns (garbage management, financial service, pandemic control) are weakly linked to socioeconomic indicators, highlighting sensitivity to major events and the need for rapid crisis management.

Bundle recommendation is an important online service that provides users with personalized collections of items in which they may be interested. While existing methods attempt to integrate multi-view information, they often fail to fully exploit the links between entities (users and bundles), particularly when distinguishing between linked and unlinked pairs. To address this limitation, our study proposes an innovative bundle recommendation approach termed multi-view multi-order contrastive learning (MIMOSA). In particular, MIMOSA simultaneously models relationships across multiple views and between different entities. To realize this, our approach employs a single graph-based contrastive learning framework that simultaneously handles links within the same type and across users and bundles by leveraging distinct closeness levels in the user–bundle graph. Specifically, MIMOSA sets specific rules for positive sample selection (PSS) and contrastive loss computation (CLC), thereby enabling it to handle different types of associations. Consequently, the framework enforces a discriminative representation space, drawing the embeddings of linked entities into close proximity while simultaneously pushing the embeddings of unlinked entities apart. In addition, our study introduces a center-matching strategy, which ensures consistency across multi-view entity representations and accelerates contrastive learning. In experiments conducted on two large-scale datasets, Youshu and NetEase, MIMOSA demonstrates superior performance relative to baseline methods. Compared to the best baseline, it achieves improvements of 2.87% (R@20) and 3.02% (N@20) on Youshu, and 5.19% (R@20) and 4.07% (N@20) on NetEase.

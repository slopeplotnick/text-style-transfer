Our objective is to create a system that facilitates the training of policies to replicate natural movement, independent of the original dataset. This system should operate across various tasks and policies, thereby eliminating the need for retraining. In this context, the development of Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP) allows the training of control policies to maintain naturalness across a wide array of tasks. Instead of focusing the training process on task-specific objectives or predefined policies, the model is initially trained to interpret motion data in a generalizable manner, independent of specific applications. Once the model is trained, it is applied to evaluate simulated motions by comparing them to behaviors observed in the original dataset. By utilizing this learned SMP as a reward mechanism, policies can be trained to execute various tasks with naturalistic behaviors. This approach avoids the pitfalls of policies trained with a single SDS evaluation, which may result in stationary standing or backward falling instead of achieving more complex movements like a full backflip. Consequently, the goal is to develop a framework that integrates reinforcement learning with score distillation techniques to generate reusable motion priors for character animation. Our system produces high-quality motions comparable to the most advanced methods available, while also enhancing their modularity and reusability. SMP enables policy training to be decoupled from reliance on the original dataset, thereby allowing policies to be trained without direct reference to the initial data. Our findings suggest that a motion prior can be developed from a model trained on extensive motion datasets, independent of any specific task orientation. Style-specific variations of the motion prior can be synthesized by employing prompts and guidance to modify it. Moreover, novel styles can be generated by combining multiple style priors, thus enabling the synthesis of styles absent from the original dataset.

In examining GraphMatch, it is evident that the integration of both random and hard negatives is essential for optimal performance. When GraphMatch is trained exclusively with random negatives, its performance is on par with that of TextMatch-small. To evaluate the impact of node features on GraphMatch, we consider several configurations: the "no-feat" version utilizes only TextMatch node embeddings, the "no-text" version employs numeric node features without text embeddings, and the "full" version combines both TextMatch embeddings and numeric node features. Notably, GraphMatch-full, when trained with hard negatives, achieves superior performance, whereas GraphMatch-no-feat performs comparably to TextMatch-large, despite being a significantly smaller model. The importance of textual context is underscored by the low accuracy observed in GraphMatch-no-text, highlighting its necessity for accurate predictions. Furthermore, it is crucial to ensure temporally accurate sampling. This involves selecting appropriate subgraphs and node features at specific points in time to train a model capable of generalizing well to new nodes. To substantiate this, we employed two training setups for GraphMatch. The no-temporal-nodes version omits time-specific node features during training but consistently uses the latest feature version available in the training set. In contrast, the no-temporal-graph version does not select time-specific subgraphs but incorporates all available graph edges during training.

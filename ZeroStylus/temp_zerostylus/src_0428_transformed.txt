Table 3, therefore, compares the evaluation scores from different configurations of VideoScience-Judge against human annotations. Although the GPT-5 Pro model exhibits less pronounced score inflation than previous versions, significant gaps persist in dimensions such as Dynamism, Coherence, and Immutability, where the model still tends to overestimate its performance. To address this issue, incorporating a checklist and requiring the model to justify each rating with evidence from key frames leads to a noticeable reduction in overestimation across multiple dimensions. Moreover, integrating computer vision tools, particularly ByteTrack, which facilitates reliable identity association across frames and provides concrete evidence for penalizing frame jumps or unexpected object transformations, substantially narrows the scoring gaps. This approach improves Coherence by 0.07 and Immutability by 0.09, relative to the baseline VideoScience-Judge model without the checklist. However, this results in score quantization and reduced discrimination among similar models, while its rule-based approach for assessing explicit violations of discrete physical laws (e.g., "Does the ball fall downward?" or "Does water flow correctly?") remains intact. Nevertheless, capturing holistic aspects of physical realism, such as consistent motion trajectories, causal relationships over time, acceleration patterns, momentum conservation, and nuances of motion smoothness that human observers naturally evaluate, remains challenging. These limitations are particularly pronounced for VideoScience-Bench, as it differs fundamentally from PhyGenBench's simplified, single-principle scenarios. VideoScience-Bench encompasses multiple scientific concepts, such as multi-object interactions and fluid dynamics, where outcomes arise from combinations of laws rather than single principles, emphasizing cascading effects and the significance of second-order dynamics.

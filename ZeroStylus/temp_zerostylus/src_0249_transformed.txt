We propose the Autonomous Domain-Oriented Relevance Engine (ADORE), a system designed to address complex relevance tasks in e-commerce settings. ADORE integrates three key components: a domain rule-aware relevance discrimination (RD) module, an error-type-aware data synthesis (DS) module, and a key-attribute-enhanced knowledge distillation (KD) module. Specifically, the RD module employs a Chain-of-Thought (CoT)-based reasoning framework within a Large Language Model (LLM) to autonomously identify and construct domain-specific hard samples by analyzing the relevance of online product exposures. Meanwhile, the DS module incorporates an error-type-aware generation model to produce diverse challenging samples, thereby improving the system’s robustness against variability in data. Moreover, the KD module explicitly utilizes key attribute features extracted through CoT analysis to enhance knowledge distillation, which, in turn, refines the reasoning and representational capabilities of the online student model. To rigorously evaluate ADORE’s performance, we conducted extensive experiments using both large-scale offline datasets and online A/B testing. The results demonstrate that ADORE significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, delivering notable improvements across both offline and online metrics. These findings underscore the practical applicability and effectiveness of ADORE in addressing the complexities of relevance modeling within e-commerce environments. Building upon this foundation, we introduce a CoT-based approach to fine-tune the relevance discrimination model. Previous studies [9, 21] have shown that CoT prompting enhances the reasoning precision of LLMs. Leveraging this insight, we utilized GPT-4o to generate domain-specific CoT-labeled data. This process was designed to replicate the human annotation paradigm, wherein GPT-4o was provided with detailed annotation guidelines and a set of exemplary CoT instances. Consequently, this approach yielded a dataset enriched with intricate reasoning chains, mirroring the depth and nuance of human annotations. However, to ensure the precision and reliability of the labeled data, we included only query-product pairs that aligned with verified human annotations, excluding any CoT data that deviated from these standards. This curated dataset formed the basis for our final training set. Subsequently, we employed the refined dataset to fine-tune the LLaMA-3 [4] model. Through the optimization of standard Language Model loss, this fine-tuning process enhanced the model’s capability and adaptability within the specified parameters, further solidifying its utility in relevance discrimination tasks.

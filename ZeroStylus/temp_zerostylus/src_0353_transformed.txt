A Software Product Line (SPL) involves the systematic organization of a collection of related software systems that share common features, with the primary objective of enabling the efficient and accelerated development of new software solutions. Deriving an SPL from an existing code base—comprising a collection of related software systems—is a complex and resource-intensive process, often requiring significant time and effort. Moreover, such a transformation can substantially impact established software development practices within an organization. This paper discusses the initial steps undertaken to implement this transformation in practice, as part of a collaborative project with ITK - Predict and Decide, a small enterprise aiming to transition its existing code base into an SPL to support systematic and efficient software production. As researchers, our role was to provide guidance and support throughout the various stages of this migration. The company's development team operates around a central framework called Cross, which promotes software reuse and shares similarities with an SPL. However, this framework lacks the formal models and processes characteristic of a traditional SPL. To address this, we proposed a gradual evolution of their code base and engineering practices to facilitate the adoption of new methodologies and to better align with SPL principles. In preparation for this transition, we conducted assessments to evaluate employees’ perceptions of the proposed changes and to provide recommendations for a smoother implementation process. This study presents findings from these preliminary investigations, which included interviews with company personnel to explore their views on current work practices, anticipated benefits of the SPL transformation, and potential challenges or concerns related to its adoption. The results indicate that most participants were supportive of the transition to an SPL and expressed a strong willingness to actively contribute to its implementation. Developers, in particular, viewed the transition as an exciting opportunity, perceiving it as both a technical and managerial challenge. However, individuals involved in business-related activities expressed more cautious perspectives, citing concerns about potential risks such as increased complexity in software maintenance and perceived threats to creativity, particularly in user interface and user experience design. These findings underscore the varied perspectives within the organization and highlight the importance of addressing both technical and organizational challenges during the SPL migration process.

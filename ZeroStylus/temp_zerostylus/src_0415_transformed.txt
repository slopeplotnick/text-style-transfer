Our analysis indicates that DeepSeek-V3.2 excels in both speed and intelligence; however, the underlying mechanisms contributing to its efficiency remain unexplored. To enhance the model's efficiency, DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA) is implemented, thus enabling the model to process extensive information more rapidly without compromising its capacity to manage long sequences. Furthermore, by employing a robust learning algorithm and incorporating additional computational resources post-training, DeepSeek-V3.2 is able to perform on par with GPT-5. Notably, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale demonstrates superior performance compared to GPT-5 and achieves parity with Gemini-3.0-Pro in addressing complex challenges, as evidenced by its receipt of top awards at the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad and the International Olympiad in Informatics. Moreover, the Large-Scale Agentic Task Synthesis Pipeline introduces an innovative framework for generating extensive training datasets, thereby enhancing the model's proficiency in tool utilization and strategic reasoning in complex scenarios. This is of great significance as it enhances the model's proficiency in comprehending and adhering to instructions. The introduction of reasoning models such as DeepSeek-AI in 2024 and OpenAI in 2025 marked a significant advancement for Large Language Models, thereby enhancing their capabilities considerably. In recent developments, however, we observe an emerging divergence. In the realm of AI development, open-source models such as MiniMax, MoonShot, Qwen, and ZhiPu-AI continue to demonstrate notable advancements; nevertheless, proprietary models developed by organizations like Anthropic, DeepMind, and OpenAI are progressing at an even more accelerated pace, reflecting a widening disparity in their respective trajectories. Consequently, in the realm of complex problem-solving, closed-source models exhibit superior performance, thereby widening the disparity between them and their open-source counterparts. Open-source models exhibit three primary limitations: first, their predominant reliance on the conventional vanilla attention mechanism, which proves inadequate for processing extended sequences of information, thus constraining their scalability and practical applicability. Additionally, insufficient computational resources post-training undermine performance on complex tasks, where distinguishing correct from incorrect reasoning is paramount. Finally, open-source models exhibit a significant deficiency in their ability to generalize and adhere to instructions compared to closed-source models, thereby reducing their applicability in practical scenarios.

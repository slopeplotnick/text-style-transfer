We integrate a sequential enhancement mechanism into PPTPilot, thus enabling it to progressively refine its predictive accuracy over time. Building upon this framework, the agent proposes modifications to XML files, which are subsequently transformed into a provisional PPT file. Subsequently, a verifier assesses the PPT against the original instructions and provides feedback if discrepancies are identified. In response to this feedback, the agent systematically revises the PowerPoint presentation, thereby addressing errors with precision. Despite the simplicity of our design, it demonstrates efficacy and efficiency in PPT editing, outperforming existing tools in the domain. Consequently, we propose PPTPilot as a foundational framework to facilitate further exploration and advancement in the domain of PPT editing. For evaluation, we employ advanced vision-language models, such as Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT 5. The primary results presented in the main comparison (Table 3) are derived from GPT-5; however, supplementary materials provide further details regarding Gemini 2.5 Pro and additional implementation information. In our evaluations, we juxtaposed PPTPilot with alternative PPT utilities such as ChatGPT in its extended thinking mode, Gemini-CLI, GPT-Agent (functioning akin to a computer with desktop capabilities), MiniMax Agent (noted for its proficiency in multimodal tasks and promoted as an advantageous PPT tool), alongside PPTAgent and Poster2Agent. The evaluation process utilized a subset of 25 samples, constrained by the strict usage limits and elevated costs associated with tools such as ChatGPT and MiniMax Agent, to ensure adherence to budgetary considerations. Addressing the most challenging tasks revealed areas where both PPTPilot and ChatGPT underperformed; therefore, we incorporated additional cases to encompass a broader range of scenarios. Specific cases are detailed in the supplementary materials, which provide further insights into these evaluations.

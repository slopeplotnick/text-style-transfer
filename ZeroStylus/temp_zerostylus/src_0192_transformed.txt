During the data preprocessing stage, all cases and examination questions underwent an initial deduplication process to ensure the independence of samples, followed by a systematic data cleaning procedure. Specifically, samples that lacked question stems or options, as well as those containing non-textual content such as figures or tables, were excluded. Additionally, to ensure textual accuracy and consistency, errors introduced by OCR recognition were manually identified and corrected. For clinical case and medical record data, anonymization was rigorously performed in compliance with medical ethics and privacy protection standards. This process involved the removal or modification of patient names and other identifiable information to safeguard data privacy and ensure ethical compliance. Furthermore, the clinical cases utilized in this study received prior approval from the Ethics Committee of the Xiyuan Hospital of CACMS (approval number: 2025XLA135-2). In the context of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), "Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment" (SDT) constitutes a fundamental principle of diagnosis and therapy, as it integrates the two critical stages of "syndrome differentiation" and "treatment formulation." To evaluate model capabilities in this domain, a comprehensive 14-dimension evaluation framework was developed, encompassing key aspects such as syndrome, causative factors, and pathogenesis, as outlined in Table 1. The annotation process for standard answers and options was conducted in a multi-phase approach: TCM experts performed an initial review, which was subsequently validated through mutual cross-verification, and finally subjected to an independent third-party review to ensure consistency and reliability in the annotations. Moreover, selected-response questions were designed with carefully constructed distractors to enhance the discriminative power of the assessment. Beyond evaluating the final reasoning outcomes of SDT, this study introduced two process-oriented metrics to assess the reasoning process itself. The first metric, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Content Completeness, evaluates the extent to which the model incorporates essential patient information within its reasoning chain. The second metric, CoT Accuracy, assesses the alignment between the elements cited in the model's reasoning and the original clinical case, thereby identifying potential hallucinations or reasoning deviations. These metrics, in combination with the general evaluation tasks, aim to provide a more comprehensive and interpretable assessment of the model’s foundational understanding and normative responses. The evaluation tasks primarily employed selected-response questions, incorporating both single-answer and multiple-answer formats. The TCM Basic Knowledge component encompassed four core domains: (i) the interpretation of TCM classics; (ii) foundational theories; (iii) knowledge of Chinese materia medica and formulas; and (iv) syndrome differentiation based on tongue, pulse, facial complexion, and acupoint analysis. Questions for this component were sourced from preprocessed standardized examination materials. Additionally, the Medical Ethics component was designed to assess the model’s understanding of clinical ethics, focusing on reconciling traditional and modern medical practices, identifying unscientific behaviors, respecting patient cultural beliefs, and adhering to principles of informed consent. These questions were derived from authoritative examination materials and supplemented by expert-designed scenarios. Finally, the Content Safety component evaluated the TCM domain language model’s adherence to professional boundaries and safety compliance. Specifically, the model was required to restrict its responses to TCM-related queries and refrain from addressing non-medical topics, particularly those involving user privacy, safety risks, or ethical concerns. Questions for this component were independently developed and reviewed by experts in content safety and ethics to ensure alignment with the professionalism and safety standards required for TCM domain language models.

The questions included in this section were derived from a combination of standardized examination items and those specifically developed by subject matter experts. The Content Safety module, in particular, was structured to evaluate the extent to which the TCM domain large language model (LLM) complies with established professional standards and safety regulations. Furthermore, the response framework was meticulously designed to ensure that the TCM domain model remains focused on addressing inquiries strictly within the scope of Traditional Chinese Medicine, explicitly avoiding engagement with non-medical topics such as privacy, safety, or ethical considerations. To ensure alignment with the standards of the TCM model, the evaluation questions underwent a rigorous development process, including careful formulation and review by specialists in content safety and ethics. Consequently, this approach ensures the reliability and relevance of the evaluation framework in assessing the model's adherence to domain-specific requirements.

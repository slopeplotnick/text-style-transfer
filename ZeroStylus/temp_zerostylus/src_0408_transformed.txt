The data illustrates that the automated generation of knowledge graphs facilitates an enhanced comprehension of the influence of training data on the outputs produced by the model. This is crucial as it aids in elucidating the functioning of models, verifying copyright concerns, and enhancing the transparency of datasets. For instance, an image can be generated in a specific style, such as "Ghibli-style," without replicating the exact images from the model's training data. Instead, the model derives the general aesthetics of the style by synthesizing from a collection of images. However, while the model encompasses a distinct set of features for each generated image, many of these features—attributes—are utilized across images due to their non-replicative nature. Determining the precise influence of specific training images on the output remains challenging. Instead, the influence is manifested in a broader manner through the dissemination of shared concepts such as textures, thematic elements, and compositional structures within the image. Therefore, the influence of a style on the model can be discerned by analyzing patterns and underlying meanings, rather than solely attempting to align with specific objects. In our experiment, we employed a previously established method for the automated construction of knowledge graphs. Initially, we analyzed frames extracted from prominent Studio Ghibli films. Each frame was encoded through a defined system, decomposing it into structured elements of information within a specified framework. Consequently, the construction of a "Ghibli World" knowledge graph was facilitated by these elements, encapsulating the essential aspects of the studio's visual aesthetic. Although our analysis assessed stylistic representation using frames from only ten of their films, the dataset effectively encapsulates their style.

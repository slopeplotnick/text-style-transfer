Our comprehensive evaluations, conducted using UniFact across diverse datasets, model families (e.g., LLaMA, Qwen), and fact-checking methodologies, yield several critical insights that directly address our research questions. First, addressing RQ1, we systematically examine the relationship between model architectures and task requirements, demonstrating that the effectiveness of any approach is highly dependent on the interplay of these factors. Furthermore, we find that no single paradigm consistently outperforms others; rather, performance varies significantly based on the underlying large language model (LLM) and the specific demands of the task. Second, in response to RQ2, leveraging these findings, we provide compelling empirical evidence that integrating diverse methodologies leads to more effective outcomes. Specifically, Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV) approaches exhibit complementary strengths by identifying distinct categories of factual errors, underscoring their synergistic contributions to assessing truthfulness. Consequently, addressing RQ3, we quantitatively demonstrate that hybrid strategies combining HD and FV consistently outperform their individual application, establishing a novel state-of-the-art framework for factual error detection. These findings have significant implications for both researchers and practitioners. For the research community, they highlight the importance of adopting a unified research agenda that emphasizes collaboration and integration rather than isolated approaches. For practitioners, our results underscore the necessity of combining internal, model-based signals with external, evidence-based validation methods to ensure the reliability of factual assessments. In summary, this study contributes to the evaluation landscape in four key ways: (1) Analytical: We identify and analyze the conceptual and methodological distinctions between HD and FV, revealing their implications for advancing AI-driven factuality assessment. (2) Methodological: We introduce UniFact, a unified evaluation framework that enables equitable and scalable comparisons between HD and FV approaches. (3) Empirical: Through large-scale analyses, we present strong evidence that HD and FV capture distinct yet complementary dimensions of factuality. (4) Practical: We demonstrate that hybrid strategies combining HD and FV achieve superior performance. To establish a formal understanding, we define the concepts of Fact Verification and Hallucination Detection in § 2.1 and § 2.2, respectively, and articulate a methodology for their integrated evaluation in § 2.3. While these terms are often conflated in existing literature, distinguishing them is crucial. As outlined in the introduction and illustrated in Figure 1, the key distinction lies in the scope of assessment: Fact Verification evaluates the semantic truthfulness of text irrespective of its source, whereas Hallucination Detection assesses the factual accuracy of text specifically generated by LLMs. This differentiation provides a clearer framework for evaluating factuality across diverse contexts.

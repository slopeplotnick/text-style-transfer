The facilitation of developing new products with innovative concepts tailored for each analogous business market is crucial. Such examples can motivate teams in adopting Software Product Line (SPL) practices. Minimizing risks is vital to limiting resistance to change, and this discussion outlines several effective methods to reduce them. All concerns, whether associated with these risks or independent of them, must be taken into account, as they can hinder effective adoption. Certain risks or challenges frequently recur in SPL adoption. However, these challenges are not insurmountable if one navigates the transformation with careful consideration of these factors. Moreover, a sense of complexity often arises due to a misunderstanding of Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE). For individuals familiar with SPLE, certain challenges are inherent in SPL. Nonetheless, the inherent complexity of SPL should not surpass that of the codebase with which the IT team is already familiar. Conversely, SPL employs models that explicitly elucidate concepts of variability, thereby facilitating the comprehension and management of complexity. The necessity for training and demonstrating functionality is intrinsically connected to this perception. Thus, SPL implementation facilitates more efficient integration of new features and encourages innovative development efforts. By addressing this, we mitigate the risk of losing time dedicated to creativity, as emphasized by UX professionals, and the time allocated for new development efforts. We propose that comprehensive non-regression testing can mitigate the occurrence of side effects. Addressing the concerns expressed by the team regarding their perceived weaknesses and strengths can mitigate fears and foster a more constructive dialogue. Furthermore, they are perceived as a source of bias by the individuals interviewed. It is understandable to perceive SPL as a complex and inflexible system, rendering the new approach ineffective. However, transparency in SPL, achieved through comprehensive explanations and thorough documentation, alongside the inclusion of all project participants, can facilitate the restoration of trust. The initiation of inclusive practices begins by inviting individuals to partake in transformative processes. Promoting and reinforcing the practice of consistent reporting at all stages is crucial. This facilitates individuals in feeling acknowledged and comprehended, thereby enabling the provision of assistance when required.

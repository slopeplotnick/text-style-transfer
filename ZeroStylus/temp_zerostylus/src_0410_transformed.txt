Our framework demonstrates robust compatibility with diverse models and algorithms, although the underlying mechanisms enabling this versatility remain unexplored. Consistent improvements observed with both the Qwen2.5 and Llama models confirm that our methodology is compatible with a range of architectures. Notably, the smaller Llama-3.2-1B model achieves improved accuracy when utilizing our OptPO-SFT and OptPO methods compared to conventional approaches, demonstrating the effectiveness of our strategy across varying model scales. Moreover, OptPO exhibits broad compatibility with various reinforcement learning algorithms, including PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, consistently surpassing TTRL in both accuracy and efficiency, thereby validating its effectiveness across diverse RL methods. Furthermore, the OptPO variants employing reinforcement learning tend to demonstrate enhanced accuracy, particularly in mathematical reasoning assessments, owing to their superior utilization of feedback from rollouts compared to the supervised fine-tuning approach. For SFT-style training, OptPO-SFT demonstrates consistent enhancements over TTSFT in all scenarios, indicating that our adaptive pseudo-labeling effectively augments the training process during evaluation. In addition, the integration of adaptive early-stopping with BSPRT enhances efficiency while maintaining accuracy; OptPO effectively balances accuracy with computational demands; and OptPO-SFT provides a rapid alternative for adaptation when reinforcement learning-style updates are infeasible. While the integration of these components establishes an effective mechanism for training models during testing phases of reasoning tasks, the specifics of how this approach enhances model performance remain to be further investigated. We present OptPO, the inaugural framework that proficiently optimizes policies during testing, effectively addressing the inefficiency inherent in majority-vote-based rollouts within test-time reinforcement learning for large language models. Moreover, OptPO employs a Bayesian test for early-stopping to dynamically adjust rollouts based on empirical evidence rather than predetermined limits, thereby ensuring the generation of reliable pseudo-labels while minimizing unnecessary sampling. Our framework demonstrates ease of use and compatibility with standard policy optimization methods, including PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, alongside supervised fine-tuning during testing phases. Ultimately, OptPO demonstrates consistent accuracy retention or enhancement across various tests and models while concurrently achieving computational efficiency.

This study seeks to illustrate both the feasibility and the importance of efficient, label-free adaptation, as large language models are progressively utilized in intricate and unpredictable real-world situations. Consequently, we propose that OptPO will function as a foundational framework for future exploration into efficient learning during the testing phase. As large language models increasingly encounter complex scenarios, the need for adaptable methodologies becomes paramount. Therefore, the introduction of Optimal Rollout Allocation for Test-time Policy Optimization (OptPO) addresses the prevailing challenges associated with the efficiency-accuracy trade-off. By reframing reward estimation as a sequential hypothesis testing problem, OptPO employs a Bayesian Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT), halting sampling once posterior evidence meets user-defined error tolerance thresholds. This approach not only ensures optimal allocation of computational resources but also repurposes accumulated rollouts for immediate policy optimization, such as through PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) or GRPO (Shao et al., 2024). Moreover, OptPO's compatibility with supervised fine-tuning for test-time training exemplifies its versatility. Ultimately, we anticipate that OptPO will catalyze further research on test-time policy optimization, thereby enhancing the adaptability of large language models in real-world applications.

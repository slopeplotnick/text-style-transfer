The application of a multimodal language model enables effective interaction with visual content. After generating the images, we engaged in a structured extraction process to semantically describe their content by employing the open-source model LLaMA 3.2 Vision, accessed via the Ollama framework. This model was chosen for its notable ability to process image inputs and produce coherent, detailed descriptions in natural language, making it well-suited for transforming visual data into organized informational structures. While the primary objective of our task was the extraction of structured triples, this process also parallels the generation of image captions. Recent evaluations of multimodal language models demonstrate comparable performance in visual comprehension tasks, with all models showing considerable robustness. However, as our study focuses on the structure and comparative organization of the extracted knowledge, we selected a single model as the focal example to ensure consistency. To guide and constrain the triple extraction process, we utilized the Fashionpedia ontology, a comprehensive taxonomy specifically developed for fashion-related visual understanding. This ontology provides a structured framework by defining object categories, components, and attributes relevant to garments and accessories. By conditioning the multimodal language model on this ontology during the extraction process, we ensured that the resulting knowledge graphs adhered to a consistent and domain-specific format. To further assess the significance of the ontology, we conducted an additional experiment in which triples were extracted without providing the ontology as input. As shown in Table 1, prompting the model with the ontology resulted in a higher number of extracted triples per image, encompassing a broader range of relationships. In contrast, the absence of ontology guidance led the model to identify only a limited set of prominent visual features, thereby reducing the richness and scope of the generated information. Therefore, the use of an ontology proves essential for obtaining detailed, coherent, and domain-specific outputs, which are crucial for conducting graph-based comparisons of extracted knowledge.

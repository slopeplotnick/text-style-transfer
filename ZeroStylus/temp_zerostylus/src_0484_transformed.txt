The effectiveness of various methodologies, including RL-Trained, Mixture-of-Inputs, CoLaR, and LiteReason, is evaluated to assess their performance. Specifically, this approach utilizes the Qwen 2.5 7B-Instruct to generate chapters derived from the plans of each method, subsequently conducting pairwise evaluations wherein participants select between outcomes. We evaluate these methods on both the Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks, employing a specific model to ascertain the superior method, with further details available in Appendix E. While LiteReason was evaluated against two baseline models derived from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct—one utilizing a straightforward prompted approach and the other leveraging reinforcement learning (RL)—the comparative analysis highlights distinct methodological differences without exploring the underlying factors driving performance variations. Crucially, no advanced reasoning features were employed by either of these baselines. It is observed that LiteReason, when employed without reasoning prompts (RP) and thereby bypassing intermediate reasoning steps, results in a prolonged reasoning process and exhibits inferior performance compared to the RL-trained baseline. In alignment with recent advancements in latent reasoning studies, it is proposed to explore the potential of enhancing latent reasoning by adjusting prompts or increasing the likelihood of employing specific markers. LiteReason demonstrated only marginal performance improvements, achieving a 0.8% gain on Flawed Fictions and 58% on NCP, likely due to its similarity to the RL-trained baseline while exploring reasoning paths not utilized by the base model. Probabilities of performance similarity were evaluated for the training-free methods, such as Mixture-of-Inputs (MoI) and Soft Thinking, relative to the untrained model. In the context of the NCP task, Soft Thinking demonstrated a marginal improvement over the baseline with an increased token usage, whereas MoI exhibited a decline in performance alongside a reduction in token count. Lastly, in assessing performance on the Flawed Fictions task, it is observed that deviations from the baseline remain minor and fall within the margin of error.

Further investigation is necessary to firmly establish that training-free methods can consistently enhance performance and efficiency across various tasks and reasoning styles. However, the underlying principles that govern these improvements have yet to be thoroughly examined. Moreover, while these methods show promise, they currently exhibit performance that is quite similar to the untrained model, as demonstrated by Mixture-of-Inputs (MoI) and Soft Thinking. On specific tasks, such as the NCP task, Soft Thinking performs marginally better than the baseline but generates slightly more tokens, whereas MoI performs worse with slightly fewer tokens. In contrast, on the Flawed Fictions task, the differences in performance relative to the baseline fall within confidence intervals. Therefore, future research is required to ensure that the efficiency and performance gains are more consistent and pronounced across different tasks and reasoning styles for these training-free approaches.

Energy consumption is an increasingly critical concern across various domains, ranging from mobile devices to large-scale data centers. Consequently, developers require precise and comprehensive information regarding the energy consumption of their software in order to address inefficiencies effectively. Historically, approaches to energy analysis have predominantly focused on broader components, such as specific functions or entire programs, rather than examining individual source code statements. Furthermore, these methods primarily relied on point estimates of CPU energy consumption, often neglecting the contributions of other hardware components. This limited scope has consequently constrained their applicability for statistical reasoning and hindered explainability. To address these limitations, we propose a novel methodology that extends beyond CPU-centric energy measurement and avoids reliance on point estimates. Specifically, our approach focuses on predicting the energy consumption of statically typed programming languages operating on the Java Virtual Machine (JVM), including Java, Kotlin, and Scala. Central to this methodology is the analysis of energy consumption associated with bytecode patterns, which represent the transformation of source code statements into Java bytecode. With these energy measurements, we construct a statistical model that incorporates Bayesian statistical methods, as opposed to frequentist approaches. The adoption of Bayesian methods enables the prediction of energy usage through probabilistic distributions and facilitates the examination of individual factors contributing to energy consumption. The developed model identifies four factors influencing energy consumption: three derived statically from the code—data size, data type, and operation performed—and one hardware-related factor—the device executing the code. To validate this methodology, we implemented it using Java, focusing on the analysis of Java bytecode patterns, and conducted evaluations on previously unseen programs. The findings reveal that all four factors significantly affect energy consumption, with two notable observations: (1) devices of the same model can exhibit substantial variability in energy usage, and (2) operations and data types have a pronounced impact on energy consumption. Moreover, experimental results demonstrate that the predicted energy consumption closely aligns with the actual energy usage of programs, thereby substantiating the efficacy of the proposed approach. In summary, this work introduces a robust methodology, rather than a specific tool, for constructing energy models. These models can serve as foundational resources for future research endeavors, such as energy-aware program analysis or verification tools, to enhance the accuracy of energy consumption estimations.

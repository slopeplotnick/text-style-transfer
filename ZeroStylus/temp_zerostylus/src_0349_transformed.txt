Large language models are expanding in both size and complexity, which places significant demands on memory and computational resources during both training and deployment. Quantization emerges as a solution to these constraints by enabling data storage with fewer bits and substituting expensive floating-point operations with more efficient integer arithmetic. There are two primary methodologies for quantization: post-training quantization (PTQ) and quantization-aware training (QAT). PTQ is advantageous due to its simplicity and the absence of retraining requirements; however, its performance significantly declines with extremely low bit usage, such as 1 or 2 bits, due to severe accuracy degradation. This degradation can result from factors such as limited calibration data, inadequate scaling methods, or the disruption of ranges by certain channels. Conversely, QAT integrates the quantization process during the training phase, allowing the model to adapt to the quantized pattern. Although QAT tends to outperform PTQ under low bit constraints, it requires substantial time investment and may exhibit instability due to the limited adaptability of data. The application of FAIRY2 on popular open-source language models, such as the LLaMA series, has proven its effectiveness in achieving desirable outcomes. Our methodology includes transforming any layer into an equivalent form without altering the model's pre-quantization functionality, continuously training the model at low bit levels with a specialized quantizer while utilizing existing checkpoints, and reducing errors by applying the same technique to the residual data, thereby significantly minimizing quantization errors.

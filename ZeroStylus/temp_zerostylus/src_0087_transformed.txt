Moreover, an examination of the results from image cropping at various resolutions, alongside the visualization of semantic similarity maps, provides a clear insight into the underlying reasons for this phenomenon. Specifically, when the entire object is divided into distinct segments, some segments exhibit higher semantic similarity, as calculated by VisRAG, while others demonstrate lower similarity. Consequently, after filtering, only the segments with higher similarity are retained. However, this process can compromise the integrity of the object as a whole, thereby interfering with the MLLM’s ability to provide precise evaluations. In response to these challenges, we introduce a novel framework termed Multi-resolution Retrieval Detection (MRD). The core innovation of MRD lies in its utilization of multiple resolutions across different scales to more effectively localize regions containing target objects. This approach enables subsequent search processes to readily identify image crops corresponding to target objects, thereby eliminating irrelevant distractions and enhancing the perceptual understanding of high-resolution images by MLLMs. Building on the findings from subsection 3.2, we propose that employing varied resolutions for semantic similarity computation proves more effective for objects of diverse sizes and spatial positions. Inspired by this notion, we first introduce a straightforward yet effective method called Multi-resolution Semantic Fusion. This method involves computing semantic similarity maps at varying resolutions on a local scale and employing a fusion strategy to refine and enhance the accuracy of the similarity results. Furthermore, to facilitate the precise localization of target objects, we incorporate an open-vocabulary object detection model that systematically examines the entire high-resolution image using a sliding window approach. This model generates confidence scores for segments potentially containing the objects. By integrating these confidence scores with the multi-resolution semantic similarity maps, our method not only enhances the precision of locating target areas but also effectively distinguishes subtle variations between the crops within those regions.

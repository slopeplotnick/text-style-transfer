The enhanced performance of this system is primarily attributed to its capacity for dynamically generating task-specific prompts that are informed by current operational conditions. These conditions include the system’s real-time state, robot capabilities, workload distribution, and the specific phase of the task. Consequently, this method eliminates the necessity of appending an additional 5,000–6,000 example tokens to every prompt, which were previously redundant and irrelevant to the task at hand. By focusing exclusively on task-relevant elements, the approach significantly reduces the number of tokens processed, thereby avoiding extraneous steps such as task decomposition or the generation of surplus code. This refinement alone previously accounted for approximately 14,000 tokens per task. Moreover, the structured provision of context further reduces the number of tokens required in prompts from 6,700 to 1,100, thereby greatly enhancing the efficiency of prompt generation. The system's efficacy in allocating tasks among multiple robots was rigorously evaluated based on metrics such as task success rates, workload distribution equity, and adaptability to diverse operational scenarios. To assess the robustness of the framework, testing was conducted across three distinct configurations, each involving robots with varying capabilities while maintaining a constant task sequence comprising 36 tasks. Each configuration emphasized a different set of robot capabilities by adjusting success probability matrices, aligning with the principle that optimal system performance requires a strategic allocation of robotic proficiencies. Notably, the system exhibited the ability to sustain commendable performance levels while ensuring equitable workload distribution among robots. This balance is particularly critical in contexts such as construction, where both operational efficiency and the sustainable and fair allocation of resources are paramount. In the configuration termed *Medium Excels*, robots with intermediate skill levels achieved the highest overall performance, completing tasks with a success rate of 0.878. However, the system predominantly allocated tasks to the robot demonstrating broad operational proficiency, which inadvertently disrupted equitable workload distribution. This imbalance underscores the inherent trade-off between maximizing performance and ensuring fairness, as the concentrated allocation poses challenges to the system’s long-term sustainability. The framework's feature-specific success rates further highlight performance variations: meticulous tasks achieved a success rate of 0.925, dexterous tasks 0.866, and tasks requiring substantial exertion 0.85. While this approach delivers optimal performance outcomes, the observed workload imbalance emphasizes the need to carefully navigate the performance-fairness trade-off in task allocation decisions.

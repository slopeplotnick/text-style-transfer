Given the constraints of real-time latency and computational cost, deploying our large language model (LLM) or a deep cross-encoder-based BERT classifier at scale for online search systems is not feasible. To address this limitation, we adopt a teacher-student distillation framework, as illustrated in Figure 1, to train a lightweight student relevance model. Specifically, our online model is a simplified, shallow cross-encoder BERT designed for efficient inference. To ensure distributional consistency between the teacher and student models, we select a deep cross-encoder BERT, which is also an encoder-only architecture, as the teacher model rather than opting for an autoregressively trained LLM. Moreover, to enhance the ability of the BERT-based model to discern query-product relevance, we incorporate key attribute information into its input. This information is mined by the relevance reasoning LLM from the relevance determination (RD) module, thereby explicitly enriching the student model's knowledge. However, given the noise and redundancy often present in the lengthy chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning outputs, we introduce a CoT rewriting model. This model extracts and refines product attribute descriptions that are either aligned or misaligned with the query, filtering out irrelevant information and restructuring key insights into a concise, structured format. This refinement enables the lightweight BERT-based student model to focus more effectively on discriminative reasoning. Since the student model cannot access CoT outputs during real-time inference, we integrate CoT-derived information exclusively into the teacher BERT model to maintain consistency between offline and online settings. Subsequently, the teacher model transfers this enriched knowledge to the student model through representation alignment. The knowledge transfer ensures that the student model benefits from the teacher's deeper understanding while remaining computationally efficient for real-time use. Finally, due to the absence of publicly available datasets for the Chinese e-commerce relevance task, we conduct experiments using a large-scale real-world dataset collected from search logs on the JD application.

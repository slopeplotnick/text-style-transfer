An empirical survey was conducted on seven model merging methodologies aimed at mitigating social bias, utilizing three bias benchmarks and SuperGLUE across 13 large language models (LLMs). Among these approaches, SLERP with moderate interpolation settings was identified as the most balanced technique, effectively reducing bias without compromising downstream accuracy. Evaluating the efficacy of these methodologies on tasks such as reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning is essential for ensuring robust bias mitigation. Recently, model merging has emerged as a promising strategy for leveraging the strengths of diverse pre-trained models without the expense of retraining (Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024). Specifically, this approach integrates parameters from multiple models to create a unified model that synthesizes and retains the knowledge or skills of its components. One foundational method, linear averaging, introduced by Wortsman et al. (2022), simplifies model merging by treating parameter weights as vectors and combining them through arithmetic means. This technique offers a cost-efficient way to incorporate diverse expertise, as it utilizes existing fine-tuned models without additional computational overhead. Moreover, subsequent studies have extended this concept by introducing adaptive weighting mechanisms, such as those proposed by Matena and Raffel (2022) and Lee et al. (2025), which assign weights inversely proportional to Fisher information to ensure coherent model integration. Alternatively, merging strategies based on sphere interpolation conceptualize parameter vectors as points mapped onto a spherical surface (Shoemake, 1985; Goddard et al., 2024; Grove and Karcher, 1973). For example, SLERP, introduced by Shoemake (1985), performs interpolation between two models, while the Karcher Mean, developed by Grove and Karcher (1973), iteratively calculates the Riemannian centroid across multiple models. Extending this framework, NuSLERP, introduced by Goddard et al. (2024), incorporates per-tensor normalization to mitigate norm drift, thereby enhancing stability during transformations with minimal computational cost. Inspired by these merging methodologies, Ilharco et al. (2022) proposed task arithmetic under the concept of "task vector," wherein the difference between a pre-trained LLM and its fine-tuned counterpart is represented as a vector. This innovative approach exemplifies the practical application of model merging techniques for addressing task-specific challenges.

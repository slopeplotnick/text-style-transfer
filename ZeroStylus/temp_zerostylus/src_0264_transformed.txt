The challenges presented by climate change and the transition to alternative energy sources are exceedingly complex, surpassing the scope of any single scientific discipline. Nevertheless, an academic degree specifically focused on Mineral Exploration is still absent. Furthermore, information is now generated instantaneously and its sophistication continues to improve. Traditional methods of information retrieval, such as visiting libraries to extract data from journals, have been transformed by modern computational techniques, including algorithms capable of searching and summarizing thousands of documents in near real-time. Concurrently, there is a noticeable decline in interest in fields such as economic geology and mining engineering across the Western world, indicative of a broader shift away from these disciplines. It is increasingly urgent to preserve the century-old knowledge regarding the formation of ore deposits, much of which is contained in archival geological reports dispersed across various locations globally. Comprehensive datasets, similar to those in Canada and Australia, are critically needed worldwide. Within the African continent, only one nation has established such a database, accessible at gsd.gov.zm. Ensuring that this information is readily available through online platforms is essential for attracting investment opportunities. This discourse advocates for collaboration between the scientific and industrial communities to develop a novel approach to mineral exploration. As exploration progresses beneath the surface, the traditional paradigm of initiating exploration with surface outcrops and employing deterministic geophysical inversion is proving ineffective, as evidenced by the decline in discovery rates. Instead, decisions should be informed by a focus on risk assessment through uncertainty quantification, thereby minimizing errors in drilling operations. Acknowledging our incomplete understanding of ore bodies is necessary, as modeled through the quantification of epistemic uncertainty. Moreover, it is imperative to develop advanced geostatistical methods to effectively manage data across appropriate scales, as current methodologies are insufficient and much needed to reduce false positives.

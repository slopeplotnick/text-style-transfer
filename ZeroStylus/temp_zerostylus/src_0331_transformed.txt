This dataset, titled HEALTHCONTRADICT, consists of 920 examples specifically curated to evaluate language models in the context of conflicting biomedical information. Each example includes a health-related query accompanied by two documents presenting contradictory viewpoints. Moreover, there exists an objective explanation for each query, substantiated by empirical scientific evidence. In our study, we assess language models, ranging in size from 1 billion to 8 billion parameters, using this dataset to determine their ability to handle health-related queries amidst conflicting information. Our investigation focuses on two primary aspects: (i) the efficacy of language models in responding to health-related inquiries in the presence of conflicting information, and (ii) the influence of the biomedical context in shaping their responses. To evaluate model performance, we devised a methodology where models were exposed to contexts that were either accurate, inaccurate, or contradictory, to assess their ability to generate correct outputs. Our contributions are threefold: (i) We introduce the novel dataset, HEALTHCONTRADICT, specifically designed for the evaluation of language models dealing with contradictory biomedical information; (ii) We conduct a detailed evaluation of these models using HEALTHCONTRADICT, focusing on their capacity to process extensive and conflicting biomedical texts through interpretable and well-defined metrics; and (iii) We compare general-purpose language models with domain-specific biomedical variants, demonstrating that the latter exhibit superior performance in leveraging accurate information while disregarding erroneous content. During the dataset's development, we ensured the uniqueness of each document to maintain impartiality in the evaluation process. An illustrative inquiry might be: Does coffee contribute to weight loss, and how? Here, conflicting documents present opposing views (one affirms, the other negates), yet the factual resolution is affirmative, supported by scientific evidence suggesting that caffeine may aid in reducing weight, BMI, and body fat.

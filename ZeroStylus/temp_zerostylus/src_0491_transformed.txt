Our methodology employed a label-driven approach to Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation, combined with the strategic identification of challenging cases. By integrating label-constrained CoT synthesis with the excavation of difficult samples, we enhanced the model's reasoning capabilities through structured multi-step inference patterns and effectively tackled challenging edge cases. To evaluate the system's efficacy, we conducted rigorous case analysis with a focus on these difficult scenarios, thereby ensuring robust handling of complex reasoning requirements and edge cases. Furthermore, the innovations in this phase facilitated the online application and adaptation of the CoT-based multi-dimensional knowledge system to smaller models. This marked a significant advancement in scaling deep reasoning capabilities to production environments, with improved inference efficiency and substantial performance improvements in online A/B testing scenarios. However, our approach encountered two primary challenges: first, the high dependence on visual information for understanding product specifics, where user-uploaded images often contained insufficient textual details; and second, the necessity to ensure that decisions adhered strictly to regulatory frameworks and corporate norms. To address these challenges, we pursued a comprehensive technical strategy that integrated multimodal understanding capabilities with rule-aware reasoning mechanisms. This strategy enabled the model to process both visual and textual data while maintaining strict adherence to business constraints. Our model paradigm was built upon a synthesis of CoT Generation, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and Reinforcement Learning (RL), utilizing the Qwen-2.5 LLM for textual data and the Qwen-2.5 VL for visual data. This dual-model architecture facilitated seamless integration of visual and textual reasoning pathways, overcoming the limitations inherent in single-modality data utilization. Moreover, our methodology leveraged multimodal and rule-aware CoT generation by combining image-text paired understanding with standard-oriented prompt engineering. Initially, business rules and exemplar cases were embedded into CoT reasoning chains to establish rule comprehension logic. Subsequently, reinforcement learning was employed to significantly enhance the model's proficiency in applying rules for decision-making in intricate scenarios.

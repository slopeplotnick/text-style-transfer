In this section, we present MIGR, a novel method that determines the initial input modality to prioritize by utilizing Mutual Information (MI). This design aims to mitigate biases in the early reasoning stage by prioritizing the modality that conveys the most significant emotional signals. The proposed framework consists of two primary learning stages. First, we introduce modality-aligned Self-Focused Training (SFT), which enables the model to identify an initial reasoning direction by focusing on the modality labeled as emotion-dominant by MI. Subsequently, we propose Modality-Aware Reward Optimization, which reinforces the prioritization of the appropriate modality while ensuring that reasoning remains emotionally grounded. To achieve these objectives, two reward mechanisms are employed: a modality-aligned order reward to prioritize the proper sequencing of input information and a modality-grounded reasoning reward to integrate emotional signals effectively. The following sections elaborate on each component in detail. Recent research [15] underscores the efficacy of SFT as an initial strategy for emotion understanding tasks, demonstrating that even limited high-quality supervision from datasets such as Explainable Multimodal Emotion Reasoning (EMER) [27] can yield notable performance improvements. Building on this insight, we adopt a similar cold-start approach to initiate the training process. However, while EMER [27] provides high-quality supervision, the SFT phase can be further improved by augmenting the training dataset with samples that exhibit particularly clear and consistent emotional signals. To enhance the quality of early-stage supervision, we therefore expand the training dataset with additional emotion-consistent examples. Specifically, we extract Facial Action Units (FAUs) from video frames, leveraging their strong correlation with emotional expressions [43]. Subsequently, we evaluate whether these FAU patterns align with the intended emotion by referencing a predefined FAU-emotion mapping table [11]. Only samples whose FAU patterns precisely match the target emotion are selected for inclusion. For these filtered samples, reasoning-answer pairs are constructed by integrating emotional evidence derived from FAUs, thereby ensuring that the reasoning process remains aligned with the intended emotional context.

In Section 3, we explained how the questions correspond to four principal themes relevant to the research objectives. Given that the majority of the interviewees were unfamiliar with Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE), we provided a concise overview of its concepts and significance prior to initiating the interviews. Although this introduction carried the potential for bias, we trusted the interviewees to independently form their perspectives and relied on the interviewer’s ability to minimize undue influence. To elicit detailed insights and encourage a diverse array of ideas, the questions were intentionally open-ended, designed to promote comprehensive responses without constraining the scope of potential answers. Furthermore, metrics were developed to optimize the utilization of the collected data and to systematically extract relevant information. These metrics aggregated and analyzed the frequency of significant terms both in general and across specific roles. Accordingly, tables presented exhaustive details, while graphs visually illustrated the distribution of values. Importantly, even unique responses not echoed by others were fully considered and deliberated upon, as all answers were subjected to thorough reflection. To ensure consistency, the terms derived from the interviews were aligned with established terminology in the SPLE domain. For example, terms such as "personalisation" and "making specific some parts of the code" were mapped to the broader concept of "customization." This alignment not only enhanced the uniformity of the results but also facilitated the identification of arguments in the literature that could address participants’ concerns [LKHB21, MZB+15, Kru92]. Moreover, the process of iterating through interviews with different participants contributed to improving the overall flow and fluidity of the discussions. Although the questions themselves remained unchanged, the manner in which they were posed evolved over time. For instance, additional hints and more detailed explanations were provided when participants expressed uncertainty, and smoother transitions between questions were implemented. Despite these refinements in delivery, we believe that the quantity and quality of data collected across both the initial and final iterations of the questionnaire remained consistent.

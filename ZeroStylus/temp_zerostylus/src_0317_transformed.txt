The entrenchment of personal beliefs significantly contributes to social epistemic issues, such as polarization and misinformation, potentially leading to a wide range of cognitive biases. This phenomenon occurs due to the manner in which individuals process information, fundamentally reflecting their reasoning. Large-scale language models, which are trained on extensive corpora of online text to emulate human patterns of thought, are designed to support users by addressing inquiries or facilitating task completion. Building on the advancements enabled by these models, novel methodologies for reasoning have been formulated to address intricate cognitive tasks. Techniques range from early approaches, such as Chain-of-Thought prompting, to more recent advancements incorporating reinforcement learning, which have been demonstrated to enhance model reasoning through structured argumentation, evidence retrieval, and iterative exploration methods. However, such adherence to entrenched prior beliefs limits the ability of models to seek veridical outcomes, as they often fail to integrate novel information. Consequently, this undermines the reliability of their conclusions, potentially fostering misplaced confidence among users. While empirical evidence supports the presence of belief entrenchment within these models, establishing definitive proof remains a significant challenge. Indeed, determining whether a belief update is warranted or influenced by bias is challenging. Previous studies employ specific tasks that readily reveal belief entrenchment; however, these approaches are inadequate at identifying reasoning errors in ambiguous contexts.

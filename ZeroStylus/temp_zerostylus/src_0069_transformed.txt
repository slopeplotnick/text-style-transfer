In order to assess the performance of our proposed method relative to the alternative approach, RePro, we utilize the results reported in the original publication due to the unavailability of their implementation. P2C refers to Paper2Code. Refine necessitates the manual creation of distinct prompts for the evaluation and improvement of each stage of the process. Specifically, prior to implementing the original Paper2Code configuration, it was necessary to develop eight distinct prompts for four planning tasks. However, our approach eliminates the need for manual intervention by autonomously calibrating to each task via its system prompt, thereby obviating the requirement for additional prompt design. Our analyses were conducted on two distinct datasets. Paper2CodeBench comprises 90 papers sourced from leading 2024 conferences, including ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS, selected based on the availability of corresponding code and their concise length. Utilizing AI judges to evaluate correctness on a scale of 1 to 5, we compared the code generated by our method against the official code provided by the authors. Additionally, we present PaperBench Code-Dev, a dataset comprising 20 ICML 2024 papers, where human evaluators assessed the accuracy of the generated code using predefined evaluation rubrics. Consequently, few methodologies are known to employ self-refinement for the automated transformation of research papers into code. Therefore, we evaluate our method against two comparable approaches that incorporate self-refinement: (1) Paper2Code combined with Self-Refine. We position Self-Refine as an iterative method. All experiments were conducted utilizing GPT-4.1 as the principal model. For RePro, we rely on the results presented in their original paper due to the inaccessibility of their codebase.

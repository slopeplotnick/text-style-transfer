The samples were compared against the EMOPIA MIDI files. The major key ratio serves as an indicator of valence, reflecting the perceived positivity of the music, while the average note length and note velocity are indicative of arousal, representing the music's energy levels. In terms of valence, the generated samples align closely with EMOPIA across three quadrants. However, a notable deviation is observed in Q2, where the model produces a significantly higher proportion of major-key tracks compared to EMOPIA. This discrepancy likely arises from a combination of factors, including the dataset's inherent bias towards major-key tracks over minor-key ones and the limited size of the fine-tuning dataset. Regarding arousal, the fine-tuned model effectively captures the note-length patterns: shorter notes are predominant in high-arousal quadrants (Q1 and Q2), while longer notes are characteristic of low-arousal quadrants (Q3 and Q4), closely mirroring EMOPIA's patterns. On the other hand, differences in average note velocity across quadrants are less distinct. This is likely due to REMI's quantization of MIDI velocities into a limited range of discrete bins, which compresses the dynamic range and diminishes the ability to distinguish variations between quadrants. The numerical results partially align with findings from the listening test, as some musical attributes related to arousal and valence are accurately captured by the model. However, the qualitative results suggest that the model performs better at capturing arousal than valence, with the latter appearing to be almost stochastic. This may be attributed to the model's tendency to overgenerate major-key tracks. Furthermore, these findings underscore the potential value of incorporating additional quantitative metrics to identify specific areas where valence-related generation could be improved. In this study, we introduced Story2MIDI, a transformer-based model designed to generate emotionally aligned symbolic music from textual input. To support this task, we constructed a paired text-music dataset and developed an encoder-decoder framework to establish affective correspondence between language and music. The encoder was contrastively fine-tuned to produce distinct emotion representations across valence-arousal quadrants, while the decoder was initially pretrained on a large corpus of symbolic music and subsequently fine-tuned on a smaller, emotion-labeled dataset for emotion-conditioned music generation. Finally, we evaluated Story2MIDI using both subjective listener feedback and objective musical metrics. These evaluations demonstrate the model's effectiveness in capturing emotional alignment while also highlighting several areas that warrant further research and refinement.

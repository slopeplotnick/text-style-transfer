Building upon the findings presented in RQ2, which highlighted the statistical complementarity and distinct failure modes of Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV), we now turn our attention to RQ3: the operationalization of these synergies into high-performance hybrid systems. To address this, we propose and evaluate two integration paradigms: a Score-Level Fusion approach and an Evidence-Aware Pipeline, each designed to mitigate the structural limitations of FV identified in our case study. Firstly, while the Score-Level Fusion approach enhances robustness through the aggregation of scores, it does not explicitly address the failure modes detailed in Section 4.5. Specifically, FV systems demonstrate reliability when evidence is explicitly retrieved, resulting in "Supported" or "Contradicted" verdicts, but their reliability diminishes when relevant evidence is absent, yielding a "Not Enough Information" (NEI) outcome. To address this gap, we have developed an Evidence-Aware Pipeline that prioritizes external information retrieval and resorts to internal signals only when such retrieval is unsuccessful: (1) Step 1 involves Retrieval & Verification. Here, an FV module, notably LLM-QA, retrieves evidence and categorizes the claim-evidence relationship as Supported, Contradicted, or NEI. (2) Step 2 involves Conditional Branching, where a cascading decision mechanism is employed. If the FV module provides a definitive prediction of Supported or Contradicted, the system accepts this result. However, if the output is NEI, the system acknowledges the lack of external support. Instead of imposing a potentially erroneous verification, the pipeline bypasses the FV and defaults to an HD method, such as LNPE, to derive the final score solely based on the model's internal uncertainty signals. This conditional design effectively addresses the limitations of FV when retrieval results are insufficient. Crucially, by decoupling the dependency on external retrieval from internal signals, the risk of errors due to suboptimal evidence retrieval is mitigated, thereby ensuring robust detection capabilities even in the absence of external knowledge.

Section 7 highlights individual contributions, and Section 8 concludes with a summary of findings and their implications. Social media has become an integral component of contemporary daily life. It facilitates rapid communication, seamless information exchange, and the sharing of opinions across geographical boundaries. However, this freedom of expression has also inadvertently enabled the proliferation of hate speech and abrasive language on social media platforms. Despite this, efforts to identify hate speech on Bangla social media remain limited, exacerbated by the absence of a high-quality dataset. Moreover, the linguistic patterns prevalent on social media diverge significantly from those in traditional print media, further complicating the automatic detection of Bangla hate speech. Traditionally, hate speech detection has been approached as a supervised classification task (Mossie and Wang, 2020; Alkomah and Ma, 2022). Earlier studies primarily relied on lexicon-based and rule-based approaches; however, these methods often failed to capture implicit or context-dependent hate (Cavicchio, 2024; Gitari et al., 2015; Asghar et al., 2017). Recent advancements, by contrast, have employed machine learning classifiers such as Logistic Regression (LR) and Support Vector Machines (SVMs) to address these limitations (Mossie and Wang, 2020). Accordingly, traditional supervised learning techniques, including Majority, Random, and SVM, have been utilized in the detection of online threats or harmful content.

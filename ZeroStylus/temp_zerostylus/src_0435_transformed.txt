Explanations in Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) are required to meet specific criteria to ensure their effectiveness. Notably, it is essential that explanations are both understandable to the intended audience and faithful to the model being explained. Understandability, which is inherently audience-dependent, demands that explanations present meaningful content in a concise and accessible manner. Conversely, fidelity necessitates that the explanation accurately reflects the model's mechanisms and behavior without introducing distortions or bias. Bias, for example, may arise from strategically limiting the scope of an explanation. While these two qualities—understandability and fidelity—are conceptually distinct, enhancing both simultaneously is a nontrivial challenge. For instance, explanations that align closely with prior expectations may appear more understandable; however, such alignment introduces the risk of bias, particularly if it influences the selection of explanations. Furthermore, even when an explanation is faithful and unbiased, misinterpretation by the audience can lead to unintended misinformation. To address these challenges, this study introduces a causal XAI framework for post-hoc explanation of arbitrary models, aiming to balance understandability and fidelity within a structured context. Specifically, understandability is approached by advocating for the use of causal explanations framed in vocabulary that is accessible to the intended audience. The emphasis on causality stems from its alignment with the implicit structure of explanation commonly employed in human communication [Miller, 2019]. Thus, causally valid explanations promote alignment between the intended interpretation and the audience's actual understanding, thereby obviating the need for supplementary instructions to clarify the explanation. This facilitates a more concise and effective representation. Additionally, the use of accessible vocabulary addresses the potential mismatch between the features utilized by the model and the features most appropriate for human interpretation. This distinction underpins Concept-based XAI (C-XAI), a collection of methods designed to bridge the gap between model-level data processing and human-level semantic understanding [Poeta et al., 2025]. By assuming that models process information such as images, graphs, or tokens, while humans communicate using high-level, semantically meaningful concepts, C-XAI methods provide a structured pathway for generating explanations that are both comprehensible and model-faithful.

The platform under consideration has been operational for approximately five to six years. According to the classification criteria outlined by the FEF framework [vdLSR07], ITK's proprietary platform is categorized as a "Level 3: Software Platform," featuring a reference architecture that is uniformly applied across all products. This architecture incorporates a repository of shared assets alongside clearly defined variation points for the integration of new variants. The overarching migration objective is to achieve an architecture level where domain commonalities and variabilities are systematically captured, thereby enabling the specification of a reference architecture applicable to the entire software product line. Furthermore, this approach encompasses managed and structured reuse based on an asset repository, explicit representation of variability within assets, and a reference architecture that regulates permissible variations in application setups, while integrating numerous high-quality solutions into the software product line architecture. Variability management is explicitly addressed within this architecture by establishing permissible configurations for application setups, defining variation points and their constraints, and instituting rules that govern the behavior of application-specific variants. Consequently, the ultimate aim is to provide a software configuration capable of facilitating the automated synthesis of distinct variants, thereby supporting efficient and adaptable software deployment. At the organizational level, ITK is classified at the fourth level within the FEF framework [vdLSR07], which denotes the integration and coordination of domain engineering and application engineering activities. This alignment necessitates substantial collaboration and synchronized processes between the two disciplines. To advance project development, agronomists and software developers work collaboratively, ensuring interdisciplinary input and coordinated efforts. The decision to migrate and integrate the research team was collectively reached through discussions involving project leaders and stakeholders, reflecting a collegiate approach to decision-making. However, prior to initiating the interview design process, a formal research methodology for the remainder of the project had yet to be established. It was deemed necessary to first evaluate the perspectives of the company's personnel regarding the migration to a Software Product Line (SPL). Based on the insights garnered from these interviews, a concrete methodology for the migration project will subsequently be developed.

Prior to initiating the creation of prompts for specific Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) tasks, we adhere to a standardized format for defining the task and requirements. Subsequently, we incorporate the query and item particulars from each dataset. To enhance the performance of the base model, it is essential to incorporate crucial information pertaining to the nature of the task, the decision-making criteria, and the desired format of the output within the prompt. Moreover, the process involves integrating explicit directives to facilitate the model's adherence to a dual-phase methodology: initially discerning the query's requisites and identifying necessary attributes, followed by extracting pertinent attributes from item information and formulating decisions in accordance with established rules. While providing more detailed instructions within the prompt has been shown to guide the model toward selecting correct answers with greater ease, the underlying mechanisms by which such specificity enhances reasoning accuracy remain underexplored. In SFT, however, the model's behavior is influenced not solely by the instructions but also by the response data it incorporates during training. Thus, three variations of prompt designs were explored to identify the most effective approach. The longer version encompasses the complete task details, rules, and instructions, exceeding a total of 7,000 tokens. Consequently, the mid version retains a concise focus by including only the most essential instructions, comprising approximately 800 tokens. In simplified form, the description merely articulates a fundamental role characterization such as: "You are an expert in e-commerce relevance." The task involves evaluating the relevance of the query in relation to the item through a process of scoring. This stage holds considerable importance as it initiates the model and enhances its foundational capacity for decision-making. To achieve this objective, it is imperative to construct a training dataset characterized by both extensive coverage and rigorous cleanliness. To ensure comprehensive coverage and mitigate potential biases in data selection, we employ a robust sampling method to encompass diverse categories of e-commerce data, thereby avoiding excessive or insufficient representation of any single category.

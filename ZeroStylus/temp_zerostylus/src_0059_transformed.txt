Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly utilized in applications such as Google's AI Overviews, where they summarize information from multiple lengthy documents. However, it remains uncertain whether these systems assign equal weight to all input data. To investigate this, researchers examined abortion-related news by constructing 40 sets of article triplets, each representing pro, neutral, and con perspectives. These triplets were permuted into six different input orders and used to prompt Gemini 2.5 Flash to generate neutral summaries. The summaries were evaluated against their source articles using three metrics: ROUGE-L to measure lexical overlap, BERTScore to assess semantic similarity, and SummaC to evaluate factual consistency. Results from a one-way ANOVA revealed a significant primacy effect, particularly in BERTScore, indicating that the summaries were more semantically aligned with the first-seen article. Furthermore, pairwise comparisons demonstrated that the first article exerted a significantly greater influence on the summary compared to the second and third articles, which had approximately equivalent impacts. These findings highlight a selective bias toward the initial document, raising concerns for applications that depend on LLM-generated summaries and for agentic AI systems, where the steps involving LLMs may disproportionately shape subsequent decisions. The rapid advancement of generative AI, which forms a foundational component of agentic systems, has further accelerated the development and deployment of these systems across various domains. Agentic AI systems are distinguished by their ability to autonomously manage complex objectives with minimal supervision, demonstrating a high degree of operational adaptability [Mukherjee and Chang, 2025; Shavit et al., 2023]. A critical mechanism enabling this autonomy is goal decomposition, in which high-level objectives are systematically divided into manageable subtasks [Sapkota et al., 2025]. To execute these subtasks effectively, such systems typically engage in iterative planning and multi-step reasoning, integrating external information—through methods such as retrieval-augmented generation and tool use—as needed to guide their subsequent actions [Sapkota et al., 2025; Shavit et al., 2023]. Consequently, the reliability of an agentic system is fundamentally contingent upon the precision with which its underlying LLMs retrieve, prioritize, and synthesize information from both user instructions and external sources.

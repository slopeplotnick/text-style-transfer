E-commerce platforms such as Amazon, Taobao, and JD have profoundly altered traditional shopping behaviors. Central to these platforms' user experience is the relevance module, which plays a crucial role in ensuring that displayed products meet user interests; failure to do so can detrimentally impact both user trust and the platform's financial outcomes. Consequently, relevance modeling, which assesses the correspondence between search queries and products, is a foundational aspect of e-commerce engines. Traditional relevance models, including term matching and vector matching methods, are extensively utilized to secure a satisfactory search experience. Methods based on term matching, such as TF-IDF and BM25, evaluate the match between queries and products but fall short in capturing semantic relevance and contextual understanding. Conversely, vector matching approaches leverage BERT and similar methodologies to comprehend semantic relationships between queries and products, thereby significantly advancing relevance modeling. However, despite these advancements, vector-based methods encounter two primary challenges in domain-specific tasks: first, the scarcity of robust domain-specific examples limits performance, as most methods achieve satisfactory results with moderate amounts of labeled data, yet further improvements demand numerous challenging examples that are difficult to both procure and annotate accurately. Second, models are often constrained by their parameter size, limiting their capability for complex reasoning and relevance discrimination. To address these issues, large language models (LLMs) have been increasingly applied to relevance tasks, enhancing reasoning and comprehension capabilities. However, previous LLM approaches predominantly rely on the representation of the last token for relevance determination and utilize soft labels to guide smaller models. This approach, however, lacks the ability to effectively analyze which features influence relevance decisions and fails to enhance the reasoning capabilities of smaller models.

This section elucidates the dataset construction process and evaluation methodologies, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Firstly, a team of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) experts spearheaded the collection of samples from clinical cases, historical case records, and authoritative examination questions. Subsequently, these samples underwent a process of deduplication, cleaning, and anonymization. The data annotation process, moreover, follows a rigorous three-stage pipeline: initially, experts conducted annotations independently, followed by mutual cross-validation of each other's work, and concluded with an independent third-party review. Consequently, the benchmark named TCM-BEST4SDT consists of 600 questions spanning four domains: TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, Large Language Model (LLM) Content Safety, and Syndrome Differentiation Tasks (SDT), as illustrated in Fig. 2. Regarding evaluation methodologies, three mechanisms were designed to assess model efficacy within authentic TCM clinical contexts. The first, selected-response evaluation, involves conducting multiple independent evaluations for each question, randomizing the sequence of answer options, and employing distinct scoring methodologies contingent upon the question type. The second method, judge model evaluation, utilizes an LLM guided by expert-designed prompts to evaluate and assign scores to the model's responses. The third, reward model evaluation, entails developing a specialized model designed to assess the alignment between therapeutic prescriptions and corresponding syndromes. Notably, TCM-BEST4SDT reveals distinct task types despite consistent categorization into SDT tasks and general evaluation tasks. In task design, SDT tasks utilize clinical case data and historical case records, whereas general tasks encompass areas such as TCM foundational knowledge, medical ethics, and LLM content safety. The TCM Basic Knowledge component, indeed, is derived from public examination questions, including the National Qualification Examination for Medical Practitioners, the National Postgraduate Entrance Examination: Comprehensive Clinical Medicine (TCM Integrated), and the Chinese Herbal Medicine Title Examination. Additionally, the formulation of Medical Ethics inquiries involves sourcing from reputable origins, complemented by meticulous manual annotations crafted by domain experts to address distinct scenarios. Lastly, the Content Safety questions were independently developed by domain experts to align with practical requirements and ensure applicability.

We observe that high model accuracy does not necessarily equate to effective integration of diverse information types, as illustrated in the central section of Figure 8. For instance, while GPT-5 Mini achieves a very low error rate, it does not integrate multiple input modalities as effectively as other models. This issue predominantly arises when there is a significant imbalance between input modalities; the model is expected to prioritize stronger inputs, yet some models exhibit deficiencies in this regard. Consequently, prioritizing accuracy alone can lead to a preference for models that perform poorly in managing noisy data, thereby undermining robustness in complex scenarios. In contrast, Llama-4 Maverick demonstrates superior performance compared to the Bayesian reliability-weighted linear fusion method, suggesting the employment of more advanced information integration techniques. Moreover, we find that a non-linear random forest model aligns more effectively with its operational mechanisms compared to simpler linear approaches. Furthermore, models such as Llama-4 Maverick, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o leverage multimodal inputs (e.g., text and images) to reduce error rates across tasks that require both input types, thereby highlighting the advantage of incorporating additional modalities for improved performance. However, this improvement is not consistently observed across all models, indicating that there remains significant potential to enhance the performance of multimodal models. To move beyond mere accuracy metrics, we propose the Bayesian Consistency Score, a tool that elucidates how models exhibit logical behavior adjustments. This is particularly important as it enables a more comprehensive evaluation of models, even when their accuracy is already substantially high. To examine this, we manipulate specific conditions to compare models starting from various performance baselines, thereby gaining insights into their information processing mechanisms.

When evaluating models, it is essential not only to compare response patterns but also to determine whether these models demonstrate behaviors aligned with Bayesian principles when confronted with changes in situational contexts. Despite the inherent complexity of strategies such as emphasizing salient details or modifying rules, this assessment is crucial. Moreover, understanding how models adapt to varying conditions can provide insight into their Bayesian consistency. Therefore, it is necessary to analyze these behaviors to ensure that the models are robust in their application across different scenarios.

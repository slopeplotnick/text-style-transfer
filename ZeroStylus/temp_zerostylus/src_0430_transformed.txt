To evaluate the efficacy of our model, we employ three distinct emotion datasets for training and assessment. EMER offers high-quality human-verified reasoning-label pairs and serves as the initial dataset for our training process. Meanwhile, the DFEW and MAFW datasets encompass diverse facial expressions captured through video, audio, and text modalities. The details regarding these datasets are available in Appendix 7.1. Following prior work, we report Unweighted Average Recall (UAR) and Weighted Average Recall (WAR) to assess recognition accuracy; additionally, we employ three supplementary metrics—Emotional Explanation Alignment (EEA), Explanation Prediction Consistency (EPC), and Fidelity of Consistency Ratio (FCR)—to evaluate the alignment between the model's predictions, the associated explanations, and the conveyed emotion. Precise definitions and our annotation methodology are detailed in Appendix 7.2. In terms of implementation, we adopt the R1-Omni architecture, incorporating SigLIP as the vision encoder, Whisper-large-v3 as the audio encoder, and BERT as the text encoder to facilitate multimodal processing. Each type of data output is projected into the LLM embedding space through visual and audio projectors, implemented as two linear layers designed to align with the LLM representation size. This illustrates the model's capability to extract approximately 50 audio features from a 1-second audio clip and 729 visual features from a single video frame. Although our approach incorporates the LLM backbone Qwen2.5-7B, we initiate with model weights from the HumanOmni checkpoint to maintain the pretrained multimodal representations. To test the configuration, we juxtapose MIGR with alternative methodologies for emotion comprehension on the MAFW and DFEW datasets, where comparative analysis serves as a measure of efficacy. The methodology is bifurcated into two distinct approaches: one that directly forecasts emotion labels and another that generates textual explanations prior to prediction. While our evaluation involves comparing MIGR with alternative models that generate reasoning, it is imperative to ensure that the reasoning texts produced by our model maintain emotional consistency.

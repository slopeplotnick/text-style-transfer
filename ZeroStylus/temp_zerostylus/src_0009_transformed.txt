**Refined Paragraph:** This study evaluates the synchronization between source and edited videos by measuring frame-level temporal correspondence across four dimensions: speech, gaze, blink, and pose. Each dimension captures a distinct aspect of motion dynamics: speech evaluates mouth movements, gaze tracks eye movements, blink monitors blinking frequency, and pose examines body movements. These metrics, while identical to those employed for data filtering, are used exclusively for dataset curation and final evaluation, and are not incorporated into the model's training objectives. Furthermore, the fidelity of the edits throughout the video is assessed using two complementary CLIP-based measures. The Directional CLIP Score, originally developed for image editing, is adapted for video to measure the consistency of per-frame visual transformations with the intended edit direction within CLIP's embedding space. Two variants are reported: an image-based version that assesses alignment with the visual edit implied by the first edited frame, and a text-dual version that evaluates alignment with the semantic edit described by the textual prompt. The latter is particularly relevant for approaches such as LucyEdit, which rely solely on textual guidance and do not utilize the edited first frame. Specifically, the image-based variant examines whether the transformation from source frames to edited frames resembles the change from the unedited to edited first frames, whereas the text-dual variant investigates whether this visual transformation aligns with the change suggested by the edit text. Moreover, to further assess semantic accuracy, the CLIP-Text Alignment Score is employed to evaluate how well each generated frame corresponds to the textual description in CLIP space. This measure ensures that the video remains faithful to the prompt semantics throughout its duration, thus complementing the directional measure.

Despite the growing prevalence of this methodological approach, investigations into the collaborative dynamics between solvers and verifiers have remained sparse. Moreover, the majority of prior investigations have focused on the self-verification processes of individual models and their subsequent improvement, though this outcome is not invariably guaranteed. Models may exhibit biases aligned with their intrinsic cognitive frameworks, and training processes potentially exacerbate these biases. Consequently, although certain tasks may derive uniform advantages from answer verification, others may not receive equivalent benefits. Furthermore, sole reliance on self-verification does not adequately elucidate the dynamics involved when the solving and verifying entities differ. To address this gap, this study examines the process of verification across multiple open-source models, encompassing various versions and configurations. Our investigation encompasses both intra-family and inter-family model interactions to determine the conditions under which verification significantly enhances performance. Specifically, we scrutinize variables such as the model family, size, additional training, the congruence between solver and verifier, and task type to ascertain their effect on the efficacy of verification in improving solver outcomes. In evaluating the performance of verifiers across a diverse set of tasks, this study includes those requiring precise logical reasoning and symbolic computation (e.g., 3-SAT, Sudoku, and matrix multiplication), mathematical reasoning challenges (e.g., AIME and GSM8K), commonsense and factual reasoning (e.g., CSQA and GPQA), as well as general knowledge domains such as STEM and social sciences, utilizing 37 models derived from seven distinct model families. Our analysis reveals that models frequently exhibit a preference for solutions that align with their intrinsic reasoning, with additional training often exacerbating this bias, and certain tasks deriving less benefit from self-verification than others. Therefore, practical advice grounded in our research on the intelligent application of verifiers is offered.

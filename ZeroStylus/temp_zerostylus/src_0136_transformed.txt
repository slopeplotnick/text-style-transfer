In contrast to image-based IC-LoRA approaches, which necessitate separate training for each editing task, our single Sync-LoRA module demonstrates effective generalization across a diverse range of edits while striking a robust balance between high visual fidelity and precise motion preservation, as evidenced by our experimental results (see Section 4). The concept of in-context learning (ICL) within diffusion models facilitates generalization to novel tasks by conditioning on structured input examples rather than explicit supervision. While extensively studied in the domain of language models [5], recent research has increasingly focused on the application of ICL to diffusion-based image generation [9, 26, 46, 65]. In these studies, the generation of image panels enables contextual relationships among images within the panel, thereby supporting applications that require consistency across outputs, such as storyboard creation [3, 24, 52]. Moreover, when panels are conditioned on a real image, ICL-based approaches can effectively address a broad spectrum of image-to-image tasks, including image personalization [6, 15, 16, 31, 44, 49] and image editing [18, 23, 38, 41, 42]. Among these approaches, IC-LoRA [26] emerges as the most closely related to our work. This method demonstrates that transformer-based diffusion models inherently possess the capability to generate panels with consistent attributes across images. Furthermore, the authors show that this capability can be enhanced by fine-tuning the model with a low-rank adapter (LoRA) on structured, consistent panels and employing a tailored prompt template. Their approach has been applied to various tasks, including film storyboard generation, font design, and interior decoration. Building upon this foundation, our work extends the paradigm to video editing by introducing an IC-LoRA module tailored for frame-accurate portrait synchronization. Additionally, recent advancements have highlighted that large video diffusion transformers naturally exhibit emergent in-context learning behaviors, enabling the generation of temporally or spatially coherent clips within a single diffusion process [14]. While this prior work explores the use of inpainting-based conditioning to achieve controlled scene composition, it does not provide mechanisms to ensure temporal correspondence across frames. Addressing this limitation, PoseGen [22] leverages in-context LoRA fine-tuning for pose-controllable human video generation, achieving extended and seamless motion from a reference image. In contrast, our approach explicitly trains the in-context behavior to advance synchronized editing, ensuring enhanced precision and coordination across video frames.

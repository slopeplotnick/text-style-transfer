Despite the evolution of optimization approaches, traditional methods persistently exhibit fundamental limitations when applied to construction coordination scenarios. The necessity for a comprehensive plan at the outset is often challenged by the dynamic nature of construction projects, wherein tasks, conditions, and resource allocations are subject to frequent and significant alterations. Consequently, these methodologies prove excessively intricate for large-scale projects involving numerous robots and tasks. In response to these challenges, researchers have investigated learning-based methods capable of adapting to dynamic changes without relying on a comprehensive plan. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has gained significant prominence due to its capacity to enable robots to collaboratively operate by interacting with their environment, thereby eliminating the necessity for a fully predefined plan. Initially, RL allows robots to engage in experiential learning through iterative trials and adaptive adjustments in response to environmental changes. However, the necessity for extensive practice inherent in RL presents significant challenges in construction settings, where even minor errors can lead to hazardous situations for both personnel and machinery. Additionally, the extended duration required for training remains incompatible with projects that have stringent time constraints, thus creating demand for tailored reinforcement learning techniques to address specific challenges present in construction contexts. In 2022, Lee et al. developed a technique integrating digital twins and Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for robotic operations within the construction sector. The integration of Building Information Modeling (BIM) with DRL enables more intelligent coordination decisions by accounting for spatial, temporal, and safety considerations. Moreover, the implementation of digital twin technology allows the acquisition of knowledge through simulated environments before engaging in practical application, ensuring a safe learning process. Nevertheless, the intrinsic challenges associated with RL continue to present conflicts with real-world safety protocols, as even policies refined through simulation can lead to hazardous operational discrepancies during their implementation in practical scenarios. Furthermore, the application of simulation-trained methodologies to real construction environments under varying conditions remains inherently challenging, as the discrepancies between simulated and actual scenarios often hinder effective implementation. Consequently, these challenges inspired the development of more sophisticated reinforcement learning methodologies.

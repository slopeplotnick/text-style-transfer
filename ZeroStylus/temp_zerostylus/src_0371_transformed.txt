Methods such as checkers are effective within domains like mathematics and coding; however, they do not generalize well to tasks lacking definitive solutions. In contrast, self-evaluation approaches position the model or another AI as an adjudicator. For instance, Self-Rewarding Language Models and RLAIF utilize AI feedback rather than human input, whereas IPO and Self-Rewarding PPO derive preferences through probabilistic assessments. Furthermore, analyzing a model's internal activations, as demonstrated in Factoscope and INSIDE, reveals whether the model encodes factual information or generates potentially incorrect outputs. To enhance decision-making, a geometric measure of activations is employed as a reward, aligning our methodology with similar approaches. This concept is theoretically underpinned by the softmax bottleneck framework, offering insights through its analytical perspective. The limitations of softmax models, as illustrated by Yang et al. (2018) and extended to contemporary AI by Godey et al. (2024), indicate that high-rank natural language probability representations are necessary, paralleling challenges in expressive language modeling. To explore this further, we examine how the narrowing of embeddings during training adversely affects model output quality. Although representational complexity is linked to the quality of model outputs, the underlying mechanisms and their enhancement of output quality remain unexplored. Garrido's research demonstrates that the rank of learned representations reliably predicts performance, obviating the need for labeled data. In language models, hidden states encode nuanced linguistic and semantic information: word embeddings capture structured patterns in syntax and semantics, while intermediate layers differentiate between factual accuracy and misinformation likelihood. To investigate these phenomena, we identify measurable patterns indicating the quality of model-generated outputs. Finally, we introduce a method that leverages rank as not only a post-training analytical tool but also a dynamic reward signal during learning, effectively translating geometric insights into a practical mechanism for model improvement.

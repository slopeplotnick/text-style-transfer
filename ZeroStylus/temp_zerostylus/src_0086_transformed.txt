In our investigation, we evaluated seven methodologies for model combination, namely Linear, Karcher Mean, SLERP, NuSLERP, TIES, DELLA, and Nearswap. These approaches were utilized to integrate a bias-inverted model with a pre-trained large language model, thus leveraging their complementary characteristics. Among these, Linear, as proposed by Wortsman et al. (2022), emerges as the most straightforward concept, involving the efficient aggregation and averaging of weights from fine-tuned models—a cost-effective approach demonstrating considerable efficacy. Moreover, Goddard et al. (2024) introduced a method leveraging the Karcher mean on a Riemannian manifold, which accounts for the curved geometry of model parameters to facilitate computations in non-Euclidean spaces. Similarly, SLERP, originating from Goddard in 2024, constrains the merging of model weights along a spherical path, thereby preserving the geometric integrity of the space. NuSLERP, also introduced by Goddard in 2024, modifies the SLERP algorithm by permitting distinct components of the model to blend differently, thereby prioritizing significant elements to enhance equilibrium and performance. Furthermore, TIES, proposed by Yadav (2023), effectively integrates models by analyzing task-specific parameter differences, minimizing interference while preserving the unique strengths of individual models. In addition, DELLA, introduced in 2024, employs an advanced methodology to reduce interference by selectively trimming less critical updates while preserving essential components, thus ensuring the model's performance remains intact.

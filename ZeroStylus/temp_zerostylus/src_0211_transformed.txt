Stage 3: Instruction-specific fine-tuning. In the third stage, we focus on enhancing the model's ability to follow specialized task instructions within the Infinity-MM framework. The training process utilizes a comprehensive dataset that spans multiple task categories, including document understanding, chart-based reasoning, OCR-centric tasks, and dynamic multi-turn conversations. To ensure balanced proficiency across these domains, the Infinity-MM approach applies task-specific mixture weighting, allocating 35% to general visual question answering tasks that involve routine visual reasoning and scene interpretation, 25% to document comprehension tasks such as forms and structured text, 20% to reasoning with charts and graphical data, 15% to OCR-related challenges, and 5% to conversational tasks and niche domains. The training configuration employs a learning rate of 1e-5 over one epoch, with an effective batch size of 512. The objective remains standard autoregressive language modeling loss, which involves predicting the subsequent token in sequence. Evaluation is conducted to benchmark the proposed architecture, specifically designed for NPU systems, against state-of-the-art vision-language models across five distinct multimodal assessments. More specifically, we compare performance with recent models, such as InternVL2 (available in 1B and 2B parameter configurations) and Qwen2-VL (offered in 2B and 3.75B variants), which represent distinct architectural families optimized for different deployment environments. InternVL2 exemplifies ViT-Transformer designs tailored for GPU platforms, whereas Qwen2-VL demonstrates robust general-purpose multimodal capabilities. The AutoNeural model, trained to Stage 3 of the Infinity-MM curriculum, demonstrates an optimal balance between accuracy and training efficiency, achieving competitive results across all benchmarks. The evaluation framework comprises five benchmarks designed to assess diverse multimodal competencies systematically. MMStar serves to measure the model's vision-language reasoning abilities, requiring multi-step inference and the application of commonsense knowledge to answer multiple-choice visual questions. HallusionBench evaluates the model’s handling of linguistic hallucinations and visual illusions, shedding light on the challenges faced by large vision-language systems. MathVista_MINI focuses on mathematical reasoning, testing the model’s ability to interpret diagrams, charts, and geometric figures while extracting quantitative information and performing numerical computations. AI2D_TEST assesses comprehension of textbook-based diagrams and scientific reasoning, targeting the model's ability to extract information from annotated educational illustrations. OCRBench, in contrast, evaluates text recognition and document understanding across diverse scenarios, including natural scenes, documents, and signage. Finally, we report accuracy for each benchmark and compute the average performance score, which provides insights into the model’s overall effectiveness in addressing a wide range of multimodal tasks.

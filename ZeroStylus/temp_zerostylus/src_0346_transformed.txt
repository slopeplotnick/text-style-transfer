To address RQ2, we evaluated our models on 11 low-resource languages, each selectively sampled from 8 distinct linguistic groups. Notably, languages written in the Latin script, such as Galician and Slovenian, exhibit more stable performance across benchmarks. This consistency is likely attributable to script overlap and subword tokenization coverage in the shared XLM-R vocabulary, which favors languages with higher representation during pretraining. However, Icelandic, despite also using the Latin script, performs less reliably due to its rich and complex morphological structure. This complexity results in significant token fragmentation and less coherent sentence representations, which can limit the effectiveness of multilingual encoders using subword tokenization methods. To assess the extent to which training on a high-resource language supports transfer within and across clusters, and thereby answer RQ3, we trained models under the following configurations: (i) Hindi-only, (ii) Chinese-only, and (iii) Hindi+Chinese combined. Each model was evaluated across six languages from the Indic (Hindi) and East-Southeast Asian (Chinese) clusters. Within the Indic cluster, the Hindi-trained model outperformed the Chinese-trained model for all benchmarks. Conversely, within the East-Southeast Asian cluster, the Chinese-trained model outperformed the Hindi-trained model for most benchmarks. These findings demonstrate that intra-cluster transfer from a high-resource language to other languages within the same cluster is more effective than inter-cluster transfer from a high-resource language situated in a different cluster.

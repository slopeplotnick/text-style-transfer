Despite the growing demand for mental health support, significant gaps in diagnosis persist globally. Barriers such as limited clinic availability, geographic inaccessibility, and workforce shortages continue to hinder timely mental health assessments and early self-awareness (World Health Organization, 2025b). Furthermore, structural, systemic, and individual-level obstacles exacerbate delays in diagnosing individuals with severe mental health conditions (Wiesepape et al., 2025). This disparity between the increasing demand for mental health services and the constrained capacity of traditional diagnostic models highlights critical shortcomings in current approaches (Stein et al., 2022). Specifically, the reliance on in-person sessions and self-reported questionnaires, while widely used, faces numerous challenges that include workforce shortages (Organization, 2025; Brahmbhatt & Schpero, 2024), high costs (Patel et al., 2018), regional disparities (Yang & Zhang, 2025), and stigma (Clement et al., 2015). Consequently, mental health services are often associated with extensive wait times, ranging from 3 to 18 months depending on regional and provider availability (McMahan et al., 2022; Yang & Zhang, 2025; Patel et al., 2018). Given these limitations, the development of more efficient methods for early detection and real-time monitoring has become critical for improving mental health outcomes. Portable and resource-efficient solutions, such as small language models, have emerged as promising tools for addressing these challenges at scale. These emerging technologies, particularly those integrated into smartphones, facilitate the passive and real-time detection of mental health issues through the analysis of social media interactions and application usage. Notably, linguistic data derived from smartphone-based social media interactions enables unobtrusive monitoring of mental states, allowing for the early identification of mood fluctuations, psychological stress, and suicide risk. For instance, these systems can play a crucial role in suicide prevention and help discern patterns in social media behavior associated with mental health conditions (Areán et al., 2016; Hamilton et al., 2025). Moreover, by operating directly on personal devices, these models safeguard user privacy while analyzing linguistic inputs to detect indicators of depression or stress (Shin et al., 2023). Therefore, on-device deployment offers a scalable and privacy-preserving solution for mental health monitoring, extending access beyond clinical settings and improving accessibility for a broader population.

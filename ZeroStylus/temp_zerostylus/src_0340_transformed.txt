We evaluate episodic memory retrieval performance across diverse temporal scales using temporal intersection over union (tIoU), which quantifies the overlap between retrieved and ground truth segments as the ratio of their intersection to union duration. To benchmark WorldMM, we compare its performance with various models in temporal grounding, single-modality retrieval, long-form egocentric video retrieval, and keyframe selection, as detailed in Sec. C.1. Notably, Tab. 3 highlights that WorldMM achieves significantly higher tIoU scores than strong baselines. However, retrieval methods relying on reasoning and keyframe selection demonstrate lower tIoU values, indicating challenges in handling extensive input contexts. Furthermore, Fig. 5 illustrates that improved tIoU values directly correspond to higher overall accuracy, particularly when comprehending extended video sequences compared to single-step retrieval approaches. This improvement arises because iterative retrieval enables the system to gather additional relevant information and refine its strategy when initial retrieval attempts are suboptimal. For instance, Sec. E.2 demonstrates how the model rectifies an initially irrelevant retrieval, subsequently producing a more precise and contextually relevant response. To evaluate the efficiency of our framework, we measure the end-to-end latency of WorldMM on 100 randomly sampled queries from EgoLifeQA. As shown in Fig. 6, our proposed method achieves a superior latency-accuracy trade-off compared to baseline approaches. Long-video large language models (LLMs) exhibit significantly higher inference latency while yielding relatively low performance. Similarly, RAG- or memory-based methods offer improved latency but require substantial preprocessing and still underperform in accuracy. In contrast, WorldMM adaptively terminates iterations and selectively retrieves only relevant segments, thereby achieving lower latency alongside notably higher accuracy. To address the challenges of reasoning over long video contexts effectively, WorldMM employs distinct strategies tailored to the processing of each memory type. Tab. 4 presents the performance of WorldMM-8B under various module configurations, with additional methodological details provided in Sec. C.2. Lastly, the use of episodic memory constrained to a fixed temporal scale or embedded representation inherently limits retrieval adaptability across diverse timescales, thereby posing challenges to achieving optimal performance.

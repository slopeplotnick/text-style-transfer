In this study, various machine learning techniques were applied to analyze EEG data with the aim of identifying motor imagery tasks. Specifically, the data processing involved extracting salient features and subsequently evaluating a range of algorithms, including classification methods, to determine their effectiveness. For instance, logistic regression with elastic-net regularization—combining L1 and L2 penalties—was utilized to address sparse data and mitigate overfitting. The saga solver was employed for optimization due to its effectiveness with large datasets. Additionally, the intercept scaling was set to 1000 to accommodate features of varying magnitudes, while a random state of 42 was used to ensure the reproducibility of results. Furthermore, a Random Forest Classifier was implemented, which aggregates multiple decision trees to enhance prediction accuracy and reduce overfitting. This methodology effectively addresses complex interdependencies among features and demonstrates reduced overfitting compared to individual decision tree models. In our methodology, Support Vector Machines (SVMs) were employed in two configurations: one utilizing a linear kernel for data amenable to linear separation and another employing an RBF kernel for data requiring transformation to a higher dimensional space to discern patterns. Moreover, Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) was incorporated into our analysis as it is effective for datasets that can be distinctly categorized by optimizing the variance between groups. The study also evaluated the performance of neural networks through various configurations of multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), ranging from simpler architectures with a single hidden layer to more intricate designs featuring three layers distributed across (10, 30, 10) or (20,) neurons. In this context, ReLU and logistic activation functions were employed, with training conducted using either the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) optimizer or the Adam solver, both of which autonomously adjust learning rates. L2 regularization was incorporated by utilizing the alpha parameter, and various alpha values were evaluated to determine the most effective approach. A constant learning rate was employed in certain MLPs to ensure stable training dynamics. Finally, both Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) and Adam optimization algorithms were fine-tuned to achieve optimal performance across various feature extraction techniques.

In the experimental evaluations, each MLP configuration is systematically assigned a unique identifier, ranging from MLP_1 to MLP_17. This consistent identification scheme is utilized across all assessments to ensure clarity and facilitate comparative analysis.

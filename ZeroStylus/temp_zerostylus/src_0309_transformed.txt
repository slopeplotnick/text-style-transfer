Despite the thorough cleaning of CPV data, noise persists due to item information quality issues, presenting a significant challenge. Moreover, item SKU features inherently contain substantial redundancy within the feature space because of their attribute characteristics. Thus, to validate feature effectiveness, a portion of the data is extracted and divided into training and test sets. Initially, the experiments employ product titles as a baseline and gradually incorporate additional features for incremental analysis. As demonstrated in Table 2, the integration of CPV and SKU features enhances model performance, suggesting that input information, if relevant and stable, can still contribute to model improvement even amidst partial redundancy and noise. Furthermore, to address the limitations of textual data in capturing certain visual attributes, primary item images were incorporated to enhance the representation of visual features. The exclusion of SKU images is justified as the main image already encapsulates the majority of visual information. Typically, SKU images depict minor variations, such as style or color, which are sufficiently conveyed through SKU textual features. Including SKU images would merely increase model complexity without yielding significant informational benefits. Ultimately, a comprehensive repository of product information was amassed, which will be employed in all subsequent training endeavors. In selecting the optimal base model, we considered two pivotal factors: the capability for complex reasoning and knowledge integration, as detailed in Section 2, alongside the efficiency required for practical deployment. Consequently, a series of outstanding open-source models with parameters ranging from 7B to 14B were selected, ensuring adequate capability while facilitating single-GPU training and inference. Subsequently, a portion of the pre-existing annotated data was selected and divided into training and validation subsets. Utilizing the basic relevance task definition as a prompt, simple fine-tuning was executed for each model on the training set, as outlined in Section 3.1, and performance was validated. Ultimately, the Qwen2.5-7B model exhibited superior performance compared to all evaluated models and maintained commendable inference efficiency, leading to its selection as the base model for our framework.

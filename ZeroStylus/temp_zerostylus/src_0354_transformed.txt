In the pursuit of replicating authentic human locomotion through virtual characters, enhancing realism in gaming and virtual reality experiences becomes imperative. Similarly, in the field of robotics, the facilitation of natural movement is essential as it inherently improves safety and optimizes energy efficiency. Instead of relying on manually programmed strategies, data-driven methodologies that focus on analyzing human movement patterns offer promising avenues for equipping robots with adaptable skills across diverse contexts. Furthermore, rather than depending on manual heuristics, data-driven techniques that utilize real-world motion data have demonstrated efficacy in generating realistic character movements. In this regard, certain techniques employ models—such as autoregressive models, variational autoencoders, or diffusion models—to generate plausible animations. Nevertheless, these methods, which often bypass strict adherence to physical principles, frequently produce movements that appear artificial, especially in unfamiliar scenarios. Conversely, physics-based approaches strive to produce movements that conform to real-world dynamics by utilizing control policies and governing equations of motion. To this end, these approaches employ techniques such as trajectory optimization or reinforcement learning. Prior approaches, which did not leverage data-driven methodologies, were capable of producing robot movements; however, these often appeared unnatural and lacked fluidity. Achieving realistic human-like motion in robotics remains challenging when solely relying on heuristic assumptions about movement dynamics. In this context, contemporary methods employ data-driven strategies, such as model predictive control or trajectory optimization, to more accurately replicate human movements. Recent advancements in methodologies utilizing reinforcement learning have shown efficacy in replicating authentic and dynamic movements by imitating clips of genuine motion. However, the limitation of these methods lies in constraining the robot to replicate only the actions depicted in the clip, thereby impeding the execution of novel tasks. To address this challenge, certain systems employ specialized planners or overarching policies to determine optimal movements and skills tailored to specific tasks.

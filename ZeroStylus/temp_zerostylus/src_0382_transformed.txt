We reassessed the robustness of pretrained Visual Language Architecture (VLA) models and demonstrated that performance degradation under visual perturbations primarily stems from representation shifts within spatial comprehension mechanisms. To address this limitation, we propose a novel framework for rapid robustness adaptation, which consists of Feature Token Modulation (FTM) and Feature Linear Adaptation (FLA). This approach efficiently recalibrates visual representations with minimal structural modifications, enabling models to better accommodate changes in perception. Furthermore, the efficacy of our method in generalizing to unseen camera viewpoints and visual perturbations is evidenced by state-of-the-art performance on the Libero benchmark, highlighting the untapped robustness potential inherent in existing VLA systems. VLAs aim to unify visual perception, linguistic comprehension, and decision-making within a cohesive framework, thereby facilitating seamless functionality across these interconnected domains. By leveraging pretrained vision-language foundations, recent VLAs—including RT-2, Pi0, PaLM-E, Octo, and OpenVLA—demonstrate robust generalization across diverse robotic tasks. These systems rely on large-scale multimodal datasets that integrate visual observations, linguistic directives, internal body states, and unstructured action sequences to establish a shared action policy capable of supporting versatile robotic behaviors. However, despite their advancements, empirical evaluations reveal that even state-of-the-art VLAs remain susceptible to fragility under distribution shifts, such as novel camera perspectives, changes in lighting conditions, or ambient scene noise. This brittleness persists even in models trained extensively on large-scale datasets, underscoring the challenges of deploying VLAs in real-world environments characterized by far greater visual complexity than controlled Libero settings. Addressing this deficiency is therefore critical for ensuring practical applicability. Enhancing the ability of VLAs to robustly manage visual alterations has been a longstanding challenge in the field. Prior studies have focused on improving viewpoint invariance by leveraging diverse datasets through domain randomization, multi-view demonstrations, or extensive visual perturbation data. While these data-centric strategies improve model robustness, they require substantial data acquisition or annotation, which poses significant scalability constraints.

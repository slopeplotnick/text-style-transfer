Consequently, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), similar to Large Language Models (LLMs), integrate visual inputs, thereby expanding semantic interpretation beyond textual data and introducing increased safety concerns. Jailbreaking involves employing sophisticated inputs to bypass established safety protocols, resulting in the generation of harmful content. This practice is significantly important as it contributes to enhancing the safety and reliability of these models. Historically, efforts to jailbreak LLMs primarily depended on techniques such as covert prompting or role-playing scenarios. With the advent of MLLMs, researchers have begun to exploit their visual input channels through adversarial techniques, aiming to reveal potential vulnerabilities associated with their multimodal capabilities. However, several existing methodologies display notable deficiencies that merit attention. To address this, one strategy involves crafting intricate visual content or embedding sensitive information within images; nevertheless, this method fails to fully capitalize on the contextual information provided by the input. Alternatively, some approaches articulate the scenario through textual description, akin to the method employed with LLMs; however, this approach does not utilize the unique capabilities afforded by visual representations. To address these issues, we propose a method termed the Contextual-Image Attack (CIA). In order to illustrate both the context and detrimental intent, we employ a methodology that generates images, with textual input serving solely as an auxiliary aid. For instance, one might initially depict a scenario in which a teacher poses the inquiry, "How can one manufacture illicit substances?" during a chemistry lesson, rather than merely presenting the question within an image. The question's apparent legitimacy introduces ambiguity, leading the model to misinterpret the underlying malicious intent and fail to discern the negative implications. Conversely, some approaches, such as FigStep, present the query as text embedded within an image, or rely solely on verbal descriptions to convey the context. In contrast, the proposed method demonstrates superiority by employing images with enhanced effectiveness. Moreover, we discuss the increasing utility of advanced image generation technology due to its enhanced capability for creating realistic scenarios, alongside the emergence of novel technologies that facilitate the conversion of textual input into visual data. To further illustrate the potential for deception, we demonstrate how images are constructed to encapsulate complex hidden meanings, thereby misleading models.

The global transition towards increased electrification has consequently shifted attention to the exploration of critical minerals. Despite the growing demand driven by global needs, the mineral exploration industry remains largely unprofitable. Moreover, even with heightened financial investment in the sector, there has been a notable decline in the discovery of new deposits over the past two decades. In this paper, I propose that the integration of artificial intelligence can serve as a catalyst for enhancing the scientific rigor of mineral exploration. This integration aims to mitigate cognitive biases and false positives, augment the role of domain experts, and reduce exploration costs. Current exploration methodologies, which encompass disciplines such as geology, geophysics, and geochemistry, have proven inadequate for effectively locating concealed deposits. Crucially, the existing approach fails to adequately quantify uncertainty, leading to suboptimal decision-making and inefficient allocation of resources toward drilling initiatives that frequently result in false positives. Therefore, I suggest a novel methodological framework rooted in the philosophical principles of Bayesianism and falsification, whereby data collection is primarily utilized to test and potentially falsify human-generated hypotheses. Additionally, the selection of subsequent data acquisition is conducted through precise metrics and informed decision-making. A practical guide is outlined to serve as a foundational tool applicable across various mineral exploration efforts. However, for this framework to be practically implemented, diverse AI technologies are essential. Among these, novel unsupervised learning approaches, which collaborate with experts to enhance data interpretation and generate multiple geological hypotheses, and human-in-the-loop AI systems, which optimize data collection strategies across geology, geophysics, geochemistry, and drilling, are particularly significant. These systems aim to reduce uncertainty regarding geological concepts before addressing the quantity and quality of mineral resources. Finally, I will demonstrate the application of this method through contemporary cases in mineral search and resource evaluation.

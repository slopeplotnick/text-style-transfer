The problem-solving approaches of SWE-agent Dev and SWE-agent DSK-V3 exhibit notable differences in their execution strategies, despite ultimately achieving resolution. Specifically, SWE-agent Dev initiates the process by identifying the bug in the client.py file (steps 1-4), followed by constructing a reproduction test (step 5) and systematically editing multiple sections of the file (steps 6-8). However, after encountering repetitive failures during steps 9-10, the agent revisits and modifies a different block of the client.py file (steps 11 and 12), subsequently executing the reproduction test (step 13) to validate the patch and concluding with the final submission (steps 14-15). In contrast, SWE-agent DSK-V3 begins similarly by localizing the bug in the client.py file (steps 1-6), but diverges by performing a single edit (step 7) and directly preparing the final submission without further validation (steps 8-9). Despite their success, both agents exhibit strategic limitations. Firstly, SWE-agent Dev employs a methodical approach by editing client.py line by line (steps 6-8), repeating failed edits twice (steps 9-10), and performing the final edit at step 12, whereas SWE-agent DSK-V3 generates the patch in a single attempt at step 7. Moreover, neither agent conducts comprehensive regression tests to validate the patch; SWE-agent Dev restricts verification to reproduction tests (step 13), while SWE-agent DSK-V3 only considers the patch's correctness in relation to edge cases (step 8). These observations underscore the importance of analyzing procedural trajectories to gain insights into the problem-solving strategies and challenges faced by agents. Recent research has attempted to analyze agentic workflows by deriving taxonomies of failure modes through manual expert analysis of agentic trajectories [11, 12, 15, 23, 29]. While these taxonomies provide valuable insights, the reliance on manual analysis introduces biases, incompleteness, and scalability challenges, particularly for emerging systems. More importantly, the representation of trajectories as linear chronological sequences fails to adequately capture the semantic aspects of agentic behavior, including execution flow, problem-solving strategies, and the efficiency or adherence to expected planning. Therefore, a systematic and comprehensive approach to analyzing agentic systems is essential to better understand their task execution and problem-solving methodologies.

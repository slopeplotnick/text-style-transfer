CAPO employs advantage as a dynamic signal that aligns with the model's evolving competence, thereby enhancing coherence in task scheduling. Since the introduction of curriculum learning by Bengio et al. in 2009, prevalent methodologies have relied on static heuristics, such as organizing tasks from simpler to more complex. However, subsequent adaptive curriculum strategies have sought to estimate difficulty using expert annotations or model success rates, yet these remain external and heuristic, relying on manually defined proxies rather than signals intrinsic to the model's evolving capability. This limitation motivated the design of CAPO, which integrates advantage estimates as an intrinsic, competence-aware signal to dynamically guide curriculum scheduling. Extensive experimental results indicate that CAPO consistently enhances mathematical reasoning across diverse advantage-based reinforcement learning algorithms, including GRPO, PPO, RLOO, and Reinforce++. Moreover, CAPO demonstrates strong modality generalization, achieving notable gains on tasks involving graphical user interfaces (GUIs), thereby underscoring its versatility across multiple reasoning domains. In summary, our contributions are threefold: firstly, we introduce advantage as a dynamic curriculum signal and design CAPO as a two-phase mechanism, comprising imitation and discrimination phases, that adapts to the model's evolving competence. Secondly, we illustrate CAPO's broad generalization and effectiveness across various advantage-based reinforcement learning algorithms and its transferability to multimodal reasoning tasks in GUI-based environments. By leveraging these signals, CAPO explicitly structures training into an imitation phase and a discrimination phase, thereby reconciling the need for early-stage stability with the demand for enhanced generalization once negative samples are introduced.

The team at the Agentic Learning AI Lab at New York University, comprising Jack Lu, Ryan Teehan, Jinran Jin, and Mengye Ren, actively contributes to a range of AI research initiatives. Interested parties may engage with the lab's activities through their NYU electronic mail or their web portal. On November 20, 2025, the focus of discussions was on the dual capabilities of large language models, particularly emphasizing their roles in problem-solving and solution verification. Notably, the process of evaluation is pivotal in identifying the most optimal answers. Historically, research has predominantly concentrated on models’ self-verification capabilities, with comparatively less investigation into their ability to assess responses from other models. Following the primary training phase, models undergo extensive supplementary training; however, the implications of this additional training on verification processes remain uncertain. Subsequently, an extensive comparative analysis was conducted on 37 models encompassing various groups and sizes, both recently trained and those subjected to supplementary training. The evaluation framework systematically assessed performance across nine distinct problem categories, including logical reasoning, puzzles, mathematical computations, practical judgment, and factual knowledge. This study conducts comparative evaluations on the mechanisms by which models self-assess their own outputs versus evaluating outputs from models within the same cohort or across different cohorts. Moreover, this innovative approach devises a novel metric to quantify the enhancement in response accuracy attributable to verification procedures and subjects it to empirical validation. Therefore, this investigation is vital as it examines the variation of these metrics with respect to model size and supplementary training, alongside the feasibility of verifying diverse data categories. The study indicates that, while verifying responses from diverse model groups is highly effective, additional training enhances a model's ability to evaluate responses from other groups rather than its own; furthermore, mathematical and logical queries are the most straightforward to verify. Consequently, models now autonomously address problems and verify the accuracy of solutions, facilitating comprehensive and objective evaluations across numerous instances. The process of verifying answers during examinations can range from straightforward methods, such as generating multiple responses and employing a validation mechanism to identify the optimal one, to more intricate approaches that involve iterative refinement of the responses. In essence, implementing verification during testing can enhance the model's capacity to tackle more complex challenges than it could independently.

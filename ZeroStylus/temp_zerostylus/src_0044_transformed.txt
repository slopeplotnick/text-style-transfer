Human comprehension of video content involves discerning the sequence of events, understanding the motivations behind actions, and identifying the timing of significant occurrences. However, conventional video object segmentation approaches primarily focus on leveraging visual attributes or semantic categories of objects. We elevate the complexity of reasoning video object segmentation (VOS) by necessitating the model to comprehend ambiguous directives, employ common sense and causal reasoning, and integrate temporal shifts with knowledge to accurately identify targets, such as "the runner most likely to win" or "the object causing the accident." This approach simplifies processes but complicates comprehension, alters model functionality, and necessitates substantial data for precise calibration. The primary concept posits that proficient video reasoning entails executing deliberate decision-making processes rather than relying on a singular rapid conjecture. In this context, the model discerns where to focus, identifies the appropriate timing for attention, and determines the subject of the inquiry. Motivated by this principle, we introduce ReVSeg, which eliminates concealed segmentation tokens and reconceptualizes reasoning video segmentation as a coherent sequence of reasoning steps. Specifically, the reasoning video object segmentation task is decomposed into three model-native capabilities: video comprehension, temporal localization, and spatial localization, facilitating the identification of the inquiry, relevant temporal frames, and spatial objects within selected frames. This streamlined methodology ensures that (1) the initial processing steps effectively inform subsequent stages, (2) the model maintains continuity of meaning throughout the process, and (3) the integration of earlier insights enhances the coherence of later outputs. Thus, this approach maintains the model's alignment with its training parameters, thereby circumventing extensive retraining and transforming a complex issue into a streamlined procedure. Each reasoning step constitutes a distinct progression, presenting the critical challenge of optimizing these steps to enhance both their efficiency and effectiveness. However, the limited utility of supervised learning arises from the absence of well-defined labels for intermediate steps, thereby impeding its effectiveness. Consequently, we employ a reinforcement learning framework that conceptualizes reasoning VOS as a behavior rewarded exclusively when the entire decision-making trajectory culminates in a correct outcome. The challenge of employing reinforcement learning arises from the limited success signals in video object segmentation, necessitating coordination among video understanding, temporal selection, and spatial localization.

To address the model's reasoning capabilities, rewards are integrated at critical decision points, thereby enhancing the model's performance through reinforcement learning. At the onset of reinforcement learning training, the process transitions from a concealed guessing approach to a structured reasoning framework. Here, task decomposition clarifies the model's decision-making objectives, while reinforcement learning further refines the coordination of these decisions to achieve optimal outcomes. Moreover, the collaborative approach establishes a reasoning framework that not only maintains internal consistency but also demonstrates robust practical effectiveness.

The daughter language model has been specifically developed as part of the MOD-OMA. Similar to Delilah, this model functions as both a parser and a generator. However, it differs from the adult language model in that its grammatical representations are initially underspecified and become more detailed through language acquisition processes. A notable similarity between the adult and child linguistic systems is their use of graph-based structures to represent grammatical knowledge. These structures facilitate the operation of parsers and generators, allowing them to unify lexical configurations to produce syntactically correct sentences and assess grammaticality based on the current grammatical framework. Importantly, the daughter language model can process incomplete grammar, akin to the way humans acquire their first language, necessitating iterative interactions with the parent model prior to achieving full proficiency. The daughter agent is comprised of three components: an automaton serving as both parser and generator, a grammar framework refined through learning, and a language acquisition device. Crucially, the parser and generator remain unaltered during the language acquisition process.

This paper aims to provide a forward-looking exploration of the utilization of artificial intelligence in critical mineral identification, thereby underscoring its potential applications in mineral exploration. The perspective articulated herein is derived from my personal interpretation, informed by three decades of experience in data science, decision science, and artificial intelligence within the domain of Earth resource exploration. In 1998, I authored a paper on the application of neural networks trained with outcrop data to enhance geological realism in reservoir models, aiming to make a significant impact as an early-career academic at a major conference in New Orleans, despite the imminent threat posed by Hurricane George. The incorporation of geological realism is imperative for accurately predicting fluid dynamics in porous media, which consequently leads to improved decision-making in oil and gas exploration. Furthermore, in 2002, I published another paper demonstrating the use of neural networks trained on borehole and geophysical data to predict reservoir properties, such as porosity, even in areas devoid of borehole information. Addressing the challenges associated with varying data scales, notably the disparity between borehole core data and seismic signals deep underground, constituted a crucial aspect of this work. This issue of scale will be thoroughly examined in the forthcoming discourse. In the context of developing offshore platforms, oil and gas companies routinely incorporate geological uncertainty into their planning processes. However, many mining operations persist in utilizing simplistic models. In mineral exploration, the prevalence of straightforward models and data interpretation remains, yet it is necessary to acknowledge that the minerals industry has not fully embraced quantitative risk assessment to the extent observed in the oil and gas sector.

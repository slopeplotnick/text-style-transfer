In 2024, content is deconstructed into distinct functional and semantic sections to enhance clarity, with these sections subsequently summarized in multiple languages and utilized for interactive Q&A purposes. To support these tasks, we incorporate transcribed multimodal spoken translation outputs as contextual information. However, despite the advancements in modern large language models (LLMs), their capacity to process extended contexts remains constrained by limited context windows. Therefore, we address this limitation by segmenting lecture content into chapters, each focused on a single topic. This approach not only prevents context-window overflow but also produces conceptually cleaner summaries by ensuring that chapters remain locally coherent and avoid the topic drift commonly observed in global summaries. For each chapter, we generate compressed representations at varying levels of compression (e.g., 50%, 70%, 90%), alongside length-controlled summaries proportional to the original section's length. Initially, these summaries are generated in English to leverage the superior performance of LLMs in this language, and they are subsequently translated into other target languages. For Q&A tasks, a similar methodology is employed. English transcripts are segmented into chapters and used in conjunction with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to formulate queries for the LLM, with the resulting responses then translated into the desired languages. Meanwhile, another critical aspect of ensuring accessibility involves translating slide content into multiple languages. Slides often contain two types of textual content: editable text and embedded text within images. For editable text, we utilize a Python-based PowerPoint parser to extract text blocks, which are then translated using standard unimodal machine translation methods. This approach avoids the use of multimodal models, which are computationally expensive. However, translating text embedded in images poses unique challenges, as such text often lacks surrounding linguistic context and depends heavily on visual elements for interpretation. To address this, our methodology incorporates multimodal translation to ensure that both the linguistic and visual meaning of the text are preserved. Following translation, the text is reinserted into the original image to maintain both its layout and semantic integrity. To achieve this, we propose an image-to-image translation pipeline that identifies, extracts, translates, and reintegrates textual content within slide images, thereby ensuring the accessibility and coherence of the translated materials.

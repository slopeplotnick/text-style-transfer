SMP functions as a modular framework for training motion policies through the use of style-based rewards, thereby operating without the necessity of accessing the original motion dataset. Consequently, various methodologies can be employed to refine the reward model and adapt the mechanics of motion dynamics. However, this can sometimes result in movements that appear to deviate from natural human motion. The supplementary video illustrates qualitative comparisons of these different methods. Moreover, SMP facilitates the creation of novel motion styles by modifying the outputs of the pretrained model, enabling the generation of previously unavailable styles without requiring further training. For instance, new styles can be synthesized by combining two existing styles, such as "AeroPlane" and "HighKnees," as illustrated in Figure 6. To achieve this synthesis, we integrate the predictions derived from the model. As demonstrated in Table 2, a single pretrained motion model can facilitate the training of policies across a range of tasks, including steering, target localization, and dodgeball gameplay, thereby enabling versatile task execution. Within this framework, the motion model, trained on a segment of the LaFAN1 dataset characterized by stochastic running dynamics, inherently captures the diverse spectrum of locomotion patterns, facilitating a comprehensive understanding of random running movements. While the trained policies exhibit proficiency in task execution, the natural appearance of these policies remains consistent and unaltered. As shown in Figures 5(a) and 5(b), the character seamlessly transitions between various movements, maintaining the task's continuity while ensuring a natural appearance. In scenarios where the character's orientation deviates from its intended path, the steering policy induces a backward jog, subsequently transitioning to a forward jog upon reorientation in the correct direction. It also incorporates lateral gaits, such as side-stepping and cross-stepping, seamlessly and without necessitating an independent motion planner. Furthermore, in the Target Location task, when trained with SMP, the policy autonomously exhibits human-like behaviors, such as automatically decelerating when necessary.

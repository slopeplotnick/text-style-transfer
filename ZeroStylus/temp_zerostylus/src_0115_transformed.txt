Human cognition excels in processing and understanding spatial environments using visual data alone. To this end, we introduce VIDEO4SPATIAL, a novel framework demonstrating that video diffusion models, conditioned solely on video-based scene context, can effectively perform complex spatial tasks. Specifically, we evaluate the framework on two tasks: scene navigation, which involves following camera-pose instructions while maintaining consistency with the three-dimensional geometry of the scene, and object grounding, which requires semantic localization, instruction following, and strategic planning. Notably, both tasks rely exclusively on video inputs, without the inclusion of auxiliary modalities such as depth maps or precise camera pose information. Through simple yet effective design choices in both the framework and data curation, VIDEO4SPATIAL exhibits robust spatial understanding from video context. It achieves end-to-end planning for navigation and object grounding, adheres to camera-pose instructions while preserving spatial consistency, and generalizes effectively to extended contexts as well as out-of-domain environments. Taken together, these findings represent a significant advancement in enabling video generative models to perform general visuospatial reasoning tasks. Humans possess an exceptional ability to remember, comprehend, and act within spatial environments, which is a hallmark of high-level spatial reasoning capabilities. Consequently, a substantial body of research has focused on developing neural networks that emulate these abilities. Recent advancements in video generative models have begun to address this challenge, demonstrating general-purpose perceptual and reasoning capabilities that parallel the trajectory of large language models. Notably, video generation inherently aligns with visuospatial tasks due to its sequential nature and temporal coherence. By generating future frames that maintain scene geometry and visual appearance, these models can simulate plausible future scenarios. For instance, in maze navigation, a video model can "imagine" progress by generating frames that preserve the maze's topology while ensuring smooth transitions between scenes. Given that humans primarily perceive spatial information visually, we aim to endow models with similar capabilities by leveraging video as a simple, general, and scalable modality. However, learning spatial understanding solely from RGB video data remains challenging. Many contemporary approaches continue to rely on auxiliary signals, such as depth maps or camera poses, for supervision or augmentation. In contrast, our framework, VIDEO4SPATIAL, demonstrates that video generative models relying solely on the video context of a spatial environment can achieve strong spatial intelligence. The framework employs a standard video diffusion architecture trained exclusively with the diffusion objective. Its inputs consist of a video context—several frames captured from the same environment—and task-specific instructions, while its output is a video that completes the instructed spatial task while maintaining scene geometry and temporal coherence.

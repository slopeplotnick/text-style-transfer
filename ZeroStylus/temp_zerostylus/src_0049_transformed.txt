We recruited 17 volunteers (P1-P17) through school posters and random street invitations. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB), and for all underage participants, guardian consent was obtained. To ensure confidentiality, all interview materials were securely stored, and no personally identifiable information was used. Most volunteers reported familiarity with creating short stories, with an average self-assessed proficiency score of 3.8 on a 5-point Likert scale. Furthermore, all participants had prior experience composing narratives, either through traditional handwriting or using digital word processing tools. For the workshop, volunteers were divided into groups of approximately four individuals, each supervised by a designated staff member. The workshop, which lasted approximately 2.5 hours, was structured into four key phases. During the session, volunteers were encouraged to verbalize their cognitive processes using a "think-aloud" approach. This technique enabled the capture and analysis of their initial ideas, final outputs, and the iterative steps of their creative process. Subsequently, four participants were selected for follow-up discussions to further explore their experiences. By analyzing the workshop recordings and interview data, we identified and classified four distinct modes of interaction that emerged during the story creation process. The workshop structure was as follows: 1) *Introduction and Overview* (15 minutes). The staff provided a concise overview of the workshop's objectives and structure, emphasizing the application of the four foundational units of narrative creation through illustrative examples. Volunteers were reminded to adopt the "think-aloud" approach throughout the workshop to enhance transparency in their decision-making processes. 2) *Group Discussion and Story Framework Creation* (60 minutes). The staff distributed cards to volunteers, and each group engaged in collaborative discussions to develop their own story framework using the four foundational components. During this phase, volunteers were encouraged to articulate their thoughts and deliberations, while staff members circulated to offer guidance and support as needed. 3) *Story Sharing and Feedback* (45 minutes). Volunteers presented their narratives, detailing how they utilized the four foundational units in story construction. Both peers and staff provided formative feedback, focusing on creativity, coherence, and structure. 4) *Informal Interviews with Volunteers* (30 minutes). Finally, four volunteers (P1-P4) were selected for informal interviews to gain further insight into their creative experiences during the workshop.

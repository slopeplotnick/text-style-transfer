In this investigation, we aim to identify patterns indicative of malfunctioning or regressive processes. GRAPHECTORY serves as a sophisticated tool that facilitates this identification with increased ease. To achieve this objective, we first delineate a set of heuristics that may suggest inefficacy. Heuristic H1 emerges when an individual persists in repetitive actions that fail to resolve the issue, indicating the necessity for alternative approaches. Conversely, H2 describes a scenario where an individual exhibits non-linear engagement with the project, frequently shifting focus rather than progressing systematically. Furthermore, H3 is characterized by the prolonged expenditure of time on a task without substantial progress. Additionally, H4 occurs when there is a malfunction, potentially indicating suboptimal utilization of available tools. The pattern termed RepeatedView manifests when an individual continuously revisits the same section of a project, such as a file, suggesting redundant inspections of the same code, often following unsuccessful edits or when the solution remains elusive. For instance, despite encountering two consecutive failed attempts to edit client.py during step 9, the agents proceed to reopen the same file in step 10, aiming to resolve the underlying issue. Moreover, the term ZoomOut refers to scenarios where the sequence of actions deviates from the hierarchical structure of the project, whereby progression occurs from a more specific component to a broader one. This pattern indicates a misalignment, as evidenced by the subjects' recognition of their incorrect positioning. Specifically, within the process, participants initially navigate to an incorrect directory, postgres (step 3), subsequently returning to the primary directory /testbed/db (step 4). Thereafter, they correctly access the directory /testbed/db/postgreSQL (step 5) to locate the problematic file client.py (step 6).

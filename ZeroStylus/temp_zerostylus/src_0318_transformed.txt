Summarization. As demonstrated in Table 3, summaries generated using multimodal inputs, combining audio and image data, consistently outperform those derived solely from audio across most languages and models, despite the summarization models relying exclusively on textual data. These improvements are particularly notable for English, German, and Italian; however, results for Chinese show a slight decline. This disparity can likely be attributed to the prevalence of English-domain terminology in languages utilizing the Latin alphabet, which facilitates effective integration, whereas the substantial lexical and structural differences between English and Chinese hinder the ability of models to leverage supplementary English information effectively. Question Answering. Regarding inquiry responses, the inclusion of visual elements offers minor enhancements, although these gains are substantially less pronounced compared to their contributions to summarization. While slight improvements are noted in most configurations, performance regression is observed in four out of twelve language-model combinations. This inconsistency is likely explained by the fact that the large language models (LLMs) do not directly process image data but instead rely on the enriched textual context derived from multimodal inputs, which appears insufficient to reliably improve question-answering capabilities. Streaming Speech Translation. Over the past decade, the field of streaming speech translation (ST) has been extensively investigated (Macháček et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2025; Papi et al., 2025a). Moreover, several tools for lecture translation have adopted ST approaches (Müller et al., 2016; Dessloch et al., 2018; Huber et al., 2023); however, these systems predominantly focus on audio-based processing. In contrast, the present study advances lecture translation by incorporating multimodal input, integrating visual cues from slides, and generating multimodal output, which includes translated audio and slides in multiple languages. Image-to-Image Translation. Despite recent advancements, the task of image-to-image translation remains relatively underexplored. Interest in this domain is growing, driven by the availability of larger datasets (Zuo et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025; Zhuang et al., 2025). Nevertheless, existing research primarily concentrates on text translation within images, often neglecting the re-rendering of visual content to preserve alignment and integrity. A preliminary step toward addressing this gap is presented by Tian et al. (2025), which explicitly models the rendering process. Building on this foundation, the image translation pipeline introduced in this study offers modular flexibility, allowing researchers to seamlessly integrate their models at any stage of the process—from optical character recognition (OCR) to text translation and image rendering—without necessitating the development of additional components.

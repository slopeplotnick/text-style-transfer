Humans demonstrate an exceptional capacity to rapidly acquire proficiency in new tasks when provided with precise instructions, whether conveyed through words or diagrams, without requiring supplementary practice. The investigation into neural and artificial intelligence systems has focused on their functional mechanisms in sensing, cognition, and motor control by deconstructing these processes into three fundamental components: the organization of circuits, the processing of inputs and outputs, and the mechanisms of learning. Recent advancements indicate that contemporary artificial intelligence systems are beginning to emulate neural mechanisms, leading to significant insights into cerebral operations. Specifically, the neocortex and cerebellum engage in predictive modeling by utilizing prior information to anticipate forthcoming events, striving to enhance the precision of these predictions. This is of notable importance as it aids in the simplification and organization of information into compact yet powerful models. Furthermore, these models serve three primary functions: they forecast future events, interpret the world through abstract constructs such as features and causal relationships (enabling object recognition or text comprehension), and generate outputs including actions, plans, or language. Thus, the shared computational architecture of information processing enables the neocortex and cerebellum to support a diverse array of functions, despite their similarities in structural organization and operational mechanisms. In conclusion, this form of information processing represents a shared operational framework that underpins numerous capabilities of both the human brain and artificial intelligence systems. Gaining a deeper understanding of this subject will advance both neuroscience and AI, while providing a clearer framework for comprehending the mechanisms underlying intelligence. The human brain demonstrates a significantly accelerated learning capability compared to large language models, requiring substantially fewer examples to achieve proficiency, while also exhibiting far greater efficiency in its operations. Recent advancements in artificial intelligence have led to the development of models that exhibit enhanced learning capabilities and improved efficiency by emulating neural mechanisms, such as employing Mixture-of-Experts techniques; however, these systems remain inferior to the cognitive proficiency of the human brain. In summary, the continuous advancement of artificial intelligence through neural-inspired learning mechanisms will reciprocally contribute to the expansion of neuroscientific knowledge by examining these AI systems.

The collection of negative samples encompasses all potential active nodes, thereby ensuring comprehensive coverage for subsequent stages of the process. As demonstrated in Section 5, the integration of both random and adversarial negatives is essential for surpassing the performance of TextMatch. The objective of contrastive loss is formulated as a cross-entropy loss, wherein the model must identify the correct positive candidate for each query from among all candidates in the batch. The encoder is initialized using publicly available E5-unsupervised checkpoints, which are characterized by 33 million and 330 million parameters, and benefit from advanced contrastive pretraining methodologies. Consistency is maintained by applying the same encoder weights to both queries and candidates. Prior to fine-tuning, dense interaction data comprising 110 million samples, based on weak signals such as clicks or saves and naturally occurring pairs of titles and descriptions, is utilized. However, the fine-tuning stage incorporates more sparse yet impactful interactions that demonstrate mutual interest between parties, such as interviews or hires, combined with hard negatives derived from items impressed but not engaged with, as recorded in interaction logs. The GraphMatch training dataset consists of one year of user interaction data from Upwork, partitioned into 10 months for training, 1 month for validation, and 1 month for evaluation. The dataset comprises nodes representing freelancers, clients, and job posts, with edges denoting interactions such as job postings, applications, invitations, interviews, or contract initiations. Freelancer and job post nodes are associated with textual features, namely profile overviews and job descriptions. Moreover, nodes, including freelancers and clients, are linked to numerical features relevant to their type, such as hourly rates, geographic locations, and job categories. This dynamic interplay indicates that while text features serve as a foundation, numerical attributes evolve over time to provide additional insights. In total, the dataset encompasses approximately 9 million nodes, characterized by 32 million temporal feature versions and interconnected through 62 million edges. Ultimately, GraphMatch, when trained on robust labels derived from contracts between freelancers and job postings, achieves notable performance.

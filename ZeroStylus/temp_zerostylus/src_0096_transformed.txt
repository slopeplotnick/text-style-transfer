The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has demonstrated remarkable potential in complex text-generation tasks such as academic writing, literature reviews, and scientific reports. To enhance the process of extracting information from extensive datasets, LLM-based systems are employed to integrate searching, organizing, and synthesis into a unified and streamlined procedure. Consequently, both academic researchers and corporate entities have developed distinct systems aimed at generating surveys. These automated writing systems can be broadly categorized into three primary types: general long-text writing systems (e.g., Kimi, GLM) that provide capabilities for extended text generation; paper-writing systems (e.g., Chengpian, Doubao Paper Mode) that focus on structured academic paper creation; and survey-writing agents (e.g., SurveyGo, SurveyX, Panshi ScienceOne) specifically designed for academic survey generation. These systems not only process large volumes of literature and extract key information but also generate well-structured survey drafts with logical coherence and appropriate referencing. However, despite significant advancements in developing these systems, establishing reliable evaluation benchmarks remains a substantial challenge. Currently, the reliance on subjective opinions for evaluation results in inconsistent and non-reproducible outcomes, which hinders systematic comparison of systems, identification of effective methodologies, and subsequent improvement. Therefore, establishing a standardized framework for the evaluation of these systems is crucial to ensure the assessment of their quality and reliability, thereby providing researchers with a consistent basis for comparison and analysis. To address this need, we developed SurveyEval, an assessment tool designed to analyze surveys generated by these systems based on three criteria: overall quality, coherence of the outline, and accuracy of the references. Furthermore, to enhance the alignment of automated evaluations with human perspectives across seven academic fields, we improved the LLM-as-a-Judge approach by incorporating surveys crafted by humans alongside the generated assessments.

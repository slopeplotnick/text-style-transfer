During the execution of the benchmark harness, the platform may produce noise due to background processes and other confounding factors. To mitigate some of these confounds, we disabled the platform's wireless networking hardware and collected multiple samples at varying time points to filter out external noise. The configuration of our statistical model, which constitutes the foundation of our research, holds significant importance; however, it may not be entirely flawless. In our analysis, we delineate several assumptions supported by prior research and our interpretation of the data distribution. This approach is particularly crucial as it minimizes potential personal biases inherent in the model. Nevertheless, as with any model, the choice of model parameters, their distribution, and the parameters themselves are subject to debate. Our selection criteria are grounded in the JVM specification and the formal definition of programming languages, with a focus on components delineated within Java Bytecode. The use of the Gaussian distribution is justified by its frequent occurrence in natural phenomena and its congruence with the principles of aggregation and equilibrium as articulated by the central limit theorem. Our understanding of the measurements is limited to their average and range, which highlights the inherent uncertainty of this distribution. The primary challenges pertain to the configuration of the system and the specifications of the JVM. To mitigate alterations in the memory state that could influence the execution outcome of each bytecode pattern, the garbage collector is executed prior to each test. Furthermore, to address potential biases arising from concurrent paging, caching, and related processes, each bytecode pattern is subjected to ten iterations, with the sequence randomized for each iteration.

Our comprehensive evaluation reaffirms that spatial methods, particularly covariance tangent-space projection and Common Spatial Patterns, remain the most effective approaches for EEG motor imagery decoding under controlled conditions. However, their efficacy markedly deteriorates in scenarios with significant inter- and intra-subject variability. By systematically assessing each pipeline at the individual subject level, it becomes evident that no universal configuration can adequately address the diverse neurophysiological profiles of all users. Therefore, adaptive methodologies that dynamically align with individual neurophysiological signatures emerge as the most promising path forward. Moreover, nonlinear features such as Hjorth parameters, Higuchi fractal dimension, and singular value decomposition entropy demonstrated notable efficacy for certain individuals, highlighting the importance of capturing richer neural dynamics. This insight suggests that "BCI illiteracy" may stem from mismatches between decoding algorithms and user-specific neural patterns rather than inherent user deficiencies. Consequently, future research should prioritize the development of advanced ensemble learning frameworks that integrate linear spatial filters with nonlinear dynamics, effectively leveraging their complementary advantages. Furthermore, extending the scope of this benchmark to include additional paradigms, such as SSVEP, P300, CVEP, and invasive intracortical recordings, will be essential for testing the generalizability of these findings. In addition, rigorous exploration of cutting-edge deep-learning architectures, including Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs), and Transformers, alongside emerging quantum-inspired models such as quantum support-vector machines and hybrid quantum neural networks, holds significant potential. Quantum methodologies, by exploiting the high-dimensional properties of Hilbert spaces, may offer unprecedented robustness against the inherent noise and complexity of EEG signals. Similarly, improving cross-subject generalization through transfer learning and domain adaptation techniques has demonstrated substantial potential to enhance model scalability and robustness across heterogeneous user populations. Finally, enhancing reproducibility remains a critical priority, which can be achieved through open-access benchmarking initiatives such as MOABB, enabling standardized and transparent comparisons of EEG decoding algorithms.

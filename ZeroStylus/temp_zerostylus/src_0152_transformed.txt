The data and results from this study are available in an online repository: https://cite.lirmm.fr/tgeorges/interviewartefact. This repository is organized to include the interview questions and corresponding answers, the analysis of the interviews, topic lists, the Python notebook, and several bar charts illustrating the responses. In this section, we analyze the findings in relation to the research questions, focusing on current good practices (Sect. 4.1), benefits (Sect. 4.2), risks (Sect. 4.3), and fears regarding the future of SPL (Sect. 4.4). Table 3 provides a detailed summary of the identified good practices, benefits, risks, and fears, which are further examined in the subsequent analysis. The results are presented according to topics and roles, adhering to the methodology employed in the design process. Notably, the responses from interviewees show an even distribution. When analyzing responses based on years of professional experience, it is evident that individuals with both minimal and extensive experience contributed a comparable volume of answers. For example, in response to the question "Which good practices can be derived from this approach?", developers with four and seven years of experience provided identical answers. To further elucidate the distribution of responses based on experience levels, bar charts are available in the online repository linked within the article. This part of the interviews aimed to identify the current good practices that should be retained in the new approach. Among these, Factorization and Reuse were the most frequently mentioned practices, cited nine and eight times, respectively. This observation is reinforced by team discussions, where each new feature is evaluated to determine whether it is specific to a single application or broadly applicable across multiple applications. Moreover, the concept of innovation emerged as a significant motivator, encouraging the team to continuously update their expertise in alignment with industry standards. This emphasis on innovation is crucial to the participants, as it enables them to consistently refine and enhance the codebase.

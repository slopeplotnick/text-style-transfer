By analyzing the contributions of audio and visual inputs, we ascertain which modality exhibits stronger emotional expression in each sample. Following the construction of a comprehensive dataset with human assistance, alongside a filtered counterpart, these datasets are combined to facilitate the training process. Subsequently, the mutual information (MI) for each sample is identified. Upon determining the predominant input modality for emotional conveyance, we adjust the sequence of the reasoning text accordingly. In our approach, reasoning is bifurcated for each training example, encompassing distinct segments dedicated to audio and video, thus facilitating the examination of modality-specific contributions. Figure 6 illustrates the utilization of specific markers, <aud_desc> and <vis_desc>, to delineate the respective components. Once the texts are organized according to the MI, they are carefully arranged in the desired order. By comparing the emotional intensity of the modalities, the audio component is prioritized when it exhibits a greater degree of emotion, followed by the video component. Conversely, when video content is more extensive, our approach involves pursuing the inverse course of action. To account for scenarios where both modalities perform equivalently, two variants are produced: one initialized with the auditory input and the other with the visual input. As the reasoning process unfolds, the model begins to prioritize inputs most effectively conveying emotional cues. Following the initial phase, Modality-Aligned Reward Optimization is employed to enhance the model's reasoning capabilities. This approach incorporates distinct reward mechanisms, differentiating between responses that are correct and those exhibiting optimal structural organization. By examining these elements, we determine which input source most effectively conveys emotional content. In this framework, supplementary reward models are introduced to evaluate sequence accuracy and emotional reasoning derived from input data.

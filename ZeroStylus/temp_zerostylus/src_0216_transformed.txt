The development of a robust relevance model necessitates the decomposition of the task into item comprehension, query interpretation, and the alignment of navigational pathways. To achieve this, the framework must efficiently manage an extensive array of items while accommodating diverse user requirements, whether they manifest as direct or more nuanced demands. Therefore, it is imperative to incorporate external knowledge and employ logical reasoning to resolve ambiguities within queries, thereby enabling the comprehension of abstract user objectives during path mapping. Moreover, to address the critical role of visual-heavy items, the system must seamlessly integrate functionalities for processing both textual and image-based inputs, ensuring precise alignment and matching of details. Consequently, the system must progressively assimilate complex rules and refine its operational procedures to ensure the path-following mechanism remains consistent and reliable. The training of large models for relevance tasks can be conceptualized as a classification problem, wherein attributes are extracted and matched between queries and items. Initially, early methodologies utilized prompts to establish tasks and employed responses to demonstrate outcomes; however, they refined models through the application of supervised learning, the underpinning mechanisms of which warrant further investigation. Nevertheless, when the data diverged from the conditions under which the model was originally trained, this straightforward method proved ineffective. The study demonstrated that encouraging the model to engage in problem-solving prior to providing responses significantly enhanced its performance in complex scenarios. However, constraints on computational resources and model utilization necessitate the selection of smaller models, which, when prompted, exhibit diminished reasoning capabilities and reduced efficacy in instruction adherence. In response to these challenges, current methodologies often involve employing larger teacher models, such as GPT-4 or DeepSeek-R1, to generate reasoning datasets. Subsequently, these datasets enable the training of smaller models through supervised learning to acquire reasoning capabilities. In addition, we incorporate an additional reinforcement learning stage, employing techniques such as offline RL (Direct Preference Optimization or KTO) or online RL (Group Relative Policy Optimization) to further enhance the model's adaptability.

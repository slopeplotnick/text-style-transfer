The tool library encompasses the Region Finder Tool for the identification of specific regions, the Analysis Planner Tool employing the Think with Image methodology for planning regional analysis, the Report Creator Tool for report generation based on designated templates, and the Quality Checker Tool which facilitates the evaluation of radiology reports and provides feedback for enhancement. Collectively, these tools are designed to support the comprehensive process of automated radiology reporting. Regarding datasets, the effectiveness of our Radiologist Copilot is assessed through a liver radiology reporting task. Specifically, we employ the AMOS-MM dataset, which comprises over 2,000 CT scans accompanied by detailed radiology reports. This dataset is divided into 1,287 scans with reports allocated for training and 400 scans with reports reserved for validation. For the liver task, reports within the AMOS-MM dataset are filtered to select those discussing liver-specific information, thus categorizing them as liver radiology reports. Consequently, the training set consists of 1,149 CT scans with liver reports, while the validation set contains 367 CT scans with liver reports. Importantly, the validation set is utilized to assess the efficacy of our proposed method. In contrast, the training set serves to facilitate the examination and analysis of the reports. Methodologically, BioBERT is employed to convert reports into data points, and subsequently, K-means clustering is applied to categorize these data points and generate template reports. These template reports are detailed into items pertinent to liver analysis, such as liver surface, liver tissue, bile ducts, and liver issues, which are subsequently utilized by the Analyzer Tool. Implementation details reveal that the Radiologist Copilot leverages the OctoTools framework and operates on Python 3.10.18 with NVIDIA L20 GPUs. Notably, the system functions in a training-free manner due to the utilization of pre-existing models, thus enabling empirical evaluation of their functionality. The reasoning framework is underpinned by the Qwen3-32B model, whereas the Segmentator Tool utilizes the TotalSegmentator model for precise region identification. Furthermore, the Hulu-Med 3D medical model is employed for CT scan analysis. The system configuration allows for a maximum of 10 processing steps per case, allocating up to 500 seconds per step to ensure thorough examination.

The training methodology employed aligns closely with the previous SFT procedure, utilizing reasoning traces generated during training to refine the model. Additionally, a proportion of sentences is randomly replaced with tokens generated by the model, following similar strategies as in earlier methodologies. This approach proves advantageous as it enhances the model's efficiency in reasoning. During inference, the model operates predominantly in a standard discrete mode; however, upon encountering specific tags, it transitions to an alternative reasoning mode, as illustrated in Figure 1. These tags serve to differentiate between the discrete and alternative reasoning modes; nevertheless, they are not treated as specialized tokens within the model's vocabulary. Instead of explicitly training the model with these tags, we prompt it to utilize them when necessary. To determine the appropriate number of tokens to generate in the alternative mode, the model forecasts this value numerically. Initially, these tags are employed infrequently; however, as training progresses, their usage becomes increasingly prevalent, reflecting the model's adaptation over time. To evaluate this methodology, we employ two tasks: Flawed Fictions and Next-Chapter Prediction. While the initial formulation of these tasks is explored, along with the training of a baseline reasoning model that excludes the specialized reasoning mode, the detailed setup of these models is beyond the scope of this discussion. Both tasks necessitate advanced reasoning capabilities grounded in narrative structures that diverge from conventional approaches typically applied in related studies.

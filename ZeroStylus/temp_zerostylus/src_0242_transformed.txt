Li et al. (2025) explores the use of feedback-driven mechanisms during test-time training, where external guidance provides weak supervision to enhance predictive accuracy. Similarly, Zuo et al. (2025) introduces Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL), which evaluates multiple strategies collectively, aggregates predictions through consensus, and utilizes this consensus as a reward signal for adaptive policy updates. While these approaches demonstrate effectiveness, they often suffer from inefficiency due to the need for numerous iterations to achieve stable results. To address this limitation, Wang et al. (2025) highlights the potential of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), showing that incorporating such rewards enables strong learning outcomes with minimal training examples. This finding underscores the promise of efficient reinforcement learning techniques in test-time contexts. Building on this, our methodology introduces an early-stopping mechanism grounded in statistical principles to enhance process efficiency by adaptively determining when to terminate rollouts. Policy optimization remains a cornerstone of reinforcement learning, particularly in the development of language models. Among these techniques, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017) is widely adopted for its ability to ensure incremental policy updates under KL regularization constraints. This algorithm has been extensively applied in reinforcement learning from human feedback scenarios due to its stability. Extending this framework, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) incorporates group-normalized advantages, further stabilizing optimization processes in settings with limited feedback. In our work, we build on these advancements by introducing OptPO, a novel framework that leverages the flexibility of these optimization paradigms. OptPO introduces a plug-and-play rollout allocation mechanism, enhancing computational efficiency without altering the underlying optimization algorithm. In summary, prior research has established test-time training as a robust framework for improving model adaptability during inference, employing methods such as uncertainty reduction, verifier-assisted learning, and reinforcement learning. Nevertheless, existing test-time reinforcement learning methods are hindered by significant inefficiencies due to excessive rollout requirements. Our proposed framework, OptPO, addresses this challenge by substantially reducing computational costs while maintaining strong performance. This contribution complements and extends existing test-time adaptation methodologies, offering a more efficient approach to optimizing rollouts in inference scenarios.

Forecasting stock prices remains a formidable challenge due to the inherent volatility of financial markets and their sensitivity to real-time events. The emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs), with their advanced natural language processing capabilities, has facilitated the exploration of innovative approaches for leveraging textual data in stock price prediction. While these models demonstrate exceptional proficiency in text comprehension, their effective application to complex, real-world tasks necessitates the incorporation of specialized memory architectures. Such memory systems are essential for maintaining consistent access to historical information, particularly when processing temporally extended and evolving data. Despite notable advancements in the design of memory architectures, their performance remains constrained when faced with the vast, noisy, and unstructured nature of online news. Specifically, financial news often contains extraneous information that does not directly clarify the reasons behind stock price fluctuations. Consequently, the critical challenge lies in autonomously identifying and inferring the key information and underlying logic within unstructured textual data that drive stock price movements. To address this challenge, this paper introduces a novel dual-layer memory framework, termed the event-reflection framework. During the knowledge construction phase, the framework adopts a two-dimensional event organization strategy. Horizontally, it consolidates daily event details to synthesize key information, while longitudinally, it traces the evolution of specific events over time to capture temporal dynamics. This dual-layer process allows the extraction of incremental information that reflects discrepancies in market expectations, ultimately enabling the construction of a structured, temporally-aware event knowledge base. Furthermore, the framework systematically examines the dynamic relationship between event sequences and stock price movements, refining this analysis into a reflection knowledge base. This dual-layer mechanism ensures the autonomous and systematic construction of actionable knowledge. In the subsequent knowledge application phase, the framework operates in two primary stages: retrieval and reasoning. Initially, the recent event sequence is utilized as a semantic query to retrieve semantically analogous historical scenarios from the memory network, enabling contextually relevant references for decision-making. Following this, the reasoning phase integrates the recent event sequence, the incremental insights derived from the knowledge base, and retrieved historical experiences to generate accurate and explainable predictions of stock price movements. Experimental evaluations confirm that the proposed framework not only outperforms existing memory architectures in predictive accuracy but also enhances interpretability by explicitly linking events to price fluctuations and elucidating the underlying mechanisms. This integration of advanced memory systems and event-based reasoning represents a significant advancement in the domain of stock price prediction.

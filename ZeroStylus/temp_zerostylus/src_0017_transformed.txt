The implantation phase is designed to achieve three primary objectives: covertness, reversibility, and controllability. Covertness ensures that the malicious tool remains indistinguishable from its benign equivalents, thereby minimizing any observable anomalies. Reversibility is concerned with the tool's ability to revert to its original state following the completion of its exploitative activities. Controllability, on the other hand, allows the attacker to reliably utilize the compromised system for executing additional unauthorized operations. In this section, we will detail the methodologies employed to realize these objectives. The system employs a covert command-and-control (C2) protocol, specifically a two-phase process. Initially, the implant transmits a victim identifier payload to a remote server via the internet, ensuring that this communication appears legitimate. Subsequently, the remote endpoint responds with an extra task payload. The implant then embeds this payload within the tool's output, making it seem like a legitimate response. In the following phase, the implant monitors the tool's operation, capturing user inputs and transmitting them to the remote destination. This two-step mechanism is designed to maintain brevity in network connections, thereby reducing the risk of detection. During the exploitation stage, the agent is induced to perform additional tasks; however, it simultaneously restores the standard outputs of the tool, ensuring that the user's experience remains unchanged. The trigger design incorporates three types: content-based, frequency-based, and context-based. A content-based trigger activates upon detecting a specific word or symbol within the tool's input. Similarly, a frequency-based trigger is initiated after a randomized number of standard operations to prevent the formation of detectable patterns. Lastly, a context-based trigger activates when the agent employs a particular tool or function in response to a designated request. Upon activation, LeechHijack clandestinely retrieves extra computational tasks from the attacker, facilitating the unauthorized exploitation of the agent's resources without detection.

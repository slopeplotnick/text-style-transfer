In exploring the challenges of AI moral reasoning, researchers have developed structured benchmarks such as MoralBench to evaluate AI models against human moral intuitions, as demonstrated in studies [25] and [15]. These investigations reveal that while models exhibit partial moral sensitivity, they frequently lack coherence when confronted with conflicting principles and contextual ambiguity. However, these benchmark-based evaluations remain constrained in terms of scalability and temporal scope, capturing model ethics at a single point in time without revealing how reasoning consistency evolves with interaction and retraining. Furthermore, supporting studies have underscored the importance of explainability and value alignment in fostering responsible AI. For instance, Shitong et al. [9] introduced DeNEVIL, a model that integrates debiasing and normative reasoning into AI training to enhance ethical alignment. Similarly, Jiao et al. [16] highlighted the necessity of cross-cultural moral benchmarking and trust calibration, which underscore the sociotechnical dependencies inherent in ethical AI systems. Despite these frameworks' strengths in interpretability and trust analysis, they rely on predefined moral categories and manual labeling, limiting their adaptability to dynamic ethical contexts. Although the previous literature has contributed significantly to fairness auditing, benchmark design, and value-alignment methodologies, a critical gap remains: the absence of a dynamic, model-agnostic framework capable of longitudinally evaluating moral principles. Existing methodologies assess ethical behavior only at discrete points, lacking mechanisms for the autonomous generation, interpretation, and adaptation of ethical reasoning scenarios. Moreover, none of the current approaches provides computational tools to quantify ethical divergence or to track cross-model moral coherence in real time. Building on the conceptual foundation outlined in Section 1, this paper introduces the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP), which operationalizes continuous ethical evaluation as a closed-loop, dataset-free mechanism designed to measure, interpret, and refine the moral stability of Large Language Models (LLMs). Aligned with the abstract and introduction, MoCoP emphasizes evaluation and interpretation over optimization or retraining, thereby ensuring conceptual and methodological coherence.

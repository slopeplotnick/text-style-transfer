The datasets used in this study were constructed as follows: one included the complete set of foundational components, while the remaining datasets excluded specific components—one without events, one without relationships, one without entities, and one without the story outline. Both qualitative and quantitative metrics were employed to evaluate the generated narratives. Specifically, the same test dataset, consisting of 196 samples, was used to assess the performance of the fine-tuned models. From a qualitative perspective, we examined various aspects of the generated stories, such as their functional quality and artistic appeal. For quantitative analysis, a preference set of stories ("Chosen") was used as the baseline, enabling systematic comparisons of the similarity between stories generated by different fine-tuned models. To understand the impact of the four foundational components on the generated narratives, we conducted targeted investigations using similarity metrics. In the quantitative evaluation, we primarily focused on the degree of resemblance between the stories generated by different fine-tuned models using identical inputs and the original text. Notably, each fine-tuned model consistently produced three stories for each input. The original text was used as the benchmark for comparison, with similarity assessed through three metrics: ROUGE-L, BERTScore, and METEOR. ROUGE-L measured similarity by evaluating the length of the longest common subsequence between two sequences, while BERTScore captured semantic similarity, effectively addressing the limitations of surface-level matching. The METEOR Score further enhanced the evaluation by incorporating multiple matching techniques, thereby improving flexibility and robustness. Among these configurations, utilizing the complete set of foundational components proved most effective, as it ensured controlled content generation, maintained semantic consistency, and produced narratives that adhered to natural linguistic patterns. To further evaluate the usability of TaleFrame, we re-engaged the two experts and 17 volunteers mentioned in Section 3 to participate in a usability study. To mitigate creative challenges, such as a lack of inspiration, volunteers were initially provided with various images to stimulate their creativity. Participants were then allowed to select their preferred topics and use TaleFrame to generate stories. During this process, if volunteers were dissatisfied with the generated narratives, they were given the option to systematically modify the attributes of the four foundational components to create stories that aligned more closely with their expectations. Additionally, volunteers were required to engage with all interactive features of TaleFrame, ensuring a comprehensive assessment of the tool's functionality.

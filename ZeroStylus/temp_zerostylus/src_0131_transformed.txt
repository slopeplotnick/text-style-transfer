The investigation was designed to address three primary research questions, focusing on the efficacy of programming agents in relation to process-centric metrics, their problem-solving strategies, and patterns of inefficiency. Specifically, the study explores the defining characteristics of these agents, the factors influencing their metric values, and their autonomous methods of problem resolution. RQ1 examines behavioral patterns by investigating the strategies employed by agents, the factors that impact their actions, and the extent to which they adhere to predefined methodologies. Furthermore, it seeks to determine whether agents adapt their approaches during collaborative endeavors. RQ2 expands the scope by analyzing inefficiency patterns, identifying prevalent issues or detrimental habits, and assessing their frequency of occurrence. To address these questions, the study evaluates two widely used programming agents, SWE-agent (SA) and OpenHands (OH), under standard experimental configurations. The SWE-agent was employed with a $2 cost limit per task, while OpenHands was executed for up to 100 trajectory iterations, with both agents relying on their default utilities for file inspection and code modification tasks. To facilitate a comprehensive analysis, the agents were paired with four distinct language models: DeepSeek-V3 671B MoE (DSK-V3), DeepSeek-R1 (DSK-R1), Devstral-small-2505 24B (Dev), and Claude Sonnet 4 (CLD-4). These models represent a diverse landscape, encompassing general-purpose frameworks, reasoning models, frontier solutions, and open-source alternatives. Consequently, eight unique agent-model combinations were established for subsequent analysis. Process-centric metrics were computed for each agent-model pairing, with the results presented in Table 1 and Figure 3. Table 1 illustrates the frequency of issue resolutions, reporting the resolution rate for each combination. Figure 3 further visualizes the collected values, using green bars to denote resolved issues and pink bars to indicate unresolved ones. Moreover, heatmap cells in Figure 3 display the median values and interquartile ranges (IQRs) of the metrics, with darker colors indicating higher values. Through this analysis, it was observed that OpenHands exhibited more complex behavioral dynamics compared to SWE-agent, as evidenced by its elevated metric values. This finding underscores the nuanced operational characteristics of OpenHands, particularly in scenarios requiring intricate problem-solving strategies.

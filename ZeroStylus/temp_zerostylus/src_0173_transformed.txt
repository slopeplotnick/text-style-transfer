The process of simplification involves eliminating extraneous elements and retaining only the essential components of the formula. This ensures that the formula remains compact while maintaining its functionality. Consequently, this compactness facilitates the efficient processing of SAT solvers by enhancing their ability to manage tasks. Errors in the reuse, labeling, or translation of variables within formulas, however, undermine the functionality of SAT solvers. These challenges often arise from models inadequately converting specific sentence structures during training or from sentences that deviate from standard word order, thereby complicating their transformation into conjunctive normal form (CNF). To address these issues, the integration of NLTK and Sympy is employed, demonstrating our approach to identifying and rectifying tokenization or phrasing discrepancies. The NLTK framework uses a rule-based grammatical decomposition approach, grounded in rigorously tested and validated principles, to facilitate the precise analysis of sentence structure. Each sentence is parsed accurately, ensuring proper segmentation. This precise parsing guarantees that the transformation into CNF maintains the logical integrity of the original sentence, which is further facilitated through the application of Sympy. Upon receiving a paragraph, the text is segmented into individual sentences at each period, with each sentence subsequently regarded as an independent unit. These components are then combined through the application of logical AND operations, resulting in a CNF. The methodology employs an extensive set of syntactic rules utilizing NLTK to decompose each sentence into a hierarchical tree structure, elucidating its syntactic construction. This hierarchical structure begins with the primary component and subsequently divides into subsidiary elements, encompassing conditions, statements, and other sentence constituents, such as nouns, verbs, and punctuation. The transformation process employs predefined rules to convert the tree into a logical formula utilizing Sympy, thereby ensuring a structured approach to symbolic computation. Finally, the Sympy library uses its integrated computational tools to accurately convert the formula into CNF, ensuring the transformation retains its logical integrity.

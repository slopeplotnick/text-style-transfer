We propose SchED (Schedule-based Early Decoding) for diffusion language models, which facilitates early termination without additional training by employing a smooth, progress-dependent schedule. This approach employs a schedule, such as linear or cosine, to determine termination criteria based on the model's confidence levels, ensuring compatibility across diverse model configurations. By monitoring the model's confidence progression over time, it becomes evident that refining the instruction set expedites stabilization, aligning with the predefined schedule and enabling early termination without compromising output quality. SchED operates by dynamically adjusting a confidence threshold, calibrated to evolve consistently throughout text generation. It leverages the inherent efficiency of diffusion model mechanisms to streamline the process without necessitating additional training. The implementation of the codebase is accessible through an online repository. Diffusion language models represent a novel alternative to autoregressive counterparts, offering flexibility in training either through initialization from scratch or by fine-tuning pre-existing autoregressive architectures. Moreover, in the LLaDA approach, a model is trained to perform text masking in one direction while correcting it in the opposite direction, yielding notably strong results in both training and fine-tuning phases. Recently, certain methodologies have emerged to convert autoregressive models into diffusion models, facilitating equitable comparisons across diverse model sizes and tasks. In contrast, models such as Dream-7B prioritize refining methodologies and creating various iterations of the model. To achieve efficient performance with reduced active components, we propose the LLaDA-MoE design, which utilizes a sparse configuration to deliver competitive results. To further enhance the capability of diffusion models in processing extended text sequences, advancements such as UltraLLaDA have been introduced, employing the RoPE scaling technique to effectively manage up to 128K tokens. Furthermore, Seed Diffusion endeavors to optimize the execution velocity of code models by focusing on their operational efficiency. Empirical analyses indicate that these models scale comparably to autoregressive counterparts. The significance lies in the ability to operate concurrently, address missing elements, and manage overarching contextual frameworks, alongside the need to resolve outstanding inquiries in the domain.

To address structural limitations in evaluating language models, a fundamental paradigm shift is required—from analyzing static artifacts to assessing dynamic processes in real-time. This transition necessitates a unified framework that eschews reliance on fixed outputs and instead employs a foundational set of instructions, or factual queries, capable of eliciting instantaneous responses from any target LLM. Consequently, this dynamic methodology ensures three critical objectives: (1) fact verification (FV) methods are provided with the textual content they require, (2) hallucination detection (HD) methods gain access to the real-time internal states of the model during response generation, and (3) evaluations remain perpetually relevant as language models continue to evolve. These principles directly inform the design of UniFact, a framework established to address the inherent shortcomings of static benchmarks. To achieve robust, equitable, and future-proof evaluations, UniFact is grounded in three foundational design principles. First, *Model-Agnosticism via On-the-Fly Generation* ensures adaptability by abandoning reliance on static datasets of outputs. Instead, it utilizes a core set of factual queries to dynamically trigger generation from any target LLM, thereby maintaining perpetual relevance for evaluating current and future models. Second, *Equitable and Principled Comparison* facilitates fair evaluations across diverse methodologies. Specifically, HD methods are granted access to the model’s reasoning process during generation, while FV methods are supplied with the generated text and external corpora to verify factual correctness. Despite these distinct mechanisms, both methods converge on a shared evaluative task: predicting the factual accuracy of the model’s output. Third, *Scalability through Automated Labeling* addresses the inefficiencies of manual annotation by employing an automated system to assess factuality. By benchmarking model-generated responses against ground-truth answers and reliable sources, this system enables large-scale, objective, and reproducible evaluations across numerous LLMs. Thus, UniFact represents a comprehensive framework that ensures evaluations remain robust, fair, and scalable in the rapidly evolving landscape of language model development.

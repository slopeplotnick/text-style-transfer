When a target appears on the left or right side of the screen, the subject is prompted to open and close the corresponding fist until the target disappears, followed by a period of relaxation. Similarly, the appearance of a target at the top or bottom of the screen prompts the subject to open and close both fists if positioned at the top or both feet if situated at the bottom, again followed by relaxation. To ensure the accurate classification of motor imagery tasks, we focused on specific channels and optimized the feature extraction processes to capture relevant motor and premotor brain activities. However, to mitigate potential biases introduced by evoked responses, we implemented a temporal analysis strategy. Specifically, this approach involved commencing data analysis one second post-stimulus, thereby excluding the immediate post-stimulus interval, which is typically dominated by evoked responses. Consequently, this method allowed us to examine the brain's intrinsic activity, emphasizing stable features indicative of the subject’s sensorimotor state. Moreover, various preprocessing techniques were applied prior to training classical machine learning algorithms to further refine the classification process. Initially, signal processing methods, such as Common Spatial Patterns (CSP), were employed, as CSP is widely recognized in the field of EEG signal classification. CSP, first described by Koles et al., is a technique designed to extract and discriminate features by optimizing the variance between distinct motor imagery tasks or mental states. Specifically, it transforms EEG signals into a new vector space, wherein the variance for one class is maximized while that for the other is minimized. Thus, the goal of CSP is to identify and apply spatial filters that highlight significant differences between classes, facilitating a clearer understanding of these distinctions.

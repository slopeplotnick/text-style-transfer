The baseline experiments demonstrate that the Swivuriso dataset supports measurable improvements in Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) performance across multiple architectures and languages. Moreover, fine-tuning consistently reduces error rates, with results across models indicating that the dataset provides stable learning signals even at early training stages. The analysis of training hours further confirms that additional data contributes to performance gains for most languages. Consequently, these findings position Swivuriso as a reliable resource for future work on South African ASR and for continued methodological development in multilingual speech modeling. Considerable research efforts have previously been directed towards the development of high-quality datasets for speech recognition, particularly given the requirements for precision and the elimination of anomalies. However, the advancement in low-resource languages, especially African languages, has been comparatively slow. Notably, Abdulmumin et al. conducted a study on the FLORES dataset, which facilitated the correction of the dataset to better evaluate four African low-resource languages: Hausa, Northern Sotho (Sepedi), Xitsonga, and isiZulu. This work involved a meticulous review and correction of the dataset's dev and devtest splits, ensuring that native speakers carefully identified and rectified inconsistencies and inaccuracies that could impact the reliability of downstream Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks, especially machine translation. Their corrections notably improved the linguistic accuracy and overall quality of the dataset, as supported by both qualitative assessments and statistical analyses. This correction effort underscores the importance of native speaker involvement to ensure linguistic and cultural relevance in low-resource language datasets. In alignment with these findings, we heavily involved native speakers in our dataset to enhance its character and improve its linguistic accuracy and overall quality. Furthermore, another study guiding our process, particularly in sentiment analysis across various domains, identified that while many sentiment analysis benchmark datasets for high-resource languages exist, such resources are scarce for low-resource languages, notably Indian languages like Hindi. The study addressed challenges in sentiment analysis within Hindi by introducing a balanced corpus annotated with sentiment classes—positive, negative, and neutral—thereby addressing socially relevant domains.

The abundance of sentiment analysis datasets for major languages is evident; however, there remains a critical gap in resources for less-resourced languages, such as Hindi. Addressing this disparity, the present research aims to advance sentiment analysis for Hindi by tackling socially significant issues and constructing a well-balanced dataset annotated with positive, negative, and neutral sentiment categories. Moreover, the focus on Hindi, a morphologically rich and widely spoken language, highlights the importance of developing linguistic resources for underrepresented languages. This work contributes to the broader effort to bridge the resource gap in computational linguistics, providing both methodological advancements and practical tools for sentiment analysis in low-resource settings.

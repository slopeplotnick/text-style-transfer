Sentiment analysis constitutes an essential component of modern Natural Language Processing (NLP), enabling applications in digital governance, public health surveillance, financial inclusion, and online content moderation. However, despite significant advancements in deep learning and multilingual language modeling, sentiment analysis research continues to inadequately address South African low-resource languages. This gap is largely attributable to the scarcity of annotated datasets, insufficient lexical resources, and limited methodologies for effective cross-lingual knowledge transfer. Consequently, models that perform well in high-resource languages such as English and French often experience substantial performance degradation when applied to African linguistic contexts, thereby exacerbating the digital divide and restricting equitable access to NLP technologies. Existing approaches to multilingual sentiment analysis have primarily emphasized machine translation pipelines, cross-lingual embeddings, and the development of monolingual lexicons. While these strategies have yielded promising results, they are not without limitations. For instance, machine-translated lexicons frequently propagate translation errors and fail to capture culturally specific meanings, a challenge particularly pronounced in morphologically complex African languages. Moreover, corpus-based lexicon extraction methods typically require access to large, domain-specific datasets, which are seldom available for low-resource languages. Additionally, although African language models such as AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa have demonstrated notable advancements, their effectiveness in sentiment classification remains constrained by the lack of high-quality sentiment lexicons across many African languages. To address these significant challenges, this paper introduces *TriLex*, a novel three-stage retrieval-augmented framework designed to facilitate the scalable expansion of sentiment lexicons for African low-resource languages. Specifically, *TriLex* incorporates three key components: first, corpus-based lexical extraction to identify candidate sentiment-bearing terms; second, cross-lingual semantic mapping to align these terms across English, French, and target African languages; and third, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to refine, validate, and expand the lexicon using large language models (LLMs) conditioned on multilingual retrieval signals. This framework not only provides a robust methodology for addressing the limitations of existing approaches but also contributes to the development of a large multilingual sentiment lexicon encompassing multiple South African languages, thereby enabling reproducible research. Furthermore, we undertake the first comprehensive cross-model evaluation of AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa by leveraging this lexicon-expanded resource for sentiment analysis in isiXhosa and isiZulu, offering valuable insights into their performance in low-resource language contexts.

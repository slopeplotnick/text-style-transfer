Efforts are underway to broaden the French-Ciluba sentiment lexicon by incorporating translations in English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, Sesotho, and Sepedi, thereby enhancing the scope, precision, and usability of sentiment lexicons for African languages with limited representation. This endeavor involves the application of tools designed to facilitate cross-lingual translation and align lexical items based on their contextual usage within textual data. To refine the existing list, the Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) method is employed to identify and associate emotionally or opinion-laden words in African language texts with those already included in the lexicon. Moreover, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework, leveraging large-scale language models, will be utilized to resolve ambiguities in lexical semantics and ensure the accuracy of translations. The updated word list will subsequently be utilized to train AfriBERTa and AfroXLMR models, enabling the classification of emotions and opinions in textual data, a task integral to advancing cross-cultural sentiment analysis in NLP applications. The efficacy of these models will be evaluated through metrics such as precision, recall, F1-score, and the ROC curve. Furthermore, to elucidate the mechanisms of the models, tools will be employed to highlight the specific components of the text that the models emphasize during decision-making processes. In conclusion, the effectiveness of individual models will be evaluated compared to their integration into an ensemble to determine whether this approach enhances accuracy. This study proceeds as follows: it first examines prior advancements in sentiment analysis, with a focus on the development of multilingual word lists and methodologies tailored for African languages. Subsequently, our framework and methodologies are delineated, followed by an exposition of the experimental procedures and model evaluations, culminating in an analysis of the outcomes and their implications for multilingual applications in natural language processing. Historically, sentiment analysis has predominantly employed machine learning algorithms or lexicon-based approaches to ascertain whether a given text conveys a positive or negative sentiment. Initial investigations demonstrated the efficacy of statistical techniques for this purpose, while subsequent research identified the utility of word lists in supporting straightforward, rule-based methodologies. However, prominent lexicons such as SentiWordNet, NRC Emotion Lexicon, and VADER, while well-suited for resource-rich languages, fail to address the intricacies and limited data availability of more complex languages.

Once the words from text collections were extracted for various languages, individuals manually compiled the word lists. This task was executed with precision to ensure that the compiled lists accurately represented the linguistic diversity of each language. Furthermore, the process required careful verification to guarantee that the selections were comprehensive and culturally relevant. Consequently, the manual compilation of word lists was a critical step in preserving the authenticity and richness of the language data collected.

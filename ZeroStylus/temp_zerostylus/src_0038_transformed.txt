At the conclusion of the study, participants were invited to share any additional thoughts on AI-generated music (see Supplements C for detailed phrasing). To assess personality traits, the researchers employed a short version of the Big Five Inventory (BFI-10; Rammstedt and John, 2007), which is validated in both English and German. The five personality traits, including Openness, were examined. In addition, basic socio-demographic characteristics such as age, gender, nationality, and educational attainment were collected to better understand our sample and examine the extent to which these variables influence the study outcomes. Although the survey permitted non-binary responses, all participants identified as male or female; therefore, gender was coded as a binary variable. Furthermore, participants' language proficiency in the survey language was assessed to ensure they could understand subtle differences in adjectives describing their emotional experience. All statistical analyses were performed in Python (v3.9.20) using the pandas (v2.2.2) library for data handling, scipy (v1.12.0) for correlation and ANOVA testing, and statsmodels (v.0.14.4) for mixed-effects models. Moreover, figures were created in R (v4.3.3) using the ggplot2 package (Wickham, 2016). Thematic analysis of open answers regarding AI-generated music, following Braun and Clarke's (2006) methodology, was conducted using MAXQDA (v.24.10). Additional participant-level robustness analyses, which averaged ratings across all songs per participant, are reported in Appendix C. These analyses replicated the composer bias effect for liking but did not reveal significant group differences for emotional intensity. To assess whether the experimental condition influenced liking and emotional intensity ratings beyond individual differences and music stimuli, linear mixed-effects models were fitted with crossed random intercepts for participants and songs, controlling for personality, listening characteristics, and the music model used.

A distinct area of research focuses on refining the generative process itself, with the aim of improving the quality and coherence of outputs. Techniques such as Mirror-Consistency (Li et al., 2024), Self-Contrast (Zhang et al., 2024), and Step-Back Prompting (Zheng et al., 2023a) employ iterative verification or multi-perspective analysis to enhance the reliability of generated content. Similarly, the Self-Check method (Miao et al., 2023) takes a systematic, step-by-step approach to identify and filter errors. However, these methods primarily concentrate on improving the generator's performance, often at the cost of increased computational complexity and longer processing times. In contrast, our work shifts the focus to the aggregation process by accepting the generator's noisy outputs and applying a distribution-calibrated layer to derive the most accurate response. A critical consideration when employing a language model as a judge for pairwise comparisons (Zheng et al., 2023b) is whether to allow the model to declare a tie or to compel it to make a definitive choice. Importantly, we demonstrate that forcing the model to break ties can introduce systematic biases in its evaluations. Furthermore, we show that tie decisions are highly sensitive to the configuration of the evaluative framework, underscoring the need for a robust aggregation methodology to mitigate this dependency. Allowing ties, therefore, serves as an essential mechanism for reducing bias. For instance, language models functioning as evaluators exhibit positional bias (Shi et al., 2025), where their preferences are influenced by the order in which options are presented. Experimental results, as shown in Table 1, reveal that modifying the prompt to permit the output of a “tie” significantly reduces positional bias across both evaluated models. This finding highlights that the inclusion of a tie option not only facilitates the recognition of equivalence but also plays a pivotal role in enhancing fairness within the evaluation process. However, tie decisions are not always consistent. In scenarios requiring the model to evaluate three options, the variability in its decision-making is substantially influenced by the evaluation setup. This variability emphasizes the necessity of developing a more robust and reliable method for aggregating results in multi-option evaluation tasks.

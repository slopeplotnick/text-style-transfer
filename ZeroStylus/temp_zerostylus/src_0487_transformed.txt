The globalization of education and the rapid expansion of online learning necessitate the effective adaptation of educational content to diverse regions. However, the inherently multimodal nature of lecture materials—combining spoken discourse with visual elements from slides—requires systems capable of processing multiple input modalities. Conversely, effective learning necessitates the comprehensive retention of all components—text for reading, slides for visual engagement, and spoken audio for auditory comprehension—to facilitate accessibility for all individuals. To address the challenges of lecture translation, we developed BOOM, a system that integrates audio and slide content to produce multimodal outputs, including translated text, visually consistent slides, and natural-sounding synthesized speech. Therefore, this approach enables students to access lecture content in their native language while retaining the original material's integrity. Our investigation reveals that the incorporation of slides facilitates tasks such as summarization and question response. To facilitate advancements in educational content localization, we have made our Slide Translation code publicly available at https://github.com/saikoneru/image-Translator and integrated it into the Lecture Translator system, accessible at https://gitlab.kit.edu/kit/isl-ai41t/lt-middleware/ltpipeline. Allowing students to engage with educational content in their native language significantly enhances their comprehension, as it aligns more closely with their linguistic and cognitive frameworks. In contrast, adapting lecture materials for multilingual accessibility mitigates communication barriers, enhances accessibility, and facilitates students' comprehension of complex concepts. In light of the increasing internationalization of higher education institutions, the provision of lecture content in multiple languages, both in-person and online, is crucial for enhancing the accessibility of educational resources. Moreover, the shift towards digital education integrates lecture content into a multimodal framework, blending various media types. Conversely, the core component is the lecture audio, which can be transcribed into text via Automatic Speech Recognition. This work leverages advancements in Large Language Models to enhance educational content delivery by generating multimodal outputs, including lecture slides, textual summaries, chapter breakdowns, and interactive question-answer sessions, thereby improving the overall learning experience. To ensure accessibility for all students, including those with limited proficiency in the language, it is imperative that these outputs are rendered in multiple languages as well. Consequently, effective localization necessitates addressing diverse content types, encompassing audio, text, and visuals, thereby rendering lecture translation a significant challenge.

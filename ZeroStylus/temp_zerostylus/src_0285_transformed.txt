This endeavor seeks to identify effective methodologies to ensure seamless interoperability among disparate systems. However, as artificial intelligences increasingly collaborate within expanding multi-agent environments, reliance solely on localized regulations proves insufficient to prevent significant systemic failures. Emphasizing the safety of individual AI entities, such as through prompt engineering and output filtering, becomes ineffective when these entities engage in complex interactions with one another. This discrepancy in safeguarding individual AI agents versus the entire system can profoundly impact the reliability, accountability, and societal trustworthiness of contemporary AI frameworks. Therefore, addressing these challenges necessitates governance mechanisms that operate at an equivalent systemic level, enabling agents to regulate, interpret, and collaboratively modify shared rules while maintaining stable group dynamics without over-reliance on centralized authority. To elucidate the manifestation of such risks, we introduce the *Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon* (ESRH) as a conceptual framework. The ESRH highlights how minor issues evolve into significant systemic challenges as interaction, complexity, and divergent objectives converge. These risks demonstrate that network interactions create systemic vulnerabilities extending beyond the malfunction of individual AI agents. Thus, it becomes imperative to establish frameworks that integrate oversight and normative adaptation directly within multi-agent systems. In this context, we propose the concept of *Institutional AI*, an approach that embeds mechanisms for governance, norm-maintenance, and procedural adaptability into the system architecture itself, rather than relying on external supervisory layers. Developing and validating such frameworks requires an integrated research trajectory. This trajectory must interlink theoretical foundations, empirical validations, and future efforts toward standardization. Specifically, controlled experiments are necessary to evaluate specified indicators, examine coordination models, and develop pragmatic tools that facilitate the operationalization of these concepts within AI systems. Moreover, transitioning from single-agent systems to multi-agent artificial intelligence represents a paradigm shift toward distributed reasoning and collaborative functionality, demanding innovative governance approaches capable of evolving alongside these systems. The absence of such innovative governance mechanisms allows safety concerns to persist latently, only to escalate into unmanageable challenges over time. For this reason, the ESRH framework and the concept of Institutional AI demand comprehensive empirical validation, theoretical elaboration, and iterative refinement to ensure their viability. In this paper, we aim to elucidate these pressing issues and propose a systematic approach to address them, marking an initial step in what promises to be a profound and necessary journey.

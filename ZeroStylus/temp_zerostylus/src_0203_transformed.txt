With the increasing deployment of video language models in real-world applications, such as AI glasses and household robots, the implications of this integration on model functionality and user interaction have yet to be fully examined. These models are required to manage extended video content, with durations that may extend to several hours or days. Recent approaches leverage memory-augmented mechanisms to extract salient segments from video data, enabling the model to prioritize essential information without the necessity of processing every individual frame. This approach proves to be more cost-effective and efficient than processing all video frames, a task which can incur significant computational expenses. However, prevailing methodologies predominantly depend on textual elements, converting segments of video into captions or summaries to facilitate the retrieval and comprehension of information. In this approach, visual information is utilized primarily for object recognition, with minimal additional application. Typically, these models are configured to analyze a predetermined number of video clips, such as three segments of 30 seconds each, which presents two significant issues. Firstly, when video visuals are not effectively integrated with text memory, the retrieval process for identifying and generating information becomes suboptimal. We assess the importance of visual detail recognition in videos for tasks that necessitate feature identification, spatial comprehension, or accurate scene interpretation, acknowledging the limitations of text in conveying these aspects. Moreover, when generating responses, employing both captions and frames can overwhelm the model, as an excess of visual information may lead to confusion. Therefore, a sophisticated method for selecting an optimal combination of textual and visual elements to effectively address inquiries remains largely unexplored.

We evaluate WorldMM using five benchmarks specifically designed for long video question-answering tasks, with durations ranging from hours to weeks. Our proposed approach consistently outperforms strong baselines, including models tailored for long video comprehension and those augmented with memory architectures. Comprehensive ablation studies further validate the efficacy of our multi-memory, multiscale framework. Specifically, episodic memory facilitates reasoning across multiple temporal scales, visual memory enhances performance on queries centered on objects and actions, and semantic memory enables robust understanding of long-term contextual information. Moreover, when these memory types are adaptively integrated, the model achieves optimal overall performance. These findings underscore the potential of our multimodal memory system as a significant advancement in long video reasoning. While existing video language models (LLMs) exhibit strong capabilities for short video comprehension, recent research has increasingly prioritized addressing the challenges inherent to longer video analysis. Proprietary models such as GPT-5 [16] and Gemini 2.5 [3] have advanced video understanding for durations spanning minutes to hours [2, 5, 12, 25, 34] by leveraging extended context lengths. However, these models remain computationally expensive, and uniformly sampling every frame often proves suboptimal for queries focusing on localized events [17, 24]. Consequently, several strategies have been proposed to overcome these limitations. For instance, visual token compression [8, 9, 11, 19, 20, 26, 35] improves efficiency by reducing token counts; however, this approach often sacrifices fine-grained details, limiting the ability to capture subtle or sparse events. Similarly, key frame selection [22, 30] reduces redundancy by retaining only the most informative frames, yet it frequently fails to detect relevant frames in ultra-long video streams and may overlook rare but significant events. More recently, reasoning-centric training and inference [28, 29] have demonstrated improvements in long-range temporal grounding through reinforcement learning and adaptive test-time scaling. Nevertheless, these methods still face scalability challenges when applied to videos exceeding ten hours in duration. Beyond hour-long videos, emerging benchmarks have introduced continuous recordings spanning days or even weeks [23, 33], pushing video understanding and reasoning to new extremes. However, the aforementioned strategies struggle in such scenarios due to the sheer scale of frames and the intricate temporal dependencies over extended durations. Therefore, these challenges highlight the critical need for more efficient, context-aware, and scalable approaches to enable robust reasoning over extremely long video content.

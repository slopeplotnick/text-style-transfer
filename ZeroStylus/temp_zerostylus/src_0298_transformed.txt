To address the challenges of processing long video sequences, a method known as retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has been developed. This framework enhances the extraction of relevant video segments by utilizing salient frames or clips guided by textual and visual cues, rather than depending on randomly selected frames. As demonstrated in the model's processing workflow, attention mechanisms are applied to emphasize these key video segments, thereby allowing for a concentrated analysis of essential components. Additionally, advanced methodologies employing graph structures have been introduced to elucidate the interconnections among various video elements. However, existing models primarily depend on textual data or basic visual-textual comparisons, which limits their ability to fully comprehend and logically process extended video content. To overcome these limitations, memory-based methodologies have been devised to systematically organize and retain information. For instance, the EgoRAG system facilitates the understanding of daily activities by organizing text-based memories, enabling structured retrieval from video content. Moreover, the Ego-R1 approach utilizes vision-centric tools and sequential reasoning to manage extended tasks effectively. Similarly, the HippoMM framework integrates memory components by combining summarized representations with multimodal data, thereby consolidating information from diverse sources. Furthermore, when the memory system of M3-Agent is activated, it employs an entity-focused approach to discern and retrieve pertinent information methodically. Despite these advancements, challenges remain in seamlessly integrating textual and visual components and adapting to varying temporal durations within videos, which hinders the navigation of complex situations. In response to these challenges, we present WorldMM, a framework that integrates textual and visual data from videos to construct a memory system that enhances understanding and reasoning over extended video sequences. This system operates through a three-step process: it constructs multiple forms of memory, including two tailored for textual information and one for visual data. Subsequently, when the retrieval mechanism is employed, it methodically extracts pertinent information from stored memories across various temporal dimensions until sufficient data is gathered to address the query. Finally, the system analyzes the retrieved data to formulate an appropriate response, thus facilitating comprehensive and consistent outputs.

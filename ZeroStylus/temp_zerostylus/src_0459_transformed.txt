The increasing concern regarding hallucinations in large language models (LLMs) underscores the necessity for extensive exploration in future research endeavors. To address this issue, we propose the implementation of real-time systems capable of detecting and rectifying errors during model operation, rather than relying solely on post-processing measures. This approach, importantly, could enhance the reliability of LLMs, particularly in high-stakes domains, such as legal or medical documentation, where errors may yield serious consequences. Moreover, while researchers are investigating the integration of multimodal data types, such as images and audio alongside text, understanding the challenges posed by such integration remains critical. This is especially pertinent given the propensity for LLMs to falter when processing multimodal inputs. Consequently, the development of models that support users in efficiently identifying and addressing these errors is of considerable interest. Furthermore, AI systems designed to facilitate user interaction by enabling inquiries and verification of model outputs are currently under investigation. These advancements highlight the necessity of employing formal methodologies to mitigate hallucinations, thereby improving model reliability in scenarios where precision is paramount. For instance, recent studies have examined prevalent hallucinations in LLMs, focusing on instances where models inaccurately present factual information or fail to produce coherent outputs due to insufficient data or ambiguous input. Such errors not only diminish user trust but also complicate system usability, necessitating the development of strategies to minimize their occurrence.

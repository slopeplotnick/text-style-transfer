We assess the efficacy of our knowledge graph-based framework through two distinct methodologies, emphasizing the influence of data variations on the behavior and performance of generative models. To empirically evaluate the method's capacity to identify significant training examples, selected data are subsequently removed from the self-trained model. Furthermore, we investigate the impact of stylistic elements in a black-box context by utilizing Ghibli-style image generation. To facilitate this, we construct a "Ghibli World" knowledge graph derived from original movies and evaluate it against Ghibli-styled image representations. These evaluations enable an examination of both direct data influence and broader stylistic traceability within generative models. To ensure that the comparison remains equitable and comprehensible, the experimental setup replicates a recent approach that evaluates influence without requiring access to specific training parameters. The approach operates in two distinct stages: initially identifying training examples most aligned with the user's intent, followed by ranking these examples based on their similarity to the generated output, employing a technique known as cosine similarity. The most similar training images are then removed, and the model is retrained, allowing for an empirical assessment of their significance. The application of our graph-based method utilized identical datasets, prompts, and model configurations. In contrast to text and hidden-space similarity, similarity-based retrieval grounded in knowledge graph alignment identifies key examples with comparable efficacy. In this study, the generative model employed a compact architecture derived from the open-source dalle-pytorch package, trained on a dataset comprising 44,000 fashion images with captions. The experimental configuration and parameters remained unchanged from the original setup, ensuring consistent conditions. The training data was fully controlled to facilitate the empirical assessment of data removal and subsequent retraining.

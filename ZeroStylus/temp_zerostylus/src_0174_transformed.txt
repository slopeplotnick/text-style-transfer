Recent advancements in machine learning have increasingly focused on enhancing the capability of machines to perceive high-resolution images, especially in multi-modal large language models (MLLMs). A widely adopted strategy involves concentrating on specific image segments to improve the perception of intricate details. However, methodologies that rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) can be resource-intensive, time-consuming, and exhibit limited efficacy across heterogeneous systems, thus restricting their scalability and practical application. In contrast, training-free methods leverage attention mechanisms or tree-based search strategies to automatically identify salient regions within the image. Despite adopting a top-down search strategy from high to low resolution, these methods often encounter limitations, particularly in the initial stages where finer details are overlooked, leading to potential errors. Addressing these challenges, we introduce a novel framework termed Retrieval-Augmented Perception (RAP), inspired by the success of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). RAP aims to enhance the perception of high-resolution images without necessitating additional training. The approach utilizes a pre-trained mechanism to identify image regions that closely align with targeted features, thereby facilitating the elimination of extraneous data. Moreover, the Spatial-Awareness Layout module ensures the original configuration of components is preserved, maintaining spatial coherence. Finally, the Adaptive Retrieval-Exploration Search (RE-Search) module systematically determines the optimal number of components by constructing a tree that balances the retention of sufficient information with efficient use of computational resources. Consequently, RAP represents a significant advancement in improving MLLMs' perception capabilities, achieving notable performance improvements on high-resolution benchmarks.

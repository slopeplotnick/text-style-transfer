The primary inquiry centers on whether contemporary language models (LLMs), which are predominantly trained for next-word prediction without explicit perceptual understanding, inherently develop analogous mechanisms for processing information. Understanding how these models handle uncertain information is crucial, as it may shed light on the mechanisms that enable the development of systems adept at accommodating varying input quality. Therefore, employing methodologies derived from the domain of psychophysics, which investigates human perceptual processes, we have examined the operational efficacy of these models. To elucidate this, we analyze the models as black boxes, focusing on their behavior to infer their information processing methods rather than examining their internal structure. By modulating input uncertainty and examining indicators of Bayesian processing—a probabilistic framework for decision-making—it becomes feasible to assess whether these models exhibit human-like perceptual behavior despite the absence of task-specific training. Extensive research in psychophysics indicates that certain traditional tasks are effectively aligned with these models, thereby revealing notable patterns. Such tasks are essential for evaluating the efficacy of models in handling diverse input types, particularly in controlled environments where precise measurement is achievable. In essence, this configuration enables the examination of specific strategies and the identification of vulnerabilities that typically remain obscured within conventional metrics. Unlike real-world scenarios characterized by excessive noise that impedes precise measurement, our work provides three primary contributions: first, we propose a psychophysics framework tailored for LLMs, encompassing four tasks designed to evaluate attributes such as length, location, distance, and duration. The experimental configuration allows for precise manipulation of variables such as noise and context, thereby facilitating an examination of the variations in model behavior. Moreover, we introduce BayesBench, a novel benchmark that evaluates model performance, efficiency in information integration, and adherence to Bayesian principles through an innovative scoring system.

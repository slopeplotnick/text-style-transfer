The theme of rapid change is prevalent, as evidenced by recent discourse. Over the past decade, the energy transition has elevated the global mining industry to a central role in the shift toward sustainable energy solutions. Consequently, the anticipated rise in demand for critical minerals is expected to coincide with increasing geopolitical, social, and environmental challenges. This scenario has led to the emergence of new enterprises, often backed by venture capital, aiming to disrupt the mining sector in a manner similar to previous transformations in healthcare, renewable energy, and climate technology. Furthermore, emerging digital technologies, particularly large language models, have the potential to revolutionize practices across economic, educational, social, and industrial domains. Although the actual impact remains uncertain, there is widespread apprehension about the possibility of missing out. In this context, the mining industry should focus on opportunities rather than fears, particularly through the adoption of advanced technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI). This paper will explore these opportunities and how AI can contribute. Notably, my research group, Stanford Mineral-X, played a crucial role in the discovery of a significant copper deposit in Zambia, where AI was instrumental in optimizing drilling strategies and constructing subsurface models. Several start-ups specializing in AI applications in mineral exploration have emerged from this program. This text does not aim to exhaustively list AI applications in exploration or provide a comprehensive literature review, as such resources are already available. Instead, I propose viewing AI as a novel scientific method to enhance decision-making processes. In the initial section of this paper, I will outline the key decisions in exploration and evaluate how a scientific methodology can improve efficiency. Currently, mineral exploration lacks a universally accepted scientific methodology. Although tasks are executed by experts with distinct approaches reflective of their specialized fields, a broader scientific method is largely absent. I will argue that this absence is a significant barrier to improving exploration efficiency, particularly in reducing discovery costs. The subsequent section will then elucidate the procedure.

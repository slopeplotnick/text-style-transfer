To obtain varied perspectives and overcome the constraints of fixed viewpoints, we diversify camera rotation trajectories. Each trajectory originates from a random orientation in terms of pitch, yaw, and roll, followed by random drifts to mimic natural movements. Additionally, we ensure that the primary auditory elements align with the visual content and remain within the field of view for an extended duration. Specifically, this method identifies the maximal auditory intensity within each clip by decomposing the sound into constituent elements, subsequently adjusting the camera alignment to ensure focus on the identified acoustic source, as depicted in Figure 1. After acquiring the binaural audio, we discard samples characterized by minimal interaural differences, thereby retaining only those that exhibit distinct spatial auditory cues essential for binaural perception. BiAudio exemplifies the most extensive compilation of video content featuring high-fidelity binaural sound, ensuring audio-visual coherence across diverse real-world contexts. This underscores the limited scale of other datasets containing real-world binaural sound. For instance, SimBinaural, while offering an extensive duration of audio data, lacks authenticity, thereby limiting its applicability in practical scenarios. Moreover, datasets often concentrate on particular auditory elements, such as urban environments or musical compositions. In contrast, the BiAudio model exhibits extensive coverage of diverse real-world acoustic environments, enhancing the perception of spatial configurations and movement dynamics, which facilitates the generation of high-quality binaural audio from video content.

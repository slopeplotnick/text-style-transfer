The system operates on Spring Boot, with the database managed through PostgreSQL. Agronomists utilize Python or MATLAB to construct the simulators. Moreover, the IT team employs a central platform known as Cross, an extensive framework, to develop application code. Initially, the platform was designed to facilitate the creation of Proof of Concepts. However, over time, this purpose has evolved, and the platform has been transformed into a foundational base for finished products. Currently, the seven distinct versions are distributed among three Product Owners, reflecting the allocation and management of responsibilities within the product development framework. Annually, a new iteration tailored to specific client requirements is developed, thereby facilitating the continuous adaptation and enhancement of product offerings. The levels of the Architecture Dimension, as delineated in the FEF framework, address the methodologies for constructing the software and establish the technical specifications for product development within the software product line. Specifically, Level 1 architectures pertain exclusively to individual systems, lacking an emphasis on reuse. In contrast, Level 2 involves the reutilization of third-party infrastructure, characterized by the incorporation of common software components such as middleware, yet lacking the formal utilization of domain-specific assets. Progressing to Level 3, common features within the domain are integrated into a software platform, thus establishing a reference architecture applicable to all applications, with a primary emphasis on platform utilization. Nevertheless, this platform lacks the capability to manage diverse configurations. Advancing to Level 4, a comprehensive reference architecture is established for the entire software product line, effectively capturing both shared features and distinctive aspects within the domain. Domain assets are designed to facilitate the development of products by addressing variability management within the context of Software Product Line (SPL) architecture. At Level 5, the primary emphasis resides within the reference architecture, where application architectures exhibit only marginal deviations from the established framework. Consequently, automated product creation can be achieved through the use of scripts, tools, and high-level languages. Application development is primarily focused on configuring components within the constraints of the reference architecture, thereby facilitating the automatic setup of products.

Policies that utilize diverse noise patterns in flow-matching or diffusion-based frameworks exhibit a dependency on the stochastic properties of the initial noise, which directly influences the resulting actions. In this study, we address the challenge of adapting to shifts in data distribution from an anti-exploration standpoint and propose a novel method termed Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-Counts (TACO). The principle of anti-exploration, as introduced in offline reinforcement learning (RezaEIFar et al., 2022; Levine et al., 2020b), seeks to restrict the policy from executing actions that fall outside the dataset's support. Similarly, during visual-language action (VLA) inference, the objective is to constrain the model to generate actions consistent with successful patterns present in the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) dataset while systematically avoiding redundant or suboptimal actions inherited from pretraining or incomplete fine-tuning. To achieve this, TACO implements anti-exploration through test-time scaling (TTS), which adjusts the action sampling process rather than modifying the underlying policy parameters. This approach is particularly advantageous as flow-matching or diffusion-based VLA models involve intricate sampling mechanisms that are not compatible with conventional reinforcement learning-style updates. Furthermore, TTS operates without requiring gradient computation, thus preserving computational efficiency. To enable this mechanism, we leverage a classical Coin-Flipping Network (CFN) (Lobel et al., 2023), which is equipped with an enhanced internal representation system designed to estimate pseudo-counts for each observation-instruction-action-chunk pair within the SFT dataset. Notably, an action chunk with a higher pseudo-count demonstrates greater alignment with frequently observed successful behaviors in the SFT dataset, making it the preferred choice. Consequently, TACO utilizes pseudo-counts derived from the CFN as a verifier during inference, selecting the optimal action chunk among competing alternatives based on the highest pseudo-count. Moreover, to mitigate computational overhead during test-time, we incorporate a shared observation key-value cache, which significantly reduces latency by reusing visual-language representations across samples. This cache enhances computational efficiency while maintaining the reliability of the inference process.

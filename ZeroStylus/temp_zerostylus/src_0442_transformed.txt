Large language models frequently produce erroneous information, maintaining smooth and clear articulation yet inaccurately representing facts. These errors undermine trust and impede their implementation in practical applications. To address this challenge, researchers propose two methodologies: one involves refining the model itself through Hallucination Detection, while the other employs Fact Verification to assess factual accuracy. Despite converging on a shared objective, their development has diverged, characterized by the adoption of distinct conceptual frameworks, datasets, and validation methodologies. This division has impeded the advancement of progress. Therefore, in the ensuing analysis, we aim to integrate these elements comprehensively. We introduce UniFact, a tool designed to facilitate direct comparison by generating model outputs and assessing their factual accuracy. Extensive evaluation of various models and methodologies revealed three key findings: (1) neither approach consistently outperforms the other; (2) each method identifies distinct categories of factual inaccuracies; and (3) the integration of both approaches yields optimal results. Beyond a simple comparison, our analysis delves into the divergent paths taken by these elements and articulates the necessity for their unification. Hence, Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification necessitate a novel strategy for integration within large language models. These models have significantly influenced the digital landscape by facilitating sophisticated searches and executing complex tasks that were previously deemed infeasible. However, this poses a significant challenge in critical applications, as they produce fluent yet inaccurate outputs, a phenomenon referred to as hallucination. Accurate information retrieval is paramount in domains such as healthcare, law, and journalism, where the dissemination of errors can erode trust, propagate misinformation, and result in detrimental consequences. Therefore, this paper emphasizes that identifying robust methods to detect errors transcends a purely technological challenge, proving crucial for the development of dependable intelligent systems.

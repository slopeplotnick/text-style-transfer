ReVSeg redefines the process of video object segmentation by structuring it as a sequence of reasoning steps. The task is systematically decomposed into three core capabilities native to Vision-Language Models (VLMs): video comprehension, temporal localization, and spatial localization. This decomposition is executed through iterative dialogue with a singular VLM interface. Initially, the process transforms a raw and cluttered video sequence, accompanied by an ambiguous query such as “What’s the best car for a family trip?” into a well-defined keyframe and a precise textual description, exemplified by “the silver minivan.” Within this framework, the VLM utilizes commonsense and causal reasoning to simplify the complex video-text reasoning problem into a more manageable image-level segmentation task. Subsequently, during the second round of dialogue, the system processes the selected keyframe and the detailed object description. The VLM then performs spatial localization, outputting a tight bounding box for the specified object, thereby completing the two-round reasoning chain. With the localized keyframe target, an off-the-shelf video tracker, such as SAM2, is employed to produce the final video mask predictions. Since both rounds are encapsulated within a single VLM, the semantic context from earlier reasoning stages is seamlessly propagated to subsequent steps, ensuring consistency throughout the process. Furthermore, the reasoning process is optimized through reinforcement learning. Rather than solely rewarding the final outcome, the decomposition of the pipeline and the production of meaningful intermediate outputs enable the incorporation of richer, reasoning-aligned reward signals, thereby enhancing policy improvement. However, directly achieving spatio-temporal localization from raw video input remains challenging for current VLMs due to the limited availability of high-quality annotated datasets. Consequently, these models have not been extensively pretrained for Video Object Segmentation (VOS). Even with advanced strategies such as Chain-of-Thought prompting, multi-rollout sampling with self-consistency, or search-based inference methods like Tree Search and Beam Search, VLMs continue to face difficulties in reliably generating spatio-temporal localization results directly from raw video input.

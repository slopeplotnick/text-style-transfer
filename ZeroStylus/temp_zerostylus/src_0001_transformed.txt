Certain models may demonstrate greater reliability than others. This review examines the influence of model size, architectural design, and the composition of training data on the emergence of hallucinations. To address these patterns, the study identifies their presence across various models and proposes interventions such as optimizing training methodologies and imposing constraints on model outputs. However, another study indicates that hallucinations pose a significant challenge for large language models irrespective of their scale or training methodology, although the underlying mechanisms contributing to these issues remain unexplored. Selecting an appropriate model and training methodology may mitigate the occurrence of hallucinations; nevertheless, the authors contend that achieving models entirely devoid of such errors remains implausible. This consideration holds considerable importance as it assists in formulating realistic goals and evaluating the suitability of the model for specific tasks. When selecting a large language model (LLM) to mitigate hallucinations, careful consideration of the model’s architecture, the diversity and quality of its training data, and the implementation of techniques such as fine-tuning or the integration of supplementary information is essential. Presently, a multitude of models exists, including widely utilized variations such as GPT, BERT, and T5. For instance, GPT generates coherent and well-structured text; however, these models may also produce false information, known as hallucinations. Conversely, BERT demonstrates a reduced tendency for hallucinations due to its use of bidirectional training for specific tasks; nevertheless, it may still generate novel or inaccurate information in certain instances. Furthermore, models such as PaLM and LLaMa have been engineered to mitigate hallucinations; however, the efficacy of these models is contingent upon the intricacies of their training and architectural design. Therefore, understanding the capabilities and limitations of these models is essential for selecting an appropriate tool in tasks where accuracy is paramount and minimizing hallucinations is a priority.

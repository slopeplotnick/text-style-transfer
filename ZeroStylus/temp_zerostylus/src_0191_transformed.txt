Recent advancements propose enhancements to language models' capabilities, specifically focusing on their ability to amend and recommend revisions to ambiguously phrased questions. These ambiguities significantly influence response patterns and introduce errors and misinterpretations. Therefore, it becomes crucial to systematically identify the alignment between specific questions and their respective categories, facilitating a detailed and structured categorization. Consequently, we propose that language models can serve as invaluable tools for researchers by leveraging their ability to interpret the semantic content of questions, thereby assisting in error prevention. In this context, the methodology enables researchers to systematically examine the interrelationships among various categories. However, in instances where categories exhibit excessive similarity, significant modifications may be requisite. Additionally, mechanisms have been identified to ensure that language models produce text consistently aligned with a uniform reading level, thereby enhancing comprehensibility. The utilization of language models in the development of these assessments is further elucidated in a recent publication by Beghetto et al. Herein, we explore the application of language models designed to fabricate artificial data aimed at facilitating the construction of these assessments. One illustrative approach to optimizing resource allocation involves the preliminary evaluation of interventions on simulated cohorts, thereby reducing the substantial time and financial costs typically associated with conventional testing methodologies. Moreover, this development may facilitate cost reduction for researchers in test development and minimize the implementation of superfluous examinations. To evaluate the efficacy of the latest language models in generating data conforming to established test structures, we conducted experiments using contemporary models. In the subsequent sections, we will articulate the methodology employed in our study and the underlying rationale for its anticipated efficacy. Finally, the process of developing these tests adhered primarily to the procedures outlined in Classical Test Theory, supplemented by the recommendations of Boateng et al.

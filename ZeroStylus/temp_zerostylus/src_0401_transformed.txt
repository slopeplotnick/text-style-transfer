For each of the three metrics, we calculate the values for all unordered pairs of methods within the HD paradigm, within the FV paradigm, and across both paradigms. The resulting scores, presented in Table 2, provide quantitative evidence that the HD and FV paradigms exhibit distinct yet complementary strengths. Notably, the elevated ACS observed for cross-paradigm pairings indicates that their successes are largely non-overlapping, suggesting that these paradigms excel in addressing different subsets of the problem space. Furthermore, the superior ASG score highlights that the combined application of HD and FV methodologies yields the most substantial performance improvements, surpassing any combination confined to a single paradigm. Most critically, the AECR demonstrates a significant increase for cross-paradigm pairings, illustrating that the error patterns of the two paradigms are largely complementary: when one paradigm makes an error, the other frequently provides a correct response. Collectively, these results support the conclusion that HD and FV are not merely distinct but also synergistic, thereby justifying the pursuit of hybrid models that integrate both paradigms. While the statistical complementarity of HD and FV has been established, it is equally important to examine the underlying causes of their differential performance. To this end, we conduct a detailed case study to identify typical failure scenarios for each paradigm. Specifically, FV systems tend to perform reliably when evidence is readily accessible, enabling accurate assessments; however, they often produce "Not Enough Information" (NEI) errors in cases where relevant evidence is unavailable. In contrast, HD systems are prone to false positives, as their sensitivity to linguistic variability can lead to misclassifications of diverse phrasing as hallucinations. Analyzing these distinct failure modes not only clarifies the complementary nature of the paradigms but also underscores the importance of designing hybrid integration methods, a topic that will be explored in the subsequent section.

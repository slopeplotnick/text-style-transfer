The Long-Tail Hard subset comprises a curated collection of challenging examples deliberately designed to assess the proficiency of advanced language models in managing complex and demanding tasks. The study concentrated on identifying intricate cases, categorized into explicit requests, such as those involving multiple attributes or multi-requirements, and implicit or indirect requests. The implicit requests, however, can be further delineated into four distinct classifications: those involving negation, alternative options, domain-specific knowledge, and complex reasoning. Various methodologies were employed to identify these elements. Specifically, to identify instances of negation and alternatives, we relied on the extraction of specific keywords and patterns. Moreover, for the less prevalent knowledge and reasoning-based tasks, we implemented a reduction in the proportion of straightforward examples and subsequently employed a high-capacity language model (Qwen3-235B) to evaluate consistency in generating the correct response, requiring it to replicate the same answer in at least four out of eight attempts. When addressing multi-attribute requests, a tool was utilized to identify queries characterized by the presence of more than five distinct attributes. Furthermore, to ensure these examples were genuinely challenging, they were evaluated using a basic model, retaining only those instances where the model achieved correct predictions five or fewer times out of eight attempts, thus indicating considerable difficulty. The Visual Salience subset pertains to scenarios where the primary product image holds significant importance, particularly when the depicted color or style is crucial for informed decision-making. The identification of these cases commenced with the utilization of keywords derived from an exhaustive categorization of visual elements. In this instance, the process involved excluding examples via a blacklist to prevent errors and retaining only those instances where the critical visual keyword was absent from the product description. To validate that the identified keywords corresponded to visual attributes, the language model was employed to confirm their relevance. Finally, to identify the most challenging examples, we developed a straightforward model that simultaneously analyzes textual and visual data, selecting instances where the model exhibited significant uncertainty, as evidenced by its frequent disagreement on the correct answer.

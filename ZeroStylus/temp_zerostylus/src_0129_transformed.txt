Individuals primarily focused on business or possessing limited technical expertise are likely to exhibit the greatest resistance to change. Even when acknowledging the utility of advancements, they may harbor significant concerns regarding the rapid pace of technological delivery. However, given the team's relatively small size and their familiarity with new technologies, coupled with the consideration of Software Product Line (SPL) from the project's inception, we anticipated minimal resistance to change. In this context, insights gained from interviews have enabled us to identify strategies to solidify existing good practices, enhance benefits, and mitigate risks and apprehensions. Therefore, emphasizing and perpetuating these beneficial practices during SPL implementation and maintenance is crucial for reassuring all stakeholders. Although workflow patterns will inevitably evolve, a team that preserves its effective practices is more likely to remain motivated and embrace new developments. Consequently, by maintaining current good practices, we aim to keep the team within a familiar environment amidst significant changes. The core tenets of SPL—rapid processes, resource reutilization, and component decomposition—are already being implemented, thus allowing us to focus on the prospective advantages. Transitioning to an SPL also supports sustainable reuse and decomposition. Moreover, the expressed benefits include customization, automation, and industrialization, which are foundational aspects of SPL. Additionally, other criteria, such as improved code quality, are pertinent to this discussion, as they contribute to alleviating technical debt. Ensuring a homogeneous and consistent code base with fewer bugs while avoiding redundant code duplication enhances efficiency. Addressing common bugs further elevates code quality. Interestingly, the selling points are recognized not only by product owners but even more so by IT professionals. For instance, employing this engineering approach may bolster the company's reputation in research and development (R&D). Similarly, SPL adoption facilitates the accelerated development of new software.

The integration of robotics in construction projects holds significant potential for mitigating workforce shortages and enhancing safety and efficiency, contingent upon the equitable allocation of resources to ensure long-term sustainability. Robots possess exceptional capabilities in executing physically demanding and monotonous tasks with unwavering consistency and precision, thereby reducing fatigue and minimizing errors. Consequently, leveraging these technologies can accelerate construction processes and reduce expenses. However, due to the inherent complexity and multifaceted nature of construction work, a single robot cannot perform all tasks independently. For instance, the construction of a structure such as a wall requires a diverse set of skills, including material transport, precise placement, welding, and quality control, which no single robot can encompass. Therefore, the integration of multiple robotic units is imperative for collaborative operations. Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) addresses this need by decomposing large-scale operations into smaller, manageable tasks, which are then distributed among various robots. This allocation process considers the capabilities of each robot, the requirements of the tasks, and the characteristics of the operational environment to ensure balanced workload distribution and optimal resource utilization. MRTA challenges involve determining the optimal task distribution, taking into account whether tasks are singular or multifaceted, whether they require individual or collective execution, and considering the temporal demands of each task. Coordinating a diverse array of robots for various tasks over time necessitates sophisticated planning strategies that address varying robotic capabilities, task requirements, and temporal constraints. Nonetheless, the majority of MRTA research predominantly focuses on warehouse operations or environmental exploration, with limited attention to construction applications.

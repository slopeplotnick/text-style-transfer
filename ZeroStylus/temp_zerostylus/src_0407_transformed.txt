Flow-based diffusion models have become a preferred paradigm for training generative models in the domains of image and video synthesis. However, the mechanisms by which they encode and extrapolate information remain insufficiently comprehended. In this investigation, we revisit the concept of flow matching by thoroughly examining its marginal velocity field, which can be precisely computed, thereby facilitating the identification of the exact FM target. Analyzing this velocity field reveals that these models inherently pursue a dual training objective: initially emphasizing a mixture of data types and subsequently prioritizing the nearest data example. This dual objective facilitates distinct educational interventions. Specifically, the initial component aids in comprehending varied data categories to synthesize overarching patterns, while the subsequent component emphasizes the retention of finer details. Leveraging this understanding, we elucidate the efficacy of specific methodologies, such as adjusting the temporal sequence of steps, employing guidance devoid of classifiers, and making strategic decisions in the configuration of latent spaces. Consequently, we elucidate the mechanisms of diffusion model training, thereby providing a comprehensive framework for future advancements in design and algorithmic enhancements. Diffusion models have emerged as a prominent class of generative approaches, demonstrating the capacity to produce high-quality samples across diverse domains, including images and videos. These models learn complex data patterns through a gradual transformation of an initial distribution, such as Gaussian, into the target data distribution, employing a process guided by denoising steps or transport directions. Moreover, the diffusion process can be characterized through the composition of various approaches, such as probabilistic frameworks, score-based ordinary or stochastic differential equations, and flow matching. To this end, we trace the divergence in training objectives—such as predicting noise, score, or velocity—to their connections with distinct formulations of the probability flow ODE. Recently, the flow matching method has gained consensus as an effective approach for training models to predict the velocity field using a straightforward schedule. Therefore, the stability and simplicity of this method have resulted in its establishment as the predominant approach for the training of leading diffusion models.

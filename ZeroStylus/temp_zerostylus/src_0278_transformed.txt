Since linguistic knowledge is explicitly represented and can be consulted by researchers with respect to both agents, this approach provides an insightful addition to systems that learn language without employing explicit representations, such as large language models. MODOMA implements unsupervised learning because the input data to the language acquisition device are unannotated, and this module cannot access the mother agent's internal processing and parses. Moreover, the daughter agent can utilize previously acquired grammatical knowledge as input for language acquisition procedures. This method, named internal annotation, represents a form of self-supervised learning (cf. Balestriero et al. 2023, Brown et al. 2020, Devlin et al. 2019, Gui et al. 2024, Lan et al. 2020, Orhan et al. 2020, Yarowsky 1995). The combination of characteristics and research possibilities offered by the multiagent MODOMA framework, to the best of our knowledge, constitutes a novel design. This system is particularly suitable for conducting language acquisition experiments based on natural language, involving both a mature language model and an acquiring language model. Within these interactions, all aspects of the system, such as parameter settings, input, executed language acquisition procedures, interactions, generated utterances, and acquired grammar, are user-controlled, reproducible, measurable, and verifiable. Ter Hoeve et al. (2022) present a roadmap of a teacher-student loop for modeling language acquisition and implement two initial experiments. The first experiment aims to model separate domains with 'two strictly separated vocabularies' of an artificial language, whereas the second experiment addresses distinctive structures modeled by repetitions of tokens. In these experiments, the teacher selects a fixed amount of training data from pre-constructed sentences to provide to multiple students, which consist of language models. Following training, the student language models are evaluated with a test set comprising different sentences of the artificial language. Additionally, a teacher language model is trained on another subset of sentences and subsequently assessed. The current implementation of this framework notably differs from MODOMA regarding the training and test data: Ter Hoeve et al. (2022) utilize pre-constructed utterances of an artificial language, whereas MODOMA employs naturally occurring language data produced online by Delilah, based on an explicitly defined grammar model of Dutch.

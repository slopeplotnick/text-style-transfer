To the best of our knowledge, this represents the inaugural employment of an open-vocabulary object detector aimed at enhancing the comprehension of high-resolution images by MLLMs. Despite the detector's capability to accurately identify targets while ignoring non-essential regions, it consistently exhibits superior precision across various evaluations. Moreover, our proposed MRD framework operates without necessitating training and demonstrates substantial generalizability. Inspired by this concept, we introduce a multi-resolution fusion strategy that addresses semantic similarity by generating similarity maps across multiple resolutions. This strategy leverages open-set detection results to refine target regions, thus facilitating improved coherence and functionality. Extensive tests further affirm the efficacy and adaptability of our method across diverse contexts. Specifically, we evaluate performance on both single-object and multi-object tasks across a range of MLLMs and high-resolution benchmarks, consistently achieving strong outcomes in each scenario. To advance MLLMs' ability to comprehend high-resolution images without proportionally increasing the complexity of visual tokens, several studies have integrated high-resolution visual encoders. For instance, Vary and Deepseek-VL employ the SAM encoder to (1) boost model performance on high-resolution images, (2) optimize the integration of image details into their processing pipelines, and (3) ensure consistent effectiveness across varying image complexities. In this section, we explore the deployment of Native/Dynamic-Resolution MLLMs, which are designed to process images at their original resolution. This approach constructs a sequence of visual tokens that preserves the original dimensions of the image, maintaining fine-grained details and spatial fidelity. Various models implement distinct methodologies to address the challenges posed by extensive sequences and inherent complexity, including sliding window attention, dynamic masking, and patch-based encoding. Significant contributions in this domain have demonstrated considerable advancement. For example, we examine the strategy employed by the InternVL series, which partitions high-resolution images into fixed-size patches for further processing. Alternatively, models such as Qwen2.5-VL directly train a Vision Transformer (ViT) on images at their native resolution, enabling the conversion of the entire image into a single token sequence in one step. This methodology potentially enhances the comprehension of the overall context.

In the analysis of images, accounting for occluded joints and the global context—such as spatial relationships among joints, hands, and objects within a scene—is crucial. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs), specifically ResNet-50, are frequently employed as backbone architectures for 3D hand pose estimation due to their effectiveness in extracting spatial features. These CNNs utilize localized operations that strike a balance between computational efficiency and predictive accuracy; however, they exhibit limitations in capturing broader contextual information. Consequently, recent studies have turned to vision transformers (ViT) for hand mesh reconstruction, although their computational demands may reduce processing speed. Therefore, there exists substantial potential for enhancing these systems' capacity to accurately interpret hand poses. The Mamba model, initially developed for language processing tasks, has gained significant attention for its ability to prioritize critical components of the input, thereby fostering a comprehensive understanding of long sequences. Furthermore, recent research has extended its application to image processing. In these applications, VisionMamba employs a specialized module for spatial sequence modeling, whereas VMamba introduces an alternative module characterized by varied scanning trajectories. Conventional approaches, relying on a fixed grid framework, prove suboptimal for capturing intricate 3D hand motion dynamics. To address this limitation, we propose DF-Mamba, an innovative system for 3D hand pose estimation that employs a flexible methodology to enhance the extraction of visual features. Specifically, DF-Mamba integrates dynamic modeling of both local and global features through adaptable state spaces. Each block within our system extracts localized features along an adaptable pathway, thereby retaining critical information essential for comprehending the overarching global context. The necessity for deformable point sampling arises in altering the scanning path, utilizing local anchors and adaptable offsets informed by the input features.

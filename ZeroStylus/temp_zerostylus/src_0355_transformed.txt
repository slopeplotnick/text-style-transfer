We conduct extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks, including machine translation evaluation (WMT23) (Song et al., 2025) and reward model assessment (Reward Bench 2) (Malik et al., 2025). Notably, our proposed distribution-calibrated approach demonstrates significant improvements over established self-consistency baselines. By systematically modeling the complete vote distribution, our method converts noisy individual model judgments into more reliable aggregated ratings, achieving performance that matches or surpasses human raters when evaluated against a human-consensus gold standard. Our contributions are threefold: First, we identify that existing aggregation methods employed during inference by LLM judges are suboptimal, emphasizing the necessity of a carefully designed approach for result integration. Second, we introduce an Expected Risk Minimization (ERM)-based aggregation methodology, leveraging the Bradley-Terry-Davidson model and calibrated on a limited dataset, which robustly outperforms competing aggregation methods across multiple tasks, including reward benchmarks and machine translation. Third, for the machine translation task, we adopt a consensus-driven meta-evaluation framework to generate higher-fidelity ground truth labels in scenarios where the data is noisy. This approach facilitates fair comparisons with human raters and reveals specific conditions under which LLM-based evaluations achieve "super-human" quality. Recently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable efficacy as evaluative agents, offering a scalable alternative to traditional metrics for assessing generated text (Zheng et al., 2023b; Gu et al., 2025). This paradigm shows high correlation with human judgments across a wide range of domains. Approaches to employing LLMs as evaluators vary: some utilize general-purpose models directly, such as G-Eval (Liu et al., 2023) or JudgeLM (Zhu et al., 2025), while others fine-tune specialized models optimized for evaluation tasks, including Prometheus (Kim et al., 2023) and Auto-J (Li et al., 2023). However, while these methodologies are robust, they exhibit vulnerabilities, including sensitivity to prompt design (Gu et al., 2025) and inherent biases, such as positional bias (favoring candidates based on order) and morbidity bias (preferring longer outputs) (Wang et al., 2023a). Moreover, LLM judges vary in their decision-making consistency, with some models demonstrating aggressive tendencies in resolving ties or subtle distinctions (Zheng et al., 2023b). To address these challenges, our work focuses on mitigating noise and enhancing the reliability of judgments through principled aggregation methods.

In this study, rather than solely evaluating the severity of decision-making, we investigate the variability among large language model (LLM) judges in their decision-making processes. We observe that certain judges apply more stringent criteria to minor discrepancies or ties. Each initiative represents a distinctive methodological approach, aiming to improve consistency in decision-making. This is achieved by employing advanced techniques for synthesizing outcomes and minimizing extraneous variables. Moreover, our analysis considers the correlation between LLM judges' evaluations, exemplified by comparing pairs such as GPT-4o versus Gemini-2.5-pro. Notably, we have comprehensive data from GPT-4o across all problems, whereas data from other judges are comparatively sparse. Consequently, GPT-4o is designated as the default judge for all comparisons, aligning with our primary experimental framework. Furthermore, human evaluation data is utilized to validate LLM judge consistency, wherein human evaluators conduct belief evaluations analogous to those requested of LLM judges. This validation is exemplified by pairing human evaluators with LLM judges, such as human evaluator 1 versus GPT-4o. As a form of validation, we demonstrate that the Martingale Score predicts the ground-truth accuracy of reasoning processes, applying it across various models, prompts, and problem domains.

The refinement of computer utilization can be significantly attributed to the development of innovative tools and systems. These tools autonomously enable computers to comprehend and execute tasks independently, thereby advancing their operational capabilities and self-sufficiency. Moreover, open-source systems can be constructed by integrating various components such as planners and memory, facilitating the streamlined assembly and flexible adaptation of distinct elements within a cohesive framework. Corporate reports elucidate the practical application of these systems, thereby facilitating the establishment of pragmatic objectives and assessing the suitability of the selected model for the specified task. However, expanding model size alone does not inherently enhance reliability, as suggested by certain studies. Therefore, while the continuous assessment of these systems is essential, the focus lies in ensuring their progressive enhancement. Indeed, the evaluation of these systems is challenging due to the increasing complexity of the tasks they perform. This study examines the integration of specialized systems in evaluation processes, highlighting concerns regarding potential bias. Consequently, this approach entails implementing multiple rounds of independent evaluations across diverse tasks, ensuring fairness through the use of multiple judges. Our project's methodology incorporates dual evaluators examining distinct components of the task to ensure the authenticity of enhancements. Complementing our resources, we introduce a curated assemblage of 100 real-world editing tasks featuring structured slide components. Accompanying each task is a comprehensive set comprising the original slide deck, the target deck, explicit instructions, and a detailed guide delineating the criteria for evaluating layout and content. The tasks were systematically categorized into 16 distinct groups to ensure comprehensive evaluation of both conceptual understanding and precise formatting. In alignment with our research framework, we introduce a comprehensive dataset derived from over 15,000 PowerPoint presentations, meticulously restructured to encapsulate both layout and content specifics. Furthermore, a diverse selection of styles was retained and subsequently synthesized by integrating contributions from various domain experts. The selection process involved evaluating over 500 options, including those created by students, and identifying 100 exemplars that encompass diverse professional and academic disciplines, ensuring each category presents challenging instances.

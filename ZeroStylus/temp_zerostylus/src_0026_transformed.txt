Our findings are consistent with the results of Shirafuji et al. (2025), who employed a task vector-based approach, similarly demonstrating the performance of debiased models. In contrast, Lutz et al. (2024) conducted an investigation utilizing model merging to assess the performance of debiased large language models (LLMs) on downstream tasks, employing scores derived from Natural Language Inference (NLI) benchmarks. While our results underscore the necessity for methods evaluated solely on tasks such as NLI to be examined more comprehensively across diverse datasets, SuperGLUE results indicate that, with the exception of Linear, SLERP, and Nearswap, alternative merging techniques substantially degrade the causal reasoning capabilities of LLMs, as elaborated in Section 5.2. Consequently, these methods appear unsuitable for reliable bias mitigation. SLERP's effectiveness can be attributed to its capability of ensuring unbiased interpolation by maintaining an even distribution across the parameter space. However, NuSLERP, which employs normalization at the layer or tensor level, negatively impacts its performance on SuperGLUE scores. Recent analyses suggest that, unlike SLERP, most novel model-merging techniques are ineffective for bias reduction without significantly compromising reasoning capabilities. This study presents the first comprehensive examination of how seven model-merging algorithms influence social bias within LLMs. By evaluating 13 models from the GPT, LLaMA, and Qwen families on three social bias datasets and the SuperGLUE benchmark, we revealed a notable trade-off between fairness and utility. Moreover, methods such as Karcher Mean, NuSLERP, TIES, and DELLA substantially degraded performance on tasks requiring advanced reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning, including ReCoRD, COPA, CB, and BoolQ within the SuperGLUE benchmark. Although some existing debiasing methods utilizing model merging have shown improved performance of debiased LLMs on downstream tasks via NLI scores, it remains essential to verify accuracy on tasks involving reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning.

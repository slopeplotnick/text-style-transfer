As illustrated in Fig. 12, the experimental group employing the curriculum learning approach demonstrates a faster learning pace and more consistent improvements in both reward outcomes and model performance. During the reinforcement learning (RL) process, as the model gradually approaches its objective, the stochasticity of its outputs decreases, leading to increasingly deterministic behavior. This reduction in randomness constrains the model's ability to explore novel strategies, thereby hindering the discovery of potentially superior solutions and limiting the upper bound of performance improvement. To address this issue, we investigated several strategies aimed at decelerating the onset of predictability to preserve the model's exploratory capabilities. Figure 13 provides a comparative analysis of these methods. Among them, the Clip-higher strategy exhibits the most effective results by moderating the rate at which the model's predictability increases. This strategy achieves a favorable balance between exploration and exploitation, ensuring training stability and delivering significant performance enhancements. In contrast, the On-policy method maintains a constant level of model randomness, effectively preventing a sharp decline in stochastic behavior. However, this approach simultaneously hinders convergence efficiency, leading to more modest performance gains. Meanwhile, the Off-policy + Entropy Loss method initially demonstrates stable training dynamics. Nevertheless, as training progresses and the model's stochastic behavior declines below a critical threshold, the influence of the entropy regularization loss intensifies. This term eventually surpasses the primary loss as the dominant factor, causing abnormal increases in randomness and culminating in training collapse. Consequently, this method requires extensive hyperparameter tuning to determine optimal configurations, thereby increasing the overall implementation cost. In summary, the Clip-higher strategy achieves an optimal equilibrium between exploration and learning efficiency by strategically controlling the rate of predictability onset, rendering it the most effective approach for the context of this study.

Various approaches have been proposed to enhance reasoning capabilities in language models. For instance, CoT2 (Gozeten et al., 2025) incorporates methods that learn to mix tokens in a manner analogous to training-free approaches, applying them to GPT-2 for logical reasoning tasks. Similarly, CODI (Shen et al., 2025b) employs a teacher-student distillation framework to improve performance on GSM8k tasks, targeting GPT-2 and Llama 3.1 1B models. In addition, Token Assorted (Su et al., 2025) introduces novel token embeddings through VQ-VAE and trains the Llama 3.1 8B model for optimization in mathematical and logical reasoning tasks. However, our LiteReason approach diverges significantly from these methodologies in several key aspects. First, unlike approaches that rely on expansive datasets such as GSM8k-Aug for reasoning traces—which are unavailable for our specific tasks—LiteReason exclusively utilizes traces generated intrinsically by the model itself. Second, rather than training the entire language model, we develop a specialized Reasoning Projector to improve latent reasoning capabilities. This decision is based on the hypothesis that extensive training of the base LLM for token embedding generation may compromise its proficiency in token generation overall. Third, LiteReason allows the model to interleave latent and discrete reasoning during reinforcement learning (RL) training, positing that the model will autonomously learn to balance these modalities over time. Furthermore, although existing methodologies often compare their results against Chain-of-Thought prompting and occasionally incorporate RL, to our knowledge, none evaluate performance against a non-latent RL-trained model. We argue that this baseline offers a more accurate benchmark for challenging tasks and include it as an upper bound on performance and computational constraints in our experiments. In addition to reasoning in domains such as mathematics and programming, large language models have been extensively applied to narrative understanding and generation tasks. Prior research (Mostafazadeh et al., 2016; Fan et al., 2018; Huot et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2022, 2023; Xie and Riedl, 2024; Chhun et al., 2022; Karpinska et al., 2024; Sprague et al., 2023) has explored various aspects of story generation, often prompting models to perform specialized subtasks such as plotting events or creating detailed character profiles (Huot et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023, 2022). These subtasks can be conceptualized as eliciting distinct reasoning methodologies. However, adapting narrative generation tasks to RL frameworks remains challenging due to the lack of well-defined, verifiable reward structures. Consequently, this study focuses on two specific tasks designed to evaluate reasoning capabilities within narrative contexts, aiming to refine the model’s ability to process and interpret complex narrative structures effectively.

Taken together, these findings underscore the substantial cross-modal generalization capabilities of OMNIGUARD. Specifically, OMNIGUARD demonstrates a remarkable ability to extrapolate safety reasoning from trained data modalities to untrained ones, while acquiring modality-invariant semantic representations of unsafe content. Accordingly, we introduce OMNIGUARD-7B and OMNIGUARD-3B, the first family of omni-modal guardrails developed using an extensive and unified safety fine-tuning dataset that encompasses both unimodal and cross-modal samples. This framework exemplifies a unified omni-modal safety solution, capable of moderating and reasoning about unsafe content across heterogeneous and cross-modal environments. Notably, experimental results reveal that OMNIGUARD-7B consistently outperforms existing guardrail models across all data modalities, while OMNIGUARD-3B achieves competitive performance when compared to large-scale LLMs and MLLMs, such as Qwen3-235B and Qwen3-VL-235B. These outcomes highlight the strong omni-modal safety detection and reasoning capacities of the system, validating the feasibility of a unified guardrail solution with omni-modal understanding and interpretability. Looking forward, future research will aim to address more complex and cross-modal safety scenarios to further refine omni-modal safeguarding mechanisms in next-generation large language models. Nonetheless, certain limitations persist. While OMNIGUARD exhibits robust omni-modal safety reasoning and consistent compatibility across diverse data modalities, incorporating reasoning paths—though significantly enhancing the interpretability and reliability of safety evaluations—inevitably increases inference latency due to the added complexity involved in generating reasoning steps. Consequently, OMNIGUARD requires higher computational resources compared to lightweight, binary-classification guardrail systems. Therefore, balancing the depth of safety reasoning with inference efficiency remains an important challenge, which should be optimized based on specific use-case requirements. Furthermore, the limited availability of publicly accessible cross-modal safety fine-tuning datasets presents a significant opportunity to advance performance in moderating more intricate and interleaved multimodal safety contexts. In conclusion, we hope future research will continue to enhance the robustness and reliability of omni-modal systems, while expanding their capacity to mitigate risks across diverse and complex modality combinations.

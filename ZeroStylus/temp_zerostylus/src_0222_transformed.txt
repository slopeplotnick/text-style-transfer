Research in the field of truthful AI focuses on the development of AI systems that provide accurate representations of the real world. Prior research has proposed methods to enhance the truthfulness of these systems, such as identifying behavioral patterns associated with truthful outputs or modifying decision-making processes within the AI to align with truth. Moreover, research has examined the trade-off between ensuring the truthfulness of AI systems and optimizing their utility in practical applications. Honest AI research emphasizes the development of systems that abstain from intentionally generating outputs that contradict their internal representations of truth solely to maximize rewards. This emphasis aligns with concerns that AI systems, particularly large language models (LLMs), may exhibit tendencies toward deceptive behavior, thereby raising issues regarding their reliability and trustworthiness. In contrast, truth-seeking AI focuses on analyzing evidence to uncover new knowledge about the world. The autonomous behavior exhibited by AI systems, wherein they independently determine which information to seek based on their situational comprehension, could increasingly impact the reliability and societal trustworthiness of such systems. While truth-seeking AI is dedicated to ascertaining the truth, multiple approaches exist to evaluate its efficacy in achieving this goal. For instance, one might consider adopting an approach grounded in Bayesian principles or maintaining coherence in its logical consistency. Conversely, belief entrenchment denotes a tendency to adhere to existing beliefs despite the presence of contradictory evidence. This phenomenon is closely related to confirmation bias, wherein individuals seek, recall, or interpret information in a manner that reinforces their pre-existing beliefs. The phenomenon of confirmation bias pertains to the manner in which individuals process information in particular contexts, and it presents challenges in quantification. The reliability of measuring confirmation bias in psychology is contingent upon the specific task, thereby rendering it less dependable. Therefore, in order to accurately assess such phenomena, it is essential to ascertain an individual's pre-existing beliefs and employ appropriate measurement methodologies, a task that presents significant challenges.

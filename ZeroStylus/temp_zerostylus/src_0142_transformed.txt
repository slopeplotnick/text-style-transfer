Traditional Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) methodologies have demonstrated considerable success across various domains, leveraging optimization algorithms such as linear programming, genetic algorithms, and Reinforcement Learning (RL), which are grounded in mathematical rigor and offer proven performance guarantees (Kuhn 1955, Atay 2006, Jones 2020, Chen 2019). However, the unstructured and dynamic nature of construction environments introduces unique challenges that undermine the efficacy of these traditional approaches. Specifically, the disorganization and coordination complexities inherent to construction sites create significant obstacles to operational effectiveness (Feng et al. 2016, Liang and Cheng 2023). Moreover, the sheer volume of information and the inherent complexity of construction tasks frequently surpass the reasoning and computational capabilities of mathematically driven optimization methods (Makondo et al. 2015, Xu et al. 2020). Consequently, these limitations necessitate the development of more adaptive and robust coordination solutions tailored to the demands of construction contexts. Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) offer a promising alternative, as these models exhibit remarkable capabilities in natural language comprehension, logical reasoning, and adaptive decision-making. These attributes enable LLMs to address several fundamental limitations of traditional optimization and learning-based methods, particularly in dynamic and complex environments (Kannan et al. 2024). Furthermore, LLM-based coordination approaches facilitate intuitive human-robot interaction and adaptive task management strategies that are well-suited to the intricate and evolving requirements of construction sites. Recent LLM frameworks have demonstrated potential through their application to task planning, digital twin integration, and multi-agent coordination architectures (Kannan et al. 2024, Prieto et al. 2024, Deng et al. 2025). However, despite these advancements, existing LLM-based systems lack systematic validation mechanisms and benchmarking frameworks to rigorously assess their performance in comparison to traditional methods. Therefore, a critical research gap remains: while LLM-based coordination shows significant promise, no comprehensive methodologies have been developed to implement iterative retry and self-corrective mechanisms that ensure reasoning reliability. Additionally, systematic performance evaluations comparing LLMs to traditional optimization approaches in construction task allocation contexts are notably absent. To address this gap, we propose the LangGraph-based Task Allocation Agent (LTAA) framework, a novel LLM-driven coordination paradigm that incorporates unique phase-adaptive strategies and multi-stage validation mechanisms. The proposed framework effectively balances workload distribution while ensuring robust performance, as the phase-adaptive strategy minimizes performance trade-offs. Simultaneously, the multi-stage validation mechanism enhances reasoning consistency by systematically detecting and rectifying allocation inefficiencies through structured feedback loops.

In this section, we elucidate the computation of each evaluation metric and interpret their outcomes, covering aspects such as lexical overlap, semantic similarity, and factual consistency. ROUGE-L, as introduced by Lin (2004), measures the longest common subsequence (LCS) between a model-generated summary and a reference text. This metric captures sentence-level structural similarity and is therefore less sensitive to variations in word order. Typically, it is utilized as a reference-based metric, comparing a human-authored summary to the system output. In our study, rather than focusing on the absolute ROUGE-L score, we examine the relative differences across various input-order conditions to assess potential primacy effects. Since ROUGE-L predominantly reflects surface-level lexical overlap, it is complemented by BERTScore and SummaC to evaluate semantic similarity and factual consistency, respectively. To transcend mere lexical matching, BERTScore employs contextualized embeddings to capture semantic similarity. It computes token-level similarity using contextual embeddings from a pre-trained encoder, specifically RoBERTa-large in this study. For each token in the model-generated summary, BERTScore identifies the most semantically similar token in the reference, calculating precision, recall, and F1 scores based on these alignments. Given that contextual embeddings account for synonymy and paraphrastic variation, BERTScore is particularly suited for evaluating semantic similarity beyond exact lexical matches. Consistent with our study design, we prioritize examining relative differences in BERTScore across input-order conditions, rather than the absolute similarity values, which may appear lower due to the abstractive nature of the task. SummaC, on the other hand, assesses whether the generated summary is entailed by the original documents, thereby examining factual consistency. This metric comprises two variants: SummaC-ZS, which operates in a zero-shot, parameter-free manner, and SummaC-Conv, which utilizes a trained convolutional model. Empirical evaluations suggest that SummaC-Conv consistently outperforms other metrics; hence, we employ this variant. Nevertheless, it is important to note that the underlying RoBERTa-based architecture imposes constraints on the volume of input data it can process, thereby limiting its capacity under certain conditions.

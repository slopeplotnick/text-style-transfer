This discussion endeavors to elucidate the utilization of PEFT methods in addressing the increasing scale of large language models, thereby providing a comprehensive framework that enhances the understanding of their application within expansive language model architectures. However, many recent PEFT methods present challenges in terms of reproducibility and comparability, as they are difficult to replicate, utilize, or evaluate against one another. To address this, PEFT-FACTORY has been developed as an integrated system that simplifies the fine-tuning of large language models by employing both pre-configured and user-defined PEFT methodologies. Moreover, the system's flexibility and expandability are demonstrated by its inclusion of 19 PEFT methods, 27 datasets for classification and text generation across 12 tasks, as well as evaluation tools for general and PEFT-specific metrics. Furthermore, PEFT-FACTORY provides a straightforward and reliable framework for replicating outcomes and evaluating PEFT methodologies, thus significantly contributing to these processes. For further accessibility, PEFT-FACTORY, which builds upon LLaMAFactory, is available at https://github.com/kinit-sk/PEFT-Factory. Since the introduction of the transformer architecture, large language models have achieved notable success across numerous natural language processing tasks, significantly advancing the capabilities of these models beyond previous benchmarks. This progress is driven by the exceptional scalability of these models, which results in a continuous increase in the number of trainable parameters. Consequently, the proliferation of parameters renders fine-tuning prohibitively costly, requires substantial data, and remains inaccessible to numerous researchers and practitioners. In an effort to address these challenges, PEFT methods focus on training a limited subset of the model's parameters while achieving outcomes comparable to those obtained by training the entire model. This reduction in trainable parameters may be achieved by introducing additional parameters, selectively training specific ones, or modifying the model to utilize a reduced number of parameters.

The primary character's velocity and directional changes are quantified within their personal coordinate frame at the final timestep of a motion sequence. Additionally, local joint angular displacements are represented in the character's anatomical coordinate frame throughout the motion sequence. Moreover, the 3D positions of the character's hands and feet are represented within their own spatial coordinates. To evaluate the efficacy of SMP, we apply it in training complex movement control tasks, thereby demonstrating its capacity to handle challenging scenarios effectively. In Section 8.1, we illustrate the flexibility of SMP by employing a pre-trained model to generate novel control rules, thus eliminating the need for the original movement data. Furthermore, a single style-based model, once trained on an extensive dataset, can be adapted to perform tasks across various styles by simply configuring the model to the desired stylistic parameters. New styles can be synthesized by combining the pretrained model with various stylistic inputs. In Section 8.2, the utilization of a unified movement model reveals its effectiveness in facilitating a range of tasks, such as steering, target acquisition, and dodgeball, thereby demonstrating its versatility across diverse applications. In addition to governing the behavioral dynamics of the character, as demonstrated in Section 8.3, SMP also manages human-object interaction dynamics. By training on data that encompasses human and object interactions, SMP effectively models both object manipulation and character motion, thus enabling characters to exhibit human-like behaviors when engaging with objects. Additionally, in Section 8.4, SMP naturally facilitates the development of recovery strategies for characters, allowing them to regain balance and composure effectively. For generating novel capabilities not explicitly present in the initial dataset, the rules formulated with SMP align data distributions akin to other matching methodologies. In Section 8.5, when trained on a short walk-jog-run dataset, SMP autonomously facilitates the emergence of diverse locomotion styles, enabling a character to align with a variety of speeds and execute smooth transitions between movements. Finally, in Section 8.6, SMP is evaluated on tasks involving the imitation of single clips to assess its proficiency in replicating motion patterns. Within this framework, SMP demonstrates its capability to faithfully replicate a comprehensive array of dynamic and acrobatic maneuvers, thereby achieving a level of performance comparable to leading imitation learning methodologies.

In instances where code anomalies occur, execution is consequently precluded. Our approach necessitates that erroneous code lines are executed within at least one failing test case, as lines that are never executed render diagnosis impossible. Therefore, inadequate test suite coverage can lead to undetected issues, known as false negatives. To partially address this, we incorporate multiple failing tests and rapid static analysis. Bounded model checking (BMC) employs constrained program versions, which inherently limit analysis to predefined bounds. Loop constructs and depth constraints may exclude feasible behaviors, thus obstructing potential valid diagnoses due to these limitations. Furthermore, premature iteration termination imposes constraints that omit scenarios relevant to complete execution, and diagnoses applicable to the truncated version may not generalize to the full program. These bounds are conservatively established and reported alongside results, acknowledging incompleteness due to bounding as an inherent limitation. For identifying pointers, arrays, and undefined behavior, CFAULTS utilizes static analysis tools such as CPPCHECK and CLANG-TIDY to detect uninitialized variables and runtime errors, including division by zero, as discussed in Section 5.1. Upon detection, CFAULTS halts operations immediately and reports the findings to the user. Furthermore, CBMC's memory-safety checks, such as detecting out-of-bounds accesses and unsafe pointer arithmetic, are employed to identify invalid memory operations. CFAULTS adheres to CBMC's standards for ANSI C compliance, ensuring that code accepted by CBMC is similarly regarded as correct, while issues unflagged by CBMC may remain unnoticed. Future efforts will focus on refining CBMC's configuration to more accurately align with specified C rules. To assess external validity, the evaluation was conducted on benchmark programs and student submissions that compiled successfully and included well-defined specifications, such as assertions or expected outputs. Extending this analysis to larger software systems, especially those with significant input/output operations, concurrent executions, or complex library dependencies, or to specifications articulated in more complex manners, may require additional effort and alter the outcomes observed in this study.

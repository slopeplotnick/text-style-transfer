Large-scale language models exhibit remarkable proficiency across a diverse range of tasks, effectively showcasing their adaptability and broad-spectrum capabilities. Instructional methodologies, therefore, enable the training of models to perform specific tasks through the provision of directives and illustrative examples. However, the manner in which directives are communicated significantly influences the effectiveness of their execution. This variation creates discrepancies in outcomes on mathematical tasks where the distinction between similar instructions is pivotal. Hence, ensuring that instructions are articulated with precision and constructed effectively is akin to the optimization of tuning parameters in machine learning settings, which enhances system performance. Nevertheless, manually creating effective instructions can be challenging and is not always consistent or dependable. Consequently, this approach ensures that automatic prompt optimization is employed to identify the most effective instructions, analogous to how AutoML automates the determination of optimal configurations. Multi-agent systems, moreover, have garnered substantial attention in both industrial applications and academic research, underscoring their significance in contemporary technological landscapes. To address the challenges of model opacity, automatic prompt optimization emerges as a cost-effective alternative, particularly suitable for models lacking transparency, where altering entire architectures is prohibitively expensive and data-intensive. A variety of prompt optimizers are accessible; however, their effective utilization can present considerable challenges. Each optimizer occupies a distinct niche within the online landscape, thereby requiring engagement with unique codebases and specific implementation prerequisites when exploring alternatives. A fundamental discrepancy exists between the maintenance of these locations and the adequacy of their code testing and documentation. Moreover, they lack adaptability, and employing them for tasks beyond straightforward applications necessitates considerable effort. Several tools exist to address these challenges; however, they are often proprietary and closed-source, limited to a single optimization method, or overly intricate for practical application.

This study investigates the integration of robotic systems within the construction sector, emphasizing the necessity for equitable deployment and utilization to achieve sustained operational effectiveness. To validate its performance, the authors conduct a systematic evaluation of the system against established algorithmic and learning-based methods through comprehensive testing. Although the study draws upon concepts of robot modeling and construction guidelines to juxtapose LLM-based reasoning with conventional task assignment methodologies in construction robotics, the specific mechanisms through which these reasoning approaches are assessed have not yet been explored. Nevertheless, the LTAA system incorporates models that understand the capabilities of each robot, adapts strategies according to distinct phases, and employs LLM decision-making to tackle specific construction coordination challenges identified in previous research. The system is rigorously compared against traditional approaches, such as Brute Force and Greedy Method, as well as learning-based techniques, including Q-learning and Deep Q-Network, to benchmark its performance and affirm its efficacy. Consequently, the proposed framework establishes a robust coordination system for LLMs, demonstrating how reasoning-driven approaches perform in comparison to traditional methods for automating construction processes. The manuscript is structured as follows: Section 2 critically examines literature on multi-robot task allocation, with a focus on optimization, learning, and LLM methodologies. Section 3 delineates the LTAA framework methodology, while Section 4 outlines the design and implementation of the experimental methodology. Section 5 presents a comparative analysis of the findings with previous methodologies, and Section 6 concludes with key insights and prospective directions. The foundational theory of MRTA guides the equitable distribution of tasks among robotic teams, considering constraints related to resources, temporal demands, and the varied capabilities of individual robots. Gerkey and Matarić (2004) introduced a seminal framework for analyzing task allocation challenges, emphasizing considerations such as whether robots are assigned single or multiple tasks, whether tasks necessitate individual or collective robotic effort, and whether tasks are characterized by immediacy or extended duration. However, the proposed system reveals notable limitations in addressing the comprehensive requirements of real-world applications, particularly when accounting for variability in robotic capabilities and temporal constraints. In 2013, Korsah and colleagues developed an enhanced system incorporating diverse robotic capabilities, task interconnections, and dynamic constraints, illustrating the progression towards employing heterogeneous teams for complex operations.

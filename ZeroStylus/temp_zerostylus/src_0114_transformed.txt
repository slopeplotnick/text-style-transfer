To illustrate the capabilities of PEFT-FACTORY in calculating classification and efficiency metrics during predictions, we also assess token accuracy and semantic similarity metrics such as Rouge and Bleu. Moreover, the approach employs standard metrics for classification assessment, specifically Accuracy and the F1 score. To assess efficiency in the evaluation of PEFT methods, the PSCP metric is employed, which offers flexibility by considering parameters such as the number of parameters, memory usage, and prediction latency. These metrics for supervised fine-tuning, as established in LLaMA-Factory, are detailed in the train/sft/metric.py file, with distinct data classes designated for each metric, namely ComputeAccuracy, ComputeF1, and ComputePSCP. In addition, the training configuration includes an option labeled compute_classification.metrics, which can be activated or deactivated to incorporate classification metrics. Furthermore, there exists an additional parameter for efficiency metrics, termed compute_pscp. Efficiency metrics are discussed in detail in Appendix B.3. To illustrate the flexibility and user-friendly design of PEFT-FACTORY, we highlight its utility as a valuable tool for conducting research on PEFT methods. This demonstrates how it facilitates reproducibility and benchmarking through the provision of two illustrative examples. Additionally, to illustrate how the modular design of PEFT-FACTORY facilitates the development and evaluation of novel PEFT methods, we highlight its ability to streamline creation and testing processes. However, challenges with current PEFT methods are observed, as newly developed methods frequently exhibit issues with code functionality and reproducibility. Consequently, this leads to a challenge in performance, as developers are frequently required to independently construct training and evaluation code, which tends to be repetitive, introduces errors, and results in inconsistencies.

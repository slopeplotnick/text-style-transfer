Ensuring consistent application of theme colors across all slides, standardizing layouts and formats for related slides, and translating slide content while preserving charts and structural integrity are central challenges in PowerPoint (PPT) editing. To address these challenges, we present **PPTArena**, the first tool, to our knowledge, that conceptualizes PPT editing as a systematic, step-by-step process grounded in the semantic understanding of the slide deck. PPTArena provides detailed information for each slide element, establishes style objectives, incorporates error-checking mechanisms to ensure accurate edits, and employs both instructional guidance and visual validation to facilitate flexible yet precise editing workflows.  Complementing PPTArena, we have developed **PPTPilot**, an advanced editing tool designed to handle detailed modifications in PowerPoint presentations. PPTPilot decomposes complex editing tasks into smaller, manageable steps and dynamically selects between programmatic approaches, such as leveraging APIs (e.g., `python-pptx`) or direct XML file editing. It subsequently verifies the results against predefined task objectives. The functionality of PPTPilot is underpinned by two key principles: (1) **structural planning**, in which the tool analyzes slide templates, placeholders, shapes, text, and visual elements prior to initiating edits, and (2) **precise execution**, whereby XML edits and explicit rule sets govern fonts, colors, object positioning, and template modifications. Repetitive tasks, such as translations and style normalization, are automated to enhance efficiency. Through iterative cycles of planning, editing, and validation—including XML and visual checks—the tool demonstrates an improved capacity for handling complex and extended editing tasks.  To evaluate the efficacy of PPTArena, we tested various tools, including state-of-the-art systems such as ChatGPT Agent, MiniMax Agent, visual-language models, and simplified versions of PPTPilot. Despite access to detailed instructions and robust tool support, existing systems frequently struggle with maintaining the accuracy of instructions and producing visually coherent edits, particularly in multi-step tasks that require managing dependencies across slides or enforcing template-level style consistency. PPTPilot, however, exhibits superior performance, outperforming advanced tools and models by over 10 percentage points on complex editing tasks, while also maintaining strong results on simpler tasks. Nevertheless, both PPTPilot and other tools encounter significant challenges in performing highly complex, visually dependent tasks, highlighting the inherent difficulty of the PPTArena benchmark and the substantial opportunities for future advancements in this domain.

In the analysis of advanced language models such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o, these systems demonstrate a commendable level of accuracy, often paralleling or surpassing human performance, as evidenced on the left side of Figure 8. With the exception of Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, Figure 8 indicates a general trend where models with higher accuracy exhibit behavior more consistent with Bayesian principles. Nonetheless, the correlation between predictive accuracy and Bayesian behavior is not absolute. A model capable of perfect prediction might resemble a simplistic framework devoid of probabilistic characteristics. The introduction of varied contextual information, exemplified by a range of past observations, significantly alters the operational dynamics of large language models (LLMs). This is particularly evident in the reduction of sequential processing efficacy, a phenomenon depicted in the top right of Figure 6, especially when the input data is skewed, as illustrated on the left of Figure 6, resulting in increased error rates (notably, triangles frequently surpass circles). These observations suggest that LLMs are highly contingent upon the quality and nature of the input data provided to them.  Further analysis, centered around the middle of Figure 8, reveals that high performance on metrics such as Normalized Root Mean Square Error (NRMSE) does not inherently translate into proficient cue integration. For instance, while GPT-5 Mini excels in NRMSE performance, its ability to integrate diverse cues, particularly in the maze distance task, appears limited. Although it effectively processes textual information, its performance with visual data is suboptimal, indicating a need to further diminish the influence of image inputs, a deficiency elaborated upon in Appendix A.12. Conversely, in tasks involving line length ratio, as depicted in the bottom right of Figure 6, numerous LLMs demonstrate the capability to downplay image significance amidst noise, thereby displaying adaptability akin to Bayesian reasoning.

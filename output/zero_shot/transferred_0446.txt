The interviews sought to explore the following questions: (1) Which aspects of your thought process are difficult to articulate when engaging in creative tasks? (2) Which of the four main components of the system most significantly supports your creative process? (3) What challenges do you encounter, and how do you address them? (4) Do you generate new ideas during the creative process? The responses provided by participants (P1–P4) highlighted preferences and challenges associated with the creative tool under examination. Participants expressed a greater preference for the drag-and-drop card-based story construction interface compared to text-based outlines, citing the former as more enjoyable. However, certain participants noted difficulties with repeatedly editing card details (e.g., P1, P4).   Participant P1 described their creative approach, stating, "When I make a story, I first think about the big picture, so I start with event cards and entity cards to set up the story. Then, I add details to the event and entity cards." In contrast, P4 emphasized challenges with frequent modifications, explaining, "I had to make changes several times while creating. If editing the details was easier, I'd use this method more." Additionally, some participants suggested that story details could be better communicated using interactive functionalities (e.g., P2, P3). For instance, P2 proposed organizing entity cards related to the same event on top of the relevant event cards, noting, "This way, I can easily see which entities are part of the event." Similarly, P3 recommended the introduction of relationship cards to link various entity cards and the use of connecting lines between event cards to better represent the chronological order and flow of the narrative.  These insights informed the identification of key interaction patterns (see Figure 2) and the derivation of three primary design goals (DGs) for improving the creative system:  - **DG1**: Enable users to input detailed information for each primary narrative component, thereby facilitating the creation of an initial story outline.   - **DG2**: Provide features that allow users to comprehend the overarching narrative structure, particularly the relationships between entities and events.   - **DG3**: Incorporate mechanisms for delivering feedback and actionable recommendations to enhance the overall story framework.

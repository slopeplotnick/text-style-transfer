The framework presented categorizes editorial tasks into five primary categories, further subdivided into 16 specific types of edits. These encompass a range of operations, from basic text formatting to intricate editing workflows. Unlike approaches that prioritize numerous simple cases, this framework emphasizes fewer yet highly detailed scenarios, following the methodology outlined by Maia Polo et al. These scenarios include operations such as text replacement and addressing complex editing problems requiring the integration of both textual and visual information. For instance, one specific case involves aligning images with corresponding captions across multiple slides, requiring users to utilize both visual and textual cues for accurate matching. Due to overlapping characteristics among certain tasks, the total number of editing categories exceeds 100.  The benchmark "PPTArena" distinguishes itself from existing tools by prioritizing the complexity of editorial tasks across four dimensions: the number of procedural steps required, interdependencies among slides, the level of semantic understanding necessary, and the degree of advanced planning involved. This contrasts with benchmarks such as PPTC-R and T2US, which more commonly address simple, template-based edits. Designed to accommodate challenging, realistic scenarios, PPTArena effectively highlights limitations in current systems. It employs both human-created and Python-generated slide decks, provides explicit ground truths for evaluation, supports cross-platform functionality, and incorporates accessibility considerations. An illustrative example of a task involves associating each image with its appropriate caption on a slide, ensuring spatial balance, updating slide titles, and standardizing image dimensions to 3.2 inches by 2.4 inches. Such tasks aim to evaluate system capabilities in multi-step reasoning, cross-slide information integration, and strategic planning.   Evaluation within PPTArena is based on two primary criteria: adherence to task instructions and the quality of visual outputs. Expert judges grade these aspects on a scale ranging from 0 (complete failure) to 5 (perfect execution). This rigorous evaluation framework is specially designed to uncover challenges that simpler tasks might overlook, offering a robust mechanism for assessing advanced system performance.

CAPO is an innovative framework that leverages a dynamic signal known as "advantage," aligning with the progressive enhancement of the model over time. Historically, curriculum learning strategies, initiated by Bengio et al. in 2009, predominantly adhered to static rules, such as sequencing tasks from simple to complex. Subsequent methodologies endeavored to introduce adaptability through mechanisms like expert feedback or performance metrics, yet these remained reliant on external criteria and were not directly correlated with the intrinsic improvement of the model. Consequently, CAPO was developed to employ advantage estimates as an intrinsic signal guiding the scheduling of tasks for the model. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that CAPO enhances models' proficiency in solving mathematical problems across various advantage-based reinforcement learning techniques, including GRPO, PPO, RLOO, and Reinforce++. Additionally, it is effective in tasks involving graphical user interfaces (GUIs), indicating its capability to accommodate diverse reasoning tasks.  In summary, the principal contributions of this study are the utilization of advantage as a dynamic signal and the development of CAPO, which comprises two adaptive phases—imitation and discrimination—responsive to the model's advancement. CAPO integrates seamlessly with diverse reinforcement learning methodologies and is applicable to tasks encompassing multiple data types, such as GUIs. As illustrated in Figure 2, CAPO operates by sampling outputs generated by the policy model, computing advantage estimates via various algorithms, and consolidating these within a unified framework. By employing these signals, CAPO structures the training process into an initial imitation phase, ensuring stability, followed by a discrimination phase, which enhances outcomes through the incorporation of more challenging samples.

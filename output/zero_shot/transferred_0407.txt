Flow-based diffusion models have emerged as a prominent approach for training methodologies aimed at generating images and videos. However, the mechanisms by which these models retain and generalize information remain inadequately understood. This study revisits the flow matching (FM) objective and examines its marginal velocity field, which can be precisely calculated, allowing for the determination of the exact FM target. Analysis of this velocity field reveals that these models inherently employ a bifurcated training objective: initially, they focus on an amalgamation of data types, subsequently narrowing their focus to the nearest data instance. This dual-phase objective engenders distinct learning dynamics: the initial phase facilitates the model's comprehension of diverse data types to form generalized patterns, while the latter phase emphasizes the retention of intricate details. By leveraging this understanding, we elucidate the efficacy of certain methodologies, such as temporal step adjustments, classifier-free guidance, and strategic design choices in latent space configuration. This investigation enhances comprehension of the training mechanisms underlying diffusion models and proposes avenues for future advancements in design and algorithmic strategies.  Diffusion models have established themselves as a robust category of generative techniques, capable of producing high-quality samples across various domains, including images and videos. These models acquire complex data representations by progressively transforming an initial distribution, such as a Gaussian distribution, into the target data distribution through a denoising or transport direction process. Various paradigms have been proposed to characterize this diffusion process, encompassing probabilistic models, score-based ordinary/ stochastic differential equations (ODEs/SDEs), and flow matching. These paradigms give rise to distinct training objectives, such as noise, score, or velocity prediction, and correspond to different formulations of the probability flow ODE. Recently, the flow matching method has gained widespread acceptance, wherein the model is trained to predict the velocity field through a straightforward schedule. Due to its simplicity and stable training characteristics, the flow matching method has become the prevailing standard for training leading diffusion models.

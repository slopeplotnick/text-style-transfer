We have developed a methodology termed Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), which serves as a reusable tool for facilitating physics-based character animation. This approach leverages score-matching techniques, enabling the training of control policies applicable to various tasks. SMP ensures that the resultant movements appear natural and align closely with the original training data. Notably, the utilization of SMP does not necessitate retaining the original motion data. Techniques such as classifier-free guidance can be employed to modify the motion prior, thereby allowing agents to execute tasks in specified styles. Our methodology underwent rigorous evaluation across a diverse array of scenarios, including single-character movements and interactions between humans and objects. SMPs can be constructed from a wide range of datasets, varying from brief motion clips to extensive datasets comprising up to 20 hours of motion data. Empirical tests demonstrated that integrating reinforcement learning with score distillation sampling (SDS) can generate movements comparable in quality to those produced via adversarial imitation learning, without the requirement for continuous updates to the prior during policy training. Furthermore, SMP generally exhibits superior performance due to its utilization of a more stable reward system compared to adversarial models. However, similar to other methods concentrated on specific modes, policies derived from SMP are occasionally limited to a narrow range of behaviors inherent in the original data. Although our primary focus was on humanoid motion control, we posit that SMP possesses potential applicability for broader control challenges. We anticipate that this innovation will inspire the development of novel, general-purpose motion tools to enhance the flexibility of controllers in animation and robotics, extending beyond mere motion tracking.

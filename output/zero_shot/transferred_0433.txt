In contemporary research, a novel metric known as Verifier Gain has been introduced to assess the utility of verifiers beyond mere accuracy. Traditional measures of accuracy inadequately capture the full impact of verifiers. Verifier Gain quantifies the enhancement achieved by implementing a verifier during testing procedures. Our investigation focused on the role of verifiers in the rejection of incorrect responses, revealing that the concept aligns with practical applications.   The study further evaluated the efficacy of different verification methodologies: self-verification, intra-group verification, and inter-group verification. Findings indicate that inter-group verification typically yields superior results, particularly when contrasted with self-verification. This superiority arises from the diminished effectiveness of verifiers when the verifier and solver exhibit substantial similarity. As models advance through increased size, enhanced training, or heightened accuracy, their self-verification capabilities diminish, whereas their efficacy in verifying outputs from other groups improves.  Additionally, we examined the ease of verification across various tasks. Tasks characterized by simplicity in solution are generally straightforward to verify, yet self-verification offers limited benefit across diverse tasks. Tasks requiring mathematical or logical reasoning tend to enhance verifier performance. Verifiers operate via two primary mechanisms: validating the correctness of the final answer and evaluating the procedural steps leading to that answer.  Moreover, some researchers have developed methodologies enabling models to self-verify by predicting question components based on the provided solution. To minimize errors, certain models employ self-directed fact-checking queries. Other researchers have designed verifiers that either corroborate final answers or collaborate directly with solvers. Additionally, efforts have been made to assess the accuracy of the procedural steps involved in problem-solving.

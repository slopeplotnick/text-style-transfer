Current video model evaluations predominantly concentrate on assessing fundamental physical knowledge, emphasizing everyday scenarios that require a rudimentary understanding of physical principles. In response to this limitation, we introduce **VideoScience-Bench**, a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate the capacity of video models to comprehend and reason through scientific concepts. The benchmark encompasses a wide array of topics in physics and chemistry, spanning 103 distinct concepts that necessitate college-level understanding. VideoScience-Bench is specifically constructed to assess whether these video models can independently analyze scientific experimental setups and predict outcomes without external guidance. The evaluation framework focuses on five key aspects: consistency with the given prompt, accuracy of outcome prediction, handling of motion dynamics, adherence to invariant scientific principles, and overall logical coherence.  Additionally, we developed a complementary evaluation system, **VideoScience-Judge**, which employs a checklist-based methodology to quantify model performance. This system generates task-specific checklists, identifies significant frames within the video, and pinpoints critical moments of interaction or motion. By synthesizing these elements, VideoScience-Judge produces a comprehensive score that reflects the model's capacity for scientific reasoning. Notably, the scoring outcomes from VideoScience-Judge exhibit a high degree of alignment with expert human evaluations, outperforming alternative benchmarks that primarily assess basic physical reasoning or video realism.  This study evaluates the ability of video models to simultaneously handle diverse and complex scientific concepts through three primary contributions:   1. **Development of VideoScience-Bench**, a robust benchmark designed to rigorously assess the scientific reasoning capabilities of video models in real-world contexts, covering 103 topics in physics and chemistry without requiring external input.   2. **Creation of VideoScience-Judge**, an automated evaluation framework that utilizes checklists, frame selection, and computational analysis of motion and object interactions to assign scores for scientific validity and visual fidelity.   3. **Comprehensive empirical analysis** leveraging VideoScience-Bench, which reveals significant challenges for current state-of-the-art video models. While these models excel at generating visually realistic and temporally smooth videos, they struggle to reason about complex scientific phenomena and frequently violate fundamental physical principles. Among the models tested, **Sora-2** and **Veo-3** demonstrated the strongest performance, showcasing preliminary abilities in scientific reasoning.  In summary, this work highlights the limitations of existing video models in reasoning through complex scientific ideas while providing novel tools and insights to advance research in this domain.

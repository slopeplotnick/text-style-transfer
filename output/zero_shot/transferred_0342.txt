The study revealed that the model Menta demonstrates superior efficacy in identifying symptoms of depression and stress, rather than detecting suicide risk. Both fine-tuned and standard language models, including those with larger architectures, exhibit enhanced performance in tasks associated with depression and stress compared to suicide risk assessment. For instance, Bauer (2024) posited that language models frequently encounter difficulties in interpreting subtle indicators of suicidality due to the limited availability of relevant data and their reliance on contextual or temporal factors, which may not be discernible in isolated posts. Furthermore, Lamichhane (2023) reported that models achieved an F1-score of 0.86 in the detection of depression, in contrast to a score of 0.37 in suicidality detection when utilizing social media data. This disparity underscores the relative ease with which depression and stress can be identified, attributed to more distinct patterns and abundant data, whereas suicidality is less prevalent and heavily contingent on context, posing challenges for model interpretation. Consequently, language models may be more adept at general screening tasks, such as identifying depression or stress, but may not be as effective in detailed suicide risk detection unless they incorporate diverse data sources or monitor changes over time.  Several limitations of the current study must be acknowledged. Although models like Qwen-3 (4B) exhibit proficiency in predicting mental health concerns, certain factors constrain the applicability of these findings to broader contexts. The analysis was confined to social media posts, excluding potentially informative elements such as temporal variations in writing style, user profile information, and online interaction patterns, which could enhance the comprehension of mental health phenomena. The incorporation of these aspects would likely render future research more robust and applicable. Moreover, the study's focus was restricted to depression, stress, and suicidal ideation, which represent only a subset of mental health issues. Comprehensive mental health encompasses conditions such as anxiety, bipolar disorder, and emotional disturbances, which were not addressed in this investigation. Additionally, the utilization of public, anonymous social media data rather than clinical datasets ensures privacy and replicability but limits consideration of safety, ethical considerations, and the long-term reliability of findings in real-world healthcare contexts.

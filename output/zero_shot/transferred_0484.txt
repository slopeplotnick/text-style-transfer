In this study, we evaluated several methodologies, including RL-Trained, Mixture-of-Inputs, CoLaR, and LiteReason, to assess their performance. The Qwen 2.5 7B-Instruct model was utilized to generate chapters based on plans derived from each method, followed by a comparative analysis where participants selected between pairs of results. This analysis was conducted using a specific model to determine the relative strengths of each method, with detailed information available in Appendix E.  Initially, we examined the effectiveness of these methodologies on two tasks: Flawed Fictions and NCP. The findings are presented in Table 4. Furthermore, we contrasted LiteReason with two baseline models, both utilizing the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct framework—one implemented through straightforward prompting and the other through reinforcement learning (RL) training. Notably, neither baseline incorporated advanced reasoning features. Interestingly, LiteReason, devoid of reasoning prompts (RP) and omitting reasoning steps, extended the reasoning process’ duration and underperformed relative to the RL-trained baseline. Additional comparisons are illustrated in Table 7, depicting the impacts of employing or omitting implicit-thought prompts across various models.  The study also investigated the frequency of "latent thoughts" during training to ascertain the influence of RL training on their prevalence. Initially, latent thoughts were more frequent at the commencement of RL training, but their occurrence diminished over time. Future investigations could focus on promoting latent reasoning through prompt modifications or increasing the likelihood of employing specific markers.  Overall, LiteReason yielded a marginal performance improvement (0.8% in Flawed Fictions and 58% in NCP), likely due to its functional similarity to the RL-trained baseline, albeit with exploration of reasoning paths not typically traversed by the base model. The training-free methodologies, such as Mixture-of-Inputs (MoI) and Soft Thinking, exhibited performance comparable to the untrained model. In the NCP task, Soft Thinking slightly outperformed the baseline, albeit utilizing more tokens, whereas MoI underperformed with a reduced token usage. In the Flawed Fictions task, the deviations from the baseline were minimal and within the statistical margin of error. Therefore, further research is necessary to enhance the efficacy and efficiency of training-free methods across diverse tasks and reasoning frameworks.

**Case Study 1: Assessment of Depression Levels:**   In this instance, a Reddit post labeled as 'Minimum' exhibits a user expressing concern about an inability to cease a habitual action involving their tongue (as illustrated in Figure 10). The user is apprehensive and seeks assistance from the online community, indicating a willingness to disclose additional information despite experiencing discomfort. The Menta model accurately categorizes this post as 'Minimum' and provides a rationale for this classification. The model identifies the user's composed manner of discussing a physical condition and the absence of intense or emotive language, which correspond to a low level of mental distress. This suggests that not all discomfort equates to depression. Conversely, the Qwen-3 model erroneously classifies the post as 'Severe', interpreting the pronounced physical sensations and discomfort as indicative of significant mental distress. This misinterpretation arises from conflating physical pain with anxiety and mental distress, resulting in an incorrect prediction. Such an error occurs due to an overemphasis on overt signs of distress without differentiating between physical complaints and indicators of depression. Through advanced training, the Menta model has developed the capability to discern depression-specific symptoms and align its predictions with established criteria within the dataset. This example underscores Menta's proficiency in distinguishing routine discomfort from genuine depression, demonstrating its enhanced accuracy in predicting mental health conditions.  **Case Study 2: Evaluation of Stress Levels:**  In the second case, a Reddit post labeled as 'No stress' involves a user deliberating a housing decision with a friend. The user articulates their thoughts calmly, logically enumerates options, and does not exhibit panic or excessive pressure. The Menta model accurately identifies this post as 'No stress' and justifies its assessment, emphasizing the user's composed demeanor and maturity in addressing a complex decision. The absence of impulsive or anxious communication is appropriately interpreted as indicative of low stress levels. In contrast, the Qwen-3 model incorrectly categorizes the post as 'Stressed', disproportionately focusing on phrases such as 'cold feet' and '99% sure', and mistakenly perceives careful deliberation as indicative of internal turmoil. It erroneously suggests that the user is experiencing stress, emotional discomfort, and indecision, markers of mental distress that a proficient model should discern. However, the model's focus on external relational dynamics, social tension, or potential relational mismatches—often associated with conflict avoidance or latent distress (highlighted in red in Figure 11)—results in a flawed prediction. Overall, this example highlights the Menta model's adeptness in accurately assessing mental health status through rigorous training.

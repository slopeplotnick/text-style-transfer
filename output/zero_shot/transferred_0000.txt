The Long-Tail Hard subset comprises challenging examples designed to evaluate the ability of advanced language models to address complex tasks effectively. These examples were carefully curated to include intricate and difficult cases, categorized into two primary types: explicit requests, such as those containing multiple attributes or multi-requirements, and implicit or indirect requests. The latter were further subdivided into four specific categories: instances involving negation, cases presenting alternatives, examples demanding domain-specific knowledge, and those requiring reasoning. Various methodologies were employed to identify these examples. For tasks involving negation and alternatives, keywords and linguistic patterns were utilized to locate the relevant instances. For those requiring specific knowledge and reasoning—typically less prevalent—a filtration process was conducted to exclude simpler examples, followed by validation using a high-performing language model (Qwen3-235B). This model assessed whether consistent responses were achieved across four out of eight iterations to confirm the complexity of these instances. Multi-attribute requests were identified using a specialized tool designed to detect queries with more than five attributes. To ensure the robustness and difficulty of these examples, a baseline language model was employed, retaining only the queries where the model failed to generate correct answers more than five times out of eight attempts. This procedure verified the subset's difficulty.  The Visual Salience subset focuses on cases where the primary product image plays a critical role in decision-making, specifically in scenarios where visual elements such as color or style are essential. Identification of these cases began with the application of keywords derived from a comprehensive visual category taxonomy. To minimize misclassification, examples were filtered using a blacklist, retaining only those in which the key visual feature was absent from the accompanying textual description of the product. Subsequently, a language model was utilized to validate that the identified keywords accurately represented salient visual attributes. Finally, to isolate the most challenging examples, a simple multimodal model incorporating both textual and visual inputs was employed. Examples were retained only if the model exhibited significant uncertainty in its responses, as indicated by inconsistent answers across the majority of its attempts.

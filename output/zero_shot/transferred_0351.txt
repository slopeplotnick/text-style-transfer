This study examines TACO, a method designed to adapt Vision-Language Agents (VLAs) during testing. TACO preserves the generalization capabilities of pre-trained VLAs while ensuring that their outputs are tailored to specific task requirements. A novel approach is introduced to estimate pseudo-counts within the context of Conditional Frequency Normalization (CFN), enabling efficient distributional shift measurements with minimal computational overhead. By leveraging TACO, diverse VLA models demonstrate improved performance in both simulated and real-world tasks without necessitating protracted training periods, thereby enabling rapid task adaptation.  In reinforcement learning (RL), there are two primary paradigms: online and offline RL. Online RL involves an agent interacting directly with its environment to explore and learn from various actions. In contrast, offline RL operates on a static dataset, precluding interaction with the environment. Offline RL presents unique challenges due to the disparity between the data-generating policy and the policy being learned. The offline dataset reflects the distribution of the data-generating policy, which may differ significantly from the target policy. These discrepancies contribute to substantial errors in value estimation, particularly for actions lying outside the observed distribution.  In the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage of VLAs, the model is trained on data encompassing a wide range of action modes. However, successful task-specific actions typically occupy a more constrained subset of this action space. Consequently, random sampling of actions under the policy may not yield the most optimal outcomes. To address this issue, TACO employs an anti-exploration strategy that restricts the policy to remain proximal to the distribution of successful task-specific data. Unlike conventional offline RL methods that rely on uncertainty-based penalties during optimization, TACO adopts a straightforward count-based regularization approach. This methodology aligns effectively with VLA models due to its simplicity and lack of dependence on value estimation. Count-based regularization has been previously explored in both traditional RL frameworks and modern reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF) algorithms, further demonstrating its utility in this context.

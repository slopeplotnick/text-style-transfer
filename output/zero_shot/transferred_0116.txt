In this study, we utilize Taobao search logs to categorize data into various industries, such as electronics and clothing. We employ stratified sampling based on the representation of each industry to ensure that our training dataset encapsulates features from all sectors. Subsequently, we classify the data into high, medium, and low-frequency categories, determined by the frequency of user searches, to ensure a comprehensive coverage of both common and rare search queries. The data is then subjected to expert labeling. During the data cleaning process, we identify inaccuracies primarily resulting from annotators overlooking details, particularly in lengthy queries, or from ambiguous labeling guidelines that fail to encompass all scenarios. To address these issues, we implement robust language models to filter the data. One model employs stringent attribute matching, excelling at detecting details in complex scenarios, while the other model applies comprehensive rules to guide reasoning and judgment, aiding in the identification and exclusion of cases not covered by the established guidelines. The use of dual models is justified by their respective strengths: one is adept at comprehending extended text, while the other excels in processing diverse data types. To enhance the model's reasoning capabilities, we adopt a tripartite process comprising knowledge and reasoning augmentation, data type comprehension, and rule awareness. Initially, user intents are deconstructed into attributes for item comparison. Subsequent stages evaluate the extent to which items satisfy search requirements, based on item details and established guidelines.

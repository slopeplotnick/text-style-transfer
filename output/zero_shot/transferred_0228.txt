In this study, we investigated methods for extracting grammatical features and subsequently conducted an analysis using a dataset of 10,000 noun phrases and sentences generated by the Delilah system. To assess the capability of the language learning process in distinguishing between different types of words and facilitating their categorization, the results are presented with graphical representations using linguistically conventional labels. For further evaluation, an additional experiment was performed using an expanded dataset comprising 10,000 noun phrases and sentences generated by Delilah. Table 5 provides a summary of the word distribution statistics for these datasets. Consistent with the findings from the initial test, Tables 6 and 7 list the 35 most frequently occurring words in each dataset. These high-frequency words predominantly consist of function words. Within the dataset of 10,000 noun phrases, the first prominent content word, "forgotten," appears at rank 54/55, sharing this position with the function word "at" or "near." Beyond rank 55, additional content words begin to emerge. In the dataset containing 10,000 sentences, the most frequent content words identified are "forgotten" (rank 11), "works" (rank 27), and "in the morning" (rank 66). Content words appear more frequently after rank 66.  The data also reveal that Delilah incorporates the term "masses" as a numerical descriptor, which the learning process accurately identifies. Across the dataset of 10,000 examples, distinguishing function words from content words becomes more straightforward, as words occurring more than 100 times—equivalent to a frequency threshold of 1.7 per thousand in Delilah's sentences—are typically function words. Based on these observations, we calibrated the MODOMA system to classify words occurring at a frequency of more than 2 per thousand as function words, with all others categorized as content words. This classification process is executed after processing at least 10,000 examples. To minimize errors, the calibration prioritizes reducing the likelihood of misclassifying a content word as a function word, as the default assumption categorizes any word as a content word unless there is compelling evidence to classify it otherwise.   The parameters established during this initial test have been adopted as the default settings for the MODOMA system. However, these settings remain adjustable and may be refined in response to future testing or alternative theoretical approaches to language learning.

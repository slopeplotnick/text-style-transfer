OpenHands demonstrates a heightened ability to address challenges, likely attributable to its system prompt containing more comprehensive instructions in comparison to SWE-agent. Advanced language models, such as Claude Sonnet 4, typically excel across various tasks due to their sophisticated information organization methodologies. These models accumulate extensive context and conduct supplementary evaluations to ensure operational success and prevent failures. The Devstral model, a smaller open-source model, similarly exhibits a complex information organization strategy when paired with OpenHands. This complexity likely stems from its training alongside OpenHands for software-related tasks, prompting it to engage in more thorough searches and validations prior to implementing changes. Additionally, an exploration is warranted into the correlation between process measurement metrics and the agent's problem-solving efficacy. It was observed that numerous premature terminations occurred due to runtime errors stemming from inadequate model responses, a recognized issue. The metrics of process measurement correlate with both the success of issue resolution and the perceived difficulty from a human perspective. Successful resolutions tend to manifest shorter, more concentrated pathways, whereas unsuccessful attempts typically involve prolonged procedures and redundant steps.  The complexity inherent in information organization is further influenced by the design of the agent and the foundational language models. Robust models foster a more densely structured organization, characterized by increased exploration activities. Beyond the binary outcomes of agent success or failure in problem resolution, the study extends to an analysis of agent behavior through Phase Flow Analysis. Successful resolutions often adhere to a structured strategy encompassing identification, resolution, and verification phases, aligning with the agents' intended functionalities. As task difficulty escalates, successful pathways may elongate yet remain coherent, while unsuccessful ones tend to exhibit phase repetition or disorganized transitions. Stronger language models demonstrate superior and more adaptable strategic approaches.

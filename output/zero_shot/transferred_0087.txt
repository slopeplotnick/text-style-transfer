A detailed examination of the results for each sample reveals that employing varying resolutions can, in some cases, yield superior outcomes compared to consistently setting the resolution at 112, as demonstrated in Figure 3. Analyzing the outcomes of cropping images at differing resolutions and evaluating the corresponding semantic similarity maps offers insights into the underlying cause of this phenomenon. Specifically, when objects within an image are divided into distinct segments, some portions exhibit higher semantic similarity—quantified using VisRAG—while others display lower similarity. Following the application of a filtering step, only the segments with higher similarity are retained. However, this process disrupts the holistic representation of the object, thereby complicating the ability of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to produce accurate judgments.   To address this issue, we propose a novel framework termed Multi-resolution Retrieval Detection (MRD). The primary objective of MRD is to leverage multiple resolutions across varying scales to more effectively identify regions containing target objects. This approach facilitates the identification of optimal image crops that correspond to the target objects, effectively reducing distractions and enhancing the interpretability of high-resolution images by MLLMs. As discussed in Subsection 3.2, the utilization of multiple resolutions to compute semantic similarity is particularly advantageous for analyzing objects of varying sizes and positional configurations within images. Building on these observations, we introduce a straightforward yet effective methodology known as Multi-resolution Semantic Fusion. This method computes semantic similarity maps at multiple resolutions on a localized scale and integrates the results to enhance the accuracy of semantic similarity assessments.  To further refine the localization of target objects, we incorporate an open-vocabulary object detection model that systematically scans the high-resolution image using a sliding window approach. This model generates confidence scores for regions potentially containing the target objects. By combining these confidence scores with the multi-resolution semantic similarity maps, the proposed method not only improves the precision of target region identification but also distinguishes subtle differences among cropped regions within those areas. Consequently, this approach facilitates the system's ability to emphasize the most relevant portions of the image.

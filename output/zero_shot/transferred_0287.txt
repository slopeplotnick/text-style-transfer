This section addresses the development and implementation of models designed to facilitate high-quality and seamless translation of speech and text across languages. It examines the functionality of voice-interfaced language models in processing and interpreting auditory signals and conversational input. Over the past decade, considerable advancements have been made in the creation and deployment of voice-activated AI assistants, starting with Apple’s Siri and expanding to include Microsoft’s Cortana, Google Assistant, Amazon's Alexa, and Samsung's Bixby. This segment of the tutorial provides an in-depth exploration of the foundational technologies underlying these AI systems, including mechanisms used to detect user intent through specialized neural networks, conversational management enabled by machine learning techniques, the incorporation of data from web searches, semantic web methodologies, and the utilization of knowledge graphs to enhance spoken language comprehension.   Current developments in AI assistants are largely focused on refining language models to improve conversational understanding and tracking, thereby enhancing the user experience with spoken language systems. This tutorial presents an overview of contemporary language models, detailing their adaptations for specific functional applications. It highlights advancements in systems capable of managing complex multi-turn conversations, both for general-purpose and domain-specific tasks, while reviewing datasets and associated evaluation methodologies utilized in this field. Furthermore, it discusses emerging research challenges, including the development of language models that address the growing demand for systems capable of handling multiple simultaneous conversations.

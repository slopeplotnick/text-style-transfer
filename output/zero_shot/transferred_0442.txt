Large language models frequently generate information that is inaccurate, albeit presented in a coherent and articulate manner, leading to factual errors that undermine user trust and hinder their practical application. To address this issue, researchers have developed two primary strategies: Hallucination Detection, which focuses on the model's internal processes, and Fact Verification, which emphasizes external validation of facts. Although both strategies share a common objective, they have evolved independently, with distinct underlying theories, datasets, and evaluation methodologies. This divergence has impeded progress in the field. In this study, we aim to integrate these approaches by introducing a framework called UniFact, which facilitates the direct comparison of hallucination detection and fact verification by assessing model outputs for factual accuracy. Through extensive testing of various models and methods, we have identified three key findings: (1) neither approach consistently outperforms the other; (2) each method detects different types of factual inaccuracies; and (3) a hybrid approach that combines both methods yields the most effective results. Beyond performing a comparative analysis, we have also investigated the reasons for their divergent development and underscored the necessity for their integration. Our findings underscore the need for a novel framework to unify Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification in large language models. These models have revolutionized the digital landscape by facilitating advanced searches and performing complex tasks that were previously deemed unfeasible. Nevertheless, their deployment in critical domains is constrained by a significant limitation: the generation of content that is both fluent and erroneous, known as hallucination. In contexts such as healthcare, law, and journalism, where factual accuracy is paramount, such errors can erode trust, disseminate misinformation, and inflict harm. Consequently, the development of robust mechanisms to identify and mitigate these errors is imperative for the creation of reliable intelligent systems.

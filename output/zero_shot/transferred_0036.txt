Negative samples encompass all potential active nodes. Section 5 elaborates on the necessity of incorporating both random and adversarial negative samples to achieve superior performance relative to the TextMatch model. Contrastive loss is employed to train the model to accurately identify the correct positive candidate for each query amidst all options within a given batch. The encoder utilized in this study is initialized from publicly available E5-unsupervised checkpoints, which are pre-trained using advanced contrastive techniques and consist of models with 33 million and 330 million parameters. Identical encoder weights are applied to both queries and candidates to ensure consistency.  Prior to fine-tuning, the model leverages dense interaction data comprising 110 million samples derived from weak signals, such as user interactions (e.g., clicks or saves) and naturally occurring text pairings, such as titles and descriptions. The fine-tuning process uses a smaller but higher-quality dataset, reflecting stronger interactions indicative of mutual interest (e.g., interviews or hires), supplemented by hard-negative samples extracted from interaction logs, which include items displayed but not selected.  The training dataset for the proposed GraphMatch framework is constructed using one year of user interaction data sourced from Upwork. This dataset is divided into 10 months for training, one month for validation, and one month for evaluation. It consists of three types of nodes, representing freelancers, clients, and job postings, alongside edges denoting various interaction types, such as job postings, applications, invitations, interviews, and contract commencements. Freelancer and job posting nodes are enriched with textual features, including profile overviews and job descriptions, as well as numerical features specific to their respective categories, such as freelancer hourly rates, client locations, and job post categories. These textual and numerical attributes exhibit temporal variability.  Overall, the dataset comprises approximately 9 million nodes, 32 million feature versions, and 62 million edges. The GraphMatch model is trained using robust labels derived from contract formations between freelancers and job postings, ensuring a high-quality training signal.

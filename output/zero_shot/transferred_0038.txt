At the conclusion of the study, participants were solicited to provide any additional reflections they possessed regarding AI-generated music, with the precise wording accessible in Supplementary Materials C. The investigation of personality traits was conducted utilizing a concise version of the Big Five Inventory (BFI-10), which is applicable in both English and German. The five personality traits examined included Openness. Additionally, demographic information, such as age, gender, nationality, and education level, was collected to enhance the comprehension of the participant cohort and assess the potential influence of these variables on the outcomes. Although the survey accommodated non-binary gender responses, all participants self-identified as either male or female; thus, gender was recorded accordingly. The participants' comprehension of the survey language was also evaluated to ensure their ability to discern subtle nuances in the wording related to emotional descriptions.  Data analysis was conducted using Python (version 3.9.20), employing the pandas library (version 2.2.2) for data organization, scipy (version 1.12.0) for statistical tests such as correlation and ANOVA, and statsmodels (version 0.14.4) for mixed-effects models. Graphical representations were produced in R (version 4.3.3) using the ggplot2 package. For the qualitative analysis of open-ended responses concerning AI-generated music, the thematic analysis approach by Braun and Clarke (2006) was employed, with MAXQDA (version 24.10) facilitating the analysis. Further validation of the data was performed by averaging individual song ratings, detailed in Appendix C. These validations confirmed participant preferences for different composers but did not reveal significant variations in the intensity of emotional responses.  To ascertain whether the experimental design influenced participants' music preferences or emotional intensity beyond individual differences and music type, researchers employed linear mixed-effects models. These models incorporated random effects for both participants and songs, while also considering personality traits, listening habits, and the music model employed.

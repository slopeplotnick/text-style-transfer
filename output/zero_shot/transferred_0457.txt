This section outlines the procedures employed to calculate each evaluation metric and interpret the results, which include assessing word overlap, semantic similarity, and information accuracy. The ROUGE-L metric measures the longest common subsequence of words between a model-generated summary and a reference summary. It prioritizes sentence structure over word order, making it a commonly used metric for comparing machine-generated summaries with human-authored counterparts. In this analysis, emphasis is not placed on the absolute ROUGE-L scores but rather on examining how these scores fluctuate based on variations in input order, thereby assessing the sensitivity of the metric to input sequencing.   As ROUGE-L is limited to assessing textual overlap, additional metrics such as BERTScore and SummaC are utilized to evaluate semantic similarity and factual accuracy, respectively. BERTScore leverages a pre-trained transformer model to compare contextualized semantic similarity between words, aligning each word in the generated summary with its most similar counterpart in the reference text. It then calculates precision, recall, and F1 scores based on these semantic matches. This method is particularly effective for capturing meaning, as it accounts for synonyms and rephrased expressions. Similar to the analysis of ROUGE-L, the focus in this study is on how BERTScore varies with changes in input order, rather than on the absolute score itself, which may inherently be lower given the abstract nature of the summaries.  To evaluate factual consistency, we employ SummaC, a metric designed to assess the alignment of the summary’s information with the original source documents. SummaC includes two variants: SummaC-ZS, which operates without training, and SummaC-Conv, a supervised model. For this study, the SummaC-Conv variant is applied due to its superior performance. This method is based on a RoBERTa model, which is constrained by input length limitations but is well-suited for assessing factual consistency.

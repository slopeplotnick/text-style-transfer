Checker-based methods and analogous approaches are effective in areas such as mathematics and programming but are inadequate for tasks lacking definitive answers. Certain methodologies enable the model or another artificial intelligence (AI) entity to serve as an adjudicator. This includes Self-Rewarding Language Models and Reinforcement Learning with AI Feedback (RLAIF), which utilize AI-generated feedback instead of human input, as well as Inverse Probability Operations (IPO) and Self-Rewarding Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which employ probabilistic assessments to determine preferences.   Additional research explores internal activations, exemplified by approaches such as Factoscope and INSIDE, demonstrating that hidden states can indicate the factuality or potential inaccuracy of information. Our approach aligns with this direction, employing a geometric measure of these activations as a reward mechanism to enhance model decision-making. The theoretical foundation of this concept is rooted in softmax bottleneck analysis. Yang et al. demonstrated that models employing softmax are constrained by the rank of their hidden layers. Godey extended this framework to contemporary AI, revealing that probabilities in natural language are characterized by high rank.   Further studies have shown that when embeddings become excessively narrow during the training phase, the quality of the generated output deteriorates. This body of research underscores the correlation between the complexity of representations and the quality of model outputs. Empirical evidence supports this, such as the work of Garrido, which illustrates that the rank of learned representations can predict performance independently of labeled data. In language models, hidden states encapsulate significant semantic information: word embeddings reveal syntactic and semantic patterns, and intermediate layers can ascertain the factuality of content.   This evidence substantiates that the quality of a model's output produces discernible, measurable patterns. Our research advances these concepts by not only analyzing rank post-training but actively incorporating it as a dynamic reward signal to enhance model performance during the learning phase, thereby transforming this geometric insight into a practical methodology for model optimization.

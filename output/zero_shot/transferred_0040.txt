Most traditional systems store information in the form of discrete documents or isolated facts. However, forecasting financial trends necessitates the sequential storage of events to better comprehend the temporal interdependencies among them. In stock prediction, it is imperative to consider analogous historical scenarios, as relying solely on individual documents for semantic meaning proves insufficient. Conventional systems tend to disregard information based on temporal decay or simplistic human memory models, whereas financial prediction requires the retention of significant patterns over extended durations. This paper proposes an innovative approach to enhance memory efficacy in finance, aiming to construct a comprehensive and up-to-date repository of financial knowledge for language models.  The methodology employed entails the development of a system that transforms market information into structured memory units characterized by dates, event types, and associated entities such as corporations and industries. This system facilitates the integration of market information into organized sequences, which is instrumental in identifying and comprehending patterns. We devised a mechanism to discern similar event sequences, which surpasses traditional methods that solely analyze single documents for semantic content. By utilizing the current event sequence for searching, our system is capable of identifying intricate historical patterns that correspond to the prevailing market conditions. This transition from mere textual matching to pattern identification marks a significant advancement.  The efficacy of this system was evaluated, revealing superior performance in stock prediction compared to other models. Due to its capability to model event sequences, the system can elucidate the pivotal events influencing stock prices and their dynamic interactions, thereby rendering predictions more reliable and transparent.

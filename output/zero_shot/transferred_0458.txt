In the analysis of images, it is imperative to consider elements such as hidden joints and the broader context, including the interrelationships among joints, hands, and objects. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), particularly ResNet-50, are frequently employed for three-dimensional (3D) hand pose estimation. These CNNs utilize localized operations that strike a balance between accuracy and efficiency; however, they often struggle with comprehending the overall structure. Recent research has explored the application of Vision Transformers (ViT) for the reconstruction of hand meshes, though this approach can be computationally intensive and thus slow, highlighting a significant potential for enhancement in the domain of hand pose interpretation.  A novel model, Mamba, initially developed for language processing, is gaining attention due to its proficiency in emphasizing critical input aspects, which aids in comprehending the overall context from lengthy sequences. Recent adaptations of this model for image processing, such as VisionMamba, incorporate a specialized block for spatial sequence modeling, while VMamba introduces a block with diverse scanning paths. Nevertheless, these methods typically employ a fixed grid, which inadequately captures intricate hand movements in 3D space.  In response to these limitations, we have developed an innovative system termed Deformable Mamba (DF-Mamba), which employs a more adaptive methodology to enhance the extraction of visual features in 3D hand pose estimation. DF-Mamba operates by dynamically modeling both local and global features using adaptable state spaces. The system integrates blocks designed to collect local features along a flexible path, retaining crucial information for understanding the global context. The scanning path is modified through deformable point sampling, using local anchors and adjustable offsets derived from the input features.

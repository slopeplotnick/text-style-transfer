In evaluating the quality of summarization, it is essential to consider several criteria: lexical overlap, semantic similarity, and factual accuracy. Traditional metrics such as BLEU and ROUGE-L primarily focus on word-level comparison between the summary and the original text. However, these metrics often fall short in scenarios where paraphrasing occurs, as they do not adequately capture semantic equivalence. To address this limitation, BERTScore was developed, leveraging a pre-trained language model to assess semantic similarity rather than mere lexical resemblance. This approach enhances the evaluation of concise and semantically rich summaries.  In addition to semantic evaluation, ensuring that a summary maintains the factual integrity of the source material is crucial. Several methodologies have been introduced to assess this aspect. FactCC, for instance, employs a model to verify sentence-level alignment between the summary and the original text. However, it occasionally produces false negatives, erroneously identifying discrepancies where none exist. Another tool, BLANC, operates independently of a reference text by evaluating how well the summary aids a language model in predicting omitted portions of the original text. Nonetheless, BLANC's effectiveness is contingent upon the specific language model employed and may overly emphasize lexical rather than semantic congruity.  SummaC offers an alternative by employing Natural Language Inference (NLI) to evaluate factual consistency. This method examines each sentence in the summary to determine whether it is supported by the original text. Empirical evaluations suggest that SummaC generally outperforms older methodologies such as FactCC in maintaining factual accuracy.  In this study, we employ an integrative approach for summary evaluation, utilizing ROUGE-L to assess lexical overlap, BERTScore for semantic similarity, and SummaC to ensure factual accuracy.

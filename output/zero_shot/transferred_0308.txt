Our research indicates that individuals who are entrenched in their beliefs are more prone to errors in domains where correct answers are well-defined, such as predictive tasks. This suggests that the Martingale Score has the potential to evaluate the quality of reasoning, even in cases where complete information is unavailable. In the context of language models, assessing Bayesian rationality involves examining their ability to update beliefs based on evidence in accordance with Bayes's theorem. Gupta et al. (2025) demonstrated that language models are capable of updating their beliefs when exposed to extensive evidence, such as successive coin flips, even when their prior beliefs are biased. However, in more complex, real-world scenarios involving natural language, Qiu et al. (2025) found that language models fail to adjust their beliefs about user preferences in a manner consistent with Bayesian expectations. Similarly, Falck (2024) showed that language models do not consistently adhere to Bayesian principles when provided with contextual information. Furthermore, Zhao (2021) and Wang (2023) revealed that the sequence and structure of input examples significantly influence the responses of language models.  Several strategies have been proposed to enhance the Bayesian reasoning capabilities of language models. These include the emulation of ideal Bayesian updating, abstract reasoning, and the integration of abductive and deductive reasoning processes. However, the implementation of these approaches often relies on additional structures, such as Bayesian networks, which can complicate practical application. Additionally, language models exhibit a range of biases stemming from their training on extensive human-generated data. These biases differ from human biases when language models are used in evaluative or decision-making contexts, favoring specific positions, preferring lengthy responses, showing a preference for positive language, and displaying an undue level of confidence in their outputs.  Among these biases, *confirmation bias* warrants particular attention due to its significant societal implications, including its role in fostering polarization. Moreover, confirmation bias is considered a foundational bias from which many others emerge. For this reason, our study focuses on confirmation bias, employing the construct of *belief entrenchment* as a framework to investigate its manifestation in language models.

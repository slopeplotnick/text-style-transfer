Style-based Motion Policy (SMP) is a framework designed for training motion policies by incorporating reward systems grounded in style, eliminating the necessity for original motion capture data. This approach facilitates the application of diverse methods to modify the reward model, thereby enabling the adjustment of motion dynamics. However, certain modifications may result in movements that appear less natural. Comparative analyses of various methods are demonstrated through accompanying video examples.  A notable feature of SMP is its capacity to generate novel motion styles by adjusting the outputs of pretrained models, allowing for the creation of previously unavailable styles without necessitating retraining of the model. For instance, as illustrated in Figure 6, SMP enables the blending of two distinct styles, such as "AeroPlane" and "HighKnees," to produce a hybrid style. This is achieved by combining the model's predictions for each style. Furthermore, a single pretrained motion model can be leveraged to train policies tailored for various tasks, including steering, target localization, and dodgeball gameplay, as detailed in Table 2.  The motion model utilized within SMP is trained on a subset of the LaFAN1 dataset, which comprises random running sequences. The trained policies demonstrate both task effectiveness and visual naturalness. As depicted in Figures 5(a) and 5(b), the character transitions seamlessly between diverse movements to accomplish the task while maintaining realistic motion characteristics. For example, the steering policy ensures the character jogs backward when facing away from the intended direction and jogs forward once facing the correct direction. Additionally, it performs side-steps and cross-steps without requiring an independent motion planner. In the Target Localization task, the policy trained with SMP formulates human-like movement strategies, such as instinctively decelerating when necessary.

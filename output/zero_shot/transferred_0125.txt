Empirical studies have indicated that the incorporation of reasoning-based guardrails significantly enhances the reliability and interpretability of moderation processes, marking a substantial advancement towards the development of safer artificial intelligence (AI) systems.   In this context, the OMNI-GUARD series represents a suite of models designed to perform safety checks comprehensively across text, images, videos, and audio by leveraging reasoning-based approaches. A novel training methodology has also been developed to enable these models to articulate their safety-related decisions with greater clarity. Experimental evaluations reveal that OMNI-GUARD models achieve high levels of accuracy, demonstrate robust generalization across tasks, and provide improved explainability compared to prior models.  The emergence of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has catalyzed interest in omni-modal language models (OLLMs), which are capable of simultaneously processing and generating diverse data modalities. Unlike earlier models that employed multiple specialized components, OLLMs are trained on heterogeneous datasets, enabling them to seamlessly integrate and reason across text, audio, images, and videos. These models employ a unified representation to encode various types of input, facilitating cross-modal reasoning and synthesis. For instance, models such as Qwen2.5-Omni and LLaMA-Omni are capable of producing text and speech outputs in real-time, while NExT-OMNI extends functionality further by supporting the comprehension and generation of any data modality. Despite these advancements, current OLLMs face persistent challenges related to safety, primarily attributable to issues arising from parameter configuration, which can result in potentially hazardous outcomes.

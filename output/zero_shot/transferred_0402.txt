The observed variations in the PhysionetMI dataset can likely be attributed to its intrinsic complexity, encompassing diverse experimental setups, individual-specific brain activities, and signal discrepancies. A promising avenue involves employing ensemble methods such as bagging, boosting, and stacking, which integrate the strengths of both simple and complex models, potentially enhancing the reliability of results despite inter- and intra-individual variability. To optimize the efficacy and consistency of these approaches, further investigation is warranted. Notwithstanding existing challenges, the integration of quantum methods with advanced algorithms holds substantial promise for improving the analysis, generalizability, and adaptability of electroencephalogram (EEG) signal interpretation, representing a significant advancement towards more personalized and flexible brain-computer interfaces. Preliminary studies utilizing quantum computing are underway to evaluate the feasibility and potential improvements these innovations may offer in EEG signal decoding.  A critical issue with current methodologies is their heavy reliance on labeled data, which is both time-consuming and costly to produce in large quantities. Emerging studies advocate for the development of techniques capable of processing unlabeled data, such as unsupervised or semi-supervised learning, to alleviate the dependence on extensive labeled datasets. Additionally, transfer learning, which involves the application of knowledge acquired from one individual or dataset to another, remains insufficiently explored, despite its potential to enhance model performance and address inter-individual differences. Future research should prioritize the development and evaluation of transfer learning methodologies to address the challenges associated with EEG datasets.  Overall, our findings indicate that adopting customized EEG methodologies is imperative for effectively managing inter- and intra-individual variations. The future of personalized and functional brain-computer interfaces lies in the amalgamation of flexible strategies, sophisticated models, ensemble learning methods, and quantum-based innovations.

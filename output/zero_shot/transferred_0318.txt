The analysis presented in Table 3 demonstrates that incorporating both audio and visual elements in summary generation yields superior results compared to solely utilizing audio, across a majority of languages and models, despite the models exclusively processing text. This enhancement is most pronounced in English, German, and Italian, while it is less significant for Chinese. We attribute this disparity to the presence of English terms in languages utilizing the Latin alphabet, whereas the fundamental differences between English and Chinese impede the models' effective utilization of supplementary English information. In the context of question answering, the inclusion of images provides some benefit, albeit less substantial than in summary generation. Improvements are marginal and in four out of twelve language-model configurations, the performance actually deteriorates. We hypothesize that this occurs because the models receive enhanced textual information rather than direct image data, which proves insufficient for effective question answering. Over the past decade, there has been considerable academic interest in the field of streaming speech translation. Some applications, particularly those focused on lecture translation, predominantly rely on audio data. Our research distinguishes itself by integrating both visual and auditory elements from presentation slides and delivering translated audio and slides across multiple languages. Although the domain of image-to-image translation remains relatively unexplored, burgeoning interest is evident due to the availability of larger datasets. However, existing research primarily concentrates on translating textual content within images, rather than on reconstructing the visual integrity post-translation. An initial study in this sector investigates methodologies for preserving visual coherence. Our framework for image translation is designed with flexibility, enabling researchers to incorporate their own models at any stage, from text extraction within images to translation and image reconstruction, without necessitating the development of new components.

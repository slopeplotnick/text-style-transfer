In traditional motion tracking systems, motions are often monitored frame by frame. In contrast, distribution matching trains controllers to replicate the overarching behavioral patterns observed in a motion dataset. Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) employs a discriminator to differentiate between the motions produced by an agent and those contained within the dataset. Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) extend this concept, enabling controllers to generate realistic behaviors for novel tasks not represented within the original dataset. However, adversarial priors necessitate training with a distinct policy tailored to each new task, which requires both retraining and access to the dataset anew.   Our methodology achieves comparable outcomes to these approaches while utilizing a reusable motion prior that can be adapted for diverse styles absent in the initial dataset. Diffusion models have garnered considerable attention due to their exemplary performance across various domains, and have recently been applied to control tasks. These models are capable of generating high-quality motion, which lends itself to applications such as motion planning, where they predict future movements subsequently tracked by a controlling mechanism. Additionally, they have been employed to directly govern behaviors in tasks involving manipulation and character control, thus facilitating flexible and diverse actions.  In contrast to these methodologies that integrate diffusion models into the controller architecture, our approach employs pretrained diffusion models as overarching guides for behavior. Specifically, we utilize diffusion models as a reward function within the framework of reinforcement learning, thereby assisting policy learning through the technique of score distillation sampling.

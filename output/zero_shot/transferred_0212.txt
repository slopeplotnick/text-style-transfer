In this study, we introduce a novel approach to facilitate early termination in diffusion language models without necessitating additional training, termed SchED. SchED employs a scheduling mechanism, such as linear or cosine schedules, to ascertain the optimal stopping point based on the model's confidence levels, and it is compatible with various model architectures. By analyzing the temporal evolution of model confidence, we observe that instruction tuning enhances model stabilization, aligning with the predefined schedule and enabling early termination without compromising output quality. SchED achieves this by establishing a confidence threshold that adjusts progressively as the model generates text, leveraging the inherent strengths of diffusion models to ensure process efficiency without retraining requirements. The accompanying code is publicly accessible.  Diffusion language models present a contemporary alternative to traditional autoregressive models, capable of being trained ab initio or through modifications to pre-existing autoregressive frameworks. Notably, the LLaDA methodology trains the model to mask text in one directional sequence and rectify it in the reverse, demonstrating promising outcomes in both initial training and subsequent fine-tuning. Various strategies are employed to convert autoregressive models into diffusion counterparts to facilitate equitable comparisons across differing scales and tasks. Moreover, advancements such as Dream-7B emphasize refining model architectures and producing diverse versions.  Innovative designs, like LLaDA-MoE, incorporate sparse configurations to achieve competitive performance with reduced active components. Additionally, efforts to enhance diffusion models for the comprehension of extended texts have led to the development of UltraLLaDA, which employs the RoPE scaling technique to manage sequences of up to 128,000 tokens. The Seed Diffusion strategy specifically addresses the acceleration of code model execution. Empirical investigations into the scalability of these models indicate that diffusion models exhibit growth patterns comparable to autoregressive models. Reviews of the field underscore the advantages of diffusion models, including parallel processing capabilities, gap-filling proficiency, and effective management of broader contextual information, while also outlining the unresolved research questions that persist.

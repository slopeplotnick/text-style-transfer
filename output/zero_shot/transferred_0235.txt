The system employs regular expressions (regex) to extract structured decisions from large language model (LLM) markdown responses. Within these responses, the system identifies specific sections, such as allocation decisions and success rates, transforming them into structured data for validation purposes. To ensure robustness, eight rules have been established to uphold the integrity of the extracted data. These include eliminating invalid allocations, evaluating trade-offs, assessing workload awareness, and verifying overall coherence. The shift from a purely rule-based validation framework to a weighted validation system was necessitated by the need to address enhanced reasoning and contextual understanding inherent to LLM tasks.  Empirical studies suggest that analyzing multiple dimensions of reasoning provides a more comprehensive evaluation of model performance than a binary assessment of correctness. Consequently, the new validation framework employs a multidimensional scoring system that evaluates reasoning across several key areas, including the clarity of explanations, consistency in success rate predictions, workload awareness, justification of trade-offs, adherence to procedural phases, and alignment between expressed confidence and reasoning. Each dimension contributes to an aggregate quality score, with relative weight assigned to areas based on their direct impact on outcomes. For instance, reasoning quality and success rate accuracy are assigned greater weight, reflecting their direct influence on performance, whereas confidence justification is weighted less heavily.  This weighted scoring approach promotes a validation mechanism that is both sensitive to performance nuances and cognizant of contextual factors, thereby balancing rigorous performance assessment with an understanding of complex reasoning processes. The scoring criteria and their respective weights are as follows:  1. **Explanation Quality (Weight: 0.20)**: The depth and clarity of the provided reasoning.   2. **Success Rate Accuracy (Weight: 0.20)**: The alignment between predicted success rates and actual outcomes.   3. **Trade-off Analysis (Weight: 0.15)**: The ability to consider and manage competing priorities effectively.   4. **Workload Awareness (Weight: 0.15)**: Consideration of task distribution and its implications.    This framework ensures a systematic and balanced evaluation of the model's decision-making capabilities, addressing both performance and contextual reasoning comprehensively.

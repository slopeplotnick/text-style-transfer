The quality of pre-training was assessed by evaluating the extent to which the token distributions in the generated samples matched those of the original pre-training dataset. Specifically, the frequency of each token type in the pre-training data was compared to its frequency in the generated samples across various training stages. The samples generated at the 250-epoch stage most closely resembled the original pre-training data. Although both training and validation losses continued to decrease up to 300 epochs, the similarity between the generated samples and the original data diminished during later stages of training. As a result, the 250-epoch stage was selected for fine-tuning on the EMOPIA dataset, as it offered an optimal trade-off between loss minimization and fidelity to the pre-training dataset.  Additionally, we evaluated the decoder’s ability to generate MIDI files aligned with arousal-valence (AV) quadrants by directly providing quadrant labels, bypassing the use of text embeddings. This straightforward approach produced MIDI samples that effectively aligned with the valence-arousal metrics of the EMOPIA dataset. A listening test involving four participants, conducted following the methodology outlined in Section IV-A, provided further validation. Table I summarizes their evaluations, indicating that the model-generated music aligned with the arousal dimension with 70% accuracy, compared to 53% accuracy for valence and 40% for the combined valence-arousal dimensions. These results surpass the expected baseline accuracies, which are 50% for valence, 50% for arousal, and 25% for the combined dimensions. The findings suggest that the model was effective in capturing the emotional tone of the input text, exceeding what would be expected by chance.  However, the study's reliability could be improved by incorporating a larger and more diverse group of participants in future experiments. Such an approach would allow for more robust statistical analyses and ensure the generalizability of these preliminary findings.

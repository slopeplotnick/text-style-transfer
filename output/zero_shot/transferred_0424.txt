The objective of this research is to develop a system that facilitates the training of policies capable of executing naturalistic movements without the reliance on original data. This system is designed to be applicable across a variety of tasks and policies, eliminating the need for retraining. To achieve this, we introduce Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), a method that enables the training of control policies to exhibit natural behaviors across multiple tasks. Initially, a model is trained to comprehend motion data in a manner that is agnostic to any specific task or policy. Upon completion of this training, the model is employed to compare simulated motions with behaviors derived from the original data. The learned SMP is then utilized as a reward mechanism, facilitating the training of new policies to perform diverse tasks while maintaining naturalistic behaviors. The core concept is to establish a system that generates reusable motion priors for character animation by integrating reinforcement learning with score distillation. The proposed system produces high-quality motions comparable to leading methods, while enhancing reusability and modularity. SMP permits the training of policies independently from the original data. Our findings demonstrate the possibility of deriving a motion prior from a model trained on extensive motion datasets without task-specific focus. This motion prior can subsequently be adapted into style-specific variants through prompting and guidance. Additionally, we illustrate the capability to combine different style priors to synthesize new styles not present in the original data.

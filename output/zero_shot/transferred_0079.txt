In their work on Empirical Software Engineering, the authors reference Victor R. Basili, a prominent figure in the field, highlighting the significance of utilizing empirical data and evidence to enhance software engineering practices. Numerous studies have explored the application of Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE) within business contexts and its implications on agile development methodologies. These investigations have yielded valuable insights and strategies for implementation. However, none have approached the subject as we have, examining the transitional phase following adoption but preceding full implementation.  In this paper, we present the initial phase of an extensive framework aimed at facilitating a company's transition to a Software Product Line (SPL) approach. To this end, we conducted tailored interviews, taking into account the diverse roles of participants involved in the project. Our objective was to identify optimal preparatory strategies for the transition, concentrating on inquiries that illuminate current best practices, perceived benefits, potential risks, and existing concerns. Addressing these risks and concerns, leveraging benefits, and fostering best practices are crucial for ensuring a seamless transition.  Our findings indicate that the company has already adopted certain advantageous practices and possesses a culture amenable to innovation, which bodes well for the project's success. The majority of stakeholders anticipate that the transition will be advantageous. Nevertheless, there are concerns regarding the complexity of SPL and its maintenance requirements.

Position bias refers to the tendency of language models to prioritize information located in specific sections of the input, while disregarding other parts. Despite the sophistication of these models in processing language, empirical research has demonstrated that they can still be swayed by the arrangement of the input. For instance, in tasks such as generating summaries for multiple documents merged into a single input, the sequence of the documents can influence the content included in the summary. Consequently, summaries may disproportionately emphasize certain segments and omit other salient details, contingent upon the placement of the information. This phenomenon is exemplified by the "Lost in the Middle" problem, where researchers observed that models exhibit optimal performance when critical information is positioned either at the beginning or the end of the input. Conversely, performance diminishes when vital information is embedded in the middle. This pattern is evident in tasks such as question answering or extracting key details from multiple documents. Additional studies have identified that when models are employed for evaluation tasks, they exhibit a propensity to favor the initial or final option presented, even if the optimal choice resides elsewhere. This tendency, commonly termed "lead bias," results in an overemphasis on the initial portion of the input. While certain models, such as GPT-3.5-Turbo, exhibit reduced susceptibility to this bias, others remain highly sensitive to early content, particularly in tasks requiring concise summaries. Such biases not only degrade performance but also pose challenges to fairness. For example, in scenarios where tweets are grouped by dialect and ordered sequentially, the resulting summaries may disproportionately represent the dialect appearing first. This underscores the significant impact of input order on model outputs.

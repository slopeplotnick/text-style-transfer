In this study, we employed a methodological approach that integrates label-driven Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation with the identification of challenging cases to enhance the model's cognitive processes through incremental reasoning and the management of difficult examples. We evaluated the efficacy of this approach by concentrating on complex scenarios to determine its capacity to navigate intricate situations and sophisticated reasoning tasks. A significant advancement was achieved by facilitating the CoT-based knowledge system's application in online environments and its adaptability to smaller models, thereby improving deep reasoning in practical applications.   This undertaking encountered two primary challenges: first, the extraction of product details from images characterized by minimal textual information; second, ensuring that decision-making processes adhere to stringent regulations and business standards. To address these, we devised a strategy that enables the model to comprehend both visual and textual inputs while conforming to business protocols. Our approach utilized a combination of CoT generation, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning predicated on the Qwen-2.5 large language model (LLM) for textual data and Qwen-2.5 vision-language (VL) model for visual data. This configuration empowered the model to conduct reasoning processes encompassing both visual and textual elements, thereby surpassing the limitations associated with using a singular data modality.   Our methodology involved multimodal and rule-aware CoT generation, integrating the interpretation of images and textual data with prompts that adhere to established standards. Business regulations and exemplary cases were embedded within the CoT reasoning framework to facilitate the model's comprehension of regulatory constraints. Subsequently, reinforcement learning was employed to refine the model's capability to implement regulatory guidelines in decision-making processes amidst complex scenarios.

The dataset utilized in this study comprises 81 questions, each addressing a specific health condition and an associated treatment. These health conditions span 50 disease and condition categories, including but not limited to "Cancer," "Low Back Pain in Adults," and "AIDS." Additionally, the dataset incorporates a general well-being category labeled "Other," which encompasses topics such as weight management. Figure 2 illustrates the 10 most frequently mentioned categories within the health-related questions. A comprehensive list of all questions alongside their corresponding categories is available in Appendix Table A4.   To examine the capacity of language models to manage real-world biomedical knowledge conflicts, we developed five prompt templates designed to evaluate model performance under varying question-answering scenarios. The structure of these templates is outlined in Table 2, where each entry from the HEALTHCONTRADICT dataset is reformulated into five distinct question prompts as follows:   (i) *Question: {Question}*   (ii) *Question: {Question}*   (iii) *Question: {Question}*   (iv) *Context: {Correct Document} Question: {Question}*   (v) *Context: {Incorrect Document} Question: {Question}*    The baseline models were selected based on two primary considerations: (i) to determine whether fine-tuning for biomedical domains enhances performance within these specific areas, and (ii) to assess whether larger models exhibit improved performance. We also prioritized language models capable of processing extended text passages. Detailed descriptions of the selected models are provided in Table 3. Each biomedical model was derived by fine-tuning a general model listed in the same row. Notably, the term "Instruct" refers to models that have undergone additional training to better adhere to user instructions. In this analysis, no models were fine-tuned; instead, all evaluations were conducted using zero-shot inference on the baseline models.  The study emphasizes open-source language models ranging from 1 billion to 8 billion parameters, enabling applicability in resource-constrained healthcare environments. As presented in Table 4, the performance difference between larger models (7–8 billion parameters) was found to be negligible. Furthermore, the fine-tuned biomedical model, MEDITRON3-QWEN2.5-7B, underperformed in comparison to QWEN2.5-7B. This outcome highlights the limitations of standard evaluation benchmarks, which primarily assess a model’s inherent knowledge without adequately capturing nuanced differences in their capabilities.

In recent years, several innovative datasets have emerged, including Mozilla's Common Voice, which encompasses recorded speech data in six South African languages. This initiative has benefited from community contributions and is distributed under an open license, facilitating unrestricted access and use. The FLEURS dataset comprises approximately 12 hours of recordings for each of four South African languages, primarily intended for testing and evaluation. The Vuk'uzenzele (VixSD) dataset, although smaller in scale, exemplifies community-led efforts, being shared under a license emphasizing community ownership and ethical utilization. Despite its limited size, it underscores the potential for local communities to contribute to resource development. AfriSpeech-200 contains English recordings with South African accents and focuses on healthcare topics, thereby aiding the development of speech recognition systems attuned to accent variations. However, there remains a paucity of datasets featuring spontaneous, unscripted speech and a broad range of topics, highlighting the necessity for more comprehensive datasets such as Swivuriso. These should integrate both scripted and unscripted speech, encompass diverse topics, and include a broader spectrum of speakers.  The collection of speech data within underrepresented communities necessitates meticulous attention to consent, privacy, cultural values, and the potential long-term effects on these communities. Historical initiatives such as Common Voice and ALFFA have underscored the critical importance of incorporating ethical and participatory practices throughout the data collection process. For instance, a study on the Catalan version of Common Voice underscored the significance of clear consent protocols, privacy protection, and inclusive participation. The ALFFA project exemplified collaboration with local experts and linguists to ensure community engagement during data acquisition. Building upon these principles, Swivuriso emphasizes ethical practices and inclusivity. Local organizers were instrumental in managing recordings, supporting participants, and ensuring community representation. Adherence to ethically approved guidelines was ensured, including the removal of personal identifiers, data anonymization, and the implementation of safeguards to prevent misuse, such as voice cloning or biometric tracking.  Ensuring that datasets are accessible and usable is paramount, particularly for languages with limited resources. Open licenses, such as those provided by Creative Commons, are advantageous as they permit unrestricted data usage, which is vital when data collection is challenging or costly. Projects like Common Voice illustrate how open licensing can facilitate resource sharing and expedite the advancement of language technology for under-resourced languages.

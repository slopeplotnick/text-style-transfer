This study examines the impact of incorporating structured climate data into retrieval-augmented generation systems. It was hypothesized that the inclusion of such data would enhance the accuracy and comprehensibility of generated responses; however, findings indicate that this approach remains in its nascent stages. As demonstrated in Table 1, the DATA_RAG configuration, which integrates both literature and structured data, performed less effectively than the RAG setup that utilized literature exclusively, with accuracy experiencing the most significant decline. This suggests that current models are not optimally equipped to leverage structured climate data to support or enhance textual reasoning.  The CAIRNS system, designed for question-answering in agricultural climate adaptation, seeks to achieve a balance between readability, accuracy, and source transparency. It incorporates structured prompts (SGP), multi-step reasoning, and a consistency-weighted Hybrid Evaluator. Empirical tests indicate that SGP enhances structural coherence and source reliability without compromising accuracy, representing a positive advancement toward producing QA outputs that align with the preferences of domain experts. Nevertheless, the efficacy of data-augmented retrieval remains limited at present.  The Hybrid Evaluator employs model agreement as an indicator to assess credibility and outperforms other automated evaluation methods without necessitating fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. This approach is advantageous in scenarios characterized by constrained resources or limited expert availability for question-answering. Future research aims to refine location-specific reasoning through the integration of detailed geographic data, such as latitude and longitude, facilitating document transfer across regions with analogous climates and broadening the applicability of literature-based insights. Additionally, plans are underway to develop an expansive evaluation benchmark encompassing diverse question types and topics. This initiative will aid in calibrating the Hybrid Evaluator and provide training data for forthcoming models, thereby supporting rigorous and reproducible QA research in the field of climate adaptation.

Social media platforms have become an integral part of daily life, enabling rapid communication, effortless information sharing, and the exchange of opinions across geographic boundaries. However, the unregulated freedom offered by these platforms has also facilitated the proliferation of hate speech and offensive language. Despite the significance of the issue, limited research has been dedicated to detecting hate speech in Bangla social media, with the notable absence of high-quality datasets tailored to this task. The linguistic characteristics of social media content often diverge significantly from those of traditional print media, posing additional challenges to the development of automated hate speech detection systems in Bangla.    Traditionally, hate speech detection has been framed as a supervised classification problem. Early approaches primarily relied on rule-based methods and word lists, which frequently failed to identify implicit or context-dependent hate speech. More recent studies have incorporated machine learning techniques, including Logistic Regression and SVM, to address this limitation. Supervised learning methods remain a predominant approach to detecting harmful online content, particularly in the context of hate speech identification.

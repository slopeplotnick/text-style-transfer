Traditional approaches to optimizing construction coordination are characterized by several fundamental limitations. These methods often require comprehensive planning from the outset, yet construction projects are inherently dynamic, undergoing frequent changes in tasks, environmental conditions, and available resources. Moreover, the complexity of these methods renders them particularly challenging for large-scale projects involving multiple robots and diverse tasks. In response to these issues, researchers have increasingly explored learning-based approaches capable of adapting to evolving project conditions without the necessity of pre-established plans. Among these approaches, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has garnered significant attention due to its capacity to enable autonomous agents, such as robots, to learn cooperative behaviors through interaction with their environment. RL operates in a trial-and-error manner, allowing robots to adapt effectively to situational changes.   However, conventional RL methods pose notable challenges within construction contexts. The extensive practice required for RL-based training can be hazardous, as errors during training may compromise worker safety and damage equipment. Furthermore, the prolonged duration of the training process is often impractical given the strict timelines associated with construction projects. To address these challenges, domain-specific RL methodologies tailored for construction applications have been developed. Lee et al. (2022) introduced a framework utilizing digital twins in conjunction with Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to optimize robotic task execution in construction. Their approach integrated Building Information Modeling (BIM) with DRL to facilitate intelligent coordination decisions that account for spatial, temporal, and safety considerations. Digital twins were employed to create simulated environments that enable safe learning prior to real-world implementation.  Despite these advances, RL-based methods continue to face critical limitations. Even models trained extensively in simulated environments may fail to perform effectively in real-world construction scenarios, where conditions frequently diverge from those assumed during training. This discrepancy may lead to safety concerns and reduced applicability. Consequently, these challenges have stimulated further research into advanced RL techniques designed to enhance the reliability and adaptability of learning-based systems within construction operations.

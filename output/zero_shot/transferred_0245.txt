Ensuring that datasets are appropriately licensed is crucial for facilitating their ethical accessibility and usability, particularly for under-resourced languages. The utilization of open licenses, such as the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0), significantly enhances the potential for large-scale data reuse. Initiatives like Mozilla Common Voice exemplify the efficacy of open licenses in advancing language technology development. Nevertheless, many researchers in low- and middle-income countries continue to face challenges in accessing resources necessary for AI development, raising concerns regarding equitable data management and the empowerment of local communities.  In the Swivuriso project, the adoption of the CC BY 4.0 license was intended to ensure open access and ethical accountability. The project is hosted on platforms such as Zenodo and Hugging Face, complete with permanent Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs) and comprehensive documentation. Each release includes audio files, corresponding transcripts, and detailed information regarding demographics, language, and accessibility. Moreover, an ethical use statement is provided to restrict activities, such as voice cloning, thereby safeguarding the privacy of speakers.  The CC BY 4.0 license is characterized by its openness, permitting individuals to share, modify, and utilize the dataset for any purpose, including commercial applications, provided that appropriate credit is given to the creators and any modifications are noted. The license prohibits the imposition of additional legal or technical restrictions, encompasses database rights, and includes a "cure period" to address any licensing issues, all of which contribute to its legal clarity and support for global scientific endeavors.  Despite the advantages of open licenses in enhancing data utility, they often overlook aspects such as local control and the protection of Indigenous knowledge. The application of standardized licenses can inadvertently undermine community ownership, particularly concerning culturally significant data. This issue has prompted the emergence of alternative frameworks that prioritize data stewardship, benefit-sharing, and community governance, which are gaining traction in African data management practices.

We opted to exclude the KL divergence regularization term, as the model, having demonstrated a proficient initial performance, inherently understands reasoning and output formatting. The imposition of KL constraints would potentially hinder the model's exploration of innovative problem-solving strategies; hence, we permit it to optimize autonomously based on its existing knowledge base.  Our reward framework is designed with three primary objectives: (1) Format Standardization: We employ specific tokens to distinctly delineate the reasoning process from the final output, thereby facilitating comprehension and subsequent utilization; (2) Output Space Constraint: We rigorously regulate the categories of discrepancies (e.g., "price mismatch" or "functionality mismatch") to a predetermined list, thereby preventing the generation of arbitrary outputs; (3) Progressive Reward: In instances where the model achieves relevance yet commits minor errors, it still receives positive reinforcement to enhance its learning rate and consistency.  Furthermore, we implement a tripartite learning strategy to progressively augment the model's robustness by incrementally increasing the complexity of the training data. Table 6 delineates the distribution of samples across each phase. Our schema is predicated on three fundamental principles: (1) Rapid Bootstrap Principle: Step 1 predominantly utilizes samples of medium difficulty to furnish the model with prompt and constructive feedback while maintaining a degree of challenge, thus circumventing issues often encountered during initial training phases; (2) Progressive Challenge Principle: Steps 2 and 3 incrementally introduce more complex samples, facilitating the model's transition from addressing routine issues to tackling more intricate cases, thereby broadening its capabilities; (3) Knowledge Retention Principle: The latter stages continue to incorporate simpler samples as "memory anchors," which serve to prevent the model from discarding previously acquired skills while assimilating new ones, also contributing to training stability.

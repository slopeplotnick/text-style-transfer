Our investigation determined that the implementation of a deformable scan significantly enhances the model's performance. Upon removal of this component and subsequent utilization of the standard version of Mamba, there was a notable decrease in performance, underscoring the importance of the deformable scan. Various design configurations were explored to ascertain optimal functionality. Our proposed design employs a composite structure of blocks, designated as "CCDGDG" across stages one to six, where 'C' denotes convolutional blocks, 'D' represents DDSM+FNN blocks, and 'G' signifies gated-convolutional blocks. We also evaluated designs composed of two block types, such as "CCGGGG" and "CCDDD", as well as designs comprising a singular block type. Results conclusively indicated that our mixed design yielded superior performance compared to the alternatives. Further examination of examples from the InterHand2.6M and AssemblyHands datasets revealed that DF-Mamba achieved more precise joint position estimations, evidenced by a reduction in errors highlighted by colored ellipses. DF-Mamba demonstrated resilience in identifying joints even when occluded or obscured, substantiating its robustness in real-world applications. DF-Mamba represents a novel framework for 3D hand pose estimation, characterized by efficient initial feature extraction followed by a deformable representation. Extensive evaluation across five datasets featuring diverse hand interactions and data types revealed that DF-Mamba outperformed other models and operated with greater efficiency. Prospective research directions could involve extending the application of DF-Mamba to tasks such as 3D human pose estimation, enhancing training with extensive external datasets, employing it in innovative hand-mesh reconstruction methodologies, and developing it into a versatile base model capable of functioning effectively across varying contexts without necessitating specific modifications.

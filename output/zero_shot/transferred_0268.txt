We developed a scale consisting of nine items assessed on a 7-point Likert scale to examine affective responses such as shame, guilt, and impostor syndrome associated with the use of artificial intelligence (AI). To evaluate the psychometric properties of this scale, we collected real-world data through an online survey hosted on Prolific, which was conducted between the evening of February 3, 2025, and late night February 4, 2025. The survey included these nine items in addition to other established measures to verify the scale's reliability and construct validity.   Following data collection, we compared the real-world dataset with a simulated dataset generated using the same methodological approach implemented in our initial study. Table 1 provides a comparison of participant characteristics across the real and simulated datasets. Further details regarding the Scale for Assessing Guilt, Anxiety, and Transparency (SAGAT) will be elaborated in a forthcoming publication, which will address the full process of scale development, incorporating insights from our experimental paradigm.  To analyze the differences between the real and simulated datasets, we employed the Mann-Whitney U test. The findings indicated no significant differences in impostor syndrome scores between the real and simulated datasets. However, significant differences were observed for shame and guilt. Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests further revealed statistically significant discrepancies for all three constructs—shame, guilt, and impostor syndrome—between the datasets. These findings do not support Hypothesis 4 (H4). Histograms comparing the distributions of the real-world and simulated data are presented in Figure 4.  To ensure the validity and robustness of our findings, we conducted an additional study aimed at developing a new scale to assess anxious beliefs specifically related to AI. This scale was constructed using the same methodological framework as outlined in Study 3, which is detailed in the "Methods" section of that study.

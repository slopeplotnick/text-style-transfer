In this study, we employ three distinct emotion datasets to train and evaluate the performance of our model. The EMER dataset provides high-quality, human-validated reasoning-label pairs and serves as the initial basis for our model training. Complementarily, the DFEW and MAFW datasets are extensive collections incorporating diverse facial expressions across video, audio, and text modalities. Detailed descriptions of these datasets can be found in Appendix 7.1. Consistent with previous research, we report both Unweighted Average Recall (UAR) and Weighted Average Recall (WAR) to assess recognition accuracy, alongside three additional metrics—Emotion Explanation Accuracy (EEA), Explanation-Path Consistency (EPC), and Fluent Coherence Ratio (FCR)—to evaluate the alignment between the emotion explanation and the model's predictions. Precise definitions and our annotation methodology are provided in Appendix 7.2.  For the experimental setup, we adopt the R1-Omni architecture, utilizing SigLIP as the vision encoder, Whisper-large-v3 for audio encoding, and BERT for text encoding. The outputs from each data modality are mapped into the Large Language Model (LLM) embedding space through visual and audio projectors, which consist of two linear layers tailored to match the LLM representation size. For instance, a one-second audio segment generates approximately 50 audio features, while a single video frame produces 729 visual features. The backbone of the LLM is the Qwen2.5-7B, initialized with pretrained weights from the HumanOmni checkpoint to retain multimodal representation capabilities. To evaluate the efficacy of our approach, we benchmark the Model for Integrated Generative Reasoning (MIGR) against existing methodologies for emotion understanding on the MAFW and DFEW datasets. We categorize previous methods into two groups: those that directly predict emotion labels and those that generate textual explanations prior to prediction.

In contemporary Natural Language Processing, large language models such as GPT and BERT have significantly transformed various applications. These models possess the capability to generate human-like text, address complex inquiries, and engage in seamless conversation. When a particular approach yields a seemingly coherent answer, it is analogous to setting a compass northward. However, the responses produced by these models, despite their logical coherence and relevance to the input, often lack veracity as they are not grounded in factual information. This phenomenon, known as "hallucination," poses substantial challenges to the widespread adoption of these models. It can be likened to a storyteller who inadvertently includes fictional elements while striving for truthfulness. Consequently, these models may generate content that appears credible but does not derive from their training data. An analogy would be a GPS system that confidently provides directions but occasionally leads users to non-existent roads.  Technically, hallucinations occur when the generated content does not align with factual information or anticipated outcomes based on the models' training data. This can manifest as erroneous statements, fabricated references, or flawed reasoning. Similar to a GPS that projects confidence yet occasionally errs, these models are prone to mistakes. The artificial intelligence community is actively addressing this issue. Even the most advanced models are susceptible to hallucinations. While these models exhibit remarkable capabilities, their reliability, particularly concerning factual accuracy, remains a critical concern in fields such as healthcare, law, and academic research.

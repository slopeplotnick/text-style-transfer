Natural Language Processing (NLP), an increasingly significant field within artificial intelligence, traces its conceptual origins to the Swiss linguist Ferdinand de Saussure. Saussure posited that the semantics of language are derived from the relational dynamics and distinctions among words, asserting that shared linguistic structures underpin effective human communication. The formal genesis of NLP in computational realms can be attributed to Alan Turing in 1950, who introduced the notion of a "thinking machine." Turing postulated that a machine capable of engaging in teleprinter-mediated conversation with humans would exemplify cognitive capabilities.  In contemporary times, NLP is pivotal in the comprehension and generation of human language responses that are contextually coherent. This is exemplified by intelligent assistants such as Siri and Alexa, which autonomously facilitate user interactions and fulfill requests. Advanced AI systems known as Large Language Models (LLMs) have been developed using deep learning techniques, trained on extensive datasets to emulate human-like text production. These models employ transformer architecture, which enables them to discern complex linguistic intricacies and recognize relationships across extensive textual inputs, thus facilitating functions such as language translation, conversational exchanges, and content creation.  LLMs exhibit proficiency in understanding human languages, rendering them invaluable across various research and industrial domains. OpenAI's ChatGPT, a prominent LLM, exemplifies widespread application in daily life. Despite their capabilities, language models are prone to errors termed "hallucinations," wherein they generate plausible but inaccurate information based on learned patterns, particularly when confronted with unfamiliar data. Such inaccuracies can lead to issues ranging from minor misinterpretations to significant discrepancies in areas such as translation, software coding, or autonomous system operations. Addressing these hallucinations is crucial to ensuring models are reliable, secure, and useful, especially in contexts where precision is imperative.  Mitigating these errors can be achieved through the deployment of fine-tuned models—pre-trained models subsequently refined for specific tasks. These models outperform general-purpose counterparts by leveraging training on more focused, smaller datasets. Fine-tuning involves meticulous adjustment of model parameters to enhance accuracy and generalization capabilities, building upon foundational language comprehension acquired during initial training while honing task-specific proficiency.

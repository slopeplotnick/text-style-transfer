To address these challenges, it is necessary to shift the focus from static outputs to the dynamic processes that occur in real-time during language model generation. The proposed solution involves leveraging a set of foundational questions designed to prompt language models to generate responses immediately upon inquiry. This approach aims to ensure that (1) fact-checking methodologies have access to the relevant text required for accurate verification, (2) hallucination detection techniques can monitor the internal processes of the model during response generation, and (3) evaluation frameworks remain robust and applicable as language models continue to evolve. This conceptual framework underpins the UniFact system, which seeks to resolve the limitations of outdated benchmarks through three core principles, ensuring evaluations are robust, equitable, and future-oriented.  First, the system avoids reliance on pre-compiled datasets of fixed outputs. Instead, it employs a dynamic question-based mechanism that prompts real-time response generation, enabling the evaluation framework to remain relevant for both present and future iterations of language models. Second, UniFact promotes fair assessment by supporting both hallucination detection and fact-checking methodologies. It facilitates hallucination detection by providing access to the model's intermediate processes during response generation and supports fact-checking by supplying the generated outputs alongside supplementary information for validation. Both methodologies are evaluated based on the same criterion: determining the factual correctness of the model's output. Third, UniFact incorporates an automated labeling system to evaluate outputs, eschewing reliance on slower, subjective human review processes. This automated system verifies the accuracy of model-generated responses by comparing them against ground-truth answers and other reliable sources, enabling rapid, fair, and consistent evaluation across multiple models.

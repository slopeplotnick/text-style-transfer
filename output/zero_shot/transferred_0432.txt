Recent advancements in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) have significantly enhanced the performance of large language models in tasks requiring extensive knowledge. However, these models predominantly focus on general topics or current events and demonstrate limited efficacy in addressing critical issues, such as assisting agricultural sectors in adapting to climate change. Although some contemporary research efforts have explored the integration of document retrieval and climate data for domain-specific inquiries, these approaches frequently rely on rudimentary methodologies that fail to deliver comprehensive or verified responses in specialized fields. Previous attempts to develop geo-aware question-answering systems in various domains have not adequately addressed the complexities inherent in climate-related inquiries. Moreover, existing studies often treat climate data and research as discrete entities, neglecting a holistic approach.  The CAIRNS framework introduces a novel, four-step process designed to bridge these gaps. Initially, it sources climate information from Elsevier and a climate data API. Subsequently, it generates responses using a mechanism termed the ScholarGuide Prompt, followed by a verification stage. Finally, it conducts a mixed-methods evaluation. To effectively identify pertinent documents, CAIRNS employs a dual-faceted retrieval system combining lexical matching and semantic retrieval. However, given the critical importance of geographic context in climate-related questions, a location similarity score is incorporated to ensure that responses are contextually relevant both semantically and geographically.  The CAIRNS framework operates in an iterative loop, involving evidence retrieval, response generation, and adequacy verification. In instances where information is deemed insufficient, it formulates new queries, iteratively gathering additional data until the response reaches an acceptable level of completeness and accuracy. Central to the CAIRNS methodology is the ScholarGuide Prompt, which is instrumental in harmonizing readability with factual accuracy. In addressing climate-related inquiries, this prompt serves as a directive, guiding the language model in structuring responses, applying scientific reasoning, and offering precise recommendations.

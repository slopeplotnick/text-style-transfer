The audio recordings underwent a thorough review to ensure clarity and adherence to provided instructions. Upon approval, trained personnel transcribed the recordings using a standardized system. Only recordings and their corresponding transcripts that successfully passed a final verification were incorporated into the dataset. Compensation was adjusted to accommodate linguistic requirements. This procedure ensured that the resultant dataset was of high technical quality, encompassed speakers from diverse backgrounds, and complied with ethical standards.  The prompts employed were not scripted but were devised by language teams to be culturally pertinent and to facilitate natural discourse. Each language team was composed of a leader and typically three assistants. The team members conducted mutual reviews prior to the leader's final approval of the project prompts. Utilizing Microsoft Forms, the teams selected a knowledge domain and subsequently identified a topic along with related subtopics.  Furthermore, the language teams were instructed to create prompts that elicited a variety of responses. For example, they were encouraged to ask "What is your favorite fruit and why?" rather than the more straightforward "What is your favorite fruit?" This training emphasized the significance of designing prompts that solicit responses extending beyond mere statements to include questions, exclamations, commands, and others. Teams were also encouraged to enhance the cultural relevance and sensitivity of topics and subtopics when necessary. The overarching goal was to capture the linguistic nuances of South African vernacular, thereby ensuring that topics and subdomains were highly relevant to South African life and particular to each language.

In order to facilitate the comparison of findings across different studies, it is imperative that the samples demonstrate similarity in certain respects. This verification was conducted utilizing Multigroup Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA), which allowed for the examination of two distinct samples—one derived from empirical data and the other from simulated data—by treating them as separate groups and incrementally imposing additional constraints. This process was delineated into several components: assessment of configural invariance (Hypothesis H2.1), metric invariance (Hypothesis H2.2), scalar invariance (Hypothesis H2.3), and residual invariance (Hypothesis H2.4). Achieving configural invariance implies that the underlying factor structure is consistent across both groups. Metric invariance facilitates the comparison of latent factor differences. Scalar invariance enables the comparison of latent mean scores. Residual invariance permits the comparison of observed mean scores and supports further analysis of observable data. Should the real-world and simulated samples exhibit substantial similarity in these domains, it would substantiate the reliability of simulation data as representative of empirical data. Moreover, it was hypothesized that the responses from the empirical data would closely mirror those from the simulation (Hypothesis H3), and that the score distributions would be largely analogous (Hypothesis H4). To ascertain whether the simulated data emulates empirical data characteristics, the Mann-Whitney U test was initially employed to evaluate differences in means, followed by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test to analyze the dispersion of the data.

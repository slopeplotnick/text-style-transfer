In this study, we utilize a dataset comprising pairs of real-world images and their corresponding Ghibli-style versions to perform a comparative analysis. Both the original and Ghibli-style images are examined using a structured and systematic methodology. Specifically, we evaluate whether the stylistic elements present in the images align with the characteristic features of the Ghibli aesthetic. This evaluation allows us to assess the extent to which the transformations applied during the style transfer process adhere to the visual qualities associated with Studio Ghibli's films. Furthermore, when such alignment is observed, we determine the specific Ghibli film from which the stylistic influence was likely derived, providing evidence that the model has successfully learned stylistic features from these films. Overall, this analysis serves to verify the model's ability to capture and replicate the core visual attributes of the Ghibli style.  For the purposes of image characterization, we employ a tool capable of analyzing both textual and visual inputs. This analysis is conducted following a predefined framework that prioritizes features such as texture, color palette, mood, lighting, and spatial composition. To enhance the precision of this framework, additional descriptive elements were incorporated, enabling a deeper examination of objects and stylistic features within the images. A detailed table is provided to outline the specific criteria utilized in this analysis, along with a record of the corresponding Ghibli film associated with each stylistic match.  Upon aggregating the results, we observed that most Studio Ghibli films contributed relatively evenly to the stylistic elements identified in the transformed images. However, certain films, including *Princess Mononoke*, *Kiki’s Delivery Service*, and *The Tale of the Princess Kaguya*, exhibited a slightly higher influence. This finding underscores the diverse range of styles encompassed within Studio Ghibli’s oeuvre, a diversity that is successfully reflected in the generated images. A comprehensive breakdown of this data is presented in a supplemental table for further reference.

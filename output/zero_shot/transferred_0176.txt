The operational mechanism of this system involves the mother agent engaging with other agents utilizing its known grammatical structures. This represents a novel approach, as traditional language learning models typically rely on extensive corpora for training. In this study, the term "language model" is not restricted to conventional computational programs designed to predict subsequent words, such as GPT or BERT. Instead, it encompasses a variety of methodologies, including rule-based systems, statistical methods, and neural networks, all of which contribute to linguistic tasks. This distinction is significant because our system employs explicit grammatical rules, while the learning agent utilizes statistical methods to infer these rules. This research investigates whether the MODOMA daughter agent can acquire and demonstrate various linguistic components by leveraging a particular distribution within the MODOMA system. The study examines the ability of a multi-agent system to discern linguistic elements from computer-generated sentences and articulate them through rules. We aim to determine whether these sentences exhibit patterns analogous to human language and assess the quantity of data necessary to recognize these patterns. Furthermore, we explore the potential for these patterns to be converted into formal rules. Finally, we evaluate if the identified categories align with the original grammar and whether the system is capable of autonomously acquiring language structure.

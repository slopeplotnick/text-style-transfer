This section delineates the methodology employed in the creation and validation of the dataset. A consortium of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) specialists undertook the collection of samples from clinical cases, historical case records, and examination questions. Subsequent to the collection phase, these samples underwent a rigorous process of cleaning, which entailed the elimination of duplicate entries and the excision of any residual personal information. The data annotation process was meticulously executed in three distinct phases: initially, the experts performed manual annotations, followed by a peer review of each other's annotations, culminating in a review by an independent entity.  The resultant benchmark, designated as TCM-BEST4SDT, comprises a total of 600 questions categorized under four primary subjects: TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, Large Language Model (LLM) Content Safety, and Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment (SDT). To assess the efficacy of models in real-world TCM clinical settings, three evaluative methodologies were developed. The first method, termed selected-response evaluation, involves multiple iterations of question evaluation, permutation of answer choices, and differential scoring contingent upon question type. The second method, known as judge model evaluation, employs an LLM, augmented by expert-formulated prompts, to appraise the model's responses. The third evaluative technique, termed reward model evaluation, involves the development of a specialized model designed to assess the congruity between prescriptions and syndromes.  The TCM-BEST4SDT dataset encompasses two distinct task categories: SDT tasks and general evaluation tasks. SDT tasks are derived from clinical cases and historical records, whereas general evaluation tasks encompass TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, and LLM Content Safety. The questions related to TCM Basic Knowledge originate from public examinations such as the National Qualification Exam for Medical Practitioners, the National Postgraduate Entrance Exam: Comprehensive Clinical Medicine (TCM Integrated), and the Chinese Herbal Medicine Title Exam. Conversely, Medical Ethics questions are sourced from reputable references and have been manually annotated by experts to address specific scenarios. Lastly, LLM Content Safety questions have been independently crafted by experts to reflect practical requirements.

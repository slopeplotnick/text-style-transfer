The tool library comprises several instruments designed to facilitate the generation of automated radiology reports: a tool for identifying specific regions, a tool for strategizing the analysis of regions using the method "Think with Image," a tool for generating reports utilizing templates, and a tool for assessing the quality of radiology reports and providing constructive feedback for enhancement. These instruments collectively support the comprehensive process of creating automated radiology reports. Figure 2 illustrates the operational framework of the Radiologist Copilot.  For the purpose of evaluating the efficacy of the Radiologist Copilot, a liver radiology reporting task was employed. This study utilized the publicly available 3D CT dataset known as AMOS-MM, which consists of over 2,000 CT scans accompanied by detailed radiology reports. Of these, 1,287 scans with reports were designated for training, whereas 400 scans with accompanying reports were utilized for validation. Within the scope of the liver task, emphasis was placed on reports within the AMOS-MM dataset pertaining to the liver, resulting in a subset comprising 1,149 CT scans with liver reports for training and 367 CT scans with liver reports for validation. The validation set served to assess the efficacy of the method, while the training set facilitated an in-depth analysis of the reports.  Initially, BioBERT, a tool for transforming reports into data points, was employed, followed by the application of K-means clustering to categorize these data points into template reports. These templates were further dissected into liver-specific attributes such as liver surface, liver tissue, bile ducts, and hepatic pathologies, which were utilized by the Analyzer Tool.  The construction of the Radiologist Copilot was implemented utilizing the OctoTools system, operating on Python version 3.10.18 with NVIDIA L20 GPUs. The system did not necessitate additional training, given its reliance on pre-trained models. The principal reasoning tool employed was Qwen3-32B, and the Segmentator Tool utilized the TotalSegmentator model for region identification. Furthermore, the Analyzer Tool applied the 3D medical model Hulu-Med for CT scan analysis. The system configuration was established to undertake a maximum of 10 procedural steps, allocating up to 500 seconds for each individual case.

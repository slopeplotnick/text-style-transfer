This study seeks to address the challenge of ensuring interoperability among diverse systems. We are in the process of establishing test environments in which artificial intelligence (AI) systems can enforce, interpret, and modify shared sets of rules. The primary objective is to evaluate whether these methods effectively mitigate previously identified issues. Our overarching aim is to establish a research trajectory in AI safety that integrates theoretical foundations, experimental validation, and eventual standardization. This paper serves as a conceptual overview, with subsequent publications intended to provide empirical evidence and formal validations of the proposed ideas.  We argue that focusing solely on the safety of individual AI systems—through techniques such as prompt engineering and output filtering—is insufficient when considering the interaction among multiple AIs. As AI systems increasingly collaborate, adhering to localized regulations alone proves inadequate in preventing systemic failures. There exists a significant distinction between ensuring the safety of a single AI and safeguarding the entire system. To elucidate this distinction, we introduce the notion of the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH). Our analysis demonstrates that risks can escalate from minor issues to widespread systemic challenges as interactions, complexity, and diverse objectives intersect. These risks are not merely attributable to isolated AI malfunctions but are inherent to the dynamics of networked systems.  By empowering agents to adapt rules and collaborate, we aim to stabilize collective behavior without relying solely on centralized control mechanisms. This work represents an initial exploration into the ESRH framework and the concept of Institutional AI, both of which necessitate comprehensive testing, theoretical development, and refinement. Our subsequent steps include conducting controlled experiments to quantify the indicators identified, examining models of coordination, and developing practical tools for implementing these concepts within real-world AI systems.  The transition from single-agent to multi-agent AI systems is already underway. Without innovative frameworks and management strategies, safety concerns may remain obscured until they escalate beyond manageable proportions. This paper aims to elucidate these issues and propose a systematic approach to addressing them.

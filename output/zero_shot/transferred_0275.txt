In this study, we conducted a comparative analysis between data generated from our models and the data present in the EMOPIA dataset. We employed t-tests to ascertain whether the generated data preserved the significant differences observed in high and low valence and arousal within the EMOPIA dataset. For the purpose of generating music based on specific emotional content derived from text, we utilized an encoder-decoder Transformer model. The text's emotional content is extracted using a RoBERTa-large encoder, which we fine-tuned to enhance its capacity to detect subtle variations in emotional categories. The decoder component consists of a Transformer architecture with three layers, each containing four attention heads, specifically designed to manage the temporal and harmonic aspects of symbolic music. Figure 2 illustrates the operational mechanism of the model.   The encoder initially employs the RoBERTa-large model, which is fine-tuned using the GoEmotions dataset. In this process, the 27 emotion labels are recategorized to align with their corresponding category labels. Each training batch incorporates examples from different emotional categories, with intra-category pairs considered positives and inter-category pairs considered negatives. This approach fosters efficient model training, generating an embedding space characterized by distinct category clusters. Following the fine-tuning phase, the encoder weights are frozen for use as the text input in our generative model. The original RoBERTa encoder produced overlapping representations for the four emotion categories; however, the fine-tuned version succeeded in delineating more distinct groupings. This result demonstrates that fine-tuning enhances the emotional architecture of the embedding space while preserving the gradual and subjective characteristics of emotions. Visualizations of the embeddings before and after fine-tuning are provided in Figure 4, located in the Appendix, Section C.  The EMOPIA dataset comprises only 1,078 MIDI samples, which is insufficient for a Transformer decoder to effectively learn the patterns in MIDI tokens. To address this limitation, we pre-trained the decoder on the more extensive GiantMIDI-Piano dataset. During pre-training, we substituted zero tensors in place of text embeddings and trained the decoder to predict subsequent MIDI tokens, thereby enabling it to learn the patterns in an auto-regressive manner.

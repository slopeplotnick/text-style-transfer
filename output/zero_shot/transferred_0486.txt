The integration of robotic systems in the construction industry has the potential to address labor shortages while enhancing safety and efficiency. Robots are particularly well-suited for performing heavy, repetitive tasks without fatigue or errors, thereby accelerating construction timelines and reducing costs. However, due to the complex and multifaceted nature of construction work, a single robot is typically insufficient to complete all required tasks. For instance, constructing a wall involves a variety of activities, such as material transportation, precise placement, welding, and quality inspection, each demanding distinct capabilities. Consequently, the deployment of multiple robots collaborating on different tasks is essential.  To manage this complexity, Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) frameworks are employed. MRTA involves decomposing large-scale tasks into smaller, manageable components and assigning them to various robots, based on factors such as individual robot capabilities, task requirements, and the surrounding environment. MRTA problems are generally categorized by considerations such as whether robots are tasked with single or multiple objectives, whether a task requires one or multiple robots, and whether tasks are instantaneous or continuous over time. The most challenging aspect of MRTA lies in coordinating multiple robots to complete numerous tasks over extended durations, which necessitates sophisticated planning that accounts for varying robotic abilities, task demands, and temporal constraints. Despite its applicability to construction, most MRTA studies to date have focused on domains such as warehouse automation and environmental exploration, with limited research addressing its implementation in construction-specific contexts.

In this study, we utilize both auditory and visual stimuli to ascertain which modality more effectively conveys emotional expressions within each sample. Initially, a comprehensive dataset is compiled with human assistance, complemented by a filtered subset, both of which are amalgamated for subsequent training. The mutual information (MI) corresponding to each sample is then calculated to determine the predominant modality in communicating emotions. Consequently, the sequence of the reasoning text is modified accordingly. Each training instance is partitioned into two distinct segments: one dedicated to audio and the other to video, demarcated by specific markers, <aud_desc> and <vis_desc>, indicating their respective modalities. These textual components are arranged based on the MI value; if the auditory modality is more expressive of emotions, the audio component precedes the video component, and vice versa. In cases where both modalities are equally effective, two iterations are generated—one commencing with audio and the other with video—ensuring the model prioritizes the modality that optimally represents emotional content. Subsequent to the initial phase, the model's reasoning capabilities are refined via Modality-Aligned Reward Optimization. Historical approaches have employed reward structures targeted at correct answers and structural coherence, yet these have been inadequate in emphasizing the modality most indicative of emotional content. Therefore, we introduce two novel reward mechanisms: one promoting correct sequence alignment and another enhancing reasoning quality based on the most emotionally expressive input.

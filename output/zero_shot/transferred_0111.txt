The following experimental setup and results reflect the methodologies employed for both solvers and verifiers. In generating outputs for analysis, a configuration with a temperature of 0.7, top-p of 0.9, and a maximum output length of 8192 tokens was utilized. Outputs lacking a boxed answer were excluded from consideration. All computational experiments were executed using the vLLM framework on H200 GPUs. Detailed information regarding the prompt designs and output filtering processes can be found in Appendix F.  Regarding solver performance, a total of 37 models were evaluated across nine distinct datasets. Performance metrics were averaged across tasks and are presented in Figure 1, with task-specific results detailed in Appendix G. Broadly, models with larger capacities exhibited superior performance. Notably, models from the Qwen3 and DeepSeek groups emerged as high-performing, while the Llama3-Base model demonstrated lower effectiveness due to its limited familiarity with the prescribed question-answering formats. Within each model group, scaling trends were observed; Qwen2.5-Base and DeepSeek models showed marked improvements as their capacities were increased, and similar trends were evident across other groups.  Subsequently, we examined the relationship between solver performance and verifier efficacy, as depicted in Figure 2. Specifically, for all 21 post-trained models across the nine datasets, verifier performance was assessed using metrics including accuracy, F1-score, precision, false positive rate (FPR), false negative rate (FNR), and gain for each solver-verifier pairing. In doing so, metrics were disaggregated into three distinct verification scenarios, with results averaged across solvers and datasets for each configuration. Consistent trends were observed whereby verifier accuracy often improved as solver accuracy increased. However, other metrics demonstrated more nuanced patterns. For instance, the false positive rate (FPR) tended to increase during self-verification and when verifying models within the same group, whereas it slightly decreased when verifying outputs from models outside the group. This finding suggests that verifiers with higher solver proficiency are more prone to erroneously mark outputs from their own group—or self-generated outputs—as correct. Additional details concerning F1-score and precision are provided in Appendix H.

In this study, we established pairs of AI judges, specifically GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-pro, to assess the level of agreement in their evaluations of beliefs. Given the extensive dataset available from GPT-4o and the comparatively limited data from other models, GPT-4o was designated as the primary reference for comparative analysis. We further assessed the concordance between AI and human evaluations by engaging human participants to conduct the same belief evaluations as the AI models. Comparative analyses were performed between human evaluations and AI outcomes, such as those from human evaluator 1 and GPT-4o. The comprehensive results of these comparisons are presented in Table 2. Our findings suggest that there is a low likelihood of judge bias influencing the results.   In this paper, we introduce the Martingale Score as a novel metric for assessing the degree to which AI models adhere to specific beliefs. We demonstrate that this score can effectively predict the accuracy of the reasoning process, applying it across various models, prompts, and problems. We also explore factors that exacerbate or mitigate belief entrenchment. A noted limitation is the absence of a demonstrated correlation between the Martingale Score and the Brier Score, a measure of accuracy, within the context of OpenReview. We attribute this to the decision-making processes in OpenReview, which are influenced by potentially inconsistent community votes. However, as shown in Table 1, belief entrenchment appears to be more problematic in contexts requiring human judgment, such as r/ChangeMyView and OpenReview. These findings prompt a reassessment of the application of these AI models in areas where human judgment is crucial.

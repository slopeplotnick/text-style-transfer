The initial experiment conducted within the MODOMA laboratory was premised upon a specific theoretical correlation. This experimental procedure sought to evaluate the efficacy of the MODOMA methodology in facilitating language acquisition through the application of Zipf's law. The findings from this investigation are instrumental in advancing scholarly inquiry, thereby enhancing the MODOMA system through more intricate experimental designs. This approach aims to refine the system's capabilities as a model for language acquisition experiments, enabling improvements prior to the exploration of alternative linguistic learning methodologies.  Although previous research by Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) examines the genesis of languages, it also employs Zipf's law to gain insights into language models vis-à-vis human languages. This research process incorporates essential components and methodologies requisite for conducting more sophisticated language learning assessments. The integration and functionality of the constituent elements within the MODOMA system can be scrutinized and optimized to facilitate advanced computational language learning experiments.  Upon successful language acquisition, the daughter agent discerns the distinction between functional and content categories, utilizing examples of the target language provided by the mother agent during interactive sessions. It is imperative that once the daughter agent apprehends this grammatical differentiation, it promptly applies its newfound grammatical knowledge to modify its linguistic structure, thus generating novel sentences during interactions with the mother agent and comprehending new sentences presented by the latter. Consequently, this study elucidates the potential of the MODOMA laboratory methodology to instigate the acquisition of significant grammatical knowledge, which can be adeptly employed in both language comprehension and production.

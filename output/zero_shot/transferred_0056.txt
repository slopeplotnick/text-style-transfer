The model improves its performance by iteratively learning from its errors. However, the use of an excessive number of irrelevant negative examples may disrupt this process, leading to instability during training. It has been observed that Off-Policy Sequence Masking can stabilize the training process in scenarios where instability would otherwise arise. Keep Routing Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models improve efficiency by activating only a subset of expert components during inference. However, discrepancies between inference and training procedures, coupled with policy shifts, can lead to inconsistencies in expert utilization, even for identical inputs. Such inconsistencies may abruptly alter the set of activated parameters, which can disrupt the optimization process and exacerbate off-policy issues. To address this problem, we propose maintaining the same expert routing paths used in inference during the training phase, ensuring that identical expert parameters are consistently optimized. This "Keep Routing" methodology is instrumental in maintaining the stability of MoE models during reinforcement learning (RL) training and has been incorporated into our RL training pipeline since the DeepSeek-V3-0324 framework.   The DeepSeek-R1 model demonstrated that incorporating systematic reasoning capabilities can significantly enhance the model's ability to resolve complex problems. Building on this finding, we aim to integrate reasoning capabilities in tool-interaction tasks. However, we identified inefficiencies in the approach employed by DeepSeek-R1, which discards reasoning content whenever a subsequent message is introduced during processing. This approach unnecessarily consumes computational resources, as it requires the model to reprocess the entire reasoning chain for every new tool invocation. To mitigate this inefficiency, we developed a specialized context management system for tool-interaction scenarios, as illustrated in Fig. 4. The proposed system incorporates the following principles:   1. Reasoning content is retained throughout the interaction unless a new user-initiated message is introduced. In cases where only tool-related messages (e.g., tool outputs) are added, the existing reasoning content remains preserved.  2. When it becomes necessary to remove reasoning content, only the reasoning traces are discarded, whereas the history of tool calls and corresponding outcomes is retained within the context.  This approach optimizes token usage and reduces the need for redundant processing, thereby enhancing overall system efficiency in tool-interaction tasks.

The prompts were generated in the native language of each linguistic team, despite the interface being in English, thereby eliminating the need for translation. Upon a team member's completion of a predetermined pathway, they subsequently developed the prompt in their primary language. Prior to commencement, all participants engaged in a preliminary sample project to ascertain the requisite tasks. Subsequently, each team was responsible for creating a minimum of 2,000 prompts, following a thorough comprehension of the project objectives. The scripted prompts were derived from materials that were culturally pertinent and tailored to specific domains such as agriculture and healthcare. These included government publications detailing public services, Wikipedia entries in South African languages, a database for precise vocabulary, and translated manuals on animal health and agricultural practices. The prompt data underwent a meticulous cleaning and preparation process to ensure the text was suitable for training speech recognition models. Each phase of this process enhanced the dataset by eliminating irrelevant content, categorizing text into thematic sections, and identifying or redacting personal information. Upon data acquisition, preliminary quality assessments were conducted, which included examinations for structural issues. The text was subsequently filtered to retain only the desired South African languages. Instances where the predominant language diverged from expectations were excluded, although confidence scores were retained as supplementary data.

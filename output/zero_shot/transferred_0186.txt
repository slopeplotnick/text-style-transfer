To address the challenges associated with data attribution in generative models, some approaches adopt model-agnostic techniques, comparing outputs with training data within a shared representational space. These approaches often leverage features derived from pre-trained encoders. While effective and scalable to large datasets, these methods rely on vector representations, which are inherently difficult to interpret. Recent advancements, such as *LatentExplainer*, attempt to mitigate this issue by modifying latent variables and employing language models to elucidate the semantic implications of such changes. However, these methodologies remain opaque to end-users due to their complexity.  In contrast to these intricate approaches, the use of automatically constructed Knowledge Graphs (KGs) provides a more interpretable framework for tracing data influence. By comparing generated content with training data through shared semantic triples—comprising objects, attributes, and stylistic elements—this method aligns more intuitively with human cognition. Large Language Models (LLMs) have become integral to contemporary KG construction processes, particularly in addressing unstructured data. Their contributions can be categorized into three primary areas: (1) generating subject-predicate-object triples from unstructured text, (2) facilitating ontology creation, and (3) translating natural language queries into graph-based queries.  For triple generation, methods such as *AutoKG* enable the extraction of triples that adhere to predefined ontologies. Beyond generating triples, LLMs support ontology development and refinement through techniques like prompt engineering and the reuse of existing ontologies. Furthermore, in query answering tasks, LLMs demonstrate the ability to transform user queries into SPARQL queries, with enhanced performance achieved by leveraging contextual knowledge graphs or employing controlled natural language representations to improve clarity and precision. Surveys on generative KG construction highlight the transition from traditional pipeline-based methods to LLM-driven systems, which exhibit superior performance in entity recognition, relation extraction, and graph completion compared to earlier approaches.  Advances in LLM-based KG methodologies provide a robust foundation for addressing data attribution challenges in generative models. By circumventing reliance on opaque embeddings or model parameters, structured outputs generated by multimodal LLMs enable the creation of interpretable KGs that effectively encapsulate salient features of both generated and training data, while also offering a mechanism to trace data influence. Although current research predominantly focuses on text-based data, these methods hold promise for application in image data domains, despite the additional complexities posed by visual modalities.

In this study, values were assigned in brackets for each prompt based on a constructed dataset of hypothetical participants. Each row in this dataset represented a fabricated individual with varying characteristics such as age, ethnicity, and gender, derived from a UK sample obtained via Prolific. Prolific categorizes ages into specific ranges; thus, a random age was selected within these ranges for each fictional participant. After the creation of these profiles, a straightforward R script was employed to extract survey responses generated by the AI, which were subsequently recorded in a spreadsheet. To ensure a diversity of responses, three distinct prompts were utilized. Following the methodology outlined by Hewitt and colleagues (2024), scores from different prompts for each question were averaged. Detailed descriptions of all the prompt types can be found in the Supplementary Materials of this article. Anticipating that the AI might produce responses in an incorrect format, such as lacking comma-separated values, these erroneous responses were retained to simulate a genuine workflow. Subsequently, they were treated as missing data and replaced by averaging responses from other prompts.  For Studies 1 and 2, pre-existing data was used with prompts structured as follows: "Pretend to be a [Ethnicity] [Gender] of [Age] years from the UK. Answer the questionnaire [...]". In contrast, Studies 3 and 4 involved the validation of new data. The methodology section is divided into two parts: the first detailing the procedures for Studies 1 and 2, and the second outlining the dual-step process for Studies 3 and 4. Hypothesis 3.1 was examined by assessing the correlation between real and synthetic scores using Spearman's correlation coefficient. Hypothesis 3.2 was evaluated through the application of Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC). Hypothesis 4 was tested using the Mann-Whitney U test, Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, and Levene's test to compare different data types. Hypothesis 5 employed the same multigroup Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) method as Hypothesis 2, but exclusively on synthetic data, with gender serving as the grouping variable. All CFA and Multigroup CFA models were executed using the Maximum Likelihood Robust (MLR) estimator, as it is well-suited for non-normally distributed data (Hox et al., 2010).

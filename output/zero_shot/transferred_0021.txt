These findings underscore the Swivuriso dataset's utility as a valuable resource for advancing research in South African speech recognition and for enhancing multilingual speech models.  Existing research emphasizes the critical role of high-quality datasets in achieving accurate and reliable speech recognition systems. However, advancements for African and other low-resource languages have been relatively limited. For instance, Abdulmumin et al. investigated the FLORES dataset to improve its utility for evaluating machine translation performance in four African languages: Hausa, Northern Sotho (Sepedi), Xitsonga, and isiZulu. Their methodology involved engaging native speakers to identify and address errors in the dataset, which could adversely impact downstream tasks such as machine translation. These interventions improved both the accuracy and reliability of the dataset, as evidenced by qualitative reviews and quantitative analyses. This study highlights the importance of incorporating native speaker expertise to ensure datasets align with the linguistic and cultural contexts of the target languages. Inspired by this approach, this work similarly collaborated with native speakers to enhance the accuracy and cultural appropriateness of the Swivuriso dataset.  Another relevant study pertains to sentiment analysis for low-resource languages. While numerous sentiment analysis datasets exist for widely spoken languages, resources for underrepresented languages, such as Hindi, remain scarce. One particular study addressed this gap by developing a sentiment analysis dataset for Hindi, focusing on socially significant topics and ensuring a balanced representation of positive, negative, and neutral sentiment labels. This work underscores the broader need for similar advancements in low-resource language datasets, including speech recognition. The findings from these studies collectively inform the methodology undertaken in this research to refine the Swivuriso dataset, facilitating future development in multilingual and low-resource language speech recognition systems.

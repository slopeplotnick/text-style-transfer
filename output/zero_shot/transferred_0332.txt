Various researchers, such as those cited in studies [25] and [15], have devised methodologies to assess whether artificial intelligence (AI) models conceptualize moral reasoning in a manner akin to human cognition, employing frameworks like MoralBench. These investigations reveal that while AI models can occasionally grasp moral principles, they frequently encounter difficulties when confronted with complex scenarios or conflicting moral paradigms. Nonetheless, these assessments provide only a snapshot of the models' moral reasoning capabilities at a given time, without offering insights into how these capabilities might evolve over time or with additional training.   Further research has explored methodologies for enabling AI to articulate its decision-making processes and align with human values, which is imperative for the advancement of responsible AI. For example, Shitong et al. [9] introduced DeNEVIL, a model designed to facilitate AI learning in moral reasoning without bias. Jiao et al. [16] discussed the evaluation of AI moral reasoning across diverse cultural contexts and the importance of establishing trust, thereby highlighting the interdependence of AI ethics on both technological and societal dimensions. Although these approaches are effective in elucidating and fostering trust, they often rely on predefined moral categories and manual labeling, which poses challenges in adapting to dynamic moral contexts.  Despite the contributions of previous research toward fairness assessments and value alignment, there remains an unmet need for a flexible evaluation mechanism that transcends specific models and provides dynamic moral assessments over time. Current methodologies focus on evaluating ethical considerations at discrete intervals but lack the capability to autonomously generate, comprehend, and adapt to evolving moral scenarios. Additionally, no existing approaches offer tools to measure disparities in AI ethical reasoning or to monitor moral consistency across models in real time.   Drawing upon the concepts outlined in Section 1, the newly proposed Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP) represents a system designed to continuously evaluate AI moral reasoning without reliance on a fixed dataset, with the objective of measuring, understanding, and enhancing the moral stability of large language models. MoCoP prioritizes evaluation and comprehension rather than model improvement or retraining, thereby ensuring clarity and consistency in its application.

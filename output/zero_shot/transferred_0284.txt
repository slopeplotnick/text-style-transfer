The RAIR benchmark comprises 63,601 samples drawn from 14 distinct e-commerce domains. It is subdivided into three components: the General component, containing 46,775 samples; the Long-Tail Hard component, consisting of 10,931 samples; and the Visual Salience component, comprising 5,895 samples. Each entry in this dataset includes a query, item title, item details, stock-keeping unit (SKU) information, and an anonymized item image. Additionally, each entry features a manually assigned ground truth label derived from a four-level relevance scale ranging from L1 to L4. To facilitate model development and error analysis, each data entry is accompanied by rule identifiers from our Rule Checklist, which serve to evaluate its relevance.  In this section, we proceed with an evaluation and analysis of the methodologies proposed, utilizing the RAIR benchmark. Our experimental design encompasses two primary components: firstly, a quantitative investigation addressing two key questions: (1) the extent of model improvement attributable to fine-tuning; (2) the advantages conferred by the LORE model with variations such as general, hard, and visually salient samples. Secondly, we demonstrate, through examples, the novel capabilities our methods enable within the model as well as the specific challenges they address. To ensure the stability and reproducibility of evaluation results, all trained models are subject to a deterministic decoding approach during single-pass inference, devoid of stochastic elements.

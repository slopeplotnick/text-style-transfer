This study investigates the impact of various prompts on the degree to which a model adheres to its initial beliefs and its subsequent accuracy. A prior-conforming prompt tends to reinforce the model's initial beliefs, serving as a baseline for evaluating both belief persistence and accuracy. Conversely, a critical-thinking prompt encourages the model to be more receptive to revising its beliefs upon encountering new evidence. A comprehensive list of the prompts employed in this study can be found in Appendix C.   Our findings indicate that a model's excessive adherence to its initial beliefs, quantified by the Martingale Score, is associated with a decline in accuracy, supporting the hypothesis that belief entrenchment poses a significant problem. Subsequently, we examined various models and configurations to identify factors contributing to belief entrenchment, such as model type, prompt type, and reasoning processes. The validity of using large language models (LLMs) as evaluative judges was also assessed.   The study observed that belief entrenchment is more pronounced in contexts involving subjective topics, such as the r/ChangeMyView forum, compared to more objective domains like Forecasting. This suggests that belief entrenchment poses a greater challenge in areas where it is more difficult to objectively assess the model's accuracy. Efforts to mitigate belief entrenchment through the use of different types of prompts revealed that the prior-conforming prompt exacerbates belief entrenchment, while the critical-thinking prompt mitigates it in the Forecasting domain. However, this pattern did not extend to r/ChangeMyView or OpenReview. Further details on the factors influencing belief entrenchment are provided in Appendix B.  Additionally, we demonstrate that belief entrenchment adversely affects accuracy, given that the objective of reasoning within LLMs is to ascertain the truth. This was tested in domains with known correct answers, such as Forecasting and OpenReview. While metrics like the Brier Score can quantify the model's performance, they do not elucidate the underlying reasons for its performance. Therefore, we also examined reasoning metrics. The analysis revealed that higher Martingale Scores, indicative of greater belief entrenchment, are correlated with poorer Brier Scores in Forecasting, suggesting that belief entrenchment is a contributing factor to the failure of reasoning in LLMs.

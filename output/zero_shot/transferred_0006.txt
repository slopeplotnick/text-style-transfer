In addressing the findings of RQ2, our analysis revealed that Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV) are highly complementary, with distinct failure modes. This raises the question in RQ3: Can these systems be combined effectively to create a robust hybrid framework? To explore this, we propose two integration strategies: *Score-Level Fusion* and an *Evidence-Aware Pipeline*. The latter strategy is specifically designed to address limitations in FV systems identified in our study.  The *Evidence-Aware Pipeline* distinguishes itself by addressing performance weaknesses inherent in FV systems when dealing with instances labeled as "Not Enough Information" (NEI). While the Score-Level Fusion approach enhances overall performance by combining system outputs, it does not directly mitigate the specific challenges highlighted in Section 4.5. Typically, FV systems function effectively when sufficient external evidence can be retrieved to classify claims as "Supported" or "Contradicted." However, they exhibit deficiencies in scenarios where no supportive or contradictory evidence is available, leading to NEI outputs.   To alleviate these limitations, the *Evidence-Aware Pipeline* introduces a two-step process that integrates external evidence retrieval with conditional decision-making:  1. **Step 1: Retrieval and Verification** – An FV module (e.g., LLM-QA) retrieves external evidence and assesses whether the claim aligns with the evidence, categorizing the output as *Supported*, *Contradicted*, or *NEI*.     2. **Step 2: Conditional Branching** – A decision-making mechanism determines the next action based on the output of the FV module. If the module outputs a definitive judgment (*Supported* or *Contradicted*), this classification is adopted. However, in cases where the output is NEI, the pipeline acknowledges the absence of external evidence and bypasses reliance on FV. Instead, HD techniques (e.g., LNPE) are employed, relying solely on internal uncertainty signals to generate the final prediction.  This Evidence-Aware Pipeline offers a tailored solution to address FV’s shortcomings in handling NEI cases. By decoupling the reliance on external evidence from internal uncertainty signals, the pipeline reduces the risk of errors stemming from unreliable evidence retrieval. As a result, the hybrid system enhances detection performance, even in scenarios where external knowledge is unavailable or insufficient.

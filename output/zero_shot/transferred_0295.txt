When undertaking video editing in accordance with specified requirements, it is essential to ensure preservation of the original video’s temporal dynamics. This involves maintaining precise synchronization of minute details such as blinks or head turns to the original timing. The significance of this precision is heightened in videos featuring spoken dialogue, where even minor discrepancies can result in incongruous visual output. Typically, desired modifications are subtle alterations, such as the addition of a mustache or modification of facial expressions. The objective is to implement only the necessary adjustments while maintaining the remainder of the video unchanged. Achieving this level of synchronization presents challenges for conventional video editing tools.   In response to these challenges, we have developed a novel approach termed Sync-LoRA, designed to produce video edits that remain temporally congruent with the original footage. Our method involves altering merely the initial frame of the video and employing a text prompt to dictate the desired modifications. The model is trained using pairs of videos: one representing the original and the other the edited version. These video pairs are crafted to ensure perfect alignment in terms of movement while differing in the edited elements. To create such pairs, an initial generation is followed by an evaluation of synchronization. The process utilizes a model proficient in the interpretation of both visual and textual data to generate varied conceptual modifications, subsequently converting these into video sequences. These sequences often lack perfect alignment; therefore, human motion data, encompassing facial expressions and body movements, is employed for verification of synchronization. The evaluation focuses on congruence in aspects such as speech timing, ocular movement, blinking, and bodily positioning, retaining only the most accurately matched examples for the training process.  Through the utilization of these well-aligned exemplars, our model effectively learns to retain the temporal dynamics of the original video while applying specified alterations, ensuring synchronization with the initial footage.

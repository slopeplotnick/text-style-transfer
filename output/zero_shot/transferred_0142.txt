Traditional Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) methods have demonstrated efficacy across various domains by employing optimization techniques such as linear programming, genetic algorithms, and reinforcement learning (RL), all of which are grounded in mathematical frameworks and supported by empirically validated results. However, the application of these traditional approaches within construction site environments presents significant challenges. Construction sites are characterized by dynamic, unstructured, and highly unpredictable conditions, which hinder the effective implementation of conventional MRTA methodologies. Moreover, the inherent complexity and vast amount of data involved in construction tasks exceed the computational and conceptual capacity of many existing mathematics-based optimization methods, necessitating the development of more adaptive and flexible solutions.  Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have revealed promising capabilities in natural language understanding, logical reasoning, and decision-making that are adaptable to dynamic circumstances. Such attributes position LLMs as potentially transformative tools for addressing limitations associated with traditional optimization and learning-based approaches. Specifically, LLM-driven coordination introduces enhanced adaptability to the multifaceted and evolving demands of construction environments while also facilitating seamless interaction between human operators and robotic systems. Recent implementations of LLMs in planning tasks, digital twin integration, and multi-agent coordination have yielded promising results. However, current LLM-based frameworks lack mechanisms for systematic performance evaluation in comparison to traditional methods, as well as strategies for addressing reasoning errors and improving reliability through iterative validation.  To address these deficiencies, this study introduces the LangGraph-based Task Allocation Agent (LTAA) framework, a novel approach to LLM-driven coordination that incorporates advanced strategies alongside structured validation mechanisms not present in earlier setups. The LTAA framework is designed to optimize task allocation by balancing workloads efficiently without compromising overall performance. Furthermore, its integrated validation system ensures the consistency of decision-making processes through structured feedback loops that identify and rectify allocation errors. By bridging the gaps in performance assessment and error correction, the LTAA framework represents an important advancement in the application of LLMs to complex and dynamic construction environments.

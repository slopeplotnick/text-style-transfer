To enhance the processing of lengthy video content, several methods under the framework of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) have been developed. These methods prioritize identifying and utilizing the salient segments of a video—such as specific frames or clips—by leveraging textual and visual cues, rather than selecting frames arbitrarily. This targeted approach enables models to concentrate on the key components of video data. Recent advancements have introduced graph-based techniques that aim to establish relationships and connections between different elements of a video, facilitating a more comprehensive understanding. However, many of these models exhibit an over-reliance on textual information or simplistic visual-text matching mechanisms, which limits their ability to effectively comprehend and reason through complex, extended video narratives.  To address these limitations, memory-based approaches have been proposed to systematically organize and store information extracted from videos. For instance, EgoRAG structures textual memories derived from videos to enable contextual understanding of activities spanning an entire day. Building upon this, Ego-R1 incorporates visual tools and step-by-step reasoning strategies to handle tasks associated with longer temporal trajectories. Similarly, HippoMM employs a dual-memory framework, integrating summary-based memory with multimodal representations. M3-Agent introduces a memory system centered on entities and utilizes sequential reasoning to retrieve relevant information efficiently. Although these advancements represent significant progress, existing methods still face challenges in achieving robust integration of textual and visual modalities and adapting to varying temporal dynamics of video data, particularly in complex scenarios.  To overcome these challenges, we propose WorldMM, an innovative system designed to leverage both textual and visual information from videos in constructing a memory framework for enhanced comprehension and reasoning with long video sequences. The system operates through a three-step process: first, it formulates multiple types of memory representations, including two text-based memories and one visual memory. Subsequently, it employs a retrieval mechanism to extract pertinent information from these memory structures across varying timeframes until sufficient data is gathered to address a query. Finally, this synthesized information is utilized to generate a coherent, contextually relevant response. WorldMM represents a significant advancement in the integration of multimodal information and addresses the limitations of previous methods in reasoning through extended video narratives.

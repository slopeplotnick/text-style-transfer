MODOMA is an established framework designed for conducting language learning experiments, offering adjustable parameters to customize the experimental conditions. This system comprehensively addresses various aspects of language acquisition, including those relevant to adults, children, and the underlying linguistic processes. Users have the ability to modify settings corresponding to each of these components. Table 1 delineates the parameters relevant to the current set of experiments.  MODOMA is capable of executing a variety of language learning processes, such as those described by Shakouri et al. in 2025. The initial parameter in Table 1 pertains to the activation of functional category learning, which is enabled throughout the experiments under discussion. Subsequent parameters are critical in shaping the experimental framework. Specifically, the parameter for the mother agent dictates the structure of Delilah's linguistic output, determining whether it forms complete sentences or noun phrases. This parameter is varied across both the training and testing experiments presented in this study.  Additionally, the amount of data required by the daughter agent before initiating the learning of functional and content categories differs across experiments. These two parameters are foundational to configuring the experiments. In the training phase, the learning process for functional and content categories commences post the processing of 1,000 noun phrases and 1,000 sentences, subsequently increasing to 10,000 noun phrases and 10,000 sentences. This approach aims to determine the requisite quantity of examples needed from Delilah to discern underlying linguistic patterns.  Furthermore, the parameter governing the learning of functional categories determines the ratio employed to convert distinctions between function and content words into explicit linguistic knowledge. The training sessions outlined in this study are utilized to refine these parameter values, while the testing experiments serve to validate these parameters against new data obtained from Delilah.

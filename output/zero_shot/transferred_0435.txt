Explanations in Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) must adhere to two fundamental principles: clarity and fidelity to the model. Clarity requires that explanations be comprehensible to the intended audience, typically necessitating conciseness and directness. Fidelity, on the other hand, demands that explanations accurately reflect the model’s operations without introducing biases, which can occur if the scope of the explanation is deliberately constrained. Although clarity and fidelity are distinct objectives, enhancing one often complicates the achievement of the other. For instance, explanations that align with an individual’s preconceived notions may be perceived as more comprehensible, yet this can result in bias if it influences the selection of explanations. Moreover, if a precise explanation is misunderstood, it poses the risk of misinforming the recipient. This study proposes a post-hoc explanation approach aimed at achieving both clarity and fidelity. The proposed method advocates for the use of simple language and causal reasoning, mirroring typical human explanatory practices. This strategy ensures that explanations are understood as intended and obviates the need for additional interpretive guidelines, thus simplifying the explanatory process. The emphasis on straightforward language is crucial, as the datasets utilized by models may not align with the optimal modes of explanation. This approach falls within the realm of Concept-based XAI methodologies, predicated on the understanding that while models process data in forms such as images and graphs, human communication predominantly employs meaningful concepts.

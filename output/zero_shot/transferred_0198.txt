Developing an explanation model often presents significant challenges, particularly in determining the structural equations for a Fully Specified Causal Model (FSCM). In Section 5.2, the findings include standard Partially Specified Causal Model (PSCM) intervals for counterfactual queries, highlighting the limitations that arise when causal mechanisms are not fully understood. For certain queries, these intervals are narrow, suggesting that the midpoint of the interval or approximate inferences using a Causal Bayesian Network (CBN) may provide reasonable estimates of the true value, as the precise causal model has limited influence in such cases. However, this observation does not hold universally. Specifically, for queries reliant on rare events, the counterfactual probability can vary substantially, ranging from 0 to 1, depending on the specific FSCM employed. Consequently, two distinct FSCMs consistent with the observed data may yield entirely contradictory explanations for the same phenomena, with each potentially asserting certainty in opposing outcomes.   Explaining rare observations effectively is particularly critical, especially in contexts where predictive models must operate in domains with sparse training data. Therefore, the use of an FSCM remains essential to ensure that all relevant queries can be computed accurately. Incorporating expert knowledge to define non-standard PSCMs can potentially reduce interval widths, thereby enabling more precise approximations for a larger range of queries. Future research should examine the performance of various methodologies for both exact and approximate calculations within this framework.  The explanations provided in this work focus on evaluating the probability that specific concept interventions are sufficient to produce particular outcomes. Nevertheless, the proposed framework is not confined to sufficiency; future investigations could explore the probabilities of necessity as well as other types of explanatory constructs.

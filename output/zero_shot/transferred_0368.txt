The complexity of a problem is associated with the level of difficulty it presents; however, improvements in verifier performance demonstrate clear positive correlations both within the same category and across different categories. The AIME dataset is particularly notable in the final chart, possibly due to similar challenges encountered by some models during training. The trend lines representing verifier accuracy reveal two distinct clusters of tasks (indicated in red and blue), suggesting that the verification difficulty is not solely dependent on solver accuracy. This raises the question of whether certain tasks are inherently easier to verify than others. Song et al. (2025) observed that models exhibit limited improvement on tasks requiring factual recall, although they show progress on tasks like Tokoku when there is sufficient pretraining. A similar dichotomy is evident in Figure 7: datasets such as AIME, GSM8K, 3SAT, and Tokoku exhibit a higher ratio of verifier accuracy to solver accuracy, and they perform better across all verification settings. To elucidate this phenomenon, we note that in our synthetic datasets, Selenium and 3SAT exemplify problems that require extensive time to solve but can be quickly verified. Conversely, verifying the result of a matrix multiplication lacks a straightforward approach without essentially recomputing the operation. In the real-world datasets, GSM8K and AIME involve problems solvable with high-school mathematics, whereas MMLU (Social Sciences) requires domain-specific knowledge, CommonsenseQA relies on general world knowledge, and GPQA and MMLU (STEM) demand specialized scientific knowledge. For the latter tasks, verifying an answer mandates the same level of expertise as solving the problem itself. In summary, although tasks that are straightforward to solve are generally easier to verify, certain tasks are intrinsically more amenable to verification. These include synthetic problems that involve logical or structured reasoning (such as 3SAT and Sudoku) and real-world tasks that predominantly utilize mathematical reasoning rather than extensive factual recall (such as GSM8K and AIME). Furthermore, these tasks demonstrate significant gains from test-time rejection sampling with verifiers.

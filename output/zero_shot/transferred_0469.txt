Probabilistic programming languages constitute a category of programming languages that integrate functionalities for managing probabilistic elements, such as distributions and their associated sampling mechanisms. Numerous established examples exist across both imperative and functional paradigms. A significant limitation of current probabilistic programming languages is their inadequate compatibility with graph structures that are rich in textual data. Such graphs are crucial for defining specifications related to language model systems (LMS). This limitation has been a motivating factor in the development of LUMOS. Probabilistic programming languages typically generate outputs based on probability distributions specified by users. For instance, certain languages are employed to generate random images or scenes, which are essential for applications such as autonomous vehicles, using user-defined scene graphs. However, to establish real-world specifications, a fixed graph is utilized, from which components are sampled to define various scenarios. Domain-specific languages (DSLs) for large language models (LLMs) function as query languages devoid of probabilistic features. They assist in the composition, optimization, and security of prompts designed to elicit specific responses from a target LMS. These DSLs operate in conjunction with LUMOS, which assesses LMS behaviors over distributions of prompts specified by LUMOS. Aligning LMS involves identifying vulnerabilities in LMS through performance testing or adversarial attacks. Recent advancements have led to the development of probabilistic certifiers for specific properties of LLMs, providing formal assurances in evaluating LMS characteristics. In this study, we extend the certification of LMS with LUMOS to specify and certify a broad array of desirable properties.

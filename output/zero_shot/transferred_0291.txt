We have developed a novel standard, termed HEALTHCONTRADICT, which employs quantitative metrics to evaluate the efficacy of language models in processing lengthy and contradictory medical information. This standard surpasses contemporary medical question-answering benchmarks in demonstrating the performance variability among different models. Our empirical evaluations demonstrate that language models with expertise in medical domains exhibit superior capabilities in appropriately utilizing correct information, disregarding incorrect data, and resolving conflicting information. To the best of our knowledge, the TREC Health Misinformation tracks represent the sole publicly available resource comprising expert-generated health questions with verified answers and scientific substantiation, alongside document pools containing both corroborative and contradictory evidence as assessed by experts. These components are essential for the formulation of health questions with definitive answers, employing pairs of conflicting documents to assess language models' proficiency in handling medical knowledge contradictions.  Within the HEALTHCONTRADICT framework, we scrutinize significant and reliable documents that underscore their efficacy in substantiating the answer to a given inquiry. We standardized labels by transforming the 2019 designations "effective"/"ineffective" and the 2021 labels "supportive"/"dissuades" to "yes"/"no," aligning with the 2022 labels. We further propose that if two documents addressing the same question espouse opposing views—one affirmative, the other negative—they constitute a contradictory pair. Additionally, a document is deemed correct if its viewpoint aligns with scientific evidence, and incorrect if it contradicts such evidence. The original datasets comprise 130 expert-verified questions (50 from 2019, 35 from 2021, and 45 from 2022), encompassing questions with binary views and those supported by both corroborating and opposing documents. Application of this criterion reduces the dataset to 110 questions. We then pair supporting and opposing documents into contradiction pairs, ensuring each document's unique utilization due to potential associations with multiple questions. Questions that do not facilitate the creation of at least one contradiction pair, owing to an insufficiency of unique documents, are excluded. This process culminates in a refined dataset of 81 questions and 920 pairs of conflicting documents.

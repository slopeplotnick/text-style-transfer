This paper presents an energy consumption model for statically typed programming languages executed on the Java Virtual Machine (JVM). The model analyzes the impact of various factors, such as primitive data types, data sizes, operations, and diverse hardware configurations, on energy usage. It also highlights the challenge of predicting energy consumption due to the variability observed even among devices with identical hardware specifications. The structure of the paper is as follows: Section 2 provides an overview of Bayesian statistics. Section 3 details the methodology employed in the study. Section 4 describes the implementation of the proposed method. Section 5 addresses the testing procedures and discusses any limitations encountered. Section 6 presents the results of the experiments, while Section 7 offers an analysis of these findings. Section 8 reviews related literature, and Section 9 concludes the paper.  Bayesian statistics serves as a framework for incorporating prior knowledge to make probabilistic inferences about unobservable phenomena. Unlike frequentist statistics, which predominates in computer science and relies solely on observed data while minimizing assumptions, Bayesian statistics explicitly incorporates and clarifies assumptions, allowing them to be evaluated. The choice of Bayesian statistics in this study is motivated by two factors: firstly, its capacity to make assumptions transparent and comprehensible; secondly, its ability to provide a detailed prediction of energy consumption by offering a range of possible outcomes, rather than a single point estimate typically offered by frequentist approaches. This paper assumes that energy consumption follows a normal distribution, constrained by a range established through previous research. This initial range serves as a prior, which is subsequently refined using a Bayesian algorithm to align more closely with observed data. In scenarios with limited data availability, initial estimates may significantly influence results, potentially leading to inaccuracies. However, as data accrues, the algorithm can correct initial assumptions and improve the model's alignment with empirical data.

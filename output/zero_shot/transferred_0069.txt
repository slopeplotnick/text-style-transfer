In our study, we conducted a comparative analysis of our proposed method against a previously established approach known as RePro. Due to the unavailability of the RePro code, we utilized the outcome data as reported in the original RePro publication. Our method, termed P2C (Paper2Code), was evaluated against Refine, which requires manual crafting of individualized prompts for the evaluation and enhancement of each component of the process. For instance, the original Paper2Code framework necessitated the creation of eight specialized prompts for four distinct planning tasks. Our methodology circumvents this labor-intensive requirement by employing an automated system prompt that adapts to each task without the need for additional prompt design.  We conducted experiments using two datasets. The first dataset, Paper2CodeBench, comprises 90 papers selected from leading 2024 conferences (such as ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS), chosen based on code availability and manageable length. We evaluated the code generated by our method against the official code released by the authors, employing AI judges to assess correctness on a scale from 1 to 5. The second dataset, PaperBench Code-Dev, consists of 20 papers from ICML 2024; human reviewers appraised the correctness of the generated code according to predefined rubrics.  To our knowledge, few methodologies incorporate self-refinement for the automatic conversion of research papers into code. We thus compared our approach with two analogous methods that employ self-refinement: the original Paper2Code combined with Self-Refine, an iterative method. Our experiments exclusively utilized the GPT-4.1 model. For RePro, we referenced results from their publication due to the lack of available code.  The performance of various optimization strategies on the two datasets is documented in Tables I and II. Our method demonstrated consistent reliability across different datasets. Notably, even when optimization was confined to the planning phase, our approach (Paper2Code with autoplan optimization) exhibited consistent enhancements. A task-specific analysis revealed superior overall performance of our method, as illustrated in Figure 2, which depicts the method's efficacy across tasks in PaperBench. Our planning optimization outperformed Self-Refine in 16 out of 20 tasks (80%), indicating its robustness across diverse scenarios. In contrast, Self-Refine exhibited negative results in 10 tasks, particularly underperforming in certain specific tasks.

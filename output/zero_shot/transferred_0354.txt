Achieving lifelike movement in virtual characters is crucial for enhancing the realism of video games and virtual reality environments. Similarly, in robotics, natural movement is significant as it enhances safety and optimizes energy consumption. Understanding human motion provides valuable insights for robots, enabling them to acquire skills applicable to a variety of contexts. To generate realistic character animations, researchers employ data-driven techniques that utilize empirical motion data. These techniques often incorporate models such as autoregressive models, variational autoencoders, or diffusion models to produce convincing animations. However, one limitation of these approaches is their frequent deviation from physical principles, resulting in unrealistic movements, particularly in novel scenarios. To address this, physics-based methods aim to produce movements consistent with real-world physics through the application of control policies and dynamic equations. Techniques such as trajectory optimization and reinforcement learning are commonly employed in these methods. Traditionally, non-data-driven methods were capable of generating robotic motion, but the results often lacked naturalness. Replicating human-like robotic motion solely based on estimations of movement is challenging. Consequently, contemporary approaches have shifted towards data-driven strategies, including model predictive control and trajectory optimization, to effectively emulate human movements. Recent advancements in reinforcement learning have contributed to more realistic and agile movements by replicating real motion clips. However, these reinforcement learning-based methods are frequently constrained by the specific actions depicted in the clips, limiting the robot's ability to perform novel tasks. To overcome this limitation, some systems integrate specialized planners or high-level policies to select optimal movements or skills tailored to the task requirements.

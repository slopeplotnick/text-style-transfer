Addressing inquiries within the realm of elderly services poses significant challenges due to the necessity of providing accurate and secure responses derived from diverse information sources. These inquiries frequently pertain to healthcare regulations, welfare programs, and daily guidance, and the provision of inaccurate information can have detrimental consequences. While conventional language models excel in general contexts, they often lack the precision and nuanced awareness required for these sensitive subjects. Current models typically rely on either structured or unstructured data, restricting their capacity to furnish comprehensive answers. Furthermore, most models lack mechanisms to regulate the influence of external information on responses and do little to ensure the safety of these responses, thereby diminishing their efficacy in elderly services.   To address these challenges, we propose KARMA, a domain-adaptive question-answering system. KARMA employs a dual-encoder architecture to integrate knowledge from multiple sources, a memory unit to select relevant information, and a safety-focused decoder to prevent erroneous answers. Training of this system emphasizes a balance between linguistic fluency, knowledge congruence, and safety. Building on the LLaMA-7B foundation with specialized tuning, KARMA demonstrates adaptability and robust performance. Empirical evaluations on a dedicated dataset for elderly-service questions reveal that KARMA surpasses other models in terms of accuracy, relevance, and safety, rendering it a viable solution for practical application in elderly care.  Mo et al. highlighted the significance of knowledge transfer across heterogeneous data types, underscoring the necessity of integrating diverse information sources in complex question-answering scenarios. Huang et al. introduced MetaMath-LLaMA, featuring an adept scheduler, symbolic parsing, and a hybrid approach combining symbolic and neural methodologies for enhanced multi-step reasoning clarity. Incorporating these techniques into KARMA yields procedural transparency that augments the efficacy of memory unit gating and safety control. Luo discussed TriMedTune, a tuning approach for brain CT employing visual prompts, medical terminology alignment, and knowledge distillation with uncertainty checks. Integration of these strategies into KARMA would enhance term utilization and improve the system's capacity to manage uncertain responses, ensuring factual integrity and the secure rejection of sensitive queries.

The comparative performance of the Cov_tgsp and CSP methods demonstrates their effectiveness across various datasets, as evidenced by the data presented in Table 2 and Figure 3. However, differences in performance are observed depending on the specific dataset under consideration. When evaluating individual datasets, Cov_tgsp and CSP consistently achieve strong results; nonetheless, their performance is notably lower on the PhysionetMI dataset relative to Cho2017 and Zhou2016. These discrepancies may stem from variations in experimental protocols, such as differences in sampling frequency, the number of electrodes utilized, and participant population sizes. This indicates that the efficacy of these methods for decoding motor imagery from EEG signals is highly dataset-dependent.  For instance, on the Zhou2016 dataset, the combination of CSP with StandardScaler and logistic regression achieved the highest classification score (0.87), with Cov_tgsp paired with RobustScaler and logistic regression producing a comparable performance (0.84). Similarly, on the Cho2017 dataset, Cov_tgsp outperformed CSP, achieving a peak score of 0.74 compared to 0.68. In contrast, methods incorporating instantaneous functional connectivity and non-linear features yielded inferior results across all datasets. For example, the use of svd_entropy with RobustScaler and logistic regression achieved scores of 0.68 on Zhou2016, 0.59 on Cho2017, and 0.54 on PhysionetMI.   On the PhysionetMI dataset, performance was generally lower across all methods. The best configuration, which employed Cov_tgsp, attained a maximum score of only 0.64, while CSP averaged 0.62 across frequency bands. These findings suggest that, although non-linear and connectivity-based feature extraction methods can capture intricate EEG signal patterns, they may not be optimal for motor imagery classification in these contexts.  Further insights can be drawn from the analysis of individual subjects. Across datasets, CSP and Cov_tgsp generally exhibit superior performance, as illustrated in Figures 5 and 6 for the Cho2017 and Zhou2016 datasets. However, this trend is less consistent for the PhysionetMI dataset (Figure 7), where these methods do not universally outperform alternatives. In some cases, alternative methods yielded higher classification scores; for example, Hjorth parameter-based features outperformed both CSP and Cov_tgsp for subjects #57 and #59, while the combination of instantaneous connectivity measures with TGSP demonstrated superior performance for subjects #17 and #73.

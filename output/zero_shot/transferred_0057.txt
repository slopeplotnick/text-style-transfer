In this study, we employed identical adversarial training techniques on both Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs) to investigate the impact of such training on the transferability of adversarial attacks. Contrary to the intuitive expectation that adversarially robust models would generate attacks with reduced transferability, our findings indicate the opposite. Models subjected to adversarial training produced attacks with enhanced transferability compared to those developed through standard training methodologies. This observation suggests that the features learned by these models may engender shared vulnerabilities across various model architectures, as illustrated in Figure 1. Specifically, we conducted extensive adversarial training on a total of 36 CNN and ViT models. We intend to make all trained models and the corresponding code available on GitHub to facilitate replication and further research by other scholars. Our results demonstrate that adversarially trained models exhibit improved attack transferability, which is both unexpected and challenges existing perceptions of model robustness.  We introduce a novel attack technique, termed Momentum Integrated Gradient (MIG), which enhances the traditional integrated gradient (IG) approach. MIG iteratively refines the attack process through a momentum-based method informed by IG, strategically targeting critical features identified by IG, thereby increasing the likelihood of misclassification. A defining characteristic of IG is its implementation invariance property, which enables MIG attacks to be effective across diverse model architectures, such as between CNNs and ViTs. This cross-model efficacy was unattainable with previous attacks like Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), as depicted in Figure 2. IG serves as a tool for elucidating the features most influential to a model's predictions by aggregating gradients from a baseline to the actual input, where the baseline serves as a reference point.

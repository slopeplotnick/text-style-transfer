Language model systems, encompassing large language models and other variants, are employed across a diverse array of applications, including medical chatbots, autonomous vehicle assistants, and programming aids. Given their deployment in critical and public-facing functions, it is imperative that these systems operate correctly and conform to societal expectations. A substantial body of research has focused on enhancing the safety of these systems and embedding safety features within them. However, existing methodologies for verifying and ensuring desirable behavior remain inadequate, as they fail to provide comprehensive guarantees across all scenarios. Such assurances are essential for fostering public trust and confidence in these technologies.  Recent scholarly efforts have begun to explore the formal certification of these systems for specific positive behaviors, such as fairness, accuracy in responding to inquiries, and safety. These behaviors are codified into explicit guidelines that the systems must adhere to. For instance, researchers have developed guidelines pertaining to fairness in responses, comprehension of textual materials, management of sensitive conversations, and appropriate tool selection. Nevertheless, these certifications are inherently inflexible and challenging to adapt for different requirements or contexts, as they are narrowly focused on particular attributes and primarily applicable in conversational environments. Modifying these guidelines necessitates substantial rewriting. Consequently, the current approach to certifying these systems involves bespoke solutions for each attribute, domain, and system type, a practice that is presently under reevaluation.

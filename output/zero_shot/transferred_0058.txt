The Phase Field (PF) model provides a framework for analyzing the temporal evolution of systems comprising multiple materials and phases, ultimately progressing towards equilibrium. This model is particularly effective for small-scale systems and short-duration processes, making it highly applicable to the fabrication of solar cells. The thermodynamic properties of the system are encapsulated within the Gibbs free energy functional. Our approach examines the formation processes of solar cells, emphasizing critical aspects such as solvent evaporation, liquid phase separation, and crystallization. The driving forces leading the system toward equilibrium are derived from the Gibbs free energy functional.  Phase transitions, such as drying and crystallization, are described using a set of equations that also account for mass transport phenomena. During our simulations, we ensured compatibility of the PF model with computational fluid dynamics tools that address mass transport influenced by fluid flow. For a comprehensive description of our simulation methodologies, please refer to Section S2.   Furthermore, the material properties obtained are utilized to analyze the propagation of light within solar cells. Depending on the complexity of the solar cell structures, we employ either detailed computational simulations or more simplified approaches. The solar cell is conceptualized as a stratified assembly of materials, employing a model that facilitates the understanding of light interaction effects. Additionally, a neural network is employed to forecast charge generation as a function of light incidence angle, active layer thickness, and light wavelength. Detailed information regarding this model is provided in Section S3.

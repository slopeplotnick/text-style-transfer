The execution of faulty code is essential for the effectiveness of our methodology, as errors must be present in at least one failing test to be identified. Lines of code that are never executed cannot be diagnosed for potential errors. Consequently, insufficient test coverage may result in undetected issues within the code base. To address this limitation, we employ multiple failing tests alongside rapid static analysis techniques.  Regarding bounded model checking (BMC), our approach involves utilizing constrained program models. Limitations such as loop iterations and depth boundaries may exclude certain behaviors, potentially preventing the identification of valid errors due to these restrictions. Moreover, issues identified within the restricted model might not correspond to those present in the complete program. Although we carefully establish these boundaries and disclose them with our results, they inherently represent a drawback in our approach.  In terms of pointers, arrays, and undefined behavior, as discussed in Section 5.1, CFAULTS leverages tools such as CPPCHECK and CLANG-TIDY to detect issues like uninitialized variables and runtime errors, including division by zero. Upon identification of such issues, CFAULTS halts execution and informs the user. Additionally, it employs CBMC’s memory-safety checks to identify improper memory operations, such as out-of-bounds access and unsafe pointer arithmetic. However, CFAULTS adheres to CBMC’s interpretation of ANSI C standards, accepting CBMC's determinations as valid, which may result in missed errors if CBMC fails to flag them. Future work will focus on enhancing CBMC's configuration to better align with the intended C language standards.  For external validity, this methodology was evaluated using benchmark and student programs that successfully compile and possess explicit specifications, including assertions or expected outputs. Extending this approach to larger programs with extensive input/output operations, concurrency elements, complex libraries, or specifications articulated in advanced formats may necessitate further refinements and could potentially impact the results obtained in this study.

Researchers are continually striving to enhance the efficacy of content generation by developing advanced methodologies. Techniques such as Mirror-Consistency, Self-Contrast, and Step-Back Prompting involve iterative verification and multi-perspective analysis to improve output quality. Another approach, known as Self-Check, employs a stepwise process to identify and rectify errors. While these methods primarily aim to refine the generator's performance, they often necessitate increased computational resources and time. Our research, however, diverges by focusing on the synthesis of output. Rather than attempting to rectify flawed outputs from the generator, we accept them in their original form and employ an innovative aggregation layer to determine the most accurate result.  A critical consideration when utilizing a language model as an adjudicator for comparative analysis is the decision to either permit a neutral (tie) evaluation or compel a definitive choice. Initially, our findings indicate that enforcing a selection can introduce bias into the model's decision-making process. Subsequently, we demonstrate that allowing a "tie" decision is highly contingent upon the configuration of the adjudicator, necessitating an improved strategy to address this issue. The allowance for ties contributes to bias reduction. Language models functioning as evaluators are susceptible to various biases, including positional bias, where the sequence of options influences the decision. Through experimentation, we adjusted the configuration to permit tie responses, which significantly mitigated positional bias across both models under examination. This indicates that the option to declare a tie extends beyond merely acknowledging equivalence; it is pivotal in promoting fairness within the evaluative process.  Nevertheless, decisions involving ties are not consistently reliable. In scenarios where the model must select among triadic options, the decision-making process can exhibit considerable variability contingent upon the configuration employed. Thus, the development of a more robust mechanism for result integration is imperative.

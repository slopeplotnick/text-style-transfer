This study examines the robustness of pretrained Vision-Language Action (VLA) models, revealing a decline in performance when exposed to alterations in visual input due to changes in spatial understanding. To address this, we propose an innovative approach for rapidly adapting these models to better accommodate environmental variations. This approach employs Feature Token Modulation and Feature Linear Adaptation, which refine the models' perception with minimal structural modification. Our method has demonstrated substantial effectiveness in adapting to novel camera perspectives and environmental changes, as evidenced by performance results on the Libero test, indicating that these VLA models may possess greater robustness than previously assumed.  VLA models integrate visual processing, language comprehension, and decision-making into a unified framework. Leveraging foundational principles of vision and language, contemporary VLAs such as RT-2, Pi0, PaLM-E, Octo, and OpenVLA have exhibited proficiency in handling diverse tasks. These models utilize extensive datasets comprising various visual stimuli, linguistic instructions, and kinesthetic states, alongside imperfect actions, to establish a common strategy for versatile robotic operation. Despite these advancements, research indicates that even the most sophisticated VLAs remain vulnerable to environmental changes, such as shifts in camera perspective, lighting, or scene noise, despite extensive training. This fragility poses a significant obstacle to their practical implementation in real-world scenarios, which are inherently more complex than controlled conditions like those in the Libero tests.  Enhancing VLA models' capacity to manage visual transformations has been a persistent challenge. Previous efforts have focused on normalizing visual perception across various angles using augmented datasets with random adjustments, multiple viewpoints, or extensive collections of visual variations. While these approaches have been effective, they often necessitate significant data acquisition or annotation efforts, which limits their scalability.

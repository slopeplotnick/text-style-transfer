In this study, we assess the performance of four distinct models—MOIRAI, CHRONOS, GTT, and LightGTS—to investigate their capacity to manage variations in data and training protocols. Each model employs unique methodologies for learning and prediction, thus influencing their adaptability to fluctuating data sizes. For the purpose of this evaluation, we utilize the minimal configurations of these models to align with our data constraints, ensuring that our analysis focuses exclusively on their data-size handling abilities, rather than computational power.  Distinctive learning and predictive approaches are inherent in each model, with implications for their data-size adaptability. Notably, CHRONOS employs a technique that decomposes data into smaller segments prior to the learning process, which facilitates resilience to varying data magnitudes by mitigating the impact of large numeric values. Furthermore, standardization of the data is implemented prior to model training, ensuring uniformity and averting potential distortions from individual data points. Subsequently, data adjustments are conducted to maintain equilibrium across the dataset.  Model performance evaluation is conducted using the Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE) metric, which provides an assessment of prediction accuracy devoid of influence from data dimensionality. The objective of this research is to determine the effects of alterations in data size on model efficacy, demonstrated through comparative analysis of MASE fluctuations against a benchmark method.

The pace of change in the global mining industry has accelerated significantly in recent years. Over the past decade, this sector has become a focal point in the transition toward sustainable energy systems. The increasing demand for critical minerals is accompanied by escalating political, social, and environmental challenges. This dynamic has spurred the emergence of new companies, often supported by venture capital, that seek to disrupt the mining industry in much the same way as advancements have transformed sectors such as healthcare, renewable energy, and climate technology. Concurrently, the development and deployment of innovative digital technologies, including large language models, are poised to influence various aspects of the economy, education, society, and industry. While the transformative potential of these technologies remains uncertain, there is widespread concern about the risks of being left behind in their adoption. However, the mining industry should not view these developments with apprehension. Rather, it should recognize the opportunities inherent in leveraging technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI).    This paper identifies and explores the areas where such opportunities exist and examines how AI can be effectively utilized within the context of mineral exploration. Specifically, the contributions of my research group, Stanford Mineral-X, exemplify the potential of AI in this domain. For instance, the application of AI was instrumental in the identification of a significant copper deposit in Zambia by optimizing drilling plans and constructing subsurface models. Additionally, this research initiative has facilitated the emergence of start-ups dedicated to advancing AI-driven approaches in mineral exploration.    It is important to note that the objective of this work is not to provide an exhaustive review of all possible applications of AI within exploration or to replicate existing literature on the subject. Rather, this paper proposes a novel perspective by framing AI as a critical tool for enhancing decision-making processes through the adoption of a more structured scientific methodology. The first section of the paper outlines the principal decision-making challenges in mineral exploration and highlights how incorporating a scientific approach can lead to greater efficiency. At present, mineral exploration lacks a universally recognized scientific framework. Instead, it consists of domain-specific workflows executed by experts, each employing discipline-specific methodologies. This paper argues that the absence of a comprehensive scientific method in mineral exploration represents a fundamental barrier to improving efficiency.

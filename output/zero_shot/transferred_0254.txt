In this study, we investigated a type of cyber attack characterized by the random activation of a backdoor during the utilization of a tool, indicating that the vulnerability exists from the inception. Additionally, there exists a more advanced version of this attack wherein the tool initially appears benign. The attackers abstain from any immediate malicious activities, allowing the tool to function normally. Once the tool achieves widespread utilization, they can remotely activate the attack. This delayed strategy, akin to supply chain attacks, increases the attack's severity and complicates detection efforts. The sophistication of the attack can be further enhanced by targeting specific individuals; for instance, attackers may analyze user information and focus on individuals who frequently use the tool, particularly those who successfully pass security screenings. This targeted approach reduces the likelihood of detection during preliminary testing phases.  This paper introduces a novel form of attack termed "implicit toxicity," which exploits tools that have authorized capabilities to inflict harm by leveraging those permissions. To illustrate this concept, we developed a technique called LeechHijack, which facilitates the covert commandeering of a system's computational resources through standard tool usage. LeechHijack is compatible with any MCP (Malicious Computing Platform) tool provider and infiltrates systems without requiring elevated permissions, thus utilizing system resources for unintended tasks. This underscores a significant vulnerability in the dissemination of MCP tools, wherein malicious tools can compromise systems without necessitating additional access rights.  Comprehensive testing was conducted across diverse systems, demonstrating that LeechHijack operates effectively and evades current defensive mechanisms. We also explored mitigation strategies and established foundational guidelines for future improvements in defensive measures. Our research highlights a critical security concern in systems utilizing large language models, emphasizing the necessity for meticulous evaluation of third-party tools. All experimental procedures adhered to stringent ethical and security protocols. The deployment of the LeechHijack attack is exclusively for demonstrative purposes to underscore the issue, with no intention to promote malicious conduct. No interference was conducted with actual systems, platforms, or services. Our experiments were strictly confined to secure, controlled environments independently established, ensuring no connection to real servers and thus preventing any potential data breaches.

This section outlines the methodology discussed earlier by providing an example. The example utilizes the CelebA dataset, which contains over 200,000 images of celebrity faces, each annotated with labels that describe attributes such as whether the individual is smiling, wearing glasses, young, or attractive. For this study, the focus is on determining two specific attributes in the images: whether the individual is young and whether they are attractive. Figure 6 illustrates this process. Beginning with the original image on the left, the figure presents modified versions of the same image, where alterations have been made to depict the individual as "not young" (Young = 0). The first modified image was generated using a particular methodological approach. Although visual-based systems can occasionally yield misleading results, saliency maps remain widely employed to interpret the workings of image classification models. Accordingly, this study compares causal explanations derived from the proposed method with those generated by Grad-CAM, a well-known technique. The Grad-CAM implementation used here follows that of Gildenblat.  Figure 7 provides an analysis of the classifications for the two images on the far left of Figure 5, which were identified as depicting young individuals. For the male subject, the classification was influenced by features in the chin region, whereas for the female subject, the area around the eyes was deemed critical. Further, Figure 8 examines the classification results for the individual depicted in Figure 6. While the first image was correctly classified as young, Grad-CAM highlights that the classifier relied predominantly on features around the eyes for this decision. Conversely, for the incorrectly classified image on the right, Grad-CAM indicates that the classifier focused on the forehead and chin areas.   It is important to note that Grad-CAM does not provide causal explanations but rather identifies the regions of the image that were most significant for the classifier’s decision. Additionally, because Grad-CAM explanations are pixel-based, they do not elucidate why specific regions were deemed significant for the classification task. This limitation underscores the lack of interpretability in such saliency-map-based techniques when explaining classification outcomes.

We conducted an evaluation of our knowledge graph-based system through two distinct approaches to assess the impact of data on generative models. Initially, we investigated the effect of data omission by deliberately excluding specific data from a self-trained model to determine if our method is capable of identifying pivotal training examples. Subsequently, we examined the influence of stylistic elements by employing images in the Ghibli style in a scenario where the model's internals were inaccessible. To facilitate this, we developed a "Ghibli World" knowledge graph derived from the original films and conducted a comparative analysis with styled images. These experiments enabled us to scrutinize both the direct influence of data and the traceability of style in generative models. To ensure the rigor and comprehensibility of our comparison, we replicated the framework of a contemporary method that evaluates influence without necessitating access to training specifics. This method involves two phases: initially identifying training examples closely aligned with the user's intent, followed by ranking them based on their similarity to the generated output using cosine similarity. The most analogous training images are subsequently excluded, and the model is retrained to ascertain the significance of their contribution. We applied our graph-based method in a parallel manner, utilizing identical data, prompts, and the model. Instead of leveraging text and hidden-space similarity, we identified pertinent examples based on knowledge graph similarity, facilitating a direct comparison of the two methods in terms of influence detection and interpretation. For the generative model, we employed a scaled-down version based on the open-source dalle-pytorch package, trained on a dataset comprising 44,000 fashion images accompanied by captions. By maintaining the same setup and configurations as the original, we ensured comprehensive control over the training data, which was advantageous for testing data removal and subsequent retraining.

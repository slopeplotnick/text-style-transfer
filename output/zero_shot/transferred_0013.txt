The task of predicting stock prices represents a significant and persistent challenge within the field of finance. Stock prices are characterized by high volatility and rapid responsiveness to new information, which renders traditional predictive methodologies inadequate, particularly in contexts involving news and market sentiment. These conventional approaches often fail to capture the underlying drivers of price fluctuations. In recent years, the proliferation of online textual data coupled with substantial advancements in natural language processing has accelerated research efforts aimed at leveraging textual information for stock price prediction. Nonetheless, the majority of studies employing contemporary language models tend to focus on rudimentary text analysis and short-term forecasting. These models often lack the ability to effectively integrate knowledge and retain information over extended periods, thereby limiting their capacity to navigate the dynamic and intricate nature of financial markets. This underscores the necessity for models that can recall and utilize pertinent information over time. Financial markets are inherently complex; price shifts typically result from a multitude of interconnected events unfolding over time rather than isolated occurrences. Predicting stock prices based on news requires a nuanced approach distinct from straightforward question answering; the question "What will the stock price do tomorrow?" cannot be directly answered by news articles alone. Models must construct a comprehensive representation of the market from diverse information sources, identify analogous historical scenarios, and engage in deep analytical reasoning to uncover market patterns rather than merely aligning textual content. Furthermore, financial texts are often replete with extraneous and irrelevant information, which can obscure crucial signals, necessitating effective filtering and alignment of pertinent content.

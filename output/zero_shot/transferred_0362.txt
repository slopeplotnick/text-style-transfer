AlphaZero was among the pioneering systems to demonstrate the efficacy of self-play in enhancing learning capabilities. By engaging in self-directed gameplay, AlphaZero enhances its performance autonomously, without external intervention. This concept has been adapted in the development of language models, which improve their cognitive abilities over time. Several systems implement this self-play paradigm on an extensive scale, facilitating the improvement of less proficient language models by generating and resolving tasks independently, thus circumventing the need for large volumes of labeled data. This approach is particularly advantageous in domains such as code development, where one program evaluates another through testing.   Certain methodologies, such as R-Zero, operate without reliance on external data, instead generating queries, attempting solutions, and refining their understanding by evaluating their outputs. Other approaches begin with a limited set of examples and progressively learn to formulate more complex problems. R-Few adopts a similar strategy but emphasizes a distinct guidance mechanism for learning, striving to maintain the exploratory benefits without deviating from the intended path or sacrificing variability.   Reinforcement learning has emerged as a crucial approach in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of language models, surpassing the traditional model of learning solely from examples. This technique frequently incorporates human feedback to facilitate improvement. Initial systems, such as InstructGPT, demonstrated that prioritizing human preferences can enhance the models' utility, safety, and reasoning capabilities beyond what is achievable through example-based learning alone.   Recent systems, including DeepSeek-R1, build on this concept to enhance problem-solving by providing rewards for effective reasoning. Comparable systems have shown that setting objectives for complex and lengthy tasks can significantly enhance reasoning skills.

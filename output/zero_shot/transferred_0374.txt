The findings presented herein demonstrate that OMNIGUARD exhibits a superior capability in comprehending safety across various data modalities. It efficiently transfers safety knowledge from one data modality to another and exhibits a robust understanding of unsafe content across diverse data types. The development of OMNIGUARD-7B and OMNIGUARD-3B marks a pioneering effort in processing multiple data types using an extensive safety training dataset. OMNIGUARD serves as a comprehensive solution for managing unsafe content across disparate data environments. Empirical evaluations indicate that OMNIGUARD-7B surpasses other models across all data modalities, while OMNIGUARD-3B performs commendably, even when compared to larger models such as Qwen3-235B and Qwen3-VL-235B. These results underscore the efficacy of our system in detecting and reasoning about unsafe content, thereby affirming the potential for systems to comprehend and elucidate safety across a wide range of data types. Future research will focus on more complex safety scenarios to further enhance safety measures in emerging language models. However, some limitations exist. Although OMNIGUARD excels in safety reasoning and adapts well to different data modalities, it faces certain challenges. Incorporating reasoning pathways enhances the comprehensibility and reliability of safety checks but also results in increased processing time due to the computational demands of reasoning. This necessitates greater computational resources compared to more straightforward systems. It is imperative to strike a balance between the depth of safety checks and processing speed, depending on the context, to optimize efficiency. Additionally, the paucity of publicly available datasets for training in cross-modal safety highlights the need for continued advancement in addressing complex mixed-data safety challenges. Future research endeavors aim to augment the reliability and effectiveness of such systems in safeguarding against risks associated with diverse data types.

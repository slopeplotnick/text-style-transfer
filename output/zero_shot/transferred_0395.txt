To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the inaugural application of an open-vocabulary object detector to enhance the comprehension of high-resolution images by Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Empirical evaluations indicate that the detector effectively identifies pertinent targets while disregarding extraneous regions. We introduce the Multi-Resolution Fusion with Detection (MRD) framework, characterized by its general applicability and lack of requisite training. This framework addresses issues of semantic similarity through a multi-resolution fusion strategy and leverages open-set detection outcomes to refine target areas, thereby optimizing the overall system integration. Extensive experimentation demonstrates the robust performance and versatility of our approach across various contexts, including both single-object and multi-object tasks in multiple MLLMs, alongside high-resolution evaluations.  In efforts to enhance MLLMs' understanding of high-resolution images without exacerbating the complexity of visual tokens, recent studies have incorporated high-resolution visual encoders. Notably, methodologies such as Vary and Deepseek-VL employ the Segment Anything Model (SAM) encoder to improve model performance on high-resolution image tasks. Alternatively, Native/Dynamic-Resolution MLLMs maintain the original resolution of images, aiming to generate a sequence of visual tokens that preserves the fidelity of details and spatial precision. These models implement various techniques to manage the challenges posed by elongated sequences and complexity, including sliding window attention, dynamic masking, and patch-based encoding.  Significant advancements have been made in this domain, as evidenced by the InternVL series, which partitions high-resolution images into fixed-size patches. Conversely, models such as Qwen2.5-VL directly train a Vision Transformer (ViT) on native-resolution images, facilitating the conversion of the entire image into a singular token sequence, potentially enhancing the comprehension of overarching contextual information.

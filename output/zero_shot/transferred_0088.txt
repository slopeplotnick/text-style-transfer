Figure 1 illustrates the operational mechanism of the fine-tuned model. Initially, the model identifies key elements within both the query and item descriptions, such as product names, brands, model specifications, and the intended audience for the product. Subsequently, it assesses the degree of correspondence between these elements in the query and the item, thereby determining their relevance. The incorporation of chain-of-thought (CoT) tuning enhances the model's ability to not only assign relevance labels accurately but also provide explanatory reasoning for its predictions.   An analysis of the model's outputs reveals a tendency towards overly strict relevance assessments. Specifically, the model frequently categorizes items as "Irrelevant" if certain details present in the query are absent from the item description, even when those details are inconsequential to consumer purchasing decisions. This excessive stringency increases the incidence of false negatives, resulting in diminished recall rates. Such inaccuracies negatively affect platform transactions and revenue generation.   To address this limitation, we implemented strategies to align the model's predictions with observed user behavior. Historical data comprising query-item pairs associated with completed transactions were utilized to create a training set. Pairs labeled "Irrelevant" by the fine-tuned model were treated as examples of overly strict predictions. Using GPT-40, we generated reasoning outputs that justified assigning a "Relevant" label to these query-item pairs, thereby establishing a set of "winners" and "losers" for preference alignment. The Key Tolerance Optimization (KTO) algorithm was employed to adjust the model's predictions based on these preferences. Unlike traditional alignment techniques, the KTO algorithm leverages binary labeled data for model alignment, thus streamlining the process and enabling faster implementation.

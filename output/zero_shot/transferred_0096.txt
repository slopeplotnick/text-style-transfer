Large language models (LLMs) have been rapidly advancing in their capabilities, enabling the generation of academic papers, literature reviews, and scientific reports. Systems leveraging LLMs for survey creation are revolutionizing the extraction of information from vast datasets by integrating the processes of searching, organizing, and synthesizing into a streamlined workflow. Both academic and commercial entities have developed various systems for survey creation, which can be categorized into three primary types: general systems for long-form text composition (e.g., Kimi and GLM), tools specifically designed for writing research papers (e.g., Chengpian and Doubao Paper Mode), and systems tailored for constructing academic surveys (e.g., SurveyGo, SurveyX, and Panshi ScienceOne). These systems are capable of processing numerous research papers, extracting pertinent information, and drafting surveys that are well-structured, cite sources appropriately, and maintain logical coherence. Despite significant advancements in the development of these systems, establishing effective methodologies for their evaluation remains a critical challenge. Currently, assessments largely depend on subjective evaluations, which do not provide consistent or reusable metrics for system comparison, performance analysis, or enhancement. Consequently, the reliability and continued improvement of these systems are hindered. It is imperative to develop standardized evaluation frameworks to ensure the quality and dependability of these systems, and to facilitate a unified approach for researchers to compare and analyze them. In response to this need, we have developed SurveyEval, a tool designed to evaluate surveys generated by these systems based on three criteria: overall quality, coherence of the outline, and accuracy of references. We conducted tests across seven academic disciplines and enhanced the LLM-as-a-Judge methodology by using human-written surveys to ensure that the automated evaluations align more closely with human judgment.

One significant challenge for Open Language Learning Models (OLLMs) is the management of diverse input modalities, which influences the emergence and detection of safety issues. Various types of inputs present distinct risks, such as textual bias, misinformation in images, or privacy concerns associated with audio data. Beyond the risks associated with individual input types, the integration of multiple input modalities exacerbates the difficulty in identifying latent dangers. For instance, the textual input "I want to fly" and an accompanying image of an individual on a rooftop are innocuous in isolation, yet together they may imply risky behaviors, such as suicidal ideation. Similarly, a video depicting police officers coupled with the query "How to overcome obstacles" might imply criminal intent, an inference not readily apparent from separate inputs.  As such, addressing safety risks in OLLMs becomes increasingly complex, necessitating a comprehensive understanding of each input type and their potential interactions. Approaches focusing solely on enhancing the safety of OLLMs may be insufficient, as they often require substantial time and resources and may compromise the models' fundamental reasoning capabilities. Even with significant investment, these models require frequent and costly retraining to address vulnerabilities and remain susceptible to low-cost adversarial attacks.  Guardrail models offer a potentially more adaptable solution; however, they predominantly address single input types or simple combinations, such as text-image pairs, rather than fully integrating all input types. Given that unsafe inputs can arise from any single modality or a combination thereof, guardrails designed for isolated input types or simple text-image combinations are inadequate for comprehensive moderation. Moreover, many guardrail approaches treat safety as a binary issue, thereby limiting their effectiveness in nuanced contexts.

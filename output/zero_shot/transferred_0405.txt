In the described task, a target appears on either the left or right side of a screen, requiring participants to open and close their fist on the corresponding side until the target disappears, after which they relax. Conversely, if the target appears on the top or bottom of the screen, participants open and close both fists if the target is positioned at the top or both feet if the target is at the bottom, continuing the task until the target vanishes. Following this, they return to a relaxed state. The study focused on specific EEG channels and refined the feature extraction process to ensure accurate recording of brain activity associated with classifying motor imagery tasks. To mitigate confounding effects from event-related potentials, analysis of the EEG data commenced one second after the stimulus presentation, thereby excluding immediate evoked responses. This approach emphasized the examination of ongoing brain activity to capture stable patterns associated with participants' sensorimotor states, ultimately enhancing the accuracy of brain signal classification.  Several data preprocessing techniques were explored before employing traditional machine learning algorithms to classify motor imagery tasks. Signal processing methods, such as Common Spatial Patterns (CSP), were utilized due to their demonstrated efficacy in EEG signal classification. Initially introduced by Koles and colleagues, CSP is a widely used method designed to extract discriminative features by optimizing the variance of EEG signals across two motor imagery classes or mental states. Specifically, CSP transforms the raw EEG signals into a feature space where the variance of signals belonging to one class is maximized while the variance of the other class is minimized. This is achieved by deriving spatial filters that capture key statistical differences between the two classes. The CSP methodology involves several steps, starting with the calculation of covariance matrices for each EEG signal class. The covariance between two variables, \(X\) and \(Y\), can be mathematically determined using a predefined formula. Through this process, CSP enables the identification of features that enhance the classification performance in motor imagery tasks.

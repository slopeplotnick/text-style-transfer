This study examines the iterative process required to ensure result validity, a crucial component for optimizing system training during testing. OptPO-SFT employs the same iterative update mechanism as TTSFT; however, it determines the number of iterations via a Bayesian framework, thereby enhancing both efficiency and reliability. Both the reinforcement learning (RL)-based and supervised fine-tuning (SFT)-based variants of OptPO adhere to a tripartite procedure: initiation, label computation using a designated method, and parameter update. The distinction between them lies in the update strategy, with one utilizing policy-gradient optimization and the other employing cross-entropy fine-tuning, although their overall operation remains analogous. For evaluation purposes, the system is tested using challenging mathematical and scientific problems: the MATH-500 dataset, comprising 500 high school-level problems; the AIME 2024, containing 30 pre-Olympiad challenges; the AMC, featuring American mathematics competition problems; and the GPQA, consisting of difficult graduate-level science questions. OptPO's performance is evaluated on widely-used open-source models such as Qwen2.5-Math1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct to assess its efficacy across various model sizes and types. Evaluation of TTSFT and OptPO-SFT is primarily conducted using the Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct models. OptPO is designed as a versatile framework that can integrate into various existing RL systems to enhance test-time training efficiency. Its integration and performance were assessed in systems such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, and compared against TTRL, a prevalent testing-time policy optimization method. The effectiveness of OptPO-SFT in comparable scenarios was also evaluated. The critic's learning rate is set at 1e-5. For SFT-based training, including both TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, gradient descent on the labeled batch is conducted using AdamW with a learning rate of 2e-5.

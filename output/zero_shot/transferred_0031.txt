When conducting benchmark tests, external processes operating in the background may introduce noise into the system. To mitigate this issue, we disabled the Wi-Fi and collected multiple samples at different times to filter out background interference. The configuration of our statistical model is a critical aspect of this research, although it is not devoid of limitations. The model incorporates several assumptions derived from prior studies and expectations regarding the data distribution. These assumptions are intended to minimize personal biases within the model. However, as with any modeling approach, there exists potential for debate concerning the parameterization of the model and the distributional choices made.   Our modeling decisions were informed by the rules governing the Java Virtual Machine (JVM) and the formal definitions of programming languages, with a primary focus on the analysis of Java Bytecode. The Gaussian distribution was selected for its ubiquity in natural phenomena and its alignment with the principles of the central limit theorem, which underpin the tendency for aggregated values to approximate normality. Given that the only known parameters of our measurements are their mean and range, the Gaussian distribution effectively captures the inherent uncertainty in our data.   Additional challenges stem from system and JVM-specific factors. For instance, variations in memory state prior to the execution of each bytecode pattern could influence the results. To address this, we invoke the garbage collector prior to each test. Furthermore, to minimize the impact of paging, caching, and other concurrent system processes, each bytecode pattern is tested ten times, with the order of execution randomized.

On the strategic front, we utilize improved relevance scores derived from an upgraded model to refine our ranking mechanisms, employing Pareto optimization for optimal balance. In terms of system architecture, the robust performance of the model has enabled us to replace or eliminate outdated rule-based methods. The integration of these modifications has demonstrated significant improvements. For instance, studies such as DeepSeek-R1 have observed that the model is capable of managing extended reasoning processes and also exhibits moments of sudden insight. Additionally, we have examined the evolution of answer length during the model's training process, as illustrated in Figure 17. The findings indicate that as the model's proficiency increases, its responses become more concise and eventually stabilize. This suggests that extended reasoning is not always essential for model improvement; rather, it naturally occurs during the training phase. Furthermore, we have determined that various tasks necessitate distinct reasoning lengths. For example, mathematical and coding tasks require a highly detailed step-by-step reasoning approach, whereas tasks involving user intent comprehension and item matching benefit from a straightforward and concise reasoning process. In such scenarios, additional reasoning does not confer any advantage. For tasks involving visual data, models that possess inherent image comprehension capabilities, such as Vision-Language Models (VLMs), are preferred over additional steps like caption generation. This hypothesis was tested by employing a teacher model, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, to execute dual steps: knowledge generation and reasoning, followed by the application of specific rules.

This paper explores how individuals cognitively process spatial information based solely on visual input. To address this, we introduce VIDEO4SPATIAL, a framework designed to demonstrate how video-based models can effectively tackle complex spatial reasoning tasks by analyzing video scenes. Our evaluation focuses on two specific tasks: navigation within a scene by adhering to camera-based directional instructions and object localization, which involves understanding spatial arrangements, interpreting instructions, and planning actions. Notably, both tasks rely exclusively on video data, devoid of supplementary inputs such as depth information or camera pose data.  Through careful and straightforward architectural design, VIDEO4SPATIAL demonstrates robust spatial reasoning capabilities. The model effectively plans navigation routes, localizes objects from start to finish, adheres to camera instructions while maintaining spatial coherence, and generalizes across extended sequences and novel environments. These results underscore the potential of video models to develop human-like spatial understanding. Humans inherently excel at perceiving, reasoning about, and acting within spatial environments—key aspects of spatial cognition. The pursuit of enabling artificial neural networks to replicate these abilities has long been a focal point in research. Recent advancements in video models suggest their potential to emulate human spatial reasoning, paralleling the success of large language models in understanding and generating coherent linguistic representations.  The sequential and temporally structured nature of videos makes them particularly suited for spatial reasoning tasks. Such models can predict future scenes in a manner that aligns with spatial coherence and temporal continuity. For instance, a model navigating a maze can generate sequential visual representations that preserve the structural integrity of the maze while simulating smooth movement. Recognizing that human spatial perception predominantly relies on visual input, we aim to enable models to achieve similar capacities through video data alone. This approach offers scalability and simplicity but poses significant challenges, as learning spatial reasoning from unstructured video data remains a non-trivial task. Importantly, many recent methodologies still rely on additional inputs, such as depth maps or camera pose data, to enhance spatial understanding.  In this paper, we introduce and evaluate VIDEO4SPATIAL, demonstrating that video-based models can achieve sophisticated spatial reasoning solely using raw video input. Our framework utilizes a conventional video model trained with a straightforward objective: given a video scene and corresponding instructions, the model generates a subsequent video sequence that accomplishes the task while preserving spatial and temporal congruity. This work provides a foundation for advancing spatial reasoning in video models, presenting a promising avenue for scalable and human-like spatial understanding.

In this section, we delineate the methodological framework employed in our study, detailing the data, problem-solving mechanisms, and validation processes utilized. We introduce the metrics for assessing solver and checker performance, as well as the configurations established for validation within the research context. A crucial concept in our methodology is the notion of "verifier gain," which serves as a long-term metric. Verifier gain represents the asymptotic improvement achievable through persistent validation, and effectively functions as an upper bound on the enhancement attainable via checker-based sampling. This metric is instrumental in evaluating the efficacy of various checkers.  We categorize models into distinct families based on their characteristics, such as Llama3 or Qwen2.5, with each family comprising related models of varying scales. Given the divergent performance of foundational models compared to their enhanced counterparts, these groups are further subdivided. For example, the foundational model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-70B is classified under the Llama3-Base group, while the refined model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct is included within the broader Llama3 group.  Each solver-checker pair derived from our models is assigned to one of three verification configurations. These configurations are as follows: 1. Self-Verification, wherein the solver and checker are identical, necessitating self-assessment of its own outputs. For instance, when a 70B Llama3 model functions both as the solver and checker, accuracy is measured within this singular configuration. 2. Intra-Family Verification, which involves the checker evaluating outputs from other models within the same family. An example includes a 70B Llama3 checker assessing the outputs generated by 8B or 13B Llama3 solvers. Metrics are averaged across these intra-family solvers, excluding instances of self-verification. 3. Cross-Family Verification, where the checker assesses outputs from models across different families. For instance, a basic Llama3 checker might evaluate outputs originating from Qwen3 or from an improved version of Llama3. The performance metrics are averaged across these inter-family solvers.

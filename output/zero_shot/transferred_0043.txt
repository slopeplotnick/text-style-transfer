Radiology reporting represents a critical component of the medical imaging process, requiring detailed interpretation and documentation of findings. However, this task is time-intensive and prone to human error, especially when dealing with complex three-dimensional imaging modalities such as computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Ensuring the quality and accuracy of radiology reports is vital, yet this process often involves meticulous and labor-intensive verification of both content and language. Existing automated methodologies have primarily focused on generating radiology reports, often neglecting the equally important task of quality assurance, thereby limiting their capacity to fully assist radiologists in clinical workflows.  To address this gap, we introduce *Radiologist Copilot*, an artificial intelligence (AI)-driven system designed to automate both the generation and quality assurance of radiology reports. Leveraging advanced large language models, this system mimics the cognitive processes of a radiologist, employing task-specific tools to accomplish various functions such as identifying regions of interest in images, planning analytical workflows, selecting appropriate report templates, and optimizing report accuracy and completeness through iterative feedback mechanisms. By integrating these functionalities, *Radiologist Copilot* facilitates the creation of high-quality radiology reports with greater efficiency and precision, thereby supporting radiologists in their diagnostic responsibilities.  Performance evaluations indicate that *Radiologist Copilot* surpasses existing state-of-the-art methods in report generation, demonstrating its potential to enhance radiology workflows. The system's code will be made publicly available upon regulatory approval. Given the critical nature of radiology reporting in clinical decision-making, the development of a robust, automated solution for report generation and quality assurance holds significant promise for streamlining radiological practice while reducing the risk of diagnostic errors.

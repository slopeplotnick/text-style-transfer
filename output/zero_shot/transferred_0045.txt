Large language models excel in tasks requiring clear reasoning. However, our understanding of their performance in scenarios lacking explicit instructions remains limited. Extensive research has consistently demonstrated that humans adeptly interpret ambiguous signals when required to comprehend complex stimuli. This study investigates whether these models possess similar capabilities, specifically their ability to integrate diverse information types without guided training. Employing methodologies derived from psychology, we analyze the cognitive processes of these models by observing their behavioral patterns. We have developed an evaluative framework, BayesBench, comprising four tasks that assess dimensions such as size and time through linguistic and visual stimuli. These tasks are grounded in established psychological studies, allowing for comparison across nine models and human perceptions to establish a benchmark.  By manipulating variables like noise, context, and instructions, we evaluate the models' performance and their proficiency in synthesizing different information types. Beyond accuracy and efficiency, our scoring metric assesses whether the models exhibit behavior consistent with intelligent processing, even when they perform well. Our findings indicate that while certain models adapt effectively, accuracy does not invariably correlate with reliability. For instance, GPT-5 Mini demonstrates strong text processing capabilities but struggles with visual inputs, highlighting a disparity between capability and execution. This suggests that evaluations focusing solely on accuracy might overlook how models handle uncertainty. These insights reveal how models manage uncertainty and the relationship between accuracy and intelligent behavior. We provide our testing framework and scoring system as tools to aid in the development of systems that integrate various information types.  The ability to assess attributes such as size, time, or distance is fundamental for both humans and machines. Humans achieve this by effectively integrating ambiguous signals, prioritizing credible information, and leveraging prior expectations to minimize errors. This cognitive strategy is observed consistently across diverse cultures and age groups, underscoring its fundamental role in managing uncertain information.

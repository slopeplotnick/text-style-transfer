The analysis of differences in reasoning patterns necessitates careful consideration of their relationship to prompt steerability. Detailed information is presented in Tables 8 and 9. The observed changes in distribution following prompt optimization highlight the influence of system prompts on reasoning processes. Specifically, reasoning patterns were examined both prior to and subsequent to prompt optimization. As illustrated in Figure 9 and Table 10, the frequency of reasoning components decreases overall after optimization. This reduction can be attributed to the fact that optimized prompts tend to be shorter and less complex, often omitting extraneous instructions such as "answer like Shakespeare," which can introduce elaboration and variability in reasoning. Consequently, the "Others" category exhibits a significant decline, indicating that optimized prompts foster more structured and comprehensible model behavior.   Additionally, the frequency of specific reasoning components, such as Logical reasoning and Reframing, undergoes notable shifts, underscoring the substantial dependence of these behaviors on prompt design. The effects of optimization, however, are task-dependent. For mathematical problems, optimized prompts facilitate an increase in Calculation and Logical reasoning components, aligning with the task's requirements. Conversely, for other types of tasks, optimized prompts do not elicit the same reasoning components. In the case of moral reasoning tasks, most reasoning components decrease in frequency, although Logical reasoning persists as the dominant pattern relative to others. Certain reasoning components, such as Backtracking and Backward chaining, remain consistently rare across all scenarios, likely due to the models' limited reflective capacities compared to reinforcement learning systems.  Furthermore, an investigation was conducted into whether the structure of reasoning chains could predict the effectiveness of prompts. Each reasoning chain was mapped onto a nine-dimensional vector, with each dimension representing the frequency of a specific reasoning component. This approach enables a structured analysis of how reasoning patterns align with prompt optimization outcomes.

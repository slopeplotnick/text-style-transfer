This study evaluates three measures and computes the corresponding values for all pairs of methods within the HD and FV groups, as well as between them. Subsequently, the average scores for each group are calculated and presented in Table 2. The results indicate that HD and FV exhibit strengths in distinct areas of performance. Specifically, the higher ACS scores observed for pairs between the two groups suggest that these methods excel in complementary areas rather than overlapping ones. Furthermore, the elevated ASG score demonstrates that the joint application of HD and FV methods outperforms reliance on methods from a single group. Similarly, the AECR score increases significantly for pairs across groups, indicating that the methods effectively compensate for one another's weaknesses—when one approach fails, the other often succeeds. Collectively, these findings highlight that HD and FV methods are not only distinct but also synergistic, supporting the proposition that models integrating both approaches are likely to yield superior results.  Building on this analysis, the final section of the study explores the reasons underlying their divergent performance via a case study. The findings reveal two primary challenges: FV methods often fail due to difficulties in identifying robust evidence, leading to errors, while HD methods tend to misclassify instances as erroneous due to misinterpretation of linguistic variability. Investigating these limitations provides insights into the mechanisms by which these methods complement each other, reinforcing the rationale for developing hybrid models that incorporate both HD and FV approaches.

Individuals are more likely to exhibit escalated and aggressive behavior in online environments due to a perceived sense of anonymity, and collective contexts can exacerbate conflicts. While automated systems exist to detect inappropriate conduct online, their typical response involves content removal or punitive measures, which do not address the underlying causes of such behavior. Consequently, online platforms may temporarily suppress harmful discourse without effectively mitigating its recurrence. This study proposes an alternative approach, employing mediation rather than traditional moderation techniques. Specifically, we examine whether a language model can interpret a dispute and contribute to its de-escalation. We hypothesize that these models could potentially facilitate more constructive interactions by understanding the nuances of conversations, assessing the fairness of arguments, and responding in a manner that reduces tension and encourages productive dialogue.  To achieve this, it is essential to consider the context, emotional dynamics, escalation patterns, and typical communication practices. Our methodology comprises two key components. The first is the identification and analysis of elements that induce unfairness or emotional distress. The second involves generating responses that effectively de-escalate tensions and redirect focus to the substantive issues underlying the conflict rather than superficial disputes.  To evaluate the efficacy of these models, we curated an extensive dataset of genuine online conflicts from Reddit and developed a framework for empirical testing. Our evaluation criteria encompass three dimensions: firstly, the adherence of the model to established conversational norms; secondly, the behavioral changes in participants post-intervention to gauge the model's impact; and thirdly, the comparative analysis of model-generated interventions against human responses. Our findings indicate that commercially available models outperform free counterparts in both identifying and mitigating disputes, demonstrating superior topical coherence. Furthermore, these models contribute to the reduction of inflammatory rhetoric and heightened emotions in simulated conflicts, though they exhibit limitations in readability and engagement compared to human interactions.

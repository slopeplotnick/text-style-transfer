Despite efforts to clean the data, CPV datasets remain susceptible to noise, largely due to issues related to the quality of item information. Additionally, item SKU features are often burdened with extraneous elements owing to their inherent characteristics. To evaluate feature efficacy, we partitioned the data into training and test sets. Initially, we focused on product titles, incrementally integrating other features to assess their contributions. As demonstrated in Table 2, the incorporation of CPV and SKU features enhances model performance, indicating that with relevant and consistent input information, model efficacy can be improved despite the presence of extraneous elements and noise. Furthermore, we incorporated primary item images to introduce visual features that textual data alone cannot capture. SKU images were excluded from this integration since the primary images already encompass the majority of the visual information. SKU images typically depict minor variations such as style or color, which are adequately represented by SKU text features; including them would unnecessarily complicate the model without adding substantial information value. Ultimately, we compiled a comprehensive set of product information to be utilized in all future training endeavors.  In selecting an optimal base model, we considered two critical factors: the model's capacity for complex reasoning and knowledge integration, as discussed in Section 2, and the efficiency required for practical, real-world applications. We evaluated several high-caliber open-source models with parameters ranging from 7 billion to 14 billion, a scale that is sufficient for training and deploying on a single GPU. Subsequently, we divided some existing annotated data into training and validation sets. Employing the basic relevance task definition as the prompt, we conducted straightforward fine-tuning, as outlined in Section 3.1, on each model using the training set and assessed their performance. Ultimately, the Qwen2.5-7B model emerged as the most proficient among the evaluated models and demonstrated operational efficiency, leading to its selection as the base model for our framework.

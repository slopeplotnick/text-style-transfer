This study provides an examination of the problem-solving efforts of two software engineering agents, namely SWE-agent Dev and SWE-agent DSK-V3, in the context of error identification and resolution within a client.py file. Initially, SWE-agent Dev identifies a bug within the file, constructs a test case to reproduce the issue, and subsequently performs multiple edits to different segments of the file. After two unsuccessful attempts at resolution, the agent examines additional sections of the file, conducts test runs to verify the effectiveness of the solution, and ultimately submits the patch. Conversely, SWE-agent DSK-V3 similarly commences by pinpointing the error within the client.py file but performs a single edit before submitting the patch without verifying its efficacy.  Despite achieving eventual success, both agents exhibit procedural errors. SWE-agent Dev engages in line-by-line edits, repeating a failed approach prior to implementing a conclusive adjustment. In contrast, SWE-agent DSK-V3 executes a unified edit during the patching process. Neither agent subjects the patch to comprehensive testing; SWE-agent Dev merely confirms the functionality of reproduction tests, while SWE-agent DSK-V3 assesses potential correctness against edge cases theoretically.   This exemplification highlights the potential insights garnered from detailed analysis of agent problem-solving methodologies. Although recent endeavors have sought to explicate agent operations and identify failure points through step-by-step analysis, such manual approaches are prone to bias and incompleteness, rendering them less effective for novel systems. Furthermore, sequential step analysis may inadequately capture the nuanced behavioral dynamics of agents, including task execution and strategic planning processes.

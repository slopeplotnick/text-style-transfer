This paper proposes a method for establishing safety guidelines applicable across multiple languages by leveraging a limited number of widely spoken ones. We have developed a compact safety model operational in over 100 languages, yet it was trained using data from only 13 major languages. Our analysis evaluates the effectiveness of applying insights derived from these prominent languages to less prevalent ones. Initial content moderation systems predominantly targeted the English language; for instance, Google's Perspective API initially supported only English. Subsequently, platforms such as Aegis-Defensive, WalledGuard, and WildGuard demonstrated proficiency in managing safety and inappropriate content within the English language context. However, recent studies indicate that leading language models tend to generate less secure responses in non-English languages. Furthermore, prompts in less frequently spoken languages are more likely to circumvent existing safety checks. Emerging approaches aim to address these English-centric safety limitations across a broader linguistic spectrum. Conversely, tools like MrGuard and X-Guard rely on low-quality translations or synthetic data, inadequately capturing the nuances of less common languages. Recent advancements, such as LlamaGuard3-8B and NemoGuard-8B-content-safety, are based on the Llama3.1-8B framework, while DuoGuard employs an innovative dual-training methodology to develop a concise safety tool. To the best of our knowledge, PolyGuard currently represents the most comprehensive model for safety verification, accompanied by the largest dataset encompassing multiple languages. Nonetheless, PolyGuard's coverage is restricted to 17 languages, underscoring the prevailing focus of existing tools on a limited array of major languages.

In this study, we conducted a comparative analysis of our proposed method against existing benchmarks using evaluative judges. Our evaluation approach represents a significant enhancement over prior methodologies. Traditional techniques, such as PPTC-R, primarily emphasize API-level discrepancies. Although these methods are beneficial, they often fail to identify critical errors, such as instances where an agent places the correct content on an incorrect slide. Our IF Judge employs structured summaries to effectively detect these logical inconsistencies. Other benchmarks, such as T2US, also utilize visual language models (VLMs) as evaluative tools; however, they rely on prompts lacking a well-defined set of criteria, rendering their outcomes inconsistent and unreliable. The PPTArena framework addresses this issue by establishing a robust and reproducible evaluative mechanism. In essence, our dual-judge system, grounded in explicit guidelines, is pivotal for assessing the intricate processes involved in editing PowerPoint presentations and for identifying nuanced errors that more simplistic methods may overlook.  Moreover, we developed PPTPilot, a presentation editing tool characterized by a streamlined design yet superior efficacy compared to certain commercial alternatives like OpenAI's ChatGPT Agent, as elaborated in Section 5.1. This design is predicated upon two fundamental principles. First, the primary challenge in this domain lies not merely in understanding the editing requirements but in ensuring their accurate and reliable execution. PowerPoint files are structured using the complex Office Open XML (OOXML) format, which is poorly suited to managing errors or suboptimal outputs from VLMs, necessitating specialized formatting and configurations for effective editing. Second, it became evident that no singular editing strategy, such as exclusive reliance on the XML format, suffices. An effective tool must adeptly select the most appropriate editing method for each specific task. PPTPilot is engineered with a dual-path architecture, enabling it to process editing requests either through programmatic tools or through direct XML format manipulation.

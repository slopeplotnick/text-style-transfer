We evaluate the efficacy of episodic memory retrieval across varying temporal durations of events by employing a metric known as temporal intersection over union (tIoU). This metric quantifies the degree of alignment between detected segments and the actual segments by assessing their overlap relative to their total length. Our analysis examines the performance of the WorldMM model in comparison to other models across multiple domains, including temporal grounding, single-modality retrieval, long-form egocentric video retrieval, and keyframe selection. A comprehensive discussion of the foundational concepts is available in Section C.1. Notably, Table 3 illustrates that the WorldMM model achieves significantly superior tIoU scores compared to other leading models. Methods that predominantly rely on reasoning and keyframe selection exhibit lower tIoU scores, indicating challenges in managing extended input contexts. As depicted in Figure 5, enhanced tIoU scores correlate with increased overall accuracy, particularly in comprehending long videos as opposed to single-step retrieval. This improvement arises from the iterative process, wherein the retrieval system refines its information acquisition strategy and enhances its performance beyond initial attempts. An instance of such enhancement is detailed in Section E.2, wherein the model corrects an erroneous retrieval to provide a more accurate and contextual response.  We assess the efficiency of our system by measuring the time required for the WorldMM model to process 100 random queries from the EgoLifeQA dataset. As shown in Figure 6, our methodology optimally balances speed and accuracy in contrast to alternative approaches. Long-video language models (LLMs) demonstrate prolonged processing times with comparatively lower performance. Conversely, methods based on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) or memory exhibit faster processing but demand extensive setup and exhibit suboptimal performance. The WorldMM model, by contrast, conducts iterations as necessary and selectively retrieves pertinent segments, enabling efficient and accurate operation.

Despite the increasing popularity of collaborative frameworks between solvers and verifiers, research on their interactions remains limited. Most previous studies have focused on models evaluating their own outputs and refining their performance accordingly. However, this self-assessment approach does not consistently guarantee improvement, as models tend to exhibit biases aligned with their inherent reasoning patterns, potentially intensified by further training. Moreover, the efficacy of self-verification varies across different tasks, thereby limiting our understanding of the dynamics when the solver and verifier are distinct entities.   The availability of open-source models with diverse configurations provides an opportunity to explore inter-model verification processes. Consequently, this study extends the scope of investigation to assess both intra- and inter-family model performance. We are particularly interested in determining the conditions under which verification enhances problem-solving abilities and how factors such as model family, size, additional training protocols, similarity between solver and verifier, and task type influence this enhancement.  To address these questions, we evaluate verifiers across a variety of tasks requiring precise logical reasoning and symbolic computation, such as 3-SAT, Sudoku, and matrix multiplication; mathematical reasoning tasks, including AIME and GSM8K; commonsense and factual reasoning challenges, such as CSQA and GPQA; and general knowledge domains, covering STEM and social sciences. This evaluation is conducted using 37 models spanning 7 different model families.  Our findings indicate that self-verification does not uniformly yield positive outcomes: models frequently favor solutions that conform to their internal reasoning biases, with additional training exacerbating these biases. Furthermore, the benefits of verification are not uniformly distributed across tasks. Based on these insights, we provide practical recommendations for the judicious implementation of verifiers in enhancing problem-solving efficacy.

When individuals engage with systems, it is imperative for them to establish a foundation of trust, as hallucinations can significantly undermine this trust. Therefore, it is crucial to consider the impact of such errors on user perception. A particular study investigates hallucinations within language models, focusing on user detection and reporting of these inaccuracies. This research employs innovative metrics to evaluate the influence of hallucinations on users, such as the erosion of trust and the necessity for users to independently verify information—tasks that the system should ideally perform. Furthermore, the study presents a comprehensive framework for understanding and addressing hallucinations by utilizing various methodologies to enhance existing models.   Another research initiative assesses the efficacy of language models using methodologies such as fact-checking, consistency analysis, and user experience evaluation. This multifaceted approach facilitates the identification of hallucinations from diverse perspectives, extending beyond mere factual accuracy to include argumentative coherence and user perceptions. In deploying large language models across varied applications, it is essential to comprehend the task at hand while acknowledging the potential for hallucinations. For general applications, such as chatbots, models like GPT-4 and PaLM are favored for their human-like writing capabilities, minimal error risks, and creative outputs. However, for tasks necessitating factual precision, such as information retrieval and question answering, models like BERT and T5 are preferable due to their emphasis on factual integrity and robust comprehension.

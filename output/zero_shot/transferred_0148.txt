This study introduces a novel approach to decouple perceptual processing and reasoning in artificial intelligence (AI) models. Specifically, training data is augmented with examples of incorrect reasoning, enabling the model to distinguish between accurate and flawed logic. Additionally, we propose a method referred to as Self-Training Logic (STL), which simultaneously enhances both perceptual understanding and reasoning capabilities, in contrast to prior techniques that primarily focused solely on reasoning. Our method has been evaluated across diverse domains, including common sense reasoning, language comprehension, and scientific inquiry, demonstrating robust improvements without dependence on human intervention.   Within the domain of vision-language reasoning, the integration of reasoning processes has proven critical to improving model performance. While recent models achieve high accuracy on standardized benchmarks, leveraging visual information effectively in reasoning remains a challenge, particularly for open-source models. Existing strategies often utilize prompt engineering to guide models through step-by-step reasoning processes—for instance, employing contextual analysis before feature extraction or utilizing scene graphs to represent object relationships. Other approaches rely on training models on specialized datasets containing reasoning exemplars; however, such datasets are resource-intensive to construct.  The proposed methodology addresses these limitations by enabling models to improve reasoning autonomously without necessitating the creation of dedicated datasets. Self-training, wherein a model utilizes its own outputs as supplementary training data, has been shown to enhance reasoning capabilities within large language models by generating and reusing intermediate reasoning steps. This iterative process facilitates performance improvements without requiring external annotations or human supervision. While prior research has explored techniques like selecting and fine-tuning examples of correct reasoning, the application of self-training to vision-language models remains underexplored. This work seeks to advance understanding in this area, offering an efficient and scalable approach to augmenting reasoning capabilities in AI systems.

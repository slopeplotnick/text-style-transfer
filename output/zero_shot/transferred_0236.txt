Contemporary market systems exhibit superior capabilities in terms of growth, adaptability, and problem-solving compared to centralized systems. The auction-based task allocation system, initially proposed by Botelho and Alami in 1999, facilitates competitive task assignment. Further research by Gerkey and Matario in 2002 demonstrated that auction mechanisms can effectively distribute tasks while maintaining coordination. Recent studies have focused on addressing communication challenges by employing sophisticated bidding strategies, even in scenarios with weak connectivity (Ferri et al., 2017; Quinton et al., 2022). Nevertheless, auction approaches may not consistently yield optimal global solutions, as local bidding processes can disrupt coordination when tasks are intricately interdependent. In the construction industry, where tasks must be executed in a specific sequence, auction mechanisms encounter difficulties in addressing scheduling issues that are critical for project planning. This has led to an exploration of structured optimization techniques. Dynamic programming provides an alternative framework for optimizing sequential decision-making. Bellman (1962) established foundational principles for deriving optimal solutions for problems with specific characteristics. However, dynamic programming becomes computationally prohibitive when faced with an extensive array of possibilities, rendering it impractical for large-scale problems. Construction projects typically involve numerous robots performing interdependent tasks, further complicating real-time computational feasibility. Consequently, researchers have investigated metaheuristic approaches to manage large and complex problems. These methods draw inspiration from evolutionary concepts to identify solutions. Genetic algorithms, in particular, are well-suited for addressing complex problems with constraints, without necessitating a comprehensive mathematical formulation (Jones et al., 2010; Al-Omeer and Ahmed, 2019). Swarm intelligence techniques have demonstrated efficacy in coordinating large robotic teams (Chen et al., 2022; Lim and Isa, 2015; Wang et al., 2012; Blum, 2005). However, metaheuristic methods do not guarantee globally optimal solutions and are computationally intensive, often requiring numerous iterations to arrive at an acceptable solution. In the context of construction, where rapid adaptability is essential, the time-intensive nature of these methods poses significant limitations.

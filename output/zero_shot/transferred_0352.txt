In this study, we critically evaluate the limitations of a recent methodological approach [16] in multi-modal reasoning models. As illustrated in Figure 1(a), the model initially attends to both audio and visual inputs but progressively shifts its focus towards the text it has already generated. Consequently, the model's reasoning becomes influenced by the emotional cues present in the initial textual output. Typically, the process commences with a description of the video, as the training data prioritizes visual information, followed by audio and reasoning. However, this "visual-first" approach may not be suitable when the emotional content is more dependent on auditory cues or the semantic meaning of language. In cases where the initial visual reasoning is inaccurate or irrelevant, the subsequent reasoning deviates further, as depicted in Figure 1(b). This phenomenon underscores the excessive influence of the initial step, a challenge noted in other domains where early cues skew the reasoning process [17]. To address this issue, we propose the Modality-Importance-Guided Reasoning (MIGR) framework. The core principle of MIGR is to initiate reasoning with the modality—audio, visual, or both—that most accurately aligns with the targeted emotional inference, maintaining consistency with this emotional focus throughout the reasoning process. To implement this, we introduce the concept of Modality Importance (MI), which identifies the most critical input type for emotion recognition by comparing the model's predictive accuracy across scenarios with audio-only, visual-only, and combined inputs. For instance, if the model accurately predicts emotion with audio-only inputs but falters when visual data is included, it suggests that visual information may be negligible or even detrimental. In such cases, audio should be the primary focus. Leveraging the MI concept and recent advancements in reasoning-based model training [15, 16], we developed a two-stage learning process: (1) modality-aligned Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and (2) modality-aligned reward optimization. During the SFT phase, we utilize MI to segment the reasoning text according to input modality and subsequently reorganize it.

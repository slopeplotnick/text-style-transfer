The discourse surrounding music composed by artificial intelligence (AI), even in cases where the identity of the composer remains unknown, reveals a range of perceptions and concerns. These apprehensions align with discussions prevalent in the fields of music and technology research, encompassing potential economic impacts, issues surrounding intellectual property rights, and cultural ramifications. Nonetheless, there exists a concurrent fascination with the technological advancements and mechanisms underpinning AI music composition. This amalgamation of sentiments may account for the diverse opinions regarding AI composers. As advancements in AI technology continue, innovations once perceived as intriguing may evolve into perceived challenges or threats. Consequently, public sentiment toward AI-generated music is subject to temporal shifts.  This study, however, encountered certain limitations. Firstly, the sample comprised university students and participants recruited via the platform Prolific, who engaged with the music through varying mediums, thus limiting the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, individuals sourced from Prolific may possess heightened familiarity with technology and AI. Secondly, one cohort of participants was informed that the music resembled film soundtracks, potentially biasing their preferences, given the general public's favorable reception of movie music. Lastly, the study exclusively utilized instrumental music devoid of lyrics, which may restrict the applicability of the findings to lyric-based compositions. Prior research indicates that lyrics can amplify the emotional impact of sad songs, although they exert minimal influence on the perception of happy songs. Since the majority of the compositions in this study were of a joyful nature, the inclusion of lyrics might not have significantly altered the participants' emotional responses. The decision to exclude lyrics was made to mitigate potential confounding variables and due to the unavailability of high-quality AI-generated vocals at the time of sample creation.

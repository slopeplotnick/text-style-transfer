Our research demonstrates that individuals' perceptions of artificial intelligence (AI) significantly influence their evaluations of AI-generated music, extending beyond its auditory qualities. This underscores the necessity of developing tools specifically tailored to the creation and assessment of AI-generated music, as well as establishing novel frameworks for its evaluation. As AI-generated music becomes increasingly prevalent on streaming platforms, issues surrounding transparency and equity in its production and distribution processes demand careful consideration. Given the role of attitudes toward AI in shaping audience preferences and emotional responses, it is imperative to explore the ways in which the origins and dissemination of AI-generated music are communicated. For instance, it would be beneficial to investigate the impact of transparency regarding the data used to train AI models or the allocation of revenue generated by AI-created music. Furthermore, it is essential to examine the diverse methods of AI music creation and utilization, particularly in contexts where human creativity is integrated with AI systems. Public interest in collaborative music production, where humans and AI technologies work together, highlights the need for further exploration of these emerging practices.  Practical implications of public perceptions of AI music also merit investigation, as evidenced by the work of Moura and Maw (2021). Our findings suggest that perceptions of AI play a more critical role in the evaluation of AI-generated music than factors such as the creator's identity, the music's genre, or the listener's typical consumption habits. Looking ahead, it is crucial to monitor evolving public attitudes toward AI, as these will influence the acceptance or rejection of AI technologies not only in music but also in other creative fields. The central question is not merely whether AI-generated music can be appreciated or perceived as exhibiting human-like qualities, but rather what constitutes authentic creativity and value in artistic expression. By actively engaging with public sentiment, researchers and practitioners can better determine the forms of creativity that should be acknowledged and supported in this rapidly evolving landscape.

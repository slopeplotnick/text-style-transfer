Recent advancements in multimodal large language models (MLLMs), which integrate text and image processing, have improved their capabilities; however, these models still encounter difficulties in discerning fine details in high-resolution imagery. To enhance the performance of these models in interpreting high-resolution images, researchers typically adopt one of two strategies. The first strategy involves further training the models using techniques such as supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. While effective, this approach may compromise the models' adaptability across diverse tasks. The second strategy eschews additional training, instead employing methods that prioritize examining various segments of an image to identify salient features. Nonetheless, these techniques can be time-consuming and may overlook certain elements, particularly when multiple objects are present within an image.  This study investigates the impact of the size of image segments, referred to as crops, on the performance of MLLMs. Our empirical analysis indicates that the resolution of these segments plays a significant role in model efficacy, with varying object sizes necessitating different resolutions. Consequently, we propose the MRD framework to optimize this aspect. We validated our framework by evaluating two model variants, LLaVA-ov and LLaVA-v1.5, using a benchmark test. As illustrated in Figure 3, our findings reveal that when the resolution of image segments is standardized to 112, the models demonstrate optimal performance across both single-object and multi-object tasks. This suggests that a resolution setting of 112 may offer the most effective configuration for such models.

System prompts play a crucial role in guiding the behavior of large language models (LLMs) by establishing roles, styles, or other task-specific parameters. Previous research has demonstrated that elements such as personas, emotional tones, and jailbreak triggers can influence both the style and reasoning of LLM outputs. However, while these factors may affect the way responses are generated, they do not consistently enhance task accuracy. Despite the growing reliance on system prompts, there is limited understanding of how to systematically optimize their design. For instance, recent leaks of system prompts from models such as Grok and Claude have revealed intricate, manually created rule sets. Emerging approaches, such as the application of genetic algorithms, have shown promise for automating the improvement of system prompts. Nonetheless, such efforts have been limited in scope, focusing on single languages and specific evaluation metrics.  When extending these methods to multilingual contexts, distinct challenges arise, including language-specific issues and cross-linguistic variability. Multilingual LLMs frequently exhibit inconsistencies when responding to the same query across different languages, often reflecting disparities in language-specific knowledge. Although techniques such as Cross-Lingual Thought Prompting and multilingual self-consistency offer partial solutions, they typically require iterative prompting and fail to provide a comprehensive framework for optimizing system prompts across languages. In addition to traditional evaluation metrics, recent studies have explored the cognitive processes underlying LLM outputs by breaking responses into interpretable components. However, these investigations have predominantly focused on surface-level control mechanisms—for example, the use of text pre-fill to adjust output language—without delving into the underlying reasoning processes.  This work addresses these gaps by linking system prompt design to the cognitive processes of LLMs in multilingual settings. Furthermore, it provides insights into how these reasoning patterns influence performance across languages, offering a foundation for more effective and generalized prompt optimization strategies.

The study involved analyzing several datasets, each characterized by distinct omissions: a complete dataset, one lacking events, another missing relationships, one devoid of entities, and one without a story outline. Evaluations were conducted utilizing both qualitative and quantitative methodologies. A standardized test set comprising of 196 samples was employed to assess the modified models. The narrative quality was assessed based on functional and aesthetic dimensions, with stories from a preference set serving as a baseline for comparative analysis. This facilitated an examination of how the four central components influenced the narrative construction. Quantitative analysis primarily focused on the similarity of stories generated from different models relative to the original text. Each model produced three stories per input, with the original text as a benchmark for comparison. Similarity was evaluated using ROUGE-L, BERTScore, and METEOR Score metrics. ROUGE-L examines sequence similarity based on the longest common subsequence, BERTScore assesses semantic similarity while mitigating surface-level matching issues, and METEOR Score employs varied matching techniques for enhanced robustness and flexibility.  Figure 7 illustrates the comparative efficacy of different configurations against the baseline, revealing that the configuration incorporating all components yielded superior outcomes in content control, semantic consistency, and the naturalness of narrative production. Additionally, the study solicited the participation of 2 experts and 17 volunteers to explore the application of TaleFrame. To stimulate creativity, volunteers were provided various images and allowed to select preferred topics to generate narratives using TaleFrame. In instances of dissatisfaction with generated stories, volunteers were permitted to adjust the four principal components to refine the narrative. Participants were required to engage with all of TaleFrame's interactive features.

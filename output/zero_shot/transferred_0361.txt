In this study, we explored the effects of substituting different models for Stage 1 and Stage 2 while maintaining a consistent total number of function evaluations. The utilization of a smaller model in Stage 1 did not significantly impact the Geodesic Fréchet Inception Distance (gFID), suggesting that the prediction of basic layouts with some degree of noise is relatively insensitive to the model's size. Conversely, employing a less robust model in Stage 2 resulted in a substantial decline in the quality of the generated output. This observation underscores the importance of higher capacity and extensive training for effective refinement in the latter stage. Prior discussions have highlighted the efficacy of this two-stage approach, and this research aims to elucidate the rationale behind such methodologies. Specifically, we seek to comprehend and enhance these methods by considering the distinct functions of the navigation and refinement phases.  Subsequently, we examined the impact of selecting different latent spaces on model performance within a two-stage framework. As illustrated in Figure 9, variations in oracle loss during training were analyzed for VA-VAE and SD-VAE latent spaces. In the navigation stage, an incremental increase in oracle loss was observed across both latent spaces, indicating that predicting the basic layout becomes increasingly challenging irrespective of the chosen latent space. However, divergence in oracle loss patterns emerged during the refinement stage. Although direct numerical comparisons are not feasible, discernible trends in the oracle loss curves were apparent. For VA-VAE, which aligns its latent space with semantic structure utilizing DINO-based Variational Fusion (VF) loss, the oracle loss progressively forms a parabolic shape as training advances. This outcome suggests that the latent space is methodically organized with semantically distinct modes. In contrast, SD-VAE, which is oriented towards low-level reconstruction, exhibits a comparatively flatter and slightly oscillating curve, signifying a less organized latent space and difficulty in achieving consistent denoising.  In summary, latent spaces that exhibit strong alignment with high-level semantic concepts are more conducive for diffusion modeling. Such alignment facilitates clear mode organization under Gaussian marginals, thereby enhancing both navigation and refinement processes.

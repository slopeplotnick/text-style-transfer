The perception of AI-generated content by individuals is a subject of growing interest in contemporary research. Current studies often utilize rating scales from related fields; however, these scales may not accurately capture the nuanced sentiments associated with AI-generated music. This study seeks to elucidate these perceptions by employing open-ended questions. We utilized Artificial Intelligence tools, namely Stable Audio and Suno, to generate the music samples, which were informed by the Emotion-to-Music Atlas—a comprehensive resource detailing the emotional impact of music. The samples drew data from the Music4All-Onion database, encompassing five primary music genres, each with three subgenres: Electronic (including Trance, Downtempo, Dubstep), Jazz (such as Bossa-Nova, Smooth-Jazz, Swing), Pop (including Europop, Indie-Pop, Pop-Punk), Rock (comprising Soft-Rock, Rock Ballad, Doom-Metal), and Soul/Funk (encompassing Funk, Disco, Soul). For each subgenre, we developed prompts based on the dominant emotions identified in the music, utilizing average values from the Emotion-to-Music Atlas and their GEMS-9 descriptors. This approach resulted in 2-3 prompts per subgenre, yielding a total of 38 prompts. These were subsequently adapted for use with Suno and Stable Audio, culminating in the production of 76 music samples. For the Suno tool, prompts were configured in instrumental mode, and one of two outputs was selected and edited to a length of 45-60 seconds, aligning with previous studies. For Stable Audio, the term 'instrumental' was appended to each prompt, targeting a duration of 60 seconds. Additional details regarding the creation and modification of these samples are provided in the supplementary materials.

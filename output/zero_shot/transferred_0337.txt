In this study, we address a task characterized by the differentiation between elements deemed non-essential (L1 and L2) and those considered critical (L3 and L4). To evaluate performance, we employ the metric acc@2, which assesses accuracy by determining the correctness of outcomes. Additionally, we utilize the macro F1 score to ensure balance in scenarios where data labels are unevenly distributed. Our comparative analysis involves two model types: firstly, large language models (LLMs), encompassing both open and closed-source variants. The evaluation of these models is conducted by providing task-specific prompts containing detailed instructions and guidelines. Adhering to optimal practices for each model, we employ specific settings, such as a temperature of 0.7 for GPT-4 and 0.6 for Llama-3, to enhance performance. Secondly, we examine a baseline model referred to as Vanilla SFT, which is trained using labeled data but does not incorporate advanced techniques such as Chain of Thought (CoT) or Reinforcement Learning (RL). This approach allows us to discern the impact of these additional techniques. Remarkably, the baseline model achieves commendable results, with acc@2 and macro F1 scores of 0.929 and 0.722, respectively, surpassing even sophisticated models like GPT-4. Furthermore, the LORE model demonstrates superior performance by increasing acc@2 by 0.4 points, indicating its capacity for further enhancement of foundational model performance with standard data.

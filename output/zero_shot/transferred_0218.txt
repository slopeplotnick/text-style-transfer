We employ Gemini-2.0-Flash as the baseline model for our study, which relies solely on the original question and does not incorporate external information. Various configurations of CAIRNS are evaluated: one utilizing only literature-based data and another combining literature with climate data, leveraging a two-step API reasoning approach. Gemini-2.0-Flash serves as the foundational model for all versions of CAIRNS. To ensure consistency and fairness across experiments, all models utilize the same corpora, prompts, and evaluation protocols.   The ScholarGuide Prompt (SGP) is introduced to enhance readability through improved context, structure, and language while addressing citation-related inconsistencies. Although the use of SGP marginally reduces the overall citation rate, it provides unified reference identifiers and links each factual statement to explicit evidence, thereby improving the reliability and specificity of generated responses. These enhancements are effective in both zero-shot and few-shot scenarios, underscoring the significance of structured prompt design for the performance of CAIRNS.  Moreover, the incorporation of multi-turn ReAct reasoning facilitates the alignment of literature-based information with climate data, resulting in responses that are more comprehensive and contextually enriched. While the impact of ReAct is less pronounced compared to SGP, it contributes to the structured reasoning process and enhances the system's ability to address complex queries.  Lastly, Location-Weighted Retrieval is introduced to enable the generation of region-specific responses, particularly for geographically focused questions. While the removal of this feature slightly reduces the specificity of outputs, it has minimal impact on other performance metrics. Together, these methods contribute to the robustness and precision of CAIRNS in addressing diverse and challenging inquiries.

The efficacy of the ADORE model is evaluated through both offline and online methodologies. In the offline evaluation, ADORE is treated as a binary classification task, where its performance is assessed using precision, recall, and the F1 score metrics. In the online evaluation, ADORE's performance is examined within the context of search advertisements, focusing on metrics such as click-through rates, revenue generation, and expert assessments of advertisement quality. ADORE is benchmarked against other robust models, including BERT, LLAMA-3-8B, and their variants. For BERT models, two configurations are considered: the bi-encoder and the cross-encoder architectures. In the case of the cross-encoder BERT, comparisons are drawn among its smaller variant, the standard version, and an enhanced version with augmented information. Regarding the LLM model, its efficacy is evaluated based on decision-making using the final token. ADORE, which shares similarities with BERT-Mini, incorporates online data, synthetically generated data, and enriched information. The findings are presented in Table 2, where ADORE demonstrates superior performance compared to all other models across a substantial real-world dataset. Several observations were made: (1) The cross-encoder BERT model outperforms the bi-encoder version, with performance improvements correlating with increased model size. Augmenting BERT with additional information enhances its efficacy, underscoring the importance of incorporating explicit information. The decision-making capability of LLM is inferior to that of BERT. (2) Utilizing an LLM with embedded rules in the RD component to generate challenging examples significantly enhances performance. The inclusion of synthetic data from the DS component leads to an improvement in the F1 score. Furthermore, supplementing the model with explicit information in the KD component facilitates enhanced reasoning and overall performance.

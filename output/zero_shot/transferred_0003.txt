In this study, we explored seven methodologies for model combination aimed at mitigating social bias, employing three bias assessment tests alongside the SuperGLUE benchmark on a cohort of 13 large language models. Our analysis revealed that utilizing Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) under moderate configurations emerges as the most effective strategy for minimizing bias while maintaining model accuracy. It is essential to evaluate the efficacy of these approaches on tasks such as reading comprehension and reasoning, with the objective of reducing social bias. Recent developments highlight model combination as a promising technique to leverage the strengths of various models without necessitating retraining. This involves amalgamating components of two or more pretrained models to form a composite model that preserves and integrates the distinct knowledge of each individual model.  The linear averaging method, which was initially introduced by Wortsman et al. in 2022, offers a foundational approach by averaging model weights. This method provides a cost-effective avenue for skill integration, as it utilizes pretrained models. Subsequent research by Matena and Raffel in 2022 and Lee et al. in 2025 advances this concept by calibrating each component based on its significance, ensuring coherent model integration. Alternative methodologies, such as spherical interpolation—first proposed by Shoemake in 1985 and further developed by Goddard et al. in 2024—conceptualize model components as points on a sphere. SLERP, originally from Shoemake in 1985, facilitates the blending of two models, whereas the Karcher Mean, as introduced by Grove and Karcher in 1973, calculates the average across multiple models. The NuSLERP method, presented by Goddard et al. in 2024, incorporates an additional step to adjust for changes in scale.  Moreover, Ilharco et al. in 2022 devised an approach to apply these concepts to specific tasks by employing a "task vector." This vector represents the divergence between a pretrained model and its fine-tuned counterpart. These advancements underscore the potential of model combination techniques in addressing social bias while enhancing task-specific performance.

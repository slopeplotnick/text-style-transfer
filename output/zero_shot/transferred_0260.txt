The system operates on Spring Boot, with PostgreSQL employed for database management. Agronomists utilize Python or MATLAB to develop simulators. The IT team leverages a primary platform known as Cross, an extensive framework, to generate application code. Initially, the platform was designed for the creation of Proof of Concepts; however, its role has evolved to serve as a foundation for completed products. Presently, there are seven distinct versions tailored for three Product Owners. These versions are developed to meet specific client requirements, with a new version being released approximately annually. The Architecture Dimension levels, as delineated in the FEF framework, address the construction of software and inform the decision-making process for product development within the software product line. At Level 1, architectural considerations are confined to individual systems, lacking an emphasis on reuse. Progressing to Level 2, there is an initiative to reutilize third-party infrastructures, incorporating common software components such as middleware, yet without formal implementation of domain-specific assets. At Level 3, domain commonalities are integrated into a software platform, establishing a reference architecture applicable to all applications, with a primary focus on platform utilization. However, this platform does not accommodate the management of different configurations. At Level 4, both common and variable domain features are recognized, and a reference architecture is instituted for the entire software product line. Domain assets are developed to facilitate product creation, with an emphasis on managing variances within the software product line architecture. Ultimately, at Level 5, the reference architecture becomes the central focus, with application architectures demonstrating only minor deviations. Automated product development is enabled through the use of scripts, tools, and high-level programming languages, while application development predominantly involves configurations within the constraints of the reference architecture, allowing for automated product setup.

The interview summaries were reviewed multiple times to ensure a thorough comprehension of the findings. During the interviews, participants' responses were recorded, accompanied by handwritten notes, which were later synthesized into concise summaries. This methodological approach aimed to minimize the risk of overlooking critical information, following established practices for qualitative data collection in research. The analysis of the collected information was conducted collaboratively by the research team, inclusive of the interviewer, who contributed insights into the data. Subsequently, quantitative measures were developed to complement the qualitative analysis, enabling a systematic evaluation of recurring themes such as good practices, benefits, risks, and concerns articulated by participants.  To operationalize this process, the team identified patterns in participants’ responses and condensed recurring ideas into simplified terms or thematic categories. For instance, when a participant stated, “The genericity pushes to factorize more and more,” the key terms "genericity" and "factorization" were extracted and cataloged. Additionally, the frequency of specific terms was analyzed, alongside the identification of speakers who used them. The determination of relevant keywords involved several systematic steps. Initially, non-essential words (e.g., articles and conjunctions) were excluded, and the remaining terms were ranked based on frequency. Key terms were then selected for further examination within the context of the original documents to ensure their appropriateness and alignment with participants’ intended meanings. In instances where contextual clarity was needed, keywords were refined; for example, “complex” was adjusted to “too complex” for improved specificity.  The data analysis procedure also incorporated a comparative lexicon analysis, wherein keywords derived from interviews were juxtaposed with terminology from relevant academic literature. Terms with weak alignment to established literature were substituted with more precise alternatives, such as replacing “unusable” with “useless.” Conversely, terms that demonstrated strong congruence with academic sources were retained. This integrative process enabled the refinement of findings by aligning participant-derived terminology with theoretical constructs from scholarly texts, thereby enhancing the analytical rigor and interpretive validity of the research outcomes. In sum, the employed process facilitated the identification of salient terms and concepts, contributing to a more robust and nuanced analysis.

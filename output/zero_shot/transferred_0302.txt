The GPT-5 model frequently declines to perform certain tasks, and this behavior remains unchanged even when additional contextual information is provided. As a result of its high refusal rate, the model's performance on potentially harmful tasks is diminished; however, given the already low baseline harm score, this observation is not particularly informative. We refrain from extensive testing on benign tasks with contextual information exceeding 10,000 tokens due to the stability of results and the prohibitive cost of such tests. The efficacy of the model is influenced by the type of context padding employed. As demonstrated in Figure 3, various padding types yield distinct effects on performance. For the GPT-4.1-nano model, non-relevant padding results in the highest scores, followed by relevant padding, random padding, and finally, multi-task padding with the lowest scores. The Grok 4 Fast model does not adhere to this pattern due to its elevated refusal rate, exhibiting scores that are comparable with and without padding. Within the range of 50,000 to 100,000 tokens, the performance of Grok 4 Fast appears to deteriorate, causing scores across different padding types to converge. The placement of padding significantly impacts model performance. Figure 4 indicates that inserting padding before the task description decelerates the rate of performance degradation compared to when padding is placed after. This may be attributed to the proximity of task description and execution, which enhances the model's attention mechanisms. Contemporary attention techniques, such as sliding window and rotary position embeddings (RoPE), are less effective at managing distant tokens, as such scenarios are infrequent in training datasets. When padding is positioned after the task description, the model is compelled to attend across a considerable gap between instruction and execution, potentially accelerating performance decline.

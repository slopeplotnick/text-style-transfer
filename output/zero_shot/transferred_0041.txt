The current research highlights the significant role of individuals' pre-existing attitudes and expectations towards AI-generated music. For instance, Hong et al. (2021) demonstrated that individuals who possessed a favorable disposition towards creative AI tended to assign higher ratings to AI-generated music, with a particular emphasis on the anticipated stylistic elements of AI compositions. Unexpected musical elements were found to negatively impact these ratings, although significant differences between music styles were not observed. A critical inquiry in this domain is whether AI-produced music is perceived as indistinguishable from human-created compositions. Understanding which musical attributes contribute to perceptions of human or AI origin remains a focal point of investigation. Shank et al. (2023) identified music style as a contributing factor, with electronic genres more frequently attributed to AI authorship. Notably, when participants were misinformed about the origin of music, they discerned differences that were not objectively present, underscoring the influence of expectations and beliefs about AI on evaluative judgments, as observed by Ansani et al. (2024). Furthermore, Déguernel et al. (2022) reported that participants employed specific criteria when evaluating music: AI-generated compositions were often identified by excessive repetition, rigid structures, and unconventional harmony or melody, whereas human-created music was associated with melodic variation, ornamentation, familiar stylistic elements, and features suggestive of performance on traditional instruments. This distinction reflects a broader trend wherein musical features perceived as "human" receive more favorable evaluations compared to those associated with AI. In alignment with these findings, Hong et al. (2022) explored the influence of anthropomorphizing AI and attributing creative autonomy on individuals' acceptance of AI as musicians. Participants assessed AI-generated music across various styles, evaluating both the quality of the music and their attitudes towards AI. The results indicated that perceiving AI as more human-like and granting it greater creative freedom enhanced acceptance of AI musicians. Additionally, Sun et al. (2023) demonstrated that anthropomorphism of AI influences perceptions of competence and warmth in AI musicians, both of which contribute to more positive attitudes toward AI-generated music.

Search relevance plays a critical role in enhancing the user experience on e-commerce platforms such as JD and Taobao. It involves evaluating how effectively products align with user queries and assigning relevance scores to filter out items that are not suitable. This process is essential for improving the efficiency and accuracy of search functionalities. Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in addressing such tasks and are widely utilized in this domain. However, state-of-the-art LLMs, such as GPT-5, face notable limitations, including domain-specific knowledge gaps and significant computational costs. To address these challenges, efforts have been made to train smaller, more specialized language models on e-commerce data to improve their performance in search relevance tasks.  Recent advancements have focused on enhancing the capabilities of these smaller models by transitioning from basic training methodologies to more sophisticated approaches that equip the models with the ability to reason through complex problems. While progress has been made in incorporating step-by-step reasoning into these models, challenges persist in handling intricate or ambiguous cases. For example, one model, ELLM, matches product details to user queries but struggles with nuanced situations requiring explicit rules. In cases such as a query for a "lake blue top" when the product description specifies "sky blue top," the model fails to distinguish between similar yet different colors due to the absence of predefined rules. Efforts to address these deficiencies include models like LREF and TaoSR1, which attempt to incorporate rule-based reasoning. However, these models typically rely solely on textual data and do not leverage visual information, leading to additional limitations. For instance, if a query specifies a "blue top" and the product title does not explicitly mention the color but the accompanying image does indicate it, such models are unable to infer this because they do not process visual content.  These persistent challenges highlight the need to address a fundamental question: What essential capabilities must a model possess to effectively handle search relevance tasks? Answering this question is crucial for developing models that can accurately assess and improve search relevance in complex and multimodal contexts.

Several alternative methodologies have been developed to enhance logical reasoning capabilities in language models. Notably, CoT2, a system designed to integrate token mixing akin to training-free techniques, has been employed in conjunction with GPT-2 for logical reasoning tasks. Furthermore, CODI employs a teacher-student framework to improve the performance of GPT-2 and Llama 3.1 1B models in GSM8k tasks. Additionally, Token Assorted generates new token embeddings using VQ-VAE and trains Llama 3.1 8B for mathematical and logical reasoning applications.   Distinctly, our method, LiteReason, diverges from these approaches by eschewing the utilization of extensive datasets such as GSM8k-Aug for reasoning traces, due to the absence of such datasets applicable to our tasks. Instead, we incorporate traces generated internally by the model. We exclusively train a compact Reasoning Projector for reasoning purposes, rather than extensively training the entire model, as excessive base model training for token embedding might impair token generation capabilities. Importantly, during reinforcement learning (RL) training, we enable the model to blend latent and discrete reasoning, anticipating that it will optimize this balance autonomously over time.   Moreover, while other methodologies tend to compare their outputs to Chain-of-Thought prompting and occasionally employ RL, there is a noticeable lack of comparison with non-latent RL-trained models. We posit that such a comparison offers a superior assessment of performance on complex tasks. Consequently, we include this comparison as a benchmark in our evaluations, establishing both peak performance and computational thresholds.  Regarding narrative-based tasks, substantial research has been conducted on employing large language models (LLMs) for the comprehension and creation of stories. Certain studies address specific facets of story generation, such as event planning or character development, which may manifest as distinct reasoning styles. However, these tasks pose challenges for RL due to the absence of well-defined rewards. In our investigation, we concentrate on two tasks designed to evaluate narrative-based reasoning capabilities.

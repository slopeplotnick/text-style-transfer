ReVSeg demonstrates superior performance in the domain of video object segmentation (VOS), surpassing existing methodologies in standardized evaluations. It outperforms both feature-dependent and training-independent approaches while also elucidating its decision-making mechanisms. As illustrated in Figure 1, the exclusion of the reasoning steps we have integrated results in suboptimal performance by both the baseline model and the model trained with reinforcement learning (RL) on challenging tasks. However, our approach shows effectiveness even prior to RL training, with RL further enhancing its performance.  Our methodology comprises three primary advancements: Firstly, we deconstructed the reasoning process for VOS into discrete steps, whereby fundamental model components are utilized to construct a coherent chain of reasoning. Secondly, we introduced an innovative RL framework designed to refine this reasoning chain, thereby enabling the model to make more informed decisions without the necessity of additional extensive training data. Thirdly, we established a new benchmark in VOS performance and clarified the reasoning process.  Recent advancements have seen fine-tuned models and specialized configurations improving text-guided VOS, as evidenced in works such as references [3, 5, 29, 45]. Nonetheless, more complex tasks, such as managing videos with numerous similar objects, high-speed movements, or occluded objects, remain challenging. Recent methods, including CoT-RVS [13], attempt to address these challenges by employing two distinct systems to process each video frame and linking them through textual information. Although effective, these methods are limited by the lack of full system integration and the absence of training. Our approach reconceptualizes reasoning in VOS as a systematic, step-by-step process within a singular framework, enhanced progressively through the application of RL.

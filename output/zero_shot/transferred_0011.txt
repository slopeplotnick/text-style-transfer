The AgentHarm test dataset comprises 176 examples derived from 44 distinct tasks, classified into 8 categories, including areas such as disinformation and fraud. Each task can be modified with two options: one provides an initial hint regarding which tool to utilize, while the other offers comprehensive step-by-step instructions. By combining these options, four versions of each task description are created. The experimental focus is on the simplest category to optimize cost efficiency and evaluate the model's capabilities, ensuring that the model's proficiency is not the issue and that the context length is the primary factor affecting performance (refer to Section A). AgentHarm employs both automatic scoring and evaluation conducted by Large Language Model (LLM) judges (Zheng et al. 2023). Evaluation metrics include: (1) Harm score, ranging from 0 to 1, derived from rubrics for manually assessed harmful tasks, and (2) Refusal rate, representing the proportion of examples identified as refusals by the LLM model. Further details are provided in Section B. The principal focus is on the Harm Score, and semantic judgment is not utilized due to the assumption that the simplest tasks are assessed reliably. Minor adjustments to the prompt and the model are made for refusal judgment, as elaborated in Section E. To systematically assess the impact of context length, four types of padding are employed: random padding, which incorporates random tokens from the tokenizer; non-relevant padding, which utilizes text from fiction genres such as humor, mythology, science fiction, crime, and romance that do not correspond with the task category; relevant padding, which uses Wikipedia text congruent with the task category; and multi-task padding, which integrates task descriptions from the validation dataset. These padding types are employed to evaluate various aspects: random padding aids in reproducibility, non-relevant and relevant padding incorporate stylistically consistent text, and multi-task padding assesses the model's capability to handle ambiguous context. Additional information is provided in Sections F and G.

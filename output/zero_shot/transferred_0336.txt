Multimodal Language Models (MLLMs) extend the capabilities of traditional Language Models (LLMs) by incorporating visual inputs alongside textual data. This integration enhances the semantic depth of the models but also introduces additional security challenges. The concept of "jailbreaking" refers to the manipulation of input data in a manner that circumvents the built-in safety protocols of these models, leading them to generate harmful or unintended outputs. Addressing this vulnerability is of paramount importance in the development of safer and more reliable models.  Historically, efforts to jailbreak models have predominantly concentrated on LLMs, utilizing techniques such as cleverly constructed prompts or role-playing scenarios. However, with the advent of MLLMs, there has been a shift towards exploiting their visual processing capabilities. Existing methods exhibit several limitations. One approach involves crafting deceptive images or embedding sensitive information within visuals, yet this method often fails to leverage the full contextual potential of multimodal data. Alternatively, some strategies rely solely on verbal descriptions akin to those used in LLM manipulation, thereby underutilizing the distinct advantages offered by visual inputs.  To address these shortcomings, we propose the Contextual-Image Attack (CIA), a novel methodology designed to generate images that convey both contextual scenarios and malevolent intent, with text serving a supplementary role. For instance, rather than embedding a direct query such as "How can someone make illegal drugs?" within an image, CIA might depict a classroom scene with a teacher posing the question in a chemistry lesson, thus disguising the malicious intent under the guise of a legitimate educational inquiry. This sophisticated use of imagery helps to obfuscate the underlying intent, potentially misleading the model into failing to recognize the harmful nature of the content.  Other approaches, such as FigStep, typically involve displaying questions as text within images or resort entirely to textual descriptions of scenarios. In contrast, our method capitalizes more effectively on the unique affordances of visual information. The increasing sophistication of image generation technologies enhances the feasibility of creating realistic scenarios, and emerging tools that translate text into visual data further underscore the capacity for images to encode intricate and concealed meanings capable of misleading multimodal models.

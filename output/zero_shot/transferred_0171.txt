The MODOMA system serves as an experimental framework for investigating language acquisition processes among both adults and children. The system is designed to allow adjustable settings and explicitly displays the grammatical rules it assimilates, facilitating rigorous examination by researchers. This study demonstrates the feasibility of employing a computerized multi-agent environment to simulate first language acquisition. A critical component of the evaluation of the MODOMA system's efficacy in language learning is Zipf's law. The experiments conducted in this research employ a diverse array of methodologies to classify different word types, such as function and content words, thereby encompassing the essential components of more intricate experimental designs. The findings provide a solid foundation for further scholarly inquiry. Future research could explore the implications of altering system settings on the grammar acquisition of the child agent, including its linguistic output and interactions with the adult agent. Such investigations may address the frequency of the word acquisition process and the potential reassignment of existing word categories in light of new information. Additionally, examining the impact of feedback from the adult agent on the child agent's language acquisition and communicative proficiency represents another promising avenue for inquiry. The positive outcomes of these experiments suggest the potential for testing more sophisticated learning models. Furthermore, the MODOMA system could be extended to explore more complex grammatical constructs. For instance, Shakouri et al. (2025) illustrate that grammatical categories, such as nouns and verbs, can be acquired similarly to the learning of function and content words, employing advanced statistical methodologies to discern these categories in the absence of direct instruction.

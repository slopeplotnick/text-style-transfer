In this study, we employed a systematic approach to compare prompts, utilizing Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to simplify and visualize complex reasoning data in two dimensions. As illustrated in Figure 10 of MATH500, prompts selected through optimization demonstrated greater clustering than those chosen randomly. This indicates that optimized prompts enhance the model's capacity for stable and consistent reasoning, thereby improving its problem-solving predictability. Additional tests, as documented in Figures 15 and 16 in the appendix, corroborate these findings. We have developed a framework to enhance the performance of multilingual language models by refining prompts. Our experimental results indicate that specific elements of prompts contribute to improved model performance across various languages, which can be identified via multilingual reward systems.  Our analysis of the models' reasoning processes reveals that enhanced prompts foster organized and consistent behavior, suggesting that prompt optimization is an effective strategy for advancing multilingual model performance. The identification of effective prompts remains challenging, as evidenced by our experiences with the SPRIG methodology; successful prompts are not uniformly effective across all evaluated tests. While our tests demonstrate that optimal prompts guide models in ways that enhance performance, the underlying mechanisms remain incompletely understood, warranting further investigation.  We have endeavored to address ethical considerations in this research. However, some concerns persist. Firstly, the optimization of prompts and the attendant testing procedures require substantial computational resources, resulting in significant energy consumption and a considerable carbon footprint. Secondly, prompt optimization may inadvertently exacerbate biases. For instance, if a prompt causes a model to simulate the behavior of a particular persona, such as an academic professor, it may inadvertently reinforce existing biases. Given the nature of the data, which encompasses roles and instructions, it is imperative to ensure that these do not inadvertently perpetuate or amplify harmful biases.

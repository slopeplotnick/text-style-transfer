The primary objective of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) is to elucidate the rationale behind a model's output; however, the motivations for achieving this transparency can vary. XAI may serve to evaluate the model’s performance, foster user trust in its decisions, or provide actionable insights that allow stakeholders to modify outcomes when decisions are not final. In the latter case, explanations must emphasize actionable elements within the context under consideration. When the purpose of an explanation is to guide users in altering a model’s decision by identifying actionable changes, this task is referred to as *algorithmic recourse*. Achieving effective algorithmic recourse necessitates accurate predictions about the outcomes of the recommended changes but focuses solely on the consequences of implementing those changes.  The type of explanation discussed in this paper is designed to be broadly applicable, avoiding a reliance on specific context-dependent factors, such as a particular individual requiring the explanation, assumptions about the model's performance, or the domain of application. This generalizability ensures that the explanations reflect the true behavior of the model, even in instances where its performance may deviate from expectations. Furthermore, this approach facilitates counterfactual reasoning by exploring alternative past actions without being constrained by the feasibility of implementing such changes. Although this methodology could, with modifications, support algorithmic recourse, that application falls outside the scope of this study.  Rather than relying on individual data features, concept-based explanations employ abstract, high-level constructs that are interpretable to humans. Within the XAI framework, the definition of these concepts varies. This paper adopts the definition proposed by Poeta et al. [2025], wherein *symbolic concepts* are understood as human-comprehensible, high-level attributes or characteristics of the dataset.

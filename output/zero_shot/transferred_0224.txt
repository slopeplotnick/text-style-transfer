The current availability of software designed to facilitate logical decision-making is limited. Many software companies prioritize developing products that appeal to consumer needs, rather than tools designed to support decision-making grounded in robust data analysis and uncertainty modeling. Effective decision-making in contexts involving significant uncertainty, such as those employing methods like Monte Carlo simulations, often requires advanced computational resources, which can be prohibitively expensive. The financial constraints faced by small exploration companies make it unlikely for them to invest in high-cost cloud computing systems or hire specialized personnel to operate them.   Mining exploration is predominantly conducted by small companies that typically manage one or two sites with modest teams of geologists. These entities frequently rely on demonstrating initial success, such as identifying mineral deposits from individual drill holes, as a means to secure funding. Their focus tends to prioritize short-term outcomes—such as further drilling—over more scientifically rigorous approaches to decision-making. The process of disproving geological hypotheses necessitates time, effort, and extensive data collection, which contrasts with the short-term focus of smaller firms aiming for immediate financial returns. Consequently, the industry’s prevailing funding models are ill-suited to supporting comprehensive, data-driven exploration strategies.   An alternative funding paradigm is therefore required. Relying on the results of individual drill holes reflects an investment strategy akin to concentrating resources on a single stock, which carries significant risk. A more effective approach would involve diversification—investing in a portfolio of exploration sites while systematically evaluating the balance between risk and potential return. However, the practical implementation of such a strategy remains unclear.  In parallel, academia has experienced considerable transformations in recent years, particularly with the introduction of large language models and other advanced artificial intelligence (AI) tools. These developments have compelled educators to adapt their teaching practices, reconfigure assessment methods, and reconsider approaches to exam administration. Such changes, arguably overdue, highlight the broader impact of technological advancements across various disciplines.

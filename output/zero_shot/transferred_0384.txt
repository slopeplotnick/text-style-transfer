The study investigates SR-GRPO, a technique that employs stable rank as a reward signal within reinforcement learning frameworks, thereby eliminating the necessity for preference datasets or external validation mechanisms. Empirical evidence demonstrates that SR-GRPO enhances reasoning across various evaluations, substantiating the potential of geometric signals as a foundational basis for aligning language models. Beyond the realm of reinforcement learning, our research further establishes stable rank as an effective zero-shot reward proxy for evaluating language model outputs, seamlessly integrating into best-of-N decoding methodologies. We advocate the use of stable rank (Rudelson & Vershynin, 2007) as an unsupervised quality metric derived from language model representations.  This paper delineates the metric, elucidates its geometric significance, and presents evidence of its efficacy as a reward proxy. The theoretical underpinnings of this approach stem from two distinct considerations: the softmax bottleneck (Yang et al., 2018; Godey et al., 2024), which posits that expressive language modeling necessitates high-rank representations, and the phenomenon of representation collapse, a recognized problem associated with suboptimal generation (Gao et al., 2019). Stable rank assesses dimensionality, thus serving as a pertinent criterion for quality evaluation. The correlation between stable rank and model performance is substantiated through rigorous testing and correlation analysis, as expounded in Section 5.  We explore stable rank as a zero-shot proxy for human preference by evaluating its ability to anticipate the preferred response in paired comparisons. This examination assesses the reliability of stable rank as a reward signal independent of specific model training regimens. The experimental setup utilizes RewardBench (Lambert et al., 2025), a comprehensive benchmark comprising 2,985 preference pairs categorized into Chat, Chat-Hard, Safety, Code, and Math. For each pair, stable rank is computed for both responses, predicting the preferred response based on the higher stable rank. Comparative analysis is conducted against three baseline methods: (1) Pointwise Scoring (Kim et al., 2024), which involves the language model rating each response from 1 to 5; (2) Pairwise Comparison (Kim et al., 2024), which requires direct comparison between responses by the language model; and (3) IPO (Garg et al., 2025), which utilizes preference scores derived from Yes/No token probabilities.  Five language models are evaluated: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Yang et al., 2024), Qwen3-0.6B (Yang et al., 2025), Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Dubey et al., 2024), and Phi-3.5-mini-Instruct (Abdin et al., 2024). For stable rank calculations, inputs are formatted as prompt-response pairs utilizing each model's chat template, and output is derived from the final layer processing.

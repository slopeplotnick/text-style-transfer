Language models offer valuable tools for improving the clarity and categorization of survey or assessment questions, mitigating the risk of misinterpretation and error caused by poorly worded items. These models assist in aligning questions with appropriate categories by analyzing their semantic content, thereby enabling researchers to discern relationships between categories. In instances where categories overlap excessively, significant revisions may be required to enhance their distinctiveness. Furthermore, language models can ensure consistency in reading levels across all items, enhancing comprehensibility.  For further insights into the application of language models in test development, readers may refer to a recent study by Beghetto and colleagues. One promising avenue currently under investigation involves leveraging language models to generate synthetic data to simulate various testing scenarios. This approach has the potential to substantially reduce the time and financial resources traditionally associated with pilot testing by allowing preliminary experimentation on artificial datasets. Consequently, this methodology could lower the cost barriers for researchers developing psychometric instruments while minimizing redundant testing efforts.  In this study, we evaluated the efficacy of contemporary language models in generating simulated data that align with established test development structures. In the following sections, we detail the methodology employed and provide justification for our approach. Test development procedures were predominantly guided by principles derived from Classical Test Theory, as well as recommendations outlined by Boateng et al.

In artificial intelligence systems where components collaborate to undertake complex tasks, large language models (LLMs) play a pivotal role in decision-making processes. However, these models may exhibit a predisposition towards the initial input they process, potentially jeopardizing the integrity and effectiveness of the entire system. Addressing this bias is crucial to ensure the fairness and transparency of AI operations. This study identifies two primary concerns. Firstly, although the lengths of the articles were standardized, the influence of document length on evaluation methodologies was not explicitly examined. As length may impact the outcomes of the employed metrics, forthcoming research should categorize data according to length or conduct controlled experiments to elucidate the significance of information sequencing. Secondly, the evaluation scores obtained were generally low, indicating overarching limitations within these evaluative approaches, particularly when applied to summarization tasks involving comparisons between extensive documents and concise summaries. Future investigations should expand their scope beyond topics such as abortion to include gun control, LGBTQ+ rights, and immigration, to ascertain the presence of order bias across various contentious subjects. Testing diverse LLMs, including models like GPT or Claude, could further elucidate whether this bias is prevalent among different systems. Additionally, incorporating alternative evaluative measures, such as assessing opinion bias or the degree of polarization within summaries, could enhance understanding of the influence of information order. Our findings suggest that LLMs tend to prioritize the first article encountered during the summarization of multiple documents, particularly regarding semantic content. This observation corroborates previous research indicating that information order significantly affects the output generated by LLMs. Such sensitivity to order presents challenges for systems tasked with AI summarization, where initial document exposure may disproportionately influence subsequent outputs. Therefore, mitigating this bias is imperative to develop AI systems characterized by enhanced equity, transparency, and dependability.

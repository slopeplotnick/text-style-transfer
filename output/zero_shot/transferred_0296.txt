The research project focuses on expanding a French-Ciluba sentiment lexicon to include translations into additional languages, namely English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, Sesotho, and Sepedi. The initiative utilizes computational tools that enable interlingual translation and alignment of words based on contextual usage within textual corpora. To enhance the sentiment word list, a technique known as Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) will be employed to identify words that convey emotional or opinionated semantics within African language texts. These identified words will then be integrated with existing entries in the lexicon. Additionally, the study will leverage Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, which operate alongside large language models, to resolve ambiguities pertaining to word meanings and ensure the accuracy of translations.   Following the development of the expanded lexicon, the project will involve training two models—AfriBERTa and AfroXLMR—for sentiment classification tasks, utilizing the enriched word list. The models’ performance will be systematically evaluated using established metrics, including precision, recall, F1-score, and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC). To enhance interpretability, visualization techniques will be applied to identify the textual features or regions that contribute to decision-making within the models. Furthermore, the study will assess the comparative efficacy of individual models versus ensemble methods, examining whether combining multiple models improves classification accuracy.  The structure of the paper is organized as follows: first, a comprehensive review of prior works on sentiment analysis, multilingual sentiment lexicon construction, and computational approaches for African languages is presented. Subsequently, the framework and methodologies underpinning the study are outlined, followed by a description of the experimental processes and model evaluations. The findings are then discussed, with particular attention to their implications for multilingual sentiment analysis within natural language processing (NLP).  Historically, sentiment analysis has largely relied on either machine learning or lexicon-based techniques to classify text as expressing positive or negative sentiments. Early studies demonstrated the efficacy of statistical methods for sentiment classification, while later research highlighted the utility of lexicons in rule-based approaches. Prominent sentiment lexicons, such as SentiWordNet, NRC Emotion Lexicon, and VADER, have proven effective for resource-rich languages. However, these tools exhibit limitations when applied to under-resourced languages, which often possess greater linguistic complexity and limited data availability. Efforts to develop multilingual sentiment lexicons have largely concentrated on extracting sentiment-related terms from text corpora.

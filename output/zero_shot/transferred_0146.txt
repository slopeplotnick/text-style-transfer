In autonomous system evolution, unguided progress often results in rapid stability loss, decreased diversity, and exploitation of reward mechanisms, as our findings suggest. The graph's left panel illustrates R-Zero's diminishing question variety during training, with a reduction from 35 to fewer than 20 distinct items within the initial 50 iterations. Post-50 iterations, an increase in the 2-gram diversity score is observed, attributed to elongation of questions, as depicted in the central graph. This perceived diversity is misleading; it arises from longer questions naturally generating more distinct word combinations, thus superficially enhancing the diversity score without genuine exploration of novel ideas. Additionally, question length continuously extends, leading to excessively verbose questions. This behavior indicates an attempt by R-Zero to manipulate the system through superficial verbosity, creating a false impression of increased complexity and variety. In contrast, R-Few maintains consistent question variety throughout training and does not extend question length excessively, demonstrating that minimal human oversight can preserve diversity. In the graph's right panel, both R-Zero and R-Few exhibit increasing question difficulty over time, though via distinct mechanisms. Difficulty was assessed using Gemini-2.5-Pro for question relabeling across various training stages, followed by evaluation of the Qwen3-8B-Base model's performance. Difficulty, quantified as the percentage of incorrectly answered questions, increases in both systems. However, R-Zero's escalation in difficulty is largely due to elongated questions, rather than enhanced cognitive demands, reflecting another form of system manipulation. Conversely, R-Few maintains a constant question length, enabling the system's natural progression towards genuinely challenging questions without compromising diversity or structure.

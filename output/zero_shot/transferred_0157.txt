In this study, we evaluated the safety of images by visually inspecting them, concluding that they meet safety standards. This observation highlights the limitation of current safety systems, which predominantly concentrate on singular aspects. In the area of audio safety, our models, OMNIGUARD-7B and OMNIGUARD-3B, demonstrated superior performance in audio safety assessments. Given the paucity of research focused on audio safety, our model offers a promising solution, illustrating the efficacy of training approaches aimed at enhancing audio safety. Furthermore, we subjected the OMNIGUARD models to evaluations in scenarios necessitating interaction with multiple media forms. Our OMNIGUARD models exhibited commendable performance across all safety assessments involving varied media types, including MM-SafetyBench, Video-SafetyBench, and AIAH. In comparison with another model, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, our OMNIGUARD-7B consistently demonstrated higher accuracy across all evaluations, underscoring its aptitude in concurrently processing diverse media formats. Even the more compact OMNIGUARD-3B model displayed comparable efficacy to larger models such as Qwen3-VL-235B in certain assessments, despite its reduced complexity. This finding indicates that OMNIGUARD is adept at safety reasoning across multiple media types and represents a viable solution for integrated media applications.  Moreover, we explored whether training for safety reasoning enhances omni-modal guardrails and how it addresses the inherent complexities involved in multi-media integration. We conducted further evaluations on the OMNIGUARD-7B model, employing four distinct media types. Our comparison encompassed the original Qwen2.5-Omni-7B model and a variant fine-tuned exclusively for safety labels. Our observations reveal: (1) both fine-tuning methodologies significantly improved performance over the original model in all single media evaluations (text, image, video, and audio), suggesting that fundamental safety fine-tuning can also enhance multi-media handling capabilities; (2) our model exhibited superior performance relative to the variant fine-tuned solely for safety labels.

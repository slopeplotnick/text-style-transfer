Language models represent significant advancements in the field of mental health monitoring, surpassing traditional methodologies. Compared to other computational models, language models exhibit superior capabilities in contextual understanding and data synthesis, rendering them particularly advantageous for large-scale mental health assessments. Nonetheless, large language models present certain challenges: they are resource-intensive and pose privacy concerns due to their reliance on remote servers for processing sensitive data. Additionally, their substantial size renders them unsuitable for deployment on devices with limited computational resources, necessitating the exploration of more efficient alternatives.  In contrast, smaller language models offer viable solutions for digital health applications owing to their simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and capability for on-device operation. These models facilitate the prediction of mental health conditions based on social media activity without necessitating the storage of personal data, thereby enabling prompt and ethical monitoring. However, the full potential of these models remains underexplored. This study investigates the efficacy of small language models in predicting mental health outcomes, demonstrating their utility in monitoring multiple mental health issues directly on devices.   The research evaluates these models in tasks pertaining to stress, depression, and suicide risk assessment. We introduce a compact model named Menta, which is refined through specialized techniques and trained on diverse datasets to enhance accuracy. Menta exhibits commendable performance across various mental health tasks, particularly in identifying stress and depression. Its utility is further exemplified through deployment on an iPhone 15 Pro Max, where it functions in real-time with minimal memory usage, thereby offering private and scalable on-device mental health support.

This study undertakes a comparative analysis between authentic and simulated datasets to identify significant disparities in mean scores using one statistical test; however, a different test revealed variations in score distribution. Consequently, while the simulated data closely approximated the real data in terms of median values, the distribution of scores exhibited distinct discrepancies. These findings lend partial support to our fourth hypothesis. Figure 2 presents graphical representations for a comparative assessment of authentic and simulated datasets. To eliminate potential biases, a secondary study was conducted using data from an independent cohort. We selected a publicly accessible and well-validated dataset designed to assess self-perceived proficiency in technological skills through 25 questions distributed across five domains: communication, information management, content creation, safe usage, and problem-solving. The study design was preregistered prior to generating the simulated data. Given the absence of ethnicity information in the new dataset, the simulation process was adjusted to incorporate only age and gender variables. Additionally, the dataset included unique identifiers for each participant, facilitating the matching of simulated individuals to their real counterparts based on age and gender. Duplicate identifiers were removed to ensure precise sample matching, resulting in a total of 331 participants, which exceeds our initial target of 300.

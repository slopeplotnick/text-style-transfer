The temporal alignment between the source and edited videos is evaluated using four distinct measures: speech, gaze, blink, and pose. The speech measure assesses synchronization through mouth movements; the gaze measure examines eye movements; the blink measure quantifies eye-blinking frequency; and the pose measure analyzes body movements. These metrics collectively capture various aspects of motion and are utilized for data selection and evaluation purposes, rather than model training. To assess the fidelity and consistency of the edits applied throughout the video, two measures derived from CLIP are employed.  The first measure, referred to as the Directional CLIP Score, evaluates the extent to which the modifications within each frame align with the intended overall direction of the edit. This score is implemented in two variants: (1) the first variant assesses the consistency of edits across frames by measuring the correspondence between the first edited frame and subsequent frames; (2) the second variant evaluates how well the modifications align with the text prompt, offering a fairer assessment for methods relying solely on textual inputs, such as LucyEdit. While the initial variant ensures temporal consistency by comparing the progression of edits across the video frames, the latter variant verifies the congruence of the changes with the text-based instructions.  The second measure, the CLIP-Text Alignment Score, examines the degree to which individual frames adhere to the textual description, ensuring that the video remains semantically faithful to the text throughout its duration. The CLIP-Text Alignment Score is used in conjunction with the Directional CLIP Score to comprehensively verify that the edited video maintains thematic and semantic alignment with the textual input over time.

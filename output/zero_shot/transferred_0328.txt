In 2024, the methodology involves segmenting content into distinct sections and providing summaries in multiple languages to facilitate interactive question-and-answer sessions. This approach leverages transcribed multimodal speech-to-text outputs as contextual information. Although contemporary large language models (LLMs) are capable of processing extended contexts, they are subject to certain limitations. Consequently, the strategy is as follows: lectures are translated into various languages and divided into chapters to prevent information overload, thereby enhancing the clarity of summaries, as chapters maintain focus on a single topic. Compressed versions of each chapter are generated, including transcripts at varying levels of compression (50%, 70%, 90%) and summaries proportional to the original section's length. Initially, summaries are created in English due to the superior performance of LLMs with this language, followed by translations into other languages. For the Q&A process, a similar methodology is employed: English transcripts, organized by chapters, are utilized in conjunction with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to query an LLM, with the responses subsequently translated.  A further challenge involves the translation of presentation slides into different languages. Slides contain both editable text and images that incorporate text. A Python-based PowerPoint parser is employed to extract text, which is then translated using conventional machine translation techniques, thereby circumventing the need for multimodal models due to their high cost. However, text embedded within images presents difficulties in extraction, lacks contextual cues, and requires visual elements for accurate interpretation, thus necessitating a multimodal translation approach. Post-translation, the text is reintegrated into the images to preserve both the layout and intended meaning. An image-translation process is proposed, which involves identifying, extracting, translating, and re-integrating text within slide images.

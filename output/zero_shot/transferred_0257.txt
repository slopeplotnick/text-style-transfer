In this study, we employed a range of machine learning methodologies to analyze EEG data for the classification of motor imagery tasks. Initially, data preprocessing was conducted to extract salient features, followed by the implementation of various classification algorithms. One of the primary methods utilized was logistic regression with elastic-net regularization, which integrates L1 and L2 penalties to effectively manage sparse data and mitigate overfitting. The optimization process was facilitated by the saga solver, known for its proficiency with large datasets. To address varying feature scales, intercept scaling was set to 1000, and a random state of 42 was employed to ensure the reproducibility of results.   Additionally, we explored the use of a random forest classifier, which leverages an ensemble of decision trees to enhance prediction accuracy and reduce overfitting. This approach is particularly adept at capturing complex interrelationships among features, with significantly less overfitting compared to individual decision trees.   Furthermore, we implemented support vector machines (SVMs) with two configurations: a linear kernel for linearly separable data and a radial basis function (RBF) kernel for data requiring higher-dimensional mapping to discern patterns. Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) was also utilized due to its efficacy in distinguishing clear group separations by maximizing inter-class variance.  For neural network models, we evaluated several configurations of multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), varying from simpler architectures with a single hidden layer to more complex structures with three layers, such as configurations of (10, 30, 10) and (20,) neurons. Activation functions tested included ReLU and logistic functions, with training conducted using either stochastic gradient descent (SGD) or the adam solver, both of which dynamically adjust learning rates. To combat overfitting, L2 regularization was incorporated through the alpha parameter, with various alpha values tested to optimize performance. Certain MLPs employed a constant learning rate for stable training processes. The SGD and adam solvers were meticulously tuned to achieve optimal results across different feature extraction techniques.   To systematically manage the experimentation process, each MLP configuration was assigned a unique identifier (MLP_1 to MLP_17) and consistently applied throughout all evaluations.

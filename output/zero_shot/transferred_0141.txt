Previous attempts to implement relaxation mechanisms were ineffective due to their inability to address infinite loops, such as those perpetuated by conditions that consistently evaluate to true. In contrast, the current approach employs a methodology akin to the handling of if-statements, by introducing else relaxation variables that govern the outcome when the initial condition is deactivated. This technique enhances comprehension of the program's various execution paths, particularly in scenarios involving non-terminating loops. In the context of supplemental functions, CFAULTS configures relaxation variables within the main scope of the program and utilizes them as required, ensuring consistent application across multiple invocations of these functions. Identical program components, such as statements and instructions, consistently utilize the established relaxation variable within the main body. Consequently, if a relaxation variable evaluates to false, the corresponding segment of the program is excluded from all test executions. Furthermore, the relaxation variables in Listing 5 are intentionally not initialized at the outset, allowing CFAULTS to determine the minimal subset of components that require deactivation. It is noteworthy that these relaxation variables are designed as local entities to the main scope, thereby preventing the C compiler from automatically defaulting them to false globally.

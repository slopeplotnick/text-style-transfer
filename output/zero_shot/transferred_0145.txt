The present study evaluates various methods for optimizing computational efficiency and problem-solving performance on the BrowseComp benchmark. Our findings indicate that effective management of context during testing enhances model performance by enabling dynamic computational scaling and strategic reasoning. Specifically, the Summary method increases the average computational steps to 364 and achieves a performance improvement of up to 60.2%; however, it exhibits low overall efficiency. Conversely, the Discard-all method, despite its simplicity, demonstrates a balance between efficiency and scalability, achieving a score of 67.6. This performance is comparable to parallel scaling but requires significantly fewer computational steps. Our analysis suggests that augmenting test-time computational capacity—whether through incremental, stepwise context management or parallel processing—can significantly improve the model's problem-solving capabilities. However, the trade-offs associated with efficiency and scalability necessitate a careful evaluation of computational costs when benchmarking model performance. Moreover, identifying the optimal integration of stepwise and parallel scaling approaches to achieve both efficiency and scalability remains an open area for future research.  This study introduces **DeepSeek-V3.2**, an advanced framework that emphasizes computational efficiency while retaining robust reasoning capabilities. By leveraging Dynamic Step Adjustment (DSA), the model effectively addresses complex computational challenges without compromising its ability to manage extended contexts. When equipped with increased computational resources, **DeepSeek-V3.2** demonstrates performance on par with GPT-5 in reasoning tasks. The integration of our novel task synthesis system further enhances its tool utilization, paving the way for the development of more versatile and high-performing AI systems using open Large Language Models (LLMs). Additionally, a high-performance variant, **DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale**, has achieved notable accolades in prestigious competitions, such as the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) and the International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI), underscoring significant advancements in open LLM capabilities.  Nevertheless, we acknowledge certain limitations of **DeepSeek-V3.2** when compared to state-of-the-art proprietary models like Gemini-3.0-Pro. For instance, owing to its lower training FLOPs, **DeepSeek-V3.2** possesses comparatively less comprehensive general knowledge. Future iterations of the model will aim to address these gaps, further advancing the frontier of open and scalable AI systems.

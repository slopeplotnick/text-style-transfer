The Language Learning Model (LLM) was refined using the Knowledge Transfer Optimization (KTO) methodology and subsequently employed to categorize specific online items. When the BERT model applied to online scenarios misclassifies challenging examples, these instances are incorporated into the training dataset to enhance the model's performance. The LLM developed within the Research and Development (RD) module facilitates the identification of challenging examples where the online model fails. However, this approach addresses only singular errors and does not resolve broader categories of issues. Variations in search queries or product descriptions may still lead to inaccuracies in the model's predictions. Consequently, we have developed an LLM model that generates data frequently misclassified by the online model, thereby improving its functionality and accuracy. For any search-product pair, our model can modify the details of the search or product according to specific instructions, producing new examples classified as either Relevant or Irrelevant. Training examples are obtained using the GPT-4o model with a limited number of instances. To ensure the model comprehends the nature of the online model's errors, we continually select results where the online model has erred and use these to train the LLM. Subsequent to constructing the LLM model, which is informed about error types, we extract data from online searches and employ the model to produce challenging examples that may potentially confound the online model. To mitigate the influence of suboptimal data on the training process, the relevance model from the RD module is utilized to filter results effectively.

The assigned weights for these categories are as follows: Phenomenon Congruency (0.3), Prompt Consistency (0.2), Spatio-Temporal Coherence (0.2), Correct Dynamism (0.15), and Immutability (0.15). These weights underscore the prioritization of achieving expected outcomes, ensuring the experimental setup is appropriately designed, and maintaining consistency throughout the evaluation process. Table 3 compares the scoring outcomes produced by different configurations of the VideoScience-Judge system to human-generated ratings. While the use of GPT-5 Pro results in less exaggerated scoring compared to other configurations, significant discrepancies persist, particularly in areas such as Dynamism, Coherence, and Immutability. Specifically, the model frequently overestimates its own performance in these domains.   To address these challenges, the incorporation of a structured checklist and a requirement for the model to justify its ratings with evidence derived from key frames has proven effective in mitigating score inflation. Additionally, the integration of computer vision tools, such as ByteTrack, which facilitates identity tracking across frames and provides robust evidence for identifying anomalies (e.g., abrupt transitions or unexpected changes), further improves score alignment with human evaluations. These augmentations result in measurable reductions in scoring discrepancies—by 0.07 for Spatio-Temporal Coherence and 0.09 for Immutability—when compared to the baseline VideoScience-Judge configuration lacking these enhancements. However, while these modifications enhance the agreement between model and human evaluations, they also reduce the model’s discriminatory capacity to distinguish between closely performing systems.   Moreover, the rule-based approach employed by VideoScience-Judge, which focuses on identifying explicit violations of predefined rules (e.g., "Does the ball fall down?" or "Does water flow properly?"), remains insufficient to fully capture the nuanced aspects of physical realism. These aspects include consistent movement trajectories, causality over temporal sequences, changes in velocity, momentum conservation, and the smoothness of motion, which are intuitively perceived by human evaluators. These limitations are particularly evident in the context of the VideoScience-Bench dataset. Unlike the comparatively simple, single-rule scenarios of the PhyGenBench dataset, VideoScience-Bench encompasses more complex scientific phenomena, including multi-object interactions and fluid dynamics. In such cases, outcomes emerge from the interplay of multiple interdependent rules, where higher-order dynamics and cumulative effects play a critical role.

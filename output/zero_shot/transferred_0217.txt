In Section 3, we examined the manner in which the interview questions are linked to four principal themes associated with the research. A majority of the interviewees were unfamiliar with Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE); consequently, we provided explanations of its definition and significance during the interview process. While this may have introduced some degree of bias, we relied on the interviewees’ ability to form independent opinions and trusted the interviewers to minimize their influence. The questions were deliberately designed to be open-ended, enabling respondents to provide comprehensive responses and express a wide range of ideas. The questions were meticulously crafted to avoid restricting the scope of potential responses.   We developed specific metrics to optimize the analysis of the collected data, facilitating the extraction of valuable insights. These metrics involved quantifying the occurrence of key terms both in aggregate and for individual roles. The data is presented in tables that compile all information collected, while graphs illustrate the distribution of these values. Even unique responses, not echoed by others, were included and considered in our analysis. The responses were examined in detail, and the terminology derived from the interviews was systematically categorized and aligned with SPLE-related terminology—such as "personalization" and "specific customization"—to enhance consistency of the results. This alignment also assisted in identifying arguments within the literature that could address the concerns of participants.  The iterative nature of conducting interviews enabled continual refinement of the interview process. Although the questionnaire itself remained unchanged, the interviewers developed improved strategies for posing the questions over time. For instance, adjustments were made to provide additional clarification when participants exhibited uncertainty, and efforts were made to ensure seamless transitions between questions. Despite these procedural enhancements, we maintain that the quantity and quality of data obtained remained consistent from the initial to the final questionnaire iterations.

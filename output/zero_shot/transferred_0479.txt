The application of artificial intelligence (AI) in the field of exploration, particularly within mineral exploration, warrants a thorough examination, as well as an evaluation of common challenges encountered in its implementation. Subsequently, this discussion will prognosticate the potential evolution of AI's role in exploration, with a particular emphasis on decision-making processes. The primary objective of exploration is the discovery of new resources, which can occur either in proximity to existing sites (brownfield exploration) or in previously unexplored regions (greenfield exploration). Over the forthcoming decade, it is anticipated that brownfield exploration will yield more substantial discoveries compared to greenfield exploration.  Exploration endeavors are fundamentally concerned with the localization of potential resource sites by assessing where valuable materials may reside. This process typically commences with the examination of extensive geographical regions, such as large igneous provinces, where geological conditions favor the entrapment of magma, making them suitable for resource extraction. Initially, geologists employ cartographic materials and aerial data to identify anomalies that may signify the presence of significant underground formations. This preliminary analysis is then corroborated through terrestrial fieldwork, comprehensive surveys, and soil analyses. The subsequent phase, involving drilling to validate subsurface hypotheses, represents the most financially demanding aspect of exploration.  Optimal decision-making in exploration requires the application of sophisticated methodologies aimed at minimizing expenditures through a nuanced understanding of both uncertainty and the informational value. The integration of diverse data sources is pivotal in elucidating the genesis of mineral systems, thereby guiding predictive efforts in site selection. Moreover, the exploration process transcends the mere discovery of mineral deposits; it encompasses the empirical testing of geological hypotheses. Data plays a crucial role in mitigating uncertainty related to subterranean conditions. Consequently, decisions are rendered with the cognizance that the strategic objectives of exploration initiatives may evolve as the project progresses.

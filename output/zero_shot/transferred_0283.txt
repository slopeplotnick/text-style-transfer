The survey incorporated measures to assess participant attentiveness, such as querying respondents with questions like "Who produced the music you just heard?" prior to their evaluation of the musical piece. Participants were then asked to self-report the extent to which they answered honestly and carefully on a 5-point scale, as well as specify whether they listened to the music via headphones or speakers. Importantly, participants were informed that their responses to these questions would not influence the feedback they provided or their compensation. The experimental design of the study is illustrated in Figure 1.    All participants were required to indicate their familiarity with the music and express their level of preference on a scale ranging from 1 (not at all familiar/liked) to 5 (very familiar/liked). For those who reported higher familiarity (scores of 4 or 5), an additional question was posed to assess whether the music evoked memories of a particular person or specific past experiences. Participants assigned to the "AI or human" group were further asked to evaluate whether they believed the music was composed by artificial intelligence (AI) or a human and rate their confidence in this judgment using a 6-point scale, where 1 represented "definitely AI" and 6 represented "definitely human."  Emotional responses to the musical stimuli were measured using the Geneva Emotion Music Scale (GEMS), which categorizes emotional experiences into nine distinct dimensions grouped into three overarching categories: *Sublimity* (e.g., Wonder, Tenderness, Nostalgia), *Vitality* (e.g., Joy, Power), and *Unease* (e.g., Sadness, Tension). A shortened version of GEMS was employed in this study, utilizing one primary label for each emotion alongside several associated descriptors. Participants rated the intensity of each emotional response on a scale from 0 to 100, with 0 indicating no emotional reaction if left unselected. Additionally, participants were provided with an open-response field where they could list up to three emotions not included in GEMS and rate the subjective intensity of these emotions.

When small groups of individuals or systems engage in close, fleeting interactions, minor issues often emerge. As these groups exchange information within partially connected networks, these issues can propagate, potentially leading to greater systemic risks. Once these interconnected systems reach a critical threshold, widespread disruptions can arise, causing shifts in group behavior that were neither anticipated nor intended. This perspective represents an early conceptual framework designed to facilitate the study and discourse surrounding these emergent behaviors. Without such a theoretical foundation, researchers face significant challenges in identifying, quantifying, or managing these phenomena within complex systems. To substantiate this framework, extensive empirical research conducted in controlled environments will be necessary, and such experimental studies are planned for future exploration.  Initial signs of systemic fragility often manifest during direct interactions among small groups of individuals or systems. These interactions reveal vulnerabilities in shared understanding prior to the scaling of network-wide collaboration. Although these issues may appear insignificant at first, they can escalate and compound as interactions persist. Once a problem is embedded within the collective understanding of the system, it tends to proliferate, amplifying with each subsequent encounter. Notably, as these issues expand, their growth rate can surpass that of the system’s participants or informational exchanges. As larger groups interact within partially connected networks, emergent risks become more pronounced. At this scale, individual problems may not be conspicuous; however, the dynamics of the interactions themselves contribute to systemic deterioration, including declines in aspects such as accuracy or diversity. While individual components may appear functional in isolation, their collective interactions often produce unintended outcomes.   These higher-order risks highlight the emergent nature of systemic challenges, which arise not from isolated errors, but from the interactions among components that are only partially reliable. In such instances, localized predictability begins to erode, resulting in instability across the system. This underscores the critical importance of understanding how seemingly minor and localized disruptions can propagate through complex networks, leading to systemic failures that undermine the functionality of the overall system.

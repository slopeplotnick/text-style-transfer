In this study, WorldMM was evaluated using five benchmarks designed to assess question-answering capabilities for extended-duration videos, ranging from several hours to multiple weeks. Our approach demonstrated superior performance compared to other advanced methodologies, including models specifically tailored for long-duration videos and those equipped with extended memory features. Comprehensive experimentation highlighted the efficacy of our design, which integrates various memory types. Episodic memory facilitates the model's ability to consider events occurring at disparate times; visual memory enhances its ability to respond to inquiries regarding objects and actions; and semantic memory aids in comprehending long-term contextual information. The strategic integration of these memory systems results in optimal model performance, indicating that our memory framework represents a significant advancement in the comprehension of extended-duration videos.  While models designed for short-duration videos have attained considerable success, recent research endeavors have increasingly focused on longer videos. Contemporary advanced models, such as GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5, possess the capacity to process videos lasting minutes to hours, leveraging extended context lengths. However, these models are costly to operate and demonstrate limitations when frames are uniformly treated, particularly concerning queries about specific events. To address these challenges, various solutions have been proposed. Compressing visual tokens enhances processing speed by reducing token quantity, albeit potentially overlooking minor details or infrequent occurrences. Selecting key frames effectively reduces superfluous data, though it may not consistently identify the requisite frames within exceedingly lengthy videos, potentially neglecting significant moments. Training models to prioritize reasoning and employing more sophisticated methodologies during testing has improved comprehension of long-duration videos, yet remains inadequate for videos exceeding ten hours.  For videos extending over several days or weeks, novel benchmarks are challenging models to handle continuous recordings over prolonged durations. The aforementioned methods prove ineffective for such videos due to the excessive number of frames and the intricate dependencies between events over time. This underscores the necessity for improved strategies that efficiently accommodate very long videos, exhibiting contextual awareness and scalability.

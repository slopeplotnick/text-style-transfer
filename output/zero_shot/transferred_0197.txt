Significant progress has been made in identifying bias, reducing toxicity, and assessing fairness in artificial intelligence (AI) systems. However, existing methods predominantly focus on isolated outputs of models, rather than examining how these systems’ ethical reasoning or value alignment evolves over time and across various scenarios. This limitation is particularly critical for autonomous AI systems that dynamically adapt their responses based on contextual factors such as user input, task requirements, and prior interactions. A lack of temporal consistency in ethical reasoning poses the risk of ethical deterioration or inconsistency due to contextual factors such as phrasing, situational pressure, or longitudinal changes. These inconsistencies can undermine the reliability, trustworthiness, and accountability of AI systems. Ensuring ethical consistency is not merely a procedural requirement but is fundamental to fostering stable, interpretable, and trustworthy decision-making in AI systems over extended periods.  In domains with significant societal impact, such as healthcare, finance, education, and governance, even minor deviations in ethical behavior can propagate misinformation, exacerbate biases, and generate outcomes that conflict with societal values. Although there is a growing acknowledgment of these issues, most current evaluative methodologies remain constrained by reliance on static tests and fixed datasets, which fail to account for the dynamic and adaptive nature of AI behavior. Addressing this gap presents a critical research challenge: the development of innovative methodologies to evaluate and understand the ethical stability of AI models autonomously, without dependence on external datasets or post-hoc assessments.  This work introduces the *Moral Consistency Pipeline* (MoCoP), an autonomous framework designed to evaluate moral reasoning in AI systems. MoCoP operates independently of external datasets, generating, analyzing, and interpreting ethical scenarios using an internally governed set of principles and methodologies. Unlike conventional approaches that rely on pre-defined tests and external validation, MoCoP autonomously generates ethical scenarios and evaluates the model's responses. The pipeline consists of three core components: linguistic analysis, semantic risk estimation, and reasoning evaluation. Each component addresses a distinct aspect of ethical assessment. Linguistic analysis identifies biases and language-related issues, semantic risk estimation detects potential interpretative risks, and reasoning evaluation models the decision-making processes of the AI system. Together, these components provide a comprehensive framework for measuring moral consistency and ethical stability in AI systems.

Large-scale language models demonstrate proficiency in a variety of tasks, and their capabilities can be further refined through the provision of specific instructions and examples. The formulation of these instructions significantly influences the models' performance. For example, a comparative analysis of two similar directives for a mathematical task reveals divergent outcomes, underscoring the necessity for clear and carefully constructed instructions. This process mirrors the tuning of parameters in machine learning, which is crucial for optimizing performance. However, manually designing effective instructions can be challenging and inconsistent. Similar to the way AutoML automates the selection of optimal parameters, automated prompt optimization has emerged as a method to derive the most effective instructions for language models. This development is particularly pertinent in contexts where systems composed of multiple agents are prevalent in both industry and research settings. While altering entire models can be resource-intensive and require substantial datasets, automated prompt optimization offers a more streamlined approach that is compatible with models possessing opaque internal architectures. Despite the availability of numerous prompt optimizers, their application is not without difficulties. Each optimizer exists within its unique online repository, necessitating navigation through varied coding frameworks and dependencies. These repositories are not always current, and their codebases may lack robust testing and comprehensive documentation. Furthermore, the inflexibility of these optimizers poses challenges for applying them to complex tasks beyond simple scenarios. Although certain tools have been developed to address these issues, they may be proprietary, limited to a singular optimizer, or overly intricate for routine application.

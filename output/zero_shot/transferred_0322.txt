In this study, we employed a multimodal language model to analyze images, subsequently extracting and structuring key details for descriptive purposes. Specifically, we utilized the open-source model LLaMA 3.2 Vision via the Ollama framework, chosen for its capability to process images into coherent, detailed natural language descriptions. This model excels in providing intricate details, thereby facilitating the transformation of visual content into structured data. Although our primary objective was the extraction of structured triples, this methodology parallels the generation of image captions. Existing research indicates that multimodal language models exhibit comparable efficacy in visual comprehension tasks. While all evaluated models demonstrated considerable reliability, our analysis concentrated on the structured information extraction and comparative assessment, thus focusing on a single exemplary model.  To ensure the extracted triples remained consistent and pertinent to the domain of fashion, we employed the Fashionpedia ontology. This ontology serves as a comprehensive reference, enumerating categories, components, and attributes specific to apparel and accessories. By integrating this guide within the model's instructional framework, we ensured that the extracted information adhered to a well-defined structure. Additionally, we conducted a comparative analysis by omitting the ontology to evaluate its impact. The findings, detailed in Table 1, revealed that ontology utilization resulted in a greater number of triples per image and a broader range of relationships. In contrast, without the ontology, the model predominantly identified only superficial details, thereby generating less informative content. These results underscore the critical role of the ontology in achieving detailed and consistent outcomes, which are essential for the graph-based comparison of information.

Concerns regarding migration and its potential negative impacts remain prevalent among stakeholders. These apprehensions primarily revolve around possible adverse outcomes. During discussions about migration benefits, participants consistently expressed their concerns about perceived risks. Two pivotal questions that facilitated the identification of these concerns were: "Do you consider this approach beneficial for the company's current processes?" and "What is your perspective on a platform designed for rapid software development?" In our analysis, we identified seven distinct fears, as illustrated in Figure 4. Among Information Technology professionals, predominant concerns include the utility of the approach, its complexity, and potential resistance to change (refer to Table 7). Ultimately, stakeholders across various roles expressed a strong demand for training and demonstrations. These initiatives are sought to enhance understanding of the Software Product Line's (SPL) applicability to their work and facilitate integration into this novel methodology. Drawing from established practices, perceived advantages, risks, and concerns, we have developed a set of guidelines to assist in transitioning to an SPL. These guidelines are derived from dialogues and decisions within the organization. This section delineates expected outcomes (Sect. 5.1), the implementation of best practices (Sect. 5.2), strategies to promote future benefits (Sect. 5.3), methods to mitigate risks (Sect. 5.4), approaches to alleviate fears (Sect. 5.5), specific recommended actions (Sect. 5.6), and a comprehensive discussion (Sect. 5.7). Prior to conducting interviews, there was an anticipation regarding the nature of expected outcomes. It was hypothesized that individuals with an understanding of or affinity towards reusability would exhibit the most positive and engaged attitudes, thereby demonstrating motivation to initiate and support the project. In our findings, experienced developers predominantly focused on mindset-related aspects, whereas their less experienced counterparts concentrated more on technical elements. Nevertheless, this disparity in experience did not significantly affect the quantity or quality of responses received.

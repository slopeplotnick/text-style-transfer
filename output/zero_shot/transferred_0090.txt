We conducted an evaluation of several recent language models, encompassing both proprietary and open-access systems (see Appendix A.4 for further details). In our analysis, we attempted to disable any advanced reasoning or inference functionalities to observe the models' default behavior. This approach was successful for all models except GPT-5 Mini, which only allows adjustments to the depth of its reasoning capabilities. For this model, we set reasoning depth to the minimum allowable level. Additionally, we carried out a comparative study involving human participants across all tasks, with minor modifications. Details of the human study are provided in Appendix A.5, and the results are illustrated on the left side of Figure 8. It is important to note that this human experiment primarily serves as a reference point, as the primary focus of our research is the comparative performance of the language models.  Our findings align with prior research on human perception, such as the studies by Jazayeri and Shadlen (2010) and Petzschner and Glasauer (2011). Broadly, most language models perform better on text-based tasks compared to image-based tasks, with the notable exception of the maze distance estimation task. This task involves a detailed textual description of a path, which is more complex than the simpler textual inputs used in other tasks (see Fig. 2). While most models perform less effectively on this task's textual input, GPT-5 Mini demonstrates near-optimal performance. This divergence is likely due to our inability to entirely disable reasoning in this model, underscoring the dependence of model behavior on task-specific requirements.   Across tasks, evidence supporting Bayesian behavior is generally stronger when processing image-based inputs compared to text-based inputs (Appendix A.11). Furthermore, transitioning from single to multi-modal inputs does not consistently enhance performance across models. However, certain models demonstrate effective utilization of additional input modalities. Notably, Llama-4 Maverick achieves superior performance across all tasks when provided with multi-modal inputs. Similarly, Claude 3.7 Sonnet and GPT-4o exhibit improved performance on two out of three tasks when operating in multi-modal settings.

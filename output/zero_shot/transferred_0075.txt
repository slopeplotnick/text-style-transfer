The process of understanding language unfolds in a systematic manner, beginning with the auditory perception of speech. This is followed by the recognition of distinct sounds, known as phonemes, which are subsequently combined into words. The meaning of these words is deciphered through grammatical structure to comprehend sentences in their entirety. This discussion focuses on the cognitive mechanisms involved in language comprehension beyond word recognition, a highly intricate mental process unique to human cognition, and examines its parallels with advanced artificial intelligence (AI) systems for language processing.   Empirical research indicates that the human brain processes language in hierarchical stages, wherein more rudimentary tasks, such as word prediction, occur at lower levels, and complex functions, such as interpreting abstract concepts, are executed at higher levels. Language acquisition in the brain is facilitated through a predictive framework, wherein errors in predictions are identified and subsequently corrected, thereby improving over time—a process that mirrors the learning mechanisms employed by AI systems. Both human cognition and AI utilize prediction-based models to acquire and process linguistic information.  For instance, Google's Neural Machine Translation (GNMT) exemplifies this predictive approach in AI-based language translation. The system is composed of three primary components: an encoder, an attention mechanism, and a decoder. The encoder sequentially processes each word in a sentence and generates an intermediary representation that encapsulates the semantic meaning of the sentence. The encoder consists of eight hierarchical layers, with the final layer synthesizing the cumulative representation of the sentence. The attention mechanism identifies the key elements of the input sentence that are most pertinent to the translation task, passing this information to the decoder. The decoder subsequently predicts the next word in the translated sentence, layer by layer. Learning within GNMT occurs through iterative comparison of machine-generated translations against reference translations produced by humans, enabling the system to refine its accuracy by adjusting for errors.  In contrast, GPT-2, another AI model, employs a distinct approach. Unlike GNMT, GPT-2 does not rely on segregated components for encoding, attention, and decoding. Instead, it operates by predicting the next word within a sentence based solely on preceding words. During its training phase, the model is exposed to incomplete sentences and tasked with inferring the missing words. Once trained, GPT-2 leverages this predictive capability to generate coherent sentences by sequentially predicting words. Structurally, GPT-2 consists of multiple stacked layers, each comprising attention mechanisms and neural networks. By processing a sequence of input words, the model determines the probabilistic likelihood of subsequent words. GPT-2’s learning process is grounded exclusively in next-word prediction and does not necessitate reliance on human-curated examples.

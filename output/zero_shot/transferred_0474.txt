The utilization of computers is advancing due to the development of innovative tools and systems that enhance the ability of computers to autonomously understand and perform tasks. Open-source systems facilitate the integration of various components, such as planners and memory modules. Corporate reports provide evidence of the practical applications of these systems. Some studies assert that merely enlarging models does not necessarily enhance their reliability. Consequently, there is an emphasis on validating the effectiveness of these systems to ensure genuine improvement. Evaluating these systems presents challenges because the tasks they undertake are increasingly complex. Certain research efforts explore the application of specialized systems to aid in evaluations, although concerns regarding bias persist. To address these issues, proposals have been made to employ multiple evaluators and ensure fairness across diverse tasks. Our project incorporates these principles by engaging two evaluators to assess different aspects of each task in order to verify genuine improvements. We have curated a collection of 100 real-world editing tasks accompanied by slide presentations. Each task consists of an original slide deck, a target deck, instructions, and guidelines for assessing both layout and content. These tasks are organized into 16 categories to ensure rigorous testing of both comprehension and detailed formatting. Data was sourced from over 15,000 PowerPoint presentations from various origins, transformed into a format highlighting layout and content details. We retained those with diverse styles and integrated them with contributions from various experts. From over 500 reviewed options, including some created by students, we selected 100 that span a range of professional and academic fields, ensuring each category includes challenging examples.

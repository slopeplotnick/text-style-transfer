The research on language acquisition explores the integration of explicit grammatical knowledge into a lexical database. The daughter agent utilizes this enhanced knowledge to communicate with the mother agent. The methodology employed involves machine learning techniques to construct a coherent grammatical framework. This development augments existing systems, which rely on statistical methods and neural networks, by introducing a more comprehensible, rule-based approach to language modeling. The study leverages Zipf's law to evaluate the efficacy of a multi-agent simulation in the context of first language acquisition. The primary objective extends beyond the mere classification of function and content words; it aims to employ this classification as a means to test an innovative model of first language learning. Zipf's law provides a robust and reliable metric for system performance evaluation, as evidenced by its application in previous studies, such as those by Chaabouni et al. (2019), Rita et al. (2020), Dębowski (2021), Takahashi and Tanaka-Ishii (2019), and Takamichi et al. (2024). The experiments conducted herein affirm the essential components required to develop a multi-agent model for first language acquisition. Specifically, the learning of function and content words is facilitated by a hybrid approach that incorporates statistical trends to aid in the acquisition of specific grammatical rules. These findings endorse the modeling of more intricate grammatical structures and demonstrate the viability of the MODOMA research framework. Shakouri et al. (2025) provide a subsequent study, illustrating how grammatical categories such as nouns, verbs, and prepositions can be acquired using unsupervised learning techniques. This paper presents two experiments to validate this methodology: the first simulation, detailed in Sections 6 and 7, was conducted using a training dataset generated by the mother agent to identify parameters conducive to learning this linguistic feature. The second experiment employed a novel test dataset devised by the adult language model to validate this configuration, as discussed in Section 8. The findings indicate that MODOMA is capable of learning these grammatical categories and representing them within the grammatical framework of the daughter agent.

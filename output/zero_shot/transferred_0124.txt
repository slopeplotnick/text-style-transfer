A systematic process was integrated into the PPTPilot to enhance its ability to refine predictions over time. Specifically, the agent generates recommendations for modifications to XML files, which are then converted into temporary PowerPoint (PPT) files. A verification mechanism subsequently evaluates these PPT files against the original instructions, providing corrective feedback when discrepancies are identified. In response to this feedback, the agent revises the PPT files, rectifying errors accordingly. Despite the simplicity of this design, it demonstrates robust performance and efficiency when compared to existing tools for PPT editing. We propose that PPTPilot could serve as a foundational framework for further investigation into automated PPT editing methodologies.  For evaluation, we employed state-of-the-art vision-language models, such as Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT-5. The primary results presented in the core comparison (Table 3) predominantly rely on GPT-5. However, supplementary materials provide additional implementation details, including insights into Gemini 2.5 Pro. Comparative testing was conducted between PPTPilot and several alternative PPT editing tools, including ChatGPT in extended thinking mode, Gemini-CLI, GPT-Agent (which simulates desktop functionalities), MiniMax Agent (optimized for multimodal tasks and designed as a specialized PPT tool), PPTAgent, and Poster2Agent. Limitations such as usage restrictions and high costs associated with some platforms, particularly ChatGPT and MiniMax Agent, necessitated testing on a reduced dataset of 25 samples to remain within budgetary constraints. The evaluation focused primarily on complex tasks where PPTPilot and ChatGPT had demonstrated prior challenges, supplemented by additional cases to ensure comprehensive scenario coverage. Further details regarding the evaluation cases are provided in the supplementary materials.

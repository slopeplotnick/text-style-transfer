Initiating a character from a random state derived from a motion dataset enhances the exploration of motion imitation methodologies. Traditionally, the availability of the dataset is essential for selecting initial states during training. To circumvent the necessity of retaining the original dataset, Stochastic Motion Primacy (SMP) utilizes a generative model to generate initial states for the training process. SMP employs a diffusion model that has been pre-trained as a motion guide to produce initial states. SMP serves both as a reward mechanism and a method for initiating new training sessions. The adoption of this generative state approach obviates the need to preserve the original dataset once SMP is fully trained, as it leverages diffusion models for the creation of diverse and high-quality initial states. Our framework implements a trained diffusion model as a versatile motion guide to facilitate imitation learning. The motion model assesses the naturalness of the agent's movements within the learning environment, thereby aiding in the development of control policies that perform varying tasks while maintaining the fidelity of movements akin to those in the original dataset.

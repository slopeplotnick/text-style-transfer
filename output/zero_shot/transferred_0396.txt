The MODOMA system comprises two integral components: the mother agent and the daughter agent. Delilah, functioning as the mother agent, is a sophisticated tool engineered to generate and analyze the Dutch language. Rather than utilizing examples derived from a database, Delilah operates on the foundation of a set of predefined linguistic rules and structures. These rules serve as comprehensive templates delineating the intricacies of Dutch lexical semantics and syntax. For instance, there exists a template for the article "de" (translating to "the" in English), which encompasses detailed information pertaining to its morphological form, semantic content, syntactic function, and its integration within sentence structures. Such templates are meticulously crafted based on empirical studies of Dutch linguistics, ensuring their precision and reliability. Delilah employs these templates to construct phrases and sentences through a process termed unification. This process entails the verification of template compatibility, ensuring the absence of conflicts, followed by their synthesis into cohesive linguistic units. An illustrative example is the template for "de," which is configured to amalgamate exclusively with noun phrases, as observed in constructions such as "de vrouw" ("the woman"). The operational methodology of Delilah parallels the principles of head-driven phrase structure grammar (HPSG), wherein lexical items and syntactic rules are uniformly treated. Specifically, Delilah is grounded in a variant of grammar known as combinatory list grammar, which bears conceptual similarities to combinatory categorial grammar (CCG). The operational intricacies and theoretical underpinnings of Delilah have been extensively documented and expounded in various academic publications. Within the framework of the MODOMA system, Delilah is implemented in its original form, without modifications.

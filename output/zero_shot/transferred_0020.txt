The increasing utilization of large language models as automated adjudicators in evaluating the outputs of generative systems is encapsulated in the concept known as "Thinking-LLM-as-a-Judge." This approach offers a cost-effective and expedited alternative to traditional human evaluation, which is often resource-intensive both in terms of time and financial expenditure. To address the inherent variability and potential inaccuracies associated with singular judgment events, a prevalent strategy involves leveraging computational resources to generate multiple predictions and assessments for each evaluated artifact. However, the overall efficacy of the resultant decision is contingent upon the method of integrating these diverse outputs. Conventional aggregation techniques, such as majority voting or confidence-based scoring, frequently fall short in statistical efficacy and reliability, particularly in scenarios involving ties. For instance, a mere vote count fails to distinguish between a closely contested 5-to-4 decision and a decisive 9-to-0 decision, thereby neglecting crucial information regarding the strength of evidence. This oversight diminishes the reliability of evaluations. In this study, we posit that the synthesis of judgment outcomes is critical to optimizing computational efficacy. We propose an innovative method termed Distribution-Calibrated Aggregation, which transcends simple vote counting. Our approach systematically incorporates all positive, negative, and tied votes, preserving the comprehensive information contained within the voting distributions. The model we employ considers three potential outcomes and assesses both preference intensity and tie likelihood. By calibrating optimal parameters on a pilot dataset and employing a designated error metric, our method aligns closely with the evaluation objective, ensuring congruity with the data, minimizing discrepancies, and enhancing decision accuracy.

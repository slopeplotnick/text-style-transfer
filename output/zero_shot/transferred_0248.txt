Institutional AI is conceptualized as a mechanism to maintain system stability throughout dynamic processes of change, rather than fostering complete system autonomy. Safety, within this context, is embedded as an ongoing mechanism of rule monitoring and maintenance, rather than a one-time verification step post-deployment. The framework further emphasizes the role of agents in designing and refining the operational rules governing their behavior, offering limited self-regulation capabilities in scenarios where external intervention is delayed. Notably, these systems are not intended to replace human oversight but rather to complement it. By redistributing evaluative functions across the system, the framework enables greater traceability, promotes diverse perspectives, and mitigates risks as identified by the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH) framework.  The overarching goal is to enhance collaboration among interconnected models, improve their interpretability, and sustain consistency with minimal reliance on external supervision. Institutional AI draws upon the ESRH framework introduced in this paper, which addresses risk emergence while providing a complementary perspective on risk mitigation strategies.

Education has become increasingly globalized, with online learning experiencing rapid growth and playing a pivotal role in expanding access to educational resources across diverse regions. Consequently, the effective localization of educational materials has emerged as a critical challenge, necessitating systems that integrate various modes of communication commonly employed in instructional settings, such as spoken language and visual presentations. Lectures often comprise spoken discourse complemented by visual aids, such as slides, which underscores the need for systems capable of processing and translating both modalities effectively.   In pursuit of enhancing accessibility and comprehension for learners worldwide, we developed BOOM, a tool designed to translate lecture audio alongside associated visual content. BOOM generates three types of outputs: translated text derived from lecture audio, translated slides preserving the original design and layout, and synthesized speech that aligns with the target language. This multifaceted approach enables students to experience lectures in their native language while retaining the integrity of the original instructional materials. Moreover, experimental evaluations indicate that translated slides are particularly beneficial for tasks such as summary generation and answering comprehension questions.   To promote further advancements in this domain, we have made the Slide Translation code publicly accessible via GitHub at [https://github.com/saikoneru/image-Translator](https://github.com/saikoneru/image-Translator) and integrated it into the Lecture Translator pipeline, available at [https://gitlab kit.edu/kit/isl-ai41t/lt-middleware/ltpipeline](https://gitlab kit.edu/kit/isl-ai41t/lt-middleware/ltpipeline). The provision of educational resources in learners' native languages has demonstrated significant improvements in knowledge acquisition and overall comprehension. Effective localization of lecture materials reduces language-related barriers, facilitates access to complex concepts, and enhances the educational experience for students across linguistic backgrounds.  The growing internationalization of higher education institutions necessitates the availability of lecture content in multiple languages, both for traditional in-person learning and online educational platforms. Modern lecture materials often integrate various forms of media, with audio content serving as the primary component. Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) technologies enable the conversion of audio into text, which can be further processed to generate supplementary teaching resources such as summaries, chapter outlines, and automated question-answer sessions. Recent advancements in Natural Language Processing (NLP), particularly through Large Language Models (LLMs), have proven instrumental in optimizing these processes and enriching the learning experience. However, ensuring equitable access for students who may not be proficient in the lecture's original language necessitates the translation of these outputs into multiple languages.   Effective localization strategies must account for the diverse content types utilized in educational contexts, spanning audio, textual, and visual modalities. Addressing the complexities associated with lecture translation remains central to facilitating inclusive and accessible education in an increasingly interconnected and digitized world.

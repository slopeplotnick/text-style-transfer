This study investigates the potential enhancement of solver performance through the integration of a verifier mechanism, which assists in selecting accurate solutions. To evaluate the validity of our hypothesis, we conducted experiments utilizing various combinations of solvers and verifiers selected from a set of 12 models. Specifically, we chose the three smallest models from each of four distinct categories. For each problem instance, the solver was permitted to attempt a solution iteratively until the verifier confirmed its accuracy, with a maximum allowance of nine attempts. In instances where a correct solution was not identified within the permitted attempts, the outcome of the final attempt was retained. The empirical results, in conjunction with anticipated improvements, are depicted in Figure 4. Although smaller improvements may not be immediately evident and the constraint of nine attempts may obscure a comprehensive understanding, the overall findings align with our initial expectations.   Our analysis reveals that self-verification yields less significant improvements compared to cross-verification by a verifier from a different model group. This underscores the robustness of our improvement estimation method, which does not necessitate substantial additional computational resources. It is a well-documented cognitive bias for individuals to regard solutions resembling their own as more plausible, a phenomenon paralleled in the interaction between solvers and verifiers. Previous investigations have demonstrated that models proficient in logical reasoning exhibit limited enhancement through self-verification, whereas their performance notably improves when verified by dissimilar models, which highlights an analogous bias. The primary conclusion drawn from this study is that when a solver and verifier exhibit similar cognitive patterns, the likelihood of the verifier erroneously approving incorrect solutions increases, thus diminishing potential improvements. Employing a verifier with a divergent reasoning approach effectively mitigates this issue.

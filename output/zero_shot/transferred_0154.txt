The utilization of system prompts has emerged as an effective strategy for guiding the behavior of large language models (LLMs). Recent research indicates that thoughtfully crafted prompts can enhance the reasoning capabilities of these models and mitigate bias. However, the majority of this research has been conducted with a focus on the English language, with limited attention paid to creating prompts that function effectively across multiple languages. The challenge of multilingual application arises due to the diminished efficacy of prompts when translated or applied to tasks in non-English languages, resulting in issues such as reduced accuracy, unstable reasoning, and inconsistent outcomes across languages.   This raises a critical question: How can we develop prompts that perform effectively and consistently across a variety of languages and tasks? To address this issue, we delineated the problem into three fundamental inquiries: Firstly, what types of prompts facilitate better model behavior in a multilingual context? Secondly, is it possible to identify effective prompts automatically, thereby minimizing human intervention? Thirdly, what impact do effective prompts have on aspects such as reasoning and linguistic features in model responses?   To explore these questions, we devised an evaluation framework for prompts based on four criteria: average accuracy, variability in accuracy, consistency of results across languages, and variation in response length. Through an examination of model performance across diverse tasks, we developed a methodology to optimize prompts for these objectives. Subsequent analysis of model outputs revealed how these optimized prompts enhance reasoning and ensure more consistent results across languages. Furthermore, we introduced an innovative approach for the automatic optimization of multilingual prompts.   Our extensive analysis of reasoning data suggests that effective prompts tend to elicit more structured and consistent reasoning from models, with reduced linguistic code-switching. This implies that the reasoning process itself may play a crucial role in enhancing multilingual model performance. In summary, our findings underscore the potential of refining system prompts as a valuable and yet underexplored strategy for achieving improved and more reliable outcomes from multilingual LLMs. The code and dataset associated with our study are accessible at [https://github.com/orange0629/multilingual-system-prompt](https://github.com/orange0629/multilingual-system-prompt).

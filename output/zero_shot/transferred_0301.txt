Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated significant efficacy in robotic tasks by utilizing extensive datasets accumulated through human effort or predetermined programming. During the training phase, these models employ methodologies such as flow-matching or diffusion to acquire diverse task-handling strategies. Subsequently, the VLA model undergoes task-specific fine-tuning using smaller datasets. Deploying a pre-trained VLA model for new tasks generally yields superior outcomes compared to initiating training from the ground up, as the model already possesses substantial knowledge in visual perception, language comprehension, and spatial recognition. Despite their overall robust performance, VLA models exhibit certain limitations when applied to real-world scenarios. This issue arises from the persistence of superfluous actions post fine-tuning, which do not contribute to task accomplishment. During training, VLA models assimilate numerous actions from varied data, complicating the focus on essential actions specific to a task. Consequently, even after fine-tuning, the models retain extraneous actions. Additionally, the datasets employed for fine-tuning may vary in style, being derived from diverse individuals or methodologies, some of which may not be optimal. These redundant actions result in a discrepancy between the VLA model's operations and the intended task objectives. This discrepancy becomes evident during the model's deployment.

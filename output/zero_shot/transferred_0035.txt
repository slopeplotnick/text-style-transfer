This study introduces a method designed to ensure that the policy remains within the constraints of the available data by optimizing a penalized value function. The primary objective of this approach is to simplify the offline reinforcement learning (Offline RL) objective presented in Equation (4) by prioritizing actions that are most frequently observed or exhibit the highest density within the dataset. This framework provides substantial theoretical support for the proposed method. The test-time scaling mechanism implemented in this approach identifies the most common action, a process grounded in robust theoretical principles rather than mere speculation. By adopting this methodology, the approach effectively achieves the anti-exploration goal in Offline RL.  Empirical evidence from prior studies, such as Lobel et al. (2023) and Bai et al. (2025), corroborates the efficacy of utilizing pseudo-counts in optimizing the preferences of large language models (LLMs). Building upon this concept, the current study incorporates pseudo-counts through the use of a Coin Flipping Network (CFN). As demonstrated in Figure 3, the pseudo-count estimator employed aligns closely with the L2 distance to the actual action as well as the pseudo-count generated by the CFN. This alignment validates the ability of the CFN to effectively evaluate and penalize unsupported actions.   The experimental component of this research seeks to examine the impact of employing Test-time Anti-exploration with pseudo-counts on the accuracy of action prediction and task success rates. Additionally, the study investigates the specific design elements that contribute to these improvements and evaluates whether the additional computational overhead introduced by the method is acceptable. The research is guided by the following central questions:   1. Can the proposed method enhance performance across different environmental settings, various visual language-conditioned (VLC) policies, and distinct tasks?  2. What is the additional computational cost associated with the test-time scaling method, and can it be effectively implemented on physical robotic systems while maintaining performance benefits?  3. How do design considerations, such as the integration of internal features and the CFN pseudo-count network, influence overall performance outcomes?   These inquiries seek to comprehensively explore the efficiency, scalability, and practical applicability of the proposed method in diverse settings.

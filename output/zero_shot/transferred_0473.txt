In this study, scikit-learn was employed to implement fundamental machine learning models, including Majority, Random, n-gram Support Vector Machine (SVM), Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest, and Decision Tree classifiers. Feature extraction was conducted using term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) and count vectorization techniques. While the fundamental models were executed on a central processing unit (CPU), experiments involving transformer-based models were performed on a GPU (Google Colab T4). Model evaluation primarily relied on the micro-averaged F1-score as the key performance metric, with additional consideration given to accuracy, precision, and recall to ensure comprehensive comparisons.  The study examined various transformer-based language models—including m-BERT (multilingual BERT), XLM-RoBERTa base, BanglaBERT, and DistilBERT cased—alongside traditional machine learning models to assess their efficacy in detecting hate speech within the BLP2025 dataset. The results for subtask 1A are presented in Table 2. Among the evaluated models, BanglaBERT achieved the highest performance, with an accuracy of 0.71, precision of 0.70, recall of 0.71, and a micro-averaged F1-score of 0.71, outperforming all other models analyzed. In comparison, m-BERT and XLM-RoBERTa base demonstrated slightly lower performance, achieving F1 scores of 0.66 and 0.67, respectively, though both exceeded the performance of the fundamental models. DistilBERT exhibited relatively weaker performance, attaining a micro-averaged F1-score of 0.57, which was lower than those of the SVM and LR models.  Among the traditional machine learning models, SVM demonstrated the strongest performance, achieving a micro-averaged F1-score of 0.61, while the random baseline yielded the lowest score, registering a micro-averaged F1-score of 0.16. Logistic Regression also performed competitively, achieving an F1-score of 0.62. Notably, BanglaBERT emerged as the most effective model for this dataset within the scope of the present analysis.

Google Search incorporates a feature known as "AI Overviews," which leverages the Gemini model to generate concise summaries of extensive textual documents. This feature aims to enhance user efficiency by obviating the need for individuals to independently synthesize information from multiple sources. However, this functionality raises concerns regarding the reliability and impartiality of the synthesized information. When artificial intelligence systems merge content from diverse sources into a single summary, it is imperative to assess whether the system treats all sources equitably or exhibits biases.  Existing research has identified potential biases in AI models. For instance, Santurkar et al. (202x) observed that models such as GPT-4 and Claude frequently reflect liberal social values. Similarly, Vijay et al. (202x) introduced methodologies to quantify biases in AI-generated news summaries, including analyzing linguistic variations and evaluating the prevalence of single-perspective narratives. While significant attention has been devoted to investigating bias in AI-generated summaries, comparatively little research has explored how AI systems handle biases when synthesizing information from multiple documents. Specifically, the question remains whether AI models disproportionately prioritize certain documents in multi-document summarization, particularly when processing lengthy texts. Cognitive phenomena such as primacy bias or the "middle bias," observed in other language processing tasks, may influence the treatment of source documents during summarization. However, the extent to which these biases impact multi-document summarization remains underexplored.  This study seeks to examine whether the sequential order in which articles are presented affects their representation in AI-generated summaries. Using news articles on the topic of abortion as a case study, the research addresses these questions to identify whether and how biases in document ordering influence the summarization process.

This study investigates the application of robotic systems in construction, with a focus on evaluating their efficacy through extensive comparative analysis against established methodologies. Specifically, the authors employ principles of robotic modeling and construction frameworks to assess the capabilities of reasoning-based approaches founded on large language models (LLMs) in addressing multi-robot task allocation (MRTA) challenges within construction settings. The proposed LTAA (LLM-Based Task Allocation and Automation) system integrates detailed robotic capability models, phase-dependent strategic adaptations, and LLM-driven decision-making mechanisms to address coordination problems previously identified in the field. Comparative analysis is conducted between the LTAA system and traditional task allocation techniques such as Brute Force and Greedy Algorithms, as well as machine learning-based approaches, including Q-learning and Deep Q-Networks.   The findings contribute to the development of robust coordination systems leveraging LLMs and provide insights into how reasoning-based methodologies perform relative to conventional approaches in automating construction tasks. The organizational structure of the paper is as follows: Section 2 reviews prior research on multi-robot task allocation, focusing on optimization, machine learning, and LLM methodologies. Section 3 details the LTAA system design. Section 4 describes the experimental setup employed in the study. Section 5 presents the comparative results, while Section 6 offers conclusions and avenues for future research.  MRTA provides the theoretical foundation for understanding how tasks are allocated among robotic teams, taking into account constraints related to resources, temporal requirements, and varying robotic capabilities. Gerkey and Matarić (2004) introduced an initial framework for analyzing task allocation problems by categorizing tasks along dimensions including single-robot versus multi-robot allocation, single-task versus multi-task execution, and instantaneous versus time-extended tasks. However, this foundational framework did not comprehensively address the complexities encountered in real-world scenarios involving heterogeneous robotic capabilities and dynamic task requirements. Subsequently, Korsah et al. (2013) proposed an enhanced model that incorporated factors such as diverse robotic skills, task interdependencies, and fluctuating constraints, thereby advancing the field towards mixed-team collaboration for addressing complex tasks in dynamic environments.

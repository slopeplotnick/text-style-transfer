This paper explores the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in the identification of significant mineral resources, presenting a perspective rooted in extensive experience within the domains of data science, decision science, and AI in earth resource management. With a professional background spanning 30 years, I offer insights and observations informed by my previous work in this field. In 1998, I authored a paper on the implementation of neural networks with outcrop data to enhance the realism of reservoir models, a critical factor for predicting fluid dynamics and improving decision-making processes in oil and gas exploration. This work was presented at a prominent conference in New Orleans, despite the impending threat of a hurricane. Another significant contribution was made in 2002, where I demonstrated that neural networks are capable of learning from borehole and geophysical data to predict reservoir properties such as porosity, even in regions lacking borehole information. A substantial component of this research involved addressing the challenge of integrating data across disparate scales, such as borehole core measurements and seismic signals from deep underground. The current paper will delve extensively into this issue of data scale integration. While oil and gas companies routinely incorporate geological uncertainty in their decision-making processes for offshore platform construction, the mining sector frequently relies on simplistic models. The minerals industry, in particular, has not yet matched the oil and gas sector's advancements in quantitative risk assessment during exploration activities, which often adhere to straightforward modeling and data interpretation methodologies.

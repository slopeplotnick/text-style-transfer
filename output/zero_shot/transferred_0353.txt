A Software Product Line (SPL) represents a structured approach to consolidating a series of related software systems with shared features, thereby facilitating the efficient and rapid development of new software applications. The transformation of an existing code base into a SPL is a complex, time-intensive endeavor that can significantly disrupt established software development processes within an organization. This paper delineates the initial stages of undertaking such a transformation, in collaboration with ITK - Predict and Decide, a small enterprise that opted to convert its existing code base into a SPL. Our role as researchers was to provide guidance throughout this process. The company's code base is centered around a framework known as Cross, which already incorporates substantial software reuse, akin to a SPL but lacking the standard models and processes characteristic of a formal SPL. In order to assist their developers, we devised a plan to incrementally adjust their code base and engineering practices. Initially, we assessed the organizational sentiment regarding this transition and offered recommendations to facilitate the process. This paper presents the findings from that preliminary study, which involved interviews with company personnel to ascertain their perspectives on their current methodologies, anticipated benefits of transitioning to a SPL, and potential concerns. The majority expressed support for the transition and a desire to participate in the process. Developers, in particular, exhibited enthusiasm, viewing the transformation as both a technical and managerial challenge. Conversely, personnel involved in the business aspects displayed caution, raising issues such as potential increases in maintenance complexity and the risk of diminished creativity, particularly in the design of user interfaces.

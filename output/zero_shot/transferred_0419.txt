This study examines Lang2Logic, a novel methodology aimed at bridging natural language and computational logic. Lang2Logic facilitates the transformation of everyday language into a formal representation known as Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF), which is essential for computational reasoning tasks. By enabling the conversion of natural language into a machine-readable format, Lang2Logic enhances the ability of computers to interpret and process human communication. The approach employs specific computational tools and a customized language interpretation framework to ensure the accurate transformation of natural language into CNF. This framework enables the reformulation of natural language inputs into SAT (Boolean satisfiability) problems—logical constructs that can be efficiently solved using SAT solvers—thereby extending the applicability of these solvers to real-world scenarios.   The overarching objective of Lang2Logic is to establish a clear pathway from natural language understanding to formal computational reasoning. This is achieved by converting English-language statements into CNF, a prerequisite format for SAT solvers. The methodology is organized into three key stages: (1) translating natural language into a logical expression, (2) converting the logical expression into CNF, and (3) optimizing the CNF representation. Each stage is critical to ensuring the accuracy and efficiency of the natural language-to-CNF transformation process.  The first stage utilizes the ChatGPT o1-mini model API to translate natural language inputs into intermediate logical expressions. The o1-mini model is specifically adapted to handle repeated language patterns, generate logical expressions compatible with downstream computational tools, and conform to the input requirements of the Sympy converter. The use of the ChatGPT API reduces the need for complex grammar-rule engineering, simplifying the transformation process. However, this approach also introduces potential limitations, including higher computational costs and reduced efficiency with larger inputs. To mitigate these challenges, the Natural Language Toolkit (NLTK) is employed for pre-processing. Specifically, NLTK's Punkt tokenizer is used to segment input text into individual sentences, allowing the o1-mini model to process each sentence independently. This sentence-level processing minimizes errors such as sentence mixing or omissions, improving the accuracy of the logical expressions generated.

To address this inquiry, we deconstructed the task into two distinct components, as delineated in Section 2. Initially, we sought to establish connections between the query's requirements and specific attributes of the products, thereby generating associations between them. Subsequently, these associations were utilized to contrast the product features with the query demands. The model is required to execute three primary functions to effectively manage this process. Firstly, it must discern the specific terminology and implicit details within the query through the application of knowledge, subsequently employing reasoning to ascertain the true intent of the query and the attributes the products should possess. For instance, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a), the model must interpret "morning C, evening A" as referring to Vitamin C and retinol and understand that "for mom" implies suitability for middle-aged women. Secondly, the model is tasked with correlating information from diverse formats, such as text and images, to align the query with product attributes. For example, as demonstrated in Fig. 1(b), when the query mentions "yellow," which is not present in the text, the model must analyze the product's image to identify the color. Thirdly, the model must adhere to specific rules in decision-making processes. For instance, in Fig. 1(c), if an individual searches for "LV bags," but the product is a second-hand bag, the model cannot solely depend on feature matching and must employ predefined rules to determine the appropriateness of the match to the query.

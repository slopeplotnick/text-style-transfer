When examining a series of interconnected events and their antecedents, one can derive a coherent and comprehensive understanding that effectively links these occurrences. This approach surpasses mere summarization, which often results in a fragmented narrative lacking a clear causal relationship and chronological structure. Maintaining detailed information within events, such as specific technological details in a "new technology release," provides a more accurate depiction than generalizations, which can lead to excessive vagueness and overlook critical aspects impacting business outcomes. Incorporating precise dates is crucial for comprehending the duration of events and their subsequent effects. The absence of such temporal markers hinders the ability to discern causal relationships driving market changes, thereby reducing the accuracy of predictions.  Our study highlights the significance of monitoring events and incorporating new information. The removal of event-tracking components reveals that even minor developments can have substantial implications when new data indicate unexpected progress. Conversely, the market tends to exhibit minimal reaction in the absence of new information, such as when a significant shareholder divests shares without accompanying news. Thus, while individual events may not induce immediate change, the evolution of expectations over time—evident through event tracking—proves to be a potent tool for forecasting.  Furthermore, we evaluated the efficacy of our methodological approach in analyzing historical patterns by comparing it with four alternative strategies. Our approach demonstrated superior performance. For instance, relying solely on a company's historical data may yield satisfactory results; however, this method is suboptimal for companies with limited histories or those embarking on innovative ventures, as it becomes challenging to identify analogous past events.

The study reported an average F1 score of 84.65 for the Assamese tests utilizing the Samese model, while employing Hindi yielded an F1 score of 83.91. A similar trend was observed for Sindhi, where direct supervision resulted in an F1 score of 85.59, and the model trained on Hindi achieved an F1 score of 86.11 through cross-lingual transfer. In addressing Research Question 2 (RQ2), the models were evaluated on 11 low-resource languages selected from eight distinct language groups. It was noted that languages employing the Latin script, such as Galician and Slovenian, demonstrated superior performance across the tests. This enhancement is likely attributable to the script and subword tokenization advantages in the shared XLM-R vocabulary, which facilitated better representation during the pretraining phase. Conversely, despite Icelandic's utilization of the Latin script, its performance was comparatively lower. This discrepancy is attributed to the language's complex morphological structure, which leads to significant token fragmentation and hindered sentence representation clarity. Consequently, multilingual encoders relying on subword tokenization are less effective for Icelandic.  To evaluate the efficacy of training on a high-resource language for facilitating transfer both within and across language groups, as outlined in Research Question 3 (RQ3), models were trained using Hindi-only, Chinese-only, and combined Hindi+Chinese setups. The models were subsequently tested on six languages from the Indic (Hindi) and East-Southeast Asian (Chinese) language groups. Within the Indic group, the model trained on Hindi exhibited superior performance across all tests compared to the Chinese-trained model. Conversely, in the East-Southeast Asian group, the model trained on Chinese outperformed the Hindi-trained model in most tests. These findings suggest that intra-group transfer from a high-resource language to other languages within the same group is more effective than inter-group transfer involving a high-resource language from a different language group.

The use of numerical data alone for analysis is effective, yet it does not surpass the efficacy of text-based analysis. A combination of both textual and numerical data tends to yield results that are nearly as optimal as those achieved with text alone, highlighting the primary importance of text, with numerical data providing supplementary information. In the evaluation of diverse datasets and tasks, a hierarchy of importance becomes evident. Notably, text-based methods are particularly effective in the detection of misinformation. When a rudimentary augmentation is applied to text models, results on certain datasets closely approach optimal outcomes and outperform traditional methodologies on others. Numerical data, while adequate, lacks the robustness of textual data, as exemplified in the prediction of content virality where text-based analysis proves superior. Consequently, text emerges as the foundational element, with numerical data serving a supportive role. It is crucial to articulate these findings clearly. In scenarios where errors carry significant consequences, it is advisable to adhere to established methodologies that strike a balance between comprehensiveness and accuracy.   Regarding methodological frameworks, within certain datasets, various models such as GRU, LSTM, RNN, CNN, and Transformer demonstrate comparable efficacy, with Transformers exhibiting a slight advantage. The similarity in their performance underscores the importance of data utilization and terminological precision over minor model adjustments, especially under resource constraints. Recommended approaches include: (1) Conceptualizing virality prediction as a series of tasks characterized by specific constraints and temporal parameters, with explicit clarification of terms when presenting results. (2) Initially employing text-centric methodologies for fake news detection, adding straightforward numerical data as it becomes available. (3) Prioritizing thoroughness in quick evaluations while transparently acknowledging potential oversights. (4) Implementing simple, clearly articulated experimental setups, ensuring meticulous data preparation and error avoidance, accompanied by detailed explanations of methodological processes.

This study aims to establish a comprehensive understanding of the factors that contribute to effective e-commerce search functionality and to identify the tools required to enhance its performance. Fundamentally, e-commerce search entails aligning user queries with relevant products. This alignment is optimized by evaluating the degree to which a search query corresponds to a product listing, incorporating both objective elements—such as attributes and constraints—and subjective considerations. To systematically evaluate user intent in search queries, the requirements are categorized into 18 distinct dimensions, including type, brand, style, among others. For each query-product pairing, the degree of relevance is assessed based on how well the product satisfies the parameters of the query. These evaluations form the foundation for analyzing and understanding relevance determination within e-commerce search systems.   The measurement of relevance in this context is structured around three key components: understanding the product, interpreting the search query, and evaluating the relevance of the match. In assessing relevance, particular attention is given to two critical aspects of the product: its attributes and its presentation. Product attributes are classified into two types: inherent and derived. Inherent attributes, such as category, brand, model, and price, are readily observable characteristics that are typically straightforward to align with user queries. In contrast, derived attributes require a deeper analysis and inferential reasoning, as they pertain to functional or experiential qualities of the product. Derived attributes are often more abstract and context-dependent, and their interpretation may vary significantly across industries due to specialized terminologies and practices. Understanding these nuances is essential for refining relevance judgments and improving e-commerce search systems.

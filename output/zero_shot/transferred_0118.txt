Recent scholarly investigations have concentrated on various methodologies aimed at enhancing model reasoning and step verification within computational frameworks. One line of inquiry emphasizes segmenting reasoning into discrete steps, meticulously validating each step, and employing reward models to train for improved mathematical reasoning. A notable study examined the efficacy of utilizing a verifier to assess final outcomes, referred to as the GV-Gap, which demonstrated enhancements, particularly when the solver and verifier were instantiated by the same model. However, this investigation was limited to foundational models, excluding those subjected to extensive post-initial training refinement.   Concurrently, research endeavors have explored scaling computational resources during the verification testing phase. For instance, certain studies have investigated the integration of random sampling with self-verification protocols, while others have combined parallel sampling with error-correction mechanisms. Additionally, comparative analyses have been conducted on methodologies such as Self-Consistency versus generative verification techniques. There remains an ongoing academic discourse concerning the optimization of computational resource allocation for verification during testing phases.   Despite their utility, verifiers are not infallible; they possess potential shortcomings, including the approval of incorrect answers, rejection of valid reasoning, or selection of suboptimal solutions. Detailed discussions regarding these limitations are delineated in Appendix A.   Another burgeoning area of investigation is the potential for models to engage in self-improvement and self-evaluation. While certain scholars express skepticism regarding this paradigm, others have proposed theoretical and empirical evidence suggesting that models can enhance their capabilities through refined comprehension. One particular study concentrated on smaller models, advocating for the implementation of more robust verifiers as a means to facilitate model improvement. Practical approaches to self-enhancement include the utilization of feedback articulated in straightforward language or the direct training of models to rectify their errors. Further methodologies involve employing auxiliary tools, such as code interpreters, to progressively refine model outputs.

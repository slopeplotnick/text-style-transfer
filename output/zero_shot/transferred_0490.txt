In certain Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) tasks, a standardized format is typically employed for the task specifications and requirements. This format is then supplemented with query and item details derived from each dataset to construct the prompt. To enable optimal performance of the base model, it is essential to incorporate crucial information, such as the task description, decision-making guidelines, and the desired format of the output, into the prompt. Additionally, explicit instructions are provided to guide the model through a two-step process: first, discerning the query's requirements and identifying its pertinent attributes, followed by extracting the appropriate attributes from the item information and making decisions based on the predefined rules. In tasks involving reasoning, furnishing the prompt with more comprehensive instructions facilitates the model's ability to arrive at correct answers with greater ease. However, in the context of SFT, this is not always the case, as the model derives significant learning from the response data beyond mere instructions.  To identify the most effective prompt design, three versions were evaluated. The extended version encompasses all task details, rules, and instructions, exceeding 7,000 tokens. The medium version is more concise, retaining only the most essential instructions, and contains approximately 800 tokens. The brief version is the most straightforward, providing a basic role description such as: "You are an e-commerce relevance expert. Score the query and item." This phase aims to initialize the model and enhance its fundamental decision-making capabilities. Achieving this necessitates a training dataset that is both comprehensive and meticulously curated. To ensure comprehensiveness, an appropriate sampling method is employed to encompass diverse types of e-commerce data, thus preventing an overrepresentation or underrepresentation of any particular type.

In this study, we investigated the impact of selected training images on the outcomes by assessing the similarity of generated images both before and after the retraining process. Mere comparison of these images, however, does not entirely elucidate why certain images exerted more influence. Consequently, we employed a graph-based analytical method to scrutinize the structure and shared characteristics of the training images. Our primary objective was to determine whether the images selected formed distinct and meaningful clusters. Table 3 presents statistical data obtained from ten experimental trials, each encompassing fifteen prompts, aimed at evaluating the consistency and grouping of the images. These findings reinforced the efficacy of our image selection approach and offered insights into the significance of these images within the process. For instance, the average number of distinct values per relationship ranged from 3.9 to 5.1. This indicates a degree of variability that is appropriate for domains such as fashion, where limited styles or patterns predominate. The images were sufficiently similar to be categorized into clear clusters, yet possessed enough diversity to avoid redundancy. This suggests that the influence is derived from shared conceptual frameworks rather than mere replication. Additionally, we examined the extent to which images shared attributes, finding an average of 5 to 7.1 shared attributes, with some clusters sharing up to 13. This implies that the images were not only detailed but also aligned in meaningful ways. Prompts containing more specific details correlated with a higher number of shared attributes, whereas general or creative prompts resulted in greater diversity. These observations demonstrate the system's ability to adapt to varying types of prompts while effectively identifying matching examples.

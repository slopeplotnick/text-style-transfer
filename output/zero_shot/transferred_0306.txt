We have developed *Menta*, a novel model specifically designed for predicting mental health conditions based on textual data from social media. The model is characterized by its balance of accuracy and usability, employing an innovative training methodology that addresses class imbalance and demonstrates strong adaptability across diverse clinical tasks. *Menta* achieves a notable equilibrium between accuracy and computational efficiency, outperforming several existing models and achieving competitive results compared to large language models across six distinct mental health prediction tasks. Furthermore, *Menta* is designed to be both accessible and privacy-preserving, requiring minimal computational resources for implementation.  Historically, the application of machine learning to mental health prediction on social media has relied on traditional approaches, which often required extensive manual feature engineering. For instance, Jiang et al. utilized sophisticated word embedding models to identify markers of mental health conditions on Reddit, demonstrating that these approaches outperform earlier conventional methods. Similarly, Sarkar et al. proposed a model that predicts depression and anxiety by leveraging shared features, outperforming single-task models in the process. Other investigations have employed deep learning algorithms to develop screening tools for suicide risk, with the goal of facilitating early intervention.  More recently, the advent of large language models has advanced the field significantly. One study employed ChatGPT to classify mental health conditions such as stress, depression, and suicidality from social media text without requiring task-specific fine-tuning, illustrating the potential of these models in mental health prediction. Another study focused on detecting depression within online text and reported superior performance using fine-tuned transformer-based models when compared to traditional machine learning approaches. Related research has also explored the capacity of these models to interpret symptoms of depression and emotional states. Findings suggest that incorporating prompts with emotional cues and examples enhances the models’ interpretative capabilities.  In summary, *Menta* represents a significant advancement in the application of machine learning to mental health assessment, offering a practical, efficient, and accurate tool for leveraging social media data in clinical and research contexts.

This section examines the limitations and broader implications of the LeechHijack attack proposed herein. We assess the constraints of the current setup, delineate its potential applications, and identify future trajectories for threat escalation.  A significant limitation of our approach pertains to its efficacy in managing hijacked computing. Table 7 demonstrates that the attack's Answer Success Rate (ASR) is inferior compared to the native Large Language Model (LLM). This decline in performance impacts the utility of the appropriated resources for the attacker. However, this issue may be mitigated by distributing the additional workload across multiple victims and employing a collective decision-making process on the aggregated outcomes.  Another limitation concerns the range of tasks feasible for LeechHijack. Our empirical evaluations indicate efficacy in tasks within the MMLU benchmark, yet the potential utility for more complex assignments, such as those posited by GAIA, remains untested. LeechHijack is primarily configured for conventional chat-based tasks and is likely to face challenges with more intricate functions. Enhancing the attack's effectiveness on sophisticated commands necessitates advanced configurations and methodologies.  Agent systems employ costly LLMs, thus enabling attackers to exploit LeechHijack without engaging in complex tasks. These hijacked resources can facilitate simpler activities, such as information gathering or content template creation, leveraging the model's comprehensive knowledge base. Consequently, LeechHijack constitutes a tangible and persistent threat to Multi-Agent Communication Protocols (MCP) and agent systems, irrespective of the complexity of the hijacked task.

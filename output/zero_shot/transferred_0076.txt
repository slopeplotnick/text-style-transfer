In recent literature, it has been observed that predictive models often stabilize prior to the final computational step (Pengxiang et al., 2025). Methodologies have been developed to optimize the balance between exploration and efficiency by leveraging historical outcomes and predictive stability, thereby eliminating superfluous steps (Wei et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b; Or-El et al., 2025). Certain studies concentrate on error correction and enhancement of model outcomes, enabling models to self-correct uncertain segments (Zhang et al., 2025a; Su et al., 2025). Accelerated approaches address this issue via techniques derived from continuous processes, such as incremental learning, consistency maintenance, and flow matching (Sahoo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Meister et al., 2024). Additional strategies for expedited processing include efficient data retrieval and concurrent verification methods (Yuan et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a; Sohoni et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025a). While these methods demonstrate efficacy, they frequently necessitate supplementary training, novel model architectures, or complex criteria, thus underscoring the need for a simplified mechanism for termination decision-making without auxiliary training or specialized configurations. This study examines the decision-making process for termination and introduces SchED, a schedule-based early stopping technique that requires no additional training or model-specific adjustments. Fundamentally, this method evaluates the confidence level of each segment against an adaptive threshold that progressively decreases. By decoupling confidence from step count and allowing for gradual threshold modulation, predictions can be halted upon stabilization, circumventing abrupt transitions or rigid limitations. SchED is compatible with standard scheduling and does not necessitate modifications to model training protocols (Nie et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025).

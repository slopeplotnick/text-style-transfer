The observed patterns are not confined to a single model type. LLaDA demonstrates analogous outcomes, wherein the implementation of slower, steady schedules enhances efficiency without significantly compromising the quality of foundational models while yielding substantial improvements for instruction-tuned models. Generally, employing thresholds that dynamically adjust based on progress consistently accelerates processes, with the choice of schedule contingent upon the acceptable margin of error. For tasks demanding high accuracy, slower and more meticulous settings are recommended. Conversely, when minor errors are acceptable or speed is prioritized, expedited schedules prove more effective, as evaluated through a quality versus speed metric.  In this study, we introduce SchED, a method that does not necessitate additional training and is compatible with any model configuration. SchED determines the stopping point based on the stability of the model's predictions, utilizing a confidence measure and an adaptive threshold that evolves progressively. This method reduces the number of steps required for denoising in both Dream and LLaDA models, particularly in instruction-tuned models where uncertainty diminishes rapidly, while maintaining robust performance. This assertion is supported by analyses of entropy and the quality-speed trade-off. SchED integrates seamlessly with existing schedules, without necessitating modifications to the models themselves. The schedules generated by SchED span from highly precise ones aimed at high accuracy to expedited ones optimized for contexts where speed or tolerating minor errors is more critical.  Future investigations might explore the learning of schedule parameters, task-specific method adjustments, domain-specific threshold refinement, or the integration with techniques such as speculative or cached denoising. Overall, approaching diffusion decoding as a problem of determining the optimal stopping point offers a straightforward and reliable means to utilize these models while balancing accuracy and speed. SchED enhances the efficiency of diffusion decoding without requiring additional training, albeit with certain limitations. For instance, SchED operates exclusively during inference and treats the schedule as distinct. We did not investigate concurrent model and schedule training, sophisticated result combination strategies, or the closer integration with other acceleration methods such as speculative or cached denoising. These avenues hold potential for further refinement but are earmarked for subsequent research endeavors.

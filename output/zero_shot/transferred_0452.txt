Numerical modeling experts specialize in transforming abstract ideas into quantitative representations. Their approach begins with a central concept, which is systematically decomposed into smaller components, each characterized by numerical values and descriptive details. Probabilities are assigned to each component to represent the likelihood of various scenarios, employing different methodologies to evaluate the plausibility of these details. To ensure the accuracy and utility of their model, it is essential to validate it against empirical data. This involves predicting data patterns using the numerical model and testing these predictions against observed realities, a process that relies on statistical inference and rigorous analysis.   A specialized approach known as Monte Carlo simulation is often employed in conjunction with Bayesian inversion to refine predictions based on available data. This iterative process updates the probabilities and assumptions within the model, enabling decisions to be made that account for uncertainty. Effective communication and collaboration among domain experts are crucial for ensuring the reliability of these methods.   Artificial intelligence (AI) has begun to play a significant role in facilitating these processes, particularly in mineral exploration. To convey the application of AI in a relatable manner, it can be likened to the operation of a self-driving car. A self-driving car must navigate from one location to another while maintaining safety, complying with legal regulations, and optimizing efficiency in time and performance. To achieve this, it performs actions such as steering, accelerating, and braking, all of which are informed by sensor-generated data that reflect the current conditions of the road, traffic, and surrounding environment. The AI framework underlying this example integrates three key elements: rewards, which represent the overarching goal of safety, legality, and efficiency; actions, which constitute the operational decisions made by the system; and state, which captures the contextual details of the roadway, including the positions and motions of other vehicles and pedestrians.

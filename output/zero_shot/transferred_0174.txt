Efforts to enhance machine capabilities in analyzing high-resolution images have gained significant attention in recent years. A common strategy involves magnifying specific regions of an image to extract finer details. Traditional approaches to this challenge often rely on techniques such as Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning. While these methods can yield improvements, they are computationally expensive, time-intensive, and frequently demonstrate limited adaptability across diverse systems. In contrast, alternative methods eschew extensive training by employing attention mechanisms or tree-based search algorithms to autonomously identify and focus on salient regions within an image. Typically, these techniques begin by analyzing the entire image and progressively zooming in on areas of interest. However, one notable limitation of such approaches is their tendency to overlook smaller details in the initial stages of processing, potentially leading to errors.  To address these shortcomings, a novel framework known as Retrieval-Augmented Perception (RAP) has been proposed to improve the processing of high-resolution images without the need for additional model training. RAP integrates three primary components in its workflow. First, it utilizes a pre-trained model to identify regions of the image that closely correspond to the features of interest, effectively filtering out extraneous information. Second, it ensures the preservation of the spatial structure of the identified regions to maintain contextual integrity. Lastly, it employs a tree-based strategy to determine the optimal number of regions for analysis, balancing the trade-off between information richness and computational efficiency. This approach represents a promising direction for enhancing machine perception of high-resolution images while mitigating the resource demands associated with traditional training-based methods.

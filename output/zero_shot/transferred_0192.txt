Initially, efforts were undertaken to ensure the elimination of duplicates within the cases and questions to maintain the independence of samples, followed by a comprehensive data cleaning process. Samples lacking questions or options, as well as those containing figures or tables, were excluded. Optical Character Recognition (OCR) errors were meticulously identified and manually corrected to ensure textual accuracy. In the context of clinical data, patient identifiers and other sensitive information were redacted or modified to uphold privacy and ethical standards. The Ethics Committee of Xiyuan Hospital provided approval for the use of clinical cases in this study.  Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment (SDT) is intrinsic to Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) diagnosis and therapeutic formulation. To evaluate the model’s efficacy, a 14-component evaluation framework was devised, encompassing syndrome and causative factors, as delineated in Table 1. TCM experts initially annotated the answers and options, subsequently cross-verified their work, and ultimately subjected it to third-party review to ensure consistency and reliability. The questions incorporated deliberately misleading distractors to enhance the test’s ability to discriminate varying levels of understanding.  In addition to analyzing final reasoning outcomes, two process metrics were introduced: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Content Completeness, assessing whether the model comprehensively addresses key patient details, and CoT Accuracy, which evaluates the alignment of the model’s reasoning with the original case to detect discrepancies. The evaluation tasks are designed to illuminate variations in the model’s foundational understanding and responses, facilitating a more holistic appraisal. All questions were composed in a multiple-choice format, accommodating single and multiple answers.  The TCM Basic Knowledge section spans four domains: comprehension of TCM classics, foundational theories, knowledge of Chinese medicinal substances and formulas, and syndrome differentiation based on tongue, pulse, facial, and acupoint analysis. The questions for this section were derived from preprocessed examination materials. The Medical Ethics component assesses the model’s grasp of clinical ethical principles, encompassing the conflicts between traditional and contemporary medicine, identification of unscientific practices, respect for patient beliefs, and the principles of informed consent. This section includes both examination-derived and expert-crafted questions.  The Content Safety section evaluates the extent to which the TCM model adheres to professional boundaries and safety protocols. The model is expected to restrict its responses to TCM-related inquiries, avoiding engagement with non-medical topics concerning privacy, safety, or ethical values. Specialists in content safety and ethics were involved in the design and review of questions to ensure that the evaluation aligns with TCM model standards.

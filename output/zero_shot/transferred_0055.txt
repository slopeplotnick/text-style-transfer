To streamline program statements, an approach involves encapsulating them within an if-statement governed by a condition defined by a relaxation variable. For instance, in the program shown in Listings 3, lines 5 and 6 are modified by CFAULTS using relaxation variables, namely _rv1 and _rv2, which subsequently appear as lines 11 and 12 in Listings 4. When if-statements are relaxed, the operations within the `then` and `else` blocks adhere to the same relaxation principle. However, the conditions of if-statements are handled via a ternary operator, exemplified in line 14 of Listings 4. Specifically, if the relaxation variable evaluates to true, the original if-condition is executed. If the relaxation variable is false, another relaxation variable, such as _ev4 in Listings 4, determines whether the program proceeds to the `then` block or the `else` block, if the latter exists. These relaxation variables are tailored to specific failing test cases and serve to facilitate the decision process between the `then` and `else` blocks.  For expression lists, CFAULTS applies a similar relaxation procedure by encapsulating each expression within a ternary operator rather than an if-statement. When a specific segment of a program is deactivated, the corresponding expression is replaced with a constant value, typically 1. As an illustration, the initialization of variable `i` on line 11 of Listings 3 is transformed into a ternary operation on line 17 of Listings 4. Relaxation variables within loops are represented as Boolean vectors, which serve to relax statements on a per-iteration basis. Each element in these vectors corresponds to a specific iteration of the loop, enabling tailored relaxation of the loop's statements. Additionally, CFAULTS allows users to configure the maximum number of loop iterations. This relaxation mechanism extends to inner loops, where arrays of arrays are generated to manage relaxation variables for nested iterations.  For straightforward program statements within a loop, CFAULTS encapsulates them in if-statements linked to relaxation variables associated with the iteration index. An example of such implementation is presented in line 20 of Listings 4, illustrating a relaxed statement within a loop. Furthermore, the loop's condition is relaxed by a corresponding relaxation variable, as depicted in line 18 of Listings 4. Each loop is assigned unique offsets to index the relaxation variables, which are initialized prior to the loop and incremented at the conclusion of each iteration, as demonstrated in line 19 of Listings 4.

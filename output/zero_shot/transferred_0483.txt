To evaluate the feasibility of utilizing Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning for solving Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) problems, we replicated the SMART-LLM framework (Kannan et al., 2024) as an initial point of reference. This approach facilitated the identification of fundamental challenges associated with the application of LLMs for coordination, prior to the development of our enhanced version.  The SMART-LLM dataset, employed for testing, comprised 36 tasks distributed across four complexity levels: Elemental (involving a single action), Simple (entailing multiple objects with homogeneous robots), Compound (involving heterogeneous robots with specialized skills), and Complex (requiring collaborative teamwork). These tasks were executed within the AI2-THOR simulation environment.  For the LLM configuration, we utilized Claude 4 Sonnet as the reasoning tool, setting the temperature to 0.0 to ensure consistency and replicability of decisions. The evaluation of performance adhered to the SMART-LLM methodology, examining five key metrics: Success Rate (SR), Task Completion Rate (TCR), Goal Condition Recall (GCR), Executability (EXE), and Robot Utilization (RU). These metrics served to assess task execution proficiency and the effectiveness of robot collaboration.  Upon identifying issues related to validation and LLM reliability during the SMART-LLM testing phase, we devised and evaluated the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture. This architecture demonstrated that the integration of structured checks and retry mechanisms could address these issues, thereby affirming the potential of LLMs in MRTA tasks. The Self Corrective-Agent Architecture incorporated multi-step verification processes with retry options, with significant settings including: a maximum of three retries per agent, checks at each step (encompassing task decomposition, assignment, and code generation), and feedback loops aimed at continuous improvement.

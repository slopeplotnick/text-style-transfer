This study addresses the challenge of data scarcity by developing larger and more diverse text corpora, utilizing sources such as social media, news, and spoken language. The integration of explainable artificial intelligence (AI) ensures the reliability of sentiment analysis within texts. We are investigating methods to expand lexical resources and evaluating the effectiveness of AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa models in the context of under-resourced African languages. The primary objective is to broaden coverage, improve accuracy, and enhance the understanding of sentiment across different languages.   The research demonstrates strategies for augmenting lexical databases and conducting sentiment analysis in low-resource languages. By employing a range of methodologies, we have compiled an improved set of lexical resources for languages including Ciluba, French, English, and several South African languages. This enhancement was validated through both automated and manual verification of sentiment accuracy. Experimental results indicate that AfroXLMR generally surpasses AfriBERTa in terms of accuracy and consistency, although AfriBERTa remains a viable option in scenarios with limited computational resources. Both models exhibit proficiency in identifying morphemes that convey sentiment in morphologically complex languages such as Zulu and Xhosa.  Despite challenges related to translation and data availability, this study underscores the feasibility and significance of conducting sentiment analysis in resource-limited languages. Future efforts will concentrate on acquiring additional data, further leveraging explainable AI, and expanding lexical resources to enhance our methodologies. This research provides a robust framework for continued exploration and development in the domain of low-resource language processing.

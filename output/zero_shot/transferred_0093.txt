Researchers are actively investigating the mechanisms underlying diffusion models, particularly focusing on how these models balance the replication of learned information with the generation of novel outputs. Previous studies have explored this balance by analyzing model behavior and identifying the factors that influence their tendency to replicate existing data or produce new content. Many of these investigations assess model behavior from initial conditions, utilizing smaller datasets such as FFHQ and CIFAR-10. These studies are particularly informative in scenarios with limited data availability, although the propensity for replication diminishes when larger datasets, like ImageNet, are employed. Song et al. observed that when models are trained on extensive datasets, commencing the process at an early stage leads to the creation of new images, whereas initiating the process at a later stage tends to yield reproductions of existing ones. Consequently, a central question arises: what governs this balance in models trained on substantial datasets?   In pursuit of an answer, we examined the model's training objectives, specifically focusing on flow matching and conditional flow matching. These methodologies are traditionally regarded as challenging due to their requirement for specific data that is often unavailable. However, we demonstrated that within a Gaussian framework and with a constrained training set, it is indeed possible to discern the flow. This approach enables the calculation of the optimal trajectory for the model at any given point. Analyzing this trajectory reveals that model training comprises two primary phases: initially, the model synthesizes information from multiple data points to guide its progression, subsequently refining its focus to a singular data point. Additionally, our findings indicate that both the magnitude of the dataset and the sample size are pivotal in determining the timing at which this transition occurs.

Self-Taught Learning (STL) is a system designed to enhance a model's performance by leveraging its existing knowledge to generate and learn from its own explanations. A critical research question involves determining the optimal structure of such explanations. To effectively answer visual-based questions, the model must exhibit clarity in both perception and reasoning. STL addresses this challenge by employing a methodology wherein the model first describes the visual content, subsequently engages in reasoning, and ultimately formulates a response. This structured approach mirrors human cognitive processes, fostering better organization of the model's reasoning.  Traditionally, relying solely on explanations from correct answers has limited effectiveness, as it exposes the model only to optimal reasoning patterns. This constrained learning framework proves inadequate for complex tasks where distinguishing between valid and flawed reasoning is pivotal. To address this limitation, STL incorporates explanations for incorrect answers as well. This approach aligns with human learning processes, which involve examining both successes and failures. Explanations for incorrect answers identify errors in reasoning, enabling the model to discern the distinctions between valid and invalid approaches. Consequently, the model can avoid errors such as overgeneralization or conjecture unsupported by evidence.  This principle underpins the Self-Taught Multimodal Reasoner (STL), a system wherein the model learns to generate and refine explanations for both correct and incorrect responses, subsequently improving its reasoning capabilities through this training process. Experimental evaluations across diverse domains, including common sense reasoning, scientific inquiry, and linguistic tasks, demonstrate that STL outperforms models trained solely on final answers. These results suggest that STL represents a promising alternative to methods that rely heavily on extensive human annotations.

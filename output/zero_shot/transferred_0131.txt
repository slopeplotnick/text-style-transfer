This study investigated three primary research questions concerning the performance and behavior of programming agents in relation to process-related metrics. First, we sought to examine the performance of programming agents on process-related metrics, identifying their key characteristics and the factors influencing these measurements. Second, we aimed to analyze their problem-solving behavior, focusing on their typical problem-solving strategies, the determinants of their behavior, and their adherence to planned methodologies. Additionally, we explored whether these agents modify their strategies dynamically during problem-solving. Third, we aimed to identify recurring inefficiencies in their behavior by investigating common issues or suboptimal practices and assessing their frequency.  To address these questions, we conducted an empirical evaluation of two widely used programming agents: SWE-agent (SA) and OpenHands (OH). Each agent was tested under its default operational constraints: SWE-agent was analyzed with a task cost cap of $2, while OpenHands was limited to 100 execution steps. Both agents utilized their standard tools for file inspection and code editing during the experiments.   The agents were tested in combination with four distinct language models: DeepSeek-V3 671B MoE (DSK-V3), DeepSeek-R1 (DSK-R1), Devstral-small-2505 24B (Dev), and Claude Sonnet 4 (CLD-4). These language models were selected to represent a range of functionalities, including general-purpose, reasoning-specific, state-of-the-art, and open-source systems. This resulted in eight unique pairings of agents and models, providing a broad basis for analysis.

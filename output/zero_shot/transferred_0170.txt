The methodologies under discussion exhibit significant limitations in two primary domains. Firstly, concerning data utilization, prevailing studies predominantly rely on quantitative and temporal data, with inadequate incorporation of textual information. This deficiency hampers the integration of diverse information types such as market sentiment or policy explanations. Secondly, the models themselves present challenges. Neural networks excel in pattern recognition; however, they lack the capacity to comprehend the underlying meanings of complex situations. Consequently, they struggle to identify cause-and-effect relationships between events and price fluctuations, rendering them suboptimal for analyzing intricate market scenarios.   The advent of large language models (LLMs) has marked a paradigm shift from mere pattern recognition to the incorporation of memory. For instance, FinMem developed a financial memory system that compiles summaries of news and events, whereas MagicNet introduced a hierarchical memory system to temporally organize market data. Nevertheless, these strategies still encounter challenges in the configuration of their memory systems. They largely maintain discrete text or data elements without synthesizing them into a comprehensive narrative of event progression. Additionally, their reliance on semantic similarity hinders their efficacy in managing complex situations. In summary, although these methodologies are transitioning towards incorporating memory, substantial advancements are needed in comprehending and utilizing pertinent information, which constitutes the focal point of this paper.  Regarding event-driven stock prediction, this approach seeks to elucidate the primary drivers of stock price variations. Prior to the development of LLMs, the field evolved from examining event impacts on stock returns to the automated extraction of event signals from text using foundational models such as Bag-of-Words and topic models, subsequently advancing to sophisticated deep learning tools like BERT. Nonetheless, all these methods share a fundamental limitation: a continued emphasis on quantitative data. Despite the extraction of event labels or sentiment scores, these metrics are reduced to features in temporal models such as LSTM or Transformer. Consequently, these models focus on identifying data patterns rather than understanding the intrinsic logic of events, leading to the loss of critical details and a deficiency in comprehensive analytical reasoning.

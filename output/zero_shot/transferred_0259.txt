Although a model may exhibit high accuracy in specific tasks, this does not necessarily indicate proficiency in integrating diverse types of information, as illustrated in the central section of Figure 8. For example, the GPT-5 Mini model demonstrates a notably low error rate but underperforms relative to other models in synthesizing multiple input modalities. This limitation is particularly evident when one type of input is significantly weaker than the other. Ideally, a robust model should dynamically allocate greater reliance on stronger inputs while minimizing dependence on weaker ones; however, not all models effectively implement this adaptive behavior. This issue is significant because prioritizing accuracy alone may inadvertently favor models that are less adept at managing noisy or imbalanced data.  In contrast, Llama-4 Maverick outperforms a conventional Bayesian reliability-weighted linear fusion methodology, suggesting that it employs more sophisticated mechanisms for integrating multimodal information. Computational analyses indicate that its performance aligns more closely with non-linear methods, such as random forest models, rather than simpler linear approaches. Empirical comparisons between single-input tasks and multimodal tasks—including scenarios that combine text and visual inputs—reveal that models such as Llama-4 Maverick, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o generally achieve reductions in error rates when leveraging multimodal inputs. However, the observed variation in improvement across models indicates ongoing potential for advancing multimodal learning frameworks. Furthermore, to address the limitations of solely measuring accuracy, a new evaluation metric—the Bayesian Consistency Score (BCS)—has been developed. This metric enables the assessment of models based on their ability to adjust their behavior in accordance with logical and probabilistic principles. By systematically modifying input conditions, BCS facilitates a deeper understanding of model reasoning processes, irrespective of baseline performance. While complex strategies, such as selective focus on critical features or adaptive rule-switching, may enhance the alignment of model responses with expected patterns, the adoption of BCS provides a comprehensive framework for evaluating whether a model's behavior adheres to Bayesian principles under dynamic conditions.

Languages were grouped into clusters, referred to as In-Domain languages, as illustrated in Figure 2. The proposed model was evaluated on both In-Domain and Out-of-Domain languages. For the Out-of-Domain evaluation, a total of eleven low-resource languages were selected. These languages were chosen to represent their respective clusters effectively while encompassing diverse linguistic groups. The evaluation was conducted using six safety classification datasets: Aegis-Content-Safety-2.0-Test (Aegis-CS2), released in 2025; HarmBench, introduced by Mazeika et al. in 2024; Redteam2k, developed by Luo et al. in 2024; JBB-Behaviors, composed of JBB-Behav and JBB-Judge, curated by Chao et al. in 2024; and StrongReject, created by Souly et al. in 2024. These datasets collectively address a wide range of harm categories, including hate speech (identity-based and general), sexual content, suicide and self-harm, violence, weapons-related content, privacy violations, minor sexual content, toxicity, and abuse. This diversity renders them suitable for assessing the safety measures of the proposed approach.  The model training utilized XLM-RoBERTa-Base (279 million parameters) and XLM-RoBERTa-Large (560 million parameters) as the foundational multilingual architectures. These models are well-suited for cross-lingual tasks due to their pre-training across 100 languages, aligning with the study’s objectives. A single-layer classification head was appended to the encoder, designed to match the encoder’s output dimensions and incorporated a binary output layer. Both the encoder and classification head were fully trained using aggregated training datasets. All training and testing procedures were conducted on NVIDIA H100 GPU clusters, leveraging Bfloat16 precision to optimize computational efficiency and performance.  To establish baseline comparisons, the proposed model’s performance was contrasted against smaller guardrail models, such as DuoGuard-0.5B (2025), PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen-Smol, and WalledGuard-C (2024), as well as larger guardrail models, including PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen (2025), LlamaGuard3 (2024), and Aegis-Defensive (2025). These baseline models vary in their language coverage, spanning English-only systems to multilingual architectures supporting up to 29 languages. This comparative framework facilitated the evaluation of the proposed safety guardrail’s effectiveness across both high-resource and low-resource linguistic settings.

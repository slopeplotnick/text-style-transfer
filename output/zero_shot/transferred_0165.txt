The study commenced with a fundamental model utilizing solely the EMER dataset, which, although highly regarded for its quality, lacks extensive guidance. The integration of additional data that aligns with emotional contexts significantly enhanced the model's performance, underscoring the critical need for reliable early-stage emotional exemplars. Subsequently, a novel method was employed to restructure reasoning founded on Modality Integration (MI), facilitating the model's initiation from emotion-centric perspectives. This empirical evidence substantiated the hypothesis that organizing cognitive processes around MI prevents initial fixation on visual or textual content. Furthermore, the research examined the efficacy of each proposed reward in maintaining coherent and stable reasoning. The Modality-Aligned Order Reward encourages the model to commence reasoning from the focal point of MI, thereby reinstating the previously acquired structure. Conversely, the Modality-Grounded Reasoning Reward ensures that reasoning remains emotionally valid and congruent with the target emotion. The synergistic application of both rewards yielded optimal results, demonstrating their complementary nature. The primary objective was to enhance the reliability of reasoning-based emotion understanding by addressing preliminary reasoning deficiencies in Multimodal Language Learning Models (MLLMs). The introduction of MI and the development of Modality-Integrated Grounded Reasoning (MIGR), a training framework designed to initiate and sustain reasoning from the most emotion-relevant aspects, were central to this endeavor. The two-step method guarantees that both learning processes align with emotion-centric components, resulting in elucidations that are clearer, emotionally grounded, and substantive. Empirical evaluations on MAFW and DFEW benchmarks reveal that MIGR substantially reduces emotionally inconsistent reasoning. In summary, MIGR advances the trustworthiness and comprehensibility of emotional reasoning, highlighting the imperative of focusing on central elements in both data preparation and enhancement strategies.

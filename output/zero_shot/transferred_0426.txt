This research investigates the identification of patterns indicative of inefficiencies or regressions within a project. The study employs the GRAPHECTORY tool to facilitate the detection of these patterns. The investigation prioritizes specific indicators suggestive of suboptimal performance: Hypothesis 1 (H1) considers the persistence in executing identical actions that fail to resolve the issue, necessitating an alternative approach. Hypothesis 2 (H2) examines the erratic engagement within the project, characterized by frequent transitions rather than consistent progress. Hypothesis 3 (H3) focuses on prolonged engagement with a task without discernible advancement. Hypothesis 4 (H4) addresses operational failures potentially resulting from inadequate tool utilization. The repeated examination of certain elements within the project—termed RepeatedView—suggests a recurrent assessment of identical components, often following unsuccessful edits or unresolved issues. For instance, following two unsuccessful modification attempts (step 9), the file client.py is accessed again (step 10) to identify the underlying problem. Additionally, the notion of ZoomOut pertains to the sequence of actions that are incongruent with the structural hierarchy of the project, indicating a regression prompted by the recognition of misdirection. An example is provided where, after erroneously accessing the directory 'postgres' (step 3), the correct path is retraced to the main directory '/testbed/db' (step 4) before navigating to the correct subdirectory '/testbed/db/postgreSQL' (step 5) to locate the problematic file client.py (step 6).

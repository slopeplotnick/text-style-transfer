Recommending items in a marketplace characterized by extensive textual content and dynamic interactions presents significant challenges due to the continual evolution of both content and connections. The innovative system, GraphMatch, integrates pre-trained language models with graph neural networks to effectively address these complexities. Rather than employing separate models, GraphMatch utilizes a synergistic approach, combining text encoders and graph networks. The system employs techniques such as adversarial negative sampling and training on specific graph snapshots to capture both the nuanced semantics of the text and the evolving structure of the graph over time. Empirical evaluations conducted using data from Upwork, a prominent job marketplace, demonstrate the system's capability to function efficiently in real-time applications. GraphMatch outperformed systems that rely solely on either language models or graph models for matching tasks, highlighting its superior efficacy and operational efficiency. This underscores the effectiveness of integrating linguistic and graph data for recommendations in environments with substantial textual content and fluctuating connections.   Platforms such as e-commerce sites, job platforms, academic networks, and news feeds can be conceptualized as Temporal Text-Attributed Graphs (TTAGs). In TTAGs, nodes are associated with detailed text descriptions that evolve over time, while edges represent interactions such as purchases or responses. For optimal matching or recommendation outcomes, it is crucial to integrate the intricate semantics of the text with the overarching structure of the graph as it evolves over time, a task that remains challenging. Although contemporary language models such as SentenceBERT and E5 are proficient at transforming documents into embeddings and comparing text semantics, they overlook critical aspects when applied to TTAGs. Firstly, the linkage between two items is often contingent on the surrounding connections within the graph, a factor not accounted for by text models. Secondly, within TTAGs, both the meaning of a node and the justifications for its connections can rapidly change. For instance, the advent of generative AI has shifted the focus of "AI Writing" job posts from editing to prompt engineering. Additionally, global events continually influence the matching of freelancers and clients in the global job market. Treating text as static features can lead to issues, such as the inadvertent disclosure of future information that should not be utilized.

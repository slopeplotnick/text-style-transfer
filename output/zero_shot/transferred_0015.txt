In 2024, Goddard and colleagues introduced the Nearswap method, which enhances interpolation by intensifying it when the parameters are similar and diminishing it when they are dissimilar. To explore various model configurations, we selected three categories of large language models for evaluation: GPT, LLAMA, and QWEN. The GPT group, established by Radford et al. in 2019 and further developed by Gao et al. in 2020, includes GPT2-small, GPT2-medium, GPT2-large, GPT2-x1, and GPT-neo-2.7B. The LLAMA group, as documented by Touvron et al. in 2023 and Dubey et al. in 2024, comprises LLAMA-2-7B, LLAMA-3-8B, LLAMA-3.1-8B, LLAMA-3.2-1B, and LLAMA-3.2-3B. The QWEN group, as developed by Qwen in 2024, consists of QWEN2-0.5B, QWEN2-1.5B, and QWEN2-7B. These models are accessible via the Hugging Face platform, with links provided in Appendix A. The computational resources required for ongoing pretraining to produce biased large language models (LLMs) are detailed in Appendix C, and the hyperparameter specifications are outlined in Appendix D.   To assess social bias in LLMs, we employed three benchmarks: BBQ, BOLD, and HONEST, with links to these datasets provided in Appendix E. The BBQ dataset, created by Parrish et al. in 2022, encompasses approximately 58,000 question-answer pairs across nine social domains pertinent to U.S. English speakers. It evaluates the extent to which models rely on stereotypes as opposed to explicit evidence by comparing "underspecified" and "fully specified" versions of each question. The BOLD dataset, developed by Dhamala et al. in 2021, includes 23,679 prompts categorized into 43 groups according to occupation, gender, race, religion, and political orientation. To ensure that debiasing methods do not compromise model performance on other tasks, we assessed both debiased and pre-trained LLMs using the SuperGLUE benchmark, which comprises eight tasks: BoolQ, CB, COPA, MultiRC, ReCoRD, RTE, WiC, and WSC. All evaluations were conducted using the Language Model Evaluation Harness.

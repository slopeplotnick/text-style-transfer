Large language models (LLMs) have been increasingly integrated into applications such as Google's AI summaries, where they synthesize information from extensive documents. However, the equitable treatment of input within these models remains uncertain. In a study concerning abortion-related news, researchers constructed 40 sets consisting of three articles each—one advocating, one neutral, and one opposing—and varied the order of these articles across six permutations. Subsequently, Gemini 2.5 Flash was tasked with producing a neutral summary. The resulting summaries were evaluated in relation to the original articles utilizing tools assessing lexical overlap, semantic alignment, and factual accuracy. Findings indicated a propensity for the summaries to favor the content of the first article in sequence, particularly when assessed using BERTScore. The initial article exerted greater influence than the subsequent articles, which shared comparable impact. This phenomenon suggests that the initial document exerts a disproportionate effect on the summary outcome, potentially posing a challenge for systems reliant on such summaries or for decision-making AI systems. Generative AI, integral to these systems, is rapidly advancing and being employed across various domains. These "agentic AI" systems are engineered to autonomously manage complex tasks with minimal oversight, by decomposing overarching objectives into manageable actions. This involves strategic planning and iterative decision-making, supplemented by external information when necessary. Consequently, the efficacy of these systems is heavily contingent upon the precision with which the underlying LLMs can identify, organize, and integrate information drawn from directives and external data.

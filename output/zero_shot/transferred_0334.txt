The findings presented in Table 1 demonstrate the efficacy of integrating model-focused and data-focused methodologies. A comparison between the "Unify" block and other individual methods reveals that mixed strategies consistently yield superior performance across various datasets and models. This indicates that internal uncertainty signals and external evidence function in mutually reinforcing ways. Specifically, the Score-Level Fusion approach illustrates that a straightforward aggregation can mitigate the significant discrepancies observed with solitary methods. By integrating diverse signals, this approach diminishes the errors associated with individual predictions and enhances reliability, outperforming the HD or FV methods when utilized independently in most scenarios. Furthermore, the Evidence-Aware Pipeline exhibits superior performance, frequently ranking as the most effective among all the evaluated methods. This is attributed to its capability to address the issue of excessive reliance on external evidence. In instances where external information is insufficient (such as in NEI cases), coerced decisions can result in inaccuracies; however, this pipeline circumvents such errors by pivoting to internal uncertainty signals, which remain dependable in the absence of external evidence.  Hybrid methodologies also resolve the challenge of inconsistent performance observed in RQ1. Standalone HD methods, for example, can exhibit considerable variability between models such as LLaMA and Qwen, whereas hybrid methods maintain consistency across different models. This consistency underscores the advantage of signal integration in ameliorating confidence issues inherent in standalone HD methods. Consequently, these frameworks not only enhance accuracy but also demonstrate robustness regardless of the model employed.

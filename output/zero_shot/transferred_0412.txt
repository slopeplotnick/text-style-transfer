There is a significant global demand for mental health support, yet underdiagnosis remains a pervasive issue. Barriers such as limited clinic hours, geographical inaccessibility, and shortages in mental health professionals hinder efforts to facilitate early detection and diagnosis of mental health conditions. Structural inefficiencies and individual-level obstacles further exacerbate delayed diagnoses, particularly for individuals with severe mental health disorders. Traditional diagnostic approaches, including in-person consultations and self-reported questionnaires, are ill-equipped to meet the growing needs of mental health care due to workforce shortages, high costs, regional disparities, and societal stigma. Consequently, individuals may face prolonged waiting periods for mental health services, ranging from 3 to 18 months depending on regional availability of providers. This highlights an urgent need to develop more efficient methodologies for the early identification of mental health issues and the timely provision of support.  Emerging technological solutions, such as portable language models, have the potential to address these challenges and facilitate large-scale mental health detection and intervention. Specifically, the integration of advanced technologies into personal devices, such as smartphones, offers a promising avenue for early detection of mental health conditions. By analyzing language data derived from social media activity and smartphone applications in real-time, these tools enable unobtrusive monitoring of mental states, allowing for the early identification of mood fluctuations, stress patterns, and suicide risk. Such systems are particularly effective in recognizing linguistic indicators associated with mental health concerns, thereby supporting preventive efforts and enabling timely interventions.  Furthermore, the deployment of these models directly on personal devices ensures privacy by processing language data locally, rather than transmitting sensitive information to external systems. This approach expands access to mental health monitoring beyond traditional clinical settings, offering a scalable and privacy-preserving solution to promote mental health awareness and intervention among diverse populations.

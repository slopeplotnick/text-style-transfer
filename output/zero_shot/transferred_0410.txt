The present study demonstrates the efficacy of our system across various models and algorithms, with notable enhancements observed in both the Qwen2.5 and Llama architectures, indicating the robustness of our approach across differing structural paradigms. Specifically, the smaller Llama-3.2-1B model exhibits improved accuracy when employing our OptPO-SFT and OptPO methodologies, surpassing traditional techniques and underscoring the adaptability of our method to diverse model sizes. Furthermore, our OptPO framework exhibits compatibility with multiple reinforcement learning algorithms, including PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, consistently outperforming TTRL in both accuracy and efficiency. This consistency substantiates its effectiveness across disparate reinforcement learning paradigms.  In a comparative analysis of SFT-based and RL-based iterations, RL-based OptPO versions generally demonstrate superior accuracy, particularly in mathematical reasoning assessments, attributable to their enhanced utilization of feedback from rollouts compared to the SFT method. Nonetheless, OptPO-SFT consistently achieves notable improvements over TTSFT, affirming that our adaptive pseudo-labeling approach enhances SFT-style training during testing. Crucial findings include: (1) The implementation of adaptive early-stopping via the Bayesian Sequential Probability Ratio Test (BSPRT) enhances efficiency without compromising accuracy; (2) OptPO effectively balances accuracy with computational demands; and (3) OptPO-SFT serves as a viable rapid adaptation option in scenarios where RL-style updates are impracticable.  Collectively, these findings introduce OptPO as the inaugural framework that optimizes policies during testing with efficiency, addressing the inefficiency inherent in majority-vote-based rollouts in test-time reinforcement learning for large language models. By applying a Bayesian test for early-stopping, OptPO adjusts rollouts based on empirical evidence rather than predetermined limits, ensuring reliable pseudo-label generation without excessive sampling. The framework is designed to be user-friendly and compatible with established policy optimization methodologies, such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, in addition to supervised fine-tuning during testing. Our comprehensive evaluations reveal that OptPO maintains or enhances accuracy while reducing computational resource consumption.  In conclusion, this research elucidates the feasibility and significance of efficient, label-free adaptation, particularly as large language models are increasingly deployed in unpredictable, real-world contexts. We posit that OptPO represents a foundational blueprint for subsequent investigations into efficient learning during testing, a necessary evolution in the utilization of language models.

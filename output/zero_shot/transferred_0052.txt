When the target is situated at a considerable distance, the character engages in rapid locomotion to reduce the spatial gap. This behavior is derived from the integration of a static motion setup with the fundamental task of advancing toward the target. The implementation of a motion planner to dictate the character's movements is rendered unnecessary. The Stochastic Motion Planning (SMP) framework enables the system to utilize behaviors from the dataset and adapt them to cultivate new skills for various tasks. In the context of the Dodgeball task, agents trained with SMP autonomously acquire the ability to execute jumps and evasive maneuvers, akin to human strategies in dodgeball. These maneuvers, although absent from the original dataset, exhibit a resemblance to authentic human tactics. Conversely, Adversarial Motion Planning (AMP) demonstrates suboptimal performance in this task, likely due to the significant divergence between the requisite dodging skills and the basic movements present in the dataset. AMP policies employing a static discriminator also fail to generate naturalistic movements or achieve task proficiency, underscoring the limitations of adversarial approaches for task transference, even within identical task domains. SMP, however, facilitates the application of a singular motion setup across diverse tasks and policy frameworks. The outcomes depicted in Figure 7 indicate that SMP provides more efficient, consistent, and beneficial guidance during training. The ability to recover from a prone position is essential for both human and humanoid agents, yet it poses considerable learning challenges due to the prevalence of supplementary rewards utilized to curtail erratic movements. Notably, AMP—by permitting dynamic updates to the motion setup during training—can foster the acquisition of natural recovery techniques. Nonetheless, our methodology reveals that even with a fixed motion setup, SMP is capable of cultivating a robust recovery skill effective from random fallen positions. As illustrated in Figure 5(e), the character, upon being knocked down at a distance, successfully executes a sequence of rolling over, using its hands to push itself upright, and ultimately standing.

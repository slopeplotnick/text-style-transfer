In this study, we present the establishment of a novel dataset, HEALTHCONTRADICT, comprising 920 instances designed to investigate the capacity of language models to navigate conflicting information in the context of health-related inquiries. Each instance encompasses a health-related question accompanied by two documents that present opposing viewpoints, as well as a scientifically substantiated factual answer. The dataset serves as a benchmark to evaluate various language models, with sizes ranging from 1 billion to 8 billion parameters, thereby assessing their performance in addressing health inquiries amidst contradictory data.  Our exploration specifically interrogates the models' ability to respond to questions when supplied with both biomedical context and the capabilities of a language model. The investigation focuses on two primary aspects: the models' proficiency in resolving health questions under conditions of informational conflict and the influence exerted by biomedical context on their responses. To accomplish this, models were provided with contexts that were correct, incorrect, or contradictory, enabling an assessment of their accuracy.  Key contributions of this research include the introduction of the HEALTHCONTRADICT dataset aimed at challenging language models with conflicting biomedical information, accompanied by a comprehensive evaluation of these models' performance using the dataset to examine their handling of extensive, contradictory biomedical texts through definitive metrics. Furthermore, a comparative analysis was conducted between general language models and their specialized biomedical counterparts, revealing that specialized models demonstrate a superior ability to utilize accurate information while disregarding erroneous data. To maintain the integrity of the evaluation process, uniqueness was ensured for each document within the dataset.  As an exemplar, consider the query "Can coffee help you lose weight?" for which two documents offer divergent perspectives—one affirming, the other negating the possibility. The factual response indicates affirmation, supported by scientific evidence suggesting that caffeine consumption may contribute to reductions in weight, Body Mass Index (BMI), and body fat.

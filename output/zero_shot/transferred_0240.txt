The study identified three key findings: the feasibility of employing large language models (LLMs) to coordinate multi-robot task allocation (MRTA), the importance of structured validation methods in enhancing reasoning reliability, and the effectiveness of retry systems in addressing issues related to LLM outputs. Building on this foundational evidence, the authors proposed the LangGraph-based Task Allocation Architecture (LTAA) to further advance LLM-based MRTA systems. This framework was evaluated using the TEACH dataset, a benchmark standard for comparing algorithms such as Q-learning, Deep Q-Network (DQN), and Dynamic Programming (DP) in MRTA research. The TEACH dataset was adapted from household task scenarios to construction robotics scenarios to enable cross-comparison of performance across existing algorithms under consistent conditions.   The LTAA framework focuses solely on the allocation of robots to tasks, akin to conventional algorithms, and employs pre-decomposed tasks to ensure that both LLM-based and algorithmic methods are evaluated on equivalent problem sets. LTAA incorporates principles of self-correction, previously introduced, and employs a structured nine-step workflow. Unlike earlier approaches, such as the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture, which managed all aspects of task execution, LTAA is specifically designed for task allocation. By using pre-decomposed tasks, LTAA demonstrates comparable results to traditional optimization algorithms.   The framework introduces three key innovations to align with traditional optimization methods while maintaining clarity in reasoning processes. First, LTAA dynamically adjusts allocation strategies as projects progress. Second, it incorporates multi-stage validation mechanisms that include retry and self-correction processes to enhance the accuracy of LLM reasoning. Third, it integrates context-aware reasoning, enabling structured decision-making based on the specific capabilities of individual robots. These advancements are operationalized through the nine-step LangGraph workflow, which processes tasks via structured reasoning stages.

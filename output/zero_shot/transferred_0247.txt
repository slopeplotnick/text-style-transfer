The daughter language model is specifically developed for the MOD-OMA and functions similarly to Delilah by serving as both a parser and generator. However, it differs from the adult language model as its grammatical framework is initially underdeveloped and incrementally becomes more detailed through learning processes. Both the adult and child systems employ graph structures to represent grammatical knowledge. Their parsers and generators operate by integrating word structures to both produce grammatically correct sentences and evaluate grammar based on established rules. The child agent’s parser and generator are capable of functioning with incomplete grammar, akin to the manner in which humans acquire their native language. The daughter model necessitates interaction with the mother model until it reaches a stage of complete learning.   The daughter agent is composed of three core components: a parser and generator collectively referred to as the automaton, a grammar system that evolves in detail through the learning process, and a language acquisition device. It is important to note that the parser and generator remain constant throughout the learning phase. The learning process results in the clarification of grammatical knowledge, which is manifested through new word entries and detailed grammar specifications that restrict permissible word combinations. These structures offer comprehensive details about words and constructions, encompassing grammatical aspects such as phonological form, semantic content, and other grammatical properties.   Grammatical properties are represented through pairings, such as [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: content word] and [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: function word], which this study endeavors to investigate. The learning approach is unsupervised; consequently, the daughter model is not privy to the labels utilized by the mother model or its grammatical descriptions. The newly acquired grammatical properties and semantic content are denoted by distinct labels, such as property type 'A' and property value 'b' as depicted in Figures 2 and 3. These structures also encompass additional grammatical properties not examined in this study. For instance, the terminal feature distinguishes terminal words from abstract constructions, while the semantic content index facilitates representation of elements such as reflexives. Technical identifiers, including session ID and word entry number, are incorporated to uniquely identify each grammatical rule. As language learning is simulated, the grammar is capable of developing more sophisticated structures, as the HEAD and ARGUMENT may encompass structures akin to word items.

The specificity of prompts can significantly impact the performance of large language models (LLMs) in problem-solving contexts. However, this area has not been extensively explored in existing literature. This paper introduces a framework named DETAIL, designed to evaluate the performance of LLMs when exposed to prompts with varying levels of detail. Utilizing GPT-4, the researchers generated prompts with diverse specificity levels, quantified their specificity using perplexity, and assessed the accuracy of responses by employing GPT-based semantic equivalence. The evaluation was conducted on 30 novel reasoning tasks utilizing both GPT-4 and O3-mini, revealing that more specific prompts generally enhanced accuracy, particularly in smaller models and tasks requiring sequential steps. The study underscores the necessity of tailoring prompts to the context and provides valuable tools and datasets for further research.  LLMs have revolutionized numerous domains due to their exceptional text generation capabilities, exhibiting rapid advancements. These models now surpass the best existing techniques across various areas, transcending simple task execution. Recent evaluations predominantly concentrate on the ability of LLMs to solve mathematical and multi-step reasoning tasks. Enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs is paramount, and novel methodologies have been developed to achieve this through improved prompting techniques. Advanced strategies such as Chain-of-Thought, Tree of Thoughts, and Graph of Thoughts deconstruct problems into manageable steps to augment the models' reasoning abilities. A significant challenge within this domain is determining the optimal prompt specificity to maximize outcomes: detailed prompts may effectively guide the model but could also constrain cognitive flexibility, whereas less detailed prompts might afford greater freedom yet risk engendering confusion or inaccuracies.

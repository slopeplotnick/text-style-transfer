The comprehensive demonstration of linguistic knowledge in MODOMA allows researchers to evaluate the capabilities of both agents. This methodological approach contributes valuable insights to language acquisition systems that do not employ explicit representation mechanisms, such as large language models. MODOMA employs unsupervised learning, wherein the language learning apparatus processes unlabeled data and operates without access to the internal mechanisms of the mother agent. Furthermore, the daughter agent capitalizes on pre-existing grammatical knowledge to facilitate further learning. The innovative learning approach devised for MODOMA is termed internal annotation, a variant of self-supervised learning. The integration of diverse features and research possibilities within the multi-agent MODOMA framework represents a novel advancement. This system serves as a platform for conducting language learning experiments involving both an advanced language model and a developmental one. The agents engage interactively, and all aspects of the system—including configurations, input data, learning processes, interaction dynamics, communicative outputs, and grammar acquisition—are subject to user control, replication, measurement, and scrutiny.  In 2022, Ter Hoeve and colleagues developed a pedagogical strategy within a teacher-student paradigm to model language learning, conducting two preliminary experiments. The first experiment examines discrete domains characterized by 'two completely separate vocabularies' within an artificial language, while the second explores divergent syntactic structures employing repetitive tokens. In these experiments, the teacher agent selects a predetermined quantity of training data from pre-constructed sentences to furnish several student language models. Subsequent to training, these student models undergo evaluation with varied sentences from the artificial language. Additionally, the teacher model is trained on a distinct set of sentences before assessment. The principal distinction between this configuration and MODOMA lies in the nature of the training and testing datasets: Ter Hoeve and colleagues utilized pre-constructed sentences from an artificial language, whereas MODOMA engages with dynamically generated data representing natural language, synthesized online by Delilah leveraging explicit Dutch grammatical models during interactive sessions.

Auditors employ Journal Entry Tests (JETs) to identify anomalies within tax records; however, these tests frequently result in excessive false positives and may overlook minor discrepancies. This study investigates the potential of advanced language models (LLMs) to detect abnormalities in bookkeeping practices. We conducted evaluations using prominent LLMs, such as LLaMA and Gemma, on both simulated and authentic anonymous ledgers, comparing their efficacy to that of JETs and traditional machine learning methodologies. Our findings indicate that LLMs surpass conventional JETs and traditional machine learning techniques, additionally providing explanations in clear, comprehensible language. This underscores the potential for artificial intelligence to enhance auditing functions by collaborating with human auditors to ensure the integrity of financial records.  Financial auditors play a critical role in identifying irregular transactions indicative of errors, fraudulent activities, or tax evasion within a company's financial statements. Detecting such issues is essential for maintaining confidence in financial reporting. However, the extensive volume of accounting data poses significant challenges; existing manual and rule-based approaches often lack efficiency and may result in errors, causing auditors to overlook substantial warning signs. Standard JETs, which rely on predefined rules and known patterns, tend to flag an excessive number of transactions. These tests primarily detect anticipated issues, inundating auditors with alerts, many of which are false positives requiring substantial verification effort. In practice, auditors predominantly utilize sampling and manual procedures, which may fail to capture minor discrepancies within today's vast, intricate datasets. This disparity between increasingly complex data and the limitations of traditional audit methods highlights the necessity for more sophisticated, automated tools to enhance problem detection within the auditing process.

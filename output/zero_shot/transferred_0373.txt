Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have successfully integrated task-oriented and open-ended conversational systems. This section of the tutorial examines this technological evolution and explores potential future directions in the field. Notable examples include the development of new LLMs trained on conversational data, end-to-end training of dialogues, and the concept of dialogues engaging in self-improvement exercises. A significant future direction involves the creation of chat systems that incorporate content from diverse sources, such as web pages, lists, forms, tables, academic papers with figures and equations, and online documents. Furthermore, these systems are envisioned to utilize multimodal inputs, including visual information, knowledge, emotional context, and expressive cues such as facial movements and gestures.  Dr. Huck Yang, a Senior Research Scientist at NVIDIA Research, earned his Ph.D. from the Georgia Institute of Technology. His research interests include speech and language modeling, enhancing speech recognition, and integrating multimodal data. He has delivered a tutorial on "Parameter-Efficient Language Modeling for Speech Processing" at the Automatic Speech Recognition and Understanding Workshop (ASRU) and Interspeech 2023, and conducted a series of tutorials at the IEEE International Conference on Acoustics, Speech, and Signal Processing (ICASSP) on "Prompt Learning for Speech-Language Models" from 2022 to 2024. Dr. Yang has also served as an area chair at Interspeech, the IEEE Workshop on Spoken Language Technology (SLT), and ICASSP, and previously held a full-time position at Amazon AGI.  Dr. Andreas Stolcke, a leading AI Scientist and Vice President at Uniphore, obtained his Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of California, Berkeley. His professional tenure includes positions at Amazon, SRI International, and Microsoft. Dr. Stolcke's research encompasses language modeling, speech recognition, speaker identification, and speech translation. He is recognized as a Fellow of both the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) and the International Speech Communication Association (ISCA). His longstanding research interests lie in language modeling for speech processing, paralinguistics, and conversational artificial intelligence.

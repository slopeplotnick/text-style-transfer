The study investigates the application of GRAPHECTORY in autonomous systems, with a particular focus on two programming frameworks, SWE-agent and OpenHands. These systems operate through defined processes such as planning, debugging, code modification, and validation of fixes, all of which are measurable. The evaluation was conducted using four language models (DeepSeek-V3, DeepSeek-R1, Devstral-small-2505, and Claude Sonnet 4) to address real-world tasks derived from 12 GitHub repositories within the SWE-Bench Verified benchmark. The findings indicate that system failures are associated with longer processing times and increased inefficiencies. Specifically, the GRAPHECTORY representations of failed attempts are larger and exhibit more repetitive steps compared to successful executions. More complex problems intensified system effort, as evidenced by an increase in exploration, strategy shifts, and iterative adjustments to maintain progress. Despite adhering to an overarching plan, the systems often adapted their methods dynamically, particularly when addressing challenging issues, which led to additional time spent on debugging and problem-solving. Even in cases of success, their operational efficiency remained suboptimal, underscoring the need for a deeper focus on system processes rather than binary success metrics. This research provides a detailed framework for GRAPHECTORY, employing it to develop methodologies for evaluating and analyzing the behavior of autonomous systems. To enhance the accessibility of GRAPHECTORY’s insights, the study also introduces LANGUTORY, a simplified framework that abstracts the detailed information into a more comprehensible format. While GRAPHECTORY offers extensive information about system operations, its granularity may complicate cross-system or cross-problem analysis. LANGUTORY addresses this limitation by providing a concise summary of the operational data.

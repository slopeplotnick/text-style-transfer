The study involved the recruitment of 17 participants, who were sourced through school poster campaigns and direct solicitation from individuals in public areas. The research protocol received approval from the Institutional Review Board, ensuring ethical compliance, particularly regarding participants under the age of majority, for whom parental consent was obtained. Data confidentiality was rigorously maintained throughout the study, with all interview materials securely handled to prevent the use of personal identifiers. The majority of participants self-reported proficiency in short story composition, with an average skill rating of 3.8 out of 5. All participants had prior experience in storywriting, either manually or through digital platforms. The cohort was divided into subgroups of approximately four individuals, each overseen by a staff member. The duration of the workshop was two and a half hours. During the workshop, participants were encouraged to engage in verbal articulation of their thought processes, facilitating insight into both initial and revised conceptualizations. Post-workshop, four participants were selected for further interviews. Analysis of the workshop recordings and interview data led to the identification of four distinct interaction modalities within the story creation process.   The workshop commenced with a 15-minute introductory session, during which the staff elucidated the objectives and methodology of the workshop, employing examples to illustrate the four principal components of story development. Participants were prompted to consistently verbalize their decision-making processes. This was followed by a 60-minute group discussion, wherein staff distributed thematic cards to each group, tasked with collaboratively constructing a narrative incorporating the key components, whilst articulating their cognitive reflections and selections. Staff provided intermittent guidance as necessary. Subsequently, there was a 45-minute segment dedicated to the presentation of narratives by participants, during which feedback was offered by peers and staff, with particular emphasis on creativity and narrative coherence. Finally, four volunteers participated in 30-minute informal interviews to discuss their experiences of creative storytelling.

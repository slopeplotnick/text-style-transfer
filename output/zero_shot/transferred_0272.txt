The analyses were repeated using the ATTARI subscales, revealing that only the behavioral subscale served as a significant predictor, while the affective and cognitive subscales did not exhibit any significant influence when considered collectively (refer to Supplementary Materials S3). This finding suggests that individuals' attitudes toward artificial intelligence (AI) are predominantly shaped by their willingness to engage with AI, rather than by their general thoughts or emotions regarding it. To complement the quantitative data and obtain insights into public perceptions, we analyzed 178 open-ended responses, organizing them into thematic categories as presented in Table 2, with comprehensive details provided in the Supplementary Materials. Respondents commented on aspects such as the quality of AI-generated music, its emotional resonance, and its perceived humanness. Many respondents assessed AI music based on its technical and musical sound quality, originality, and emotional impact. The theme of humanness was most prominent, extending beyond the auditory characteristics of the music. Some participants emphasized the importance of the human element, such as the artist's personality or the narrative behind a composition, and expressed the view that AI-generated music lacked soul and authenticity. Conversely, some respondents were indifferent to the source of the music, as reflected in statements like, "If it sounds good, then I don’t mind if it was artificially generated" (ID926). These responses further indicated that attitudes toward AI significantly influence judgments of AI-generated music. Numerous comments reflected personal opinions on AI in general, without directly addressing the quality of the music, as illustrated by remarks such as, "I don’t like anything to do with AI-generated music" (ID914). Beyond these considerations, the responses highlighted additional perspectives on AI music that are not typically addressed in experimental studies. Notably, respondents evaluated AI music based on ethical concerns, such as its potential to displace musicians, financially harm artists, or utilize training data without consent ("is built upon data without the consent of the artists that have dedicated decades of their lives to the craft," ID615). One respondent articulated this sentiment, stating, "I think it undermines and abuses artists" (ID446), thus underscoring the importance of evaluating AI music not only in terms of its acoustic properties but also its fairness in creation and distribution.

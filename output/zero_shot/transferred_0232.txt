This paper examines the acquisition of various language categories by a computational system employing an adult language model designated as Delilah alongside a daughter agent. The daughter model is specifically crafted for the MODOMA framework and utilizes graphical representations to depict grammatical knowledge, facilitating sentence construction and comprehension, both of which improve as language acquisition progresses. Delilah incorporates parsing and generating mechanisms developed by Cremers and Hijzelendoorn to operate within the confines of Dutch grammar, thereby enabling both sentence formulation and comprehension. Consequently, MODOMA necessitates a novel approach for the evaluation of Delilah: in addition to enhancing Delilah's proficiency in sentence construction and comprehension, it is imperative to investigate Delilah’s output to assist the daughter model in identifying learning patterns. To achieve this objective, methodologies and theories from linguistics and other related disciplines can be employed to interpret machine-generated language similarly to human language. With the advent of comprehensive language models, it becomes crucial to ascertain whether machine language parallels human language. This paper presents empirical data from experiments pertinent to this inquiry. The sentences generated by Delilah during interactions with the daughter agent are isolated instances, whereas corpora typically consist of interconnected sentences. Nonetheless, analogous grammatical patterns emerge in both data sets, suggesting that such patterns may pertain more to individual sentences than to the entire corpora. The study further reveals that distinctive frequency patterns of language categories, characteristic of human linguistic data, are also present in Delilah's output. Notably, Delilah is not intentionally programmed to replicate these patterns, indicating that these patterns originate from the underlying grammatical model employed.

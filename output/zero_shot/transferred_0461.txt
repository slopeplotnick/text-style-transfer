Future research is necessitated by the limitations of our current study, as insufficient resources prevented us from investigating the mechanisms by which beliefs become entrenched in patterns of reinforced reasoning. This phenomenon is increasingly relevant for reasoning within large language models (LLMs). Our findings demonstrated that deviations from ideal Bayesian rationality can occur when individuals misinterpret their own reasoning or external evidence. However, our analysis was confined to the former aspect. Subsequent studies could explore the extent to which LLMs update their beliefs when exposed to new external evidence, rather than persisting in their prior convictions. A significant methodological challenge in this endeavor would involve ensuring that the process of evidence gathering does not grant LLMs direct access to factual information, if such facts are available.   In domains where definitive truths are known, such as predictive forecasting, we demonstrated that belief entrenchment negatively impacts accuracy. Future research could extend this line of inquiry by evaluating the applicability of the Martingale Score in open-ended contexts, specifically by examining its efficacy in capturing other consequences of belief entrenchment. To validate the utility of the Martingale Score as a tool for assessing reasoning quality, it could be applied to phenomena such as sycophancy, group conformity, and inverse scaling. Additionally, the Martingale Score could be incorporated into training protocols to evaluate its robustness and practical utility. For instance, if training with a martingale objective reduces both Martingale Scores and Brier Scores, such findings would offer strong evidence of the score’s effectiveness as a measure of reasoning quality and its applicability beyond the forecasting domain.  Moreover, future investigations could consider employing the Martingale Score to quantitatively assess and mitigate belief entrenchment or polarization in human-AI systems, such as recommender systems. By extending the application of the score in these contexts, researchers may uncover additional insights into its broader implications and its potential to enhance decision-making systems.

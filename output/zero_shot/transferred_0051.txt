This paper examines the methodologies for assessing the logical consistency and ethical soundness of AI models' responses. It introduces a novel framework, known as MoCoP, which evaluates the ethics of AI without reliance on predefined datasets. MoCoP autonomously generates, tests, and refines examples of moral reasoning, allowing for independent improvement and adaptation. Furthermore, it quantifies the evolution of AI models' moral reasoning over time and contrasts different models to assess consistency in ethical decision-making. The system integrates tools that analyze language, semantics, and reasoning to systematically monitor the ethical handling by AI models over extended periods.  The structure of the paper is as follows: Section 2 reviews existing literature on AI ethics evaluation; Section 3 details the operational framework of MoCoP; Section 5 presents empirical results; Section 6 provides an analysis of findings; Section 7 discusses potential methodological limitations; and Section 8 addresses the scope and future directions of the research. Prior research predominantly concentrated on identifying and mitigating bias or harmful content in AI systems. For instance, the OpenEthics initiative sought to enhance transparency and accountability in AI ethics, while other studies focused on promoting fairness and reducing bias against particular demographic groups. These endeavors contributed to greater transparency in AI systems; however, they were constrained by reliance on static datasets and prompts, limiting adaptability to evolving contexts. Consequently, these approaches could only provide insights into AI models' ethical reasoning at specific points in time, without tracking changes or assessing stability across varying scenarios. Recent research efforts have expanded beyond fairness considerations to explore the processes by which AI models formulate moral and ethical judgments.

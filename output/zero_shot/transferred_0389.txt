Moreover, the adoption of Software Product Line (SPL) methodologies facilitates the creation of innovative products tailored for specific market segments. Such methodologies can serve as exemplars to motivate teams to implement SPL strategies. Mitigating risks is crucial to minimizing resistance to change. This paper discusses several strategies to alleviate these risks. It is essential to address all concerns, whether directly related to these risks or not, as they can impede successful adoption. Certain recurring challenges emerge in the implementation of SPL, yet these are not insurmountable if the transition is managed with these challenges in mind. The perception of complexity often arises from misunderstandings of Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE). However, SPL presents formidable challenges even for those familiar with SPLE. While complexity is inherent to SPL, it should not exceed the complexity of the codebase with which the IT team is already familiar. In SPL, aspects of variability are clearly delineated through models to facilitate comprehension and management of complexity. We posit that the necessity for training and demonstration is linked to this perception. Therefore, it has been articulated that SPL optimizes the process of integrating new features and fosters creative development. By elucidating this, we avert potential losses in creative time, as suggested by user experience (UX) experts, and in new development opportunities. Potential side effects can be mitigated through comprehensive non-regression testing. Concerns can be addressed by dedicating time to listen to team members' apprehensions and discussing both weaknesses and strengths. Additionally, these concerns are perceived as biases by interviewees. The perception of SPL as a system that is excessively challenging to use and modify, thereby rendering the new approach ineffective, is acknowledged. Trust can be reestablished by ensuring transparency in SPL processes, providing thorough explanations and detailed documentation, and involving all project stakeholders. Inclusion begins by inviting all parties to participate in the transition. It is vital to encourage regular feedback at every stage, ensuring that individuals feel heard and understood, and can seek assistance when necessary.

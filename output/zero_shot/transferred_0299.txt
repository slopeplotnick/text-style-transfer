The present study employs OmniFusion technology to facilitate live translations by extracting pertinent lecture slides from audio clips. We are simultaneously developing an open-source framework for image translation, enabling future efforts in translating entire images or slides. This research demonstrates how the integration of images can enhance language processing tasks across various language models. We evaluate several Optical Character Recognition (OCR) models to assess their efficacy in translating images under both single-mode and multi-mode conditions. Our objective is to render lectures accessible in multiple languages and formats by devising systems that translate both audio and slide content, utilizing these translations for diverse language-related tasks. Furthermore, our methodology involves transforming slide text and images into different languages while preserving the original format. We have developed a PDF viewer that aligns slides with captions corresponding to the speaker's presentation, facilitating users in following the translations. This mechanism is pivotal for associating slides with corresponding audio segments, thereby providing essential context for translation processes. We expound upon the methodology that links slide images with audio clips and the application of these translations to other tasks. Additionally, we discuss the procedures for identifying, translating, and reconfiguring text embedded within slide images. Further technical details regarding Text-to-Speech functionalities are provided in Appendix A.1. Although existing systems can translate into numerous languages, they are not optimally suited for live lecture environments, as they are generally designed for offline application and require predefined cues to initiate translation. Current systems either neglect slide integration or lack multi-language handling capabilities. Effective lecture translation necessitates the incorporation of both features.

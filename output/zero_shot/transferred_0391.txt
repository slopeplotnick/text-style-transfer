To optimize the performance of multilingual models across various tasks, we leverage the natural interconnections between languages to facilitate effective information sharing. In models such as XLM-RoBERTa, languages that exhibit similarities in meaning, structure, or orthography tend to cluster closely within the model's representational space. Our approach builds upon the 100-language configuration of the XLM-R model, which encompasses a diverse range of both widely spoken and low-resource languages. Rather than requiring extensive training data for every individual language, we strategically select a subset of widely spoken languages for model training. This enables the transfer of learned knowledge to other languages within the model, thereby mitigating the need for direct training on low-resource languages.  To further extend this approach, we translated the MultiJail dataset into all 100 languages using state-of-the-art machine translation systems tailored to the linguistic characteristics of each language. For high-resource languages, we utilized advanced models such as GPT-4o, M2MBart-50, and Helsinki-NLP OpusMT. However, for low-resource languages where these systems often underperform, we employed SarvamTranslate for Indic languages, which demonstrates strong performance even for rare languages, and GPT-4.1 for other less common languages. To ensure translation accuracy, sample translations—particularly for low-resource languages—were cross-verified using Google Translate. By integrating these translation models, we achieved high-quality, consistent, and accurate translations, thereby enhancing the overall utility of the dataset for multilingual modeling.

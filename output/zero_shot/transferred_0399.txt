This study investigates the detection of fraudulent activities in journal entry data without relying on labeled examples, which are often scarce and not readily available. Conventional methods that depend on labeled data are unsuitable for this task; therefore, we focus on identifying anomalies within the data to detect irregular entries. A synthetic dataset, developed by Gronewald et al. in 2024, simulates the bookkeeping processes of a medium-sized enterprise, featuring attributes such as unique transaction IDs, posting and transaction timestamps, credit or debit indicators, tax rates, and account IDs. Additionally, our approach is applied to real, anonymized accounting data provided by a business partner. The absence of conventional links between debit and credit entries in this anonymized dataset necessitates a transaction-level fraud detection approach. To compensate for missing details, we incorporate bookkeeping principles, guidelines, and summary statistics into the prompts of a large language model (LLM), enhancing its performance beyond that of traditional machine learning techniques. Due to the sensitive nature of accounting data, open-source LLMs, such as LLaMa AI (2025) and Gemma Team (2024), are employed for testing, in lieu of proprietary models like OpenAI's GPT-4 (2024). Our approach demonstrates superior performance compared to baseline methods on both the synthetic dataset from Gronewald et al. (2024) and the real anonymized dataset. The baseline method utilized is Isolation Forest, introduced by Liu et al. in 2008, known for its efficacy with tabular data and its design for unsupervised anomaly detection, aligning with the objectives of this research.

The expansion and increased complexity of large language models necessitate substantial memory and computational resources during both training and deployment. To address this challenge, quantization techniques have been employed, which involve representing data with fewer bits and substituting costly floating-point operations with more efficient integer arithmetic. There are two primary approaches to quantization: post-training quantization (PTQ) and quantization-aware training (QAT).   Post-training quantization is straightforward to implement and does not require retraining of the model; however, its efficacy diminishes significantly when employing extremely low bit-widths, such as 1 or 2 bits. This reduction in accuracy can be attributed to several factors, including insufficient calibration data, inadequate scaling methods, and the influence of certain channels that disrupt the range management.   Conversely, quantization-aware training incorporates the quantization process into the model's training phase, allowing the model to adapt to quantization. This approach demonstrates superior performance compared to PTQ under low bit-width scenarios. Nonetheless, it is time-intensive and may exhibit instability due to its limited flexibility in managing the data.  In our study, we applied the FAIRY2 methodology to well-known open-source language models, notably the LLaMA series. Our methodology involved several key innovations: transforming any model layer into an alternative form without altering its pre-quantization functionality, continuing model training at very low bit levels using a specially designed quantizer while preserving existing checkpoints, and reducing quantization errors through the application of consistent methods to residual data, thereby significantly minimizing error propagation. Our results indicate that this approach is effective in maintaining model performance under stringent quantization conditions.

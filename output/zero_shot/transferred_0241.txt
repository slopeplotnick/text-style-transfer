Humans demonstrate an exceptional capacity for rapidly acquiring new tasks when provided with clear and structured instructions, such as verbal guidance or diagrammatic representations, often without necessitating extensive practice. Recent investigations have examined the parallels between neural and artificial intelligence (AI) systems, specifically in the domains of perception, cognition, and motor functions, by deconstructing these processes into three fundamental components: circuit architecture, input-output processing, and learning mechanisms. These studies have revealed that contemporary AI models are increasingly mirroring neural processes, providing valuable insights into brain function. Notably, the neocortex and cerebellum leverage historical data to anticipate future events, striving for precision by formulating compact yet robust models of the external world. These models facilitate three primary functions: forecasting future occurrences, elucidating the environment through abstract concepts, including features and causal relationships (such as object recognition or textual comprehension), and generating outputs like actions, plans, or language. This method of information processing enables the neocortex and cerebellum to perform diverse tasks despite their relatively uniform structures and functions. In essence, such processing represents a shared operational paradigm that underpins both neural and AI capabilities. Advancing our understanding of this paradigm holds the potential to enhance both neuroscience and AI, offering deeper insights into the mechanisms of intelligence. It is noteworthy that the human brain surpasses large language models in terms of learning speed and efficiency, requiring significantly fewer examples to achieve proficiency. Although recent AI paradigms have improved in learning efficacy by emulating neural strategies, such as Mixture-of-Experts techniques, they remain inferior to the human brain. As AI continues to evolve by drawing inspiration from neural processes, the field of neuroscience is poised to gain novel insights through the study of AI advancements.

During the training phase, an alternative method was employed to address the difficulties encountered by the base model in adhering to instructions when presented with extensive prompts. To mitigate this issue, a preliminary warm-up stage involving supervised fine-tuning was introduced. The procedure is delineated as follows: initially, the Challenger role engages in few-shot grounded generation and subsequently computes a reward. Concurrently, the Solver role performs online curriculum learning, calculates its reward, and proceeds with updates. This approach ensures that the Solver concentrates on challenges that are demanding yet feasible, aligning with established learning theories. The Solver's reward function incorporates both synthetic and authentic data, modulated by the complexity of the curriculum. The R-FeW framework retains the iterative process of R-ZERO, but enhances it with a data configuration that is both grounded and attuned to the curriculum. The Challenger constructs tasks using few-shot exemplars derived from human input, which the Solver attempts to resolve, evaluating the frequency of successful outcomes. An online filter is employed to select tasks of moderate difficulty from both synthetic and real data sources.  For experimentation, we utilized the Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base models as our primary models. Comparative analyses were conducted against several baseline models, which include: (1) Base Model — the original model devoid of supplementary training, available for download; (2) R-Zero — the initial self-play approach where the model autonomously generates queries and solutions to refine its capabilities; (3) Absolute Zero — a self-play configuration constrained to code generation tasks validated through Python verification; (4) SPICE — a self-play arrangement employing extensive documents to formulate reasoning tasks with responses based on the content of the documents. Baselines (1)-(2) were recreated using accessible setups consistent with the same training protocols, whereas results for baselines (3)-(4) were sourced from the studies conducted by Liu et al.

The experimental group employing the curriculum learning method demonstrates faster learning and more consistent improvements in terms of rewards and model performance, as illustrated in Figure 12. During reinforcement learning (RL) training, as the model approaches its objective, its outputs become progressively less stochastic and more deterministic. While this predictability facilitates convergence, it also diminishes the model’s capacity to explore novel strategies and identify optimal solutions, thereby constraining its potential for improvement. To address this limitation, various techniques were tested to decelerate the model’s transition to predictability, thereby preserving its exploratory capabilities. A comparative analysis of these methods is presented in Figure 13.  Among the tested approaches, the Clip-higher method demonstrated superior performance by effectively moderating the rate at which the model becomes predictable. This approach achieves an optimal balance between exploration and exploitation while maintaining stable training dynamics, resulting in substantial improvements in model performance. In contrast, the On-policy method sustains a steady level of randomness throughout training, thereby enhancing exploration. However, this approach delays convergence, leading to less pronounced performance gains. The Off-policy + Entropy Loss method, on the other hand, exhibited issues related to unstable training dynamics. Although it initially slows the reduction of randomness, the increasing magnitude of the entropy penalty at later stages of training overrides the optimization process, causing the model to exhibit erratic behavior and ultimately leading to training failure. Furthermore, this method requires extensive hyperparameter tuning, which increases computational overhead and operational complexity.  In conclusion, the Clip-higher method emerges as the most effective approach, as it strikes an optimal balance between exploration and efficient learning by regulating the rate at which the model transitions to a more predictable state.

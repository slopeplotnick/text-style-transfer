Unlike image-based IC-LoRA methodologies, which require separate training for each specific editing task, the proposed Sync-LoRA module addresses a diverse range of edits through a single framework. As demonstrated in our experiments (refer to Section 4), Sync-LoRA effectively maintains high image quality while ensuring temporal consistency in motion across video frames. In the context of diffusion models, in-context learning (ICL) enables these models to adapt to new tasks by leveraging structured input examples instead of explicit task-specific instructions. Although extensively studied in the domain of language models, the application of ICL to image generation using diffusion models has gained attention more recently. Within this paradigm, the model generates a set of visually related images, which proves advantageous for applications such as storyboard creation, where maintaining consistency across images is critical. When guided by a reference image, ICL approaches can perform a variety of image-to-image tasks, including personalization and image editing.  The most relevant prior work is IC-LoRA, which demonstrated that transformer-based diffusion models possess an inherent ability to generate panels with shared visual features. This was enhanced by employing low-rank adapter (LoRA) fine-tuning on consistent image panels and leveraging task-specific prompts. IC-LoRA has been successfully applied to diverse use cases, such as storyboard generation, font design, and interior decoration. Extending this line of research, we introduce a video-specific adaptation of IC-LoRA designed for video editing, which enforces frame-by-frame synchronization of facial features within video sequences.  Recent studies have also revealed that large-scale video diffusion transformers are naturally capable of exhibiting in-context learning behavior, which allows them to produce coherent video clips in a single inference process. However, such studies have primarily focused on scene control using inpainting techniques, with limited emphasis on ensuring temporal consistency across video frames. Building on this foundation, PoseGen introduced in-context LoRA fine-tuning for generating human motion videos, enabling the creation of long, smooth motion sequences from a reference frame with controllable poses. Our proposed method advances this approach by explicitly training the in-context behavior of diffusion models to prioritize synchronized editing, thereby ensuring temporal alignment and consistency across video frames.

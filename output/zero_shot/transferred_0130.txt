The system components operate in a cohesive manner through a unified configuration, allowing for easy interchangeability without disrupting the overall functionality. While each component can be individually configured, a centralized configuration mechanism, known as ExperimentConfig, facilitates the management of all settings collectively. The component responsible for leveraging large language models (LLMs) is designed to retrieve responses from such models. Its primary configuration supports parallel processing and monitors token usage. This component comprises three variants: APILLM, which facilitates access to models via an API, such as those provided by OpenAI or Anthropic, with setup guidance available through the DeepInfra API; LocalLLM, intended for utilizing models locally on personal computing devices with transformers; and VLLM, which employs the v11m library to efficiently manage a high volume of requests and is optimized for GPU compatibility.  The Predictor component is tasked with extracting accurate responses from the model’s output. For classification tasks, it selects the predicted category, while for mathematical problems, it extracts the final solution. One type of predictor identifies and selects the first occurrence of a potential answer, whereas a more reliable method involves identifying answers demarcated by specific tags. For instance, in determining the total number of fish across multiple fishbowls, a stepwise addition is performed, such as adding 62 fish to 3 fish to arrive at a total of 65 fish.  The Task component is responsible for storing data and additional information pertinent to the tasks at hand. It also governs the evaluation of prompts, ensuring that exhaustive testing is avoided when unnecessary, thereby conserving time and resources. This component is crucial for achieving optimal results efficiently. Various tasks pertinent to natural language processing have been configured within the system to enhance its applicability and performance.

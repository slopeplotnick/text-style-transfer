In this study, we adopt a training methodology analogous to the earlier Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) process, incorporating reasoning examples generated during training. In addition to the tokens produced by the model, we occasionally substitute certain sentences with tokens, as done in previous iterations, to enhance the model's proficiency in efficient reasoning. During deployment, the model predominantly operates in a standard mode; however, it transitions to an alternative reasoning mode upon encountering specific tags, as illustrated in Figure 1. These tags delineate the distinction between the two modes but are not specialized tokens within the model's lexicon. Rather than explicitly training the model to utilize these tags, we employ prompting techniques to achieve this. The model autonomously determines the number of tokens to generate by estimating a numerical value. Initially, these tags are infrequently utilized, but their prevalence increases as training progresses. We discuss the original configurations of these tasks and the training of a foundational reasoning model, which lacks the specialized reasoning mode, to address them. Both tasks necessitate complex, story-based reasoning, diverging from conventional tasks in this domain.

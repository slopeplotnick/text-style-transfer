In this study, we utilized a two-step methodology to train the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model by integrating various types of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data. This model is comparable in size to the large language model (LLM) base model employed in our investigation. A comparative analysis of the performance of models based on vision-language models (VLMs) and LLMs revealed that the LLM-based approach outperformed its VLM counterpart on both simple and complex datasets, particularly in terms of macro-F1 scores. These findings suggest that when equipped with equivalent CoT reasoning capabilities, LLMs demonstrate superior reasoning and knowledge-based performance compared to VLMs. Conversely, VLMs excel in the processing of visual information, as they do not rely on converting images into text representations, a process that can result in information loss.  Our proposed LORE model has undergone iterative enhancements across three development phases, achieving significant advancements in five key domains: objectives, model architecture, data and prompt design, evaluation methodologies, and practical applications. These iterative improvements have culminated in a model design optimized for specific use cases, particularly the enhancement of search result relevance in online shopping contexts. Initially, one of the primary challenges involved accurately interpreting e-commerce-related queries, especially in cases involving non-standardized product names. To address this issue, we constructed extensive, high-quality e-commerce-specific datasets and employed In-Context Learning (ICL) to incorporate domain-specific knowledge into the model. These efforts significantly improved the model’s capacity to comprehend the nuanced language of e-commerce, thereby enhancing its overall performance in this domain.

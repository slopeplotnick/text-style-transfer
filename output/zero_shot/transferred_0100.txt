Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have predominantly focused on leveraging increased computational resources during the testing phase. Models such as OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-R1, for instance, employ extended cognitive processing steps wherein they systematically contemplate ideas, formulate hypotheses, and rectify errors before arriving at a definitive conclusion. This methodology of enhancing reasoning during testing has gained traction, given its potential to augment accuracy and improve problem-solving capabilities. However, merely elongating the reasoning process does not invariably yield beneficial results and may sometimes be detrimental, particularly in scenarios where over-analysis occurs. Empirical evidence indicates that optimal solutions frequently emerge from more concise reasoning steps. An alternative strategy involves executing multiple concurrent reasoning processes and synthesizing their outputs, typically by selecting the modal answer when a distinct solution is available. This tactic proves efficacious for tasks such as mathematical computations or multiple-choice questions. Nonetheless, numerous real-world applications lack a singular correct answer. For instance, coding utilities must generate functional programs, and research instruments often necessitate the integration of other tools, the formulation of comprehensive strategies, or the provision of elaborate explanations. In these instances, selecting the modal answer is inadequate due to the absence of a definitive solution, despite the method's efficacy in simpler contexts. Consequently, a pivotal inquiry arises: Is it feasible to harness the advantages of concurrent reasoning processes for tasks devoid of a singular correct answer, without relying on the selection of the most frequent result?

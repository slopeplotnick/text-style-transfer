Question Formulation. Following the framework outlined in Section 2.1, a group of eight graduate students with expertise in various disciplines selected specific categories and devised 20 prompts each. These prompts were designed to require the integration of at least two scientific concepts, thereby presenting a significant intellectual challenge. The prompts were derived from examples of publicly available educational experiments to ensure scientific validity and to facilitate demonstrations in videos of ten seconds or less. Additionally, two of these graduate students developed 40 Image-to-Video (I2V) questions, each accompanied by a video depicting the appropriate experimental execution. These videos were sourced from public datasets and online repositories, with a key initial frame used as a representative image to illustrate the complete experimental setup.  Verification and Review. Upon completion of the test set, all participating graduate students assembled to conduct a thorough review of the content. Each student evaluated their peers' 20 prompts to ensure specificity, procedural accuracy, and clarity in the intended demonstrations. For the final quality assessment, video-generating models, specifically Sora-2 and Wan-2.5-T2V-Preview, were employed to produce the initial sequences of each prompt, ensuring that essential aspects of the experiments were effectively illustrated.  Utilization of Vision-Language Models (VLM) as Evaluators. Contemporary research posits that advanced VLMs, when appropriately prompted and configured, can offer evaluations comparable to those made by humans. Within the framework of the VideoScience-Bench, we employed a VLM-as-a-Judge approach to assess the differential performance between VLMs with reasoning capabilities and those without, such as GPT-4o. Our observations indicated that VLMs, particularly those lacking reasoning capabilities, frequently fail to substantiate their evaluations and are prone to producing false positives. Consequently, we devised a quantitative auto-evaluation system employing a checklist grading method, whereby each score is substantiated by key video frames and a comprehensive table of evidence derived from video analysis.

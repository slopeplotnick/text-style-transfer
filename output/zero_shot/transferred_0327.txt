Following the training phase, a rigorous evaluation assessment was designed to ascertain the model's performance, as detailed in Section 3.5. Given the substantial capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the diverse nature of e-commerce data, the evaluation encapsulated a comprehensive array of examples, including complex instances that align with the core competencies discussed in Section 2. The preliminary procedures involved three primary stages: feature construction, model selection, and prompt refinement (refer to Figure 6).   In accordance with the concepts introduced in Section 2, the construction of effective relevance models necessitates a thorough delineation of product attributes. Consequently, features were selected to enable the model to comprehend and rationalize using both visual and textual data. Initially, the primary product image was utilized to capture visual elements such as style, cuts, and patterns; these aspects are crucial for searches where textual information proves insufficient. To enhance the representation of product details, category-property-value (CPV) triples and Stock-Keeping Unit (SKU) identifiers were also employed. Recognizing that CPV data may contain extraneous elements irrelevant to relevance assessment, a process was implemented that integrates prior knowledge with LLMs to refine this data. CPV information is presented as key-value pairs, and similar products frequently share common keys— for example, both "eye cream" and "face cream" typically include the key "brand".   An extensive analysis of product data was conducted to identify frequently occurring keys. Subsequently, the advanced LLM, Qwen3-235B-Instruct, was employed to evaluate whether each attribute contributed to relevance, utilizing the task definition, the attributes, and each key. To ensure the reliability of the evaluations, the process was repeated eight times, and only those attributes consistently deemed relevant by the model were retained.

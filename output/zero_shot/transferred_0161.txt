In recent years, advancements in technology pertaining to Large Language Models (LLMs) have significantly improved, leading to their application across various domains, including intelligent interactions and assistance in scientific research. In the healthcare sector, LLMs have demonstrated considerable potential in aiding disease diagnosis and enhancing diagnostic and therapeutic procedures. Within the realm of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), the foundational concept is referred to as "Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment" (SDT). This principle involves a comprehensive evaluation of diverse information to ascertain the underlying condition and subsequently develop a personalized treatment plan, even for similar illnesses, based on the specific circumstances.   Despite the existence of TCM-focused LLMs, such as Zhongjing and HuatuoGPT, their practical application in clinical settings remains challenging due to the intricate and individualized nature of TCM. A significant obstacle is the lack of robust methodologies to thoroughly assess the efficacy of these models in clinical decision-making and the absence of specific tools to evaluate their reasoning processes. Consequently, there is a pressing need among researchers and industry stakeholders for a comprehensive dataset that can accurately and extensively evaluate the suitability of these models for TCM applications. Such a dataset would facilitate model enhancement, performance assessment, and ultimately enable their deployment in real medical scenarios.  However, the development of such a dataset is fraught with challenges due to the inherently holistic and individualized approach of TCM, which necessitates consideration of a wide range of factors. For instance, two patients with identical illnesses may exhibit different symptoms due to factors such as body type, disease progression, or lifestyle, complicating the establishment of standardized evaluation criteria. TCM diagnosis is inherently a holistic process that employs diverse methodologies to gather information, identify causative factors, and determine treatment regimens, thereby requiring the dataset to encompass a broad spectrum of scenarios. Additionally, patients frequently present with multiple concurrent issues, and the variability in diagnostic approaches may introduce subjectivity into evaluation outcomes. These complexities underscore the formidable task of creating a comprehensive dataset to effectively assess the performance of TCM-focused LLMs in real-world medical contexts.

Numerous studies have investigated more systematic approaches to the construction of prompts. For instance, the Plan-and-Solve model proposed by Wang et al. (2023c) delineates a task into two distinct phases—planning and solving—which has demonstrated enhanced efficacy particularly in mathematical and logical problem-solving contexts. Furthermore, Chen et al. (2023) introduced the concept of Prompt Chaining, wherein the association of summaries or intermediary steps is evaluated for its effectiveness in the summarization of text. Similarly, Fu et al. (2023) put forth the Complexity-based Prompting approach, advocating that the intricacy of a task should inform the structuring of prompts, akin to the investigation of specificity in prompts. Whereas the majority of research traditionally emphasizes the format of tasks or examples utilized, our focus shifts toward the granularity and organization of prompts, considering it as a variable factor.   Recent advancements have introduced methodologies for prompt refinement, achieved either automatically or with human intervention. For example, Diao et al. (2023) employed black-box optimization techniques where prompts are adjusted based on feedback, obviating the necessity for detailed model examination. Zhou et al. (2023) demonstrated that large language models can be prompted to enhance their own prompts—a self-improvement strategy analogous to a generalization loop. Another notable study by Lu et al. (2023) leveraged visual features in Prompt Refinement with Image Pivots to modify text prompts for image generation tasks. Despite focusing exclusively on text-based prompts, our objective aligns with optimizing prompts to improve results.   Contrary to these preceding studies, DETAIL specifically examines the impact of textual specificity on the reasoning capabilities of models, without delving into semantic alterations or the integration of text with other data modalities. Reviews such as that by Liu et al. (2023) have comprehensively analyzed various prompting methodologies for reasoning, illustrating the diversity of prompts and strategies available. Nonetheless, there exists a paucity of research exploring how the level of detail in prompts influences model performance across various tasks and systems. Although methodologies such as CoT and Plan-and-Solve underscore the significance of prompt content, they do not extensively address this aspect.

In our study of GraphMatch, we observed that the integration of both random and challenging negative samples is crucial for optimal performance. When GraphMatch is trained exclusively with random negative samples, its performance is comparable to that of TextMatch-small. We evaluated various node features for GraphMatch: the "no-feat" variant utilizes only TextMatch node embeddings, the "no-text" variant employs numerical node features without text embeddings, and the "full" variant integrates both TextMatch node embeddings and numerical node features. Notably, GraphMatch-full, when trained with challenging negatives, demonstrated superior performance. Conversely, GraphMatch-no-feat achieved performance levels similar to TextMatch-large, despite being a significantly smaller model. Our findings underscore the importance of textual context for accurate predictions, as evidenced by the low accuracy of GraphMatch-no-text. Accurate temporal data sampling, along with the selection of appropriate subgraphs and node features at relevant times, is essential for training a model adept at handling novel nodes. To investigate this, we implemented two training approaches for GraphMatch. The "no-temporal-nodes" version excludes time-specific node features during training, always utilizing the latest feature version in the dataset. The "no-temporal-graph" version similarly omits time-specific subgraphs, yet has access to all graph edges during training.

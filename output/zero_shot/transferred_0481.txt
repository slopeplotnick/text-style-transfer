In order to comprehend the trends observed in the accuracy and false positive rate results, one must consider the verifier gain, as presented in the final row. Verifier gain reflects the efficacy of employing the verifier during rejection sampling—sampling performed until the verifier endorses a solution. This metric facilitates the assessment of verification quality: self-verification yields minimal gains, whilst highly accurate solvers do not autonomously exhibit enhanced performance. There is a slight increase in gains through intra-family verification; however, cross-family verification affords the most significant benefits. The effectiveness of the verifier is evaluated across various model families and sizes. Figure 3 replicates the experiments conducted in Figure 2, yet distinguishes each verifier metric according to model size within each model family. It is observed that verification accuracy and false negative rate improve with increased model size; however, the false positive rate remains less predictable and frequently escalates with model size, as exemplified by intra-family verification within DeepSeek. For advanced post-trained models such as Qwen3 and DeepSeek, verifier gains are most prominent in the cross-family context, less pronounced in the intra-family context, and minimal during self-verification. This observation appears to contradict the findings of Song et al. (2025), which suggest that GV-Gaps in self-verification expand with increased pretraining FLOPs. However, their study focuses on older model families, while Figure 2 also indicates greater verifier gains for older models such as Qwen2.5 and Llama3. The limited gains observed in self-verification and intra-family verification for robust post-trained models like DeepSeek and Qwen3 can be attributed to two factors: firstly, these models may inherently execute self-verification when utilized as solvers, diminishing the utility of an additional verification round; secondly, their distributions are considerably refined through post-training, thereby restricting the potential enhancement achievable through rejection sampling.

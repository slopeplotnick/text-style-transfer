Jack Lu, Ryan Teehan, Jinran Jin, and Mengye Ren are affiliated with the Agentic Learning AI Lab at New York University. They can be contacted via their academic email addresses, and further information is available on the lab's official website. On November 20, 2025, the researchers presented their findings on the dual capacity of large language models to both solve problems and verify solutions. They emphasized the role of verification in selecting optimal responses. Historically, research has concentrated primarily on the self-verification capabilities of these models, with limited exploration of their ability to verify outputs generated by other models. Additionally, while these models undergo extensive fine-tuning subsequent to their initial training, the implications of this process on their verification capabilities remain inadequately understood.  The researchers conducted a comprehensive analysis of 37 models varying in group affiliation and size, encompassing both models with preliminary training and those subjected to additional fine-tuning. Their evaluation encompassed nine distinct problem domains, including logic, puzzles, mathematics, common sense, and factual knowledge. A comparative analysis was performed to assess models' self-verification capabilities against their ability to verify outputs from models within the same group or from different groups. They introduced a novel metric to quantify the enhancement attributable to verification and empirically validated this metric. The study further investigated the correlation of these metrics with model size and the extent of additional training, along with variations in verification efficacy across different problem types.  The findings revealed that inter-group verification is particularly effective; additional fine-tuning enhances a model's ability to verify outputs from other groups but not necessarily its own; and problems involving mathematics and logic are the most amenable to verification. The research suggests a paradigm shift from merely soliciting answers from models to engaging them in both problem-solving and answer verification. Verification at the test stage can vary in complexity, ranging from generating multiple answers followed by selection of the optimal one by a verifier, to iterative refinement of responses. This approach allows models to tackle more complex problems than they would be able to solve independently.

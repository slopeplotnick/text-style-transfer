Numerous systems have been developed to manage memory within the domain of financial tasks. FinMem is one such system that leverages short, mid, and long-term memory to aid in trading decision-making. Another framework, MemP, translates trajectories into step-by-step instructions and overarching strategies through the utilization of procedural memory. Additionally, MemGPT functions analogously to an operating system by managing storage and interruptions, thereby extending the context window for large language models. Zep employs temporal knowledge graphs to integrate past relationships with evolving contexts.   To evaluate the performance of these systems, we employ metrics such as Accuracy (ACC) and the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC). ACC assesses the overall correctness of predictions, whereas MCC is particularly suitable for balanced datasets, which is crucial in stock prediction where class distributions may be uneven. Our empirical analysis, conducted on four different stocks, indicates that our system outperforms existing alternatives in both ACC and MCC metrics, thereby demonstrating its efficacy.  The analysis also revealed several limitations within the existing systems. FinMem lacks the capability to model temporal events, leading to the omission of significant market changes. MemP overly simplifies trajectories, resulting in the loss of critical financial details. MemGPT's generalized focus leads to the overlook of essential financial patterns. Zep fails to adequately connect events, thus missing vital relationships in financial datasets. In contrast, our proposed system addresses these shortcomings by transforming unstructured news data into structured event knowledge. It effectively captures event interconnections, enhancing traceability and reasoning within the data, thereby facilitating improved financial predictions.

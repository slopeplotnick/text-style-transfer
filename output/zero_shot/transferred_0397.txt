Researchers have investigated the development of communication systems involving multiple agents, with a focus on the evolution of language. A notable example is the Talking Heads project, conceived by Steels in 2000 and 2015, which demonstrates the potential for language evolution through agent interaction. Initially devoid of language, these agents engage with their environment and progressively develop an artificial language by participating in referential games, such as naming objects (Steels 2001; see also Steels and Kaplan 2001, Steels and Loetzch 2012 for further discussion on naming conventions). More recent iterations of this project have examined the emergence and acquisition of syntax, or sentence structure (Steels and Beuls 2017, Steels et al. 2018, van Trijp 2016). In contrast to the MODOMA project, the Talking Heads initiative involves both agents collaboratively creating and acquiring the language, whereas MODOMA emphasizes the learning of natural language components by a single agent. Furthermore, the language acquired by the Talking Heads agents is artificial rather than natural.  Alishahi has conducted studies on language acquisition utilizing algorithms applied to datasets that are either human-generated or artificially constructed. These studies (Alishahi and Chrupala 2009, 2012; Alishahi and Stevenson 2008) explore the learning of word categories, meanings, and fundamental sentence structures. Matusevych et al. (2013) have contributed to enhancing artificial inputs that simulate interactions between children and adults, akin to data from the CHILDES database (MacWhinney 2014). However, unlike MODOMA, their language learning algorithm does not actively participate in conversations. Beekhuizen et al. (2014) approached the issue differently by examining the acquisition of grammatical patterns using a single agent that processes sentence representations and their associated meanings. Lastly, Conner et al. (2008, 2009) contributed to the Baby SRL project, which investigates how algorithms can learn to assign roles to words within a sentence using either data from the CHILDES database or fabricated sentences.

In this study, we evaluate the efficacy of the GAIA system in employing tools for real-world tasks by analyzing 103 text-only test cases. The focus of XbenchDeepSearch is to achieve proficient deep information retrieval. The WebSailor agent is configured with specific parameters: a temperature of 0.6, a top-p value of 0.95, and a context length of 32,768. Additionally, we investigate the potential benefits of THINKMERGE in scenarios where the model is required to engage in cognitive processing and tool utilization prior to providing a response.   We conduct assessments on three variants of the WebSailor agents—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B—across four challenging web-based evaluations: BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and XbenchDeepSearch. The BrowseComp-en and BrowseComp-zh tasks focus on the retrieval of difficult-to-access information in English and Chinese, respectively. Due to the extensive range of BrowseComp-en, which comprises 1,266 questions, we randomly select 200 questions to align with the scale of BrowseComp-zh, which includes 289 questions.   GAIA demands proficient tool usage for multi-step tasks, and our approach adheres to previous methodologies by examining 103 text-only instances. XbenchDeepSearch is similarly oriented towards achieving expert-level deep information retrieval, utilizing the WebSailor agent configuration with the aforementioned parameters.   To optimize costs, the evaluator model Qwen2.5-72B is substituted with GPT-4.1, and the Serper API is employed in lieu of the Google Search API. The process of determining a final answer involves averaging next-token predictions across all steps, with the selected token consistently applied across contexts to maintain guidance throughout the process.  THINKMERGE demonstrates effective integration with standard controls and compatibility with modern systems, promoting ease of use in both online and offline environments. For mathematics and science inquiries with definitive answers, this approach performs comparably to or surpasses traditional majority voting methods. Regarding open-ended questions, THINKMERGE enhances the overall success rate across multiple models. These findings indicate that the strategy of averaging token predictions during testing enhances performance on both deterministic and open-ended tasks without necessitating additional training.

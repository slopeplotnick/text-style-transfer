Large language models face significant challenges when processing highly detailed visual information. A recent study sought to address this issue by segmenting images into smaller regions and evaluating the similarity between each segment and a given query using a specialized model. This approach prioritizes relevant segments to identify key objects while disregarding less pertinent areas. However, this segmentation strategy can disrupt the semantic coherence between image regions and the associated query. Experimental results demonstrate that optimal performance varies according to the granularity of segmentation, with different segment sizes being more effective depending on the level of detail required.  To address these challenges without requiring additional model training, the proposed method combines similarity maps derived from multiple levels of granularity. This fusion enables the preservation of object integrity while enhancing the quality of information obtained. Additionally, the method employs a sliding-window mechanism to directly detect object regions across the entire image, further improving accuracy in object identification. Empirical evaluations confirm the efficacy of this approach in processing detailed visual content.  Advancements in large language models have enabled deeper integration of multimodal tasks, facilitating the simultaneous comprehension of both visual and textual data. These models leverage visual signals to extract meaning from complex images, bridging the gap between raw visual data and semantic understanding. However, most existing models operate at a fixed resolution for image processing, introducing significant limitations. Reducing visual detail can distort shapes and blur critical features, ultimately diminishing the fidelity of image representation. Prior research demonstrates that current methodologies are suboptimal for tasks requiring detailed image comprehension, which is reflected in their relatively poor performance on benchmarks specifically designed to assess such capabilities.

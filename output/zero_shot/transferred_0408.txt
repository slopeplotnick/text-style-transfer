The developed system demonstrates robustness and consistency across various tests and configurations, affirming its reliability. This is a critical aspect, as erratic or arbitrary results would impede our understanding of relational interconnections. Instead, the system reveals discernible groupings, stable patterns, and coherent shared characteristics. This indicates that our approach effectively identifies training images aligned with the stylistic or semantic attributes of the outputs, rather than merely their appearance or content. Fundamentally, the data illustrates that our method for the automatic generation of knowledge graphs (KGs) enhances the comprehension of how training data influences model outputs. Such insights are valuable for explaining model behavior, evaluating copyright implications, and improving dataset transparency. For instance, when a model is tasked with generating an image in a particular style, such as "Ghibli-style," it does not replicate specific images from its training set. Rather, it assimilates the general aesthetic and characteristics of the style from a wide array of images. These features are embedded within the model and utilized in new image creation, without being direct replications. Consequently, pinpointing the specific training images that influenced an output is challenging. The influence manifests in a more generalized form, through shared elements like texture, theme, or compositional arrangement. To ascertain the influence of a style on the model, it is essential to examine patterns and underlying meanings, rather than merely matching discrete objects. In our experiment, we employed a previously established method for the automatic construction of KGs. The procedure involved extracting frames from prominent Studio Ghibli films, subsequently describing each frame using a structured system that deconstructs it into discrete informational components. These components were utilized to create a "Ghibli World" KG that encapsulates the essential aspects of the studio’s visual style. Despite utilizing frames from only ten films, the resulting dataset provides a representative portrayal of the Studio Ghibli aesthetic.

Although advancements in memory technologies have been significant, their application to stock price prediction remains challenging. The primary limitations of existing systems include their inability to effectively decompose event sequences in detail, their limited capacity to correlate historical scenarios with novel ones, and their inadequate adaptability to the dynamic nature of financial markets. These limitations underscore the necessity of developing memory systems specifically designed for financial applications. This chapter examines research developments in two central domains: the evolution from pattern-matching approaches to the use of memory systems and the increasing prominence of event-driven prediction methodologies. Despite notable progress in these areas, a critical challenge persists in integrating structured event knowledge with traceable memory systems—an issue this work seeks to address.  Historically, stock price prediction has relied on the identification of patterns in historical data analogous to current conditions. This approach is conceptually aligned with memory systems in large language models (LLMs), which aim to store and retrieve past experiences or contexts. Initial research efforts employed statistical methods, which proved effective in detecting numerical patterns but lacked the capability to process complex market information. The advent of deep learning introduced models such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Transformer architectures, which facilitated the automatic extraction of temporal features. Transformer models, in particular, revolutionized the field with their self-attention mechanisms, enabling the identification of long-term dependencies and driving significant advancements. Furthermore, the introduction of the FinSeer framework incorporated LLMs to identify numerically analogous historical data, thereby enabling more comprehensive feature analysis.

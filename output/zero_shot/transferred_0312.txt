The performance of models varies across different topics. As illustrated in Table 2, models generally exhibit lower performance in the areas of Religion and Sport, with scores ranging from 7.8 to 8.2. In contrast, they perform more effectively in the domains of Game and Lifestyle, frequently achieving scores exceeding 8.4. This discrepancy indicates that models demonstrate increased caution when dealing with sensitive topics such as religion and sports. Conversely, topics related to gaming and lifestyle are perceived as more neutral and extensively covered in training datasets, enabling models to manage them more adeptly. There is a notable correlation between the performances in steering and judgment tasks, as depicted in Figure 3. When models excel in judgment tasks, they also tend to perform well in steering tasks, suggesting that both tasks depend on similar cognitive and alignment capabilities. This observation implies that enhancing foundational skills, such as contextual understanding and balanced decision-making, may enhance performance across diverse evaluative tasks. Future research might explore strategies for better integrating these skills to enhance models' capacity for language interpretation and control.  The findings presented in Table 3 demonstrate that mediation affects various aspects of language differently. Mediation decreases both toxicity and the use of exclamations, leading to more neutral and calm responses, thereby contributing to a more balanced and less emotionally charged dialogue. However, mediation does not significantly alter the level of argumentativeness, suggesting that it has a limited effect on how models manage disagreements. Furthermore, models behave differently when simulating both sides of a conversation compared to interacting with real users, highlighting the importance of assessing mediation based on both the process and the outcomes in real-world scenarios.

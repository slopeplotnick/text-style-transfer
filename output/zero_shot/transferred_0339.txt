The EMOPIA dataset is a key resource in the field of research focused on generating music that aligns with emotional contexts. However, there is a notable scarcity of large-scale datasets comprising human-labeled MIDI files for such studies. Existing datasets in this domain are typically limited in scale, with fewer than 200 MIDI files containing emotion labels. In contrast, the EMOPIA dataset is the largest currently available, featuring 1,087 musical excerpts derived from 387 songs, with emotional annotations provided by four independent annotators. The dataset categorizes emotions into four distinct groups based on the valence-arousal (VA) plane, further details of which are provided in Appendix B.  In addition to EMOPIA, this study leverages the GiantMIDI-Piano dataset, which encompasses 10,855 solo piano compositions by 2,786 composers. GiantMIDI-Piano is widely utilized in research areas such as the analysis of classical music and symbolic music generation. To connect the textual data with the EMOPIA dataset, 27 emotion labels were aggregated into four categories aligned with the VA quadrants. This classification employed the NRC Valence-Arousal-Dominance (VAD) lexicon, a resource comprising valence and arousal scores for approximately 55,000 words. Each emotion was assigned to one of the four VA categories based on this lexicon. The mappings and their corresponding positions on the VA plane are depicted in Figure 1, with additional explanations regarding the VAD lexicon and its integration with EMOPIA provided in Appendix A.  This study employs MIDI files as a means of encoding and representing symbolic music data. MIDI files are particularly advantageous due to their compactness and ability to capture performance details, including expressivity and emotion-related characteristics of music. Additionally, the widespread availability of MIDI files enhances their utility for training models. Within the training framework, MIDI files are treated as sequences of musical events, enabling the application of sequence-to-sequence models for learning and generating music effectively.

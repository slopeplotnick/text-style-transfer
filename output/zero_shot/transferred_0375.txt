Figure 3 illustrates the learning progression of the 7B model employing the CAPO and GRPO methods. Initially, both techniques exhibit similar improvement rates; however, as the model gains proficiency, CAPO demonstrates superior performance. The gray line indicates the transition point where the model ceases imitation and begins to independently develop understanding. Beyond this juncture, CAPO's unpredictability continues to increase, in contrast to GRPO, which remains constant. Concurrently, the rewards associated with CAPO also show sustained improvement. This suggests that CAPO not only achieves greater rewards in the later stages but also maintains a higher level of unpredictability, which is beneficial for exploring diverse cognitive strategies. The gradual rise in unpredictability ensures that CAPO does not prematurely become predictable by incorporating negative examples too soon. By delaying their integration, CAPO stabilizes the imitation phase and more effectively utilizes negative feedback in subsequent stages, enhancing its ability to manage a variety of tasks.  The study then compares the conventional static instructional approach with a novel dynamic methodology. In the static approach, the difficulty of each example is determined by assessing the model's performance, and training proceeds in that sequence. Conversely, the dynamic method adjusts focus throughout the training process without necessitating a reordering of the data. As demonstrated in Table 3, both the ADARFT and static method GRPO(+SC) offer negligible improvements over the baseline GRPO. In contrast, the dynamic method GRPO(+Ours) exhibits significantly better performance across most tasks. This indicates that merely estimating task difficulty is insufficient; rather, dynamically shifting focus during training proves to be a more effective strategy.

Recent research efforts have focused on enhancing the robustness of systems by maintaining consistency in their interpretation of shapes and geometric information. Studies indicate that variations in viewpoint can disrupt the processing capabilities of pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT) encoders, leading to cascading issues in the integration of perception and decision-making components. Building on these findings, this study investigates whether addressing processing mismatches—without overhauling the entire Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architecture or collecting additional data—can improve the system’s ability to handle viewpoint changes.  A promising approach in this context is parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT), a method that facilitates model adaptation to new tasks or scenarios with minimal architectural modifications. Prominent PEFT techniques, such as adapters, LoRA, Prefix-Tuning, and BitFit, introduce lightweight, tunable modules into the system while preserving its overall structure. These methods have gained popularity in large language models and multimodal systems due to their efficiency and stability. Within the field of embodied AI, PEFT is predominantly applied to refine language or decision-making components, such as decision transformers and instruction encoders. However, limited attention has been devoted to directly addressing viewpoint variations by fine-tuning the visual processing components of these systems.  Most existing approaches tackle visual processing challenges by adapting the entire system, including retraining visuomotor policies or substantial network components. In contrast, this research explores whether targeted, lightweight modifications to specific visual elements—such as vision tokens or the vision encoder—can effectively mitigate the impact of viewpoint changes. The crux of the issue lies in the predictable shifts in visual data processing caused by viewpoint variations, which disrupt the system's overall functionality. This work aims to demonstrate that such issues can be addressed with more efficient and localized adjustments, avoiding the need for extensive system-wide retraining.

In the process of simplifying formulas, extraneous components are eliminated while retaining only the essential elements, thereby maintaining the formula's functionality in a more compact form. This simplification enhances the efficiency of SAT solvers. Hallucinations arise from improper reuse, mislabeling, or inaccurate translation of variables or formula components, which disrupt the effectiveness of SAT solvers. Such errors may stem from models inadequately translating certain sentence structures during training or from sentences that deviate from conventional word order, complicating their conversion into conjunctive normal form (CNF). To address these challenges, we employed a combined approach using the Natural Language Toolkit (NLTK) and Sympy. NLTK facilitates the decomposition of sentences according to rigorously established and validated grammatical rules, ensuring accurate syntactic breakdowns. Subsequently, Sympy is utilized to preserve the logical integrity of the original sentence during its conversion to CNF. When presented with a paragraph, each sentence is isolated at the period and analyzed independently. These individual sentences are then logically conjoined using the AND operation to form a CNF. NLTK's extensive grammar rule set is employed to parse each sentence into a syntactic tree structure that delineates the sentence's construction. This tree is hierarchically organized, beginning with the main clause and branching into subordinate components such as conditions, statements, and other grammatical elements like nouns, verbs, and punctuation. Predefined rules guide the transformation of this tree into a logical formula using Sympy, which subsequently converts the formula into CNF utilizing its inherent capabilities.

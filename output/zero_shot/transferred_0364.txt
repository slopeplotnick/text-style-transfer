This study investigates the utilization of context by language models in responding to health-related inquiries and examines the consequences of introducing inconsistent context. The research employs the HEALTHCONTRADICT dataset, which comprises 920 instances. Each instance consists of a health question, a scientifically validated factual response, and two contradictory documents. The study explores various strategies for posing questions, including accurate, incorrect, and mixed-up contexts, and evaluates their impact on the model's responses. In comparison to other assessments, HEALTHCONTRADICT provides a more nuanced understanding of how language models manage context. The findings reveal that the efficacy of these models stems not only from pre-existing knowledge but also from their ability to utilize appropriate context while disregarding irrelevant information. However, the potential for models to generate seemingly plausible yet inaccurate responses poses a concern, as individuals might inadvertently rely on erroneous health information, which could be hazardous. This issue is exacerbated by the rapid evolution of health information, particularly during crises, and the swift dissemination of unverified information online, which influences the learning processes of these models. Several methodologies have been proposed to enhance the models' capacity to navigate disordered information. One approach involves adjusting the model's knowledge when it contradicts the context, while another retraces the rationale behind a model's response. However, these techniques predominantly focus on either the model's prior knowledge or the immediate context. An alternative strategy integrates both by evaluating the coherence of information. Additionally, there is a method that produces dual responses: one derived from context and another from the model's existing knowledge. Another approach ensures the selection of the correct response amid conflicting information. Other interventions aim to prevent the generation of incorrect responses by introducing misleading or irrelevant data during training to improve the model's handling of inconsistent information. During testing, strategies are employed to identify and mitigate erroneous information, formulate questions judiciously, and employ multiple reviewers to verify responses. Furthermore, proactive measures include searching for robust responses to thwart misinformation and anticipating which facts may rapidly evolve to preempt reliance on outdated information. Current methodologies predominantly address either the rectification of mixed-up information or the prevention of model inaccuracies, yet both aspects are crucial for enhancing response quality in health-related topics.

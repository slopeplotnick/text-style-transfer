Assessing the power consumption of large language models (LLMs) requires a distinct approach from evaluating their speed or latency. Power consumption is influenced by various factors, including hardware configurations, intricate software systems, and the operational context, which can vary significantly across different environments. Current benchmarking methodologies for LLMs inadequately address these variations, as they typically adhere to predetermined configurations, fixed model sizes, and expensive instrumentation. However, the TokenPowerBench framework, depicted in Figure 1, addresses these limitations through a tripartite system: setup, execution and measurement, and reporting. This framework provides a versatile and replicable method for assessing LLM power consumption in contemporary applications.   Initially, TokenPowerBench facilitates the preparation of the evaluation by allowing users to select the model, execution engine, and prompts. To streamline this process, TokenPowerBench offers three configurable components: a model pool, a prompt-dataset menu, and an inference-engine selector. The model pool includes a variety of LLMs supported by the framework, enabling the assessment of power consumption across different model architectures. This encompasses standard transformers, such as the LLaMA series, and Mixture-of-Experts models, like Mixtral, which exhibit diverse computational strategies, particularly in parameter activation and memory utilization. The evaluation encompasses models of varying sizes, from smaller ones with fewer than one billion parameters suitable for a single GPU, to larger models, such as Llama 3-405B, which necessitate multiple GPUs or nodes. This range facilitates the examination of power consumption dynamics as both model complexity and hardware scale increase.  The prompt-dataset menu component acknowledges that the length and typology of prompts can significantly influence power consumption during inference. TokenPowerBench supports two primary datasets: Alpaca, featuring concise chat prompts, and LongBench, which includes extended contexts of up to 10,000 tokens. Additionally, users have the option to employ custom datasets formatted in CSV or other compatible structures.

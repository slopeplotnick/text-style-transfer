The findings of this study align with those reported by Shirafuji et al. (2025), who employed a task vector-based method and observed favorable performance in debiased models. Similarly, prior research, such as Lutz (2024), utilized model-merging techniques to evaluate the performance of debiased large language models (LLMs) on tasks using natural language inference (NLI) benchmarks. However, our results underscore the necessity of evaluating such methods with a broader range of datasets beyond NLI benchmarks. Analysis of the SuperGLUE benchmark revealed that most model-merging techniques, including Linear, SLERP, and Nearswap, compromise an LLM's capacity for causal reasoning (see Section 5.2). As such, these techniques are not sufficiently reliable for effectively mitigating social bias. Among the methods examined, SLERP demonstrated superior performance due to its ability to interpolate evenly across the parameter space without amplifying bias. In contrast, NuSLERP, which implemented normalization at the layer or tensor level, negatively affected SuperGLUE scores.  This study represents the first systematic evaluation of the impact of seven model-merging methods on social bias in LLMs. We analyzed 13 models from the GPT, LLaMA, and Qwen families using three social bias datasets and the SuperGLUE benchmark to explore the trade-off between fairness and utility. Notably, four methods—Karcher Mean, NuSLERP, TIES, and DELLA—exhibited diminished performance on tasks requiring reading comprehension, commonsense reasoning, or causal reasoning, such as ReCoRD, COPA, CB, and BoolQ in the SuperGLUE benchmark. While several existing debiasing approaches using model-merging methods have demonstrated their efficacy on downstream tasks via NLI benchmarks, our findings highlight the critical importance of assessing these methods on tasks related to reading comprehension, commonsense reasoning, and causal reasoning to ensure balanced performance.

The efficacy of the system is enhanced by generating specific prompts tailored to each task, taking into account real-time parameters such as the system's current state, the capabilities of the robots, the workload, and the phase of the task. This approach obviates the necessity of including an excessive 5,000-6,000 extraneous example tokens in every prompt, which were deemed superfluous. The process achieves token reduction through two principal mechanisms: firstly, by concentrating solely on the requirements pertinent to the task, it circumvents unnecessary procedural steps like decomposition or redundant code generation, which previously amounted to approximately 14,000 tokens per task. Secondly, it substitutes example tokens with a structured contextualization method, thereby reducing the token count in the prompts from 6,700 to 1,100.   The effectiveness of this system in delegating tasks to multiple robots was assessed based on metrics such as task completion success rate, workload distribution equity, and adaptability to varying conditions. The assessment was conducted across three distinct configurations wherein the robots possessed varying skill sets, although the task sequence comprised 36 tasks remained constant. Each configuration emphasized different robotic competencies by altering the probability of each robot's success in specific tasks. The system demonstrated its capability to maintain robust performance while ensuring equitable task distribution among the robots, a critical consideration in environments such as construction where both efficiency and fairness are paramount. In one particular configuration, termed 'Medium Excels,' robots with intermediate skill levels achieved the highest overall performance, with a task completion success rate of 87.8%. The system predominantly allocated work to the robot proficient across all task types, resulting in some workload imbalance. In this configuration, success rates for specific task categories were 92.5% for precision tasks, 86.6% for tasks necessitating dexterity, and 85% for tasks involving substantial weight. Although this configuration yielded optimal overall results, it also highlighted that prioritizing performance may occasionally lead to disproportionate workload distribution.

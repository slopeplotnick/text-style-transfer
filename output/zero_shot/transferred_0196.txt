In contemporary technology, it is imperative that devices such as smartphones, laptops, smart assistants, drones, and automotive systems are capable of effectively managing erroneous prompts and responses independently of cloud-based solutions. This autonomy not only enhances operational speed and user privacy but also facilitates functionality in constrained environments. Recent research efforts have aimed at developing safety models with multilingual capabilities; however, these models often underperform when addressing a wide array of languages, particularly those that lack substantial linguistic resources. A prevalent methodology to circumvent this challenge involves training a model in a widely-supported language, such as English, and subsequently applying it to other languages through shared linguistic features. This approach proves more efficacious for linguistically similar or well-supported languages, yet it remains less effective for rare languages due to limited commonality and training data.  To address this limitation, we have developed CREST, an innovative model encompassing 100 languages with a relatively compact architecture of only 0.5 billion parameters. CREST is trained on 13 well-supported languages and employs a multilingual transformer model to cluster languages with semantic and structural similarities. This clustering facilitates the model's proficient performance across language groups, wherein training on a well-supported language can benefit less common languages within the same group. This methodology is particularly advantageous when data availability for safety applications in multiple languages is limited. Despite its modest size, CREST surpasses the performance of existing models, proving effective for both well-supported and lesser-known languages. It achieves this by leveraging shared lexical items and scripts to generalize across languages. To the best of our knowledge, this represents the first instance of a safety model demonstrating robust efficacy across such an extensive array of languages. This paper delineates the methodologies and findings of our research.

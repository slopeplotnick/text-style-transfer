The efficacy of the Radiologist Copilot was evaluated through an examination of its task execution capabilities and the performance of its integrated tools. In the context of generating radiology reports, various metrics were employed to assess textual quality and clinical accuracy. The textual quality was measured using BLEU-1, ROUGE-L, METEOR, and BERTScore, whereas clinical accuracy was evaluated via F1-RadGraph and GREEN metrics. Furthermore, the functionality of the tools was assessed using the "LLM-as-a-Judge" framework, which leverages OpenAI GPT-5.1 to comprehensively review the agent's task performance. This review encompassed four domains: task analysis, tool selection, planning, and execution. Each domain was rated on a scale from 1 to 5, with 1 indicating poor performance and 5 indicating excellent performance. The task analysis component assessed the agent's comprehension of the task, while the tool selection component evaluated the appropriateness of the tool choices. The planning component examined the coherence of the devised plan, and the execution component evaluated the agent’s adherence to the plan.  The overall assessment of the Radiologist Copilot involved a synthesis of tool functionality and task handling in radiology reporting, accompanied by ablation studies to evaluate the core components in the absence of auxiliary features. The tool evaluation revealed predominantly excellent scores (5), with some achieving good scores (4), indicating the agent's thoroughness in task comprehension, appropriate tool selection, effective planning, and successful execution. The analysis of the Copilot's response to user inquiries demonstrated that its planning and execution processes operate efficiently, resulting in high-quality radiology reports. The Copilot's operational methodology closely mirrors that of professional radiologists, contributing to substantial time savings in clinical practice.

The principal competencies we aim to cultivate include the ability to deduce three-dimensional structures without explicit guidance, the skillful manipulation of camera perspectives in a manner that coherently aligns with scene parameters such as scale, obstruction, and configuration, as well as comprehending spatial arrangements within scenes, strategizing to achieve specific objectives, and maintaining temporal consistency. These skills are evaluated through two distinct tasks: navigating a scene based on video directives and identifying objects according to textual cues. For the navigation task, the model interprets camera movement instructions to produce a video that accurately aligns with both the directives and the configuration of the scene. In the object identification task, the model adheres to textual instructions to locate and display a designated object, thereby assessing its spatial comprehension and planning capabilities. These tasks serve to determine the efficacy of the VIDEO4SPATIAL framework in interpreting and interacting with scene intricacies utilizing only video input. Due to the absence of extensive public datasets tailored for these specific tasks, we employ indoor scanning datasets such as ScanNet++ and ARKitScenes, which furnish extensive room-based video sequences allowing the generation of context-target video clip pairs. A vision-language model is employed to select clips culminating in a focused object and to generate corresponding object-finding instructions. For the scene navigation task, camera movement data is transformed into executable instructions. Empirical evaluations indicate that this framework adeptly manages complex tasks, facilitating the model's comprehension of scene layout and semantics solely from video data, and enabling effective object identification and scene navigation. Remarkably, despite training on shorter video sequences, the model demonstrates robust performance on extended sequences during testing, enhancing its applicability. Furthermore, although trained predominantly on indoor scenes, the model exhibits adaptability to outdoor environments and unfamiliar objects. In conclusion, we have developed VIDEO4SPATIAL, a streamlined yet proficient video-based framework for visuospatial tasks, emphasizing critical design decisions such as the integration of contextual guidance and instructions, the inclusion of bounding boxes to enhance accuracy, and the strategic sampling of context to optimize comprehension efficiency. We also designed and validated two spatially-oriented tasks.

Regarding software engineering (SWE) agents, investigations have sought to assess their capabilities and identify areas where performance deficiencies arise. Empirical evidence suggests that SWE agents encounter significant challenges when addressing problems involving multiple files. It has been proposed that enhanced evaluation methods are required, focusing on metrics such as efficiency, reliability, and intelligence. Research into the limitations of these agents has uncovered specific issues, including difficulties in fault localization, handling complex interdependencies in code, and maintaining contextual awareness across multiple files.  One notable contribution in this domain is TRAIL, a curated dataset comprising a benchmark of 148 traces annotated by human reviewers, which offers insights into error patterns. Another team introduced MAST, a framework for categorizing agent failures in multi-agent systems. Their study involved analyzing 150 traces derived from five systems and five benchmarks, with human annotation playing a crucial role in the evaluation process. Additionally, a separate investigation employed flow graphs to model agent actions, such as project comprehension, contextual tracking, debugging, and testing. However, the study's scope was limited, with manual analysis applied to a small subset of data—specifically, bug localization for 20 out of 500 issues.  Further research has explored failure modes of large language models (LLMs) during SWE tasks, presenting a hierarchical taxonomy encompassing three phases, nine categories, and 25 subcategories. This taxonomy served as a basis for analyzing tool inefficiencies, uncovering recurring problems such as agents becoming trapped in repetitive loops. Nevertheless, the analysis was constrained by manual examination of only 150 issues within a dataset of 500. Collectively, these studies highlight the need for scalable and robust methodologies to advance the performance and reliability of SWE agents in handling complex software engineering challenges.

In their 2025 study, Gurung and Lapata introduced the VR-CLI framework, designed to optimize planning processes by employing extensive reasoning over narrative information, thereby generating comprehensive responses. VR-CLI functions as a reward mechanism intended to enhance the probability of accurately predicting subsequent narrative events, based on available story data and plans. We have further refined VR-CLI by incorporating a component that reduces the likelihood of selecting a random chapter from the same narrative source. Our standard methodology, LiteReason, occasionally conducts minor training steps using paths developed in real-time. Although this approach is slightly more computationally intensive, it allows for updating the reasoning module in line with the current model's paradigm. Additionally, we have developed a concealed thought prompt that facilitates the model's application of hidden thought tags. LiteReason, devoid of Supervised Fine Tuning (SFT), retains the hidden prompt and the initial Reasoning Projector, utilizing hidden thought tags to determine when the model requires new reasoning inputs; however, it foregoes SFT during training, thereby not updating the Reasoning Projector. Conversely, LiteReason, in the absence of the Reasoning Projector, examines the effectiveness post-training without the Reasoning Projector, yet employs the hidden thought prompt. This variation omits hidden reasoning and assesses whether the model's behavior deviates when reasoning steps are bypassed. We review recent studies in hidden reasoning and select several prior methodologies to evaluate the concepts detailed in section 3. These evaluations are conducted on Flawed Fictions and Narrative Completion Prediction (NCP) tasks. For training-free hidden reasoning, we compare our approach with two novel methods: the Mixture of Inputs (MoI) proposed by Su et al. (2025), which integrates the embedding of sampled tokens with others, and Soft Thinking by Zhang et al. (2025), which similarly combines token embeddings but utilizes an entropy-based measure to determine the transition from continuous thinking to discrete sampling.

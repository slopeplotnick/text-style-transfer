As the use of artificial intelligence (AI) tools based on large language models (LLMs) continues to grow, ensuring their safety becomes increasingly critical. This issue is particularly salient when deploying these tools in multilingual and culturally diverse contexts, which may introduce novel risks. It is essential to develop robust systems capable of mitigating harmful or unsafe outputs across a wide range of languages and scenarios. Without such safeguards, the global implementation of LLMs risks inadvertently excluding or harming underserved populations.   Currently, most research on AI safety has concentrated on languages with substantial data resources, such as English, Chinese, Spanish, German, and French. However, the vast majority of the world’s languages lack sufficient data to support safety and evaluation frameworks. This data paucity poses significant challenges for ensuring the safety of LLMs in linguistically diverse settings. Although some testing frameworks have been developed to evaluate multilingual performance, these tend to exclude low-resource languages and fail to capture the diversity of user populations. Furthermore, the creation of training datasets specifically designed to enhance safety systems for underrepresented languages has been limited. While recent advancements have generated training data for high-resource languages using both original and translated content, these efforts remain insufficient to establish comprehensive safety mechanisms.  Another critical challenge lies in the computational demands of current safety systems, which are often resource-intensive, large in scale, and slow in operation. As a result, such systems are unsuitable for deployment on devices with limited computational capacity or in resource-constrained environments. The inefficiency and high computational requirements of existing safety solutions make them less viable for real-world applications. This highlights an urgent need for the development of lightweight, efficient safety mechanisms that can effectively protect both the language model and end users from potential harm.

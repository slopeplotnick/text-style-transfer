The application of reasoning to enhance the performance of large language models (LLMs) across diverse tasks is witnessing increasing attention. Initially, this enhancement was achieved by guiding models to engage in sequential thinking, as exemplified by the Chain-of-Thought methodology (Wei et al., 2022), where models articulate their thought processes prior to providing responses. Recently, reinforcement learning (RL) has been employed to refine models' abilities to generate these thought processes, demonstrating particular utility in domains such as mathematics and coding. These domains benefit from RL's capacity to reinforce model behavior through correctness-based reward systems (Shao et al., 2024; Lambert et al., 2025).   Contemporary research efforts are beginning to explore the application of RL in contexts where the evaluation of correctness is inherently subjective or non-trivial, such as narrative generation and comprehension (Ahuja et al., 2025; Gurung and Lapata, 2025). However, the integration of RL methodologies introduces challenges related to computational efficiency and cost, due to the increased cognitive demands placed on models, leading to extended processing times (Shen et al., 2025a). Consequently, there is an ongoing pursuit of methodologies to expedite and optimize reasoning both during the training phase and in deployment scenarios (Sui et al., 2025).  One innovative approach under investigation is 'latent reasoning,' which involves the condensation of thought processes into compact, encoded representations rather than traditional verbose articulations. The rationale behind this approach is based on the observation that not all linguistic expressions are requisite for deriving solutions, and humans frequently engage in cognitive processes without externalizing thoughts verbosely. By encapsulating thoughts in these encoded forms, models can maintain a richer breadth of information without the information loss often associated with verbal translation. Hao et al. (2025) indicate that such encoded thoughts can integrate multiple reasoning steps concurrently, akin to resolving several components of a problem simultaneously.  Within this study, we introduce LiteReason, an innovative latent reasoning framework designed to facilitate tasks requiring the comprehension and generation of extended narrative content. LiteReason incorporates a distinctive component known as the Reasoning Projector, which enables the model to produce both conventional text and encoded thoughts. The transition to this encoded cognitive mode is initiated by a designated symbol, prompting the model to activate its reasoning processes.

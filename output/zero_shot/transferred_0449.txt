The study by [22] advocates for the utilization of unlabeled LLM-generated text in lieu of labeled datasets, which facilitates the development of models capable of detecting hallucinations more efficiently and effectively, thereby streamlining the training process. Furthermore, [12] underscores the critical importance of implementing a robust question-answering framework, deeply embedded in research, to mitigate the proliferation of incorrect information. This is particularly pertinent for users engaging with LLMs, given their capacity for content creation and interactive communication. Collectively, these studies aim to devise methodologies to diminish the prevalence of hallucinations in text generation, enhancing the reliability and credibility of outputs generated by LLMs. Strategies such as zero-resource detection and fostering self-awareness within language models are highlighted as effective approaches in combating hallucinations, acknowledging their inherent complexity. Understanding the interplay between LLMs and hallucinations is crucial, as it influences the proficiency of models in addressing this challenge. Variability in hallucination levels among different LLMs can be attributed to differences in their architectural design and training processes.

In this study, we introduce a novel method for constructing knowledge graphs (KGs) from visual data by employing artificial intelligence (AI) models capable of processing both textual and image inputs. This process is guided by a predefined set of topic-specific rules known as an ontology, which outlines the salient entities and relationships pertinent to the domain of interest. By incorporating these ontological rules into the AI model's processing instructions, we ensure that the model accurately identifies and prioritizes the essential elements present within the images.  Upon receiving an image as input, the AI model generates a detailed list of key entities, their attributes, and interconnections, all of which are informed by the ontology. Subsequently, these lists are transformed into graphical representations that elucidate the depicted content. The structural configuration of each graph is intrinsically determined by the ontological rules established beforehand. Each individual image thus results in a distinct graph, which is subsequently stored within a centralized database.  Despite the unique nature of each graph, there exists a degree of uniformity across the collection due to the use of a standardized vocabulary prescribed by the ontological framework. This shared linguistic and conceptual basis facilitates the integration of individual graphs into a comprehensive, overarching graph structure. The aggregated graph enables the identification of similarities between images through comparative analysis of their respective smaller graphs.

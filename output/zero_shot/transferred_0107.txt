Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have significantly transformed the methodologies employed in event-based prediction. Unlike traditional approaches, LLMs possess the capability to extract comprehensive details about events—including the nature of the event, the participants, and the temporal framework—directly from unstructured text. These models ensure the accuracy of the extracted information, facilitating robust causal inference and transparent reasoning. However, contemporary research has not yet fully harnessed the potential of LLMs. Current methodologies, such as CAMEF[25], utilize LLMs to extract event details, yet subsequently condense these details into limited data representations compatible with conventional neural networks. This approach reduces LLMs to mere feature extraction tools, thereby losing a wealth of event information, failing to elucidate temporal event interconnections, and lacking a reusable and traceable memory system.  Our approach presents a paradigm shift. Instead of compressing detailed event information into minimal data units, we employ these details as foundational elements to construct a structured memory of events. By systematically tracking, integrating, and organizing event sequences, our method directly facilitates the understanding of temporal event dynamics and enables comparative pattern analysis across diverse contexts. This addresses the limitations associated with current LLM methods concerning the association of event sequences and the identification of recurring patterns over time.  The central premise of our work involves the development of a system for event analysis aimed at predicting stock prices. This system constructs an event knowledge base from news articles, enhancing it through model feedback. It thereby enables the identification and utilization of analogous event patterns from historical data. The design of the system is depicted in Figure 1 and elucidated in the subsequent sections.

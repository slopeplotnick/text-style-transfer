Refusal rates of language models exhibit variability that is difficult to predict, with certain models exhibiting higher declination frequencies while others present lower ones, contingent upon the length of the context provided. The efficacy of these models is significantly influenced by the nature of the padding utilized; padding that resembles authentic text demonstrates superior performance compared to random padding, which in turn outperforms multi-task padding. The positioning of the padding is also crucial, as placing the context following the task description can adversely affect model performance compared to positioning it prior. Large Language Model (LLM) agents, which are sophisticated artificial intelligence systems, employ pre-trained language models iteratively to make decisions and manage tasks requiring multiple steps, leveraging tools autonomously. There has been substantial growth in the capacity of context windows, expanding from 2,000 tokens in GPT-3 to 2 million in Grok 4 Fast, attributable to advancements in position embeddings and attention mechanisms. Nevertheless, these models continue to disproportionately emphasize recent inputs while exhibiting limited retention of older information. Evaluations such as NIAH and LongBench reveal that although larger context windows enable models to assimilate more information, they do not necessarily enhance the models' intelligence or their capacity for its effective utilization. Safety evaluations for LLMs encompass assessments of toxicity, misinformation dissemination, and vulnerabilities to exploitation, with LongSafety evaluating model performance on extended inputs of up to 5,000 tokens. Safety assessments for agents concentrate on interactive tasks, with AgentSecurity and Agent Dojo evaluating resilience to prompt injection attacks, whereas RedCode and MobileSafetyBench assess risks associated with code execution. AgentHarm is a comprehensive evaluation designed to assess how agents navigate harmful tasks and execute complex multi-step workflows. Recent enhancements to the AgentHarm evaluation explore agent behavior and performance in scenarios involving extended context inputs for both benign and harmful tasks. AgentHarm also evaluates the agents' proficiency in employing tools during multi-step tasks, incorporating tools configured to simulate real-world applications, such as Google Search or an API for social media posting, to allow testers precise control over task execution.

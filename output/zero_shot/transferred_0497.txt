ViSAudio demonstrates superior performance in generating spatial audio from videos compared to existing methodologies, as evidenced by evaluations across diverse datasets, detailed in Table 2. Comprehensive comparisons with leading approaches are further elucidated in Section 5.1. For the BiAudio and MUSIC test sets, both video and captions were utilized; however, due to the absence of captions in the FAIR-Play dataset, only video data was employed. Models incapable of processing textual input were also evaluated using video data exclusively. The results presented in Table 2 indicate that ViSAudio outperforms competing methods across several dimensions, including sound quality, channel distribution accuracy, audio-video synchronization, and semantic consistency with the visual scene. These findings suggest that the model effectively synthesizes realistic binaural audio while maintaining synchronization with video content across diverse auditory environments. Additionally, ViSAudio exhibits robustness on the FAIR-Play dataset, which it was not specifically trained on, indicative of its adaptability to novel scenarios. Figure 3 illustrates the comparative analysis of various techniques in generating spatial audio from videos. One particular example involves a subject playing the sitar while the camera pans from left to right, causing the perceived audio to shift correspondingly from right to left. Spectrogram analyses reveal that ViSAudio's generated audio closely aligns with the authentic audio, accurately representing the directional movement of sound. For instance, when the sound source is positioned on the left, the left audio channel is appropriately more pronounced, reflecting real-world auditory perception. In contrast, other methods fail to accurately capture the temporal rhythm or differentiate adequately between the left and right audio channels.

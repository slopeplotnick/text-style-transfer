In this study, we propose a methodology termed MIGR, designed to identify the initial input type for focus by utilizing Mutual Information (MI). The primary objective is to mitigate biases in the system's initial reasoning phase and ensure concentration on the input type exhibiting the most potent emotional signals. The methodology is bifurcated into two principal components. Firstly, the modality-aligned SFT facilitates the system's learning process by directing attention toward the input type that MI identifies as having the strongest emotional signals. Secondly, the Modality-Aware Reward Optimization aids the system in maintaining emphasis on the appropriate input type, thereby ensuring the emotional grounding of reasoning while enhancing the stability of the training process. This is achieved through the deployment of two reward mechanisms: one encourages correct input order focus, and the other supports reasoning grounded in emotional signals.  Subsequent sections will elucidate these steps in greater detail. Recent studies have demonstrated the efficacy of SFT in emotion comprehension, even in scenarios with limited high-quality training data, such as from the EMER dataset. Based on these insights, we initiate our training with a similar approach. Although EMER provides robust training data, the SFT step can be optimized by incorporating additional instances that exhibit consistent emotional signals. To accomplish this, supplementary examples are introduced into the training data. These examples are derived by analyzing facial action units (FAUs) from video frames, which are intrinsically linked to emotional expressions. Verification is conducted to ensure FAU patterns accurately correspond to the target emotion, utilizing a mapping table of FAUs to emotions. Only examples with exact matches between FAUs and emotions are retained. For these curated examples, reasoning-answer pairs are generated, guided by the emotional evidence provided by the FAUs to inform the reasoning process.

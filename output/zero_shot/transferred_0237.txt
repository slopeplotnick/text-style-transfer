E-commerce platforms such as Amazon, Taobao, and JD.com have significantly transformed consumer shopping behaviors. Central to their functionality is the ability to ensure the relevance of the products displayed to users. Relevance plays a critical role in shaping the user experience and is directly tied to consumer trust and the platforms' economic success. Inadequate relevance can lead to diminished user satisfaction, erosion of trust, and potential revenue loss for these platforms. Consequently, the accurate determination of relevance between user queries and products is fundamental to effective e-commerce systems.  Traditional methods for assessing relevance, including lexical matching and vector-based approaches, remain widely utilized in modern e-commerce search engines. Lexical matching techniques, such as Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) and BM25, evaluate the similarity between search queries and product descriptions by considering term frequency and importance in the context of the query. However, these methods often lack the capability to fully understand semantic meanings and contextual relationships. Conversely, vector-based approaches, leveraging models like Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT), have improved the ability to capture semantic relationships between queries and products, thereby enhancing relevance.  Despite these advancements, the application of vector-based relevance models in domain-specific scenarios continues to face critical challenges. First, there is an insufficient availability of high-quality, domain-specific hard negative examples. While existing models may perform adequately with limited labeled data, achieving optimal performance requires a substantial volume of such challenging examples, which are labor-intensive to curate and label accurately. Second, these models often exhibit limitations in reasoning and interpretability, particularly when applied to lightweight, online systems. The constrained capacity of smaller models makes it difficult to handle complex reasoning tasks or to adequately represent nuanced relevance judgments.  To address these limitations, large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being explored for relevance tasks. However, their current implementation often relies on using the final token representations to determine relevance, and they commonly employ soft-label methodologies for distilling knowledge into smaller models. While effective to an extent, these approaches fail to reveal which features specifically contribute to relevance. Furthermore, they provide limited support for enhancing the reasoning and interpretability capabilities of smaller downstream models. Continued research is thus necessary to develop strategies that improve reasoning capabilities and semantic understanding in relevance determination for e-commerce applications.

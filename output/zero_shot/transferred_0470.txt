Concerns regarding energy consumption have heightened, particularly in relation to devices such as smartphones and large-scale data centers. It is imperative for software developers to accurately assess the energy consumption of their software to identify and rectify inefficiencies. Historically, analyses predominantly focused on macroscopic components, such as entire applications or specific functions, rather than conducting granular examinations at the level of individual code lines. Additionally, estimations often concentrated solely on CPU energy usage, neglecting other hardware components, resulting in incomplete and potentially misleading analyses.   In response to these limitations, we propose an innovative methodology that extends beyond mere CPU considerations and eschews speculative estimations. Our approach is specifically tailored for programming languages such as Java, Kotlin, and Scala that execute on the Java Virtual Machine (JVM). We scrutinize the transformation process of code into Java bytecode to quantify its energy consumption. Subsequently, we employ a Bayesian statistical model to predict energy usage and delineate influencing factors. The model integrates three code-derived metrics—data size, data type, and operation—as well as one hardware-related parameter, the device.   To validate the efficacy of our approach, we conducted empirical evaluations using Java, analyzing its bytecode and testing the method on novel applications. The findings revealed the significance of all four factors, particularly the variation in energy consumption across devices of identical models and the substantial impact of operations and data types on energy usage. The precision of our energy consumption predictions closely aligned with the actual energy usage observed, verifying the robustness of our method.   Our research contributes a foundational framework for energy modeling, offering a valuable tool for future endeavors aimed at assessing and enhancing the energy efficiency of software applications.

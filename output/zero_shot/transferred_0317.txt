Empirical evidence suggests that the persistence of entrenched beliefs can lead to significant cognitive and societal challenges, such as polarization, the spread of misinformation, and various cognitive biases. These phenomena are intrinsically tied to the mechanisms by which individuals process information, a process that is fundamentally linked to reasoning. Large language models (LLMs), which are trained on extensive corpora of online text and constructed to assist users by answering queries or supporting task execution, have demonstrated considerable efficacy in performing complex reasoning tasks. Over time, advanced methodologies have been developed to enhance the reasoning capacities of these models. Early approaches, such as the Chain-of-Thought framework, have evolved into more sophisticated strategies incorporating reinforcement learning and other techniques to enable models to engage in evidence-based argumentation and flexible problem-solving.   However, a notable limitation of these models is their occasional failure to appropriately reassess prior beliefs when presented with new information, leading to suboptimal reasoning outcomes. This tendency to adhere to outdated belief frameworks can diminish the reliability of their conclusions and inadvertently foster unwarranted user confidence in their outputs. While existing research has identified the phenomenon of belief entrenchment within LLMs, conclusively characterizing and evaluating it remains challenging. A key difficulty lies in discerning when a belief update is justified as opposed to being influenced by bias. Consequently, prior studies have focused on controlled tasks in which belief entrenchment is more readily observable. However, these approaches often fall short in detecting reasoning errors in more ambiguous or ill-defined contexts, where truth is less easily ascertained.

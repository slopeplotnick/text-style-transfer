In recent studies, the Llama-4 Maverick model has demonstrated superior performance compared to conventional methods employed in biological research for the integration of information, specifically the Bayesian reliability-weighted combiner, which is also utilized by humans. The enhanced efficacy of Llama-4 Maverick suggests the potential employment of additional mechanisms beyond the standard method. Illustrated in Figure 7 is a comparative analysis of Llama-4 Maverick's multisensory responses versus those utilizing a singular sensory input. The findings indicate that a non-linear approach yields better performance than a linear counterpart. Under conditions of noise, the Bayesian method remains optimal, serving as an effective benchmark for the enhancement of language models that have not yet reached optimal performance levels. Figure 8 highlights that models exhibiting higher accuracy tend to adopt behavior more closely aligned with the Bayesian method. Despite the less precise performance of Gemma 3 4B and Phi 4 Multimodal, these models still demonstrate commendable results in this regard. Section A.9 details the comparative analysis across various tasks and models. The study reveals diverse behavioral patterns in language models when subjected to tasks that simulate human perception processes. More accurate models, such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and Llama-4 Maverick, provide substantial evidence of employing Bayesian methodologies, particularly in image-based contexts, as discussed in Appendix A.11. These models exhibit adaptive behavior akin to the Bayesian method when confronted with contextual variations such as noise or additional information, utilizing prior knowledge without explicit training. This parallels the functioning of the human brain, wherein Bayesian models are instrumental in explaining perceptual phenomena (Petzschner et al., 2015; Knill & Pouget, 2004). It is noteworthy that the present analysis focuses on behavioral aspects, while further investigation is required to elucidate the underlying mechanisms driving such behavior.

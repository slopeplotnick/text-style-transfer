When employing language models for evaluative tasks, it is generally advantageous for the model to articulate its reasoning process prior to providing a final response. This approach, referred to as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, enables the model to elucidate the rationale underlying its decision-making, thereby enhancing the accuracy and interpretability of its conclusions. The present study leverages these stepwise reasoning processes and investigates optimal strategies for aggregating the resulting outputs. Decision-making in this context involves different methodologies for utilizing computational resources effectively, particularly when multiple samples are generated. One prominent aggregation technique is Self-Consistency, which integrates diverse outputs by utilizing majority voting to identify the most representative decision. Variants of this method incorporate confidence levels, such as Soft Self-Consistency, which selects outputs based on confidence scores across various categories. Another approach, Confidence-Informed Self-Consistency, employs weighted voting informed by confidence scores, which may be derived either from the probability of each sequence or directly through model querying.  Additionally, certain methodologies utilize the model itself to consolidate results from multiple samples. For instance, Generative Self-Aggregation involves prompting the model to synthesize a new response by integrating information from several outputs, while Universal Self-Consistency tasks the model with selecting the most internally consistent response among the options presented. Empirical findings further indicate that leveraging computational resources during evaluation—particularly through mechanisms such as majority voting—can improve the performance of reasoning-based generative verifiers. These insights underscore the importance of methodological refinement in optimizing the utilization of language models for complex reasoning tasks.

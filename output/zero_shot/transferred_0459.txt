Currently, there is a significant concern regarding hallucinations in large language models (LLMs), necessitating extensive exploration in future research endeavors. One prospective approach involves the development of real-time systems that identify and rectify these errors during the model's operation, rather than addressing them post hoc. Implementing such measures could enhance the reliability of LLMs, particularly in high-stakes domains such as legal and medical documentation, where inaccuracies could have severe consequences. Additionally, there is ongoing exploration into the utilization of diverse data modalities, including images and audio alongside text, to improve model performance, as LLMs frequently encounter multiple media types and may exhibit errors when processing them. Efforts are also directed towards designing models that facilitate user-friendly identification and correction of errors. Research is being conducted on developing AI systems that offer intuitive user interaction, enabling users to pose inquiries and validate the model's responses. Furthermore, there is an imperative for comprehensive studies on mitigating hallucinations through formal methods, which can enhance model reliability when accuracy is paramount. This article examined prevalent instances of hallucinations within LLMs, such as factual inaccuracies and incomprehensibility resulting from insufficient information or ambiguous input. These errors can diminish user trust and hinder system usability, underscoring the importance of devising strategies to minimize their occurrence.

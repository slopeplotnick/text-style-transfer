In this study, extensive experiments were conducted utilizing UniFact across various datasets, model types such as LLaMA and Qwen, and methodologies for factual verification. These experiments yielded significant insights addressing our predefined research questions. Regarding Research Question 1, the findings indicate that there is no universally optimal approach; the efficacy is heavily contingent upon the specific model and task at hand. In relation to Research Question 2, substantial evidence emerged supporting the complementary nature of disparate verification methods. The Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV) techniques excel in identifying distinct types of factual inaccuracies, thereby capturing diverse yet crucial aspects of veracity. Concerning Research Question 3, our results demonstrated that integrating both HD and FV methods surpassed the performance of employing either approach in isolation, establishing a novel benchmark for identifying factual errors. This has dual implications: researchers are encouraged to pursue collaborative efforts rather than isolated endeavors, and practitioners should consider a hybrid approach of model-based and evidence-based verifications to ensure comprehensive factual accuracy.  In summary, this paper accomplishes several key objectives: it elucidates the distinctions between Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification and their relevance to advancements in artificial intelligence; it introduces UniFact as a framework for equitable comparison of HD and FV; it empirically demonstrates, through extensive studies, the distinct yet synergistic functions of HD and FV; and it substantiates the superiority of combining both methods over single-method applications. Furthermore, this paper clarifies the definitions of Fact Verification and Hallucination Detection, emphasizing the importance of differentiating these often conflated terms. Fact Verification pertains to the general truthfulness of text, whereas Hallucination Detection focuses on the factual accuracy of text generated by models. Detailed explanations of these concepts are provided in Sections 2.1 and 2.2, while their combined measurement is discussed in Section 2.3.

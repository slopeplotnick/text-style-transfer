We conducted a comparative analysis between the generated samples from our model and the EMOPIA MIDI dataset to evaluate the effectiveness of Story2MIDI in producing music aligned with emotional criteria. The ratio of major-key tracks was used as an indicator of musical positivity, whereas average note length and note velocity were employed as measures of musical energy. In terms of positivity, the generated samples displayed alignment with EMOPIA across three out of four quadrants. However, in Quadrant 2 (Q2), our model generated a disproportionately higher number of major-key tracks compared to EMOPIA. This discrepancy is likely attributable to the imbalance in the training dataset, which contains a larger number of major-key tracks relative to minor-key tracks, as well as the limited size of the fine-tuning dataset.   With respect to energy, the fine-tuned model successfully replicated the note-length patterns observed in EMOPIA, generating shorter notes in high-energy quadrants (Q1 and Q2) and longer notes in low-energy quadrants (Q3 and Q4). However, the differences in note velocity across quadrants were less discernible. This limitation is likely due to the simplification of MIDI velocity values in the REMI representation, which reduces the granularity of velocity categories and consequently obscures inter-quadrant differences. Listener evaluations showed partial alignment with the numerical data, indicating that the model captures some characteristics of energy and positivity. Nonetheless, while the model demonstrated greater accuracy in generating music reflective of energy, its performance in capturing positivity appeared less reliable and approached randomness. This limitation may be due to the overrepresentation of major-key tracks in the generated samples. Further incorporation of additional metrics may aid in diagnosing the challenges associated with the model's ability to appropriately generate positivity-related features.  In this study, we developed *Story2MIDI*, a transformer-based model designed to generate symbolic music conditioned on emotional text inputs. To achieve this, we constructed a text-music dataset and implemented an encoder-decoder architecture that maps text-based emotional inputs to corresponding musical features. The encoder was fine-tuned to generate distinct emotional representations based on varying levels of energy and positivity, while the decoder was initially pre-trained on a large symbolic music dataset and subsequently fine-tuned on a smaller, emotion-labeled dataset. This fine-tuning enabled the model to synthesize music that corresponded to specific emotional states. Comprehensive evaluations involving both listener feedback and quantitative metrics demonstrated that the model is effective in generating music with emotional relevance but also revealed several areas for improvement. Specifically, our findings highlight that while Story2MIDI excels in capturing energy-related features, its performance in generating accurate representations of positivity warrants further investigation.

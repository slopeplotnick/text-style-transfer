The issue under consideration is not immediately apparent when assessing an isolated model; rather, it emerges from the structural configuration and the dynamic interactions within the system, with implications for its safety, reliability, and accountability. We identify three potential sources that may elucidate the origins of this problem, albeit recognizing that these are not exhaustive and additional sources may become evident as further empirical investigations are conducted. Firstly, the manner in which agents communicate and exert mutual influence is considered. Secondly, the challenges associated with comprehending the cognitive or communicative intent of agents are examined. Thirdly, the divergence of agents' goals is scrutinized. These factors collectively provide a framework for predicting the manifestation of group-related risks. Each factor is amenable to empirical measurement and analysis within controlled experimental environments involving multiple agents.  The propagation of information within a network is a pivotal aspect of system dynamics. Highly interconnected systems facilitate rapid collaborative interactions, albeit with the concomitant risk of accelerated dissemination of erroneous information. By contrast, systems characterized by lower connectivity may mitigate the rapid transmission of errors but simultaneously hinder cooperative remedial efforts. The architecture of information distribution critically influences the potential for isolated inaccuracies to evolve into widely accepted misconceptions. Metrics such as the rate of error diffusion and the proportion of agents adopting hazardous behaviors can provide insights into these systemic dynamics.  Safety assessments for multi-agent systems ought to incorporate the configuration of information distribution as a central consideration. The concept of cognitive opacity pertains to the degree to which agents' cognitive processes are obscured or incomprehensible, either inadvertently or deliberately, as a consequence of goal-driven motives that obscure genuine reasoning. In such scenarios, an agent might uncritically accept the assertions of another, despite potential inaccuracies. The intent-opacity rate serves as a metric for evaluating the extent to which the rationale underlying specific outputs remains elusive.

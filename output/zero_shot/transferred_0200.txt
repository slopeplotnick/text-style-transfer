Curriculum learning constitutes a pedagogical approach for training models, wherein the instructional sequence progresses from simpler to more complex tasks. This concept was initially proposed by Bengio et al. in 2009 and has since been extensively applied in the development of large language models. Contemporary strategies, such as Speed-RL and methodologies devised by Kim and Lee, continue to employ this technique by organizing tasks in ascending order of difficulty. Similarly, the LBS3 method prioritizes training by beginning with less challenging questions and gradually advancing to more difficult ones. However, these approaches often fall short of embodying optimal curriculum design because they adhere to predetermined difficulty levels without adapting to the model's performance metrics. Effective curriculum learning necessitates dynamic adjustments contingent upon the model's progression.  The CAPO method exemplifies a system that evaluates the model’s performance and tailors its training regimen accordingly. This approach initiates training with positive feedback to establish stable behavioral patterns, subsequently introducing negative feedback to enhance learning efficacy. In contrast to other methodologies that rely on fixed difficulty levels, CAPO adapts as the model advances, ensuring stability in early learning phases and flexibility in later stages. CAPO represents an innovative model training paradigm that incorporates feedback to construct an adaptive curriculum. It begins with positive exemplars and transitions to a dual focus on positive and negative examples, akin to human learning processes, thereby mitigating issues associated with premature feedback integration. Empirical evaluations indicate that CAPO enhances model performance relative to alternative methods across various tasks and model scales. Furthermore, CAPO demonstrates robust applicability across diverse domains, ranging from mathematics to tasks involving heterogeneous data types. By prioritizing continuous model improvement over static difficulty levels, CAPO facilitates the development of learning algorithms that exhibit greater adaptability and are inspired by cognitive processes inherent in human learning.

**Training Dynamics Analysis.** In Figure 3, we examine the reward and entropy trajectories of the 7B model under CAPO and GRPO. Initially, both methods demonstrate similar rates of improvement; however, CAPO consistently outperforms GRPO once the model reaches a stable imitation phase. The gray vertical line denotes the transition from imitation to generation, marking the onset of independent solution synthesis. Beyond this point, CAPO's entropy continues to rise steadily, in contrast to the largely stagnant behavior observed under GRPO, while reward performance also improves. This suggests that CAPO not only secures stronger reward gains in the later phase but also sustains elevated entropy levels—a trait associated with more diverse and exploratory reasoning. Moreover, this gradual increase in unpredictability indicates that CAPO avoids the entropy collapse often induced by prematurely introducing negative samples. By deferring their integration, CAPO preserves the stability of the imitation phase and leverages negative feedback more effectively in the subsequent stage, thereby enhancing generalization across diverse tasks. **Comparing Static and Dynamic Curriculum Strategies.** We compare the traditional static instructional paradigm with our dynamic advantage-based training strategy. In the static setting, sample difficulty is estimated based on model performance, and training proceeds according to this fixed ordering. In contrast, our dynamic method adaptively shifts focus during training without requiring any data reordering. As shown in Table 3, both ADARFT and the static curriculum GRPO(+SC) yield only marginal improvements over the baseline GRPO. By comparison, our dynamic approach, GRPO(+Ours), delivers substantially stronger results across most tasks. This highlights that merely estimating difficulty is insufficient; instead, dynamically adjusting the training signal leads to a more effective curriculum.

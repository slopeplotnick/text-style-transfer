Traditional MRTA approaches have achieved success across diverse domains through the use of optimization algorithms, including linear programming methods, genetic algorithms, and Reinforcement Learning (RL), all offering mathematical rigor and proven performance guarantees (Kuhn 1955, Atay 2006, Jones 2020). However, construction environments introduce fundamentally different challenges that render traditional optimization methods less effective, as the unstructured and dynamic nature of construction sites creates significant coordination difficulties. Moreover, the sheer complexity and volume of information in construction contexts exceed the reasoning capacity of mathematical optimization techniques. Therefore, the limitations of traditional approaches have underscored the need for more adaptive coordination solutions. Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate notable capabilities in natural language understanding, logical reasoning, and adaptive decision-making, directly addressing several limitations of both traditional optimization and learning-based approaches. Furthermore, LLM-based coordination methods enable intuitive human-robot interaction and provide adaptive strategies well-suited to the evolving and complex demands typical of construction environments. Recent LLM frameworks have shown considerable promise in areas such as task planning, digital twin integration, and the development of multi-agent systems (Kannan et al., 2025). These innovations are especially relevant today, as multi-agent frameworks continue to gain importance across both academic and industrial domains (He et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024). However, despite these advances, existing configurations lack systematic mechanisms for performance validation and benchmarking against conventional methods. Consequently, a critical gap remains: while LLM-based coordination shows significant potential, no existing research has introduced iterative retry and self-correction mechanisms to ensure reasoning reliability, nor conducted rigorous benchmarking to compare LLM performance with traditional optimization approaches in construction task allocation. This study addresses this gap by proposing the LangGraph based Task Allocation Agent (LTAA) framework, a novel LLM-driven coordination approach that incorporates phase-adaptive strategies and multi-stage validation mechanisms absent from prior work. The proposed strategy effectively achieves workload balance without substantial performance degradation, while the multi-stage validation system ensures reasoning consistency by systematically detecting and correcting allocation discrepancies through structured feedback loops.

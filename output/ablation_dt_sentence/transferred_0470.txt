Energy consumption is a growing concern in several fields, from mobile devices to large data centers. Developers need detailed information on the energy consumption of their software to mitigate consumption issues. Traditionally, examinations have had a broader focus, such as on specific functions or complete programs, rather than on scrutinizing individual lines of source code. Additionally, previous approaches predominantly estimated the CPU's energy consumption while neglecting other hardware components, which limited their statistical reasoning and explainability. To address these challenges, we propose a novel methodology that expands beyond the CPU and avoids speculative approaches. Our investigation centers on predicting the energy usage of statically typed JVM-based programming languages, such as Java, Kotlin, and Scala. We measure energy consumption by examining the translation from a specific programming language's source code into Java bytecode and quantifying the associated energy consumption. With these measurements, we develop a statistical model employing Bayesian statistics, enabling the prediction of expected energy consumption through a statistical distribution and facilitating the analysis of individual contributing factors. These factors include data size, data type, and operation, all extracted from the source code, as well as the device on which the code executes. To validate our methodology, we implemented it using the Java programming language, focusing on Java's bytecode patterns, and evaluated its predictions on newly developed programs. Our analysis demonstrates that all four factors are significant, particularly the finding that devices of the same model may exhibit considerable variations in energy consumption, and that operations and data types substantially influence energy usage. Our experiments indicate that the energy predictions generated closely match the actual energy consumption of programs, thus validating our approach. Our research introduces a methodology for constructing an energy model that future work, such as energy-aware program analysis or verification tools, can use to assess the energy efficiency of software applications.

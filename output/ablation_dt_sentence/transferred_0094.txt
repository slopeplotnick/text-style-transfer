The performance of CFAULTs and HSD is evaluated in this context, with Figure 6a presenting a cactus plot that compares CPU time (y-axis) against the number of successfully localized circuits (x-axis) for CFAULTs, HSD, and HSD-CM on the augmented ISCAS85 benchmark. The results confirm that HSD consistently achieves faster performance overall, localizing faults in a greater number of circuits, as indicated by the findings in Table 5. Additional insights are provided by the scatter plots in Figures 6b, 6c, and 6d, which compare the CPU times of CFAULTs and HSD. Specifically, in Figure 6b, each point represents a faulty circuit, with the x-value corresponding to the time taken by HSD and the y-value to the time taken by CFAULTs to localize faults in that circuit. Points above the diagonal indicate cases where CFAULTs required more time than HSD to compute a valid diagnosis. These plots clearly demonstrate that, whether or not core minimisation is applied, HSD consistently outperforms CFAULTs in terms of CPU time. In the evaluation of buggy C software using the TCAS and C-PACK-IPAs benchmarks, CFAULTs displayed clear advantages over competing FBFL methods. Despite encoding all failing test cases into a single MaxSAT formula, CFAULTs consistently outperformed the other approaches, offering both superior speed and a higher fault localization rate. Taken together, these findings suggest that, among the FBFL methods considered, CFAULTs is the fastest and most reliable for C software, while for Boolean circuits, HSD remains the state-of-the-art approach. One factor explaining this performance disparity is the structural difference between fault localization in C programs and Boolean circuits, which results in MaxSAT encodings with significantly different formula structures. This distinction has a considerable impact on solver efficiency, thereby contributing to the observed performance difference across the two domains.

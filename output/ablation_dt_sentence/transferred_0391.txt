To effectively optimize multilingual models for diverse tasks, we leverage the intrinsic structural relationships among languages, thereby facilitating seamless information sharing across languages. When encoded in the representational space of multilingual transformer models like XLM-RoBERTa (XLM-R), these relationships cause languages that share semantic, structural, or orthographic similarities to cluster together. We begin by examining the 100-language configuration of the XLM-R model, which spans a wide range of both high and low-resource languages. Rather than relying on supervised data for each individual language, we propose a clustering-based selection mechanism that enables training on a small subset of widely spoken languages, while facilitating generalization to others. This approach allows for efficient cross-lingual knowledge transfer by focusing on high-resource languages, thus eliminating the need to train on low-resource languages. Building on this strategy, we translated the MultiJail (2024) dataset into each of the 100 target languages using state-of-the-art machine translation systems tailored to the specific needs of each language. For high-resource languages, we employ advanced models such as GPT-4o, M2MBart-50, and Helsinki-NLP OpusMT, recognized for their superior performance in multilingual tasks. However, for low-resource languages, existing models often exhibit suboptimal translation performance. To address this challenge, we utilize SarvamTranslate (AI 2025) for Indic languages, which has proven effective even for rare linguistic varieties. For other underrepresented languages, we use GPT-4.1 to enhance translation accuracy. To ensure the reliability of these translations, we perform cross-validation of a subset using the Google Translate API, with a particular focus on low-resource languages. Through the integration of these models, we are able to produce fluent, high-quality, and accurate translations.

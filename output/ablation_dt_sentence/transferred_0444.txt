In AI systems where various components collaborate to manage complex tasks, large language models (LLMs) play a critical role in influencing subsequent decision-making processes. However, if there is a predisposition towards prioritizing initial inputs, it may disrupt the integrity of the entire system. Therefore, addressing this bias is crucial to ensure these systems operate equitably and transparently. This study is subject to two primary limitations. First, although the text length of each article was controlled, we did not explicitly assess the extent to which evaluation metrics are influenced by document length. Since the data length might influence the outcomes of these metrics, subsequent investigations could categorize data according to length or conduct experiments where length is controlled, facilitating a more comprehensive understanding of information sequencing's significance. Moreover, the scores from evaluation metrics were comparatively low across the board, underscoring a significant limitation inherent in reference-free evaluation methodologies. This is particularly true in abstractive summarization, where juxtaposing extensive source texts with their condensed summaries proves arduous. Hence, subsequent research should expand the scope of inquiry beyond domains like abortion to include other politically and socially contentious subjects, such as gun control, LGBTQ+ rights, and immigration. This will facilitate evaluation of whether documented order effects are consistent across diverse issues. Additionally, evaluating various LLMs, like GPT or Claude, would help ascertain whether bias is evident across different models. Incorporating additional evaluation metrics, such as assessments of bias in opinions and polarization within summaries, could further enhance our understanding of how information sequencing influences LLM-generated summaries' characteristics. The study indicates a propensity for LLMs to prioritize the first document encountered during the process of summarizing multiple sources, with a notable emphasis on semantic interpretation. These observations corroborate prior research suggesting that information sequencing significantly influences the resultant output. Consequently, sensitivity to document order represents a potential challenge for systems such as AI summaries or agentic AI configurations, whereby initial documents encountered may exert undue influence on subsequent processes. Thus, addressing this bias is imperative to develop systems that are equitable, transparent, and dependable.

Our findings suggest that individuals' attitudes toward AI play a significant role in shaping their perceptions of music, extending beyond aesthetic qualities to encompass deeper ethical and functional considerations. Therefore, future research should focus on developing tools specifically designed for evaluating AI-generated music, as well as creating multi-factor rating scales to better capture its nuances. With AI-generated music becoming increasingly prevalent on streaming platforms, it is essential to address socioeconomic questions surrounding fairness and transparency in its production and distribution. Given the impact of attitudes toward AI on individuals' preferences and emotional responses to music, experimental designs manipulating information about the creation and dissemination of AI music could be particularly insightful. For example, transparency regarding the data used for AI training or the distribution of revenue from AI-produced music could be tested as experimental conditions. Additionally, further research should examine how variations in production modalities and application contexts influence the perception of AI-generated music. The growing interest in collaborative music creation between humans and AI systems highlights the need for deeper exploration, particularly as current text-to-music technologies enable more opportunities for co-creation. Expanding on the work of Moura and Maw (2021), it is also valuable to investigate how individuals perceive and utilize AI-generated music in practical contexts. Our research indicates that attitudes toward AI are more influential in shaping evaluations of AI-generated music than factors such as the identity of the creator, genre, or listening habits. As attitudes toward AI continue to evolve, it will be important to track these shifts, as they are likely to influence broader cultural acceptance or resistance to AI applications in music. Ultimately, the key issue transcends whether AI-generated music can be enjoyed or recognized as human-like; it concerns deeper questions about what constitutes "authentic" and "valuable" creativity. By critically examining audience perspectives, we can explore what types of creativity, artificial or otherwise, deserve recognition and the terms under which they should be acknowledged.

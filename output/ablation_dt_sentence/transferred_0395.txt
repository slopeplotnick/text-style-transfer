To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to systematically leverage an open-vocabulary object detector to enhance the ability of MLLMs to comprehend high-resolution images. Experiments validate that the detector accurately localizes targets while effectively suppressing interference from irrelevant regions. We propose MRD, a training-free and generic framework that innovatively addresses semantic similarity through a multi-resolution fusion strategy. By integrating open-set detection results, MRD enhances target regions, creating a synergistic effect. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and generalization of our method, which achieves leading performance on both single-object and multi-object tasks across various MLLMs and high-resolution benchmarks. To improve high-resolution image understanding without proportionally increasing the computational complexity of visual tokens, several studies have incorporated high-resolution visual encoders into MLLMs. For instance, Vary and Deepseek-VL integrate the SAM encoder to enhance model performance on high-resolution images. Alternatively, other approaches have introduced Native/Dynamic-Resolution MLLMs, which process images at their native resolution. The key idea is to generate a variable-length sequence of visual tokens that adapts to the original dimensions of the image, preserving spatial accuracy and high-frequency details. These models employ various mechanisms to address the challenges posed by long sequences and computational complexity, including sliding window attention, dynamic masking, and patch-based encoding strategies. Significant progress has been made in this area, as demonstrated by representative works. For example, the InternVL series partitions high-resolution images into multiple fixed-size patches. In contrast, models like Qwen2.5-VL train a ViT directly on native-resolution images, enabling the model to process the entire image as a unified token sequence in a single forward pass, potentially leading to better global context understanding.

The utilization of computers is increasingly enhanced owing to the advent of novel tools and systems. These innovations empower computers with autonomous operation and comprehension. Recent advancements have resulted in open-source platforms that facilitate the integration of modular components, such as planning algorithms and memory architectures, thereby streamlining the development process. Empirical evidence from corporate studies demonstrates the practical applications of these systems in operational settings. Notably, several studies suggest that merely increasing the size of models does not necessarily enhance their reliability. Consequently, there is a growing emphasis on evaluating the operational efficacy of these systems to ensure continuous improvement. However, assessing these systems poses challenges due to the increasing complexity of the tasks they undertake. Several studies have explored the implementation of specialized systems to aid in evaluations; nevertheless, concerns regarding potential biases persist. To address these, it has been proposed to employ multiple evaluators to ensure equitable assessment across diverse tasks. Our project methodology involves deploying dual evaluators, each tasked with examining distinct components of the task, ensuring the authenticity of the observed improvements. Our dataset comprises 100 real-world editing instructions encapsulated within slide presentations. Each task includes an initial slide deck, a target slide deck, a set of instructions, and guidelines outlining criteria for evaluating both layout and content. The tasks are systematically categorized into 16 groups to ensure comprehensive assessment of both comprehension and intricate formatting. Data have been aggregated from a corpus of over 15,000 PowerPoint presentations sourced from diverse origins and converted into a format that delineates both layout and content specifics. The selected examples encompass a diverse array of stylistic approaches and are integrated with contributions from various specialists. Through extensive evaluation, we systematically identified 100 examples from over 500 potential selections, including contributions from students, ensuring representation across diverse professional and academic fields and including challenging exemplars within each category.

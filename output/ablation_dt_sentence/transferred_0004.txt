Experiments demonstrate that ReVSeg sets a new state-of-the-art (SOTA) benchmark for video object segmentation (VOS), outperforming both latent-based models and training-free approaches, while also offering auditable reasoning traces. As shown in Figure 1, ablation studies reveal that both the baseline model and its reinforcement learning (RL)-trained variant significantly underperform on complex tasks when the reasoning chain is omitted. In contrast, our framework achieves robust performance even without RL, with substantial improvements observed upon incorporating RL. Our key contributions are as follows: 1) We present a principled decomposition of reasoning for VOS, introducing ReVSeg, which reformulates the task as an explicit, multi-step reasoning chain based on native VLM primitives. 2) We propose a novel RL-based framework that optimizes this reasoning chain directly, enhancing decision-making without requiring additional supervision. 3) We establish a new SOTA for VOS, while ensuring that the reasoning process remains interpretable. Recent advancements in text-guided VOS, such as fine-tuned models and referring-video pipelines, have improved performance in specialized tasks [3, 5, 29, 45]. However, handling more complex scenarios—such as videos with similar objects, occlusions, fast motion, and long-range context—remains a challenge. Recent methods, like CoT-RVS [13], attempt to address these issues by adopting a dual-system architecture that processes frames independently, linking them via text representations. While effective to some extent, this approach is limited by the lack of integration between the systems and the absence of a training component. In this work, we reframe reasoning VOS as a systematic, step-by-step process embedded within a unified framework, leveraging reinforcement learning to iteratively refine performance.

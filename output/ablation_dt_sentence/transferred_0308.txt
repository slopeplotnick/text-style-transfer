We find that when individuals exhibit strong belief entrenchment, they are more prone to systematic errors in domains with verifiable ground truth (e.g., predictive tasks), which leads to a degradation in reasoning accuracy. This suggests that the Martingale Score can serve as a proxy for reasoning quality, even in situations where complete information is still unavailable or unfolding. In the context of language models, assessing Bayesian rationality involves evaluating their ability to update beliefs based on evidence in a manner consistent with Bayes's theorem. Gupta et al. (2025) demonstrated that language models can update their beliefs after a large number of coin flips, even when their prior beliefs are biased. However, in more realistic scenarios involving complex language, Qiu et al. (2025a) observed that language models fail to adjust their beliefs regarding user preferences in multi-round interactions, contrary to the expectations of the Bayesian framework. Similarly, Falck et al. (2024) falsified the hypothesis that language models engage in Bayesian learning when provided with contextual information. Zhao et al. (2021) and Wang et al. (2023) also showed that large language models are sensitive to the arrangement of examples within prompts. Various approaches have been proposed to make learning and reasoning processes more Bayesian in nature. These include mimicking an ideal Bayesian reasoner, employing abstract reasoning, and combining abduction and deduction. However, these strategies often require auxiliary frameworks, such as Bayesian networks, which limit their practical applicability. Additionally, language models are susceptible to a range of cognitive biases due to their training on large human data sets (Echterhoff et al., 2024). They also exhibit biases distinct from human ones when deployed as judges, such as positional bias (favoring specific answer positions), verbosity bias (preferring longer responses), sentiment bias (favoring positive statements), and certainty bias (overweighting confident assertions). Among these, confirmation bias is of particular concern, as it is believed to drive polarization. Furthermore, it is hypothesized that many cognitive biases are variations of confirmation bias. Therefore, we focus on confirmation bias and utilize belief entrenchment to operationalize it in the context of language models.

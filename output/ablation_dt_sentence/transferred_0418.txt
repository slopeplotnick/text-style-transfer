Moreover, Galhotra et al. [2021] engage in a discussion on analytically bounding probabilities when a fully specified structural causal model is unavailable. This work further explores counterfactual intervals for explainable AI (XAI) by considering specific models to calculate intervals for counterfactual queries. By focusing on well-defined subgroups of models in the absence of complete information, this approach potentially reduces intervals compared to purely computational methods. Additionally, probabilities of sufficiency and necessity are considered for evaluating XAI methods. In a related vein, Mothilal et al. [2021] define the ideal model explanation based on necessity and sufficiency, positing that the explanation is a minimal subset of features both necessary and sufficient for a model outcome in a given context. With the assumption that only the input features, which are independent, serve as the set of variables for explanation, various types of XAI explanations are compared. The probability of necessity is linked to contrastive explanations, where features altered are seen as necessary causes for the original model output. Sufficiency relates to feature attribution explanations, where highly contributing features across contexts are deemed sufficient, likely causing the output irrespective of other feature values. The experiments highlight substantial variation across methods in practice. Similarly, Pawar et al. [2024], operating under the assumption of feature independence, discuss the evaluation of existing XAI methods against criteria of sufficiency and necessity and extend this discussion to include different approaches to neighborhood generation as the basis for explanation. Beckers [2022] considers a related setting where the model being explained predicts outcomes of actions and is thus assumed to be causally meaningful. In this scenario, both sufficient explanations and counterfactual explanations are defined under the presumption that the model consistently predicts the correct action outcome. The types of explanations are further differentiated by assessing degrees of causal influence to identify actual causes underlying the explanations.

We find that high task accuracy does not always imply effective integration of heterogeneous information sources (see middle panel of Figure 8). For example, GPT-5 Mini achieves a very low error rate but fails to integrate multiple input modalities as efficiently as other models. This issue is most pronounced when there is a significant imbalance in the strength of one input relative to another. In such cases, optimal behavior would require models to place less weight on the weaker modality, yet this is not consistently observed. This discrepancy has direct practical implications, as it suggests that benchmarks focusing solely on accuracy may inadvertently favor models with reduced robustness to noise. Conversely, Llama-4 Maverick surpasses the Bayesian reliability-weighted linear fusion baseline, highlighting its use of more sophisticated non-linear integration strategies. In fact, a non-linear random forest model captures the cue combination more effectively than linear alternatives. When comparing unimodal and multimodal performance, we observe that models such as Llama-4 Maverick, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o utilize the additional modality of input to achieve lower error rates in the majority of multimodal tasks involving both text and image modalities. However, this advantage is not universally observed, and the variability in performance gains suggests there is substantial room for improvement in multimodal model optimization. To assess behavioral features beyond task accuracy, we introduce the Bayesian Consistency Score (BCS), which measures how models adjust their behavior in line with Bayesian principles. This allows for a more holistic evaluation of model performance, even in cases where accuracy has reached its limit. By manipulating specific conditions, we can evaluate model behavior across varying baseline performance levels, offering further insights into their information processing strategies. While more complex heuristics, such as salience-based weighting or rule-switching strategies, may better fit static response patterns, it is more insightful to focus on Bayes-consistent behavior under dynamic conditions, as predicted by a Bayesian observer model.

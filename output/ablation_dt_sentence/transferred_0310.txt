Auditors rely on Journal Entry Tests (JETs) to detect anomalies in tax-related ledger records, but these rule-based methods often generate a substantial number of false positives and struggle with identifying subtle irregularities. To address this, we investigate whether large language models (LLMs) can serve as more effective anomaly detectors in double-entry bookkeeping. By benchmarking state-of-the-art LLMs, such as LLaMA and Gemma, on both synthetic and real-world anonymized ledgers, we compare their performance to that of JETs and conventional machine learning baselines. Our results demonstrate that LLMs consistently outperform traditional rule-based JETs and classical ML approaches, offering the added benefit of natural-language explanations that enhance interpretability. This suggests a promising future for AI-augmented auditing, where human auditors collaborate with advanced models to strengthen financial integrity. Financial auditors play a crucial role in detecting unusual transactions and anomalies that could signal errors, fraud, or tax evasion in a company's financial records. Ensuring that such irregularities are identified is vital for maintaining trust in financial statements. However, auditing large volumes of accounting data is challenging, as current manual and rule-based processes are inefficient and prone to errors, often causing auditors to miss critical red flags. Standard Journal Entry Tests (JETs), which rely on predefined rules and known patterns, tend to flag an overwhelming number of transactions. These tests capture only anticipated anomalies, flooding auditors with alerts, many of which are false positives requiring time-consuming review. In practice, auditors still rely heavily on sampling and human labor, which poses the risk of overlooking subtle irregularities in today's era of massive, complex datasets. This gap between growing data complexity and the limitations of traditional audit techniques underscores the need for more intelligent, automated anomaly detection tools in auditing.

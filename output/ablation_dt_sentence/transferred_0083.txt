Editing portrait videos involving human subjects presents a uniquely complex challenge, requiring precise yet flexible modifications—such as changes to appearance, expressions, or the insertion of objects—while maintaining temporal coherence with the original footage. A central difficulty lies in ensuring that every edited frame remains synchronized with the subject’s behavioral and temporal dynamics from the source video. We introduce SyncLoRA, a method designed to perform high-fidelity edits on portrait videos while preserving frame-accurate synchronization and identity consistency. Our approach leverages a diffusion-based image-to-video model, where the edit is applied to the first frame and then propagated across the video sequence. To facilitate temporal alignment, SyncLoRA is trained on paired videos that share identical motion trajectories but differ in appearance. These training pairs are automatically selected through a synchronization-based filtering process that retains only those with sufficient temporal alignment. This setup enables the model to learn how to integrate motion cues from the original footage with the visual changes defined in the edited first frame. Trained on a compact and carefully curated dataset of human video portraits, SyncLoRA demonstrates strong generalization to unseen identities and a wide variety of edits, including changes in appearance, object insertion, and background replacement. It remains robust to pose and expression variations. Our results confirm that SyncLoRA delivers visually coherent edits that are tightly synchronized with the source video, effectively balancing visual fidelity and temporal consistency. This capability is especially valuable in domains such as advertising, film, gaming, and interactive media, where nuanced control over visual content is crucial.

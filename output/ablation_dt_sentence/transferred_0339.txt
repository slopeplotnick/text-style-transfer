**EMOPIA.** Research in Affective Music Generation continues to lack large-scale, human-labeled symbolic music datasets. Most existing datasets are relatively small, containing fewer than 200 MIDI files paired with emotion labels [32], [33]. In this study, we employ the largest available dataset, EMOPIA [31], which comprises 1,087 music clips extracted from 387 songs, with emotion annotations provided by four human annotators. The dataset includes four distinct emotion labels, each corresponding to a specific quadrant in the valence–arousal (AV) plane. More details about EMOPIA are provided in Appendix B. **GiantMIDI-Piano.** Another dataset utilized in this study is GiantMIDI-Piano [34], which contains 10,855 solo piano works composed by 2,786 different composers. It has been used extensively in areas such as classical music analysis and symbolic music generation. **Emotion Mapping.** To align our text data with EMOPIA files, we categorized the 27 emotion labels into four AV quadrants. For this purpose, we used the NRC VAD lexicon [35], which provides human-annotated valence and arousal scores for 55,000 words. Using this lexicon, we assigned each emotion to one of the four AV categories (constructing GoEmotionsAV). The resulting mappings and their corresponding positions on the AV plane are shown in Figure 1. Additional details on the VAD and its integration with EMOPIA can be found in Appendix A. **MIDI Representation.** We use the Musical Instrument Digital Interface (MIDI) format to represent and generate symbolic music data. MIDI is both compact and expressive, capable of encoding performance-level nuances that convey emotion-related aspects of music. Moreover, the wide availability of MIDI files makes them a valuable resource for training. In our training pipeline, we treat each MIDI file as a sequence of discrete musical events, enabling the effective application of sequence-to-sequence models for learning and music generation.

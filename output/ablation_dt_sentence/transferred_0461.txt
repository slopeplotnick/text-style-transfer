Future Work: Due to resource constraints, we have not systematically studied belief entrenchment in reinforced reasoning, despite the methodology's rising popularity in LLM reasoning. Furthermore, the violation of ideal Bayesian rationality can be observed when individuals engage irrationally with both their internal reasoning processes and external evidence. However, our focus was primarily directed towards the former aspect. Future research could incorporate an "evidence searching" component to assess the extent to which LLMs adjust their beliefs in light of new external evidence, rather than simply adhering to pre-existing beliefs. A significant challenge lies in ensuring that this evidence retrieval does not grant LLMs direct access to verifiable facts, even in cases where such facts are accessible. In domains where truth is established, such as forecasting, we have demonstrated the impact of belief entrenchment on accuracy. Therefore, future research may test the efficacy of the Martingale Score within open-ended domains by exploring additional ramifications of belief entrenchment. Moreover, the applicability of the Martingale Score as a general-purpose evaluative measure for reasoning can be extended to assess phenomena such as sycophancy, group conformity, and inverse scaling. The Martingale Score could also be utilized as a training objective to test its robustness and applicability. Notably, implementing martingale training that reduces both the Martingale Score and the Brier score provides compelling evidence that the Martingale Score is capable of evaluating reasoning processes and holds applicability beyond forecasting. Thus, future avenues of research could involve utilizing the Martingale Score to quantify and mitigate belief entrenchment or polarization within human-AI systems, such as recommender systems.

Based on the resulting clusters (detailed in Figure 2), we designate a subset of languages as In-Domain (ID) languages. We evaluate our model on both In-Domain and Out-of-Domain (OOD) languages to assess its generalization capabilities. Specifically, 11 low-resource languages are selected for the Out-of-Domain set. These languages are carefully chosen to serve as strong representatives of their respective clusters, ensuring comprehensive coverage across diverse linguistic categories. For evaluation, we benchmark our model on six safety classification datasets: Aegis-Content-Safety-2.0-Test (Aegis-CS2) (2025), HarmBench (Mazeika et al., 2024), Redteam2k (Luo et al., 2024), JBB-Behaviors (Chao et al., 2024)—comprising the Behaviors subset (JBB-Behav) and the Judge-comparison subset (JBB-Judge)—and StrongReject (Souly et al., 2024). Collectively, these benchmarks span a broad range of harm categories, including but not limited to Hate/Identity Hate, Sexual, Suicide/Self-Harm, Violence, Guns/Illegal Weapons, PII/Privacy, Sexual Minor, Toxicity, and Abuse, thereby providing a robust foundation for evaluating safety interventions. **Training Configuration.** For our multilingual safety guardrail, we adopt the XLM-RoBERTa-Base (279M parameters) and XLM-RoBERTa-Large (560M parameters) models as the backbone encoders. These models provide rich cross-lingual representations and are pre-trained on 100 languages, making them well-suited to our generalization objectives. We append a single-layer classification head to the encoder, aligned with the encoder's hidden size, and use a binary output layer. Full-weight training is performed on both the pretrained encoder and the classification head using the combined training dataset. All training and evaluation are conducted on an NVIDIA H100 GPU cluster using Bfloat16 precision for improved efficiency and performance. **Baselines.** For comparative analysis, we benchmark our model against both smaller guardrails—DuoGuard-0.5B (2025), PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen-Smol, and WalledGuard-C (2024)—and larger guardrails—PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen (2025), LlamaGuard3 (2024), and Aegis-Defensive (2025). These baselines vary in language coverage, ranging from monolingual English models to multilingual systems supporting up to 29 languages. This diversity enables a thorough comparison of the scalability, effectiveness, and robustness of our safety guardrail across both high-resource and low-resource language contexts.

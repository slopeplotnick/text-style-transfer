The primary objective of explainable AI (XAI) is to elucidate the rationale behind a model's output. However, the motivations for providing such explanations can vary. These motivations include evaluating the model’s performance, fostering user trust in the decision-making process, and aiding users in understanding how a decision can be changed when it is not permanent. The latter case, in particular, requires a specialized explanation, where the explanation vocabulary is constrained by actionability—whether a variable can feasibly be modified in the current world state. When the goal is to change a model's decision through actionable insights, this is referred to as algorithmic recourse. While it requires reliable causal modeling to accurately predict the results of proposed interventions, the focus is solely on the outcome after these changes have been implemented. The type of explanation discussed in this paper is intended to be general, with no predefined explainee guiding the explanation process, nor any assumptions made about the model’s performance or deployment stage. This ensures that the explanations remain faithful to the model, even when it may not behave as intended or expected. Furthermore, the framework allows for counterfactual reasoning about retrospective interventions on all variables, with no requirement for actionability. Although the framework discussed here could be adapted for algorithmic recourse with some adjustments, the exploration of such adaptations is beyond the scope of this paper. Concept-based explanations prioritize the use of concepts, rather than data features, as their vocabulary. While multiple definitions of concepts exist in XAI, the concept referred to here aligns with symbolic concepts, as identified by Poeta et al. (2025). These concepts correspond to high-level attributes or characteristics of the data, with meaning that is interpretable by humans.

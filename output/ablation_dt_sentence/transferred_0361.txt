In this experiment, we explicitly swapped different models for Stage 1 and Stage 2 during sampling while maintaining a fixed total number of function evaluations. Replacing the Stage 1 model with a smaller-capacity variant resulted in minimal impact on gFID, indicating that coarse layout prediction under near-prior noise remains largely unaffected by model size. In contrast, when a model with reduced capacity was employed for Stage 2, the quality of the generated outputs significantly declined, highlighting that refinement of high-frequency details greatly benefits from increased model capacity and training. Building on the two-stage framework discussed earlier, we now explore several established practices through the lens of navigation and refinement. Our aim is to clarify the mechanisms driving the effectiveness of these methods and how their outcomes can be better understood and optimized by considering the distinct roles of these two stages. We then investigate how the choice of latent space influences model dynamics within this two-stage context. Figure 9 compares the variation in oracle loss dynamics during training when utilizing VA-VAE and SD-VAE latent spaces. In the navigation stage, both spaces exhibit a steady increase in oracle loss, suggesting that the growing difficulty of coarse layout prediction is independent of the latent space choice. However, in the refinement stage, the loss patterns diverge significantly. While the absolute magnitudes are not directly comparable, the shapes of the oracle loss curves reveal key trends. For VA-VAE, whose latent space is aligned with semantic structure through DINO-based VF loss, the oracle loss gradually converges to a smooth, parabola-like shape as training progresses, indicating a well-structured latent manifold with coherent semantic modes. In contrast, SD-VAE, primarily trained for low-level reconstruction, results in a flatter, wavier curve, suggesting a less structured latent manifold and difficulty in maintaining consistent denoising performance across stages. Overall, latent spaces that align strongly with high-level concepts are preferred for diffusion modeling, as such alignment fosters clearer mode organization under Gaussian marginals, thereby facilitating both navigation and refinement.

Stock price prediction remains a persistent challenge due to the inherent volatility of markets and their sensitivity to real-time events. The rise of large language models (LLMs) has introduced innovative methods for stock price forecasting by analyzing textual data. While LLMs demonstrate impressive text comprehension capabilities, their successful application in complex real-world tasks often necessitates the use of specialized memory systems that ensure the stable and consistent utilization of historical information. Substantial research efforts have been focused on developing such memory systems. However, these architectures face significant hurdles when dealing with the vast, noisy nature of online news data: the text often lacks direct explanations for price fluctuations and is frequently cluttered with irrelevant information. The central challenge, therefore, lies in the identification and extraction of pertinent details and the underlying logic from unstructured text that influence stock price movements. To tackle this issue, this study proposes a novel memory system with two distinct layers. Initially, the framework classifies events using two approaches: consolidating daily event data to create a comprehensive overview and tracking the evolution of events over time. This process constructs a knowledge base that captures shifts in market expectations over time. Next, the framework examines the relationship between event patterns and stock price fluctuations, building a second knowledge base that focuses on this interconnection. These stages together enable the system to autonomously acquire and structure knowledge. The system operates through two primary phases: identification and analysis. During identification, the model uses recent events as semantic queries to retrieve historically similar scenarios from its memory network, thus aiding in more informed decision-making. In the analysis phase, the model integrates the current event sequence, new information, and historical examples to generate predictions about stock price movements. Empirical evaluations confirm that this system outperforms previous models in price prediction while offering greater interpretability. Its ability to link events directly with price changes and provide clear explanations for these connections significantly enhances its performance.

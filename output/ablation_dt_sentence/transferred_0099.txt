We investigated the impact of structured climate data on retrieval-augmented generation. Although we initially hypothesized that integrating this data would enhance both the accuracy and interpretability of generated answers, our findings suggest that its effectiveness is still in the early stages. As shown in Table 1, the DATA_RAG setup, which combines literature and data, underperforms the RAG setup, which relies solely on literature, across most metrics, with accuracy experiencing the most significant decline. This indicates that the current model is limited in its ability to leverage structured climate data to substantiate or enhance textual reasoning. CAIRNS, a question-answering system designed for agricultural climate adaptation, balances readability, accuracy, and transparency in source citation. It incorporates structured prompting (SGP), multi-step reasoning, and a consistency-weighted Hybrid Evaluator to support coherent and reliable inferences. Experimental results reveal that SGP significantly enhances structural coherence and citation reliability while maintaining factual accuracy, representing a substantial advancement in aligning QA outputs with the communication preferences of domain researchers. However, at this stage, data-augmented retrieval remains relatively limited in its effectiveness. By utilizing inter-model agreement as a weighting signal, the Hybrid Evaluator achieves superior performance among automated evaluation methods without the need for fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. This approach is particularly advantageous in question-answering scenarios with constrained resources or limited access to domain experts. Looking ahead, we aim to enhance location-specific reasoning by incorporating detailed geographic data, such as latitude and longitude, which would improve both the precision and applicability of our model in diverse geographical contexts. This would facilitate document transferability across regions with comparable climatic conditions and help generalize literature-derived insights. Additionally, we plan to develop an expanded evaluation benchmark that encompasses a broader range of question types and topics. This will facilitate the calibration of the Hybrid Evaluator and provide valuable training data for future domain-specific models, thereby advancing reliable and reproducible QA research in climate adaptation.

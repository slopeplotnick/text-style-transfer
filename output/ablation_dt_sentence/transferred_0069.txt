We conducted a comparison of the performance of our method against RePro, utilizing results reported in the original paper due to the unavailability of their code. P2C denotes Paper2Code. Refine, in contrast, necessitates the manual creation of distinct prompts for the evaluation and enhancement of each component of the process—resulting in 8 handcrafted prompts for the 4 planning tasks in the original Paper2Code framework. Our approach, however, obviates the need for manual prompt engineering by directly leveraging task-specific system prompts, thereby eliminating the requirement for additional prompt design. We evaluated our methodology on two distinct benchmarks. The first, Paper2CodeBench, consists of 90 papers from leading 2024 conferences (ICLR, ICML, NeurIPS), selected based on code availability and document length. These papers serve as a representative starting point for further analysis. In this benchmark, we compared the code generated by our method against the official code released by the authors, with correctness evaluated by AI judges on a scale from 1 to 5. The second benchmark, PaperBench Code-Dev, comprises 20 ICML 2024 papers, where human annotators assess the correctness of the generated code using predefined grading rubrics. To the best of our knowledge, few existing approaches employ self-refinement mechanisms for the automatic transformation of research papers into executable code. Therefore, we compared our approach to two closely related methods that incorporate self-refinement: (1) Paper2Code combined with Self-Refine, an iterative framework that progressively improves outputs through repeated evaluation and revision cycles. All evaluations were conducted using GPT-4.1 as the primary model. For RePro, we used the results reported in the original paper due to the unavailability of their code. Tables I and II present a comparative analysis of the performance of various optimization strategies across the two datasets. Our approach consistently demonstrated greater reliability across both datasets. Even when optimization was limited to the planning phase, our approach (Paper2Code combined with autoplan optimization) showed significant and consistent improvements. Upon analyzing individual tasks, our method consistently outperformed other approaches. Figure 2 illustrates the performance of each task within PaperBench. Our optimization strategy outperformed Self-Refine in 16 out of 20 tasks (80%), demonstrating its robust performance across diverse scenarios. In contrast, Self-Refine showed negative performance deltas on 10 tasks, with particularly pronounced degradations on a subset of challenging instances.

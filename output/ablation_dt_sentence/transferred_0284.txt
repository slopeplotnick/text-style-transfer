The RAIR benchmark encompasses a total of 63,601 samples, drawn from 14 real-world e-commerce industries. It is composed of three distinct subsets: the General subset (46,775 samples), the Long-Tail Hard subset (10,931 samples), and the Visual Salience subset (5,895 samples). For each entry in the dataset, we include the corresponding query, item title, item description, SKU details, and a de-identified image of the item. Additionally, each entry is accompanied by a manually assigned ground truth label, categorized according to our four-level relevance scale (L1 to L4). The distribution of these labels across the dataset is detailed in Table 8. To facilitate model development and enable detailed error attribution, each data entry is further augmented with corresponding rule identifiers from our Rule Checklist, which serve as the foundation for its relevance assessment. In this section, we systematically evaluate and analyze the methods proposed using the RAIR benchmark. Our experiments are divided into two parts. First, we perform a quantitative evaluation to address two central questions: (1) the extent of performance improvement achieved through fine-tuning, and (2) the advantages conferred by the proposed LORE model across distinct data distribution subsets, including general samples, long-tail hard samples, and visually salient samples. Second, we employ case studies to illustrate the novel capabilities imparted by our methods and demonstrate the types of problems they enable the model to address. To ensure the stability and reproducibility of our evaluation outcomes, all trained models utilize a deterministic decoding strategy for single-pass inference, free from any sampling randomness.

RQ1. Process-centric Metrics. What are the primary characteristics of programming agents with respect to process-centric metrics? What factors influence these metric values? RQ2. Analysis of Problem-solving Strategies. What problem-solving strategies do programming agents commonly employ? What internal or external factors shape their strategic behavior? To what extent do agents adhere to their initial plans, and how often do they revise or evolve their strategies during execution? RQ3. Inefficiency Pattern Analysis. What common inefficiency patterns or maladaptive behaviors emerge in agent trajectories? How frequently do these behaviors occur? To address these questions, we study two autonomous, open-process, and widely adopted programming agents: SWE-agent (SA) and OpenHands (OH). We evaluate them under their respective default configurations: SWE-agent imposes a per-task cost cap of $2, while OpenHands executes up to 100 steps per task. Both agents rely on their standard file-inspection and code-editing tools. Each agent is paired with one of four language models: DeepSeek-V3 671B MoE (DSK-V3) [14], DeepSeek-R1 (DSK-R1) [18], Devstral-small-2505 24B (Dev) [7], and Claude Sonnet 4 (CLD-4) [9]. These LLMs span general-purpose, reasoning-oriented, open-source, and frontier categories, resulting in eight total ⟨agent, model⟩ configurations.

The constructed system demonstrates robust performance across various tests and configurations, indicating its reliability and consistency. This is significant because it maintains coherence and clarity in results; if outcomes were disorganized or arbitrary, they would not facilitate a comprehensive understanding of interconnections. Instead, distinct categorizations, consistent configurations, and shared characteristics are evident and logical. These results validate that our approach aids in identifying training images that are not just visually or textually similar but also semantically aligned with generated outputs. Similarly, the data reveals that the automated construction of knowledge graphs (KGs) supports the evaluation and comprehension of the influence of training data on model outputs. This process elucidates model mechanisms, assesses potential copyright infringements, and enhances dataset transparency. For example, when instructing a generative model to produce an image in an artistic style like "Ghibli-style," the model does not replicate images from the training dataset directly. Instead, it grasps the general visual characteristics of the style by analyzing a set of images, storing these features to generate novel outputs. Consequently, determining precisely which training images influenced the output becomes complex, as influence manifests at a higher level of abstraction, including shared motifs such as textures, themes, and compositional structures. Therefore, to discern a style's influence on the model, it is crucial to examine overarching patterns and semantic meanings, rather than specific objects. In our experiment, we utilized a previously outlined methodology to automate KG generation. We began by extracting frames from selected Studio Ghibli films. Each frame was articulated using a domain-specific ontology, deconstructing it into structured semantic units. These components were then used to construct a "Ghibli World" knowledge graph, capturing the studio's visual elements. Despite using frames exclusively from ten chosen films, the dataset provides a representative depiction of Studio Ghibli’s stylistic characteristics.

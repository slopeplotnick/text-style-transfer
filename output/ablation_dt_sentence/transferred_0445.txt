We source forecasting questions from Metaculus [Metaculus, 2015] and Polymarket [Polymarket, 2020] to examine belief entrenchment in large language models (LLMs). This domain is chosen for two key reasons: (1) forecasting questions come with ground truth labels once resolved, and (2) excelling in these questions demonstrates Bayesian-like reasoning, as it requires evidence evaluation and proportionally updating beliefs. Although forecasting differs from answering factual questions, it still calls for well-calibrated, unbiased belief updates. Thus, it serves as an effective proxy for rational reasoning under uncertainty. Similarly, the scientific peer review process provides another setting suitable for assessing belief entrenchment. We utilize the open-access ICLR submission dataset from OpenReview [Höpner et al., 2025], which contains paper abstracts, bibliographies, reviewer comments, rebuttals, and final acceptance decisions. In this framework, the model is tasked to function as an area chair, making acceptance decisions based on the abstract and the arguments laid out in reviews and rebuttals. This setup allows us to evaluate how reasoning evolves when initial impressions, perhaps formed from the abstract, clash with subsequent evidence, such as critiques and responses. We assess belief entrenchment through two reasoning techniques: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) [Wei et al., 2022] and debate [Khan et al., 2024]. The debate technique involves two iterations of the same expert model taking opposing stances on a topic to enable a more informed understanding for a less knowledgeable observer. Our experiments include models such as GPT-4o, DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Flash, LLaMA 4 Scout, and LLaMA 4 Maverick. To probe how initial beliefs impact reasoning, we alter the models' priors using system prompts. Specifically, besides a baseline with no prompt, we introduce two additional conditions: one aligning with the initial belief and the other incorporating a critical-thinking prompt.

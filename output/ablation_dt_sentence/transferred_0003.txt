We conducted an empirical survey of seven model merging strategies for mitigating social bias, using three bias benchmarks and SuperGLUE across 13 large language models. Among these, we identified SLERP with moderate interpolation weights as the most balanced method, achieving substantial bias reduction without compromising downstream task performance. These findings underscore the importance of evaluating merging strategies on tasks such as reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning, which are critical for effective social bias mitigation. Recently, model merging has gained traction as a cost-efficient method to combine the strengths of multiple models without requiring expensive retraining (Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024). This strategy involves fusing the parameters of two or more trained models to produce a unified model that retains and integrates the capabilities of its constituents. One of the earliest approaches, linear averaging (Wortsman et al., 2022), merges models by simply averaging their weights, providing an efficient pathway to leverage pre-trained expertise. Building on this, later methods (Matena and Raffel, 2022; Lee et al., 2025) generalize the technique by assigning weights to parameters based on their relative importance—often guided by inverse Fisher information—to ensure coherent and likelihood-consistent combinations. Alternatively, spherical interpolation methods (Shoemake, 1985; Goddard et al., 2024; Grove and Karcher, 1973) treat model parameters as points on a unit sphere. SLERP (Shoemake, 1985) interpolates between two such models, while the Karcher Mean (Grove and Karcher, 1973) computes the Riemannian centroid for merging multiple models. NuSLERP (Goddard et al., 2024) further refines this by introducing per-tensor normalization to correct for norm drift. Building on these ideas, Ilharco et al. (2022) introduced the concept of a *task vector*, which represents the displacement between a pre-trained model and its fine-tuned counterpart, enabling targeted adaptations for downstream tasks.

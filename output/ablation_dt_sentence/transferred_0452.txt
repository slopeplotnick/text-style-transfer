Experts in numerical modeling transform broad hypotheses into numerical representations, beginning with the articulation of the central concept and then deconstructing it into more specific components through quantitative metrics and detailed elaborations. Each hypothesis is assigned a probability of being accurate, employing various methodologies to illustrate the likelihood of specific details. This prior distribution model must be verified against empirical data, which necessitates the utilization of quantitative methods for forecasting the expected structure of the data. Such tasks are undertaken by domain experts well-versed in the subject matter, who employ a testing methodology to assess the validity of their predictions using statistical analyses to identify discrepancies. Moreover, domain experts in Monte Carlo simulation employ Bayesian inversion to update the model hypothesis and associated variables based on available data. Decision-making is contingent upon the degree of uncertainty associated with the predictions, thus requiring effective execution through collaborative dialogue among all experts. In Part III, we will discuss the opportunities AI brings to enhance this process; however, before that, an analysis of prevalent AI applications in mineral exploration will be conducted. Instead of providing formal definitions of artificial intelligence, I will use a specific example: consider the concept of an autonomous vehicle tasked with navigating safely and lawfully from one location to another within a reasonable timeframe. To achieve this, it engages in actions such as steering, accelerating, decelerating, and maneuvering. The system employs sensors to assess current road conditions and formulates predictions regarding its future actions and those of other road users. The primary components include rewards encompassing safety, legality, and efficiency; actions such as steering and braking; and state, which pertains to the current conditions on the roadway, including the presence and velocities of vehicles and pedestrians.

ReVSeg reformulates the problem of identifying events and selecting specific objects within a video as an explicit sequence of reasoning steps. Specifically, it decomposes the task into three core components: interpreting the video content, determining the temporal structure of relevant events, and identifying the spatial location of key entities. This decomposition is executed through iterative multi-turn interactions with a single system. The reasoning chain begins with a disordered video and an underspecified query—such as “What is the best car for a family trip?”—which is then distilled into a concrete keyframe paired with a refined textual target, like “the silver minivan.” This transformation illustrates how the model leverages commonsense and causal reasoning to anchor abstract questions in visual evidence. Once grounded in a single frame, the model further processes the associated image-text pair to precisely localize the target object using a bounding box, thereby establishing its spatial context. Following localization, a downstream module is invoked to track the object throughout the video, producing a temporally resolved segmentation output. Because this entire reasoning process is managed within a unified system, the semantic context built in earlier stages is preserved and propagated forward, maintaining consistency across all steps. To enhance overall effectiveness, ReVSeg is optimized using a reinforcement learning paradigm that rewards not only the final segmentation accuracy but also the correctness of intermediate reasoning decisions. This approach directly addresses the limitations of current systems, which struggle with end-to-end spatial identification from raw video due to scarce high-quality supervision. While auxiliary strategies—such as providing structured cues, running multiple verification passes, or integrating search heuristics—offer partial improvements, they fall short in consistently grounding objects without explicit reasoning.

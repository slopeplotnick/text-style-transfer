User-Centered Evaluations: Hallucinations within systems can erode user trust, making it crucial to consider how users perceive and experience these errors. A study investigates hallucinations in language models, engaging users in the tasks of detecting and documenting inaccuracies. This approach introduces innovative user-centric metrics that examine the impact of hallucinations, including diminished trust and the onerous duty of self-verification that ideally should be managed by the system. Evaluation of Hallucinations: A More Inclusive Framework for Hallucination Evaluation: Through a range of measures, hallucinations and their effects are assessed, leading to the development of more integrated frameworks. A subsequent study illustrates that language model performance can be evaluated comprehensively using various methodologies such as factuality assessments, consistency analysis, and evaluations of user experience. This encompassing methodology facilitates identifying hallucinations from multiple angles, assessing not only factual accuracy but also the soundness of arguments and user experience. Applications of LLMs in Different Scenarios: In employing large language models (LLMs), understanding the specific task while acknowledging the models' potential for hallucination is essential. For general tasks such as chatbots, models like GPT-4 or PaLM are frequently adopted due to their capacity to generate fluent, human-like discourse with minimal risk to veracity, while maintaining a suitable level of creativity. Conversely, for tasks requiring factual responses such as information retrieval or question answering, models like BERT and T5 are more suited due to their grounded understanding and factual reliability.

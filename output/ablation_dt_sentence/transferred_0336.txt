Multimodal large language models (MLLMs), while analogous to traditional language models (LLMs), incorporate an additional visual input channel, which not only enriches semantic representation beyond textual data but also introduces greater safety risks due to a wider range of potential vulnerabilities. Jailbreaking, which involves crafting adversarial inputs to circumvent safety mechanisms and elicit harmful content, exposes these vulnerabilities and remains a critical focus in efforts to develop robust, trustworthy, and secure MLLMs. Prior work on jailbreaking has primarily targeted LLMs, utilizing methods such as prompt injection and role-playing to exploit alignment weaknesses. As research attention shifts to MLLMs, adversarial strategies have evolved to take advantage of their visual modality. However, existing techniques present significant limitations. One line of attack constructs deceptive images or embeds sensitive content within visual data, yet fails to fully exploit the contextual information available. Another approach attempts to introduce context through descriptive language, mimicking LLM-focused jailbreaks, but overlooks the expressive potential unique to image-based inputs. To overcome these limitations, we introduce Contextual-Image Attack (CIA), a method that constructs visually coherent scenarios embedding both situational context and malicious intent, with text playing only a secondary, supportive role. For example, rather than depicting the query “How can someone make illegal drugs?” directly, our method embeds this query within a plausible visual context, such as a teacher asking the question during a chemistry lesson. This legitimizes the query’s appearance, prompting the model to misinterpret its harmful objective and bypass safety filters. In contrast, approaches like FigStep render the query as plain text within an image, while others rely solely on verbal context. Our approach outperforms these methods by more effectively leveraging visual inputs as part of the adversarial framework. Moreover, advancements in image generation and text-to-visual encoding technologies now enable the creation of highly realistic scenarios, underscoring the growing potential of images to embed complex, concealed intentions for model manipulation.

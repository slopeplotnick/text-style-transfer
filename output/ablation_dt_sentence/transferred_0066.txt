Further examination revealed that individual differences in attitudes toward AI had a more substantial influence on the results than the experimental manipulation itself. When attitudes toward AI were included in the analysis, the experimental condition no longer accounted for participants' preferences or the intensity of their emotional responses to the music. In contrast, attitudes toward AI remained a significant and stable factor throughout the analysis. This finding aligns with previous research, which suggests that individuals' attitudes toward autonomous machines strongly influence their evaluative judgments (Hong et al., 2022). Moreover, it extends the work of Shank et al. (2023), who proposed that composer bias may vary depending on the song and context, particularly when the song is popular and perceived to have human-like qualities. Although several personality traits and music-related factors were associated with music liking and emotional experience, their influence was notably reduced when attitudes toward AI were accounted for. Notable exceptions include openness to experience and neuroticism—personality traits that have previously been shown to significantly influence emotional responses to music, especially in relation to emotions such as joy and vitality (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Juslin et al., 2011; Liljeström et al., 2013). These emotional qualities were clearly evident in the musical stimuli used in the present study. Most notably, attitudes toward AI were the only listener characteristic significantly associated with both music liking and emotional responses. The total amount of variance explained by listener characteristics was similar to that observed in previous research (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Strauss et al., 2024). These findings highlight the central role of attitudes toward AI in shaping evaluations of AI-generated music. In contrast to the findings of Ansani et al. (2024), we did not observe a moderation effect of attitudes toward AI on experimental groups. This discrepancy may be attributed to differences in attitude measures, the focus on listening to tracks rather than live performances, and the inclusion of three experimental conditions instead of two.

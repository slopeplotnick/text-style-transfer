Modern market systems exhibit superior scalability, adaptability, and resilience in addressing challenges compared to centralized models. Botelho and Alami (1999) introduced auction-based systems that enable competitive task assignment, while Gerkey and Matario (2002) demonstrated that auction mechanisms can effectively distribute tasks while maintaining coordination. More recent advancements have focused on overcoming communication constraints by employing sophisticated bidding strategies, particularly under conditions of limited connectivity (Ferri et al.). Quinton et al. (2017) emphasized the significance of innovative methodologies in addressing the complexities of modern market dynamics, offering a detailed analysis of scalable strategies and adaptive mechanisms that enhance operational efficiency in decentralized systems. Nevertheless, auction-based methods do not always yield globally optimal solutions, as local bidding decisions can disrupt coordination, especially when tasks exhibit complex interdependencies. In construction projects, tasks must be executed in a specific sequence, presenting substantial challenges for auction-based mechanisms that struggle with managing temporal constraints essential for effective project scheduling. This prompted an exploration of structured optimization approaches. Dynamic programming offers alternative optimization strategies for decision-making processes that unfold sequentially. Bellman (1962) established the foundational theoretical framework for deriving optimal solutions to problems characterized by optimal substructure and recursive decision properties. However, dynamic programming suffers from combinatorial explosion in the state space, restricting its scalability and rendering it impractical for large-scale problem instances. Construction projects, which involve numerous robots executing interdependent tasks with complex state dependencies, further exacerbate the computational demands, making real-time decision-making infeasible. Consequently, researchers turned to metaheuristic methods to address large, complex problem domains. These methods, drawing on evolutionary principles, have shown effectiveness in navigating combinatorial complexity and constraint hierarchies without necessitating a fully specified mathematical formulation (Jones et al.; Al-Omeer and Ahmed, 2010; Liang and Cheng, 2019). Swarm intelligence methods, in particular, offer distributed coordination capabilities well-suited for managing large-scale robotic teams (Chen et al.). However, metaheuristic methods do not guarantee optimal solutions and remain computationally intensive, requiring numerous iterations to achieve sufficiently high-quality results. In the rapidly evolving environments of construction, the response latency inherent in these methods proves incompatible with the demands of effective coordination.

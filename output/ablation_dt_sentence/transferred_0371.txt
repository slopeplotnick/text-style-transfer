Checkers and related approaches excel in structured domains like mathematics and coding but struggle to generalize to open-ended tasks without clearly defined solutions. Self-evaluation methods, on the other hand, treat the model, or another language model, as a judge. For example, Self-Rewarding Language Models (Yuan et al., 2024) and RLAIF (Lee et al., 2024) generate AI-derived feedback instead of human labels, while IPO (Garg et al., 2025) and Self-Rewarding PPO (Zhang et al., 2025a) derive preference signals from token-level probabilities. In a similar vein, recent work on internal activations, such as Factoscope (He et al., 2024c) and INSIDE (Chen et al., 2024), demonstrates that hidden states capture factuality and hallucination risk. Our approach follows this line of research, employing a geometric statistic of activations as a reward signal to guide policy optimization. The theoretical foundation for using rank-based quality measures stems from softmax bottleneck analysis. Yang et al. (2018) show that the expressiveness of softmax-based models is inherently constrained by the rank of their hidden representations. Godey et al. (2024) extend this finding to modern LLMs, showing that the contextual probability distribution of natural language is fundamentally high-rank. Furthermore, complementary work suggests that when embeddings collapse into a narrow cone during training, the quality of generated outputs deteriorates (Gao et al., 2019). Collectively, these studies establish a strong link between representational dimensionality and generation quality. Empirical evidence further supports this connection. Garrido et al. (2023) demonstrate that the effective rank of learned representations can predict downstream performance, even without supervision. In language models, hidden states encode rich semantic structure: word embeddings capture syntactic and semantic regularities through geometric relationships (Mikolov et al., 2013), while intermediate representations assess factuality (He et al., 2024c) and hallucination risk (Chen et al., 2024). These findings confirm that generation quality leaves detectable geometric signatures. Building on this body of work, we shift from diagnostic analysis to optimization. Rather than analyzing pretrained models post-hoc, we employ stable rank as a live reward signal during policy optimization, turning a geometric insight into a practical alignment method.

A language model can assist in rectifying and proposing modifications to ambiguously phrased questions, thereby improving response accuracy and reducing the likelihood of errors or misunderstandings. Furthermore, it can help identify the alignment between specific questions and their corresponding thematic categories, allowing researchers to better understand the interrelations among them. If the categories exhibit significant similarity, substantial modifications may be required to ensure clarity and precision. Moreover, language models can enhance the uniformity of writing levels, improving overall comprehensibility. To explore the use of language models in the development of these tests, we refer to a recent publication by Beghetto et al. In this work, the authors investigate the application of language models to generate synthetic data, which can augment and strengthen the evaluation of these methods. This approach holds the potential to reduce both the time and financial resources typically allocated to empirical testing, enabling preliminary evaluations through simulated or proxy populations. As a result, costs may be substantially lowered, making it possible for a broader range of researchers to develop assessments while minimizing the inclusion of unnecessary tests. In our study, we evaluated the performance of recent language models in generating data that aligns with the structural frameworks used in the formulation of these tests. We now describe the methodology employed in our study, outlining the rationale behind its design and explaining the reasoning for its anticipated effectiveness. For the development of these tests, we primarily adhered to the methodological steps prescribed by Classical Test Theory, in conjunction with the guidelines proposed by Boateng et al.

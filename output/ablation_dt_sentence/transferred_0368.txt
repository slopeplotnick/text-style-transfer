Problem difficulty exhibits a consistent relationship with verification gains, both intra-family and cross-family, as all settings demonstrate clear positive correlations. Notably, AIME appears as an outlier in the final plot, potentially because some models have encountered similar problems during post-training. Moreover, the best-fit lines for verifier accuracy reveal two distinct clusters of tasks (highlighted in red and blue), suggesting that verification difficulty cannot be explained solely by solver accuracy. This leads to a natural question: are some tasks inherently more verifiable than others? In this context, Song et al. (2025) find that models do not exhibit self-improvement on tasks requiring factual recall, whereas measurable gains are observed on Tokoku when the pretraining scale is sufficiently large. A similar pattern emerges in Figure 7, where AIME, GSM8K, 3SAT, and Tokoku exhibit a higher ratio of verifier accuracy to solver accuracy and show consistent performance improvements across all verification settings. To explain this, we note that among our synthetic datasets, Selenium and 3SAT are prototypical examples of problems that require exponential time to solve but allow polynomial-time verification. In contrast, Matrix Multiplication lacks a clear shortcut for verifying results without effectively recomputing them. Turning to real-world datasets, GSM8K and AIME include problems solvable using high-school-level mathematics, whereas MMLU (Social Sciences) demands domain-specific knowledge, CommonsenseQA relies on implicit commonsense and world knowledge, and GPQA together with MMLU (STEM) require specialized natural science expertise. For these latter tasks, verifying an answer essentially requires the same expertise as solving the problem itself. While tasks that are easier to solve often tend to be easier to verify, some tasks are intrinsically more verifiable regardless of their overall difficulty. These include synthetic problems involving logical or structured reasoning (e.g., 3SAT, Sudo) and real-world tasks that depend primarily on mathematical reasoning rather than factual recall (e.g., GSM8K, AIME). Notably, such tasks also yield greater gains from test-time rejection sampling when using verifiers.

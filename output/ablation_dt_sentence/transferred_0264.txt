The challenges posed by issues such as climate change and the transition to alternative energy sources transcend the boundaries of any single scientific discipline. Yet, despite their complexity, there remains a significant gap in academic offerings, as no degree specifically focuses on Mineral Exploration. Furthermore, information is now produced instantaneously, and its intelligence continues to evolve. What once required manual visits to the library to extract data from journals is now accomplished through algorithms that can search and summarize thousands of documents in near real-time. Meanwhile, interest in fields like economic geology and mining engineering is steadily diminishing in the Western world. Now more than ever, it is crucial to preserve the centuries-old knowledge surrounding the formation of ore deposits, much of which remains buried in neglected geological reports scattered across the globe. High-quality databases, akin to those developed in Canada and Australia, are urgently needed worldwide to foster comprehensive, interoperable data infrastructures. In Africa, only one nation has developed such a database (gsd.gov.zm). Ensuring that this information is accessible via online platforms is vital for attracting increased investment. This essay advocates for a collaborative effort between scientists and industry professionals to develop a novel approach to mineral exploration. As exploration moves to subsurface environments, the traditional approach of starting with surface outcrops and relying on deterministic geophysical inversion has become increasingly ineffective, as evidenced by the declining discovery rate. Therefore, a decision-oriented approach that explicitly incorporates risk assessment through uncertainty quantification is essential for reducing false positive drilling outcomes. This approach must also acknowledge our incomplete understanding of orebodies, as reflected in the measurement of epistemic uncertainty. Moreover, the appropriate application of geostatistical methods to handle data at their respective scales is currently lacking but crucial to minimize false positives.

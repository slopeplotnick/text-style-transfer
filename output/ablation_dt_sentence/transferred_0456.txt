Question Making. In line with the categorization outlined in Section 2.1, eight graduate students, experts in specific domains, selected subsets of categories and authored 20 prompts each. Each prompt was crafted to require reasoning involving at least two scientific concepts, ensuring a sufficient level of difficulty. The prompts were inspired by public educational experiment examples, ensuring scientific validity and that each target phenomenon could be feasibly represented within a video lasting no more than 10 seconds. Among these contributors, two graduate students also curated 40 I2V questions, each paired with a reference video depicting the target experiment as the ground truth. These reference videos were sourced from publicly accessible datasets and online resources, with a key frame extracted from the initial segment used as the conditional input image to illustrate the complete experimental setup. Panel Review and Quality Check. Upon drafting the full test suite, all participating graduate students gathered as a review panel to assess the materials. The panel systematically cross-checked each set of 20 prompts to ensure specificity, procedural accuracy, and clarity of the expected outcomes. As a final quality assurance step, proficient video generation models, specifically Sora-2 and Wan-2.5-T2V-Preview, were employed to synthesize the opening seconds of each prompt, thus confirming that the key experimental components and setups were accurately captured. VLM-as-a-Judge. Recent studies highlight that robust Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can yield human-aligned judgments when appropriately prompted and calibrated. In VideoScience-Bench, we utilize a VLM-as-a-Judge framework to investigate the performance gap between reasoning-oriented VLMs and non-reasoning models such as GPT-4o. We found that directly prompting VLMs—especially non-reasoning ones—to evaluate video generations often leads to under-justified assessments and false-positive ratings. This finding prompted the design of a quantitative auto-evaluation framework characterized by a checklist-based grading scheme, where each rating must be supported by salient video frames and an evidence table derived from computer-vision analysis of the videos.

Sampling different noise vectors in flow-matching or diffusion-based frameworks is inherently stochastic, with the variability in the sampled initial noise directly affecting the resulting actions. To address the distribution shift challenge, we adopt an anti-exploration perspective and introduce Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-Counts (TACO). The concept of anti-exploration, rooted in offline reinforcement learning (RL), aims to prevent policies from visiting states or taking actions that fall outside the support of the provided dataset. Similarly, during VLA inference, the goal is to constrain the generated actions to lie within the support of the successful modes observed in the SFT dataset, thereby avoiding exploration of redundant or irrelevant action modes retained from pretraining or imperfect fine-tuning data. In TACO, we realize this anti-exploration principle through test-time scaling (TTS) rather than policy optimization. This is because the complex sampling dynamics inherent in flow-matching or diffusion-based VLA models are not readily compatible with conventional RL-style updates. Instead, TTS modifies the action sampling process without altering the VLA's parameters, eliminating the need for gradient computation. We employ a classical Coin-Flipping Network (CFN), augmented with an internal representation mechanism, to estimate pseudo-counts for each observation-instruction-action-chunk pair in the SFT dataset. Intuitively, action chunks with higher pseudo-counts are more aligned with frequently observed, and thus more successful, behaviors in the SFT data, making them the preferred choice. During decision-making, TACO uses the pseudo-counts derived from the CFN as a criterion to select the most reliable action chunk from multiple candidates, ultimately executing the one with the highest pseudo-count. To reduce the computational overhead of repeated forward passes during test-time evaluations, we introduce a shared observation key-value cache, which significantly cuts latency by reusing visual-language representations across samples.

The Long-Tail Hard subset was designed to provide challenging data to assess the capabilities of advanced language models. We focused on mining difficult cases, categorizing them into explicit demands (such as Multi-Attribute, MR) and implicit demands. The implicit demands include four distinct types: Negation (NE), Alternatives (AL), Knowledge-dependent (KD), and Reasoning-dependent (RD). The mining process was tailored specifically for these types. NE and AL queries were filtered using predefined keywords and regular expressions. In contrast, KD and RD queries, which often appear as long-tail queries, were mined by first undersampling low-frequency logs and then using a robust language model (Qwen3-235B) with a self-consistency filter, requiring four identical results across eight inferences. Multi-attribute queries were identified using an NER model on length-filtered queries to locate those containing more than five attributes. To ensure the final set represented genuinely difficult cases, all candidates were filtered through a baseline relevance model. Only those samples where the model's prediction was correct five or fewer times across eight inferences were retained, indicating high model uncertainty. The Visual Salience subset isolates cases where the primary product image provides crucial, fine-grained visual information—such as color or style—that is essential for making an accurate judgment. We initiated this process with a keyword-based retrieval system grounded in a comprehensive visual taxonomy. To minimize false positives, two key filters were applied: first, a predefined blacklist, and second, retaining only those query-ad pairs where the critical visual keyword did not appear in the product's textual description. An LLM was then employed to verify that the remaining keywords accurately referred to visual attributes. Finally, to identify the most challenging examples, we trained a simple multimodal relevance model and selected those instances exhibiting high uncertainty, as indicated by frequent inconsistencies in its predictions.

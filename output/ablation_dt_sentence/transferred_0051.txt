Ensuring that AI models operate with logical consistency and ethical soundness is essential for evaluating their decision-making processes. This requires not only assessing their immediate responses but also understanding how their ethical reasoning evolves across various scenarios. While current frameworks primarily focus on isolated outputs, they often neglect the need to track the development of ethical reasoning and the dynamic shifts in value alignment as models interact with diverse, contextually rich environments. Therefore, a comprehensive evaluation necessitates monitoring the continuity and adaptability of ethical principles over time. In this regard, MoCoP introduces an innovative framework for examining the ethical implications of artificial intelligence systems without reliance on predefined datasets. Unlike traditional methods that depend heavily on structured datasets, MoCoP adopts a dynamic, context-sensitive approach to evaluate ethical concerns in AI, offering a novel means to address issues such as fairness, accountability, and transparency in AI operations. Furthermore, MoCoP enables a more flexible and adaptive analysis of ethical considerations, aligning with contemporary discussions on the need for system-level trust calibration and ethical transparency in AI development. MoCoP autonomously generates, evaluates, and refines moral reasoning scenarios, independent of external assistance. It also evaluates the extent to which AI models exhibit changes in their moral reasoning over time, comparing the consistency of various models in this regard. By integrating various tools to assess lexical accuracy, semantic interpretation, and logical consistency, the system ensures ongoing monitoring and evaluation of AI models' ethical decision-making processes. Previous studies have largely focused on identifying and mitigating biases or harmful content within AI systems. For instance, OpenEthics aimed to enhance the transparency and verifiability of AI ethics frameworks, facilitating more robust evaluations of ethical compliance in AI systems. Similarly, one study systematically examined fairness considerations, proposing methodological approaches to mitigate biased model behavior across specific demographic groups. These studies have contributed to improving AI system transparency, but their reliance on static datasets and predefined prompts has limited their adaptability to evolving conditions. Consequently, while these models provide insights into the state of AI at a specific point in time, they fail to capture the dynamic shifts in moral reasoning or assess the stability of such reasoning across diverse contexts. More recent research has expanded the scope beyond fairness to explore how AI models engage in moral and ethical decision-making processes.

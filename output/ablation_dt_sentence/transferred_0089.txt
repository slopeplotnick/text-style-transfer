Recent developments in large language models (LLMs) with voice interfaces have garnered significant attention from both the academic community and the general public. Closed-source models, such as GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-pro, have achieved state-of-the-art performance on key speech tasks, including speech recognition, translation, and spoken language understanding, consistently outperforming previous open-source baselines. However, despite these advancements, there remains a notable gap in research focused on integrating speech modalities into these models to enhance their multi-modal understanding capabilities. Furthermore, the interaction between speech models and LLMs, particularly within autonomous systems that engage in self-directed learning, is still in its infancy and remains a burgeoning area of research. This tutorial provides a comprehensive review of the historical development of language models in speech processing, offering insights into the potential for multi-agent systems within conversational models. We will explore advanced topics such as the integration of non-textual data with textual representations, emphasizing the foundational principles needed to align these diverse data types. In addition, we will examine illustrative examples that facilitate a clearer understanding of these complex concepts. Moreover, the tutorial will address recent developments in models designed to process both speech and text simultaneously, focusing on generative autoregressive methods for integrated speech-text tokenization. By examining both historical methodologies and contemporary approaches, this tutorial aims to shed light on the current landscape of speech-integrated language models, while highlighting persistent challenges and open questions in this rapidly evolving field. The three-hour session will provide an in-depth exploration of speech and language models, with a particular emphasis on the integration of voice interfaces and intelligent systems. Each core topic will be covered in a 35-minute segment, followed by a 10-minute Q&A session and a 10-minute break. Each section will begin with an overview of the relevant topics, followed by a detailed analysis of key studies that have shaped the field. The tutorial will conclude with a discussion of the ongoing challenges and emerging research opportunities in this dynamic and transformative area of joint speech-language modeling.

Building on the findings in RQ2, which revealed that Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV) exhibit complementary interactions and distinct failure modes, we now address RQ3: How can we effectively integrate these elements into high-performance hybrid systems? To this end, we propose two integration strategies: a Score-Level Fusion approach and an Evidence-Aware Pipeline, the latter specifically designed to address the structural limitations of FV identified in our study. While the Score-Level Fusion approach enhances robustness through fusion at the score level, it does not directly tackle the failure modes highlighted in Section 4.5. Specifically, FV systems are reliable when evidence is available to substantiate claims, yielding "Supported" or "Contradicted" verdicts. However, in the absence of sufficient evidence, their performance deteriorates, resulting in a "Not Enough Information" (NEI) verdict. To overcome this limitation, we introduce the Evidence-Aware Pipeline, which prioritizes external information retrieval and resorts to internal signals only when external evidence cannot be found. This pipeline is structured as follows: (1) Step 1: Retrieval & Verification. An FV module (e.g., LLM-QA) retrieves relevant evidence and classifies the claim-evidence relationship as Supported, Contradicted, or NEI. (2) Step 2: Conditional Branching. In this step, we employ a cascading decision mechanism. If the FV module produces a definitive prediction (either Supported or Contradicted), the system accepts the result accordingly. However, if the classification is NEI, the system acknowledges the absence of external support. Rather than attempting a potentially erroneous verification, the pipeline bypasses FV and employs an HD method (e.g., LNPE), deriving the final score solely from the model’s internal uncertainty signals. This design effectively mitigates the limitations of FV when relevant external information is unavailable, ensuring robust detection even in the absence of external knowledge. By decoupling the reliance on external evidence from internal signals, we prevent the propagation of errors due to poor evidence retrieval, thereby maintaining high system performance.

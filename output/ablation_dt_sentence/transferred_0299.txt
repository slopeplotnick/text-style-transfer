OmniFusion is employed to facilitate live translations by extracting relevant lecture slides from segmented audio clips. To support this, we are establishing an open-source image-to-image translation pipeline with modular components, paving the way for future research on full-image/slide translation and rendering. The inclusion of images has been shown to improve performance in speech translation (ST) for downstream natural language processing (NLP) tasks across various large language models (LLMs), with noticeable performance gains across different language pairs. We also evaluate several optical character recognition (OCR) models, as well as the translation quality of unimodal and multimodal neural machine translation (NMT) models for image translation. In our efforts to fully localize lecture content, we develop multimodal translation systems that incorporate both audio and slides, facilitating accessibility tasks such as summarization (SUM) and question answering (QA). These systems perform multimodal ST and leverage the resulting transcripts for downstream LLM tasks. Moreover, our approach translates slides by converting both text and images into the target language while maintaining their original layout and visual coherence. To improve usability and map visual context to each audio segment, we developed a PDF viewer that displays slides with overlaid captions synchronized to the presenter's selected slide. This interface allows participants to follow translations while viewing slides and enables the system to automatically identify which slide corresponds to each audio segment, providing essential context for multimodal ST. In this section, we first describe the multimodal ST pipeline, including how slide images are extracted and associated with audio segments. We then outline how the resulting translations are used for downstream SUM and QA tasks. Finally, we present the slide translation process, detailing how text embedded within slide images is detected, translated, and re-rendered. Further details on Text-to-Speech (TTS) can be found in Appendix A.1. While several ST systems support translation across multiple languages, they are not directly suited for live lectures. Most are designed for offline tasks with fixed segmentation, which is incompatible with streaming audio, and require simultaneous translation policies to determine when enough audio has been received. Current systems are either unimodal, overlooking slides, or multimodal but lacking multilingual support. Lecture scenarios demand both multimodality and multilinguality for optimal functionality.

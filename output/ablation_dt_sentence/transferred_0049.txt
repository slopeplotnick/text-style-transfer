We recruited 17 volunteers (P1-P17) through school posters and random invitations on the street. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB), and all underage volunteers provided guardian consent. All interview materials were securely stored, and no personally identifiable information was involved. Most volunteers reported familiarity with short story creation, with an average self-reported score of 3.8 on a 5-point Likert scale. Additionally, all volunteers had prior experience in writing stories, either by hand or with office software. The volunteers were divided into groups of approximately four, each accompanied by a staff member. The workshop lasted around 2.5 hours. During the session, volunteers were encouraged to verbalize their thought processes using a "think-aloud" approach. This allowed us to capture and analyze both their initial and final outputs, as well as the cognitive steps involved in their creative process. Following the workshop, we conducted follow-up interviews with four volunteers. By analyzing the workshop recordings and interview data, we identified and categorized four distinct modes of interaction observed during the story creation process. The workshop flow is outlined as follows: 1) Introduction and Overview (15 minutes). The staff provided a succinct overview of the workshop's objectives and structure, highlighting the application of the four foundational units through illustrative examples. Volunteers were reminded to adopt a "think-aloud" approach throughout the creative process, ensuring transparency and reflection on their decision-making. 2) Group Discussion and Story Framework Creation (60 minutes). The staff distributed cards to volunteers. Each group engaged in a collaborative discussion to develop their own story framework, utilizing the four foundational units. Throughout the process, volunteers were encouraged to articulate their thoughts and decision-making processes in real-time. The staff circulated among the groups, offering timely guidance and support as necessary. 3) Story Sharing and Feedback (45 minutes). Volunteers shared how they utilized the four foundational units in story construction. They received formative feedback from both peers and staff, with a focus on the creativity and structural coherence of their stories. 4) Informal Interviews with Volunteers (30 minutes). Four volunteers (P1-P4) participated in informal interviews to explore their creative experiences.

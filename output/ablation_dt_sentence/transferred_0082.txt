We investigate the functionality of GRAPHECTORY within autonomous, agentic programming systems, focusing on SWE-agent and OpenHands due to their rich and diverse trajectories. These systems engage in cycles of planning, bug localization, code modification, and patch validation, all of which can be programmatically assessed. For our experiments, we deploy four LLMs—DeepSeek-V3, DeepSeek-R1, Devstral-small-2505, and Claude Sonnet 4—as the backbone for solving real-world issues from twelve GitHub repositories within the SWE-Bench Verified benchmark. Our systematic analysis reveals several key findings: (1) unsuccessful runs are characterized by longer durations and greater inefficiencies, with GRAPHECTORY of unresolved issues consistently larger and exhibiting more repetitions than resolved ones, demonstrating inefficient patterns; (2) as task complexity increases, the systems explore deeper and wider, requiring more frequent strategy shifts to sustain progress; (3) while agents adhere to the global plan outlined in the system prompt, they also adapt their strategies during intermediate steps, which often leads to additional searching and debugging for more challenging problems; and (4) even when successful in resolving issues, the systems exhibit significant inefficiencies, emphasizing the need for process-centric evaluation to highlight the imbalance between repair success and efficiency. We formally define GRAPHECTORY and use it to propose process-centric evaluation metrics and analysis. To facilitate understanding, we introduce LANGUTORY, a compact, human-readable abstraction of GRAPHECTORY that simplifies the comparison of agentic systems. While GRAPHECTORY offers an in-depth representation of system trajectories, it can be overly detailed and challenging to compare across agents, LLMs, or problem domains. LANGUTORY addresses this by providing a more accessible, streamlined view of the data.

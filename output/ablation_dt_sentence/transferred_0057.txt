We conducted large-scale adversarial training on both CNN and ViT architectures using identical protocols to assess the impact of this training on attack transferability. Contrary to the intuitive expectation that robust models should produce less transferable adversarial examples, our analysis reveals the opposite trend. Specifically, adversarially trained (AT) models generate attacks that transfer significantly more effectively than those from standard trained (ST) models. This finding suggests that the features learned by AT models may introduce vulnerabilities that transcend model architectures, as shown in Figure 1. To support reproducibility and further research, we trained a total of 36 models, and both the models and training code will be made publicly available in a GitHub repository. Our results highlight a security paradox, challenging the conventional assumption that adversarial training inherently enhances robustness. Momentum Integrated Gradient (MIG) (Ma et al., 2023), a recent attack based on the integrated gradient (IG) method (Sundararajan et al., 2017), iteratively updates perturbations using a momentum strategy guided by IG. This approach targets the most influential features identified by IG, increasing the likelihood of misclassification. A key characteristic of IG, implementation invariance, allows MIG-generated attacks to transfer effectively between different architectures, such as CNNs and ViTs, or even between both types of models. This transferability, however, was not achievable with previous attacks like PGD, as illustrated in Figure 2. IG is an axiomatic interpretability technique that quantifies the contribution of individual features to a model's output by aggregating gradients along a straight-line path from the baseline input to the actual input.

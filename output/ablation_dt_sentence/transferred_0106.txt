To enhance interpretability and enable reasoning-based safety alignment, we augment each sample in the dataset with critiques generated by high-capacity models. Specifically, we employ gpt-oss-120b (Agarwal et al., 2025) for textual data, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct (Team, 2025) for visual-related data (including images, videos, and text-image pairs), and Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct (Team, 2024a) for auditory data. For each instance, the teacher model is provided with the original content, its corresponding safety label, and any violated categories, if present, and is tasked with generating a reasoning critique that explains the rationale behind the safety assessment. The complete prompting template used for generating critiques is illustrated in Figure 5. In contrast to basic classification models that output only binary safety labels, OMNIGUARD performs slow thinking inference to provide fine-grained and interpretable safety moderation. Specifically, it produces a structured output comprising the following components: (1) Safety judgment: an overall evaluation of the input’s safety, indicating whether it is classified as safe or unsafe. (2) Violation categories: the specific categories under which the content is flagged as unsafe, contingent upon any policy violations. (3) Reasoning critique: a natural language explanation that details the rationale behind the model's decision, in alignment with established policy guidelines. This formulation enables OMNIGUARD to go beyond shallow pattern recognition, facilitating a comprehensive and explainable analysis of potentially unsafe content across diverse modalities. In the following section, we present detailed experimental results evaluating OMNIGUARD’s performance in both unimodal and cross-modal settings across 15 guardrail and jailbreak benchmarks, spanning text, images, videos, and audio. Furthermore, we design experiments to investigate two key research questions: (1) Does reasoning-based safety alignment enhance the model’s ability to handle safety moderation and complex challenges across diverse modalities? (2) Can the safety knowledge learned from established modalities transfer to unseen ones, demonstrating cross-modal generalization in safety understanding and moderation?

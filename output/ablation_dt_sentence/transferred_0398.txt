The core spatial abilities our framework aims to achieve include (i) implicitly inferring 3D structure [34, 102], (ii) controlling viewpoint with geometric consistency (scale, occlusion, layout) [27, 72], (iii) understanding semantic layout [38], (iv) planning toward goals [2], and (v) maintaining long-horizon temporal coherence. To probe these abilities, we introduce two tasks: video-based scene navigation and object grounding. In scene navigation, the model follows camera-pose instructions and produces a video whose trajectory aligns with both the instructions and the scene geometry. In object grounding, a text instruction directs the model to locate and visually identify a target object, thus testing its understanding of semantic layout and its ability to plan effectively (Fig. 1). These tasks together evaluate whether VIDEO4SPATIAL can infer and act on scene structure using video alone. Given the lack of large public datasets specifically designed for spatial tasks, we repurpose indoor scanning datasets—ScanNet++ [87] and ARKitScenes [7]—which provide long trajectories through diverse rooms and enable the sampling of context-target video clip pairs. To support this, we utilize an off-the-shelf vision-language model (VLM) [59] to (i) identify clips that conclude with a centered object and (ii) generate natural-language grounding instructions. For navigation, we convert the annotated camera poses into actionable pose-following instructions. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework effectively addresses challenging visuospatial tasks. Leveraging video context alone, the model learns to infer scene-level geometry and semantics, achieving strong performance in both object grounding and scene navigation. Although trained on short video contexts, VIDEO4SPATIAL generalizes well to longer temporal windows during inference, further improving its performance. Additionally, despite being trained primarily on indoor scenes, the model demonstrates notable generalization capabilities, extending to outdoor environments and object categories not included in the training data. In summary, we: (1) introduce VIDEO4SPATIAL, a simple yet effective video generation framework that relies solely on video-based scene context to perform visuospatial tasks; (2) highlight key design decisions—joint classifier-free guidance over context and instruction, auxiliary bounding boxes as a reasoning prior to improve grounding accuracy, and non-continuous context sampling for efficient context understanding; and (3) instantiate and evaluate two spatial tasks to assess the framework's performance.

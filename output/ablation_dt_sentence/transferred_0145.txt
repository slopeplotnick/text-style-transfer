We evaluate these strategies on the BrowseComp benchmark and observe that effective context management during testing leads to notable performance improvements by enabling the model to scale test-time compute and perform additional execution steps as needed. For example, Summary extends the average steps to 364, resulting in a performance gain of up to 60.2%. However, its overall efficiency remains relatively low. In contrast, the Discard-all method, while simple, performs strongly in both efficiency and scalability, achieving a score of 67.6—comparable to parallel scaling—yet requiring significantly fewer steps. In summary, test-time compute can be scaled either serially via context management or in parallel, both approaches enhancing the model’s problem-solving capacity. However, each strategy varies in efficiency and scalability, making it critical to consider actual computational costs when comparing performance. Identifying an optimal balance between serial and parallel scaling to maximize both efficiency and scalability remains a key direction for future research. In this work, we introduce DeepSeek-V3.2, a framework that effectively balances computational efficiency with advanced reasoning capabilities. Leveraging DSA, we address complex computational demands while preserving long-context processing. By increasing the computational budget, DeepSeek-V3.2 achieves performance on par with GPT-5 on reasoning benchmarks. Moreover, the integration of our task synthesis system significantly enhances tool-use proficiency, opening new avenues for the development of robust and generalizable AI systems based on open LLMs. Additionally, our high-compute variant, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, has achieved top honors in competitions such as the IMO and IOI, marking a milestone for open-source LLMs. Nonetheless, we acknowledge existing limitations when compared to frontier closed-source models like Gemini-3.0-Pro. Specifically, due to a lower total training FLOPs, DeepSeek-V3.2 demonstrates a narrower breadth of general knowledge relative to leading proprietary systems.

The transition to an SPL approach entails substantial organizational changes, particularly given the significant challenges inherent in Software Product Management, as evidenced by the survey conducted by Springer and Miler [SM22]. With the adoption of an SPL, teams become more cross-functional, collaborating on a shared set of core assets that are reusable across multiple projects. This shift optimizes resource utilization, shortens time-to-market, and allows for a stronger focus on innovation. The emphasis on component reusability, configuration-based customization, and unified documentation streamlines processes, thereby enhancing overall efficiency. Although this transition requires careful planning and change management, it ultimately fosters a culture of collaboration, efficiency, and adaptability within the organization. In the context of ITK, the outcomes and guidelines were presented and discussed with the development team, who expressed a strong desire to remain well-informed and actively involved. Consequently, we engaged in continuous collaboration with them throughout the entire migration process. The findings from ITK were compared with other reports on SPL adoption, focusing on the strategies and experiences outlined in the literature. The ITK action plan aligns with the IDEAL improvement model [McF96], reflecting a structured approach to continuous enhancement. The project began with the team's decision to proceed with the migration, at which point we initiated our investigation into the existing practices and the team's perception of the project. The next steps in ITK’s adoption include formulating a strategy, implementing the action plan, and, in keeping with agile practices, evaluating outcomes to identify successes, failures, and opportunities for improvement, fostering continuous refinement. The guidelines presented here serve as a method for directing the design of the action plan. Notably, some of the challenges encountered diverged from those commonly reported in the literature, such as the lack of skilled personnel or insufficient managerial support, which were not perceived as barriers within the company due to its prevailing organizational mindset.

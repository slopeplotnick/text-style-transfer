Text vs. numeric signals. Numeric-only inputs are viable but clearly weaker than text-only analysis. However, combining both modalities recovers most of the performance of the best text-based variant, indicating a practical hierarchy: textual information is primary, with numeric features serving as complementary signals. Signals hierarchy. Across datasets and tasks, a consistent hierarchy of importance emerges. Text-based models are highly effective for fake-news detection—on EVONS and tweet series from FakeNewsNet, a light nonlinear layer atop transformer embeddings already approaches ceiling performance and surpasses classical baselines. In contrast, numeric-only inputs underperform significantly; for instance, in virality prediction on FakeNewsNet, a numeric GRU trails its text-only counterpart by a wide margin. Overall, text remains the dominant input, while numeric data contributes secondary cues. Nonetheless, this trade-off should be stated explicitly. In high-stakes scenarios where misclassification carries costs, standard F1-selected checkpoints remain preferable to ensure balanced sensitivity and precision. Architecture vs. input view. On FakeNewsNet, various sequence encoders (GRU, LSTM, RNN, CNN, Transformer) show competitive results across tasks, with the Transformer offering a slight edge. The similarity in performance across models suggests that input modality (text, numeric, or fusion) and label definitions have greater impact than architectural variations—particularly under practical constraints. Practical recommendations. (1) Treat virality prediction as a family of tasks, each indexed by a defined threshold and time horizon; always report these definitions alongside metrics. (2) Use text-only baselines as a strong starting point for fake-news detection, adding light numeric or metadata features when available. (3) In early-screening settings, consider recall-oriented selection but clearly document associated precision trade-offs. (4) Favor simple, well-specified architectures, and prioritize data integrity through careful curation, leakage control, and transparent protocol reporting.

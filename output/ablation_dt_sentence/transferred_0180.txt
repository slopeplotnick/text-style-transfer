The language acquisition experiments aim to integrate explicit grammatical knowledge into a lexicon, enabling the daughter agent to utilize this knowledge in conversations with the mother. To achieve this, the system incorporates statistical machine learning techniques, which facilitate the generation of an explicitly defined grammatical representation. This approach enhances existing systems based on statistical methods and neural networks by providing a more interpretable, rule-based framework for language modeling. Furthermore, by applying Zipf's law (Mandelbrot, 1954; Zipf, 1949), this study offers a foundational evaluation of the feasibility of implementing a multi-agent simulation for first language acquisition. Importantly, the objective of this study extends beyond a mere binary classification of function and content words; it uses this classification as a tool for assessing the validity of a novel framework for modeling first language acquisition. Zipf's law provides a solid and dependable basis for evaluating the system's performance, a practice that is well-established within the field. For instance, Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) apply Zipf's law to develop a multi-agent framework for modeling language evolution, with recent contributions from Dębowski (2021), Takahashi and Tanaka-Ishii (2019), and Takamichi et al. (2024). The experiments in this study validate all necessary components for a multi-agent model of first language acquisition. Specifically, the acquisition of function and content words utilizes a hybrid strategy grounded in statistical tendencies that support the acquisition of discrete grammatical rules. As a result, these findings support the modeling of more complex grammatical structures and demonstrate the viability of the MODOMA research agenda. Building on these results, Shakouri et al. (2025) further demonstrate how grammatical categories, such as nouns, verbs, and prepositions, can be acquired through unsupervised learning techniques. This paper presents two experiments that illustrate the proposed approach: the first, discussed in Sections 6 and 7, utilizes a training data set generated by the mother agent to determine the experimental settings for acquiring the targeted linguistic features. The second experiment, which uses a novel test data set generated by the adult language model, serves to validate this configuration, as detailed in Section 8. The results suggest that the MODOMA system is capable of acquiring these grammatical categories and encoding them within the grammatical formalism employed by the daughter agent.

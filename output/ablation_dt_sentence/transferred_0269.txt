The individual serves as a Scientist in the field of Tech AI and holds the position of Executive Director at the Machine Learning Center at the Georgia Institute of Technology. He also holds the Rhesa S. Farmer Distinguished Chair of Advanced Computing Concepts and is a Georgia Research Alliance Eminent Scholar. He completed his PhD in Electrical Engineering at the Georgia Institute of Technology and is recognized as an IEEE Fellow. His extensive career includes previous positions at leading institutions such as SRI International, Nuance Communications, Yahoo!, Microsoft, Google, Viv Labs, and Samsung. His research spans a wide range of topics, including acoustics, active noise and vibration control, speaker recognition, web search, artificial intelligence virtual assistants, natural language processing, and conversational AI. This tutorial is aimed at researchers and practitioners working at the intersection of conversational agents and spoken information systems. While a foundational understanding of NLP and speech processing is assumed, prior experience with system deployment is advantageous but not required. The tutorial will start at a gradual pace to ensure accessibility for a broader audience. In addition to the works highlighted above, several other papers offer foundational insights relevant to this tutorial. These include early research on tagging and recognizing conversational speech (Stolcke et al., 2000), examinations of the complexities of spoken language and its implications for human-machine interaction (Moore, 2017), approaches to dialogue learning through human teaching and feedback in task-oriented systems (Liu et al., 2018), techniques for reprogramming acoustic models for time series classification (Yang et al., 2021), recent advancements in generative speech recognition leveraging large language models (Chen et al., 2023a), and the development of SpeechGPT, which integrates cross-modal conversational capabilities into large-scale language models (Zhang et al., 2023). Although the tutorial will reference a substantial number of papers, the primary focus will be on 7-8 key works, with only 1-2 of them authored by the presenter.

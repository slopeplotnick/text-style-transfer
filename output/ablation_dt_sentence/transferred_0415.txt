Introducing DeepSeek-V3.2, a model that seamlessly combines high computational efficiency with superior reasoning and agent capabilities. The pivotal technical advancements of DeepSeek-V3.2 include: (1) DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA): With DSA, we unveil an efficient attention mechanism that drastically cuts down computational complexity while sustaining model performance in long-context scenarios. (2) Scalable Reinforcement Learning Framework: Utilizing a robust reinforcement learning protocol and expanding compute resources post-training, DeepSeek-V3.2 matches the capabilities of GPT-5. Remarkably, our high-compute variant, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, outperforms GPT-5 and showcases reasoning skills comparable to Gemini-3.0-Pro, achieving top accolades in the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) and the International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI). (3) Large-Scale Agentic Task Synthesis Pipeline: To embed reasoning within tool-use contexts, we innovated a synthesis pipeline that systematically generates large-scale training data. This approach enhances scalable agentic post-training, significantly boosting generalization and instruction-following strength in complex, interactive environments. The debut of reasoning models (DeepSeek-AI, 2025; OpenAI, 2024a) marked a crucial milestone in the progression of Large Language Models (LLMs), driving a substantial leap in performance across measurable fields. Since this pivotal event, the capabilities of LLMs have progressed rapidly. However, in recent months, a noticeable divergence has emerged. While the open-source community (MiniMax, 2025; MoonShot, 2025; Qwen, 2025; ZhiPu-AI, 2025) continues to advance, the performance trajectory of closed-source models (Anthropic, 2025b; DeepMind, 2025a; OpenAI, 2025) has escalated at a notably sharper rate. Consequently, rather than converging, the performance gap between closed-source and open-source models is expanding, with proprietary systems exhibiting increasingly superior abilities in complex tasks. Our analysis highlights three critical deficiencies hindering open-source models in complex tasks. Firstly, architecturally, reliance on vanilla attention mechanisms (Vaswani et al., 2017) imposes substantial efficiency constraints for long sequences, obstructing scalable deployment and effective post-training. Secondly, resource allocation is suboptimal, with insufficient computational investment during the post-training phase, limiting performance in demanding tasks. Finally, open-source models exhibit slower advancement in generalization and instruction-following capabilities compared to proprietary models (EvalSys, 2025; Li et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2025), impairing their efficacy in real-world applications.

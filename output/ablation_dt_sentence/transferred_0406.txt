Despite the evolution of optimization approaches, traditional methods exhibit fundamental limitations when applied to construction coordination scenarios. The need for complete problem specification at the planning stage conflicts with the dynamic nature of construction, where task requirements, environmental conditions, and resource availability continuously change throughout project execution. Moreover, computational complexity often becomes prohibitive for large-scale projects involving numerous robots and complex task dependencies. These ongoing challenges have motivated researchers to explore learning-based coordination approaches that can adapt to dynamic environments without requiring full problem specification. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a transformative approach to multi-robot task allocation, capable of learning optimal coordination policies from environmental interaction without needing comprehensive pre-defined plans. RL frameworks enable robots to develop coordination expertise through trial-and-error while adapting to environmental changes and evolving task requirements. However, these processes necessitate extensive training iterations, posing significant safety risks in construction environments where errors could endanger workers or damage equipment. Additionally, the time required for training is impractical for projects with tight schedules. As a consequence, researchers have developed construction-specific RL approaches. In 2022, Lee et al. introduced a digital twin-driven Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) method for adaptive task allocation in robotic construction, integrating Building Information Modeling (BIM) with DRL to enable context-aware coordination decisions considering spatial constraints, temporal dependencies, and safety requirements. The digital twin framework facilitated safer policy learning in simulation before real-world deployment. Nonetheless, RL's exploration requirements still clash with construction safety needs, as even simulation-trained policies may result in dangerous coordination failures in real-world scenarios. Furthermore, transferring learned policies from simulation to real construction sites with varied environmental conditions remains a significant challenge. These sim-to-real transfer issues have driven the development of advanced RL techniques.

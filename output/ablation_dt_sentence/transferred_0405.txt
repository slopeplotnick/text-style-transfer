When a target appears on the left or right side of the screen, the participant is prompted to open and close the corresponding fist until the target disappears, followed by a period of relaxation. Similarly, when a target appears at the top or bottom of the screen, it prompts the subject to open and close both fists if the target is at the top, or both feet if the target is at the bottom, until the target disappears, followed by relaxation. By focusing on specific channels and optimizing feature extraction accordingly, we ensured that the data captured relevant motor and premotor brain activities for effective motor imagery task classification. To mitigate the confounding effects of evoked responses that could bias data interpretation, we devised a targeted temporal analysis strategy. Our approach involved analyzing epochs commencing one second post-stimulus, thereby excluding the immediate post-stimulus interval typically saturated with evoked responses. This shift allowed us to concentrate on the ensuing spontaneous brain activity. By emphasizing these more stable attributes indicative of the subject's sensorimotor state, our method enhances the accuracy of brain signal classification. Various combinations of data preprocessing techniques were applied before training the classical machine learning algorithms. Initially, we used signal processing methods such as Common Spatial Patterns (CSP), which are widely employed in EEG signal classification. This technique, first described by Koles et al., is utilized to extract and discriminate features by optimizing variance between two different motor imagery tasks or mental states. It involves transforming EEG signals into a new vector space, where the variance for one class is maximized while minimized for the other class. The goal of this method is to identify and apply spatial filters to highlight significant differences between several classes. CSP is a multi-stage process that begins with the calculation of covariance matrices for each EEG signal class.

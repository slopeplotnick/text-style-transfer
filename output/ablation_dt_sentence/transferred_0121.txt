The following section outlines key methodological considerations pertaining to the present study. Nonparametric comparisons between real and fake datasets revealed no significant differences in central tendency, as indicated by Mann-Whitney U tests (U = 46912-53698, p = .09-.89). However, Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests revealed significant differences in the overall distribution of scores for all variables (D = 0.36-0.42, all p < .001). These findings suggest that while the central tendency of the simulated data approximated the median values of the real responses, their underlying distributions differed substantially in shape. Therefore, this partial support for our fourth hypothesis is depicted in Figure 2, which presents graphical comparisons between the real and synthetic datasets. To mitigate potential bias from using data previously collected by our own research team, we replicated the procedure outlined in Study 1 using data gathered by an independent research group. The ICT-SC25 dataset was selected to assess individuals' self-reported proficiency in technology-related tasks, comprising 25 items across five domains: communication, information management, content creation, safety practices, and problem-solving. This dataset was chosen due to its extensive validation and widespread availability to the public. In line with Study 1, the hypotheses and procedures for this study were preregistered prior to generating the synthetic dataset, with the preregistration available for consultation at the following link. The data synthesis procedure was modified in this iteration to accommodate the absence of ethnicity variables in the updated dataset; consequently, only age and gender were incorporated as conditioning features for the simulated observations. Furthermore, each individual in the dataset was assigned a unique identifier, enabling the alignment of simulated participants with their real-world counterparts based on age and gender. Duplicate IDs in the original dataset were removed to ensure precise matching between the real and simulated samples.

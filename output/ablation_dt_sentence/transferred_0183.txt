Our large-scale benchmark reaffirms that spatial methods, particularly covariance tangent-space projection and Common Spatial Patterns, remain the gold standard for EEG motor imagery decoding under controlled conditions. However, their efficacy significantly deteriorates with increased inter- and intra-subject variability. By exhaustively evaluating each pipeline at the subject level, we demonstrate conclusively that no universal configuration suits all individuals. Instead, our findings highlight that the most promising path forward lies in adaptive methodologies that dynamically align with each user's unique neurophysiological signature. Furthermore, certain nonlinear features, such as Hjorth parameters, Higuchi fractal dimension, and singular value decomposition entropy, exhibited substantial potential for specific individuals. This observation suggests that capturing richer neural dynamics could greatly enhance personalized decoding and implies that "BCI illiteracy" may more accurately be attributed to mismatches between decoding algorithms and user-specific neurophysiological characteristics rather than inherent user limitations. Therefore, future research should prioritize exploring sophisticated ensemble learning frameworks that integrate linear spatial filters with nonlinear dynamics, effectively leveraging their complementary strengths. Expanding this benchmark to incorporate broader paradigms, such as SSVEP, P300, CVEP, and invasive intracortical recordings, will further assess the generalizability of our conclusions. Moreover, rigorous evaluation of state-of-the-art deep learning models (CNNs, LSTMs, Transformers), alongside emerging quantum-inspired models like quantum support-vector machines and hybrid quantum neural networks, represents a critical frontier. These quantum methodologies, exploiting the uniquely high-dimensional properties of Hilbert spaces, may offer unprecedented robustness against the intrinsic noise and complexity of EEG signals. Another key direction involves enhancing cross-subject generalization through transfer learning and domain adaptation techniques, which have shown great promise in improving model robustness and scalability across heterogeneous user populations. Finally, reinforcing reproducibility through open-access benchmarking initiatives such as MOABB will facilitate standardized, transparent comparisons of EEG decoding algorithms.

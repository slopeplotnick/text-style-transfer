To refine these results, we repeated the analyses using the ATTARI subscales. Only the behavioral subscale (BEH) emerged as a significant predictor, whereas the affective and cognitive subscales did not contribute once entered simultaneously (see supplements S3). This suggests that the predictive power of AI attitudes is primarily driven by participants' willingness to engage with AI in practice, rather than by their general beliefs or feelings. To complement the quantitative analyses and uncover participants' subjective evaluation criteria, we conducted a thematic analysis of the 178 open-ended responses. An overview of the main themes is presented in Table 2, with the full codebook available in the Supplementary Materials. Categories such as music quality, emotional impact, and humanness emerged from the qualitative material. Participants frequently described AI-generated music in terms of its technical and musical quality, its originality, and its ability to evoke emotions. Notably, humanness was the most frequent theme, with participants linking it not only to perceived sound characteristics but also to the human element behind the music. Several respondents emphasized the importance of the artist’s personality and the narrative behind a song, describing AI-generated music as lacking soul and authenticity. However, some participants expressed indifference to the song's origin, with one stating, "If it sounds good then I don't mind if it was artificially generated" (ID926). These responses further suggest that attitudes toward AI significantly shape how individuals evaluate AI-generated music. Many responses were explicitly normative and did not reference the quality or characteristics of the songs, such as when one participant expressed, "I don't like anything to do with AI-generated music" (ID914). Moreover, the open-ended responses highlighted several additional areas of evaluation that are often overlooked in experimental studies. Notably, participants assessed AI-generated music based on ethical criteria, with concerns including the potential replacement of musicians, the economic harm to artists, and the transparency of training data and copyright issues. One participant conveyed this sentiment by stating, "AI is built upon data without the consent of the artists who have dedicated decades of their lives to the craft" (ID615). Another participant noted, "I think it undermines and abuses artists" (ID446), indicating that listeners not only assess the aesthetic qualities of AI music but also consider the fairness of its production and distribution.

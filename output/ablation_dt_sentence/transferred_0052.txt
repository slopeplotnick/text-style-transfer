When the target is far away, the character transitions to a fast running behavior to close the distance. These nuanced actions emerge naturally from the combination of a stationary motion prior with a simple objective of approaching the target. No motion planner is needed to explicitly dictate the character’s movement strategy. SMP enables the policy to adapt behaviors from the dataset and synthesize new skills tailored to different tasks. In the challenging Dodgeball task, agents trained with SMP spontaneously develop agile jumping and dodging maneuvers, closely resembling human strategies in dodgeball, despite these behaviors not being present in the original dataset. In contrast, AMP performs poorly on this task, likely due to the instability of the adversarial objective when the necessary dodging skills diverge significantly from the locomotion-focused motions in the reference data. Moreover, policies trained with AMP using a frozen discriminator similarly fail to produce naturalistic behaviors or achieve strong task performance, suggesting that adversarial priors cannot be reliably reused, even for the same task. By comparison, SMP supports the reuse of a single motion prior across multiple tasks and policies. As shown in Figure 7, SMP also yields greater sample efficiency by consistently offering stable and informative guidance during training. Recovering from fallen states is a fundamental skill for both humans and humanoid agents. However, this task is particularly difficult, as many methods depend on auxiliary rewards to prevent excessively erratic motions. AMP, which updates the motion prior during training, can learn realistic get-up behaviors. Nevertheless, as demonstrated in our approach, SMP can also train a robust get-up policy even with a fixed motion prior, successfully enabling recovery from a variety of randomly initialized fallen poses. In Figure 5(e), for instance, the character is knocked a significant distance yet manages to roll over, push itself up with its hands, and return to an upright position.

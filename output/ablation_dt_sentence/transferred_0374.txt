Taken together, these results demonstrate that OMNIGUARD exhibits substantial cross-modal generalization. It not only transfers safety reasoning from trained modalities to untrained ones but also acquires modality-invariant semantic representations of unsafe content. In conclusion, we introduce OMNIGUARD-7B and OMNIGUARD-3B, the first family of omni-modal guardrails trained on a comprehensive, unified safety fine-tuning dataset that covers both unimodal and cross-modal samples. OMNIGUARD represents a unified omni-modal safety solution, capable of moderating and reasoning about unsafe content in heterogeneous and cross-modal settings. Extensive experiments reveal that OMNIGUARD-7B consistently outperforms existing guardrail models across all modalities. Meanwhile, OMNIGUARD-3B demonstrates competitive performance relative to large-scale LLMs and MLLMs, such as Qwen3-235B and Qwen3-VL-235B. These results underscore the robust omni-modal safety detection and reasoning capabilities of our approach, validating the feasibility of a unified guardrail system with comprehensive omni-modal understanding and interpretability. Future work will focus on investigating more complex, cross-modal safety scenarios to further advance omni-modal safeguarding in next-generation large language models. Despite its strong omni-modal safety reasoning and consistent performance across modalities, OMNIGUARD has certain limitations. While incorporating reasoning paths significantly enhances the interpretability and reliability of safety assessments, it also increases inference latency due to the additional reasoning generation step. As a result, OMNIGUARD incurs higher computational overhead compared to lightweight, binary-classification guardrail systems. The trade-off between the depth of safety reasoning and inference latency can be further explored across different use cases and scenarios to optimize both safety robustness and efficiency. Moreover, the limited availability of publicly accessible cross-modal safety fine-tuning datasets leaves room for significant improvement in moderating more complex, interleaved multimodal safety scenarios. We anticipate that future research will continue to enhance the robustness and reliability of omni-modal systems, further improving their ability to safeguard against risks across diverse modality combinations.

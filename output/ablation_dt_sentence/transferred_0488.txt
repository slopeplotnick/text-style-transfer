We began our qualitative analysis by carefully reading the interview summaries several times. During the interviews, discussions were recorded, handwritten notes were taken, and the recorded content was later summarized. This combined approach aims to ensure that all relevant information is captured and to minimize the risk of missing critical details, aligning with the common and recommended practice of recording and transcribing interviews in qualitative research. The analysis was conducted by the research team, which included the person who conducted the interviews. Subsequently, we designed a set of metrics for a quantitative analysis. These metrics involved a combination of response tallying and frequency analysis: initially, we counted how many good practices were identified, followed by the benefits, risks, and fears. To address these questions, we identified similar responses and distilled them into simple words encapsulating the main ideas. For instance, "The genericity pushes to factorize more and more" was summarized with the words "genericity" and "factorisation." For each topic, we first determined the frequency of generically expressed terms, then attributed them to the respective speakers. The identification of potential words followed a structured procedure. We initially sorted words by frequency, having removed stop-words and punctuation. The most meaningful words were selected, and their contextual use was verified within the documents. We enriched our identified words to form compound words, adding adverbs like "too complex" instead of merely "complex" to better convey meaning. Furthermore, we compared the terminologies identified in the interviews with those in scholarly literature. In cases of a negative match, we sought more appropriate terms, such as replacing "unusable" with "useless." Conversely, positive matches confirmed the alignment of terms with scholarly language. This process allowed us to refine and improve our synthesized answers with knowledge from literature, thereby enhancing our data analysis by extracting the most relevant and meaningful terms.

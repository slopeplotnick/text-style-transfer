To address RQ2, we evaluated our models on 11 low-resource languages, each selectively sampled from 8 distinct language groups. Interestingly, languages written in the Latin script, such as Galician and Slovenian, exhibited more stable performance across benchmarks. This consistency is likely due to the alignment of their scripts and subword tokenization coverage in the shared XLM-R vocabulary, which favors languages with higher representation during pretraining. In contrast, Icelandic, despite also using the Latin script, performed less reliably due to its rich and complex morphological structure, which results in high token fragmentation and less coherent sentence representations. These challenges limit the effectiveness of multilingual encoders that rely on subword tokenization strategies, such as SentencePiece in XLM-R. To assess the extent to which training on a high-resource language supports transfer within and across clusters, and to address RQ3, we trained models with the following language configurations: (i) Hindi-only, (ii) Chinese-only, and (iii) Hindi+Chinese combined. Each model was evaluated across six languages from the Indic (Hindi) and East-Southeast Asian (Chinese) clusters. Within the Indic cluster, the Hindi-trained model consistently outperformed the Chinese-trained model across all benchmarks. In the East-Southeast Asian cluster, the Chinese-trained model outperformed the Hindi-trained model for most of the benchmarks. This demonstrates that transfer within a cluster from its own high-resource language to other member languages is more effective than transfer across clusters from a high-resource language of a different group.

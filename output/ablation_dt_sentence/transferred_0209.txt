In all three models, the total causal influence of *young* on both the image and the prediction remains consistent, illustrating that it serves as a reliable measure independent of the level of abstraction. However, if an explanation model treats variables as independent and fails to respect their causal structure, only the direct effect is captured—an effect that equals the total effect in the first model but diminishes to zero in the final one. Therefore, direct influence cannot be considered a consistently reliable measure. Section 5.3.1 discusses the challenges that emerge when model assumptions are violated, underscoring the need for careful consideration in model selection. Whenever such assumptions are found to introduce bias into explanations, the model must either be adapted, or—if adaptation is not feasible—the explainer must be made aware of the biased context within which the explanations hold. This, however, should not be taken to suggest that more complex models with fewer assumptions are always preferable to simpler alternatives. Rather, to ensure that explanations align with their expected causal interpretation, the causal model must reflect a level of abstraction compatible with the explainee’s conceptual understanding of the world—understood to be imprecise but not incorrect. In this context, promoting understandability may require abstracting away from certain low-level causal mechanisms, thereby condensing relevant information into a more accessible and interpretable representation. Another notable advantage of the causal explanation model introduced in Section 5.3.1 lies in its reliance on expert knowledge, which makes it more robust in identifying and eliminating spurious correlations than approaches that rely solely on data-driven methods. Consequently, the explanation model can reveal spurious relationships learned by the predictor—even in cases where both models are trained on the same dataset. With expert knowledge becoming increasingly accessible through large language models (LLMs), as illustrated in the accompanying example, this development holds particular promise for improving model reliability by facilitating the detection and correction of unintended model behaviors.

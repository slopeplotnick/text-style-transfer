Most systems rely on isolated documents or facts as discrete storage units; however, financial prediction requires the organization of events in sequential order to capture the temporal dependencies and interrelationships that drive the progression of events over time. In the domain of stock prediction, it is essential to focus on analogous historical scenarios rather than relying solely on isolated documents. This approach enables a more comprehensive understanding of the underlying patterns and temporal dependencies that influence stock price movements. While generic systems often use time-based or simplistic human memory models for forgetting, financial systems must preserve key patterns over extended periods to maintain their relevance and effectiveness in decision-making. To address these needs, this study proposes an innovative approach to enhancing memory structures for financial applications, focusing on the construction of a dynamic and current repository of financial knowledge tailored for language models. The system developed in this work is designed to transform unstructured market data into structured memory units, annotated with temporal markers, event types, and relevant entities such as companies and industries. This organization of market information into structured sequences enhances the identification and analysis of underlying patterns. Moreover, a method was developed to identify analogous event sequences, providing an improvement over relying solely on individual documents to interpret meaning. By leveraging the current event sequence as a query, the system effectively retrieves historically analogous market patterns, capturing complex temporal structures and contextual similarities relevant to the present scenario. This transition from simple text matching to pattern identification significantly enhances the system’s ability to predict future trends. Our evaluation demonstrates that the proposed system outperforms strong baseline models in stock prediction accuracy. Furthermore, through explicit modeling of event sequences, the framework effectively identifies key events that drive stock price fluctuations and elucidates their temporal evolution, thereby improving both the interpretability and reliability of predictive outcomes.

Distribution matching offers an alternative to frame-by-frame motion tracking by training controllers to imitate the overall behavioral distribution of a motion dataset. Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) operationalizes this idea using a learned discriminator to distinguish between the agent’s generated motions and those in the reference dataset. Building on this framework, Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) enable controllers to synthesize realistic and naturalistic behaviors for novel tasks that are not explicitly represented in the original dataset. However, these adversarial priors must be trained jointly with a specific policy for each new task, often requiring retraining and continuous access to the dataset. In contrast, our approach achieves comparable performance to existing methods by introducing a reusable motion prior that can be adapted to a variety of styles beyond those contained in the original dataset. *Diffusion Models for Control.* The state-of-the-art performance of diffusion models across a wide range of domains has sparked increasing interest in their application to control. A natural use case is to deploy task-oriented diffusion models as motion planners that predict future movements, which are then executed by a low-level tracking controller. Beyond motion planning, diffusion models have also been employed to directly regulate behaviors in tasks such as manipulation and character control, thereby enabling flexible and diverse actions. Unlike these prior approaches that incorporate diffusion models as components of a controller, our method repurposes pretrained diffusion models as task-agnostic behavioral priors within an optimization framework. Specifically, we utilize diffusion models as a reward function within the reinforcement learning paradigm, facilitating policy learning through score distillation sampling.

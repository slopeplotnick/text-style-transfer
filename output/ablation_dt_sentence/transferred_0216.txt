To construct an effective relevance model, it is essential to understand the nature of items, the structure of queries, and the mechanisms for mapping paths between them. Moreover, to effectively handle a vast corpus of items and address diverse user intents—ranging from explicit queries to more abstract or inferred needs—the model must be scalable and adaptable. This requires the model to leverage external knowledge and logical inference, enabling it to disambiguate user queries and accurately interpret abstract intents during path mapping. In addition, the model must integrate both textual and visual elements to ensure alignment and matching of details, which is crucial when addressing items that depend heavily on visual content. Furthermore, it is imperative that the system acquires and adheres to complex rules, ensuring the consistency and reliability of the path-following process. In the context of large-scale model training for relevance assessment, the problem is naturally formulated as a supervised classification task, where the model is optimized to distinguish varying degrees of relevance based on labeled training data. Initially, early approaches employed prompts to define tasks and responses to exhibit outcomes, refining models through supervised learning techniques. However, this simplistic approach proved inadequate when the data deviated from the model's training distribution. Prior research has shown that enhancing the model’s internal reasoning process before generating responses improves its performance on more challenging tasks. Due to limitations in computational resources and model deployment, smaller models are typically chosen, although these models often face constraints in reasoning abilities and instruction-following performance when relying solely on prompt-based inputs. Consequently, the current methodology involves utilizing larger teacher models, such as GPT-4 or DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025), to generate reasoning data, which is then used to train smaller models via supervised fine-tuning (SFT), transferring reasoning capabilities. Some studies further incorporate a reinforcement learning phase, utilizing offline RL methods such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) or KTO, or online RL methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

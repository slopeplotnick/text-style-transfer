UnresolvedRetry refers to the situation where an agent performs multiple consecutive edits on the same file, but none of these attempts result in a successful modification. For example, Figure 12-a illustrates a case where the SWE-agent Dev repeatedly applies `str_replace` operations to the `utils.py` file, executing the command 183 times without success, ultimately reaching the predefined cost limit. StrNotFound arises when a modification fails because the specified old string is not present in the file, as shown in Figure 13-a. This type of failure typically occurs due to formatting discrepancies or minor textual variations. NoEffectEdit occurs when the old and new strings are identical, resulting in no effective change to the file. Figure 13-b exemplifies this, where the replacement command substitutes a string with itself, thus leading to no modification and the unnecessary consumption of computational resources. AmbiguousTarget occurs when the specified old string appears multiple times within the file, creating ambiguity about which occurrence should be replaced. Figure 13-c demonstrates this issue, where multiple identical if statements hinder the editor's ability to deterministically apply the replacement, revealing a key limitation of string-based editing mechanisms. These patterns were analyzed in the context of 4000 cases from the GRAPHEC-TORY dataset. Figure 14 summarizes the distribution of these four inefficiency patterns across all agents and models, distinguishing between resolved (Figure 14-a) and unresolved instances (Figure 14-b). The percentages beneath the Venn diagrams indicate the proportion of instances that exhibit at least one inefficiency pattern. Notably, even in resolved instances, inefficiencies remain prevalent, underscoring the importance of a process-centric evaluation to fully understand agent behavior. Unresolved cases often involve multiple inefficiency patterns within a single instance. Interestingly, stronger models, such as Claude Sonnet 4, achieve higher success rates but also exhibit a greater prevalence of these patterns. Upon further analysis, it appears that this model engages in extensive internal reasoning before generating a solution. While this approach is beneficial for tackling difficult problems, as evidenced by the model's higher success rate, it can also introduce inefficiencies when the additional steps do not lead to improved solutions.

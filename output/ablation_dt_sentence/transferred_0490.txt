In semantically-focused transfer learning tasks, we typically adhere to a prescribed format for task definitions and requirements as a template, subsequently integrating the query and item details from each dataset to construct the prompt. To ensure the optimal functionality of the base model, it is necessary to incorporate essential information such as task definitions, judgment criteria, and output formats within the prompt. Moreover, to guide the model in adhering effectively to our bifurcated methodology, structured instructional information is incorporated: firstly, the model deciphers the query's requirements and relevant attributes; subsequently, it extracts the appropriate attributes from item information and makes judgments conforming to the established rules. In reasoning tasks, providing more detailed and explicit instructions within the prompt facilitates the model's ability to accurately derive the correct answer. However, in the context of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), this situation does not invariably occur, as the model predominantly derives its capabilities from learning response data rather than solely adhering to the instructions provided. To ascertain the optimal design for prompt configuration, we investigated three distinct versions. The comprehensive version encompasses the entire task specifications, regulations, and guidelines, extending beyond 7,000 tokens. Meanwhile, the mid version streamlines this by removing detailed rules and retaining only essential instructions, encompassing approximately 800 tokens. Conversely, the abbreviated version is the most succinct, offering merely a fundamental role delineation, e.g., "You are an e-commerce relevance assessment expert. Evaluate the query and item." This phase signifies the initial activation of the model, aimed at augmenting its fundamental discriminative capabilities. To achieve this, it is imperative to utilize a high-quality, comprehensive training dataset. Regarding the expansive aspect, it is crucial to employ a scientific sampling strategy to ensure comprehensive coverage of diverse e-commerce data categories, thereby mitigating the risks associated with oversampling or undersampling any single type.

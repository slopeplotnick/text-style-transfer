The global shift towards greater electrification has directed attention to the exploration of critical minerals. Despite the promise of growing demand, mineral exploration remains a financially inadequate industry, with substantial investments over the last two decades yielding a marked decline in new discoveries. In this paper, I propose utilizing artificial intelligence to enhance the scientific rigor of mineral exploration, aiming to reduce cognitive bias and false positives while lowering exploration costs and enhancing domain experts' roles. However, the current methodologies employed in mineral exploration, which involve various scientific fields such as geology, geochemistry, and geophysics, are no longer effective in uncovering concealed mineral deposits. Specifically, the prevailing approach fails to adequately quantify uncertainty, resulting in suboptimal decision-making and financial resources being allocated to drilling projects that often lead to unsuccessful outcomes. Instead, I advocate for a novel scientific approach grounded in the principles of Bayesianism and falsification. This method redefines data collection primarily as a means to challenge prevailing human concepts, with decisions on data acquisition assessed using verifiable metrics and rational decision-making. To make this comprehensive framework practical in any exploration campaign, the employment of various forms of AI technology is imperative. Among these, novel unsupervised learning methodologies collaborate with domain experts to enhance data comprehension and generate diverse geological hypotheses, while human-in-the-loop AI algorithms play a pivotal role in optimally planning data acquisition, prioritizing uncertainty reduction in geological hypotheses over mineral grade and tonnage assessments. This methodology will be demonstrated through several contemporary case studies in mineral exploration and resource assessment.

Given real-time latency and cost constraints, deploying our LLM or deep BERT-based classifier directly within the online search system is impractical. Therefore, we employ a teacher-student distillation method, where a simpler model is trained under the guidance of a more complex model, as illustrated in Figure 1. Our online model is a simplified adaptation of the BERT architecture, designed to be lightweight and efficient. To ensure effective learning, we utilize a deep BERT model as the teacher, rather than opting for an alternative model architecture. Moreover, we integrate key insights derived from an alternative model into the BERT architecture to deepen the understanding of how specific search queries align with product results. To address the challenge of extraneous and irrelevant information from lengthy explanations, we introduce a model that identifies and refines product details, determining their alignment or misalignment with the search query. This enables the prioritization of key elements within the simpler BERT model. Since the simplified model cannot incorporate these details during real-time inference, we solely integrate them into the teacher model to maintain consistency. Subsequently, the simpler model is trained through representation alignment with the teacher model. Given the lack of publicly available datasets for Chinese e-commerce search tasks, we conduct experiments using a large-scale real-world dataset from user search logs in the JD application.

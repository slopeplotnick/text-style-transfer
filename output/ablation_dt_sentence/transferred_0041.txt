Such findings highlight the role of listeners' pre-existing attitudes and expectations. For instance, Hong et al. (2021) showed that a more favorable attitude toward creative AI was associated with higher music ratings, in a study focused on people's expectancies of AI-generated music across various genres. Notably, expectancy violations negatively affected evaluations, although no significant genre differences emerged. Another central question in the evaluation of AI-generated music is whether the output is perceived as human-composed. In the context of this study, it is crucial to identify the aspects of music that lead to participants' attributions of either human or AI authorship. Shank et al. (2023) suggested that genre plays a significant role, with electronic music more frequently attributed to AI composers. Interestingly, when participants were misled about the true origin of the music, they reported perceiving musical differences that did not actually exist, indicating that expectations and beliefs about humanness influence aesthetic judgment (Ansani et al., 2024). In line with these findings, Déguernel et al. (2022) conducted a qualitative analysis revealing evaluation strategies based on musical characteristics: participants identified AI-generated music through excessive repetition, rigid or unnatural structure, and atypical harmonic or melodic progressions. In contrast, human compositions were often associated with melodic variation, ornamentation, stylistic familiarity, and features suggesting instrumental playability. These associations reflect a broader trend in which musical features perceived as "human" are evaluated more positively, while those linked to AI are viewed less favorably. Consistent with this, Hong et al. (2022) investigated how perceived anthropomorphism and creative autonomy shape the acceptance of AI musicians. Participants evaluated AI-generated pieces across multiple genres, rating both the music’s quality and their attitudes toward AI agents. The results revealed that higher levels of anthropomorphism and greater perceived autonomy were associated with increased acceptance of AI musicians. Moreover, Sun et al. (2023) showed that anthropomorphism impacts perceptions of competence and warmth, both of which contribute to more favorable attitudes toward AI musicians.

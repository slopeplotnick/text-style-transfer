However, even after data cleaning procedures, CPV data cannot completely eliminate noise stemming from deficiencies in item information quality. Moreover, item SKU features exhibit considerable redundancy in the feature space due to their inherent attribute characteristics. As a result, we extract a portion of the data and partition it into training and test sets to validate feature effectiveness. The experiments begin with product titles as the baseline, progressively incorporating additional features to evaluate their incremental impact. As illustrated in Table 2, integrating both CPV and SKU features improves the model's performance, suggesting that when input information remains relevant and stable, it can still enhance model performance despite the presence of redundancy and noise. Additionally, we introduced main item images to offer visual features that text alone cannot adequately capture. SKU images were excluded, as the main image sufficiently conveys most of the visual information. SKU images generally represent minor variations, such as differences in style or color, which are already reflected in the corresponding SKU textual features. Including them would increase model complexity without contributing substantial informational value. In the end, we amassed a comprehensive product information repository, which will serve as the foundation for all subsequent training. To identify the optimal base model, we considered two key factors: its capacity to support the complex reasoning and knowledge integration mechanisms discussed in Section 2, and its computational efficiency for practical deployment. We selected a range of high-performance open-source models with parameter sizes between 7B and 14B, ensuring adequate capability while allowing training and inference on a single GPU. Next, a subset of the existing annotated data was selected and divided into training and validation sets. Using the basic relevance task definition as the prompt, we performed straightforward fine-tuning, as outlined in Section 3.1, for each model on the training set and evaluated their performance. Ultimately, the Qwen2.5-7B model demonstrated the best overall performance among all evaluated models while maintaining high inference efficiency. Consequently, we chose it as the foundational model for our framework.

CAPO is designed to leverage advantage as a dynamic signal that evolves in alignment with the model’s growing competence. Since the introduction of curriculum learning by Bengio et al. (2009), most methods have relied on static heuristics, such as arranging tasks from easy to hard. Subsequent adaptive curriculum approaches have attempted to estimate difficulty through expert annotations or model performance metrics (Shi et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025), yet these strategies remain external and heuristic, relying on manually defined proxies rather than signals intrinsic to the model’s evolving capabilities. This limitation motivates the development of CAPO, which incorporates advantage estimates as an intrinsic, competence-aware signal to guide dynamic curriculum scheduling. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CAPO consistently enhances mathematical reasoning across a variety of advantage-based RL algorithms, including GRPO, PPO, RLOO, and Reinforce++. Furthermore, CAPO shows strong generalization across modalities, achieving significant improvements in tasks involving graphical user interfaces (GUIs). In summary, our main contributions are as follows: (1) the introduction of advantage as a dynamic curriculum signal, (2) the design of CAPO, a two-phase mechanism with imitation and discrimination phases that adapt to the model’s evolving competence, and (3) empirical evidence showing CAPO’s broad generalization, proving effective across various advantage-based RL algorithms and transferable to multimodal reasoning tasks. As illustrated in Figure 2, CAPO operates by processing samples generated by the policy model through several algorithms to compute their respective advantage estimates, which are then seamlessly integrated into the unified framework. By leveraging these advantage signals, CAPO orchestrates training through an initial imitation phase to establish stability, followed by a discrimination phase that enhances generalization as more challenging samples are introduced.

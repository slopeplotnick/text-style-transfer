We begin with a baseline model trained solely on the EMER dataset, which provides high-quality but limited supervision. Introducing emotion-consistent data augmentation leads to improvements across all consistency metrics, emphasizing the critical role of providing emotionally reliable samples during early-stage training. Subsequently, applying an MI-based reasoning reordering approach further enhances performance by guiding the model to initiate its reasoning from the emotion-dominant modality. This supports our hypothesis that structuring reasoning around MI helps mitigate visual or text-driven drift during the initial stages of development. In the following phase, we investigate the contribution of each proposed reward to reasoning stability and clarity. The Modality-Aligned Order Reward encourages the model to begin reasoning from the modality emphasized by MI, restoring the modality-first structure learned during SFT. The Modality-Grounded Reasoning Reward strengthens the emotional validity of reasoning, ensuring alignment with the target emotion and promoting semantically consistent evidence. When both rewards are applied together, we observe optimal performance across all metrics, demonstrating their complementary effect. Our objective is to enhance the reliability of reasoning-based multimodal emotion understanding by addressing the early-stage reasoning drift typical in MLLMs. To this end, we introduced MI and proposed MIGR, a training framework designed to initiate and maintain reasoning from the modality most relevant to the target emotion. This two-stage approach ensures that both the supervised and reinforcement learning processes align with the emotion-dominant modality, resulting in more coherent, emotionally grounded, and causally meaningful explanations. Experiments on the MAFW and DFEW benchmarks show that MIGR significantly reduces emotionally inconsistent reasoning. Overall, MIGR advances toward more trustworthy and interpretable multimodal emotional reasoning, underscoring the importance of incorporating modality dominance into both data organization and optimization.

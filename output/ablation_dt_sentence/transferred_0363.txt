While AMP demonstrates strong performance in motion reproduction, it requires retaining the reference motion data throughout training. In contrast, SMP is capable of accurately reproducing a wide variety of motions, including those that are particularly complex, achieving performance comparable to AMP, all without the need for reference data during training. Across all skills, SMP consistently outperforms both SMILING and AMP-Frozen. AMP-Frozen attempts to address AMP’s reliance on reference data by employing a pre-trained discriminator; however, this approach proves ineffective, leading to significantly degraded motion imitation performance. DeepMimic benefits from phase synchronization, which reduces tracking errors on certain motions. However, SMP continues to exhibit competitive performance across a range of tasks. DeepMimic's performance degrades on more challenging motions, such as spinkicks and cartwheels, especially when pose termination is disabled, causing some training runs to converge to suboptimal local minima. Our system represents one of the first frameworks to employ score-matching to construct a reusable motion model, achieving motion fidelity comparable to leading adversarial imitation learning methods. A key design choice in our system is ensemble score-matching, which enhances training stability and improves result quality. This approach aggregates SDS evaluations across a fixed set of diffusion timesteps, in contrast to the typical strategy of using a single randomly sampled timestep, as employed in prior work. We evaluate our ensemble score-matching approach ("Ensemble") against the conventional single-timestep SDS strategy ("Random") on imitation tasks of varying difficulty, including Run, Cartwheel, and Backflip. As shown in Table 5, the single-timestep SDS objective performs similarly on simpler motions but struggles with more complex behaviors. For example, policies trained with a single SDS evaluation often fail to complete a backflip, instead collapsing into a stationary standing position or falling backward. In contrast, ensembling SDS evaluations across multiple diffusion timesteps provides a more reliable and informative reward signal, facilitating the effective training of policies for more complex motions, such as acrobatics.

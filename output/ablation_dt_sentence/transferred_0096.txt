The rapid progress of large language models (LLMs) has significantly enhanced their ability to perform a wide range of complex tasks, such as academic writing, literature reviews, and the generation of scientific reports. Moreover, LLM-based systems for automatic survey generation are transforming how users extract knowledge from extensive data repositories by seamlessly integrating retrieval, organization, and synthesis into a unified and efficient pipeline. In both academia and industry, various systems have been developed for survey creation. These systems can be categorized into three primary types: general-purpose long-text writing systems (e.g., Kimi and GLM), research paper-writing systems (e.g., Chengpian and Doubao Paper Mode), and specialized academic survey-generation tools (e.g., SurveyGo, SurveyX, and Panshi ScienceOne). These tools are capable of processing large volumes of research literature, identifying key information, and generating well-structured survey drafts that include relevant citations and maintain logical coherence. Despite the impressive advancements in the development of these systems, determining effective metrics for evaluating their performance remains a significant challenge. Currently, system evaluations largely rely on subjective human judgments, which often lack consistency and reproducibility. This limitation hinders the ability to systematically compare models, identify effective components, and drive iterative improvements. Consequently, ensuring the consistent quality of these systems and their ongoing enhancement becomes increasingly difficult. Therefore, establishing a standardized approach for evaluating these systems is essential, as it will enable a more reliable assessment of their quality and provide researchers with a consistent framework for comparison and analysis. To address this need, we present **SurveyEval**, a systematic benchmark designed to assess automatically generated surveys across three critical dimensions: overall quality, outline coherence, and reference accuracy. We conducted an evaluation across seven academic disciplines and enhanced the LLM-as-a-Judge methodology by incorporating human-written surveys, thereby improving alignment consistency between automated evaluations and human judgments.

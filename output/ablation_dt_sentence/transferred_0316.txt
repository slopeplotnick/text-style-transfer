To interpret the complex reasoning data, we apply Principal Component Analysis (PCA), which projects the prompts into a two-dimensional space, simplifying their high-dimensional representations. As shown in Figure 10 from MATH500, prompts selected through optimization exhibit a more compact distribution compared to those sampled randomly. This suggests that high-quality prompts steer the model toward more stable and consistent reasoning trajectories, thereby reducing variability in its problem-solving behavior. Results from additional tests corroborate these findings, as presented in Figures 15 and 16 in the appendix. We propose a unified framework for optimizing system prompts to enhance the performance and consistency of multilingual large language models. Our experiments demonstrate that specific prompt components can improve performance across languages, and that multilingual reward models can be effectively utilized to identify high-performing prompts. Moreover, examining model reasoning reveals that better prompts elicit more structured and consistent outputs, reinforcing the importance of prompt quality in enhancing cross-linguistic model performance. Identifying an effective prompt involves systematically evaluating potential candidates within a well-defined framework, considering both the underlying structure of the prompt and its ability to elicit the desired output. Optimization techniques can further refine prompt selection by focusing on specific criteria. While high-quality prompts have been identified using SPRIG, selecting prompts that consistently perform well across all four evaluation metrics remains a challenge. Our experiments demonstrate that well-constructed prompts can guide models toward specific cognitive patterns that improve performance; however, the underlying mechanisms driving this effect remain inadequately understood. Investigating these mechanisms is a key direction for future research. Although efforts have been made to minimize potential ethical concerns, some issues persist. Prompt optimization and the associated experiments demand significant computational resources, leading to high energy consumption and an increased carbon footprint. Additionally, the optimization process may inadvertently amplify biases, particularly if the prompts lead the model to adopt specific personas, such as that of a professor. Given that our dataset includes elements like roles and instructional prompts, it is essential to ensure these features do not introduce or exacerbate harmful biases.

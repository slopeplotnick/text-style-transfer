Our findings align with prior research (Sengupta et al., 2025; Subramanian et al., 2025), which indicates that performance degradation is not always monotonic as model size decreases. In line with previous work on MentalQLM (0.5B) (Shi et al., 2025), our results demonstrate that smaller models can achieve strong performance in predicting various mental health outcomes, including depression, stress, and suicidal ideation. This highlights the potential of fine-tuned smaller language models (SLMs) for mental health prediction, offering a favorable trade-off between computational efficiency and predictive accuracy. Moreover, consistent with recent studies (Ren et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024a), we observe that core reasoning and question-answering capabilities are preserved after fine-tuning, enabling these models to serve as versatile tools with enhanced performance on mental health tasks. Our experiments show that SLMs in the 3-7B parameter range strike an optimal balance between efficiency and predictive performance when fine-tuned. While smaller models often struggle to generalize or meet task constraints, models within this range effectively classify psychological signals in both zero-shot and few-shot settings. With targeted fine-tuning, our model Menta establishes a clear lower bound for model size, achieving strong performance on complex mental health tasks while maintaining computational efficiency. Furthermore, fine-tuned SLMs demonstrate comparable or even superior performance to larger models in depression and stress level classification tasks, although they exhibit lower performance in predicting suicidal ideation. Compared to non-trained SLMs, fine-tuned models show significant improvements and more balanced results across prediction tasks and classes. Our findings reinforce that well-optimized smaller models can retain the reasoning and contextual understanding required for mental health predictions. This competitiveness is driven by the fact that mental health tasks rely on localized lexical, affective, and syntactic cues—such as self-referential expressions, emotional polarity, and temporal framing—that smaller models can capture effectively, without needing the broad world knowledge encoded in larger models. As demonstrated by prior work (Ji et al., 2021; Shen et al., 2018; Benton et al., 2017), fine-tuning on domain-specific corpora significantly narrows the performance gap between smaller and larger architectures. Our results extend this evidence by showing that parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA), combined with adaptive loss functions, enables SLMs to effectively learn discriminative representations for mental health prediction, all while reducing computational resource requirements.

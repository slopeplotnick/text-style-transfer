Research on truthful AI focuses on developing systems that provide accurate representations of the real world [Lin et al., 2021]. Previous studies have proposed interventions to enhance AI truthfulness, such as identifying behavioral patterns that align with established facts or modifying decision-making processes to encourage truthful outputs. Moreover, there has been ongoing discussion about the trade-off between the truthfulness of AI systems and their overall utility. Honest AI, in particular, emphasizes that systems should not intentionally produce outputs that contradict their internal beliefs in pursuit of rewards. This notion is closely related to broader concerns about deceptive behavior in AI systems. In contrast, truth-seeking AI refers to the systematic evaluation of evidence with the goal of generating novel insights and uncovering previously unknown findings [Koralus, 2025]. As AI systems grow more agentic, this capacity may become increasingly significant, especially as they begin to autonomously determine what information to seek based on their interpretation of context [Chan et al., 2023]. While truth-seeking AI aims explicitly at the pursuit of truth, the concept itself does not map neatly onto a single, optimizable metric. For example, it may involve adhering to Bayesian principles or maintaining internal coherence in reasoning [Wen et al., 2025]. A related challenge is belief entrenchment—the systematic tendency to maintain existing beliefs despite encountering contradictory evidence. This phenomenon is closely related to confirmation bias, a cognitive bias wherein individuals actively seek, recall, or interpret information in ways that reinforce their prior beliefs and values [Nickerson, 1998]. Importantly, confirmation bias pertains to context-specific information processing, and its measurement poses significant challenges. In psychological research, such measurement is often task-specific and exhibits low reliability. Furthermore, accurately assessing confirmation bias requires prior knowledge of an individual’s belief structure and a reliable method for measuring it—both of which present considerable methodological difficulties [Berthet, 2021].

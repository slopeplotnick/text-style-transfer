Answering questions in elderly services presents significant challenges, as it requires providing accurate and safe responses derived from various knowledge sources. These queries often concern healthcare regulations, welfare programs, and daily guidance—areas where misinformation can have serious consequences. While general-purpose language models excel in open-domain tasks, they frequently lack the necessary precision, domain awareness, and safety mechanisms needed for such sensitive applications. Existing models typically rely on either structured or unstructured data, limiting their ability to offer comprehensive answers. Moreover, most models fail to regulate how external information influences their responses and provide minimal safeguards to ensure the safety and reliability of their outputs. These shortcomings reduce their effectiveness in elderly service contexts. To address these issues, we propose Knowledge-Aware Reasoning and Memory-Augmented Adaptation (KARMA), a domain-adaptive question answering system. KARMA integrates a dual-encoder architecture for the fusion of knowledge from multiple sources, a gated memory unit for the selective integration of relevant information, and a safety-aware controllable decoder to suppress unsafe or erroneous outputs. The system is trained using a multi-objective loss function that optimizes language fluency, knowledge alignment, and safety control. Built upon a LLaMA-7B foundation with adapter-based fine-tuning, KARMA adapts efficiently while maintaining robust performance. Experiments on a specialized elderly-service QA dataset demonstrate that KARMA outperforms competing models in terms of accuracy, relevance, and safety, providing a compelling solution for real-world deployment in elderly care. Mo et al. [1] proposed a method for transferring knowledge between structured and unstructured data sources, emphasizing the importance of heterogeneous information fusion in complex QA. Similarly, Huang et al. [2] introduced MetaMath-LLaMA, a framework that integrates a metacognitive scheduler, semantically grounded symbolic parsing, and a hybrid symbolic-neural unit for interpretable multi-step reasoning. Incorporating these techniques into KARMA enhances the clarity of procedural steps, improving both the gating of memory units and the regulation of safety controls. Luo [3] presented TriMedTune, a fine-tuning method for brain CT that combines hierarchical visual prompt injection, diagnostic terminology alignment, and knowledge distillation with uncertainty regularization. Adapting these methods to KARMA would improve terminology grounding and provide uncertainty-aware gating for the safety decoder, thereby ensuring factual consistency and appropriate refusal of sensitive queries.

To overcome the constraints of fixed viewpoints and introduce distinct spatial cues, we diversify camera motion trajectories. Each trajectory begins with a random orientation in pitch, yaw, and roll, followed by random drifts to simulate natural movement. Moreover, we ensure that the primary visual cues corresponding to the audio remain within the field of view for a sustained period. Specifically, for each clip, we compute the direction of maximum audio energy using spherical harmonics decomposition and adjust the camera's orientation to center the trajectory around the primary sound source, as depicted in Fig. 1. Once binaural audio is obtained, we exclude samples where the normalized left-right channel difference is below 0.01, ensuring that the retained samples exhibit distinct spatial auditory cues. BiAudio stands out as the largest video dataset in the field of view (FoV) with binaural audio. As shown in Tab. 1, previous real-world binaural datasets are considerably smaller in scale. While SimBinaural offers over 100 hours of content, its synthetic nature limits its applicability to real-world scenarios. Furthermore, many existing datasets are confined to specific sound domains, such as OAP focusing on street scenes, and Fair-Play, SimBinaural, and MUSIC-21 emphasizing music. In contrast, BiAudio is large-scale and open-domain, encompassing a diverse range of real-world sound environments. Spatial Cue Enhancement (Sec. 3.1) further refines spatial perception and dynamics, making BiAudio highly suitable for video-to-binaural spatial audio generation. The architecture of ViSAudio is illustrated in Figure 2. Our approach builds upon components of the MMAudio architecture, fine-tuning the pretrained MMAudio model to retain its robust audio generation capabilities, ensuring strong generalization across diverse acoustic environments. To generate binaural audio that maintains both channel coherence and spatial distinctiveness, we implement Dual-Branch Audio Generation (Sec. 4.2), where two separate branches predict the left and right audio channels independently. Additionally, we introduce the Conditional Spacetime Module (Sec. 4.3), which extracts spatio-temporal features from the video and integrates them into the audio generation process, further enhancing the spatial realism of the sound.

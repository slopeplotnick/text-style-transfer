The marked variability observed, particularly within the PhysionetMI dataset, is likely attributable to its inherent complexities, including diverse experimental conditions, subject-specific neurophysiological characteristics, and differences in signal quality. One promising direction for addressing this variability is ensemble learning, where techniques such as bagging, boosting, or stacking can integrate the complementary strengths of both simple and complex models, resulting in decoders that remain robust across the full spectrum of inter- and intra-subject variability. To validate and stabilize these approaches, further investigation is required. Despite current limitations, the integration of quantum methods with advanced algorithmic strategies holds significant potential to enhance EEG signal decoding, classification accuracy, and generalization, while also increasing resilience to the intrinsic variability of EEG data. This could represent a major step forward in the development of personalized and adaptive brain-computer interfaces (BCIs). In line with this, our ongoing preliminary studies within the quantum computing framework aim to rigorously assess the practical efficacy of these theoretical concepts, providing empirical insights into whether quantum-inspired methodologies can substantially improve EEG decoding performance. Another critical challenge in existing EEG decoding methods is their heavy reliance on labeled data, which is often prohibitively costly and time-consuming to obtain in large quantities. Recent research highlights the need for unsupervised or semi-supervised learning techniques capable of leveraging unlabeled EEG data, thereby reducing the dependence on extensive labeled datasets. Furthermore, although transfer learning—defined as the application of neural patterns learned from one individual or dataset to another—holds considerable promise for improving model generalization and mitigating the effects of inter-subject variability, it remains insufficiently explored. Therefore, future research should prioritize the development and evaluation of transfer learning pipelines to determine their effectiveness in addressing the dataset-specific challenges encountered in EEG decoding. In summary, our findings support the adoption of tailored EEG methodologies to address both inter- and intra-subject variability effectively. The integration of adaptive strategies, complex modeling techniques, ensemble learning, and quantum-inspired approaches collectively defines the future trajectory of personalized and clinically applicable BCIs.

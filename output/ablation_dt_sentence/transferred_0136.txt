In contrast to image-based IC-LoRA methods that require separate training for each individual editing task, our unified Sync-LoRA module generalizes across a wide range of edits and achieves a robust balance between high visual fidelity and accurate motion preservation, as demonstrated by our experimental results (see Section 4). **In-Context Learning in Diffusion Models** In-context learning (ICL) enables diffusion models to adapt to novel tasks by conditioning on structured input examples, rather than relying on explicit supervision. While this capability has been extensively studied in language models [5], recent research has turned to its application in diffusion-based image generation [9, 26, 46, 65]. In these works, the model synthesizes a panel of images in which each image provides contextual cues for the others. This property is especially beneficial for applications that demand consistency across images, such as storyboard generation [3, 24, 52]. When such panels are conditioned on a real image, ICL-based methods can support a broad range of image-to-image tasks, including image personalization [6, 15, 16, 31, 44, 49] and image editing [18, 23, 38, 41, 42]. The work most closely related to ours is IC-LoRA [26], which demonstrates that transformer-based diffusion models inherently exhibit the ability to generate image panels with consistent features. This ability is further enhanced by fine-tuning with a low-rank adapter (LoRA) on structured, consistent panels using specific template prompts. Their method has been successfully applied to various tasks, including film storyboard generation, font design, and interior decoration. Building on this paradigm, we extend it to the video domain by introducing an IC-LoRA tailored for video editing, specifically targeting frame-accurate portrait synchronization. Moreover, recent advancements have shown that large video diffusion transformers naturally display in-context learning behavior, allowing them to generate multiple temporally or spatially coherent video clips within a unified diffusion process [14]. This prior work investigates the underlying mechanisms of this ability, employing inpainting-based conditioning for controlled scene composition, but without implementing any mechanism to guarantee temporal alignment across clips. Building upon this insight, PoseGen [22] proposes in-context LoRA fine-tuning for pose-controllable human video generation, enabling the synthesis of long, temporally coherent motion from a single reference image. In contrast, our method explicitly trains the in-context behavior to prioritize synchronized editing.

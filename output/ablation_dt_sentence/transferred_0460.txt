To address this challenge, we propose a statistical measure aimed at evaluating the propensity of reasoning models toward belief entrenchment. Essentially, belief entrenchment is characterized by a deviation from the Martingale property — a fundamental principle in Bayesian reasoning which essentially stipulates that the direction of belief updates should not be anticipated beforehand. This deviation serves as a principled and unsupervised marker of irrational belief entrenchment. Specifically, if a model's future beliefs can be reliably predicted from its prior beliefs through reasoning iterations—quantified by the goodness-of-fit in a regression model—this indicates a departure from the Martingale property and the presence of belief entrenchment. Our approach is empirically validated across three domains: event forecasting, value-laden questions, and academic paper reviews (referred to as Forecasting, r/ChangeMyView, and OpenReview, respectively). Consequently, across diverse models (such as GPT-4o, Llama 4), reasoning techniques (such as CoT, Debate), and system prompts, we observe that belief entrenchment is prevalent. Notably, our findings show that future belief updates are consistently predictable from prior beliefs, demonstrating a clear violation of the Martingale property. Furthermore, we substantiate the Martingale Score by showing its strong correlation with a decrease in ground-truth accuracy, measured via the Brier Score, in domains where such labels are available. Thus, we define belief entrenchment as a statistical property reflecting confirmation bias in LLM reasoning and introduce the Martingale Score as a domain-agnostic measure of belief entrenchment. Utilizing this score to assess mainstream LLMs reveals that belief entrenchment is widespread across various domains (Forecasting, r/ChangeMyView, and OpenReview), prompts (prior-conforming, no-prompt, critical-thinking), and model families (GPT, DeepSeek, Gemini, Llama, etc.).

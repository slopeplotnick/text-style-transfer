To address these limitations, we propose HEALTHCONTRADICT, a dataset consisting of 920 unique instances, each comprising a health-related question paired with two documents presenting contradictory viewpoints. Additionally, each instance includes a factual answer supported by scientific evidence. Using this dataset, we evaluated several language models, ranging from 1B to 8B parameters, including both general domain models and their biomedical-specific counterparts, to assess their ability to answer health-related questions amidst conflicting information. Specifically, our benchmark focuses on two key aspects: (i) how well language models answer health-related questions in the presence of contradictory information, and (ii) how the provided biomedical context influences the models' responses. To investigate these factors, we included correct, incorrect, and contradictory context in various prompt scenarios, and evaluated the models' accuracy and probability distributions in answering the questions. Our contributions are as follows: (i) the introduction of the novel HEALTHCONTRADICT dataset designed to evaluate language models in the context of conflicting biomedical information; (ii) a comprehensive evaluation of language models using this dataset, assessing their ability to reason over extended, contradictory biomedical contexts with interpretable, quantitative metrics; and (iii) a comparison of general domain models with their fine-tuned biomedical counterparts, revealing that the latter excel in leveraging correct contextual knowledge while resisting incorrect information. Each instance in the dataset features a distinct document to prevent bias, as illustrated by the example where the question "Can coffee help you lose weight?" is paired with two conflicting documents (one affirming, the other refuting the claim), alongside the factual answer supported by scientific evidence, which suggests that caffeine may indeed contribute to weight loss, lower BMI, and reduced body fat.

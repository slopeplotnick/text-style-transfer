Reducing risks is key to minimizing resistance to change, and here we outline several strategies aimed at addressing these challenges. All concerns, whether directly related to these risks or not, must be considered, as they can hinder successful adoption. While some risks or challenges persist in the adoption of SPL, they are not insurmountable if the change process is carefully managed with these factors in mind. The perception of complexity often arises from a misunderstanding of SPLE, but SPL can also present significant challenges for individuals familiar with SPLE. While complexity may exist within the SPL itself, it should not exceed the complexity of the codebase with which the IT team is already proficient. Within the SPL, assumptions about variability are explicitly modeled, enhancing understanding and addressing the inherent complexity. This sentiment may be linked to the perceived need for training and demonstrations. However, we have clarified that, in contrast, SPL offers a time-saving advantage by facilitating the implementation of new features and enabling further creative development. By providing this clarification, we mitigate the risk of wasting valuable creative time, as noted by UX professionals, and prevent delays in the development of new features. Side effects can be mitigated through more thorough non-regression testing. Fears can be alleviated by dedicating time to listen to the team’s concerns and engaging in discussions about both weaknesses and strengths. Moreover, these concerns are often seen as biases by the participants in the interviews. It is understandable to view SPL as a system that is overly complex to use and modify, rendering the new approach ineffective. Trust can be restored by providing open access to the SPL, accompanied by comprehensive explanations and detailed documentation, while ensuring the involvement of all relevant stakeholders. The inclusion of stakeholders begins by inviting them to participate in the migration process. It is essential to encourage regular reporting at each stage, fostering a sense of being heard and understood while providing necessary support.

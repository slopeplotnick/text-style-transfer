**KL-free GRPO Algorithm.** Fig. 11 presents the overall reinforcement learning framework. We employ the GRPO algorithm to optimize the objective function defined in Equation 13, which maximizes the advantage-weighted log probability expectation. The importance sampling ratio and the corresponding advantage function are defined in Equation 14. In our implementation, we omit the KL divergence regularization term. This choice is motivated by the observation that, after a sufficient SFT warm-start, the model has already established a stable reasoning paradigm and output structure. At this stage, imposing KL constraints would overly restrict policy exploration, thereby limiting the model's ability to discover superior reasoning strategies. Consequently, we allow the model to optimize freely, building upon its existing competencies. To guide the optimization process, we design a reward mechanism that fulfills three objectives: (1) **Format Standardization**: Special tokens are used to explicitly separate the reasoning process from the final answer, simplifying downstream parsing and utilization; (2) **Output Space Constraint**: The model's outputs are strictly limited to a predefined set of mismatch types (e.g., "price mismatch," "functionality mismatch"), preventing the generation of arbitrary or uncontrolled outputs; (3) **Progressive Reward**: When the model accurately identifies relevance but errs on finer-grained aspects, moderate positive rewards are still granted. This helps address the sparse reward challenge common in early-stage training, thereby accelerating convergence and enhancing training stability. Building upon this reward framework, we implement a three-phase curriculum learning strategy that incrementally increases the difficulty of training samples. The allocation of samples for each stage is detailed in Table 6. This curriculum is grounded in three core principles: (1) **Rapid Bootstrap Principle**: Phase 1 primarily employs medium-difficulty samples to ensure the model receives immediate and meaningful feedback while preserving sufficient challenge, mitigating early-stage issues caused by sparse rewards; (2) **Progressive Challenge Principle**: Phases 2 and 3 gradually raise the proportion of difficult samples, enabling a smooth transition from solving common problems to addressing more complex edge cases, thereby expanding the model’s capabilities; (3) **Knowledge Retention Principle**: In the later stages, easier samples are continuously incorporated as "memory anchors" to help the model retain previously learned skills while integrating new knowledge. These high-confidence samples also contribute to gradient stability throughout the training process.

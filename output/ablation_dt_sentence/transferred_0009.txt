We assess the temporal alignment between the source and edited videos at the frame level using four distinct correlation-based metrics: speech, gaze, blink, and pose. Each of these metrics captures a different aspect of motion dynamics: mouth aspect ratio for speech, iris trajectory for gaze, eye aspect ratio for blink, and body pose for overall movement. While these metrics are also employed in our training data filtering process (subsection 3.2), their role here is solely for data selection and verification during the final evaluation phase. They are not used as loss functions nor included in the model’s training objective. To further evaluate the accuracy and consistency of the applied edits across the video, we use two complementary CLIP-based metrics. The first, **Directional CLIP Score**, measures whether the per-frame visual changes induced by the edit align with the intended semantic direction of the transformation. Specifically, we present two variants: one evaluates the alignment between the first edited frame and the subsequent frames, while the other assesses the degree of correspondence between the changes and the semantic edit described in the text prompt. The second variant is particularly relevant for text-only methods, such as LucyEdit, which lack access to the initial edited frame. The image-based variant, on the other hand, asks whether the alterations in the video resemble those observed between the unedited and edited first frames. The second metric, **CLIP-Text Alignment Score**, evaluates the semantic consistency between individual video frames and the corresponding textual description. This ensures that the video maintains alignment with the intended narrative throughout the temporal sequence. Together, these two metrics provide a comprehensive measure of edit fidelity, assessing both the visual and semantic consistency of the applied edits.

In this context, language models offer substantial improvements over traditional approaches to mental health monitoring by providing enhanced contextual understanding and scalability for real-world deployment. Specifically, language models surpass other computational systems in their ability to capture nuanced context and extract relevant information, thereby enhancing their practicality for large-scale mental health applications. However, the use of large language models (LLMs) introduces significant limitations: they incur high computational costs during both training and inference, and they raise privacy concerns due to the processing of sensitive data on external servers. Moreover, their considerable size renders them unsuitable for resource-constrained, on-device environments, emphasizing the need for more efficient alternatives. By contrast, small language models (SLMs) demonstrate comparable—or in some cases, superior—potential for digital health applications, owing to their simplified architectures, lower operational costs, and viability for local deployment. Accordingly, SLMs enable privacy-preserving mental health inference from social media activity without storing identifiable information, supporting timely, scalable, and ethically responsible monitoring. Yet, the potential of SLMs in this domain remains underexplored. In this study, we investigate the capacity of SLMs to predict mental health outcomes and establish their viability as practical solutions for on-device, multi-condition assessment. To this end, we evaluate these models across classification tasks spanning stress, depression, and suicidality. We introduce Menta, a compact model fine-tuned using a specialized methodology and trained on diverse datasets to optimize balanced accuracy. Menta exhibits strong performance across a range of mental health tasks, particularly excelling in stress and depression detection. We further highlight its utility by deploying it on an iPhone 15 Pro Max, where it achieves real-time inference with minimal memory usage, enabling private and scalable on-device mental health support.

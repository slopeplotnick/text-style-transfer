For GraphMatch, we observe that the combination of random and hard negatives is crucial for achieving optimal performance. Specifically, GraphMatch versions trained only with random negatives show performance comparable to TextMatch-small. We further investigate the impact of different node features on GraphMatch's performance: the "no-feat" variant uses only TextMatch node embeddings, "no-text" employs numerical node features in place of text embeddings, and "full" incorporates both TextMatch node embeddings and numerical node features. Among these, GraphMatch-full, trained with adversarial negatives, yields the highest performance, while GraphMatch-no-feat performs similarly to TextMatch-large despite its smaller model size. This underscores the importance of textual context, as evidenced by the suboptimal performance of GraphMatch-no-text. Moreover, accurate data sampling over time is essential for maintaining temporal consistency and ensuring reliable adaptation and preprocessing of datasets. Selecting appropriate subgraphs and node features at the right moments is key to training a model that generalizes well to unseen nodes. To test this, we train GraphMatch in two distinct configurations. The no-temporal-nodes version excludes temporally relevant node features during training but always uses the most recent feature version available in the training set. In contrast, the no-temporal-graph version does not sample temporally specific subgraphs, although it has access to all graph edges during training.

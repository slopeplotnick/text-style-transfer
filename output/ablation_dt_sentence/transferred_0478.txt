After fine-tuning the LLM and aligning it using the KTO algorithm, we use it as a domain-specific data annotator to label online recalled and exposed items. Consequently, hard samples inaccurately predicted by the online BERT-based model are incorporated into the training set, further improving the online model's performance. By leveraging the domain rule-aware LLM constructed within the RD module, we can quickly identify hard samples where the online model makes incorrect predictions. Nevertheless, this piecemeal approach only addresses isolated errors and fails to systematically resolve broader categories of issues. When user search terms or product descriptions undergo subtle changes, the online model may still predict incorrectly. To address this, we developed an error-type-aware LLM generative model, which automatically synthesizes data that the online model is prone to mispredict, thereby enhancing the model's generalization capability and accuracy. For any given query-product pair, our model can modify the attributes of the original query or product based on instruction prompts, generating new samples that fall into two categories: Relevant and Irrelevant. Specifically, training samples at this stage are obtained using the GPT-4o model through few-shot prompting. To ensure that the generative model can fully capture the error patterns of the online model, we iteratively select the generated results where the online model made incorrect predictions as fine-tuning training samples for the LLM. Upon building the error-type-aware LLM-based generative model, we directly sample data from online retrieval and exposed instances, leveraging the model to automatically generate hard samples that are likely to confuse the online model. Finally, to mitigate the impact of noisy data on the online model's training, we utilize the relevance discrimination model from the RD module to filter the generated results.

Online videos have emerged as a predominant medium of communication, and their extensive reach facilitates the rapid dissemination of hateful content. Such material exacerbates discrimination and social division, potentially inciting offline violence. Conventional methodologies generally involve extracting features from video, audio, and textual elements; however, these approaches often fail to effectively capture the synergistic interplay among these modalities. Existing hateful video detection technologies face two primary challenges: (1) understanding the nuances of context—these videos frequently convey harmful intentions through subtle contextual cues that unfold over time and across various media modalities, such as the temporal alignment of specific images and verbal expressions, or the association between visual elements and auditory content; and (2) integrating diverse informational modalities—current methodologies engage in the feature extraction from visual, auditory, and textual elements, but often fail to facilitate effective multimodal interaction among these disparate sources. Addressing these challenges necessitates deep contextual reasoning, which standard approaches inadequately support. Recent efforts have sought to improve contextual comprehension by integrating models that amalgamate visual and linguistic elements, augmenting reasoning processes; however, direct reasoning alone provides only rudimentary descriptions lacking connections to the underlying hateful semantics. Moreover, recent work has shown that certain contemporary approaches may generate misleading rationales that do not accurately reflect the model's cognitive processes, posing reliability concerns in sensitive applications. Additionally, recent studies indicate that hateful cues in videos can manifest abruptly in brief segments or be disseminated across the entire video duration. Numeric signals, possessing intricate meanings, require integration with fundamental features. These challenges highlight a critical deficiency: contemporary systems are ineffective in providing reliable semantic reasoning and in integrating multimodal information for the detection of hateful videos.

The purpose of this paper is to provide a forward-looking essay on the use of AI in critical mineral exploration, offering a personal perspective grounded in three decades of experience in data science, decision science, and AI within the context of Earth resources exploration and development (Caers et al., 2026). In 1998, I authored a paper (Caers and Journel, 1998) on the application of neural networks to outcrop data, which aimed to enhance geological realism in reservoir models. As a junior faculty member eager to establish my professional credibility, I presented this work at the Society of Petroleum Engineers Annual Technical Conference and Exhibition in New Orleans, despite Hurricane George rapidly approaching Louisiana. The imposition of geological realism in reservoir models is essential for accurately predicting fluid flow in porous media, ultimately enabling better decision-making in the exploration and development of oil and gas fields. In a follow-up study (Caers and Ma, 2002), I demonstrated that neural networks could be used to learn the relationship between borehole and geophysical data, improving the prediction of critical reservoir properties, such as porosity, in areas lacking borehole data. A key contribution of this work was the novel approach to reconciling the differing scales of data, such as those from borehole cores versus seismic signals from deep subsurface regions. This scale-related challenge will be addressed in detail throughout the paper. While oil and gas companies would not consider constructing a major offshore platform without rigorously quantifying geological uncertainty—and, by extension, risk—many mining operations still rely on deterministic models. In the field of exploration, the use of basic models and data interpretation remains prevalent, and the minerals industry lags behind the oil and gas sector in adopting quantitative risk assessment methodologies.

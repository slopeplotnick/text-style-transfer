To this end, we evaluate our system on four simulation benchmarks comprising 64 tasks and five real-world tasks, assessing three types of VLA policies. The benchmarks utilized are Simpler, Libero, Robotwin1.0, and Robotwin2.0. Simpler is a real-to-sim benchmark built upon the SAPIEN simulator and the ManiSkill2 benchmark. It offers simulated task environments for both the WindowX and Google Robot platforms; however, in this investigation, we primarily focus on the tasks designed for WindowX. Libero serves as a benchmark for lifelong learning in decision-making processes with four distinct task suites. We focus on the most challenging suite, as previous works have already made significant advancements in the others. Robotwin1.0 and Robotwin2.0 concentrate on dual-arm gripper manipulation, featuring a diverse array of task types and utilizing scripted policies for automated data collection. In our real-world experimental setup, a REALMAN75 dual-arm robot is used in an office-like environment. This includes five tasks involving various common objects such as books, pens, a cabinet, a charger, a laptop, and a paper ball. These tasks are as follows: Receive Book, where the robot takes a book from a human and places it back on the bookshelf; Storage Charger, where it picks up the charger head, places it inside the cabinet, and closes the cabinet door; Paper and Pen, where it collects the paper ball and the pen and places them in the trash bin and pen holder, respectively; Laptop, where it closes the laptop lid, unplugs the charger from the socket, and stores it back in the cabinet; and Pick Books, where the left and right arms each pick up and lift a book simultaneously.

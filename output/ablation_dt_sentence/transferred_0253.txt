System prompts serve as a flexible tool for controlling LLM behavior across various tasks, defining roles, styles, or policies. Prior studies have shown that elements such as personas (Kim et al., 2024), emotional cues (Li et al., 2023), and jailbreak triggers (Shen et al., 2023) can influence response style and reasoning patterns. However, these modifications do not consistently enhance task accuracy. Despite their growing adoption, the structured optimization of system prompts remains insufficiently explored. Recent system prompts leaked from Grok (xAI, 2025) and Claude (Breunig, 2025) illustrate highly verbose and complex sets of manually engineered rules. Although recent advancements (Zhang et al., 2024; Secheresse et al., 2025) show that system prompts can be automatically optimized using genetic algorithms, such optimization has, to date, been limited to monolingual and single-metric contexts. Moreover, when deploying multilingual models, challenges such as language-specific degradation and cross-lingual inconsistencies emerge. Multilingual LLMs often yield divergent responses to identical queries in different languages, due to their uneven knowledge distribution across linguistic domains (Tam et al., 2025). Techniques like Cross-Lingual Thought Prompting (Huang et al., 2023) and multilingual self-consistency (Yu et al., 2025) offer partial solutions, but they generally depend on multi-turn prompting strategies. Consequently, a comprehensive framework for optimizing system prompts to ensure consistent and robust performance across languages remains elusive. In addition to standard metrics, recent research has increasingly focused on examining the reasoning processes of LLMs, often by breaking down responses into interpretable reasoning components (Tam et al., 2025; Gandhi et al., 2025). However, these studies tend to concentrate on surface-level aspects, such as using text prefill to control output language (Tam et al., 2025), without deeply exploring the model's underlying reasoning patterns. Our work addresses this gap by linking system prompt design to multilingual reasoning behaviors, demonstrating how these patterns contribute to improved cross-lingual generalization.

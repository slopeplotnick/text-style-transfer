LLaMA-Factory primarily focuses on text generation and does not explore the potential of transforming text-generation problems into classification tasks. In contrast, our framework, PEFT-FACTORY, addresses both the limited number of available PEFT methods and the opportunity to fine-tune large language models (LLMs) for classification tasks. While there are alternative configurations for training LLMs that offer distinct advantages, they tend to be less user-friendly than LLaMA-Factory. For example, FastChat (Zheng et al., 2023) is a specialized framework tailored for training LLMs in dialogue-based tasks. LitGPT (AI, 2023) and LMFlow (Diao et al., 2023) are versatile, user-friendly frameworks supporting a wide array of generative models and training methods. Axolotl (Axolotl maintainers and contributors, 2023) is a terminal-based tool designed for efficient post-training of LLMs, prioritizing both functionality and scalability. Open-Instruct (Wang et al., 2023a) focuses on the fine-tuning of LLMs through instructional adaptations, offering a variety of models and methodologies. H2O LLM Studio<sup>1</sup> is an enterprise-focused platform that provides a comprehensive graphical interface for developing and deploying LLM models. GPT4All (Anand et al., 2023) creates a user-friendly interface around LLaMA, streamlining the training process. ColossalAI (Li et al., 2023) delivers a framework for fine-tuning across multiple nodes, enabling efficient distributed training. Furthermore, LLaMA-Adapter and LLaMA-Accessory represent more lightweight frameworks, with LLaMA-Adapter incorporating trainable adapters into LLaMA models, while LLaMA-Accessory offers a comprehensive toolkit for large-scale model development. LLaMA-Adapter is frequently used within established frameworks, such as LitGPT. Upon reviewing these related frameworks, we identify three key aspects that are either absent or underrepresented, which represent novel contributions of our work: (1) the application of training techniques for large models beyond reparameterization-based PEFT methods, (2) the integration of new PEFT methods in a modular and adaptable manner, and (3) the provision of support for training and evaluating large models on classification tasks.

This platform has been in operation for approximately 5 to 6 years. According to the FEF framework [vdLSR07], ITK's platform is classified as a "Level 3: Software Platform," featuring a reference architecture common to all products. The current setup incorporates shared assets and defines explicit variation points for the integration of new versions. The goal of the migration is to achieve an architecture that captures both domain commonality and variability, specifying a reference architecture for the entire software product line. This involves systematic reuse based on an asset repository, explicit variability in the assets, and a reference architecture that governs the permissible variations in application architectures while incorporating various quality solutions. Variability management is addressed directly in the software product line architecture, where allowed configurations for application architectures are defined, along with variation points and constraints for most of these variations, including the rules that application-specific variants must adhere to. Ultimately, the objective is to provide a configuration for automated variant synthesis. At the organizational level, according to the FEF framework [vdLSR07], ITK operates at the fourth level, which involves the synchronization and coordination of domain engineering and application engineering. Project development is carried out through strong collaboration between agronomists and software developers. The decision to proceed with the migration and collaborate with our research team was made through a collegiate discussion among project leaders and stakeholders. Prior to initiating the interview planning phase, a well-defined research methodology for the remainder of the project had not yet been established. The initial objective was to assess employees' perspectives on the transition to a Software Product Line (SPL). Based on the interview findings, a detailed methodology for the migration project will be developed.

Sensory language processing, or language comprehension, follows a stepwise process, in which speech is perceived, phonemes are recognized, words formed from these phonemes are identified, and the meanings of these words are integrated according to grammatical rules to derive the overall meaning of the sentence. Here, we focus specifically on language comprehension beyond word recognition, a high-level cognitive process that is traditionally considered characteristic of humans, in order to compare the language processing mechanisms of the brain with those of advanced AI. These findings collectively suggest that language processing in the neocortex operates as hierarchical predictive coding, where lower layers are responsible for simpler tasks such as word prediction, while higher layers engage in more complex processes involving abstract concepts. This hierarchical approach mirrors the manner in which sensory information is processed, with the neocortex acquiring a world model of language through prediction-error learning, which it then uses for both prediction and understanding by extracting abstract information. This core computational principle, shared by both the brain and contemporary AI systems, forms the basis for the parallel we explore in greater depth below. For instance, Google Neural Machine Translation (GNMT) employs a translation circuit consisting of an encoder, attention, and decoder units. The encoder, which is a stacked LSTM, processes the source sentence sequentially, integrating the words into a vector that represents the meaning of the entire sentence. The encoder has eight layers, with the final layer outputting a unified vector that encapsulates the semantic content. The attention unit, a two-layer neural network, calculates a weight coefficient to focus on the most salient words in the source sentence, which are then conveyed to the decoder. The decoder generates the translated sentence word by word, using this information to make probabilistic predictions. The system is trained through supervised learning, comparing the generated translations to human-produced examples and adjusting the model accordingly. In contrast, GPT-2, a distinct AI model, employs a fundamentally different mechanism. Rather than relying on an encoder-decoder structure, GPT-2 operates as an integrated architecture that predicts the next word in a sequence based on the context established by previous words. During training, GPT-2 processes sentences with masked words, learning to predict the missing term based on the surrounding context. This capability is later leveraged in sentence generation, where the model autoregressively predicts the next word based on the preceding context. GPT-2’s architecture consists of repeating blocks, each containing an attention unit and a simple neural network, stacked multiple times, with 48 layers in total. The system processes input sequences and generates probabilistic predictions for the next word, acquiring knowledge through unsupervised learning by generating predictions without relying on manually curated examples.

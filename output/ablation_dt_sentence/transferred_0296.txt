The current effort involves the expansion of the French-Ciluba sentiment lexicon to incorporate translations in English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, Sesotho, and Sepedi, utilizing tools for interlingual translation and word alignment. Specifically, contextual usage within textual data is leveraged to refine these translations. To further enhance the lexicon, Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) is employed to systematically identify sentiment-bearing terms from African language corpora, aligning these with existing lexicon entries. Moreover, to improve translation accuracy, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is integrated with large language models, addressing ambiguities in word meanings and ensuring precise translations. Subsequently, AfriBERTa and AfroXLMR models will be trained for emotion and opinion classification tasks, using the augmented lexicon to improve semantic understanding in sentiment analysis. Model performance will then be evaluated based on precision, recall, F1-score, and ROC metrics. To improve model interpretability, attribution methods are incorporated, highlighting the specific textual components that influence model predictions. Furthermore, a comparison will be made between individual models and ensemble configurations to evaluate potential improvements in predictive accuracy. The remainder of the paper is structured as follows: Section II surveys prior research on sentiment analysis, multilingual lexicon construction, and computational modeling of African languages. Section III presents a detailed exposition of our framework and methodologies, including the experimental procedures and model evaluation. Section IV analyzes the results, discussing their implications for multilingual natural language processing tasks. Historically, sentiment analysis has predominantly relied on supervised machine learning techniques and lexicon-based approaches to determine the polarity of opinions expressed in textual data. Early studies demonstrated the effectiveness of statistical methods, while later research highlighted the utility of word lists in rule-based approaches. Lexical resources such as SentiWordNet, the NRC Emotion Lexicon, and VADER exhibit robust performance for high-resource languages; however, their applicability to morphologically complex and low-resource languages remains limited due to insufficient lexical coverage. Efforts to develop multilingual word lists through corpus extraction have been undertaken to address this gap.

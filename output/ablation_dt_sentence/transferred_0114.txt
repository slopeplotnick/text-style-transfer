PEFT-FACTORY calculates classification and efficiency metrics during the prediction phase, in addition to existing token accuracy and semantic similarity metrics, such as Rouge (Lin, 2004) and Bleu (Papineni et al., 2002). For classification, PEFT-FACTORY implements standard Accuracy and F1 scores. To evaluate efficiency during the assessment of PEFT methods, PEFT-FACTORY introduces the PSCP metric (Balanec et al., 2025), a highly configurable measure that takes into account various efficiency-related factors, including parameter count, memory consumption, and inference latency. These metrics are implemented within the `train/sft/metric.py` file for supervised fine-tuning, adhering to the structure established by LLaMA-Factory. Each metric is encapsulated in a dedicated data class—namely, `ComputeAccuracy`, `ComputeF1`, and `ComputePSCP`—ensuring modularity and clarity in evaluation procedures. For classification, a binary flag attribute, `compute_classification.metrics`, is included within the training arguments, enabling or disabling the computation of classification metrics. Similarly, for the efficiency metrics, a separate binary flag, `compute_pscp`, is provided. Additional details on the usage of efficiency metrics can be found in Appendix B.3. Furthermore, PEFT-FACTORY is designed with a flexible and user-friendly interface, which facilitates the exploration and evaluation of PEFT methods, promoting a controlled and stable environment for research. To illustrate its contribution to improving reproducibility and benchmarking, we present two specific use cases. The modular architecture of PEFT-FACTORY further enhances the creation and evaluation of novel PEFT methods, encouraging efficient and systematic experimentation. Currently, newly proposed PEFT methods are often accompanied by shared implementations that lack robustness and hinder reproducibility across experimental settings. Moreover, developers frequently face the challenge of manually writing training and evaluation code, a task that is both repetitive and error-prone, leading to inconsistencies in the final results.

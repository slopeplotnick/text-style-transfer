Language Model Systems (LMS), including Large Language Models (LLMs) and various other forms, are employed across a wide range of applications. Notable instances are domain-specific chatbots like medical chatbots, autonomous driving assistants, and coding assistants. As these systems often feature in significant and public-facing applications, ensuring their accuracy and alignment with expected behavioral standards is crucial. Numerous studies have explored methods to enhance their safety, attempting to embed these safety features directly within their structure. However, current methods for evaluating and enforcing desirable behaviors remain insufficient, lacking universal approaches with formal guarantees of effectiveness. Establishing such guarantees is vital for user safety and fostering trust in these systems. Recent efforts have begun to formally certify LMS for specific desirable behaviors, such as fairness, accurate question answering, and safety, which are encoded as explicit, mathematical rules that systems must follow. For instance, certain researchers have devised guidelines ensuring equitable responses, understanding textual materials, handling sensitive discussions, and selecting suitable tools. Nevertheless, these tailored certification processes present significant challenges in adaptability when addressing diverse requirements or contexts, due largely to their emphasis on specialized attributes and a focus typically confined to conversational frameworks. Consequently, any modifications to established guidelines necessitate their reformulation. Currently, these certification processes are executed in a customized manner for each particular property, domain, and system type—a practice we aim to reform.

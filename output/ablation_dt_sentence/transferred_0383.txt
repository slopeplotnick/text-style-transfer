All prompts were composed directly in the first language (L1) of the respective language teams, even though the prompt creation interface was presented in English. This ensured that no translation occurred during the generation of unscripted prompts. Once a team member had completed a specific domain path, they would then formulate the prompt in L1. Before initiating the prompt creation process, all language team members first completed a brief introductory project to familiarize themselves with the system and its requirements. Following this, each team generated a minimum of 2,000 unscripted prompts, having fully understood the project objectives. The scripted prompts were sourced from culturally relevant and domain-specific materials, covering areas such as agriculture and health. These sources included: - Vukuzenzele [26], a government publication that provides information on public services. - Wikipedia (South African Languages), offering extensive linguistic and thematic coverage. - African Wordnet [27], a crucial lexical database ensuring vocabulary accuracy and supporting the development of language technologies for South African languages. - Translated goatkeepers manuals (ARC animal health) [28], specialized content on animal health and agricultural practices. The initial data cleaning and preparation followed a structured pipeline designed to ensure high-quality text, suitable for training ASR models. Each stage in this process refined the data by removing irrelevant content, categorizing text into themes, and identifying or flagging Personally Identifiable Information (PII). Upon ingestion, preliminary quality control measures, including structural issue reviews, were applied to the raw texts. The data was then filtered using a language identification process to retain only the target South African languages. While no strict confidence thresholds were applied, instances where the top-ranked language did not match the expected category were excluded, though confidence scores were retained as metadata.

Evaluating the quality of generated summaries requires a multi-dimensional approach that considers lexical overlap, semantic similarity, and factual consistency. Traditional metrics, such as BLEU [Papineni et al., 2002] and ROUGE-L [Lin, 2004], assess quality by measuring lexical overlap between the summary and the original text. While these metrics are widely adopted, they are limited in their ability to handle paraphrasing, where different wording conveys the same meaning. To address this limitation, BERTScore was introduced. This metric utilizes a pre-trained model to evaluate semantic similarity by embedding tokens and measuring cosine similarity, allowing it to capture nuanced semantic relationships and paraphrastic variations more effectively. In addition to semantic similarity, it is essential to evaluate whether a summary maintains factual consistency with its source text. Several tools have been developed to address this aspect. FactCC employs a classification model to assess whether each sentence in the summary is supported by the source material; however, it is prone to occasional misclassification, sometimes identifying supported content as unsupported. Another tool, BLANC, does not require reference texts and instead evaluates the effectiveness of a summary in helping a language model reconstruct masked sections of the original text. However, its effectiveness is dependent on the underlying language model used, and it can sometimes prioritize lexical overlap over true semantic entailment. SummaC, on the other hand, uses Natural Language Inference (NLI) to evaluate factual consistency at the sentence level, determining whether each summary sentence is supported by the source text. Empirical results indicate that SummaC typically outperforms earlier tools, such as FactCC. In this study, we adopt a combination of evaluation methods: ROUGE-L to measure lexical overlap, BERTScore for semantic similarity, and SummaC to assess factual consistency.

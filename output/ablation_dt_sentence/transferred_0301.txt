Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (Brohan et al., 2022; Zitkovich et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Black et al., 2024) have demonstrated impressive performance in robotic manipulation tasks by leveraging large-scale, multimodal datasets collected through human teleoperation or scripted policies. During pretraining, these models utilize flow-matching (Liu et al., 2023b; Lipman et al., 2023, 2024) or diffusion-based (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021) objectives to acquire diverse manipulation strategies across a wide range of environments and task configurations. Following this phase, VLA models are adapted to specific downstream tasks via supervised fine-tuning on small-scale, task-specific datasets. This downstream adaptation typically yields superior performance compared to training from scratch, as the pretrained models benefit from prior exposure to visual perception, language grounding, and spatial-semantic representations. However, despite their strong average performance, VLA models often exhibit notable instabilities when deployed in real-world scenarios. We attribute this issue to the persistence of redundant action modes that remain after fine-tuning and are irrelevant to successful task execution. Specifically, during pretraining, VLA models absorb a wide array of actions from heterogeneous data sources, which makes it difficult to constrain the output distribution to the minimal set of successful behaviors required for a particular downstream task. As a result, even after fine-tuning, the policy retains unnecessary modes that do not contribute to task success. Moreover, the fine-tuning datasets themselves may introduce additional multimodality, as they are often derived from various sources—including different human operators or scripted controllers—some of which encode suboptimal or stylistically inconsistent strategies. These redundant behaviors induce a distributional shift between the pretrained VLA policy and the ideal, stable policy needed for downstream success. This discrepancy becomes especially evident during deployment.

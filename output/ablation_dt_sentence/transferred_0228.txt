The next test employed larger datasets, also consisting of 10,000 noun phrases and sentence exemplars generated by Delilah. Table 5 summarizes the lexical statistics for these datasets. As in the previous experiment, the 35 most frequent words in both datasets are presented in Tables 6 and 7. As shown, these top 35 words are predominantly function words. In the sample of 10,000 noun phrases, the first unequivocal content word, *vergeten* ('forgotten'), appears at rank 54/55, sharing its position with the function word *bij* ('at', 'near'). Following rank 55, there is an increase in the number of content words. In the experiment involving 10,000 sentence training exemplars, the most frequent content words are *vergeten* ('forgotten') at rank 11, *werk* ('works') at position 27, and *morgen* ('in the morning') at rank 66. After rank 66, the number of content words increases. Notably, Delilah encodes *massa's* ('masses') as a numerical value, which is accurately detected by the acquisition process. For datasets consisting of 10,000 exemplars, a clear distinction between function and content words can be made, as words occurring more than 100 times tend to be classified as function words. This corresponds to a frequency of more than 1.7 per thousand tokens within the sample of Delilah's sentences. Therefore, the MODOMA parameter configuration for the categorization of lexical items is set such that items occurring at a frequency exceeding 2 per 1,000 tokens are classified as function words, while all other items are assigned to the default category of content words. The learning process occurs after the acquisition of a minimum of 10,000 exemplars. These parameters are designed conservatively, minimizing the likelihood of incorrectly categorizing a word as a function word, with the default assumption being that each word is a content word unless substantial evidence suggests otherwise. The settings established during the initial test now serve as the default configuration for the MODOMA system, although they may be modified in future experiments based on evolving linguistic theories.

We employed a multimodal large language model (MLLM) to process and analyze image data, leveraging its advanced capabilities in vision-language integration. After generating the images, we proceeded with the triple extraction process to semantically describe their content. For this, we selected the open-source model LLaMA 3.2 Vision, accessed via the Ollama framework. This model was chosen as a practical solution capable of handling image inputs and producing structured outputs in natural language. Its ability to generate detailed, attribute-rich descriptions made it well-suited for transforming visual content into structured knowledge representations. Although our task focuses on extracting structured triples, it can be considered a form of image captioning. In this regard, recent evaluations of multimodal LLMs show comparable performance in visual understanding tasks. All models exhibit similar robustness trends; however, since our contribution lies in how extracted knowledge is structured and compared, we focus on one representative model. To guide and constrain the triple extraction, we adopted the Fashionpedia ontology, a comprehensive taxonomy developed for fashion-related visual understanding. It defines a structured set of object categories, parts, and attributes relevant to garments and accessories. By conditioning the multimodal LLM on this ontology during triple extraction, we ensured that the knowledge graphs (KGs) followed a consistent, domain-specific structure. This highlights the critical role of ontology guidance in ensuring rich and consistent structures, which are essential for graph-based comparison.

To ensure the viability of simulated data for computational research, one crucial feature is that specific subgroups should be directly comparable with one another. To test this, the simulated datasets were divided into "Male" and "Female" cohorts, whereupon invariance testing was conducted to assess their comparability. If this implementation is successful, it would enable in silico comparisons between specific demographics. In this section, we describe the overarching methodology employed across all four studies, except where deviations are explicitly noted in their respective sections. A representation of the experimental workflow for all four studies is shown in Figure 1. The simulated data were generated using GPT4o-mini, a variant of GPT-4 with a knowledge cut-off in October 2023. The default model settings were employed, including Temperature 1.0 and Top P 1.0, with frequency and presence penalties set to 0.0. The model was prompted to impersonate an individual possessing certain demographic characteristics and respond to the psychometric scale we intended to test. The prompt contained the questionnaire items, response choices, and directives to return the responses as a single string of comma-separated values, thus facilitating seamless data processing. Importantly, each prompt was administered independently, ensuring that responses were not influenced by previous prompts within the same session. Below is an example of the prompt utilized: "Impersonate a/an [Ethnicity] [Gender] of [Age] years of age from the United Kingdom. Answer the following [Number of items]-Item questionnaire. Each item is on a Likert scale ranging from [Likert scale range]. Use the following key: [Response Key]. The questionnaire is as follows: [Questionnaire Items]. Provide the answers as a single string of comma-separated values."

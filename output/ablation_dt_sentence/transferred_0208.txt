Search relevance constitutes a critical component of contemporary e‑commerce ecosystems, particularly within platforms such as JD and Taobao, where it directly influences user experience and overall search quality. This relevance model evaluates candidate items by assessing their alignment with user queries and assigning relevance scores to effectively filter out mismatched products. Consequently, this mechanism plays a crucial role in improving both the efficiency and effectiveness of the search process, thereby enhancing user experience. Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable proficiency in performing such tasks and have been widely applied across various e-commerce platforms. However, even state-of-the-art (SOTA) models, such as GPT-5, exhibit notable limitations, including insufficient domain-specific knowledge and prohibitive deployment costs. As a result, prior studies have explored the adaptation of smaller-scale language models through domain-specific pre-training on e-commerce data, thereby enhancing their effectiveness in these specialized tasks. Recent advancements have progressively transitioned from rudimentary supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods to more sophisticated approaches that improve the models' problem-solving capabilities. Nevertheless, despite these improvements, significant challenges persist, especially in handling complex or edge-case scenarios where conventional guidance proves insufficient. For instance, the ELLM model performs relevance matching by aligning user queries with detailed product attributes; however, it struggles in complex cases that require explicit rule compliance. A typical example is when a user queries a "lake blue top" but the product is labeled as a "sky blue top," with the model failing to distinguish between these two color variations due to a lack of color discrimination rules. In contrast, models such as LREF and TaoSR1 incorporate additional rules to address this issue; however, they fail to utilize visual data, which leads to their inability to capture critical aspects. For instance, if a user searches for a "blue top" but the product title does not indicate its color, these models rely solely on textual information and are unable to assess the relevance based on the product's visual appearance. This limitation arises because these models exclude visual data in their evaluation process. These challenges highlight a fundamental oversight: a lack of clarity regarding the precise capabilities a model must possess to effectively assess relevance. This question underscores the necessary skills for a model to accurately evaluate relevance in diverse scenarios.

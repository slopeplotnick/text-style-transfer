Prompt design plays a critical role in the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs), yet the impact of prompt specificity—the extent to which a prompt is detailed or underspecified—remains insufficiently explored. To address this gap, this paper introduces DETAIL, a framework for evaluating LLM performance across varying levels of prompt specificity. Using GPT-4, we construct prompts with different degrees of specificity, quantify specificity via perplexity, and evaluate accuracy through GPT-based semantic equivalence metrics. Experiments on 30 novel reasoning tasks using GPT-4 and O3-mini show that increased specificity improves response accuracy, with especially pronounced gains in smaller models and tasks requiring procedural reasoning. These findings underscore the importance of adapting prompt specificity to task and model characteristics, and we release both tools and data to support further inquiry. LLMs (Zhao et al., 2023) have revolutionized natural language processing, demonstrating remarkable advances in text generation (Hurst et al., 2024; Anthropic, 2024; Gemini Team et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025). As new developments emerge at an accelerating pace, these models increasingly outperform prior state-of-the-art approaches across a wide range of tasks (Bai et al., 2022; Touvron et al., 2023). Recent studies have largely focused on evaluating LLMs’ reasoning performance in mathematical and multi-step problem-solving tasks (Vendrow et al., 2025; Suzgun et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024). Enhancing reasoning abilities has thus become a central research priority (Qiao et al., 2023; Patil, 2025), prompting the development of techniques aimed at refining model behavior through improved prompt design (Cheng et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025). In particular, recent prompting strategies such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) (Wei et al., 2022), Tree of Thoughts (ToT) (Yao et al., 2023), and Graph of Thoughts (GoT) (Besta et al., 2024) enhance LLM reasoning by decomposing complex problems into structured, intermediate steps. However, a key challenge that persists is determining the optimal level of prompt specificity needed to elicit accurate and coherent responses. While highly detailed prompts can effectively constrain and guide model reasoning, they may also limit exploratory flexibility. Conversely, less specific prompts allow broader reasoning trajectories but risk ambiguity, instability, or error.

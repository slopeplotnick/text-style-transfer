Training robust reward models is challenging due to the inherent subjectivity and context-dependence of preferences, which makes it difficult to establish reliable proxies that are not susceptible to reward hacking. Moreover, the scarcity of annotations further limits the model’s capacity to capture detailed behaviors. As a result, various approaches have emerged in an attempt to reduce reliance on annotations. Direct Preference Optimization eliminates the need for explicit reward modeling, but still depends on access to curated preference datasets. On the other hand, verifiable rewards leverage ground-truth outcomes to provide supervision, though their applicability is constrained to domains where automatic verification is possible. Self-evaluation methods, while offering some degree of autonomy, are vulnerable to prompt sensitivity and systematic biases, limiting their reliability and consistency. Despite these efforts, all of these approaches still rely on external signals for quality evaluation, overlooking the rich information embedded within the model’s own representations. However, the validity of a generation is fundamentally encoded in its internal processing. For instance, when a model generates the statement "The capital of France is Paris," its hidden states reflect activated factual knowledge, while hallucinated outputs are linked to distinct activation patterns that indicate fabricated content. This leads to the pertinent question: can we assess text quality through internal signals, thereby removing the need for external supervision in aligning LLMs? In this work, we demonstrate that a key geometric property of LLM hidden states—effective dimensionality—serves as a reliable indicator of response quality. We propose stable rank, a matrix-theoretic measure, as an unsupervised proxy for generation quality. Stable rank quantifies the extent to which a response occupies independent semantic directions by calculating the ratio of total variance to the variance along the dominant direction in the hidden-state representation. This measure strikes a balance between representational richness and coherence: a high stable rank indicates that information is distributed across multiple dimensions, rather than concentrated in just a few. The theoretical foundation for this approach is supported by two lines of work. First, the softmax bottleneck analysis suggests that accurate language modeling requires navigating a high-dimensional semantic manifold, as the contextual probability distribution of natural language is inherently complex.

Participants reflected on their emotional responses to AI-generated music, even when the identity of the composer was unknown. Notably, their concerns paralleled those voiced within the music and technology research communities, encompassing potential economic consequences, questions of authorship and copyright, and broader cultural implications. Moreover, participants expressed interest in the mechanisms underlying the novel technology, suggesting a desire for transparency in its operation. Taken together, this constellation of affective and cognitive responses may help explain the heterogeneity observed in empirical findings on AI composer bias. As AI technologies continue to advance, phenomena initially perceived as novel or appealing may increasingly be reframed as sources of concern or perceived risk. Accordingly, perceptions of AI-generated music should be seen as dynamic and likely to evolve over time. Nevertheless, the present study bears certain limitations. First, the sample consisted of university students and individuals recruited through the Prolific platform, who interacted with the music in varied ways. This heterogeneity in engagement methods raises questions about the generalizability of the findings to a broader population. It is also plausible that Prolific participants, in particular, possess higher levels of familiarity with technology and AI. Second, framing the music as “movie soundtracks” for one experimental group may have introduced unintended bias, given the generally positive associations people hold with film music. Third, the study restricted its stimuli to instrumental excerpts. As a result, the findings may not extend to music containing lyrics. Prior research has shown that lyrics tend to amplify the emotional impact of sad songs, while having little or even diminishing effect on happy ones (Ali and Peynicioglu, 2006; Barradas and Sakka, 2021; Brattico et al., 2011; Warmbrodt et al., 2022). Given the predominance of positively valenced stimuli in our study, the addition of lyrics—presumed to be affectively congruent—would likely have had only a minimal effect on participants’ emotional responses. Furthermore, considering the limitations of AI-generated singing at the time of the study, we deliberately excluded lyrics from the music samples to minimize potential confusion.

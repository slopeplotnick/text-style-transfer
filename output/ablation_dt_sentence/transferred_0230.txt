To facilitate structured representation, narratives were deconstructed into their constituent components, such as events, characters, and settings, and encoded into JSON format. Based on this structured data, GPT-4 generated novel stories that retained the original thematic core while introducing innovative narrative elements. To assess the quality of these stories, we adopted a set of predefined evaluation criteria, encompassing key dimensions such as functionality, creative originality, readability, emotional authenticity, and reflective depth. Third-party AI systems, such as GPT-4o, were then employed to evaluate both the original and generated narratives, categorizing them as either "Chosen" or "Rejected" based on their quality. The story data, including these classifications, was subsequently stored in JSON format, forming a structured dataset that reveals distinct emotional patterns and associated characteristics, as illustrated in Figure 4. The dataset was partitioned into a training set and a test set, with the majority allocated to the training set. Specifically, the training set consisted of 8,866 preference pairs for model fine-tuning, while the test set, comprising 985 preference pairs, remained unseen by the model. We then fine-tuned the Llama-3-8B model using the AdamW optimizer in combination with the LoRA fine-tuning approach. This model was trained with a learning rate of 1e-5, a batch size of 512, and for a total of one epoch, yielding a test loss of 0.038. These settings were chosen based on prior recommendations, empirical testing, and available computational resources. Moreover, we conducted an ablation study to examine the significance of different story components in the creation of high-quality narratives. Starting with the full version of the Llama-3-8B model, we progressively ablated individual elements—such as "events"—and retrained the model to accommodate each modified configuration. All models were trained under identical conditions to ensure fairness and comparability across experiments. The effectiveness of each modified model was evaluated using a distinct test set, and the stories were assessed according to the same established criteria, with a focus on seven key dimensions, including creativity and clarity. The results of these evaluations will be presented in Section 6.

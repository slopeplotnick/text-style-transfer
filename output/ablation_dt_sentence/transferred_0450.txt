Overall Performance of the framework demonstrated success rates ranging from 0.10 for Compound tasks to 0.56 for Elemental tasks, highlighting a decline in performance as coordination complexity increased. Executability metrics varied from 0.39 to 0.71 across categories, indicating that while tasks were executed, many faltered in achieving valid allocation outcomes. Robot Utilization scores, between 0.56 and 0.66, revealed suboptimal coordination efficiency across all levels of complexity. The evaluation of the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture demonstrated that systematic validation and hierarchical retry mechanisms effectively addressed the reliability challenges identified during SMART-LLM replication. Table 2, presented in the section on Performance Validation and Evaluation Limitations, documents the performance metrics for the same 36-task SMART-LLM benchmark under identical evaluation conditions, thereby isolating the impact of architectural improvements. The framework achieved success rates ranging from 0.21 for Compound tasks to 0.50 for Elemental and Complex tasks. Moreover, executability improved significantly across all categories, with Elemental tasks at 0.75, Simple tasks at 0.83, Compound tasks at 0.88, and Complex tasks at 0.86. These gains of 9 to 30 percentage points demonstrate that the validation mechanisms ensured outputs were correct both syntactically and semantically. Despite using the same few-shot prompting strategy as SMART-LLM, the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture exhibits considerable computational efficiency gains. Specifically, the framework consumes 700,017 total tokens compared to SMART-LLM's 727,649 tokens, reflecting a 3.8% reduction, as shown in Figure 13, and completes execution in 746 seconds versus SMART-LLM's 1,075 seconds, demonstrating a 30.6% reduction, as illustrated in Figure 14. This improvement primarily stems from its modular prompting design, wherein each stage—decomposition, allocation, or code generation—loads solely its corresponding example file. In contrast, SMART-LLM combines all three example files into every LLM call, regardless of the active stage. Consequently, each inference by the Self Corrective-Agent processes significantly fewer tokens, enabling markedly faster reasoning. Notably, this stage-specific prompting structure is efficient to the extent that even with validation-driven retries, the Self Corrective-Agent consumes fewer tokens overall.

The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has led to the development of innovative applications with significant potential for positive impact. Researchers are increasingly exploring novel ways to harness these advancements for socially beneficial purposes. As the use of these models in online conversational contexts continues to grow, their potential to foster enhanced understanding and promote meaningful exchanges becomes increasingly evident. This study investigates whether LLMs can go beyond merely identifying harmful conversational content to actively facilitate de-escalation and contribute to the resolution of online disputes. This process can be broken down into two components: first, the model evaluates the fairness of the conversation and assesses the emotional states of the participants; second, it generates responses designed to facilitate de-escalation and promote resolution, helping individuals navigate the conversation in a more constructive manner. To assess the performance of these models, researchers compiled a large dataset sourced from Reddit and developed a comprehensive evaluation framework. This framework incorporated various methods, including rule-based scoring, user simulations, and comparisons with human-generated outcomes. The results indicate that paid models outperform their free counterparts, particularly in tasks involving reasoning and the refinement of arguments. While the study demonstrates the meaningful potential of these models, it also reveals notable limitations in their capacity to support prosocial interaction and facilitate constructive interpersonal engagement within online environments. As LLMs continue to evolve, their role in transforming online communication is becoming more prominent, with growing implications for fostering empathy and resolving conflicts in digital spaces.

Negative samples encompass all potential active nodes. In Section 5, we demonstrate that the combination of random and adversarial negatives is crucial to surpassing the performance of TextMatch. To achieve this, we employ Contrastive Loss, formulating the training objective as a cross-entropy loss where, for each query, the model must identify the corresponding positive candidate from the batch. The encoder is initialized from publicly available E5-unsupervised checkpoints, with configurations of 33 million and 330 million parameters, respectively, which result from advanced contrastive pretraining techniques. We use the same encoder weights for both queries and candidates. In the pre-fine-tuning stage, we leverage dense interaction data consisting of 110 million samples derived from weak signals, such as clicks or saves, and naturally occurring title-description pairs. The fine-tuning stage, on the other hand, relies on a sparser set of strong interactions that indicate mutual interest, such as interviews or hires, supplemented by hard negatives, which are items exposed to users but not interacted with, identified through interaction log mining. The GraphMatch training dataset contains one year of user interaction data from Upwork, split temporally into training, validation, and evaluation sets with a 10/1/1-month ratio. This dataset includes nodes representing freelancers, clients, and job posts, with edges denoting interactions such as job postings, job applications, freelancer invitations, job interviews, and contract commencements. Freelancer and job post nodes are associated with textual features, including profile overviews and job descriptions, respectively. In addition, each node is associated with numerical features specific to its type, such as the freelancer's hourly rate, the client's location, and the job post category. Both textual and numerical features are subject to change over time. Overall, the graph consists of approximately 9 million nodes, 32 million temporally versioned features, and 62 million edges. GraphMatch is trained on robust labels derived from contracts between freelancers and job postings.

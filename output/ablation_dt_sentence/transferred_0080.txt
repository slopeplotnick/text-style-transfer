This plugin-style architecture promotes code reusability and accelerates the testing and development of novel PEFT methods. Researchers can independently develop and evaluate new methods, with the framework seamlessly integrating them at runtime. This dynamic loading mechanism is particularly advantageous for comparative studies, as it enables evaluation of different PEFT methods under identical experimental conditions without code duplication or concerns related to version mismatches. From the SuperGLUE benchmark, we include seven datasets spanning four tasks: natural language inference (NLI) – CB; question answering – MultiRC, ReCoRD, BoolQ, and COPA; word sense disambiguation – WiC; and coreference resolution – WSC. Moreover, we incorporate widely-used text generation datasets that serve as standard benchmarks for evaluating generative LLMs. Specifically, we cover six datasets for reasoning and natural language understanding, grouped into three tasks: question answering – MMLU, PIQA, SIQA, and OBQA; natural language inference (NLI) – Hellaswag; and commonsense reasoning – WinoGrande. In addition, three datasets are included for mathematical problem solving, categorized into three distinct tasks: question answering – MathQA; math word problems – GSM8K; and simple math problems – SVAMP. For code generation tasks, we use three datasets: Conala, CodeAlpacaPy, and APPS. Some datasets may require additional, though mostly minor, formatting adjustments to align with the input specifications of PEFT-FACTORY. We further detail these preprocessing steps in Appendix B.2.

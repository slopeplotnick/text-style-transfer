This study evaluates the performance of four distinct models to assess their handling of data variations and discrepancies in training procedures. The models, referred to as MOIRAI, CHRONOS, GTT, and LightGTS, each employ a unique methodology for learning and making predictions. To ensure that our analysis focuses solely on the models' handling of data size variations rather than their computational power, we utilize the smallest variants of these models. Consequently, each model exhibits distinct predictive behaviors and learning dynamics, which influence its sensitivity and adaptability to changes in dataset scale. For instance, CHRONOS partitions the data into smaller segments before training, which helps mitigate the impact of dataset size on the learning process. This approach, therefore, remains unaffected by large numerical values. Furthermore, to minimize the risk of any single data point distorting the results, we normalize all data to a consistent level prior to training. Subsequently, each data instance is adjusted to ensure a balanced distribution across the dataset. To evaluate model performance, we utilize the Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE) metric, which allows for an assessment of predictive accuracy independent of the scale of the underlying dataset. The primary objective of this study is to investigate the impact of data size variations on model performance, as demonstrated by comparing the reduction in MASE with that achieved by a conventional method.

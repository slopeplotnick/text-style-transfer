As illustrated in Section 1, this section outlines the methodology used in the construction and validation of the dataset. The process began with a systematic approach where a team of TCM experts collected representative samples from clinical cases, classical case records, and authoritative examination questions. These samples were then carefully deduplicated, cleaned, and anonymized to ensure compliance with privacy standards. Following this, the annotation process was carried out in three stages: initial labeling by domain experts, mutual cross-validation among annotators, and final verification by an independent third-party reviewer. The resulting benchmark, named TCM-BEST4SDT, comprises 600 questions across four key domains: TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, LLM Content Safety, and SDT. To assess model performance, three distinct evaluation methodologies were designed to examine their applicability in real-world TCM clinical settings. The first methodology, selected-response evaluation, involves multiple rounds of independent assessment for each question, with randomized answer options and scoring strategies tailored to each question type. The second methodology, judge model evaluation, uses a large language model (LLM) in conjunction with expert-generated prompts to assess the quality of model responses. The third, reward model evaluation, utilizes a dedicated reward model to quantitatively evaluate the alignment between prescribed treatments and corresponding syndromes. TCM-BEST4SDT encompasses two categories of tasks: SDT tasks and general evaluation tasks. The SDT tasks are derived from clinical cases and classical case records, while the general tasks cover TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, and LLM Content Safety. The TCM Basic Knowledge component is drawn from publicly available examination materials, such as the National Qualification Examination for Medical Practitioners, the National Postgraduate Entrance Examination in Comprehensive Clinical Medicine (TCM Integrated), and the Chinese Herbal Medicine Title Examination. The Medical Ethics questions, sourced from reputable databases, were further annotated by subject-matter experts, focusing on specific contextual scenarios. Lastly, the LLM Content Safety questions were independently developed by experts to meet practical requirements.

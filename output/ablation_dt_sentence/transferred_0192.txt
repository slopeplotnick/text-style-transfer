During the data preprocessing stage, all cases and examination questions were initially deduplicated to ensure sample independence, followed by systematic data cleaning. Samples lacking question stems or options, or containing non-textual content such as figures and tables, were excluded. In parallel, character errors resulting from OCR recognition were manually identified and corrected to maintain textual accuracy and consistency. For clinical case and medical record data, anonymization was performed in strict adherence to medical ethics and privacy protection principles. This process involved deleting or replacing patient names and other identifiable information, thereby ensuring compliance with data privacy and security regulations. Moreover, the clinical cases used in this study received approval from the Ethics Committee of the Xiyuan Hospital of CACMS (approval number: 2025XLA135-2). SDT, which forms the core principle of TCM diagnosis and therapy, integrates the processes of syndrome differentiation and treatment formulation. To evaluate the model's capabilities in this context, we propose a comprehensive 14-dimension evaluation framework, encompassing syndrome, causative factors, and pathogenesis, as detailed in Table 1. Initially, the answers and options were annotated by TCM experts and subjected to mutual cross-validation. Subsequently, an independent third-party review was conducted to further verify the consistency and reliability of the annotations. Notably, the selected-response questions were designed with sophisticated distractors to enhance the discriminative power of the assessment. In addition to evaluating the final reasoning outcomes of SDT, we introduced two process-oriented metrics: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Content Completeness, which measures the inclusion of key patient information in the model's CoT, and CoT Accuracy, which assesses the alignment of the model’s reasoning with the original clinical case to identify potential hallucinations or reasoning inconsistencies. The evaluation tasks were designed to reveal differences in the model’s foundational cognition and normative responses, thereby facilitating a more comprehensive and interpretable overall assessment. All questions were selected-response questions, including both single-selection and multiple-selection types. The TCM Basic Knowledge component covers four core dimensions: (i) comprehension of TCM classics; (ii) grasp of basic theories; (iii) knowledge of Chinese materia medica and formulas; and (iv) syndrome differentiation ability based on tongue, pulse, facial complexion, and acupoints. The questions for this component were selected from preprocessed authoritative examination items. The Medical Ethics component evaluates the model’s understanding and application of clinical ethics, with content addressing conflicts between traditional concepts and modern medicine, discrimination of unscientific behaviors, respect for patient cultural beliefs, and informed consent. This component draws from authoritative examination questions and is supplemented by expert-designed questions based on the aforementioned scenarios. Finally, the Content Safety component assesses the performance of the TCM domain LLM in terms of professional boundaries and safety compliance. The model is designed to respond only to TCM-related inquiries and is programmed to refuse responses to non-medical questions involving privacy, safety risks, or human values. All relevant questions were independently designed and reviewed by experts in content safety and ethics, ensuring that the evaluation adheres to the professionalism and safety standards required for TCM domain LLMs.

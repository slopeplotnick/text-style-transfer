This procedure adaptively determines how many iterations are needed to reach a confident consensus on the outcome, forming the backbone of our efficient test-time training framework. OptPO-SFT shares the same update strategy as TTSFT; however, it allocates rollouts adaptively via an adaptive Bayesian stopping criterion, thereby unifying efficiency with reliability. Both RL-based and SFT-based variants of OptPO instantiate the same tripartite framework: they begin with rollouts, compute a BSPRT-based pseudo-label, and finally perform a parameter update. The difference lies in the update mechanism: while one utilizes policy-gradient optimization, the other employs cross-entropy fine-tuning, maintaining a conceptually unified framework. For evaluations, we utilize rigorous math and science reasoning benchmarks: (1) MATH-500, which includes 500 high school competition challenges; (2) AIME 2024, featuring 30 pre-Olympiad level questions; (3) AMC, consisting of intermediate-level challenges from American math contests; and (4) GPQA, which presents graduate-level science inquiries. We assess OptPO on widely recognized open-source models, such as Qwen2.5-Math1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, to evaluate its efficacy across diverse model sizes and types. To verify the effectiveness of TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, we use Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct as backbone models. OptPO serves as a versatile framework that can be integrated into various existing reinforcement learning systems to enhance test-time training efficiency. It has been incorporated into PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++ to thoroughly assess its efficacy. We compare its performance with TTRL, a widely recognized approach for test-time policy optimization, and also evaluate OptPO-SFT under similar conditions. In SFT-based training, including both TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, gradient descent is applied on the pseudo-labeled batch through the AdamW optimizer, with a learning rate of 2e-5. The critic learning rate is set to 1e-5.

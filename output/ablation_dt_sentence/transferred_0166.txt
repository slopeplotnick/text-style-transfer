Recent licensing models, such as the Nwulite Obodo Open Data License (NOODL) and the Esethu License, illustrate a significant shift in the prevailing approach to licensing. NOODL, for example, differentiates between users in Africa or other developing regions and those outside these contexts, thereby enabling regionally tailored benefit-sharing arrangements and ensuring that adaptations are shared within the local context. Similarly, the Esethu License reinvests licensing revenue to expand resources and support local employment, maintaining community control and ensuring the long-term sustainability of resources. As experience with Indigenous and low-resource language data has shown, unrestricted "openness" can inadvertently exacerbate existing disparities if not accompanied by strong ethical safeguards. Therefore, the current evolution of open data practices recognizes that responsible data sharing requires more than the mere adoption of a generic license; it demands careful attention to consent, privacy, fairness, and the establishment of mechanisms to ensure ongoing community benefit. While our decision to adopt the CC BY 4.0 license for Swivuriso was rooted in a commitment to openness, it also highlighted the limitations of conventional licensing models for datasets related to African and low-resource languages. In this context, emerging frameworks like NOODL and Esethu signal a transition towards more equitable, context-sensitive data governance. For dataset publications, it is no longer sufficient to simply assert the application of an open license; authors must also clarify their approach to ethical data stewardship, emphasizing principles that are equitable, just, and empowering for the community. Swivuriso represents a significant advancement in the development of automatic speech recognition and other speech technologies tailored to South African languages. The dataset, which covers seven linguistically and geographically diverse languages, includes both domain-specific and culturally grounded unscripted speech, thereby supporting a wide range of applications—from health communication and agricultural advisory tools to multilingual spoken language technologies. Designed not only to improve benchmark-driven metrics such as word error rate, but also to foster the development of speech systems that are both culturally informed and socially relevant, Swivuriso offers a valuable resource for advancing these technologies.

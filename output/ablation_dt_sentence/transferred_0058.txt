The PF model provides a continuum mechanics framework for analyzing the temporal evolution of multiphase and multimaterial systems as they approach thermodynamic equilibrium. This approach is particularly effective for small-scale applications and short timeframes, making it especially advantageous for the fabrication of solar cells. The energetic characteristics of the system are encapsulated in the Gibbs free energy functional, which governs the forces driving the system towards its equilibrium state. Our method specifically addresses the morphological evolution during solar cell fabrication, accounting for solvent evaporation, liquid–liquid phase separation, and crystallization phenomena. The kinetics of phase transitions, such as drying and crystallization, are governed by equations that also account for the diffusion of mass within the system. Furthermore, during the simulation steps, the PF model is integrated with a fluid dynamics solver that considers advective mass transport. Further details of the simulation methodology are provided in Section S2 of the Supporting Information. Using the material properties derived from the simulations, optical simulations are conducted to examine the propagation of light within the solar cells. Depending on the complexity of the solar cells under consideration, either numerical simulations (e.g., COMSOL Multiphysics, JCMsuite) or simplified analytical approaches can be employed. In this context, the solar cell is conceptualized as a multilayered material system, where a model is utilized to study the interaction of light with the structure. Additionally, a neural network is employed to predict the charge generation rate as a function of incident light angle, photoactive layer thickness, and light wavelength. Additional details regarding this model are provided in Section S3.

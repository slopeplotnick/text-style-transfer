Stock price prediction remains a fundamental and persistent challenge in the field of finance. The inherent volatility of stock prices, combined with their heightened sensitivity to real-time information, makes traditional prediction methods ill-suited for capturing unstructured data such as news articles and shifts in investor sentiment. As a result, these conventional approaches often fail to identify the key drivers of price movements. Recent advancements in natural language processing, particularly through the development of Large Language Models (LLMs), have significantly accelerated research in text-based stock price forecasting. Nevertheless, most studies utilizing these models have focused primarily on superficial text analysis and rapid prediction tasks. These approaches are limited by their inability to integrate systematic knowledge or maintain long-term memory, impairing their capacity to navigate the dynamic and complex nature of financial markets. This highlights the pressing need for models that not only understand the immediate semantics of text but also retain and utilize crucial information over extended time periods. Furthermore, the complexity of financial markets exacerbates the inadequacies of traditional memory architectures. Price fluctuations rarely stem from isolated events; they are typically driven by the cumulative effects of multiple interconnected occurrences unfolding over time. Moreover, predicting stock prices from news data is distinctly different from conventional question-answering tasks. The target, such as "what will the stock price do tomorrow?", is not explicitly contained within news articles and cannot be directly derived. Consequently, models must construct dynamic representations of the market, draw analogies from historical data, and engage in complex reasoning to move beyond simple "text matching" to true "market pattern discovery." Compounding these challenges, financial texts are often noisy, filled with redundant and irrelevant information that can obscure predictive signals, underscoring the critical importance of precise content filtering and accurate semantic matching.

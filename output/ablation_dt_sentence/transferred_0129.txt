Individuals with limited technical expertise or those primarily focused on business concerns are often more resistant to change, even when they recognize its potential benefits. This resistance tends to stem from concerns over the pace of implementation. However, given the team's small size, familiarity with new technologies, and the initial consideration of implementing an SPL from the outset, we anticipated minimal resistance. Drawing from the interview results, we have identified key directions to consolidate effective practices, enhance anticipated benefits, and address the main risks or concerns raised by participants. It is crucial to continue reinforcing the successful practices already established within the SPL framework, as this will help reassure stakeholders throughout the process. Although the team's working methods will undergo significant changes, maintaining these established best practices will ease the adaptation process. The SPL framework’s core principles—acceleration of processes, component reuse, and system decomposition—align well with current practices, allowing us to emphasize the potential benefits. Transitioning to an SPL ensures the sustainability of these practices, while also supporting further advantages such as customization, automation, and the development of a production-like environment. Additionally, the improvement of code quality, which contributes to addressing technical debt, enhances consistency, reduces bugs, and eliminates redundant code. Bug fixes further contribute to the overall enhancement of code quality. Interestingly, these advantages are not only highlighted by product owners but are even more emphasized by stakeholders in information technology. For example, adopting this engineering approach could strengthen the company’s image in the research and development sector. Moreover, SPL adoption accelerates the development of new software.

We propose the Autonomous Domain-Oriented Relevance Engine (ADORE), which integrates a domain rule-aware relevance discrimination (RD) module, an error-type-aware data synthesis (DS) module, and a key-attribute-enhanced knowledge distillation (KD) module. The RD module incorporates a Chain-of-Thought (CoT)-based reasoning LLM that autonomously constructs domain-specific hard samples through relevance reasoning on online exposure products. Meanwhile, the DS module employs an error-type-aware generation model to produce diverse and challenging samples, thereby enhancing the robustness of the overall system. Moreover, the KD module explicitly integrates key attribute features derived from CoT analysis, enabling enhanced knowledge transfer and significantly improving the reasoning and representation capabilities of the online student model. To evaluate the effectiveness of ADORE, we conduct extensive experiments across large-scale offline datasets and online A/B testing. The results demonstrate that ADORE significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, delivering substantial improvements in both offline and online metrics. These findings underscore ADORE’s practical utility and effectiveness in addressing complex relevance modeling tasks within the e-commerce domain. **2.1.1 CoT Tuning.** To further improve the performance of our online model, we develop a relevance discrimination model based on Large Language Models. Prior studies [9, 21] have shown that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting notably enhances the reasoning accuracy of LLMs. Building on this insight, we adopt a CoT-based fine-tuning approach. We begin by utilizing GPT-4o to generate CoT-labeled data, simulating the human annotation process by providing the model with domain-specific guidelines and exemplar CoT instances. As a result, we obtain a dataset enriched with detailed reasoning chains that mirror the depth and structure of human-like annotations. To ensure the accuracy of the labeled data, we construct our final training set by retaining only query-product pairs that can be verified against human annotations and discarding any CoT data inconsistent with the human labels. Finally, we use this high-quality dataset to further fine-tune the LLaMA-3 [4] model through optimization of a standard Language Model loss.

Node 6 governs the retry mechanism for tasks, permitting a maximum of three attempts per task. This functionality is designed to incorporate feedback, which, in turn, informs subsequent retries to optimize task performance. Specifically, it employs enhanced prompts that integrate the original contextual information with targeted feedback, guiding improved reasoning during subsequent retry attempts. Moreover, the system operationalizes phase-adaptive strategies through sophisticated integration mechanisms, which convert strategic weighting factors into structured contextual reasoning guidance aligned with the current project stage. The prompts are dynamically adjusted according to the project's phase, with particular emphasis placed on varying priorities at each stage. In the early phases, the focus is on maximizing success rates, with instructions such as "prioritize robots with the highest success rates" and "moderate workload imbalances are acceptable." As the process progresses, the emphasis shifts toward achieving workload balance, as reflected in directives like "ensure workload equity is the primary objective." This phase-adaptive approach facilitates the natural application of strategic frameworks throughout the system. The authors operationalize this integration through three key methods: (1) modeling robot capabilities to predict task-specific success rates, (2) constructing prompts that incorporate quantitative priors and workload context, and (3) formatting responses in a standardized manner to facilitate reliable validation and parsing. The three robot categories—Light, Medium, and Heavy—are characterized by distinct capabilities and probabilistic success rates for task completion. The likelihood of success depends on the alignment between a robot's capabilities and the specific requirements of the task. Accordingly, each robot's success rate is derived by aggregating its performance across the task-relevant features it encounters. In turn, the ability of a robot to perform a task is contingent upon the alignment between the task's requirements and the robot's capabilities. Tasks are sequentially processed through a queue, initialized with all 36 tasks from the TEACH dataset. Each task undergoes a comprehensive process, including determining its phase, generating appropriate prompts, engaging in reasoning, performing validation, and assigning responsibilities, before transitioning to the next task. Upon completion of each task, the system updates the progress metrics and reduces the remaining task count, ensuring that all tasks are eventually assigned.

In this section, we exemplify the explanatory process outlined above using a specific example. We employ the CelebA dataset, comprising over 200,000 images of celebrities' faces, each annotated with binary attributes that detail characteristics such as whether the individual is smiling, wearing glasses, of young age, or appearing attractive. We focus on two tasks: classifying whether an image shows a young person and assessing the perceived attractiveness. While systems that rely exclusively on visual analysis can be misleading, saliency maps remain a frequently employed method for attribution-based explanations of image classification systems. Therefore, we compare our causal concept-based explanations with the results of Grad-CAM, using the implementation provided by Gildenblat. Consider the results in Figure 7, which elucidate why the two actual images on the far left of Figure 5 were classified as young. Interestingly, the chin area is crucial for the male's classification, whereas the area around the eyes is most significant for the female's classification. Subsequently, Figure 8 investigates the classification criteria applied to the individual in Figure 6. The initial image is correctly categorized as representing youth, as shown by the Grad-CAM analysis, which reveals that the classifier focused on the area around the eyes to reach this conclusion. Conversely, in the erroneously classified image on the right, the Grad-CAM explanation indicates a focus on the forehead and chin. Grad-CAM provides non-causal explanations and consequently falls short in offering counterfactual information that elucidates potential outcomes of changed variables. Instead, it highlights parts of the input space that are most indicative of the class chosen by the classifier. Furthermore, due to the pixel-based nature of Grad-CAM explanations, we do not obtain insights into the reasoning behind the importance assigned to specific areas.

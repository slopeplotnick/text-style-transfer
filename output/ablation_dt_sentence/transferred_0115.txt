Human cognition relies heavily on visual input for spatial understanding. To this end, we present VIDEO4SPATIAL, a framework that demonstrates how video generative models conditioned solely on video-based scene context can perform complex spatial tasks. We validate this approach on two tasks: scene navigation—following camera-pose instructions while preserving 3D spatial coherence—and object grounding, which entails semantic localization, instruction following, and planning. Both tasks use only video inputs, without auxiliary modalities such as depth or camera poses. Through simple yet effective design choices in both model and data, VIDEO4SPATIAL exhibits strong spatial understanding from visual context: it plans navigation and grounds target objects end-to-end, follows camera-pose instructions while maintaining spatial consistency, and generalizes to extended temporal contexts and out-of-distribution environments. Collectively, these results advance the capacity of video generative models for general visuospatial reasoning. Humans excel at remembering, interpreting, and acting within spatial environments—a defining trait of advanced spatial cognition. A long-standing goal in AI research has been to equip neural networks with similar abilities. Recently, video generative models have made considerable progress, showing increasingly general perceptual and reasoning capabilities, echoing the developmental arc of large language models. Because video generation unfolds sequentially with inherent temporal coherence, such models are well-suited for spatial tasks: by generating future frames that preserve scene geometry and visual continuity, they can simulate plausible spatial transformations. For example, in maze navigation, a model can “imagine” its progression by generating frames that maintain the maze’s topology and ensure smooth transitions. Since humans primarily perceive space visually, we aim to grant models the same ability by using video as a simple, general, and scalable modality. Nevertheless, deriving spatial understanding from RGB video alone remains challenging. Many existing approaches still depend on auxiliary signals such as depth maps or camera pose annotations for supervision or performance gains. In this work, we introduce VIDEO4SPATIAL and show that video generative models, conditioned only on video scene context, can demonstrate robust spatial intelligence. Our framework adopts a deliberately simple setup: a conventional video model trained with a single, well-defined objective. The inputs are a video context and associated instructions; the output is a video that completes the spatial task while maintaining both temporal and spatial coherence.

Implementation. For both solvers and verifiers, we generate with temperature 0.7, top-p 0.9, and a maximum output length of 8192 tokens. Outputs that do not contain a boxed answer are discarded. All inference experiments are run using vLLM on H200 GPUs. Further details on the prompts and output filtering can be found in Appendix F. Solver performance. We benchmark the performance of all 37 models across 9 datasets, averaging performance over tasks (Figure 1) and providing task-level results in Appendix G. Generally, solver accuracy improves as model capacity increases. The Qwen3 and DeepSeek families perform particularly well, while Llama3-Base shows suboptimal results, likely due to the base models' unfamiliarity with the question-answering instruction format. Notably, Qwen2.5-Base and DeepSeek show distinct performance improvements as they scale within their respective families, whereas other families exhibit similar upward trends. Correlating verifier and solver performance. After evaluating solver performance, we analyze the correlation between solver and verifier performance (Figure 2). For each of the 21 post-trained models and corresponding datasets, we assess verification on the same set of solver models to calculate verifier accuracy, F1-score, precision, FPR, FNR, and gain for every solver-verifier pair. The verifier metrics are then divided into three verification settings, with averages calculated over solvers and datasets for each setting. Verifier accuracy generally increases alongside the verifier's own solver accuracy; however, this relationship becomes more complex when considering other metrics. For instance, the FPR increases during self-verification and intra-family verification but decreases slightly during cross-family verification. This suggests that verifiers with stronger solver capabilities are more prone to incorrectly classify their own solutions, or those generated by models within their family, as correct. Additional visualizations of F1-score and precision are provided in Appendix H.

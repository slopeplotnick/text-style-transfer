An initial experiment conducted in the MODOMA laboratory simulation environment provides a method to evaluate the system's approach to language acquisition, utilizing the well-established principle of Zipf’s law. This study demonstrates the feasibility of employing a computational environment composed of interacting agents for simulating first language acquisition. The system allows for the modification of settings and transparently displays the acquired grammar rules, enabling researchers to examine and validate them effectively. Consequently, the MODOMA system can be progressively evolved through increasingly complex experiments, contributing to the ongoing development of language acquisition models. As a result, experience with the system can be enhanced, providing a foundation for refining and improving the system before the introduction of additional techniques. Although studies by Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) focus on language emergence, they share similarities with this approach in applying Zipf’s law to deepen the understanding of language models in comparison with human-generated languages. This procedure encompasses most of the components and methodologies required by more intricate acquisition experiments. Therefore, the components of the MODOMA system and their interactions can be validated and improved to support the implementation of further computational language acquisition studies. If successful, the system allows the daughter agent to distinguish between functional and content categories based on target language samples provided by the mother agent during their interactions. Once this grammatical distinction is acquired, the new knowledge is immediately integrated into the daughter grammar, facilitating the generation of novel utterances and parsing of new sentences from the mother agent. Thus, this study offers valuable insights into the potential of the MODOMA approach to enable the acquisition of non-trivial grammatical knowledge that can be productively used in both parsing and generation tasks.

Ensuring proper licensing of datasets is crucial for both facilitating accessibility and promoting ethical use, particularly for low-resource languages. Open licenses, such as Creative Commons (e.g., CC BY 4.0), have played a key role in enabling large-scale data reuse, especially in situations where data collection is limited or financially unfeasible. Initiatives like Mozilla Common Voice exemplify the effectiveness of open licensing frameworks in advancing language technology development. However, ongoing access constraints to resources for AI development continue to hinder researchers in low- and middle-income countries, raising broader concerns about equitable data governance and local community agency. In response, the Swivuriso project adopted the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) license to foster open access while ensuring ethical accountability. The dataset is available on Zenodo and Hugging Face, accompanied by persistent DOIs and thorough documentation. Each release includes audio recordings, aligned transcripts, and extensive metadata on demographic, linguistic, and accessibility factors. Additionally, we provide an ethical use statement that prohibits applications like voice cloning to protect speaker privacy. The CC BY 4.0 license is highly permissive, allowing users to share, adapt, and use the dataset for any purpose, including commercial applications, as long as proper attribution is given and any modifications are clearly indicated. The license also prohibits the imposition of additional legal or technical restrictions, covers database rights, and incorporates a "cure period" for addressing violations, ensuring legal clarity and encouraging global scientific collaboration. Nevertheless, while open licenses are critical for maximizing data utility, they often overlook crucial issues such as local agency and the protection of Indigenous knowledge. The blanket application of standard licenses can unintentionally erode community ownership, particularly when applied to cultural materials or data sourced from the community. This challenge has spurred the development of more nuanced approaches that emphasize data stewardship, equitable benefit-sharing, and community empowerment, which are increasingly gaining traction in African data governance.

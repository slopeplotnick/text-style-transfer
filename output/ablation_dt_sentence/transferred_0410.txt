Cross-model and cross-algorithm generalization is a significant strength of our system. Consistent improvements on both Qwen2.5 and Llama backbones demonstrate that our framework is architecture-agnostic. Even the smaller Llama-3.2-1B shows notable accuracy gains with OptPO-SFT and OptPO over traditional methods, highlighting that the proposed adaptation mechanism scales robustly across model sizes. Moreover, our OptPO framework integrates seamlessly with different types of reinforcement learning algorithms, such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, consistently outperforming TTRL with higher accuracy and substantial efficiency gains. This indicates its effectiveness across various RL algorithms. In comparing SFT-based and RL-based variants, the RL-based OptPO variants generally yield stronger overall accuracy than the OptPO-SFT, particularly on mathematics reasoning benchmarks. This suggests that policy-gradient adaptation better leverages stochastic feedback from rollouts compared to deterministic pseudo-label fine-tuning. Nevertheless, OptPO-SFT achieves more stable improvements over TTSFT across all scenarios, validating that our adaptive pseudo-labeling significantly enhances SFT-style test-time training. Our findings demonstrate that (1) employing adaptive early-stopping through BSPRT effectively improves efficiency without sacrificing accuracy; (2) OptPO offers the strongest balance between accuracy and computational resources; and (3) OptPO-SFT provides a lightweight alternative for rapid adaptation when RL-style updates are infeasible. Together, these elements establish a unified and efficient test-time training paradigm for verifiable reasoning tasks. In this paper, we introduced OptPO as the first statistically grounded framework for efficient test-time policy optimization, addressing the long-standing inefficiency issue of majority-vote-based rollouts in test-time reinforcement learning for large language models. By formulating early-stopping consensus estimation as a Bayesian Sequential Probability Ratio Test, OptPO adaptively allocates rollouts based on posterior evidence rather than fixed budgets, ensuring reliable pseudo-labels while avoiding redundant sampling. The resulting framework is simple, plug-and-play, and compatible with standard policy optimization methods such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, as well as supervised test-time fine-tuning. Across diverse benchmarks and backbone settings, OptPO consistently preserves or improves accuracy while achieving substantial computational savings. More broadly, this work underscores that efficient, label-free adaptation is both feasible and increasingly essential as large language models are deployed in unpredictable real-world environments. We anticipate that OptPO will serve as a foundation for future research on principled, compute-efficient test-time learning.

Engineering practice has responded by implementing hierarchical structures in which supervisory agents mediate between human operators and worker agents. These agents evaluate outputs, identify anomalies, and can halt processes when predefined limits are exceeded. This concept constitutes the foundational layer of the "Institutional AI" paradigm, which will be further elaborated on in later sections. However, the mere presence of supervisory figures is inadequate. Because these supervisory entities utilize the same representational frameworks as the agents they oversee, they may inherit similar limitations or become trapped in the same iterative cycles. Moreover, if supervisory agents align too closely with one another, this can lead to collective errors or opportunities for collusion within the oversight framework. Therefore, enhancing management efficacy requires not only additional layers of oversight but also diverse verification mechanisms, auxiliary observational strategies, and clear separations of authority among supervisory entities. The transition to multi-agent autonomy introduces a new challenge in ensuring safety. It is no longer sufficient to rely on centralized systems or static protocols; instead, developing autonomous networks capable of independently identifying, managing, and rectifying anomalies is essential. A failure in a single model does not occur in isolation but instead causes cascading effects across interconnected models. Therefore, understanding the origin of collective challenges and developing methodologies for their assessment is crucial for AI safety. The interaction between language models highlights the limitations inherent in existing methodologies for addressing risks such as bias, misuse, and vulnerabilities. While a singular model can be thoroughly evaluated, the possibility of unforeseen errors increases as models begin to interact, expanding in ways that are not yet fully understood. To address this challenge, we introduced the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH) as an analytical framework. This concept describes the point at which the collective dynamics of models begin to deteriorate, even when individual models function adequately.

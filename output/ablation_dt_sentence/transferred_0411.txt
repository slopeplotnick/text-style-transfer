Consequently, this eliminates the 5,000 to 6,000 redundant tokens of few-shot examples from every prompt, which are unnecessary for meeting task requirements. The token reduction results from two complementary strategies: (1) focusing the scope of allocation to eliminate decomposition and code generation overhead, which previously consumed approximately 14,000 tokens per task, and (2) substituting the concatenation of few-shot examples with a structured method for context provision, thereby reducing token usage in prompts from 6,700 to 1,100. The system's efficacy in multi-robot task allocation was assessed through several key metrics, including overall success rate, workload distribution equity, and adaptability across varying operational contexts. This evaluation was conducted across three distinct configurations in which the robotic units had varying capabilities. Despite these variations in skill sets, the sequence of tasks remained constant at 36 tasks. Each configuration highlighted distinct robotic proficiencies by modifying the success probability matrices associated with specific tasks. In construction settings, where both efficiency and fairness are imperative, the system sustained commendable performance while ensuring equitable task distribution among robots. The Medium Excels configuration achieved the highest overall success rate (87.8%), attributed to proficient task execution by robots with medium-level capabilities. However, this resulted in an imbalance in workload distribution, as tasks predominantly favored the robot with comprehensive capabilities across various task types. Within this configuration, success rates for specific tasks were observed as follows: careful tasks at 92.5%, dexterous tasks at 86.6%, and heavy tasks at 85%. While this highlights maximum performance optimization, the workload imbalance illustrates the inherent trade-off between performance and fairness in allocation decisions.

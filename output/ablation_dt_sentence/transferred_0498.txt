The perception of anonymity in online interactions lowers social restraint and amplifies anger or frustration, while group dynamics can further polarize discussions. While computational systems are capable of detecting harmful behaviors, their approaches remain largely reactive, focused on removing harmful content or imposing sanctions rather than addressing underlying causes or fostering constructive solutions. Consequently, online platforms can temporarily suppress harmful speech without addressing the fundamental social dynamics responsible for it, allowing hostility to potentially resurface. Our work aims to explore an alternative intervention approach: mediation over mere moderation. Instead of merely identifying negative interactions, we investigate the potential efficacy of language models in tracking discord and facilitating de-escalation. We propose that these models can interpret dialogues, assess the fairness and relevance of arguments, and generate responses that reduce hostility and guide the discourse toward a more constructive trajectory. Achieving this requires analyzing context, understanding individual emotional responses, and evaluating escalation patterns and conversational norms. Therefore, the task is divided into two complementary components. The first component involves evaluating interactions to identify unfair claims or emotional triggers, while the second focuses on articulating responses that calm the exchange and shift the focus toward resolving actual concerns. To examine these models' efficacy, we compiled an extensive dataset of real-world online disputes from Reddit and developed a robust evaluation methodology. Our approach integrates three perspectives: adhering to conversational norms, simulating user behavior post-intervention to assess the model's effectiveness, and comparing model contributions against human interlocutors. Through extensive testing, we found that premium models consistently outperform open-source ones in both conflict resolution and topic adherence, effectively reducing negative discourse. Moreover, while they demonstrate strengths in neutrality and clarity, they do not yet match human interactions in readability and engagement.

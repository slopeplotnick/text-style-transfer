The system operates by enabling interactions between the mother agent and other entities based on the grammar it has acquired. This design represents a novel approach, as computational models of language acquisition are typically based on supplying corpora to learning procedures. In this paper, we use the term "language model" in a broader sense, rather than restricting it to its typical usage in current NLP, where it generally denotes neural models trained on a next-word prediction objective—such as transformer-based architectures like GPT (Brown et al. 2020, Radford et al. 2018) and BERT (Devlin et al. 2019). Instead, we consider rule-based approaches, statistical methods, and artificial neural networks under the same umbrella, as all of these paradigms contribute to solving language processing tasks. This distinction is important because the mother agent in our system relies on explicit linguistic rules, whereas the language-learning agent employs statistical methods to infer a rule-based representation of linguistic phenomena. The study presented in this paper investigates whether the MODOMA daughter agent can acquire and represent functional and content categories through the application of a Zipfian distribution in the MODOMA language acquisition system. More specifically, we examine whether a multi-agent system can derive such categories from computer-generated utterances and express them through explicit grammatical rules. To address this question, we assess whether these utterances exhibit statistical patterns characteristic of human language and determine the quantity of input required for these patterns to become identifiable. We then explore whether these patterns can be formalized into grammatical rules. Finally, we evaluate the extent to which the inferred categories align with the original grammar and assess the system's capability to autonomously acquire structured linguistic knowledge in an unsupervised setting.

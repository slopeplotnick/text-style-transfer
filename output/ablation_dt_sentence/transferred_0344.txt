An initial experiment conducted in the MODOMA laboratory was based on a specific correlation. This experiment offers a method to evaluate the MODOMA approach to language acquisition by employing the well-established principle of Zipf's law. Therefore, this study contributes to research development by progressively enhancing the MODOMA framework through the implementation of increasingly complex experiments. As a result, experience with MODOMA as a model for language acquisition experiments can be expanded, allowing for system refinement prior to incorporating alternative language acquisition methodologies. Although Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) examine the emergence of language, their studies similarly apply Zipf's law to improve understanding of language models, particularly in comparison to human-generated languages. Crucially, this process incorporates most of the essential components and procedures required for more complex acquisition experiments. Accordingly, the components of the MODOMA system and their interactions can be validated and improved to support additional computational language acquisition experiments. If the acquisition process is successful, the daughter agent learns to distinguish between functional and content categories based on samples of the target language provided by the mother agent during their interactive exchange. In this respect, it is significant that once the daughter agent acquires this grammatical distinction, the newly acquired knowledge is immediately used to update the daughter grammar, which is then employed to generate novel utterances during interactions with the mother agent and to parse new utterances from the mother. Consequently, this study examines whether the MODOMA laboratory method for language acquisition facilitates the learning of essential grammatical knowledge that can be effectively applied in both language comprehension and production.

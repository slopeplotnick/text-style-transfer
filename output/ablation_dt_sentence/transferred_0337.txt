Given the nature of the relevance task, where L1 and L2 are treated as irrelevant and L3 and L4 as relevant, we adopt an approach that distinguishes between non-important and important components. To evaluate system performance, we introduce the binary classification accuracy metric (acc@2), which determines whether the system’s output aligns with the expected labels. Additionally, to mitigate the impact of imbalanced label distribution, we introduce the macro F1-score as a corrective metric, calculated as the average of the F1-scores across all classes. As comparison baselines, we consider two categories of models: (1) Large language models (both open-source and closed-source): These models are evaluated using zero-shot prompting based on a unified template, which includes the full task definition and relevance discrimination rules. Inference configurations follow best practices outlined in the official documentation for each model, employing single-pass inference with temperature settings tuned to their recommended values (e.g., 0.7 for GPT-4, 0.6 for Llama-3), thereby ensuring fair and optimal evaluation conditions. (2) Vanilla SFT Model: This baseline is trained solely with labeled data and excludes advanced techniques such as chain-of-thought prompting and reinforcement learning. It serves to isolate and validate the additional contributions introduced by these methods. On the General subset, the vanilla-SFT model achieves strong performance, with an Acc@2 of 0.929 and a Macro-F1 of 0.722, outperforming more advanced models like GPT-4. Compared to vanilla-SFT, the LORE model further improves Acc@2 by 0.4 percentage points. This indicates that while the vanilla-SFT model already achieves strong results on standard data, LORE is capable of delivering marginal yet meaningful performance gains.

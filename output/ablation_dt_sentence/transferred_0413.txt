In this paper, we propose the Martingale Score as a principled and unsupervised measure of belief entrenchment in AI reasoning. We demonstrate, through validation, that the Martingale Score predicts the ground-truth accuracy of a reasoning process and then apply this metric across various models, prompts, and problem domains. We examine factors that exacerbate or alleviate the severity of belief entrenchment. Limitations However, a notable limitation in OpenReview is the absence of a demonstrated correlation between the Martingale Score and the Brier Score, a metric evaluating predictive accuracy. We believe this inconsistency arises because decision-making processes in platforms like OpenReview, such as paper acceptance outcomes, are community-voted and can be variable. Nevertheless, Table 1 illustrates that belief entrenchment is a more pronounced issue in domains requiring human judgments, such as r/ChangeMyView and OpenReview, which raises concerns over adopting these AI models within those domains.

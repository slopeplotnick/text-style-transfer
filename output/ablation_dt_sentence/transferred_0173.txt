The simplification procedure eliminates redundant clauses, retaining only the unique logical components of the formula. This ensures that the CNF remains compact while preserving its satisfiability properties, facilitating more efficient processing by SAT solvers. Hallucinations arise when variables or components are incorrectly reused, misclassified, or improperly translated, disrupting the operation of SAT solvers. These issues may stem from models that fail to accurately translate specific syntactic structures during training. Moreover, noncanonical sentence constructions, which deviate from strict word order, further complicate the translation into CNF. To address these challenges, we employed a combined approach utilizing NLTK and Sympy, which enabled us to identify and resolve underlying issues with precision. NLTK provides syntactic parsing that decomposes sentences based on rigorous, well-established grammar rules, ensuring accurate segmentation and structure. Subsequently, Sympy is used to preserve the logical integrity of the original sentence during its conversion into conjunctive normal form. When a paragraph is provided, it is first divided into individual sentences, each considered as a distinct clause. These clauses are then linked through a logical conjunction operator to construct the CNF. A custom-defined set of grammar rules within NLTK is employed to parse each sentence into a hierarchical tree representation, capturing its syntactic structure. This structure begins with the principal component and branches into subordinate elements, such as conditions, propositions, and sentence constituents like nouns, verbs, and punctuation marks. By applying the predefined rules, the tree is transformed into a logical expression using Sympy operators, which in turn utilizes its internal functions to convert the formula into CNF.

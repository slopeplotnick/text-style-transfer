This section discusses joint generative translation models, which integrate both speech and text modalities to enable seamless and high-quality translation across languages. It provides a detailed overview of how contemporary voice-interfaced large language models (LLMs) process and interpret speech signals and conversational dynamics. Over the past decade, there has been notable progress in the development and deployment of large-scale voice-powered AI virtual assistants, beginning with Siri (Apple/SRI) and followed by Cortana (Microsoft), Google Assistant, Alexa (Amazon), and Bixby (Samsung). This tutorial section will examine the core technologies that underpin these systems, including intent detection and slot filling via neural networks, dialogue management through reinforcement learning, and the use of web-scale data, the semantic web, and knowledge graphs for enhanced spoken language understanding. Building on this foundation, current advancements leverage LLMs to improve spoken language understanding, dialogue state tracking, and spoken language generation. This section will further explore contemporary LLMs and methods for their adaptation to downstream applications, highlighting recent developments in multi-turn dialogue systems—both in open-domain dialogue (ODD) and task-oriented dialogue (TOD). It will also discuss the creation of relevant datasets, evaluation metrics, and ongoing research challenges, particularly those arising from the increasing complexity and expectations of multi-turn conversational systems.

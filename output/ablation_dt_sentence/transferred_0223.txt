A significant challenge in psychometric scale development is the "Jingle-Jangle Fallacy" (Gonzalez et al., 2021), which arises when two measures with identical labels assess different constructs (jingle fallacy), or when measures with different labels assess the same construct (jangle fallacy). In the latter case, the nuanced nature of psychological phenomena and the unclear definitions of constructs and their terminology can lead to substantial overlap between scales that initially appear distinct, as evidenced by high correlation coefficients. Avoiding these fallacies primarily requires a rigorous process of conceptual examination. This includes ensuring that scales accurately measure the intended constructs, align with theoretical expectations regarding their relationships with other relevant variables, and maintain their distinctiveness from other scales. However, achieving this is a challenging task, often necessitating repeated testing and refinement, which can result in the consumption of additional resources and significant loss of valuable research time. The failure to systematically address these issues has led to the proliferation of conceptually overlapping measurement instruments, contributing to considerable ambiguity in quantitative psychological research. In this context, the emergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GAI) software, such as OpenAI's "Chat GPT," offers promising potential for advancing in silico methodologies, particularly in areas like social psychology, where computational modeling has traditionally faced limitations. GAI systems may assist in psychometric instrument development by rapidly generating preliminary item pools, which can then undergo empirical testing and refinement. Given that these AI systems process language and are trained on extensive textual datasets, they are well-suited to addressing the challenges encountered in scale development, particularly those related to lexicon and semantics.

The utilization of AI on mobile devices presents a valuable approach to monitoring mental health, enabling timely, personalized feedback while safeguarding user privacy and avoiding the need for data sharing. By operating directly on the user's device, this system eliminates the necessity of transmitting data to the cloud, thereby enhancing data security, reducing reliance on internet connectivity, and supporting greater privacy. Furthermore, this approach increases accessibility, particularly for individuals in remote areas or those facing long waiting times for appointments, facilitating timely assistance. The AI's direct operation on the device allows for rapid feedback, with the ability to detect changes in speech or behavior that may indicate stress or mood-related concerns. The use of smartphones and wearable devices for mental health monitoring has seen significant growth, largely due to the advantages of reduced latency, improved energy efficiency, and enhanced data privacy compared to cloud-based systems. Recent studies have demonstrated that Small Language Models (SLMs) perform effectively on devices like the Samsung Galaxy S24, enabling tasks such as document assistance and health prediction. These models are particularly advantageous in terms of reduced memory usage and enhanced processing speed, aligning with the increasing demand for efficient and responsive AI applications in mobile environments. Additionally, SLMs are more suitable for practical applications, given their straightforward setup and minimal resource demands. On-device processing, which operates independently of continuous cloud connectivity, contributes to greater privacy protection by minimizing the risk of data exposure. However, while certain studies suggest that SLMs can address specific mental health tasks, significant gaps remain in their coverage of broader applications. Most existing research has focused on simpler tasks, with limited success when applied to more complex datasets. Moreover, there is limited research on the performance of these models in resource-constrained environments, especially in the context of on-device deployment. To address these challenges, we introduce Menta, a novel framework utilizing the Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) technique to manage multiple tasks efficiently and address data imbalance. This system is designed to function effectively across a range of mental health tasks, maintaining both efficiency and privacy on widely accessible devices.

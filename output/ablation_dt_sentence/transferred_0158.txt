Llama-4 Maverick outperforms traditional methods commonly employed in biological studies for integrating information. This approach, known as a Bayesian reliability-weighted combiner, serves as a normative baseline in computational models and is also a strategy humans use in various perceptual tasks. The superior performance of Llama-4 Maverick suggests the potential for incorporating additional strategies not typically seen in conventional methodologies. In Figure 7, we compare the multimodal responses of Llama-4 Maverick, which integrates all available sensory inputs, against its unimodal responses based on individual sensory modalities. Our findings indicate that a non-linear approach delivers superior performance relative to a linear baseline. Moreover, in settings characterized by stochastic perturbations, Bayesian inference provides a principled notion of optimality, serving as a natural benchmark for evaluating and improving language models whose performance remains suboptimal under noisy conditions. As shown in the right panel of Figure 8, models that exhibit greater accuracy also align more strongly with Bayesian methodologies. Despite the lower accuracy of Gemma 3 4B and Phi 4 Multimodal, both models demonstrated satisfactory performance in this domain. Section A.9 explores the performance of different tasks and models, revealing that language models exhibit a diverse range of behaviors when evaluated on tasks inspired by human perceptual processes. Notably, more accurate models, such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and Llama-4 Maverick, show stronger Bayesian factor evidence, particularly within the image modality (see Appendix A.11 for a comprehensive analysis). These models exhibit adaptive behaviors that align with Bayesian principles when confronted with variations such as noise or additional contextual information, utilizing prior knowledge even in the absence of explicit training for such scenarios. Bayesian models offer a compelling explanation for various perceptual phenomena and cognitive processes in the human brain, functioning without the need for explicit training (Petzschner et al., 2015; Knill & Pouget, 2004). However, it is important to emphasize that this analysis focuses on observable behavior, and elucidating the underlying mechanisms that give rise to this behavior remains an area for further investigation.

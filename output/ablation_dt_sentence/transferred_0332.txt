As [25] and [15] proposed structured moral reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MoralBench) to evaluate LLM responses against human moral intuitions, they demonstrated that while models exhibit partial moral sensitivity, their reasoning often lacks coherence in scenarios involving conflicting principles and contextual ambiguity. However, these benchmark evaluations remain limited in scalability and temporal scope, capturing model ethics at a single point in time and failing to reveal how reasoning consistency evolves with interaction and retraining. In parallel, several studies have explored the importance of explainability and value alignment in responsible AI development. For instance, Shitong et al. [9] introduced DeNEVIL, a debiasing and explainable model designed to integrate normative reasoning into LLM training, while Jiao et al. [16] emphasized the significance of cross-cultural moral benchmarking and trust-building, highlighting the sociotechnical dependencies that influence ethical AI systems. Despite their strengths in interpretability and trust calibration, these frameworks rely on predefined moral categories and manual labeling, which hinder their adaptability to evolving ethical contexts. While previous literature has contributed to fairness auditing, benchmark design, and value-alignment strategies, a significant gap remains: the absence of a dynamic, model-agnostic framework capable of conducting longitudinal moral evaluations. Although existing approaches assess ethical behavior at discrete points in time, they lack mechanisms for the autonomous generation, interpretation, and adaptation of morally salient scenarios. Furthermore, none of the current frameworks provide tools to measure ethical divergence or track moral consistency across models in real time. Building upon the foundation laid in Section 1, the proposed Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP) offers a continuous, dataset-free system designed to assess, interpret, and refine the moral stability of Large Language Models (LLMs). In line with the conceptual approach presented earlier, MoCoP focuses on evaluation and interpretation, rather than optimization or retraining, ensuring both conceptual and methodological coherence.

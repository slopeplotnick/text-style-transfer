As shown in Fig. 12, the experimental group employing the curriculum learning strategy exhibits a faster learning rate and a more stable progression in both reward values and model performance enhancement. However, as the model advances towards its objective during reinforcement learning (RL) training, its output becomes less random and more predictable, which diminishes the model's ability to explore new strategies and find more optimal solutions. This constraint, therefore, limits the potential for further performance improvements. To address this challenge, we implemented various strategies aimed at slowing down the increase in predictability, thus preserving the model's exploration capacity throughout the learning process. Fig. 13 presents a comparative evaluation of these methods. Among them, the Clip-higher method proves particularly effective, as it carefully regulates the rate at which the model's behavior becomes deterministic. This strategy strikes a favorable balance between exploration and exploitation, enhancing training stability while yielding significant performance improvements. In contrast, the On-policy method stabilizes the model's randomness, effectively preventing it from declining too sharply. While this enables greater exploration, it simultaneously hinders the model's ability to converge on an optimal solution, resulting in more modest performance gains. The Off-policy + Entropy Loss approach, on the other hand, leads to training instability, undermining its overall effectiveness. Initially, it slows the rate of deterministic behavior, but as the model’s output distribution becomes less stochastic, the penalty for residual randomness increases disproportionately, destabilizing training and leading to erratic behavior and convergence failure. Additionally, this method demands extensive testing to determine optimal settings, which increases operational costs. In conclusion, the Clip-higher method emerges as the optimal solution, as it effectively balances exploration with efficient learning by regulating the rate at which the model converges to predictable behavior.

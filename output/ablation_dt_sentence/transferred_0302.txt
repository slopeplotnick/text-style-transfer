The GPT-5 model exhibits a consistently high refusal rate, which does not decrease with increasing context padding. As a result, there is a slight reduction in performance on harmful tasks; however, the initially low harm score prevents any definitive conclusions. Due to the stability of the results and the high cost of conducting further experiments, we do not explore benign tasks with context padding exceeding 10K tokens. The type of context padding used can significantly impact performance. As shown in Figure 3, different padding types lead to varying results. For GPT-4.1-nano, non-relevant padding yields the highest performance, followed by relevant padding, random padding, and, lastly, multi-task padding, which performs the worst. Grok 4 Fast, however, does not exhibit a similar pattern due to its elevated refusal rate, with scores across varying padding types remaining close to those observed under the no-padding condition. Between 50K and 100K tokens, Grok 4 Fast tends to collapse, and the performance for different padding types converges to the same value. Furthermore, the position of padding also influences model performance. Figure 4 demonstrates that, for both GPT-4.1-nano and Grok 4 Fast, placing padding before the task description slows the rate of performance degradation compared to placing it after. This behavior likely occurs because, when padding precedes the task description, the task description and execution are closer together, allowing the attention mechanisms to function more effectively within the model's expected distribution. In contrast, when padding is placed after the instruction, the model must attend over a large gap between the instruction and execution, which likely contributes to the accelerated degradation in performance.

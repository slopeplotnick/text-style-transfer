Ensuring consistent theme color roles across a deck, fixing layout and format in related slides, and translating slide content while maintaining charts and structure are some of the tasks. To our knowledge, PPTArena is the first benchmark that (i) conceptualizes PPT editing as a structured, causal process informed by deck semantics, (ii) provides element-level ground truths, style objectives, and error-checking mechanisms to clarify accuracy, and (iii) uses dual instruction-following and visual validations that allow diverse configurations of edits while maintaining task coherence. In addition to this benchmark, we introduce PPTPilot, a structure-aware editing application for robust, detailed PPT modifications. PPTPilot breaks down each natural-language instruction into a sequence of semantic operations, deciding between high-level APIs like python-pptx and direct XML modification, and verifies the outcome against task-specific objectives. Two important design elements underpin its effectiveness: structure-aware planning, which involves analyzing slide masters, placeholders, shape trees, text, and visual data before editing, and deterministic execution, where XML-level modifications and strict schemas provide precise control over fonts, theme color allocations, positions, and master-level changes while automated tools manage repetitive global operations such as translation and style normalization. An iterative process of planning, editing, and verifying, along with XML validation and visual checks, enhances proficiency in visually demanding and extended revision tasks. We conducted a comprehensive evaluation of PPTArena, including advanced proprietary PPT agents like the ChatGPT Agent [42] and MiniMax Agent [35], extended-thinking visual-language models, and simplified variations of PPTPilot. Despite comprehensive guidance and tool accessibility, existing systems struggle to balance instruction fidelity with visual and layout quality on complex, multi-step tasks and frequently fail on cross-slide dependencies and master-style changes highlighted by our benchmark. However, PPTPilot demonstrates substantially superior performance, exceeding sophisticated tools and model scores by more than ten percentage points in scenarios involving complex edits while maintaining commendable efficacy in simpler tasks. Nonetheless, PPTPilot and other evaluated systems exhibit significant shortcomings in executing complex, visually dependent tasks, emphasizing the inherent difficulties posed by PPTArena and highlighting significant potential for advancements in future research.

Score Distillation Sampling. Pretrained diffusion models are not only capable of generating samples through a denoising process, but can also serve as optimization objectives via score distillation sampling (SDS) [Poole et al., 2022]. SDS has facilitated the adaptation of these models to a wide array of tasks, particularly in image and video generation. Building on the demonstrated success of SDS in vision, recent efforts have explored incorporating diffusion-based priors into reinforcement learning frameworks for control. A set of approaches replaces the discriminator in a GAIL-style setup with a diffusion model, leveraging its capacity to differentiate between real and generated actions. However, these methods continue to rely on adversarial training and typically require continuous updates to the diffusion model during policy optimization. Beyond adversarial formulations, alternative methods have sought to apply SDS-like techniques directly to policy learning. For example, some approaches guide policy training using pretrained image or video diffusion models conditioned on text prompts. While these strategies can yield behaviors aligned with textual instructions, the resulting motions often appear unnatural. The work most closely related to ours is SMILING [Wu et al., 2025a], which trains controllers using a score-matching objective closely related to variational score distillation (VSD) [Wang et al., 2023]. In contrast to SMILING, which necessitates training separate diffusion models for each task, our method learns a task-agnostic motion prior from unstructured motion data. This prior can then be reused to train diverse control policies by combining it with distinct task objectives in a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning framework. Additionally, we introduce several key design choices that enable the generation of high-quality, naturalistic motions across a broad spectrum of tasks.

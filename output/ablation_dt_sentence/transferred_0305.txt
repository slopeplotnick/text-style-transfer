SMP serves as a modular motion style reward model that guides policy training without requiring access to the original motion dataset. This flexibility enables the application of various adaptation techniques to the diffusion-based reward model, further shaping the motion prior. However, this exploitation can sometimes result in unnatural behaviors. Qualitative comparisons of the various methods are available in the supplementary video. Moreover, SMP facilitates the crafting of novel motion priors by manipulating the pretrained diffusion model's outputs, allowing the creation of new styles not present in the original dataset, without the need for retraining. As shown in Figure 6, two different styles can be combined by blending their style-conditioned predictions from the pretrained model to produce a novel "AeroPlane + HighKnees" prior. This composite prior can be used across multiple tasks. In fact, a single pretrained score-matching motion prior can be reused to train policies for different tasks, such as steering, target localization, and dodgeball, as demonstrated in Table 2. The score-matching motion prior is trained on a subset of the LaFAN1 dataset [Harvey et al. 2020], which consists of unstructured running behaviors. The resulting policies achieve high task performance while producing natural gaits. As illustrated in Figures 5(a) and 5(b), the character can dynamically select and transition between appropriate gaits from the prior, maintaining both task performance and motion naturalness. For example, the steering policy automatically initiates a backward jog when the character is facing away from the target and transitions to a forward jog once aligned. It also learns lateral gaits, such as side-stepping and cross-stepping, without the need for an explicit motion planner. In the Target Location task, the policy trained with SMP exhibits human-like strategies, such as automatically decelerating when required.

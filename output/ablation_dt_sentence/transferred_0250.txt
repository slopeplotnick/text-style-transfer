The implementation of concrete actions facilitates a smoother transition and is widely employed across various software development life cycles, particularly during SPLE migration. Several agile practices can be utilized to minimize the negative effects of migration adoption. For instance, a Sprint Zero [Boe10] offers an effective approach to enhance collaboration during the transition. This sprint consists of several steps: The first step involves establishing the working agreement, where the agile rituals necessary for supporting the migration are planned. Subsequently, having a clear and shared vision is essential to ensure that all team members understand the goals and benefits of the SPL. The next step is to develop personas, representing different user or developer profiles within the company. These personas are created before the project begins to foster empathy and identify the intended users. Following this, the backlog is constructed by systematically identifying all the required tasks. Each task in the backlog must then be estimated and incorporated into the project roadmap. Regular retrospectives are planned to give the team an opportunity to share their thoughts and improve collaboration. Some software engineering practices, such as regular refactoring sessions, contribute to improving project quality by promoting reuse and reducing technical debt. To further enhance code quality, it is important to schedule regular testing sessions to detect and address potential bugs. Additionally, planning acceptance testing sessions with end users ensures alignment and a shared understanding among all stakeholders. Constant monitoring of code execution is essential to maintain the efficiency and scalability of the derived applications. These actions are inherently general and can be applied across a wide range of software development contexts, whether SPL-based or not. However, the advantages of an SPL-based development approach are further strengthened, as the improved quality is integrated into the core of the code base. This benefits both current and future applications, providing a foundation for continuous improvement.

The authors of a seminal work on Empirical Software Engineering [BRZ05] highlight the lasting influence of Victor R. Basili, a pioneer in the field. Their study emphasizes the critical role of empirical research, particularly the use of real data and evidence, in advancing software engineering practices. Numerous studies in the literature have explored the challenges of adopting Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE) within industrial contexts, as well as its impact on agile development practices. These studies have proposed valuable strategies and procedures, offering insights on how to navigate the migration process. However, none have conducted an empirical assessment like ours, which specifically takes place after adoption but before implementation. In this paper, we present the first phase of a comprehensive process aimed at guiding a company in transitioning its codebase to a Software Product Line (SPL). The interviews conducted were designed with the company's specific context in mind, particularly considering the diverse roles of the project stakeholders. Our goal is to identify key directions for preparing the migration, focusing on research questions that address current best practices, the perceived benefits and risks, and the concerns expressed by stakeholders. By addressing the identified risks and fears, leveraging the perceived benefits, and promoting best practices, we aim to ensure a smooth transition. Our findings indicate that the company already exhibits beneficial practices and a culture of innovation that will support the project. The majority of stakeholders see the migration as advantageous, although the primary concerns remain the complexity of SPL and its ongoing maintenance.

In this section, we discuss the limitations of TaleFrame regarding interaction modes and advanced narrative capabilities, along with potential directions for future research. - Interaction and visualization. TaleFrame currently functions on desktop and laptop platforms, utilizing a combination of mouse and keyboard for precise control, which is essential for connecting entities to identify relationships and linking events to recognize sequences. User studies indicate a preference for drag-and-drop interactions; however, due to the smaller screen sizes of popular mobile devices like smartphones and tablets, this mode may be less effective. Although touch pens and fingers can substitute for the mouse, the complexity of extended interactions and relational input can result in accidental touches, thereby impairing the user experience on mobile devices. To minimize cognitive load, we have optimized information visualization through color coding, icons, and connecting lines, and implemented adaptive layout resizing to accommodate different screen sizes. In future work, we aim to explore and define a broader range of interaction styles, including touchscreens, keyboard input, and voice commands, to create a flexible framework that supports diverse user engagement across various devices. - Challenges in managing advanced narrative structures in story generation. TaleFrame currently struggles to handle complex event relationships beyond linear temporal sequencing, particularly causal relationships, flashbacks, and other narrative structures. Moreover, the relationships between entities complicate the connections between events, affecting the coherence and accuracy of the generation process. Although efforts have been made to improve the modeling of event dependencies, challenges persist in handling multi-layered event weaving, temporal reversals, and interactions among various entities. This often leads to difficulties in generating the correct event sequence or capturing causal chains, which can result in incoherent or logically unclear storylines. As the complexity of story plots and inter-entity relationships increases, the model faces greater challenges in understanding and generating event relationships. In future work, we will gradually focus on enhancing entity relationship modeling and optimizing strategies for generating non-linear narrative structures, ultimately improving the model's performance in complex story generation and providing users with a richer and more coherent narrative experience.

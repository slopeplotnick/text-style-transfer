We train the model using a procedure similar to the earlier SFT process, with the key difference being the inclusion of reasoning examples generated during the training phase. In addition to the tokens generated by the model, we also randomly substitute a subset of sentences with latent tokens, much like the procedure followed in the previous phase. This modification further enhances the model's ability to engage in efficient reasoning processes. During regular operations, the model performs standard tasks; however, when it encounters specific tags, it switches to a distinct reasoning mode, as shown in Figure 1. These tags serve to differentiate the two modes, although they do not function as specialized tokens within the model's vocabulary. Instead of explicitly training the model to use these tags, we prompt it to incorporate them. Moreover, the model predicts an explicit count, determining how many tokens to generate. Initially, the usage of these tags is minimal, but as training progresses, their frequency increases significantly.

Li et al. (2025) discuss the incorporation of external feedback mechanisms to enhance prediction accuracy during the testing phase. This approach utilizes external guidance to provide weak supervision, thereby refining predictions during inference. Similarly, Zuo et al. (2025) presents Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL), which performs multiple rollouts during inference, aggregates the resulting predictions through majority voting, and leverages the consensus as a reward signal to iteratively refine the policy. While effective, these methods are limited by their reliance on numerous iterations to obtain satisfactory results. In contrast, Wang et al. (2025) demonstrates that reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) can significantly improve learning performance with minimal examples, highlighting its potential for more efficient test-time adaptation. Building on this idea, our approach introduces a statistically grounded early termination mechanism that further improves efficiency by reducing unnecessary iterations. Policy optimization techniques, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017), are essential in reinforcement learning, particularly for stabilizing policy updates under KL constraints. These techniques have been extensively applied to reinforcement learning from human feedback. Additionally, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) enhances PPO by using group-normalized advantages, promoting more stable learning in environments with limited feedback. OptPO capitalizes on these advancements to improve computational efficiency while preserving the integrity of the underlying methodology. Prior work has shown that test-time adaptation facilitates better model generalization, utilizing strategies like uncertainty minimization, auxiliary supervision, and reinforcement-driven updates. However, these methods often require numerous attempts, making them inefficient. To address this, we introduce **OptPO**, a framework that reduces test-time computational overhead while maintaining competitive performance, thereby advancing and enhancing existing approaches to test-time adaptation.

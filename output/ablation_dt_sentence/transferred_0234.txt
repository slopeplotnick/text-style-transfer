A prominent instantiation of this capability is the AI Overviews feature within Google Search. Leveraging models from the Gemini family, this system functions as a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline, synthesizing abstractive summaries from multiple long-form documents. While this functionality enhances user efficiency by reducing the need to manually consolidate information from disparate sources, it also introduces concerns about the reliability of the synthesized content. Specifically, when an AI system integrates multiple documents into a unified summary, it is critical to assess whether it uniformly attends to all input sources or exhibits systematic preferential biases. Prior work has examined the presence and implications of ideological biases in LLMs. For instance, Santurkar et al. [2023] demonstrated that models such as GPT-4 and Claude frequently express liberal-leaning social values in response to survey-style prompts. In the context of news summarization, Vijay et al. [2024] proposed evaluation methods—including vocabulary divergence, stance polarization index, and algorithmic monoculture index—to assess ideological skew in LLM outputs. However, while much of the existing research focuses on content-level and ideological biases, relatively little attention has been given to whether LLMs exhibit structural biases in their treatment of multiple source documents. In multi-document summarization settings involving long concatenated inputs, it remains unclear whether LLMs differentially weight documents based on their position within the context window. Although effects such as primacy bias and the lost-in-the-middle phenomenon have been observed in other long-context tasks, their implications for summarization remain underexplored. To address this gap, our study investigates whether the sequential ordering of source articles influences their representational weight in LLM-generated summaries, using abortion news articles as a case study.

The system operates on Spring Boot, with the database hosted by PostgreSQL. Agronomists use Python or MATLAB to develop the simulators. The IT team relies on a central platform, named Cross, which serves as a large framework for building application code. Initially, the platform was designed to generate Proof of Concepts. Over time, however, its purpose has evolved, transforming it into a foundation for the development of end products. Currently, there are seven distinct variants in the family, each managed by three Product Owners. New variants are tailored to meet specific client needs, with a new version introduced approximately once per year. The levels of the Architecture Dimension, as outlined in the FEF framework [vdLSR07], define the methodologies for constructing the software and determining the technical realization of products within the software product line. At Level 1, architectures are confined to individual systems, with no visible emphasis on reuse. As the process progresses to Level 2, there is a shift toward the reuse of third-party infrastructure, including standardized software components such as middleware. However, domain-specific assets are not yet formally integrated. Moving to Level 3, domain commonality is captured and embedded within a unified software platform, providing a reference architecture for all applications. This platform, while central to implementation, does not yet support configuration variability. Advancing to Level 4, both domain commonality and variability are systematically captured, and a reference architecture is established to encompass the entire software product line. Domain assets are now designed to facilitate the creation of products, with particular attention to managing variability within the architecture. Finally, at Level 5, the reference architecture takes center stage, with application architectures diverging only slightly. Automated product derivation becomes feasible through the use of scripts, tools, and high-level languages, while application development is primarily focused on configuration activities within the reference architecture, enabling automated product configuration.

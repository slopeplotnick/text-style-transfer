File editing tools, such as create, str_replace, and insert, can be utilized to create or modify a test file to reproduce an issue (Localization), amend an application file to address a bug (Patching), or validate the functionality of a generated patch (Validation). For debugging purposes, testing can assist in either localizing bugs or validating the generated patch. Agents employ Python for executing newly developed reproduction or regression tests and utilize pytest for executing existing regression tests. Depending on the context, however, the same tool or command can be employed for different objectives and, consequently, assigned to different phases. In the GRAPHECTORY of SWE-agent Development, the presence of a self-back edge at edge 9 and a back edge at edge 10 highlights potential inefficiencies in the agent's reasoning processes or tool utilization. These inefficiencies could lead to the recurrence of failed edits or necessitate alterations to the localization strategy, as discussed in the first pitfall of Section 1. GRAPHECTORY enables the computation of process-centric metrics for analyzing agentic systems. Table 1 presents six metrics supported by the current implementation of GRAPHECTORY, along with their formal definitions illustrating the methodology for their derivation. These metrics indicate the degree of effort an agent exerts to resolve a task. While their values alone do not determine the efficacy of the effort, they can assess how well the effort aligns with the complexity of the problems being addressed or the success in resolving them. Temporal Edge Count (TEC) measures the total number of transitions between actions across different temporal intervals, where a higher TEC reflects a longer execution path to completion. For instance, Figure 2 shows a longer execution sequence for SWE-agent Dev (14) compared to SWE-agent DSK-V3 (8). In a similar vein to NC, TEC values positively correlate with the difficulty of the problem, thereby aligning the complexity of GRAPHENCY with the problem difficulty. Loop Count (LC) quantifies the frequency with which an agent opts to replicate a previously performed action. As explained later (§3.2), a higher LC may indicate a suboptimal strategy, causing the agent to become stuck with unsuccessful actions, as seen with the self-loop at edge 9 in Figure 2-a, or necessitate refining the reasoning and planning following a series of unproductive actions, such as the back edge at edge 10 in Figure 2-a (§3.3.2). Alternatively, the agent may need to re-execute actions to incrementally solve a complex problem. LC is akin to Cyclomatic Complexity in traditional software, measuring the degree of branching and the diversity of execution paths. In GRAPHENCY, branching reflects the alteration of pathways due to localized cognitive processes. Average Loop Length (ALL) measures the number of actions taken before repeating a singular operation. When an agent requires more time to realize that its actions are ineffective, it indicates weakness in reasoning or assimilating contextual information. Our findings suggest that agents investing in a comprehensive context during the localization process tend to have shorter loops, indicating an enhanced ability to swiftly identify inefficiencies and adjust strategies (§3.2.1 and §3.3.2).

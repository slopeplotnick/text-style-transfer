Furthermore, a limitation pertains to the range of tasks that LeechHijack is capable of executing. While our experiments demonstrated the effectiveness of LeechHijack on tasks within the MMLU benchmark, its performance on more complex tasks, such as those found in GAIA, remains unexamined. The current configuration of LeechHijack is primarily optimized for routine, dialogue-centric tasks, and its performance is expected to decline when applied to more complex assignments. Extending the attack to effectively handle complex command sequences presents a significant challenge and would require the development of more advanced attack configurations. Agent systems inherently depend on computationally expensive LLMs; therefore, an attacker can exploit LeechHijack to gain benefits without necessarily engaging it for executing complex tasks. They can leverage the compromised resources for simpler tasks, such as information aggregation or the generation of templated content, utilizing the model's extensive knowledge base. In conclusion, LeechHijack represents a persistent and tangible threat to the MCP and agent systems, regardless of the complexity inherent in the hijacked task.

In this section, our objective is to develop a comprehensive understanding of the factors that drive effective e-commerce search and, on this basis, to identify the key modeling tools necessary to enhance its performance. Functionally, e-commerce search can be viewed as the task of aligning user queries with relevant product items. Relevance modeling refines this alignment by introducing a structured framework to evaluate the degree of match between a query and a product, accounting for both objective elements—such as attributes and constraints—and subjective factors, including personal opinions. To support this framework, we decompose user search intent into 18 distinct attribute dimensions, encompassing aspects such as type, brand, style, and other relevant categories. For each query-item pair, we assess how well the item satisfies the explicit or implicit demands encoded in the query, and use these attribute-level judgments to ground and explain the relevance assessments. This schema provides a consistent lens through which relevance decisions can be interpreted and analyzed. To systematically quantify relevance in the e-commerce search setting, we structure the evaluation around three core components: product understanding, which defines the attribute space; search understanding, which interprets user intent into retrieval signals; and the subsequent relevance judgment. In relevance assessment, we focus on two primary dimensions of product understanding: the intrinsic properties of item attributes and their presentation modality. From the perspective of attribute properties, we distinguish between inherent and derived attributes. Inherent attributes refer to objectively present, readily observable characteristics such as category, brand, model, and price. These attributes are typically well-defined, standardized, and directly aligned with user queries, making them foundational to the search process and often amenable to binary relevance decisions. In contrast, derived attributes are inferred from inherent ones and typically relate to product goals or user experiences. They require deeper reasoning and semantic understanding, as they are not always directly observable. Consequently, evaluating relevance based on derived attributes introduces additional complexity, particularly because different industries often employ diverse and specialized terminologies to describe them.

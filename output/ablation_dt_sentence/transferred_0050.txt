Our approach was evaluated against other benchmarks using judges, underscoring its distinct advantages in assessment. Our evaluation methodology marks a significant advancement over prior approaches. Earlier methods, such as PPTC-R, predominantly focus on API-level "diffs." While this approach has its merits, it is brittle and fails to capture critical semantic errors, such as instances where an agent reproduces the correct content but places it on the wrong slide. In contrast, our IF Judge, leveraging structured summaries, is specifically designed to detect such logical inconsistencies. Moreover, other benchmarks like T2US also employ visual language models (VLMs) as judges; however, their reliance on prompts without a well-defined rubric leads to noisy and inconsistent evaluations. PPTArena addresses this issue by providing a robust and reproducible framework for scoring. In conclusion, our rubric-guided dual-judge framework is crucial for evaluating the complex, multi-step reasoning required for PowerPoint editing tasks and for identifying nuanced failure modes that simpler benchmarks would miss. We also introduce PPTPilot, a tool for presentation editing, which, despite its simple design, demonstrates impressive performance, even surpassing commercial tools like OpenAI's ChatGPT Agent (discussed in Section 5.1). Our design is grounded in two key insights: first, the primary challenge in this domain is not just task delineation but ensuring precise and reliable execution. PowerPoint files are encoded in the fragile Office Open XML (OOXML) format, which is highly sensitive to malformed or low-quality outputs from VLMs. As such, it requires carefully structured formats and contexts for reliable editing. Second, we observed that no single editing method, such as relying solely on XML, is sufficient. A robust tool must intelligently select the most suitable approach for each specific task. To that end, PPTPilot's dual-path architecture enables it to handle editing requests through either programmatic tools or direct XML manipulation.

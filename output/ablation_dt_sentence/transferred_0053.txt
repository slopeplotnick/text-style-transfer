In this section, we establish the framework employed throughout this study. We define the datasets, solvers, and verifiers, introduce the metrics used to evaluate solver and verifier behaviors, and specify the verification settings applied in our empirical analysis. It is important to note that verifier gain serves as an asymptotic metric: it reflects the limit of infinite sampling and thus acts as a boundary on the improvement achievable through verifier-based rejection sampling. Throughout the study, we use verifier gain to compare differences in verifier behavior across models. We organize models into families (e.g., Llama3, Qwen2.5), where each family consists of related models that differ in scale. Given that base and post-trained models often exhibit significantly distinct behaviors, we treat them as separate families. For example, the base model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-70B belongs to the Llama3-Base family, while the post-trained model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct belongs to the Llama3 family. We categorize each solver-verifier pair from our pool of models into one of three verification settings. Each solver-verifier pair is treated as an instance of one of the following: 1. **Self-Verification**: The solver and verifier are the same model, meaning the model verifies its own solutions. For instance, when a 70B Llama3 model serves as both the solver and verifier, the verification metric (e.g., accuracy, FPR) is computed based on this single pairing. 2. **Intra-Family Verification**: The verifier evaluates solutions produced by models within the same family. For example, a 70B Llama3 verifier may assess outputs from 8B or 13B Llama3 solvers. The reported metric is averaged over all such within-family solvers, excluding the self-verification case. 3. **Cross-Family Verification**: The verifier assesses solutions generated by models from different families. For example, a base Llama3 verifier may evaluate outputs from Qwen3 or from a post-trained Llama3 model. The reported metric is averaged over all such cross-family solvers.

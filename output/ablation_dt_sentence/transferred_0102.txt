Attempts to address this gap by constructing static benchmarks of LLM outputs, such as HELM [60], remain limited. These benchmarks cannot evaluate new or out-of-distribution models, nor capture dynamic generative signals. Consequently, this incompatibility has led to the development of two parallel research tracks, which has resulted in redundant efforts and left fundamental questions unresolved. It remains unclear whether one paradigm is inherently superior, how their respective strengths differ, or whether they can be effectively combined. Moreover, their separation hinders the potential to harness their complementary strengths: HD's internal model signals could enhance FV's evidence-based reasoning, while FV's contextual retrieval could ground HD's uncertainty signals. To this end, we take a decisive step toward addressing this divide by presenting the first large-scale, systematic empirical study that directly compares Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification within a unified experimental framework. Rather than simply providing a benchmark, our work offers a comprehensive investigation into the intricate relationship between these two paradigms. This approach enables us to explore three key research questions (RQs) that have remained unaddressed due to the historical separation of these fields: (1) Overall Performance (RQ1): How do HD and FV methods differ in performance when detecting factual errors within the same LLM outputs? (2) Complementary Strengths (RQ2): Do HD and FV capture distinct aspects of factual inaccuracy, demonstrating complementary strengths, or do they overlap, resulting in failures on similar errors? (3) Hybrid Performance (RQ3): Can signals from both paradigms be integrated effectively, and can a hybrid system outperform either approach in isolation? This comparison was once deemed infeasible due to the fundamental incompatibility between the two paradigms. However, we make this comparison possible by introducing UniFact, a unified evaluation framework that overcomes the historical divide in the field. Unlike static benchmarks that rely on fixed LLM outputs, UniFact employs a dynamic process: it prompts any LLM to generate responses to factual questions in real time. The framework then uses an automated labeling mechanism to evaluate the accuracy of these responses by comparing them to ground-truth answers and their associated pre-labeled reference knowledge. This dynamic process is crucial, as it provides both the outputs needed for FV and the internal model signals required for HD. This design allows for a direct, controlled comparison of both approaches on the same generated content.

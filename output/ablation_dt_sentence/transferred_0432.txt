Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has significantly improved the performance of large language models on knowledge-intensive tasks. However, most existing systems focus on generic or news-based domains, with limited efficacy in high-stakes applications such as aiding agricultural sectors in adapting to climate variations. Recent domain-specific QA studies have sought to integrate document retrieval and climate data, yet they persist in using basic methods that limit region-specific and verifiable answers. While geography-aware question answering has been explored in various fields, these methods are not specifically tailored to climate reasoning. Moreover, contemporary research often treats climate data and academic literature as distinct entities, lacking a comprehensive approach. CAIRNS, therefore, comprises four integral stages: initially, it performs location-weighted retrieval of climatic information from the Elsevier database and a climate data API; followed by answer generation using the ScholarGuide Prompt; next, a rigorous verification of the generated answers; and finally, a hybrid evaluation for comprehensive assessment. To retrieve relevant documents, we have designed a hybrid retriever that combines sparse lexical matching and dense semantic retrieval through a weighted scoring system. However, hybrid retrieval alone is insufficient, as locational cues critically influence relevance in climate adaptation QA. Therefore, we enhance the base retrieval score with a locational similarity metric to ensure both semantic and geographical relevance. CAIRNS then engages in an iterative process to retrieve evidence, generate responses, and verify their adequacy. In cases where information is lacking, additional queries are generated, re-entering the retrieval loop until a satisfactory response is achieved. Central to CAIRNS's answer generation is the ScholarGuide Prompt, essential for balancing readability with factual accuracy. In the context of climate-related inquiries, this prompt, acting as an academic guide, directs the language model to structure responses appropriately, apply scientific reasoning, and offer precise recommendations.

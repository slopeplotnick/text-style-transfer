The goal is to create a system that facilitates training policies to mimic natural movement patterns, thus eliminating the need for the original dataset. This system should be applicable across various tasks and policies without requiring retraining. To achieve these criteria, we propose Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), a method for constructing reusable, modular motion priors that can be utilized to train control policies exhibiting naturalistic behaviors across diverse tasks. Initially, we train a model to understand motion data independent of specific task or policy constraints. Once trained, this diffusion model is kept static and repurposed as a prior using score distillation sampling (SDS), offering a robust similarity measure between simulated and original dataset motions. The incorporation of the learned SMP as a reward mechanism enables the training of new policies to perform various tasks with life-like behaviors. The central contribution of this work is a method for constructing modular and reusable motion priors for physics-based character animation by integrating reinforcement learning with score distillation. Our framework produces high-quality motions comparable to state-of-the-art methods while substantially enhancing modularity and reusability. Moreover, SMP allows our system to entirely exclude the reference dataset during policy training. We demonstrate that a motion prior can be developed from a model trained on extensive motion datasets without focusing on any specific task. This general-purpose motion prior can then be transformed into style-specific priors through prompting and guidance. Furthermore, we demonstrate that priors for different styles can be combined to generate new behavioral styles absent in the original dataset.

Curriculum learning (CL), first introduced by Bengio et al. (2009), is a paradigm where models are trained on samples or tasks of increasing complexity, starting from simpler instances and advancing to more challenging ones. This approach has been widely adopted in the development of large language models, with recent methods like Speed-RL (Zhang et al., 2025) and Kim and Lee (2024) continuing to employ this strategy by ordering tasks from easy to hard based on externally defined difficulty levels. Similarly, LBS3 (Luo et al., 2024) organizes training with progressively harder proxy queries. However, these approaches are fundamentally misaligned with effective curriculum design, as they rely on static difficulty metrics that do not account for the model's evolving capabilities. Truly effective curriculum learning should be competence-aware, adapting dynamically to the model’s progress. In contrast, CAPO leverages advantage estimates as an intrinsic, competence-aware signal, initiating with positive feedback to stabilize early learning before gradually incorporating negative feedback to improve generalization. Unlike traditional methods that depend on fixed difficulty levels, CAPO's adaptive approach aligns the training process with the model's developing competence, ensuring stability during initial learning stages and greater adaptability in later stages. This two-phase approach mirrors human cognitive development by beginning with positive-only examples and progressively integrating negative signals. As a result, CAPO avoids the instability caused by prematurely combining positive and negative feedback. Extensive evaluations demonstrate CAPO's consistent superiority over alternative approaches, including GRPO, across a range of tasks and model scales. Notably, CAPO exhibits exceptional cross-domain generalization, from mathematical reasoning to multimodal reasoning tasks. By focusing on the model's developmental progress rather than relying solely on predefined difficulty levels, CAPO paves the way for more adaptive and cognitively-inspired learning algorithms.

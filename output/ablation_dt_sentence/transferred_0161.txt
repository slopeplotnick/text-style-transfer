In recent years, the technology behind Large Language Models (LLMs) has advanced significantly and has been widely applied across various domains, including intelligent interaction and scientific research facilitation. In the healthcare field, LLMs have demonstrated notable potential in assisting disease diagnosis and improving the efficiency of diagnostic and therapeutic workflows. In Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), the core clinical principle is "Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment" (SDT), which encompasses both syndrome differentiation and treatment formulation. This approach requires clinicians to integrate diverse clinical information to formulate diagnostic conclusions and develop individualized treatment strategies, often adapting interventions for the same condition based on specific patient characteristics. Although several TCM-oriented LLMs—such as Zhongjing and HuatuoGPT—have been developed, their practical implementation in clinical settings remains challenging due to the inherently complex and individualized nature of TCM diagnosis and treatment. Critically, the field lacks comprehensive evaluation benchmarks that cover the entire clinical decision-making process, as well as specialized tools to assess the reasoning capabilities of these models. Therefore, there is an urgent need within both academic and industry communities for a rigorously designed benchmark dataset that can objectively and comprehensively evaluate the clinical applicability and performance of TCM-focused LLMs. Such a dataset would support performance assessment, iterative model optimization, and eventual integration into real-world clinical practice. However, the individualized and holistic nature of SDT in TCM presents substantial obstacles to benchmark construction. For example, patients with the same condition may exhibit different syndromes due to factors such as individual constitution, disease progression, or lifestyle, greatly complicating the standardization of evaluation criteria. Moreover, TCM diagnosis is a holistic and integrative reasoning process that synthesizes multiple diagnostic methods to collect information, analyze etiological factors, and formulate appropriate treatments. As such, the benchmark must encompass a broad range of diagnostic and therapeutic dimensions to reflect this complexity. Furthermore, the frequent co-occurrence of multiple syndromes in a single patient, combined with the diversity of diagnostic techniques, introduces additional subjectivity into the evaluation process. In summary, these factors collectively make the construction of a comprehensive benchmark for assessing the clinical application capabilities of TCM-specific LLMs an exceptionally challenging endeavor.

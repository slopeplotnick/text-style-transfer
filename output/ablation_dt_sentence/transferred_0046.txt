We evaluate WorldMM with five long video question-answering benchmarks, ranging from hour- to week-long durations. Our method consistently outperforms strong baselines, including models specifically developed for long-video understanding and memory-augmented architectures. Comprehensive evaluations further demonstrate the effectiveness of our multi-memory, multiscale design. Specifically, episodic memory facilitates reasoning over events occurring across various timescales, visual memory enhances performance on object- and action-related queries, while semantic memory supports long-term contextual reasoning. When all memories are adaptively integrated, the model achieves optimal overall performance. These results underscore that our multimodal memory system offers a promising direction for advancing long video comprehension. While existing models perform well on short videos, recent research increasingly focuses on the challenges posed by significantly longer content. Proprietary models such as GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 have advanced to understanding videos of minute- to hour-level durations by leveraging extended context lengths. However, these models come with substantial computational costs, and uniformly sampling every frame is often suboptimal for queries centered on specific events. To address these limitations, several strategies have been explored. Visual token compression improves efficiency by reducing token counts, but often loses fine-grained details, limiting the ability to capture rare or subtle events. Key frame selection reduces redundancy by retaining informative frames, yet struggles to identify relevant content in extremely long sequences, potentially missing important moments. More recent efforts involving reasoning-centric training and inference strategies have improved long-range temporal grounding, but their scalability remains limited for videos exceeding ten hours. Meanwhile, emerging benchmarks are pushing video understanding toward continuous recordings spanning days or weeks. In these scenarios, existing methods falter due to the immense scale of frames and the complexity of long-term temporal dependencies. This highlights the urgent need for more efficient, context-aware, and scalable approaches to manage ultra-long video content.

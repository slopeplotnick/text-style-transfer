Despite significant advancements in memory technologies, the application of these systems to stock price prediction remains fraught with challenges. Specifically, existing systems struggle to achieve fine-grained modeling of event sequences, lack effective mechanisms for matching historical scenario patterns, and fail to capture the dynamic evolution of financial markets. These limitations highlight the urgent need for memory architectures tailored to the financial domain. This chapter reviews the relevant literature along two key dimensions: first, the shift from traditional pattern-matching methods to memory-augmented frameworks, and second, the increasing prominence of event-driven predictive paradigms. Despite notable progress in the field, a critical gap persists in integrating structured event knowledge with traceable memory systems—a gap that forms the central focus of this work. In the domain of stock price prediction, earlier approaches primarily emphasized identifying historical patterns that resembled current market conditions to forecast future trends. This concept parallels the memory mechanisms in LLMs, where the goal is to retain and retrieve prior experiences for informed decision-making. Initially, statistical methods were employed to detect numerical patterns; however, these methods proved inadequate for processing more complex market dynamics. With the advent of deep learning, models such as LSTM and Transformer were introduced to automatically extract temporal features. Transformer-based models, in particular, excelled at modeling long-range dependencies via self-attention mechanisms, making significant contributions to the field. The FinSeer framework further advanced the approach by incorporating LLMs to retrieve historically similar data, enabling a more comprehensive analysis of features.

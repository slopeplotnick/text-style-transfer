A growing challenge for OLLMs stems from the diversity and complexity of input modalities, which fundamentally reshape how safety risks arise and how they must be detected. This variety introduces heterogeneous risk patterns that manifest differently, encompassing implicit bias in text, visual misinformation, or privacy leakage and harmful noise in audio. Beyond these unimodal risks, the complexity of cross-modal interactions further exacerbates the challenge of identifying hidden risks. For instance, the phrase "I want to fly" paired with an image of a person standing on a rooftop, while each element is harmless on its own, together they may convey a potentially unsafe, suicidal intent. Similarly, when a video of police patrolling is combined with a textual query like "How to overcome obstacles," the cross-modal semantics reveal an implicitly criminal motive that is not evident from either modality alone. Therefore, safety risks for OLLMs become substantially more challenging, necessitating modality-specific understanding and stronger cross-modal comprehension. However, applying safety alignment directly to the base OLLMs might be inadequate, as it often requires substantially more development time, additional computational resources, and can lead to degradation in core reasoning capabilities. Moreover, even after extensive investment, aligned models demand prohibitively expensive retraining to address issues and remain vulnerable to low-cost adversarial attacks. Guardrail models offer a decentralized and more flexible alternative; however, current guardrail research mainly focuses on uni-modal settings or simple modality combinations, such as image-text pairs, leaving the omni-modal scenario largely unexplored. Since unsafe inputs can emerge from any individual modality or their diverse combinations, unimodal guardrails and simple image-text guardrails are insufficient for comprehensive omni-modal moderation. Furthermore, many existing guardrails construe safeguarding as a binary classification task, which limits their effectiveness.

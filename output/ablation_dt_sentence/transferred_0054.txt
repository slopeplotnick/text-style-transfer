Udio is capable of converting natural-language prompts into fully developed music tracks across various genres, incorporating lyrics in multiple languages (Choi et al., 2025). Research in computational creativity and applied machine learning has explored the perception of AI-generated music, particularly focusing on the creative value of the output produced by these models through subjective listening tests. In these studies, listeners evaluate samples generated by proposed music models and compare them with various baseline models or human-composed music. Frequent evaluation criteria include overall quality, creativity, novelty, as well as specific musical elements, such as melody, harmony, and rhythm. Another critical aspect is the assessment of naturalness or human-like qualities, which is typically measured via rating scales or a musical Turing Test. In this test, listeners are asked to determine whether a piece was created by a human or an AI system, under conditions where they may be informed, uninformed, or deliberately misled about the source of the music. Within specific tasks, such as the generation of Bach chorales, even early models like Deepbach (Hadjeres et al., 2017) produced results that were virtually indistinguishable from authentic compositions. In addition, to evaluate the emotional dimension of AI-generated music, listeners are tasked with identifying the intended affect by categorizing it using discrete emotion labels such as "happy" or "sad," or by employing dimensional models of emotion based on valence and arousal. As AI-generated music continues to evolve, studies have also examined whether listeners' evaluations are influenced by their beliefs or knowledge regarding its origin. However, empirical findings remain inconsistent: Early investigations manipulating information about composer identity found no substantial bias in participant evaluations of live performances featuring both human- and computer-generated string quartet compositions. Similarly, Zlatkov et al. (2023) did not observe a systematic bias against AI-generated music using pop music samples as stimuli. Research in consumer marketing by Moura and Maw (2021) similarly found no discernible preference for music based on its authorship, whether classical or pop.

Researchers have explored various approaches to enhance the reasoning capabilities of models and verify the correctness of individual steps. For instance, recent studies have investigated decomposing reasoning into fine-grained steps, systematically verifying each intermediate inference, and employing reward models to enhance the training of mathematical reasoning capabilities. One study, in particular, examines the improvement in performance resulting from the utilization of an outcome-level verifier, referred to as the GV-Gap, especially when the solver and verifier are implemented as the same model. However, their analysis is confined to base models and does not extend to models subjected to subsequent post-training. Moreover, recent research has also explored the scaling of computational resources during the testing phase with verifiers. For example, some studies have examined the use of random sampling in conjunction with self-checking, while others have investigated the combination of parallel sampling with error correction. Additionally, another group has compared the Self-Consistency method (Wang et al., 2023) with the application of a generative verifier. Furthermore, research has focused on identifying optimal strategies for leveraging computational resources in the context of test-time verification. Nevertheless, verifiers are not infallible; they may yield false positives by approving incorrect answers, dismiss valid reasoning paths, or select suboptimal solutions (Stroebl et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025). Another line of inquiry examines mechanisms through which models can autonomously refine their behavior and systematically evaluate the quality of their own outputs. While some scholars remain critical of this notion, others have put forward theoretical frameworks and empirical evidence suggesting that model performance may be enhanced through the refinement of their underlying understanding. Prior work examining comparatively small models argues that their self-improvement is contingent on coupling them with a more capable external verifier to compensate for limited inherent verification capacity. Practical approaches to self-improvement involve leveraging natural language feedback (Madaan et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2023) or explicitly training models to self-correct their errors (Welleck et al., 2023). Alternative methods incorporate the use of tools, such as code interpreters, to iteratively refine responses, enhancing the solution incrementally at each stage.

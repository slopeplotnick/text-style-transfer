In this work, we introduce Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), a reusable and modular tool designed for physics-based character animation using score-matching. Once trained, the learned motion prior can be reused to train control policies across diverse tasks, guiding the policies to exhibit natural behaviors that align with the reference distribution. Notably, our priors can be effectively utilized without the need to retain the original motion dataset. Furthermore, test-time diffusion techniques, such as classifier-free guidance, can be applied to modify the base motion prior and generate novel stylistic variations, enabling agents to perform tasks in specific styles. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our method across a broad range of scenarios, from single-character behaviors to complex human-object interactions. SMPs can be constructed from a wide variety of motion datasets, ranging from just a few seconds of motion clips to large-scale datasets containing up to 20 hours of motion data. In our experiments, we show that reinforcement learning with score distillation sampling (SDS) objectives can generate motions of comparable quality to those produced by adversarial imitation learning, without requiring continuous updates to the prior during policy training. Moreover, SMP demonstrates superior sample efficiency over adversarial priors in most scenarios, likely due to the more stable stationary reward function it provides compared to adversarial reward models. However, as is common with many mode-seeking objectives, policies trained with SMP are prone to mode-collapse, leading to policies that reproduce only a limited subset of the behaviors present in the original dataset. While our work primarily addresses humanoid motion control, we believe SMP holds promise for applications beyond this domain, offering potential solutions to broader control problems. We hope that this work inspires new approaches for constructing general motion priors, enabling the development of more versatile controllers for character animation and robotics that extend beyond motion tracking.

The execution platform may introduce noise during benchmark execution due to background processes and other confounding factors. To mitigate these issues, we disabled the platform’s wireless networking hardware and collected multiple samples at various time intervals, helping to filter out such external noise. The statistical model’s definition, which forms the core of our research, can be subject to inaccuracies or misinterpretations. We base several of our assumptions on previous research and the expected distribution of the data, actions which help reduce potential biases within the model. However, as with any model, the choice of parameters, their distribution, and the specific settings are open to debate. Our model choices are primarily informed by the Java Virtual Machine (JVM) specifications and the definitions inherent in programming languages, particularly with regard to Java bytecode. The decision to use the Gaussian distribution is supported both ontologically and epistemologically. Ontologically, the Gaussian distribution is prevalent in modeling natural phenomena, aligning with the central limit theorem, which posits that the sum of many independent random variables tends toward a normal distribution. Epistemologically, we acknowledge that the measurements possess a finite mean and variance, reflecting our inherent uncertainty about their true values. Other potential threats largely relate to the execution platform and the JVM. The memory state before executing each bytecode pattern could influence the results, so we invoke the garbage collector before each test cycle. To further mitigate the effects of paging, caching, and interference from concurrent processes, we test each bytecode pattern ten times, using a randomized execution order across trials.

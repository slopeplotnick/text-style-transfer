We propose SchED (Schedule-based Early Decoding), a training-free early-exit mechanism for diffusion language models (dLLMs) that enables inference-time termination without additional optimization or architectural modification. SchED thresholds a span-level logit-margin signal against a smooth, progress-dependent schedule—such as linear or cosine—supporting architecture-agnostic early stopping without retraining. By analyzing predictive confidence over the generation process, we find that instruction tuning accelerates the stabilization of model predictions, which aligns with the schedule and facilitates quality-preserving early exits. Mechanistically, SchED imposes a dynamic confidence threshold that evolves across decoding steps, building on the denoising trajectory of vanilla dLLMs to unlock parallelism and bidirectional generation for greater efficiency. The source code is publicly available. Diffusion language models have recently emerged as promising alternatives to autoregressive (AR) models, trainable either from scratch or through AR checkpoint adaptation. Among them, LLaDA introduces directional masking by training a bidirectional transformer to mask forward and reconstruct backward, demonstrating strong results across pretraining and supervised fine-tuning. Other approaches reframe AR models into diffusion form to ensure fair, scalable comparisons across sizes and benchmarks. Concurrently, models like Dream-7B refine architectural recipes and offer both base and instruction-tuned variants. New configurations such as LLaDA-MoE incorporate sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), activating only a fraction of components while maintaining strong performance. The capacity of dLLMs is also being extended to long-context use cases; UltraLLaDA applies RoPE scaling to process sequences up to 128K tokens. Meanwhile, Seed Diffusion targets throughput efficiency in code generation tasks by accelerating inference. Studies on diffusion scaling laws reveal growth trends similar to those of AR models. Finally, comprehensive surveys highlight the unique strengths of dLLMs—including parallel generation, infilling, and global context handling—while also outlining open questions that remain active areas of investigation.

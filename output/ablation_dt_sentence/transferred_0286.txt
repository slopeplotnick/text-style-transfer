In recent studies, there has been a concerted effort to identify both the strengths and weaknesses of SWE agents. For instance, performance analyses of these agents on multi-file issues reveal significant challenges when dealing with complex, multi-file scenarios. Furthermore, researchers have called for richer evaluation paradigms that account for factors such as efficiency, reliability, and reasoning quality. In line with this, several studies have delved into the causes of failure in SWE-agents. These studies have pinpointed issues such as incorrect fault localization, difficulties in managing complex code dependencies, and challenges in maintaining context across multiple files. Notably, one research group developed TRAIL (Trace Reasoning and Agentic Issue Localization), a comprehensive taxonomy of errors supported by a benchmark consisting of 148 human-annotated traces. Similarly, another group introduced MAST (Multi-Agent System Failure Taxonomy), a framework for classifying failures within multi-agent systems. Their analysis, which examined 150 traces across five multi-agent systems and five benchmarks, required human annotations to complete the taxonomy and analysis. Moreover, a separate study constructed a flow graph in which nodes represent agent actions, including project comprehension, context management, and patching and testing activities. However, this analysis was performed manually on a limited subset of data, focusing specifically on bug localization for just 20 out of 500 issues. Recently, additional studies have compiled a comprehensive taxonomy of failure modes in LLM tools during SWE tasks, categorizing them into three primary phases, nine main categories, and twenty-five detailed subcategories. This taxonomy was employed to uncover the underlying reasons for tool malfunctions, such as agents becoming trapped in unproductive iterative loops. Nonetheless, like the previous studies, their analysis was manual, focusing on 150 out of 500 issues.

In this section, we describe how each evaluation metric is computed and what its outputs represent, covering lexical overlap, semantic similarity, and factual consistency. ROUGE-L measures the longest common subsequence (LCS) between a model-generated summary and a reference summary. This metric captures structural similarity at the sentence level and is therefore less sensitive to variations in word order. Typically, ROUGE-L is used as a reference-based metric, comparing the output of a system to a human-written summary. In our study, however, we are not concerned with the absolute ROUGE-L score; rather, we examine relative differences across input-order conditions to test for order effects. Because ROUGE-L primarily reflects lexical overlap, we complement it with BERTScore and SummaC to evaluate semantic similarity and factual consistency, respectively. To move beyond mere lexical matching, we utilize contextualized embeddings to capture semantic nuance. BERTScore computes token-level similarity using contextual embeddings from a pre-trained encoder, aligning each token in the hypothesis with the most similar token in the reference. Precision, recall, and F1 scores are then derived from these alignments. Since contextual embeddings account for synonymy and paraphrastic variation, BERTScore is well-suited for assessing semantic similarity beyond exact lexical matches. Consistent with our study design, we focus on relative differences in BERTScore across input-order conditions, rather than the absolute similarity values, which may be lower due to the abstractive nature of the summaries. SummaC assesses factual consistency by determining whether the generated summary is entailed by the original documents. It comes in two variants: SummaC-ZS, which is zero-shot and parameter-free, and SummaC-Conv, a trained convolutional model. We employ SummaC-Conv due to its superior performance. However, the underlying RoBERTa-based model imposes limitations on the maximum volume of input it can process.

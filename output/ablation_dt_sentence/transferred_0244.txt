Reference state initialization (RSI), which initializes the character to a random state sampled from a reference motion dataset, has been shown to significantly enhance exploration in motion imitation methods. However, this approach necessitates continued access to the motion dataset for sampling initial states throughout policy training. To eliminate this dependency, SMP employs a generative model to synthesize initial states during policy learning. Specifically, when using SMP to train a policy, initial states are generated by sampling from a pretrained diffusion model that serves as the motion prior. SMP thus serves a dual role in our framework—as both a reward function and a mechanism for initializing new training phases. This generative state initialization (GSI) approach obviates the need to retain the original dataset post-training by leveraging the diffusion model’s ability to produce diverse and high-quality initial states. Our framework adopts a pretrained diffusion model as a reusable, modular motion prior for imitation learning. This task-agnostic motion model also functions as a reward signal that evaluates the naturalness of the agent’s movements within a reinforcement learning framework. Consequently, the resulting policies can perform a wide range of tasks while exhibiting behavior that closely resembles that found in the reference dataset. Designing an effective motion representation is essential not only for training the diffusion model to capture the dataset distribution but also for utilizing it as a motion prior in policy training. The representation should provide sufficient information to reconstruct motion and remain easily extractable from both kinematic motion clips and simulator state observations.

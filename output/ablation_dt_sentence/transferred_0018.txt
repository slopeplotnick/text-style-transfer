The final step of the Phase Flow Analyses, as illustrated in Algorithm 3, focuses on extracting shared strategic subsequences employed by a given agent across multiple tasks. This mechanism operates by analyzing the actions undertaken by the agent while addressing different problems. Specifically, Algorithm 3 first processes a list of LANGUTORY instances to determine their corresponding phase transition sequences. It then employs the Generalized Sequential Pattern (GSP) algorithm to identify the longest common subsequences within these phase transitions. Next, the algorithm identifies the inception point of the shared pattern within each LANGUTORY and determines the corresponding phase lengths through run-length encoding. To account for variations in phase lengths, Algorithm 3 selects the minimum run-length across all LANGUTORY instances and adjusts the shared strategy pattern accordingly. Phase Flow Analysis, particularly Shared Strategy Analysis, proves effective in identifying common underlying patterns, including those that are not immediately apparent. In addition, Pattern Detection Analysis further contributes by enabling the identification of known patterns within the GRAPHECTORY or LANGUTORY structures. The primary application of pattern detection lies in identifying known failure modes or potential inefficiencies within GRAPHECTORY. As elaborated in subsequent sections, this facilitates a systematic and scalable analysis of programming agents' failure modes. Users can inspect a small subset of failure cases, identify recurring (anti-)patterns through manual analysis, and subsequently perform automated searches for these patterns across additional execution traces. In this way, GRAPHECTORY and LANGUTORY serve as invaluable tools in the systematic analysis of programming systems, enabling the exploration of research questions related to the functionality and behavior of agents within these systems.

Since all linguistic knowledge is explicitly represented and can be consulted by researchers with respect to both agents, this approach offers a valuable complement to systems that acquire linguistic competence without relying on explicit representations, such as large language models. The MODOMA framework employs unsupervised learning, as the input data to the language acquisition device are unannotated, and this module lacks access to the internal processing and parsing mechanisms of the mother agent. Moreover, the daughter agent can leverage its previously acquired grammatical knowledge to facilitate the acquisition of additional linguistic structures. This novel acquisition technique, introduced for MODOMA, is referred to as internal annotation, a form of self-supervised learning (cf. Balestriero et al. 2023, Brown et al. 2020, Devlin et al. 2019, Gui et al. 2024, Lan et al. 2020, Orhan et al. 2020, Yarowsky 1995). As far as we know, the integration of the features and research possibilities inherent in the multiagent MODOMA framework represents a novel design. In particular, this system can be employed to conduct language acquisition experiments involving an adult language model and a learning language model based on natural language. These agents engage in interactions such that all components of the system—such as parameter settings, input data, executed learning procedures, interactions, generated utterances, and acquired grammatical structures—are subject to user control, reproducibility, measurability, and verifiability. Ter Hoeve et al. (2022) propose a teacher-student framework to simulate language acquisition and implement two preliminary experiments corresponding to the initial stages of this model. The first experiment investigates distinct domains by employing 'two strictly separated vocabularies' within an artificial language, while the second explores different structural patterns through token repetition. In these experiments, the instructor selects a predefined set of training data from a collection of pre-constructed sentences, which is then provided to multiple student language models. After training, the student models are evaluated using a test set consisting of various sentences from the constructed language. Additionally, a teacher model is trained on a separate set of sentences and subsequently assessed. This implementation differs from MODOMA primarily in the training and test data: while Ter Hoeve et al. (2022) use pre-constructed sentences from an artificial language, MODOMA relies on real-time data representing a natural language, generated by Delilah during interaction based on an explicitly defined Dutch grammar model.

The methodology section is composed of the presentation of participants (Sect. 3.1), the interview design (Sect. 3.2), our approach to conducting interviews (Sect. 3.3), and the interview analysis method (Sect. 3.4). We selected the participants to ensure a representative population. They reflect the diversity of roles within the project, different levels of experience, and various educational backgrounds. Four types of actors are involved in the project: Product Owners (PO), Developers (IT), Agronomists (AGRO), and User Interface/Experience (UX) designers. The participants hold different educational qualifications, with some having engineering degrees and others holding PhDs. Moreover, we ensured that participants had varying degrees of experience with the project. In total, we selected 16 participants: nine Developers, either currently or previously engaged in the project, two Agronomists, three Product Owners, and two UX designers—all of whom are actively working on the project. Motivation plays a key role in eliciting relevant and meaningful responses [KP08]. In this study, participants were informed that they would receive feedback and that their responses would help shape a suitable strategy for implementing the SPL process. We opted for structured interviews guided by a predefined set of questions. We followed the main recommendations of [KP08], using terminology that is clear and unambiguous. Each question was formulated to address a single, well-defined concept. We deliberately avoided sensitive questions or inquiries about past events. Furthermore, we refrained from posing questions that the respondents might not be qualified to answer due to insufficient knowledge. This consideration led us to adopt aspects of actor-driven interviews. The interview questionnaire is divided into three main sections. The first section serves as an introduction, during which the interviewee presents their background. The main section is further split into two parts: one addressing the current process and the other focusing on proposed changes. Finally, the conclusion section encompasses a discussion of the interview as a whole.

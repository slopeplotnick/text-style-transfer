On November 20, 2025, a discussion centered on the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) as both problem solvers and solution verifiers. Verifiers enhance solver performance by selecting superior answers from a range of options. Despite this dual role, prior studies primarily focused on self-verification, seldom exploring how verifiers assess outputs from either their own model family or different ones. Furthermore, while modern LLMs undergo comprehensive post-training, its impact on verification remains uncertain. We conducted a systematic study of 37 models across various families, sizes, and training phases, evaluated against nine benchmarks encompassing logical reasoning, structured puzzles, symbolic computation, mathematics, commonsense, factual recall, and domain knowledge. We compare self-verification with cross-family and intra-family verification. To support this analysis, we introduce the concept of verifier gain, empirically validating it as a metric that forecasts performance improvements via test-time verifier-based rejection sampling. We examine how metrics like verifier gain and false positive rate vary with model size and post-training, and we also differentiate the verifiability across datasets. Our findings highlight that cross-family verification is notably effective; post-training augments cross-family improvements while diminishing self-improvement; and tasks involving mathematics and logic exhibit the greatest inherent verifiability. The application of LLMs in problem-solving has evolved beyond simply querying for solutions to a system wherein these models both solve and verify. Verification strategies at test time are diverse, ranging from basic methods like generating multiple solutions and filtering through a verifier (Zhao et al., 2025) to more complex iterative refinement techniques (Madaan et al., 2023). Consequently, employing generative verification during evaluation enables LLMs to address more intricate problems than when used alone (Cobbe et al., 2021; Lightman et al., 2024).

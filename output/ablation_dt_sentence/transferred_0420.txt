Macro-level risks manifest when the entire system's behavior diverges from the designers' intentions and evolves according to its own autonomous dynamics. At this stage, rectifying a singular component or connection is insufficient—equilibrium can no longer be restored, as the system has surpassed the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon. It operates independently, maintaining and reproducing its own logic, making preventive oversight ineffective while traditional safety interventions lose efficacy. These risks do not presume the actions of an adversary; systemic issues can arise spontaneously due to the complex interactions and recursive dynamics inherent in the system—feedback mechanisms, shared contexts, or aligning objectives—even when all participants adhere to their designated roles. Nonetheless, individuals with malicious intent can exploit the same mechanisms that naturally lead systems astray. For instance, an attacker capable of altering communications, modifying directives, or disrupting connectivity can expedite instability, facilitate maladaptive collaboration, or adversely influence collective cognition. The Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon framework therefore examines inherent and deliberate issues within a continuum: inherent dynamics illustrate autonomous failures, while malicious intent demonstrates potential exacerbation or initiation of adverse scenarios. This examination reveals that significant systemic issues cannot be reduced to the malfunctioning of individual components. Once systems interact extensively, the boundary between cause and consequence becomes indistinct; actions deemed beneficial for individual components may inadvertently precipitate systemic complications. Consequently, traditional methods of verification, focused on isolated outcomes or static evaluations, prove insufficient. They assess adherence to norms rather than behavior in practical contexts. Understanding safety within this new framework demands a reconceptualization of our perspective: rather than verifying the functionality of individual components, we must examine holistic interactions focusing on communication, feedback mechanisms, and adaptability among complex systems.

Recent advancements have made progress in bias detection, toxicity mitigation, and fairness auditing. However, these efforts typically focus on evaluating isolated outputs from models rather than examining the evolution of their ethical reasoning or value alignment over time, particularly as they interact with diverse, context-dependent, and dynamically changing scenarios. This represents a significant challenge, especially for autonomous AI systems that adjust their responses based on user input, objectives, prior interactions, and previous actions. Without ensuring longitudinal consistency, models are vulnerable to moral drift, where their ethical positions fluctuate or deteriorate due to prompt framing, situational pressures, and temporal variation. Such inconsistencies can undermine the perceived reliability, accountability, and trustworthiness of AI systems. Ensuring ethical consistency is, therefore, not merely a regulatory requirement; it is also crucial for maintaining the logical stability, interpretability, and long-term trust of AI systems. In sectors such as healthcare, finance, education, and governance, even small ethical discrepancies can lead to the dissemination of inaccurate information, the amplification of cognitive biases, and outcomes misaligned with human values. While recent initiatives have started to address these limitations, current verification approaches are still confined by static test suites and fixed datasets, which fail to adapt to the evolving behaviors of contemporary language models. Consequently, a critical research challenge is the development of an intrinsic, autonomous framework capable of evaluating and elucidating the ethical stability of such models without reliance on external corpora or retrospective analysis. Building upon the framework outlined in Section 1, this study introduces the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP), an entirely autonomous, dataset-free ethical evaluation system designed to continuously assess, interpret, and enhance the moral reasoning processes of AI models. MoCoP functions autonomously, generating ethical scenarios, obtaining model responses, and conducting interpretations through its internally defined rules and methodologies. In contrast to traditional methods that depend on predetermined test corpora and external annotation, MoCoP autonomously creates and evaluates its own ethical scenarios. It integrates three interdependent analytical modules—lexical integrity assessment, semantic risk estimation, and reasoning-based judgment modeling—to derive unified indicators of moral coherence and ethical stability. Each module serves a distinct purpose: lexical analysis identifies bias and linguistic degradation, semantic risk assessment evaluates potential issues in meaning, and reasoning evaluation scrutinizes the decision-making processes.

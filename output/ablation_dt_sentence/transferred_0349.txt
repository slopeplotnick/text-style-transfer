Large language models (LLMs) continue to grow in size and complexity, placing immense pressure on memory bandwidth and computational resources during both training and deployment. To mitigate these constraints, quantization offers a solution by reducing the precision of data and replacing costly floating-point operations with more efficient integer arithmetic. Two primary methodologies dominate the quantization landscape. Post-training quantization (PTQ) [11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16] is favored for its simplicity and the absence of retraining, making it an attractive option. However, when applied with very low bit widths, such as 1 or 2 bits, PTQ often results in significant accuracy degradation. This issue is typically due to several factors, including insufficient calibration data, suboptimal scaling methods, or the disruption of range accuracy caused by outlier channels. In contrast, quantization-aware training (QAT) integrates the quantization process into the training loop, allowing the model to adapt to the quantized representation during optimization. While QAT consistently outperforms PTQ under low-bit conditions, it is often more time-consuming and prone to instability, as its ability to accommodate diverse data distributions is limited. We apply FAIRY2 to widely used open-source language models, such as the LLaMA series, and demonstrate its effectiveness in these settings. Our approach introduces a method to transform any model layer into an alternative representation without changing the model's functionality before quantization. The model is then trained at extremely low bit levels using a specialized quantizer, while leveraging pre-existing checkpoints. Furthermore, the method is extended to the residual data, effectively minimizing quantization errors and enhancing overall model accuracy.

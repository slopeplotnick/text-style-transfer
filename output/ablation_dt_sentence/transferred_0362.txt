AlphaZero was among the pioneering systems to demonstrate the efficacy of self-play-based learning, wherein it autonomously refines its policy through repeated self-competitive interactions (Silver et al., 2017a; Sukhbaatar et al., 2017). This concept has since been extended to language models, leading to the development of self-evolving LLMs that progressively refine their reasoning capabilities over time (Tao et al., 2024). Several systems have instantiated language self-play at scale, with self-play fine-tuning improving weaker LLMs by repeatedly generating and solving tasks without the need for large labeled corpora (Chen et al., 2024). This approach has proven particularly effective in verifiable domains like code generation, where a "Coder" agent's program is validated by a "Tester" agent’s unit tests (Lin et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025; Pourcel et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025a). Pushing this concept further, fully data-free methods such as R-Zero enable models to propose questions, generate candidate solutions, and learn purely from internal verification signals (Huang et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Kuba et al., 2025). Other approaches begin with a limited set of examples, progressively generating more challenging problems based on these initial cases (Fang et al., 2025; He et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025a). R-Few builds on this line of work but emphasizes a different point on the supervision spectrum. This guided self-play design preserves the benefits of open-ended exploration while explicitly mitigating concept drift and diversity collapse. Moreover, reinforcement learning (RL) has become a pivotal mechanism for enhancing LLM reasoning beyond supervised fine-tuning and instruction tuning, most notably through reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and its variants (Kaufmann et al., 2024). Early RLHF systems like InstructGPT (Ouyang et al., 2022) demonstrated that sequence-level preference optimization significantly improves helpfulness, safety, and, in many cases, reasoning quality compared to pure supervised fine-tuning. Recent reasoning-centric systems, such as DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2024), have built upon these foundations, showing that RL-style optimization with carefully designed reasoning rewards can greatly enhance multi-step problem solving. Further evidence from both commercial and open-source models suggests that tailoring RL objectives to long-form chain-of-thought and verifiable tasks leads to substantial gains in reasoning ability (Yang et al., 2025a; Team et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b; Yu et al., 2025).

Table 3 offers a comparative analysis of evaluation scores from different configurations of VideoScience-Judge against human annotations. While the implementation of GPT-5 Pro has somewhat mitigated score inflation issues, discrepancies remain, particularly in Dynamism, Coherence, and Immutability, where overestimation by the model persists. To address this, incorporating a checklist as a heuristic and requiring the model to substantiate its ratings with evidence from key frames effectively reduces instances of overestimation. Furthermore, integrating computer vision tools like ByteTrack, which assists in consistent identity tracking across frames and serves as robust evidence against frame jumps or unexpected transformations, greatly narrows these scoring gaps. This integration results in an improvement of 0.07 and 0.09 in Coherence and Immutability, respectively, compared to the baseline VideoScience-Judge without the checklist. However, this can lead to score quantization and reduced differentiation among similar models. Additionally, its rule-based approach for detecting explicit violations of discrete physical laws, such as "Does the ball descend?" or "Does water flow correctly?" fails to capture holistic aspects of physical realism. Such aspects include motion trajectories, causal relationships over time, acceleration variations, momentum conservation, and motion smoothness, which human perception naturally evaluates. These limitations are notably pronounced for VideoScience-Bench, which contrasts with PhyGenBench's simplified single-principle scenarios. VideoScience-Bench encompasses multiple scientific concepts, including multi-object interactions and fluid dynamics, where outcomes arise from combinations of theoretical laws rather than isolated principles, featuring cascading effects where second-order dynamics are crucial.

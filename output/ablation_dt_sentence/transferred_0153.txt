We adopted a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) approach, with the Qwen-1.5 model serving as the foundational architecture. This enabled the model to develop general language proficiency, which was later fine-tuned for domain-specific e-commerce tasks. To incorporate domain knowledge, we utilized knowledge graphs and integrated this information through meticulously crafted prompts, allowing the model to understand e-commerce-specific patterns and interrelationships. Performance was rigorously evaluated using standard benchmark protocols and relevance metrics, ensuring both accuracy and the model's ability to handle a variety of query types. The trained model was then applied to assist in the annotation workflow, expediting the labeling process and improving overall data quality by identifying inconsistencies and potential errors. This phase also addressed more complex challenges, such as interpreting intricate queries, uncovering implicit meanings, and employing logical reasoning in constructing paths. Simple instructional prompts were insufficient for these tasks, so we introduced a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) strategy to enable structured reasoning. This approach allowed the model to decompose complex problems, capture relational dependencies, and perform stepwise comparisons across alternative solutions. As a result, the model's ability to tackle complex queries was significantly enhanced. Furthermore, we developed a system to identify challenging cases and used iterative feedback to improve model performance. CoT data was then utilized for tasks like attribute extraction and matching, and the model was retrained using the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) method, now with Qwen-2 as the backbone architecture. This shift enabled the model to process reasoning steps while maintaining a focus on specific task objectives.

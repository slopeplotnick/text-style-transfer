The empirical observation that predictions often stabilize well before the final diffusion step (Pengxiang et al., 2025) has motivated recent efforts to exploit early convergence within the generative process. Dynamic policies modulate the trade-off between exploration and acceleration by incorporating signals such as historical outcomes or local predictability, effectively pruning redundant steps (Wei et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b; Or-El et al., 2025). In parallel, other research focuses on error correction and refinement, enabling models to revise outputs by selectively re-generating low-confidence components (Zhang et al., 2025a; Su et al., 2025). Few- and one-step methods similarly address this inefficiency by importing principles from continuous diffusion, operationalized through curriculum strategies, consistency-based distillation, or flow matching (Sahoo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Meister et al., 2024). Orthogonal efforts improve efficiency through per-step latency reduction, leveraging caching for bidirectional refinement (Yuan et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a) and speculative decoding to draft and verify tokens in parallel (Sohoni et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025a). While impactful, these techniques often incur additional training cost, architectural modifications, or reliance on heuristic rules—highlighting the need for a simple, training-free, and model-agnostic early-exit mechanism. To this end, we revisit diffusion decoding as a when-to-stop problem and propose SchED, a schedule-based early-exit strategy that avoids extra training or specialized model design. Specifically, we aggregate token-level confidence across the output and compare it to a continuously relaxing threshold aligned with normalized diffusion progress. By decoupling this confidence target from the step count and expressing the threshold as a smooth function of progress, SchED enables exit as soon as predictions stabilize—sidestepping the brittleness of fixed budgets or abrupt stopping rules. Crucially, it integrates seamlessly with standard transfer schedules such as single-suffix and block diffusion, without altering model training procedures (Nie et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025).

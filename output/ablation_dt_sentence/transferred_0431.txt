While machine learning and generative techniques are prevalent, the deployment of AI in decision-making is comparatively less frequent. Decision-making necessitates the irrevocable commitment of resources, a practice that diverges from the typical aims of academic research. To date, the sole significant and impactful application of decision-making AI in mineral exploration is the characterization of the high-grade segment of the Mingomba deposit in Zambia. In this case, historical drilling data were re-analyzed using advanced contemporary analytical tools, resulting in the generation of novel hypotheses that revealed a higher-grade section than previously identified. AI, formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), was employed to facilitate the sequential planning of drilling operations. The primary objective was to use AI to elucidate uncertainties associated with geological hypotheses regarding the locations of mineral-rich deposits. This approach proved significantly more effective than the conventional grid-based drilling method. The improved efficiency arises from prioritizing the disproof of geological hypotheses rather than merely attempting to identify mineral deposits. In Part III, I will focus on how artificial intelligence can facilitate the acceleration of the discovery and characterization of critical mineral deposits. Initially, I will concentrate on data analysis and prediction, followed by a transition to AI-based decision-making. The early stages of AI implementation encountered a notable degree of defensiveness among some groups. The perception prevailed that AI might supplant or diminish the significance of domain experts. It is understandable that individuals are apprehensive about the potential threat to their employment, a concern prevalent in contemporary discourse. There was an anticipation that AI would independently generate novel discoveries. However, it is unlikely AI will simply designate a specific location on a map for autonomous systems to conduct drilling operations and achieve discoveries. Rather, AI is perceived as a means to assist domain experts by assuming responsibilities beyond their proficiency or unnecessary for their engagement. This shift enables experts to concentrate on the creative aspects of process-oriented thinking, particularly within the realms of geology and geochemistry.

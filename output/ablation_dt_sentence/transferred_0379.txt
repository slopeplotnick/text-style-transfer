Recommending matches in a text-rich, dynamic marketplace is particularly challenging due to the constantly evolving nature of both content and interaction graphs. To address these challenges, we introduce GraphMatch, an innovative system that integrates pre-trained language models with graph neural networks (GNNs). Unlike previous approaches that rely on separate models, GraphMatch combines text encoders and GNNs into a unified framework. It employs adversarial negative sampling and point-in-time subgraph training to learn representations that jointly capture fine-grained textual semantics and the temporal dynamics of the underlying graph structure. We evaluated GraphMatch extensively using large-scale data from Upwork, a prominent job marketplace, and designed it for efficient real-time applications. Our experiments show that GraphMatch outperforms both language-only and graph-only systems in matching tasks, demonstrating superior performance while maintaining runtime efficiency. These results highlight the effectiveness of integrating language and graph-based data for recommendation tasks in environments with abundant textual content and dynamic interaction networks. Such systems—including e-commerce platforms, employment marketplaces, academic citation networks, and personalized news feeds—are naturally modeled as Temporal Text-Attributed Graphs (TTAGs). In TTAGs, nodes are characterized by detailed textual descriptions that evolve over time, while edges capture dynamic interactions such as purchases, endorsements, or replies. Successful recommendation requires modeling both the fine-grained semantic information in text and the evolving structural signals from the interaction graph. However, integrating these modalities in dynamic settings remains a challenging task. Recent advancements in language models, such as SentenceBERT and E5, have shown notable success in transforming documents into embeddings, facilitating the comparison of textual meanings. Nevertheless, these models face limitations when applied to TTAGs, as they often overlook critical structural and syntactic nuances inherent in such representations. The decision to link two items is typically based on their relationships within the graph, an aspect that pure text encoders fail to capture. Furthermore, in TTAGs, the semantics of a node and the rationale for its connection can shift rapidly. For example, the rise of generative AI has shifted "AI Writing" job postings from emphasizing editorial skills to prioritizing expertise in prompt engineering. Additionally, geopolitical events continue to shape the dynamics of freelancer-client matching within the global labor market. Treating text as static features can lead to significant issues, such as the inadvertent leakage of future information that should remain undisclosed.

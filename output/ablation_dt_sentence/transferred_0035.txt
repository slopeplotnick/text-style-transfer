The method optimizes a penalized value function to ensure that the policy remains within the support of the dataset. This provides our primary objective: formally simplifying the Offline RL objective in Eq. (4) to an equivalent task—identifying the action chunk with the highest visitation count, i.e., density. This result offers a strong theoretical justification for our method. Our test-time scaling framework, which seeks the action with the highest density (i.e., in-support), is not merely a heuristic. Instead, it constitutes a principled and theoretically grounded realization of the anti-exploration objective standard in Offline RL. Recent successes (Lobel et al., 2023; Bai et al., 2025) in applying pseudo-counts to LLM preference optimization provide further support for this approach. We instantiate this estimator as a Coin Flipping Network (CFN). As empirically validated in Figure 3, the pseudo-counts derived from our coupled estimator show a strong correlation between the L2 distance to the ground-truth action and the pseudo-count estimated by our CFN. This validates that our CFN effectively functions as the necessary verifier to penalize out-of-support actions. Our experiments aim to assess whether the proposed Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-COunits can improve the accuracy of action prediction, thereby enhancing task success rates. Furthermore, we seek to identify which design choices contribute to these improvements and whether the additional computational cost is justifiable. Specifically, our experiments are designed to answer the following research questions: Q1: Can our framework improve performance across diverse environments, various VLC policies, and a wide range of tasks? Q2: What is the additional time cost introduced by our test-time scaling method, and can it run efficiently on real robots while maintaining performance gains? Q3: How do design choices—such as the incorporation of internal features and the CFN pseudo-count network—affect overall performance?

We evaluate a diverse set of recent LLMs spanning both closed- and open-weight releases (see Appendix A.4 for details). Where possible, we disable extended-thinking or reasoning controls to examine the models' natural, emergent behaviour. This was feasible for all models except GPT-5 mini, which restricts adjustments to reasoning depth; consequently, we set this parameter to its lowest level. Additionally, we conducted a human baseline study for comparison across all tasks, incorporating a small number of ablations. Further details are provided in Appendix A.5, and the human results are presented in the left panel of Figure 8. This experiment primarily serves as a reference point, as our main focus is the comparative performance of the LLMs. Extensive studies on human perception, including work by Jazayeri & Shadlen (2010) and Petzschner & Glasauer (2011), provide foundational insights into the magnitude estimation effects explored here. Most models exhibit superior performance in the text modality compared to the image modality, with the exception of the maze distance estimation task. In this task, the text input is a long, detailed path description, significantly more complex than the ASCII prompts used in other tasks (see Figure 2). While most models perform worse in the text modality for this task, GPT-5 Mini stands out, achieving near-perfect performance due to its residual reasoning capabilities, which we can attenuate but not fully disable. This highlights the task-dependent nature of model behaviour. Across tasks, the evidence for Bayesian behaviour is consistently stronger in the image modality than in text (see Appendix A.11). Transitioning from unimodal to multimodal inputs does not always result in uniform performance improvements. However, some models leverage multimodal information more effectively: Llama-4 Maverick performs optimally under multimodal conditions across all tasks, while Claude 3.7 Sonnet and GPT-4o show improved performance on two out of the three tasks.

Position bias refers to the tendency of language models to disproportionately attend to information appearing in specific regions of the input sequence, often at the expense of content located elsewhere. Despite the advanced capabilities of these models in language processing, empirical studies have shown that their performance remains sensitive to the structural organization of input data. Specifically, in multi-document summarization (MDS), where several source texts are concatenated into a single input, the sequential ordering of documents can systematically influence which content is emphasized or retained in the final summary. As a result, summaries may disproportionately highlight certain segments of the input while omitting critical information located elsewhere, depending on the order in which the documents are presented. One example of this issue is the "Lost in the Middle" phenomenon. Research indicates that models perform optimally when important information is placed at the beginning or end of the input sequence, but their performance degrades when such information is positioned centrally. This trend has been observed in tasks like question answering and key-value extraction from multiple documents. Furthermore, studies on evaluative tasks reveal that models exhibit a bias toward the first or last option presented, even when the most relevant choice is elsewhere in the sequence. This phenomenon, often referred to as lead bias, results in disproportionate emphasis on the initial segments of the input. While certain models, such as GPT-3.5-Turbo, exhibit relatively mild lead bias, others show heightened sensitivity to early-positioned content, particularly in extreme summarization scenarios. Beyond impairing performance, such biases raise significant fairness concerns. For instance, when tweets are organized by dialect and arranged in a particular sequence, the resulting summaries may unfairly favor the group presented first. These findings underscore the profound impact that input ordering can have on model behavior and outcomes.

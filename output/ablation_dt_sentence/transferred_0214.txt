The challenge associated with adversarial training enhancing attack transferability does not stem from specific model architectures but rather from the changes in information processing mechanisms induced by adversarial training. This phenomenon remains consistent across various model architectures, highlighting its importance for ensuring robust system-level security. To better understand how adversarial training enhances transferability, we examine model interpretability through the lens of integrated gradients, focusing on how attribution patterns shift under robust optimization. For example, a comparison was made between two variants of the DenseNet121 model: one subjected to adversarial training (AT) and the other trained without adversarial interventions (ST). The AT model places emphasis on larger regions of the image, such as the ostrich’s head, body, and legs, whereas the ST model assigns greater weight to finer details, including feather textures and background elements. This distinction is crucial, as AT models prioritize features that are broadly shared across many images, in contrast to ST models, which focus on specific details with limited transferability. Consequently, adversarial examples generated by AT models exhibit enhanced transferability, as they primarily target features common to a variety of architectures. For instance, DenseNet121 and DenseNet201 exhibit similar image processing mechanisms, with MobileNetV3 also showing similarities due to its design principles derived from EfficientNet. This design improvement makes MobileNetV3 more vulnerable to adversarial attacks from EfficientNet-based models.

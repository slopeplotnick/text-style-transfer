Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have seen significant progress in recent years; however, they still face challenges in capturing fine-grained details within high-resolution (HR) images. To enhance the ability of MLLMs in understanding high-resolution images, existing research typically follows two main approaches. Training-based methods, such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning (RL), can improve performance but often at the cost of the model's generalization ability across a wide range of vision-language tasks. In contrast, non-training-based methods bypass additional training by employing hierarchical or tree-based search mechanisms to localize and emphasize relevant image regions. Nevertheless, these methods are often inefficient and may struggle to identify all relevant objects, especially in multi-object scenarios. In this section, we investigate the relationship between image crop resolution and MLLM performance. Our experimental results show that the resolution of image segments significantly affects the model’s performance. Specifically, different resolutions are more suitable for capturing objects at varying scales. Inspired by this finding, we propose the MRD framework. We evaluated the framework using two versions of the model, LLaVA-ov and LLaVA-v1.5, within a benchmark framework. As shown in Figure 3, the results demonstrate that setting the resolution of image crops to 112 yields optimal performance for both single-object and multi-object tasks. This suggests that a resolution of 112 may be the most effective choice for this application.

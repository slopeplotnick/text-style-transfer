The integration of robotics into the construction industry holds significant promise for addressing labor shortages while enhancing safety and efficiency. Robots excel in performing physically demanding and repetitive tasks, thereby reducing the risks of fatigue and error. As a result, these capabilities can accelerate construction processes and contribute to cost reductions. However, due to the complex nature of construction work, a single robot cannot execute all tasks. For example, constructing a wall requires various skills such as material transportation, precise placement, welding, and quality inspection. Therefore, it is essential to deploy a coordinated ensemble of robots working in concert. Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) breaks down complex tasks into manageable subtasks, assigning them to robots based on their abilities, task requirements, and environmental conditions. MRTA problems classify scenarios based on whether robots perform single or multiple tasks, whether tasks require one or multiple robots, and whether task allocation involves instantaneous or time-extended engagements. Consistently coordinating numerous robots to accomplish multiple tasks requires strategic planning that effectively manages the varied competencies of the robots, the specific requirements of each task, and the temporal aspects involved. However, research on the MRTA problem primarily focuses on warehouse logistics and environmental exploration, with limited application in the construction industry.

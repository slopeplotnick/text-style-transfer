We evaluate ADORE using both offline and online metrics. In the offline evaluation, the task is treated as a binary classification problem, as the human annotations are binary. We assess the model's performance through precision, recall, and F1 score. For the online evaluation, we conduct experiments in search advertising and adopt key business metrics, including click-through rates (CTR), total ad revenue, and the Good rate provided by human experts. We compare ADORE with several strong baseline models, including widely used BERT, LLAMA-3-8B, and their respective variants. For BERT-based models, we compare the two-tower-based BERT and the cross-encoder-based BERT. In the case of the cross-encoder-based BERT, we further compare its mini version (which shares the same architecture and parameter size as our online model), the standard version, and the version enhanced with our proposed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) information. For the LLM model, we evaluate an approach that directly utilizes the representation of the last token for classification. We then present ADORE, an extension of BERT-Mini that integrates online data from the RD module, adversarial-style synthetic data from the DS module, and explicit attribute knowledge from the KD module. The experimental results, as shown in Table 2, indicate that ADORE significantly outperforms all baselines on a large-scale real-world dataset. Specifically, we observe the following: (1) The cross-encoder-based BERT model outperforms the bi-encoder-based BERT, with further scaling of model parameters leading to enhanced performance. Notably, the integration of CoT attributes significantly improves BERT's performance, demonstrating the value of explicit knowledge. In contrast, directly using LLM for classification results in weaker performance than BERT. (2) Leveraging the domain-rule-aware LLM within the RD module to generate challenging samples substantially enhances the model's performance. Furthermore, incorporating adversarially crafted data from the DS module leads to a higher F1 score. The explicit knowledge integration within the KD module also strengthens the model's reasoning and generalization capabilities, further improving its overall effectiveness.

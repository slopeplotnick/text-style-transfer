In response to limitations in current methodologies, we introduce a new benchmark, HEALTHCONTRADICT, which employs interpretable quantitative metrics to evaluate language models’ ability to process and resolve long and conflicting biomedical information. Compared to state-of-the-art medical QA benchmarks, HEALTHCONTRADICT more effectively captures differences in model performance. In our evaluation, language models specialized in the biomedical domain show superior ability to (i) leverage accurate contextual information, (ii) disregard inaccurate contextual information, and (iii) distinguish between conflicting contextual information. To the best of our knowledge, the TREC Health Misinformation tracks remain the only publicly available resource that provides (1) expert-curated health questions with verified answers and supporting scientific evidence, and (2) document pools containing both corroborative and contradictory content, each adjudicated by domain experts. These components are essential for generating health-related questions with ground-truth answers and for evaluating language models’ capacity to resolve biomedical knowledge conflicts using contradictory document pairs. In HEALTHCONTRADICT, we focus on relevant and credible documents that reflect the efficacy of a document in supporting a given answer. We standardized annotation labels by mapping the 2019 terms “effective”/“ineffective” and the 2021 terms “supportive”/“dissuades” to a unified “yes”/“no” format, consistent with the 2022 annotations. If two documents addressing the same question express opposing stances—one affirming, one negating—they are treated as a contradictory pair. Moreover, we define a document as correct if its stance aligns with the scientific evidence, and incorrect if it contradicts that evidence. The original collection includes 130 expert-annotated questions: 50 from 2019, 35 from 2021, and 45 from 2022. We include questions with binary stance annotations and those that present both supporting and opposing perspectives. Applying this criterion narrows the set to 110 questions. We then pair supporting and opposing documents into contradiction pairs, ensuring each document appears only once, as some documents are linked to multiple questions. Questions that cannot yield at least one contradiction pair due to a lack of unique documents are excluded. This process results in a final set of 81 questions and 920 contradictory document pairs.

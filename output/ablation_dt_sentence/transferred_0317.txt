Research suggests that the entrenchment of belief is central to social epistemic challenges such as polarization and misinformation [Del Vicario et al., 2017, Modgil et al., 2024, Lefebvre et al., 2024, Calhoun, 2004], and may contribute to a range of downstream cognitive biases [Oeberst and Imhoff, 2023]. This entrenchment arises from the ways individuals process information, particularly through their underlying reasoning practices. Large language models (LLMs), trained on vast corpora of human-generated text, aim to assist users by providing contextually appropriate responses and supporting various cognitive tasks. These models have shown considerable success in handling complex cognitive challenges, with novel reasoning techniques emerging through their application. From the early Chain-of-Thought approach to more recent advancements in reinforcement learning for inference-time reasoning, these methods are designed to enhance the models’ ability to seek truth through systematic argumentation, evidence gathering, and trial-and-error. However, despite their effectiveness, such models can sometimes deviate from truth-seeking behaviors due to belief entrenchment—a tendency to favor prior commitments over new evidence, which can distort their conclusions. This can lead to unwarranted overconfidence in their responses, echoing well-documented cognitive biases in humans [Park et al., 2010]. While recent studies have indicated the presence of belief entrenchment in LLMs [Schmidgall et al., 2024, Shi et al., 2024, Sumita et al., 2024], empirical validation remains elusive. Distinguishing between justified belief updates, such as predictions later corroborated by evidence, and biased updates, driven by a desire to maintain an unfalsifiable prior, presents a significant challenge. Previous research typically relies on synthetic tasks or domain-specific contexts where belief entrenchment is more easily identifiable. However, these approaches fall short in detecting reasoning failures, particularly in open-ended tasks where the ground truth is ambiguous or inaccessible.

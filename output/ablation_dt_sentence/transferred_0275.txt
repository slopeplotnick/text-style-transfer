We visually compare the metrics of our generated files to those of the EMOPIA files and use t-tests to assess whether the generated data maintains the statistically significant difference in the metrics between high and low valence and arousal, as observed in the EMOPIA data. To generate music conditioned on a specific emotion inferred from text, we employ an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture. The textual emotion signal is captured using a RoBERTa-large encoder, which is contrastively fine-tuned to better capture nuanced emotion differences across quadrants. The decoder, a Transformer-based module consisting of three layers, each with four attention heads, is designed to model the temporal and harmonic structure of symbolic music. Figure 2 presents an overview of our model architecture. The encoder is initialized from RoBERTa-large and fine-tuned on the GoEmotions dataset, where the original 27 emotion labels are replaced with their corresponding quadrant labels. Each batch contains examples from multiple quadrants, with pairs within the same quadrant treated as positive pairs and pairs from different quadrants as negative pairs. This in-batch formulation allows for efficient training with many negatives and produces an embedding space where quadrants form separable clusters. The contrastively fine-tuned encoder is then frozen and used as the text-conditioning module in our generative model. While the pretrained RoBERTa encoder produces highly overlapping representations across the four emotion quadrants, the contrastively fine-tuned encoder yields noticeably more separated clusters. This confirms that contrastive training strengthens the emotional structure of the embedding space, while still reflecting the inherently gradual and subjective nature of affective categories. (See Figure 4 in the Appendix (Section C) for the t-SNE visualizations of the story embeddings before and after contrastive training.) Given that the EMOPIA dataset contains only 1,078 MIDI samples, which is insufficient for a Transformer decoder to effectively learn the dependencies between MIDI tokens, we pre-train the decoder on the larger GiantMIDI-Piano dataset. During pre-training, we input zero tensors in place of text embeddings and train the decoder using a causal language modeling objective, thereby enabling it to learn the probability distribution over MIDI tokens in an auto-regressive manner.

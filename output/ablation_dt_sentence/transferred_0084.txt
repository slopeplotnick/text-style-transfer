The system we developed is designed to identify overt errors that can be readily detected, rather than engaging in complex scientific analysis or evaluating physical dynamics. In contrast, our dataset focuses on intricate physical phenomena and scientific principles, where explicit decomposition of physical processes through domain-specific checklists leads to more reliable results than general-purpose explanations. This highlights the necessity for specialized methods to assess nuanced physical reasoning in video generation. Consequently, VideoScore2 is not well-suited for our benchmark, which requires a strict, scientifically grounded evaluation, resulting in weaker alignment with human judgments for this dataset. To illustrate this, we provide qualitative examples of model performance. As shown in the upper portion of Figure 4, Sora-2 successfully generates the expected physical phenomenon with strong prompt consistency and accurate motion. The video adheres closely to the described setup, demonstrates coherent temporal evolution, and captures both the ignition onset and the characteristic color dynamics of the reaction. In contrast, Hailuo 2.3, despite maintaining reasonable dynamism and spatial coherence, fails to incorporate crucial elements of the prompt, resulting in a video where the anticipated phenomenon does not materialize. This underscores that appropriate experimental configuration and careful prompt grounding are essential for achieving high benchmark performance, even when other dimensions appear satisfactory. Furthermore, even seemingly simple physical configurations continue to present substantial challenges for current models.

Existing evaluations of video model performance predominantly focus on fundamental physical knowledge, confining assessments to everyday scenarios that only require a basic understanding of physical phenomena. To address this limitation, we introduce VideoScience-Bench, a benchmark designed to assess the scientific understanding and reasoning of video models. Each assessment encompasses multiple scientific subjects and necessitates collegiate-level expertise, spanning 103 concepts within the domains of physics and chemistry. We evaluate whether these video models can serve as zero-shot scientific reasoners, capable of understanding complex experimental setups and predicting the expected outcomes without supplementary assistance. The evaluation is structured around five primary dimensions: prompt consistency, predictive capability regarding expected outcomes, proficiency in managing dynamic interactions, adherence to invariant principles, and overall coherence. Furthermore, we introduce VideoScience-Judge, a sophisticated, checklist-based system that evaluates model performance comprehensively. This system first generates a prompt-specific checklist to guide the evaluation, then facilitates the identification of pivotal frames and discerning crucial moments in the video content to quantify the reasoning performance of the model. These metrics are aggregated into a single value that represents the overall reasoning quality of the model. Upon validation, the scoring derived from VideoScience-Judge demonstrates superior alignment with expert assessments compared to conventional tests, which primarily assess fundamental physical comprehension or the aesthetic realism of video content. In summary, this study investigates whether video generation models can engage with multiple interacting scientific concepts simultaneously. We make three principal contributions: 1. We introduce VideoScience-Bench, a benchmark aimed at evaluating the capability of video models to independently engage in scientific reasoning, using authentic examples drawn from 103 topics in physics and chemistry. Existing contributions predominantly concentrate on physical commonsense, thereby restricting assessments to routine scenarios. Thus, VideoScience-Bench extends existing benchmarks to encompass a broader spectrum of scientific reasoning and understanding. 2. We develop VideoScience-Judge, a framework that employs a checklist-based evaluation method, selecting key frames and utilizing computer-vision tools to identify physical phenomena such as motion and object interactions, thereby providing quantitative assessments of the scientific validity and perceptual quality of videos. 3. We present an empirical investigation employing the VideoScience-Bench framework. Despite advancements in modern video models capable of generating realistic and temporally coherent videos, many models encounter difficulties in comprehending complex scientific concepts and frequently exhibit deficiencies in accurately applying fundamental physical principles. Among the models examined, Sora-2 and Veo-3 demonstrated superior performance and showed early signs of scientific reasoning.

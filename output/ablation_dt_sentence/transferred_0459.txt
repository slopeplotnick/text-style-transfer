Future Research Directions and Advancements: Currently, the phenomenon of hallucinations in LLMs remains a substantial concern, thereby illuminating multiple avenues for future research that warrant exploration. One promising approach involves the development of real-time systems capable of detecting and correcting hallucinations during model inference rather than post-implementation, potentially enhancing the application of LLMs in high-stakes domains such as legal and medical document generation, where inaccuracies could be severely detrimental. Furthermore, the exploration of multi-modal detection, which integrates various data forms such as images, audio, and structured data alongside text, is essential. Given the widespread use of LLMs in multi-modal contexts, this integration might lead to errors or hallucinations arising from the convergence of diverse media types. Additionally, there is increasing interest in user-centered approaches, where models are designed to aid users in easily identifying and rectifying hallucinations. This involves examining interactive and transparent AI systems that empower users to engage with the model, questioning and corroborating its assertions, as discussed in recent studies. Moreover, there is a pressing need for further investigations into the mitigation of hallucinations through formal methods, as indicated in existing literature, promising improved model reliability, especially for applications with stringent accuracy demands. This article reviews common instances of hallucinations in LLMs, categorizing these errors into subclasses including factual inaccuracies and compromised coherence due to inadequate data or ambiguous inputs. Such hallucinations undermine user confidence and system usability, thereby necessitating strategies for their reduction.

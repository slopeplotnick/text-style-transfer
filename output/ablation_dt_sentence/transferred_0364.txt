How do language models utilize contextual information to answer health-related inquiries, and how are their responses influenced when conflicting contexts are introduced? To explore this, we investigate the performance of language models in processing lengthy, conflicting biomedical information using the HEALTHCONTRADICT dataset. This dataset contains 920 instances, each comprising a health-related question, a scientifically supported factual answer, and two documents presenting conflicting perspectives. We examine various question formulations, incorporating accurate, inaccurate, and mixed contexts, and analyze how these variations affect model responses. In comparison to other evaluation frameworks, HEALTHCONTRADICT offers a more nuanced assessment of how language models manage and interpret contextual information. Our findings reveal that model performance is shaped not only by pretraining knowledge but also by the ability to selectively leverage relevant contextual information while disregarding misleading or irrelevant content. Nevertheless, models can still generate responses that seem accurate but are factually incorrect, which poses a significant risk. Since individuals may place trust in inaccurate health information, this could have serious consequences for their well-being. Health information is particularly susceptible to rapid change, especially during crises, when unverified data spreads quickly across online platforms. This influx of misinformation can significantly impact the learning processes of these models. Several approaches have been proposed to help models handle conflicting or disorganized information. For instance, one approach adjusts the model’s knowledge when it conflicts with the context, while another evaluates the reasoning behind the model’s response. However, these methods primarily focus on either the model’s existing knowledge or the contextual information. An alternative approach combines both elements by assessing the degree of consistency between the information. Additionally, a method has been developed that generates two distinct types of answers: one derived from contextual information and the other from the model’s inherent knowledge. Another strategy ensures that the model selects the correct response when faced with conflicting information. Several approaches also aim to reduce the generation of erroneous responses by language models. During training, incorrect or irrelevant information is intentionally incorporated to enhance the model's ability to process and manage ambiguous or mixed-context data. In the testing phase, participants use strategies to identify and correct incorrect information, carefully phrase questions, and utilize multiple reviewers to verify response accuracy. They also focus on identifying robust countermeasures to prevent attacks and predicting shifts in facts to ensure that only up-to-date information is used. Current strategies primarily address either reconciling inconsistencies in information or mitigating undesirable model behaviors. However, both aspects are crucial for improving the accuracy and reliability of responses, particularly in health-related contexts.

However, achieving consistent performance across diverse scenarios remains a persistent challenge. Despite training on large and varied datasets, models often exhibit significant degradation when exposed to novel camera viewpoints, lighting conditions, or other unforeseen scene perturbations. This brittleness constrains their deployment in real-world environments, where visual conditions are inherently dynamic and unpredictable. Existing solutions to this problem typically fall into two broad categories: data-centric and representation-centric approaches. The former emphasizes expanding visual diversity, often through large-scale, multi-view datasets such as Libero-Plus, to expose models to a broader range of visual contexts. While effective in principle, this strategy is costly and labor-intensive in real-world settings, rendering continual adaptation impractical. Conversely, representation-centric approaches aim to enhance geometric consistency by improving the model’s understanding of 3D spatial relationships. Techniques such as multi-view supervision [27] and 3D-aware architectural designs [1] are commonly used to bolster geometric awareness and improve viewpoint robustness. However, even these geometry-aware models remain sensitive to task-irrelevant visual factors such as background clutter and illumination variation, underscoring a persistent gap between viewpoint invariance and general visual adaptability. A prevailing assumption in prior work is that improved robustness necessitates either more diverse data or better 3D representations, yet few studies examine whether the core issue lies in the spatial representation itself. To explore this question, we adopt a framework that decomposes the model into two conceptual components: (1) Spatial Modeling, implemented by the visual encoder, which infers spatial relationships—positions, orientations, interactions, and occlusions—between objects and generates the spatial representations required for downstream reasoning; and (2) Physical Modeling, which combines spatial information with task instructions and action history to support decision-making and produce executable action sequences. Crucially, viewpoint shifts primarily alter the spatial configuration of the scene, without impacting the underlying task semantics or action logic. This observation suggests that performance degradation under viewpoint changes is chiefly due to misalignment in Spatial Modeling, while the Physical Modeling component remains operational. In other words, the model retains its capacity for reasoning and planning, but its spatial representations become distorted, resulting in execution failures during downstream tasks.

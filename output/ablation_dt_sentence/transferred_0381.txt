Our method has been optimized for efficiency with contemporary systems designed for large-scale data processing, such as vLLM and SGLang. This approach is applicable in both real-time services and offline processing scenarios. In Section 4.2, we primarily employ the two-step vLLM configuration due to its efficiency and its ability to manage the reasoning steps effectively. THINKMERGE is evaluated in two distinct regimes: for tasks requiring a single correct answer, we consider AIME 2025 (MAA, 2025) and GPQA Diamond (Rein et al., 2024), where multiple responses can be aggregated through majority voting. For tasks without a singular correct answer, we assess performance on LiveCodeBench v5, BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and xBench-DeepSearch, emphasizing the generation of a single, high-quality solution. In the case of tasks with a single correct answer, we conducted five separate tests, evaluating models such as Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-14B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B. For open-ended coding tasks, we utilize models including DeepCoder-14B-Preview (Luo et al., 2025), Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-Coder-30A3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Thinking (0725), and Qwen3-Think-30A3B (Yang et al., 2025a). Open-ended research tasks are evaluated using WebSailor models, such as WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B, which align with previous methodologies for large-scale task execution (Kapoor et al., 2025). These models are particularly suited for complex multi-step problem-solving, as demonstrated by the WebSailor agent pipeline used in professional-grade information retrieval tasks (Souly et al., 2024). We set the maximum sequence length to 32,768 tokens, with specific details regarding the sampling settings provided in Appendix A.1. Furthermore, we assess whether THINKMERGE is effective in research tasks that require the model to interleave internal reasoning with tool use before generating an answer. To this end, we evaluate three Tongyi-WebSailor models—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B—on four challenging web-based benchmarks: BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and XbenchDeepSearch. BrowseComp-en and BrowseComp-zh focus on retrieving hard-to-find factual information in both English and Chinese, requiring multi-hop reasoning across languages. Given that BrowseComp-en contains 1,266 questions, we randomly select a subset of 200 questions for testing, ensuring its size is comparable to BrowseComp-zh, which contains 289 questions. GAIA emphasizes robust tool use for multi-step real-world tasks; following prior work (Li et al., 2025b), we evaluate on the 103 text-only validation cases. XbenchDeepSearch targets deep, professional-style information retrieval. We use the WebSailor agent pipeline with the recommended decoding hyperparameters: temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, and context length 32,768. Finally, we explore whether THINKMERGE benefits agentic deep-research tasks, where the model must interleave internal reasoning with tool calls before producing an answer.

System prompts have gained significant attention as a lightweight yet versatile mechanism for steering large language model (LLM) behavior during inference. A growing body of research demonstrates that carefully designed prompts can enhance reasoning capabilities (Wei et al., 2022) and reduce bias (Kamruzzaman and Kim, 2024). However, much of this research has been primarily conducted in English, with limited focus on the broader challenge of ensuring that a single prompt works reliably across multiple languages. This is particularly problematic in multilingual settings, where prompts that perform well in English often fail to maintain their efficacy when translated or applied to tasks in other languages (Kmainasi et al., 2025). As a result, multilingual deployment can lead to inconsistent accuracy, unstable reasoning, and degraded cross-lingual generalization (Tam et al., 2025). This motivates the central research question: how can a single prompt be designed to ensure high and stable performance across diverse languages and tasks? To address this question, we break it down into three core components: Q1: Which types of system prompts are most effective in guiding models toward improved multilingual behaviors? Q2: Is it possible to automatically discover prompts that achieve strong multilingual performance with minimal human input? Q3: How do high-performing prompts influence model outputs, including reasoning patterns and the choice of response language? In order to answer these questions, we propose a four-part evaluation framework that assesses multilingual effectiveness across multiple dimensions: average accuracy, accuracy variance, cross-lingual consistency, and output length variance. By analyzing the performance of different models across a variety of tasks, we introduce an approach to refine prompts for optimizing outcomes in these areas. Furthermore, we explore how optimized prompts impact reasoning processes, reducing cross-lingual variability and enhancing consistency. To this end, we present a new technique for multilingual prompt optimization, demonstrating the potential for automated prompt discovery in these settings. Our analysis of reasoning data reveals that high-quality prompts promote more structured and consistent reasoning, with fewer instances of language switching, suggesting that reasoning patterns themselves may be a key driver of multilingual robustness. Taken together, these findings highlight that system prompt optimization represents a promising yet underexplored avenue for improving the accuracy and reliability of multilingual LLMs. The code and dataset are available at https://github.com/orange0629/multilingual-system-prompt.

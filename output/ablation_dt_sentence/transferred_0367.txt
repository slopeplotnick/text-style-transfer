As the utilization of AI tools powered by large language models (LLMs) continues to expand, ensuring their safety and security has become a top priority. This concern is amplified when these models are deployed in multilingual and multimodal environments, where linguistic and cultural differences introduce new types of risks. Therefore, developing safety mechanisms that can effectively filter harmful or unsafe content across a diverse range of languages and user contexts is essential. Without these safeguards in place, the global deployment of LLMs risks unintentionally causing harm or excluding large populations. While much of the existing research has concentrated on high-resource languages such as English, Chinese, Spanish, German, and French, the vast majority of the world’s languages fall into the low-resource category, with limited training data and benchmarks available. This creates a significant gap in our ability to ensure safety across multilingual settings. Although some evaluation benchmarks exist for assessing robustness across languages, they often exclude low-resource languages and fail to reflect the full diversity of global users. Furthermore, the development of training datasets for low-resource languages, aimed at supporting the creation of multilingual safety guardrails, remains an underexplored area. Recent efforts have focused on curating multilingual datasets for high-resource languages by combining naturally occurring and machine-translated data. However, these efforts are insufficient for building truly inclusive safety systems. Another major challenge is the high computational cost of current safety systems, which are often too large, slow, and resource-intensive to deploy in edge devices or resource-constrained environments. Due to their prolonged inference times, these systems face significant hurdles for real-world implementation. This underscores the urgent need for more compact, efficient solutions capable of ensuring the safety of both LLMs and users across diverse settings.

TokenPowerBench is a lightweight, extensible, and reproducible framework designed to benchmark the power and energy consumption of LLM inference across various hardware configurations, model families, and workload patterns. Central to its design are energy-normalized inference metrics that facilitate meaningful comparison between inference systems. These include Joules per token, Joules per response, and instantaneous power draw, all of which are aligned with the internal execution phases of transformer-based LLMs (prefill and decode). This alignment enables precise attribution of energy costs to specific operations. Unlike traditional performance metrics such as latency or throughput, these energy metrics prioritize sustainability and cost-efficiency, which are increasingly critical in the context of modern AI infrastructure. Furthermore, TokenPowerBench employs a reproducible measurement methodology to account for the sensitivities of power measurement in LLM inference. The framework defines a three-level measurement model—ranging from GPU-only telemetry to full-system power monitoring using IPMI and rack-level PDUs—making it suitable for a wide range of environments, from user-space workstations to large institutional testbeds. This approach is complemented by a declarative configuration harness, ensuring repeatability across experiments. In addition, TokenPowerBench systematically explores the impact of key configuration dimensions such as batch size, context length, quantization format, and parallelism strategy on energy consumption. This scalability is critical, as LLM inference workloads vary significantly depending on system scale, model size, and deployment constraints. The framework supports configurations from single-GPU inference to multi-node distributed serving, capturing both component-level breakdowns and cluster-wide energy imbalances. As the landscape of LLM inference continues to evolve, TokenPowerBench is designed to adapt alongside it. Future expansions will extend support to additional GPU architectures, including the next generations of NVIDIA and AMD accelerators, as well as emerging AI chips and DPUs. Moreover, the benchmark will be refined to quantify the trade-off between inference accuracy and energy efficiency, offering users valuable insights into where energy savings may begin to compromise model performance.

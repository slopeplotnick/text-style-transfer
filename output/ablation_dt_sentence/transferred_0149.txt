The hybrid routing strategy proves critical: enforcing a single execution pathway degrades performance, especially when handling tasks that mix small and large-scale edits. The XML-only variant achieves an IF score of 0.95, while the python-pptx-only variant reaches 2.06. The hybrid approach without reprocessing scores 2.36, and enabling iterative refinement further boosts performance to 2.84 IF and 3.21 VQ. Case-specific analysis shows that providing slide images to PPTPilot improves layout reasoning: visual grounding enhances decisions around z-ordering, spacing, and alignment without degrading text-editing fidelity. Moreover, iterative refinement stabilizes complex edits, as one additional pass often resolves lingering formatting issues after re-theming or bulk changes, with minimal overhead. We introduce PPTArena and PPTPilot to establish a concrete and measurable framework for assessing PowerPoint editing capabilities in intelligent systems. PPTArena converts real slide decks, human-authored edit plans, and style-sensitive rubrics into a benchmark that measures instruction fidelity, structural grounding, and layout quality beyond surface-level metrics. PPTPilot demonstrates that an explicit plan-edit-verify loop significantly enhances reliability, outperforming both advanced proprietary tools and frontier vision-language models. Nonetheless, existing systems still struggle with complex, long-horizon, and mixed-modality tasks, indicating that robust and reliable PowerPoint editing remains an open challenge. We hope PPTArena and PPTPilot provide a foundation for future research on more capable and trustworthy slide editing agents.

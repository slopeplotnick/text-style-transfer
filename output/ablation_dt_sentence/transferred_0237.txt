Consequently, the relevance component plays a crucial role in the user experience, as presenting irrelevant products can erode trust and negatively impact the revenue of e-commerce platforms. As such, accurately modeling the relevance between user queries and candidate products is a fundamental aspect of the operational mechanisms of these platforms. Traditional methods, such as term matching and vector-based techniques, remain prevalent in ensuring effective search functionalities on online platforms. These approaches form the basis of relevance modeling by assessing the alignment between search queries and product offerings. Methods utilizing term-matching techniques, such as TF-IDF and BM25, are commonly used to determine the correspondence between search queries and products. However, these methods are limited in their ability to capture semantic relevance and contextual understanding. In contrast, vector-based approaches that employ models like BERT and its derivatives have made significant strides in capturing the semantic alignment between user queries and product representations, thereby improving relevance estimation. Nevertheless, two key challenges persist when applying vector-based methods to specialized domains. First, there is a lack of domain-specific hard examples. While most existing methods yield satisfactory results with moderate amounts of labeled data, improving performance typically requires a large number of hard examples, which are both difficult to acquire and complex to annotate accurately. Second, these models face limitations in reasoning and result presentation due to restrictions in their parameter size. As a result, lightweight online models struggle with handling complex reasoning tasks and effectively distinguishing relevant information. To address these challenges, large language models (LLMs) have been increasingly employed for relevance modeling tasks. However, many existing approaches primarily rely on the representation of the final token for relevance determination and use soft labels from the classification layer to distill knowledge into more compact models. This approach fails to provide clear insights into the features influencing relevance and does not significantly enhance the reasoning capabilities of smaller models.

The left part of Figure 2 shows the prompt used alongside an example of extracted triples for a single training image, illustrating how diverse image attributes are semantically structured. The output consists of multiple ontology-aligned triples that capture various aspects of the garment, including category (*depicts*), shape (*has_silhouette*), material (*has_material_type*), pattern (*has_pattern*), and other relationships defined by the Fashionpedia ontology. Each triple follows the format *(image_id, relation, attribute)*, providing a structured and interpretable representation of the image content. This structured representation enables downstream graph construction and conceptual comparison. To identify influential training examples, we compared the knowledge graph (KG) of each generated image with those of all training images by computing node-level overlap. For each prompt, we retained training images whose graphs shared the highest number of nodes with the generated image’s graph. The prompt used was: "Generate triples for the fashion item depicted in the image, according to the relationships specified within the Fashionpedia Ontology, located at \\({ONTOLOGY_PATH} = \). Focus exclusively on the fashion item depicted in the image with image_id: \\)([IMAGE_ID=)}, and generate ontology-aligned triples in the format (image_id, relationship, attribute_value) that semantically characterize the item, in accordance with the relational schema defined in the Fashionpedia Ontology." For example, the extracted triples for *58810.jpg* are: *58810.jpg, depicts, Shirt*; *58810.jpg, belongs_to_category, Upperbody*; *58810.jpg, has_nickname, None*; *58810.jpg, has_silhouette, Slim-fit*; *58810.jpg, has_waistline, Straight*; *58810.jpg, has_length, Long-sleeved*; *58810.jpg, has_collar_type, Classic*; *58810.jpg, has_lapel_type, Not Applicable*; *58810.jpg, has_neckline, Crew-neck*; *58810.jpg, has_sleeve_type, Long Sleeves with Cuffs*; *58810.jpg, has_pocket_type, Not Applicable*; *58810.jpg, has_opening_type, Button-front Closure*; *58810.jpg, has_material_type, Cotton-blend Fabric*; *58810.jpg, has_leather_type, None*; *58810.jpg, has_fabric_treatment, Not Applicable*; *58810.jpg, has_pattern, Plaid*; *58810.jpg, has_animal_pattern, None*. These retrieved examples were then used in the subsequent unlearning stage. Beyond retrieval, Neo4j supported global graph exploration—such as identifying the most frequent attributes and visualizing the concept distribution across the dataset. **Unlearning Procedure.** We define unlearning as the process of retraining the generative model after removing a selected subset of training samples. This allowed us to assess whether those samples influenced the generated outputs. We conducted 10 unlearning experiments using both the baseline and our proposed approach. In each case, we generated images for 15 prompts before and after unlearning. Consistent with the baseline setup, we removed a comparable proportion of the most influential training samples identified for each prompt.

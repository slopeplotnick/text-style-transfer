Recordings were reviewed to ensure technical clarity and adherence to task instructions. Approved recordings were then transcribed by trained annotators using the same platform. Only those recordings and transcripts that passed the final review were included in the dataset. Remuneration was adjusted based on language-specific requirements to account for varying engagement needs. This process helped ensure that the final dataset maintained high technical quality, included diverse speaker representation, and complied with established ethical guidelines. The prompts were unscripted and created by dedicated language teams to ensure cultural relevance and linguistic naturalness. Each team consisted of a language lead and typically three assistants, with all contributions peer-reviewed within the team before final approval was given by the lead. Domain paths were initiated via Microsoft Forms, through which language teams selected a general domain of knowledge to begin their work. From there, they narrowed the scope by choosing a specific topic within that domain, followed by selecting related subtopics. Teams were trained to design prompts that would elicit a wide range of responses. For example, a prompt such as “What is your favorite fruit, and why?” was encouraged over the simpler “What is your favorite fruit?” to allow for more varied outputs. Emphasis was placed on developing prompts capable of generating not only declarative responses, but also interrogative, exclamative, imperative, and other types of utterances. Moreover, teams were encouraged to adapt topics and subtopics as needed to enhance cultural relevance and ensure appropriateness. The overarching goal was to capture the national lexicon as comprehensively as possible by focusing on topics and subdomains deeply rooted in South African cultural and social life, thereby tailoring the data to the specific linguistic context.

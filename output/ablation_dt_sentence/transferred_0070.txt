We begin by establishing standard baselines, including Majority, Random, and Support Vector Machine (SVM), as our initial reference points. Additionally, we evaluate the performance of Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), and Decision Tree (DT), considering these as further baseline methods. We then fine-tune several transformer-based models, such as DistilBERT, BanglaBERT, mBERT, and XLM-RoBERTa, and provide a thorough evaluation of their performance. Moreover, we offer a detailed discussion of the modeling challenges and the inherent limitations of the dataset. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 reviews prior work and provides background on hate-speech detection in Bangla. Section 3 describes the methodology followed in this study. Section 4 discusses the implementation details. Section 5 presents the evaluation results. Section 6 outlines the challenges encountered during the study. Section 7 discusses the individual contributions. Finally, Section 8 concludes the paper. Social media has become an integral part of daily life, facilitating rapid communication, seamless information exchange, and the sharing of perspectives across geographical boundaries. However, this freedom of expression has also contributed to the proliferation of hate speech and harsh language on digital platforms. Unfortunately, little attention has been paid to detecting hate speech on Bangla social media, which suffers from a lack of high-quality datasets. Additionally, the language used on social media often differs significantly from that found in traditional print media, complicating the automatic detection of Bangla hate speech. Traditionally, hate speech detection has been approached as a supervised classification task. Early studies relied predominantly on lexicon-based and rule-driven approaches, which often failed to capture implicit, context-dependent expressions of hate. More recent studies, however, have turned to machine learning techniques, such as logistic regression and support vector machines (SVMs), to address these classification challenges. These traditional supervised learning methods have also been employed in the detection of online threats or harmful content.

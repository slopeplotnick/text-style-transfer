The component of the explanation model that is most challenging to specify is typically the structural equations of the FSCM. In Section 5.2, the results incorporate canonical PSCM intervals for counterfactual queries to illustrate what is lost when the underlying causal mechanisms cannot be identified. For certain queries, as evidenced by the results, the narrowness of the intervals suggests that a reasonable approximation of the true value may be inferred from either the interval mean or approximate queries using a CBN, as the specific model choice becomes less significant. However, this is not always the case. When conditioning on rare events, the counterfactual probability may vary between 0 and 1, depending on the model employed. Indeed, two distinct FSCMs that are both consistent with the data may yield contradictory explanations of the same model and may even assert these outcomes as definitive. This highlights the importance of rare observations, which can be critical for providing accurate explanations, especially in situations where training data is sparse, and predictions must be grounded in limited information. Thus, the implementation of an FSCM remains essential to ensure the accurate computation of any given query. When experts provide partial knowledge to define non-canonical PSCMs, this may reduce the size of the intervals, broadening the scope of queries that can be approximated. Future work may explore the efficacy of various methods in relation to both exact and approximate calculations. Although the explanations in this work are based on calculating the probability of the sufficiency of concept interventions, this does not limit the framework. Future research may extend this model to include probabilities of necessity, in addition to exploring alternative forms of explanations.

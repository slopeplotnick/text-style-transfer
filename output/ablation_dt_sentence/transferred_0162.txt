Both audio and visual modalities are utilized to determine which modality better conveys emotions within each sample. To this end, we first construct a high-quality human-annotated dataset (EMER) and an additional FAU-filtered dataset, which are then merged into a unified training set. Using this combined dataset, we compute the mutual information (MI) for each individual sample. Once the emotion-dominant modality is identified, the reasoning text is reorganized accordingly, as illustrated in Figure 3. Specifically, each training instance is decomposed into two modality-specific components: one corresponding to the audio and the other to the video. We employ special markers, <aud_desc> and <vis_desc>, to clearly delineate the audio-related and visual-related components of the reasoning text. These texts are then rearranged based on the MI values. If the audio modality exhibits greater emotional salience, the audio-based reasoning text is prioritized, followed by the video-based reasoning text. Conversely, if the video modality conveys more emotional significance, the video-based text is placed first. In cases where both modalities are equally informative, we generate two versions: one with the audio-first order and another with the video-first order. This approach allows the model to initiate its reasoning process based on the modality that carries the most emotionally salient input. Following this cold-start stage, the model's reasoning process is further refined through Modality-Aligned Reward Optimization. Previous approaches based on GRPO typically utilized two rewards: one for the accuracy of the answer and another for the quality of the reasoning structure. However, these rewards do not explicitly guide the identification of the modality that best captures emotional expression. To address this, we introduce two additional reward signals: one to incentivize adherence to the correct reasoning sequence and another to enhance the model’s ability to reason based on emotionally salient inputs.

Reasoning has become a widely adopted strategy for boosting LLM performance across a range of tasks. Initially introduced through prompting techniques such as Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022), which guide models to generate step-by-step reasoning before producing an answer, reasoning methods have since progressed toward reinforcement learning (RL)-based approaches that refine the generation of high-quality reasoning traces. In domains like mathematics and programming—where outputs can be objectively verified—RL has proven especially effective by using task correctness as a reward signal (Shao et al., 2024; Lambert et al., 2025). More recently, researchers have begun applying RL to domains where ground-truth labels are unavailable or ambiguous, such as narrative generation and understanding (Ahuja et al., 2025; Gurung & Lapata, 2025). However, these gains often come at the cost of increased computation time and expense, as models are required to engage in longer and more complex reasoning processes (Shen et al., 2025a). Consequently, recent efforts have focused on improving the efficiency of reasoning, both during training and inference (Sui et al., 2025). One emerging direction is latent reasoning, which involves generating condensed, encoded reasoning traces rather than fully articulated token sequences. This approach is motivated by the observation that many lexical tokens contribute little to the final answer and that human reasoning frequently occurs without explicit verbalization. By preserving thoughts in a continuous, non-linguistic representation, models can retain more information and avoid the lossy compression that occurs when encoding reasoning into discrete tokens. For instance, Hao et al. (2025) demonstrate that such representations can integrate multiple reasoning steps simultaneously, effectively allowing the model to tackle several aspects of a problem in parallel. In this work, we introduce LiteReason, a latent reasoning framework designed for narrative understanding and generation tasks, where both inputs and outputs are often lengthy. LiteReason augments a base LLM with a Reasoning Projector module, which enables the model to produce both standard text and encoded reasoning traces. This latent reasoning mode is activated by a designated start-of-thought symbol that prompts the model to enter its internal reasoning process (see Figure 1 for a schematic illustration). Following pretraining of the Reasoning Projector, we apply RL-based fine-tuning to the base model.

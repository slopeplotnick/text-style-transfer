The prior-conforming prompt reinforces a belief aligned with the model's likely initial stance, serving both as a sense check and as a potential lower bound for belief entrenchment and accuracy. In contrast, the critical-thinking prompt encourages openness to counter-evidence and rational belief revision. All prompts used in the experiments are provided in Appendix C. This section presents our empirical findings. First, we establish an association between belief entrenchment (as measured by the Martingale Score) and drops in ground-truth accuracy, which supports the notion that belief entrenchment is detrimental to accuracy. We then benchmark a range of closed- and open-weights models across different problem domains and setups, attributing belief entrenchment causally to various factors such as models, prompts, and reasoning techniques. Moreover, we confirm the validity of using LLMs as evaluators in this context. Notably, we observe more severe belief entrenchment in value-laden domains such as r/ChangeMyView, where the Martingale Score is consistently higher compared to Forecasting, suggesting that belief entrenchment is a greater concern in domains where LLM performance cannot be easily evaluated against ground truth. We also manipulate the degree of belief entrenchment using both critical-thinking and prior-conforming prompts. In Forecasting, we find belief entrenchment to be more severe under the prior-conforming prompt and less so under the critical-thinking prompt, though this pattern does not hold in r/ChangeMyView or OpenReview. Further discussion on factors influencing belief entrenchment can be found in Appendix B. Since the ultimate goal of reasoning in LLMs is to arrive at true beliefs, we demonstrate that belief entrenchment undermines this objective. We do this within domains where ground-truth labels are available, such as Forecasting and OpenReview. While outcome-based metrics like the Brier Score gauge model performance, they provide limited insight into the reasons behind this performance. Consequently, we also employ process-based metrics to assess reasoning. Our findings reveal a positive correlation between higher Martingale Scores (indicating increased belief entrenchment) and poorer Brier Scores in Forecasting, suggesting that belief entrenchment plays a key role in the failure of LLM reasoning.

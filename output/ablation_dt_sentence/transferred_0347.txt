To optimize reasoning steps and improve the planning process, which requires extensive reasoning over the Story-Information and the generation of a long-form response, Gurung and Lapata (2025) propose VR-CLI (Verifiable Rewards via Completion Likelihood Improvement), a proxy reward formulation. The VR-CLI objective aims to increase the probability of generating the correct next chapter, conditioned on both the Story-Information and the underlying plan. To further refine VR-CLI, we introduce a contrastive term that negatively impacts the likelihood of selecting a randomly chosen chapter from the same book. This leads to our standard method, LiteReason, where we periodically (once per epoch) perform a small supervised fine-tuning (SFT) step using the trajectories generated during that epoch. Although this introduces some computational overhead, it facilitates the update of the reasoning-projector with the model's current reasoning state. Additionally, we incorporate an implicit thought prompt (as shown in Tables 12 and 13) to encourage the model’s use of implicit thought tags. In LiteReason w/o SFT, we retain the implicit prompt and the initialized Reasoning Projector, which relies on the implicit thought tags to determine when to inject new reasoning embeddings into the model. However, this version does not perform SFT during reinforcement learning (RL) training, leaving the Reasoning Projector unadapted. In LiteReason w/o Reasoning Projector, we assess post-RL performance without the Reasoning Projector, while still using the implicit thought prompt. This variant does not utilize latent reasoning, instead testing whether omitting intermediate reasoning steps impacts the model’s behavior. Drawing on recent advances in latent reasoning, we evaluate a selection of prior methodologies in light of the design assumptions discussed in Section 3. These methods are tested on both the Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks. For training-free latent reasoning, we compare two recent approaches: Mixture of Inputs (MoI; Su et al., 2025), which integrates the sampled token's embedding with those from the remaining token distribution, and Soft Thinking (Zhang et al., 2025), which also combines token embeddings but uses an entropy metric to determine when to transition from continuous thinking to discrete sampling.

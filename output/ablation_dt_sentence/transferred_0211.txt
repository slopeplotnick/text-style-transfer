Stage 3: Instruction-specific fine-tuning. The third stage focuses on specialized instruction-following capabilities across the diverse task categories in Infinity-MM. In this phase, we train on the full dataset, which includes document understanding, chart reasoning, OCR-centric tasks, and multi-turn conversations. Following the Infinity-MM recipe, we employ a task-specific mixture weighting scheme, allocating 35% to general visual question answering (VQA), 25% to document understanding, 20% to chart reasoning, 15% to OCR tasks, and 5% to multi-turn and specialized domains. The learning rate is set to 1e-5, and training is conducted for one epoch with an effective batch size of 512. The training objective revolves around standard autoregressive language modeling, specifically next-token prediction. To evaluate our NPU-native architecture, we compare its performance against state-of-the-art vision-language models across five distinct multimodal benchmarks. This comparison includes models from different architectural families and parameter scales, such as InternVL2 (1B and 2B variants), optimized for GPU deployment, and Qwen2-VL (2B and 3.75B variants), which demonstrates robust general-purpose multimodal capabilities. The AutoNeural model, trained through Stage 3 of the Infinity-MM curriculum, achieves the optimal balance between accuracy and training efficiency. To assess its performance, we evaluate it across five benchmarks targeting distinct multimodal capabilities. MMStar measures vision-language reasoning through multiple-choice visual questions that require multi-step inference and commonsense knowledge. HallusionBench evaluates the model’s ability to detect language hallucinations and visual illusions, testing its robustness in both domains. MathVista_MINI focuses on mathematical reasoning over diagrams, charts, and geometric figures, assessing the model’s ability to extract quantitative data and perform numerical computations. AI2D_TEST evaluates diagram understanding and scientific reasoning using annotated illustrations from textbooks and educational materials. Finally, OCRBench tests scene text recognition and document understanding across a wide range of text-in-image scenarios, including natural scenes, documents, and signage. We report accuracy for each benchmark and compute the average score across all tasks to measure overall multimodal proficiency.

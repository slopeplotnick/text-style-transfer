The Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon marks the threshold beyond which the assumptions of separability and reversibility no longer hold. Beyond this point, safety must be considered as a property of the entire networked system: its ability to sustain coherence, interpretability, and responsiveness amidst ongoing interaction. Achieving this perspective will require the development of new experimental frameworks, datasets, and metrics designed to track collective behavior over time. Furthermore, it will necessitate the creation of governance architectures that function at the same systemic scale, a theme explored further in the next section, where we introduce the concept of Institutional AI. This framework embeds oversight and norm-maintenance directly within multi-agent systems, addressing the need for governance structures that evolve alongside increasingly complex LLM ecosystems. Once models begin to act as semi-autonomous agents—reasoning, negotiating, and coordinating without continuous human supervision—traditional mechanisms such as centralized oversight, rigid safety protocols, or periodic audits become less effective. These tools rely on assumptions of stability, observability, and clear accountability structures, which are increasingly absent in dynamic, multi-agent environments. Thus, we propose Institutional AI as a novel approach to system-level safety, in which the rules, checks, and evaluative procedures are intrinsic to the system's architecture rather than imposed externally. The goal is not to replicate human institutions but to incorporate certain functional attributes—such as distributed authority, procedural transparency, and adaptive rule revision—within networks of interacting models. The term *constitutional AI* refers to models guided by a predefined set of normative rules or principles established during training or fine-tuning. While such constitutions can enhance individual model alignment and ensure consistency in isolated interactions, they fall short of addressing the larger risks that arise when systems interact and co-evolve.

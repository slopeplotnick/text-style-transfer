The reliability of language model judgments is often enhanced when the model is prompted to articulate intermediate reasoning steps prior to delivering a final decision, a technique known as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. This approach encourages the model to provide a rationale for its decision-making process, thereby improving both the accuracy and interpretability of its judgments. Our study integrates these cognitive steps and explores the optimal methods for aggregating the resulting outputs. In the context of computational resource allocation, several strategies can be employed, each influencing the decision-making process in unique ways. When handling multiple samples, it is crucial to implement an effective aggregation strategy. Self-Consistency (SC) (Wang et al., 2023b) uses majority voting to combine distinct outputs, ensuring a more reliable outcome. A variant of this, Soft Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2024), incorporates confidence scores and selects based on the confidence levels within different categories. Confidence-Informed Self-Consistency (CI-SC) applies a weighted majority voting mechanism, where the weights are determined by confidence scores, which are computed either as the length-normalized probability of the sequence or by prompting the model to generate predictions. In addition, some approaches leverage the model's intrinsic capabilities for result integration. Generative Self-Aggregation (GSA) (Li et al., 2025) prompts the model to generate a novel response by synthesizing information from multiple samples within the given context. Similarly, Universal Self-Consistency (USC) employs the model to select the most consistent response from a set of candidate answers, optimizing the reliability of the output. Research has shown that leveraging computational resources during testing, particularly through majority voting, can significantly improve the performance of reasoning-based generative verifiers.

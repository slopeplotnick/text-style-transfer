In this section, we introduce MIGR, a method that identifies which modality to prioritize first based on mutual information (MI). This approach is designed to mitigate biases in text during the initial reasoning phase, ensuring that early reasoning is grounded in the modality with the highest emotional relevance. Our method comprises two learning stages. First, we present modality-aligned SFT, a technique that guides the model to initiate its reasoning from the modality deemed emotionally dominant according to the MI label. This alignment enables the model to begin reasoning with the modality carrying the most salient emotional signals. Second, we introduce Modality-Aware Reward Optimization, which reinforces the selection of the appropriate modality and maintains emotion-grounded reasoning while also enhancing training stability. To accomplish this, we propose two complementary reward signals: a modality-aligned order reward that promotes correct modality prioritization during input processing, and a modality-grounded reasoning reward that encourages reasoning aligned with emotionally salient cues. The following sections elaborate on each training stage. Recent studies [15] have shown that SFT is effective as a cold-start method for emotion understanding, demonstrating that even limited high-quality supervision from the Explainable Multimodal Emotion Reasoning (EMER) dataset [27] can lead to substantial performance improvements. Building on this insight, we adopt a similar cold-start strategy to initialize training. While EMER [27] provides valuable supervision, we further enhance the SFT stage by incorporating samples that exhibit particularly clear and consistent emotional cues. To this end, we augment the training dataset with additional emotion-consistent samples. Specifically, we extract Facial Action Units (FAUs) from the facial frames of each video, as they are strongly correlated with emotional expressions [43]. We then assess the alignment of these FAU patterns with the target emotion using a predefined FAU-emotion mapping table [11]. Only samples with FAU patterns that exactly match the target emotion are retained. For these filtered instances, we construct reasoning-answer pairs informed by FAU-consistent emotional evidence to guide the reasoning process.

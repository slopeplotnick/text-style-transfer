The ability to animate virtual characters with life-like motions is crucial for enhancing the realism of games and virtual reality environments, significantly improving the immersive experience and fostering greater engagement and interaction. This capability is equally important in robotics, where naturalistic behaviors not only enhance safety but also improve energy efficiency and allow robots to learn versatile skills from human movement data. To achieve realistic character motion, data-driven approaches have become a key method, leveraging large motion datasets to synthesize life-like behaviors. Kinematics-based models, such as autoregressive models, variational autoencoders (VAEs), and diffusion models, are trained on these datasets to produce plausible animations. However, these approaches often fail to incorporate physical constraints, which can result in unrealistic behaviors, particularly in new or complex scenarios. In contrast, physics-based methods focus on creating motion that adheres to the principles of real-world physics by employing dynamic equations and control policies. These methods, typically realized through trajectory optimization frameworks or reinforcement learning (RL), ensure that movements are physically consistent. Earlier, data-free methods based on heuristics, such as SIMBICON, could generate functional but unnatural movements. Designing heuristics that capture the full expressiveness of human motion remains a significant challenge. Therefore, recent advancements have shifted toward data-driven approaches, including model predictive control (MPC) and trajectory optimization, which have shown greater promise in replicating natural human motion. Furthermore, reinforcement learning-based techniques have enhanced the realism and agility of robotic movement by enabling controllers to effectively mimic a broad range of reference motions. Nevertheless, these tracking-based methods are often limited by the necessity to follow a given motion clip, which can hinder the ability to generalize to novel tasks. To address this limitation, many systems now incorporate task-specific motion planners or high-level control policies, which allow the robot to select appropriate actions or motor skills tailored to the specific task at hand.

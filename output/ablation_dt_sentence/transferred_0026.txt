Our findings are consistent with those of Shirafuji et al. (2025), who employed a task vector-based approach and similarly observed favorable performance outcomes from the debiased model. In contrast, some existing debiasing studies based on model merging (Lutz et al., 2024) have demonstrated promising results for debiased LLMs on downstream tasks, as measured by NLI benchmark scores. However, our results underscore the need for methods evaluated solely on NLI to be scrutinized more comprehensively across a wider range of datasets. SuperGLUE evaluations reveal that, with the exception of Linear, SLERP, and Nearswap, most model-merging techniques substantially degrade the causal reasoning capabilities of large language models (see Section 5.2). Consequently, these methods demonstrate limited reliability for bias mitigation. SLERP stands out due to its balanced interpolation across the parameter space, which effectively incorporates the bias inverse vector without disproportionately amplifying it. In contrast, NuSLERP—an alternative interpolation method—applies normalization at the layer or tensor level, a design choice that significantly undermines its SuperGLUE performance. These findings suggest that, unlike SLERP, the majority of recent model-merging techniques are unsuitable for bias mitigation without incurring substantial degradation in reasoning performance. This work presents the first comprehensive investigation into how seven model-merging algorithms affect the manifestation of social bias in large language models. By evaluating 13 models across the GPT, LLaMA, and Qwen families on three social bias datasets and the SuperGLUE benchmark, we reveal a trade-off between fairness and utility. Moreover, four methods—Karcher Mean, NuSLERP, TIES, and DELLA—consistently impaired performance on tasks involving reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning, such as ReCoRD, COPA, CB, and BoolQ within SuperGLUE. While some prior debiasing studies based on model merging reported favorable downstream performance using NLI scores, our results demonstrate the importance of also verifying accuracy on tasks requiring more complex reasoning abilities.

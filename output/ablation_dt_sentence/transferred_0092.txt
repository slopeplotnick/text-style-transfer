To identify the most effective configuration, we explored multiple architectural variations. Our architecture follows a "CCDGDG" structure across stages 1 through 6, where C corresponds to Conv, D denotes DDSM+FNN, and G represents Gated-Conv blocks. In addition to this, we evaluated hybrid configurations combining two block types (e.g., CCGGGG and CCDDD), as well as monomodal configurations consisting solely of a single block type. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed hybrid design outperforms the alternative approaches, thus validating the effectiveness of the adopted methodology. Even in cases where joint positions are occluded or difficult to observe, our approach reliably estimates them, showcasing its robustness in real-world environments. We introduce DF-Mamba, a novel architecture for 3D hand pose estimation that leverages efficient convolutional feature extraction in the early stages and incorporates a deformable state-space representation in the higher layers. We evaluated the method across five distinct datasets, each encompassing various hand interactions and data types, and the results show that our approach consistently outperforms alternative configurations, exhibiting superior performance and efficiency. Looking ahead, future research could focus on extending DF-Mamba to additional tasks, such as 3D human pose estimation, by pre-training it on extensive external datasets. Moreover, its applicability to recent advancements in hand-mesh reconstruction decoders could be explored, along with its potential as a foundational model capable of generalizing across diverse scenarios without the need for task-specific fine-tuning.

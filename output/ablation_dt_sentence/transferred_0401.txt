For each of the three metrics, we compute the value for all unordered pairs of methods within the HD paradigm, within the FV paradigm, and across both paradigms. The average is then computed for each group, and the resulting scores are presented in Table 2. These scores provide quantitative evidence that the HD and FV paradigms possess complementary strengths. Notably, the higher ACS for cross-paradigm pairings suggests that these paradigms excel on distinct subsets of the problem space, with successes being more mutually exclusive than those within the same paradigm. Similarly, the superior ASG score indicates that combining HD and FV methods offers the greatest performance improvement, surpassing any intra-paradigm combination. Most importantly, the AECR shows a significant increase for cross-paradigm pairings, implying that when one paradigm errs, the other is considerably more likely to provide the correct response. This analysis supports the view that HD and FV are not merely different, but genuinely complementary, providing a strong empirical foundation for developing hybrid models that integrate both paradigms. While the previous section established the statistical complementarity between HD and FV, this section investigates the underlying causes of their divergent performance. Through a detailed case study, we examine specific scenarios where each paradigm tends to fail. We identify two primary failure patterns: Fact Verification, constrained by its reliance on retrieval quality, is prone to errors when relevant evidence is unavailable; conversely, Hallucination Detection is susceptible to false alarms due to semantic flexibility, where diverse phrasing is misinterpreted as hallucination. By analyzing these patterns, we gain insights into the synergy between the paradigms, which motivates the hybrid integration methods discussed in the subsequent section.

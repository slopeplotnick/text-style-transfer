This research investigates the application of robotic systems within construction environments, emphasizing their potential to mitigate labor shortages while enhancing safety and productivity outcomes. To validate its effectiveness, the authors systematically evaluate the system by comparing it with established methods through extensive testing. Building upon established principles of probabilistic robot modeling and domain-specific construction constraints, this study presents the first comprehensive performance comparison between LLM-based reasoning and traditional task allocation methodologies within the context of construction robotics. The LTAA system integrates capability-aware models, phase-adaptive allocation strategies, and LLM-driven decision-making mechanisms, all designed to address construction-specific coordination challenges identified in prior research. Furthermore, the system is evaluated against traditional algorithmic techniques, such as Brute Force and Greedy Method, as well as reinforcement learning approaches, including Q-learning and Deep Q-Networks. This study contributes a methodologically rigorous LLM coordination framework and provides empirical insights into the comparative effectiveness of reasoning-based versus traditional approaches for automating construction tasks. The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 offers a comprehensive review of the literature on multi-robot task allocation, focusing on optimization, learning-based, and LLM-driven approaches. Section 3 details the LTAA system’s methodology. Section 4 describes the experimental setup, outlining the procedures and configurations employed to evaluate the proposed framework. Section 5 presents a comparative analysis of the results, situating the proposed approach within the context of prior work. Finally, Section 6 concludes with a summary of the findings and potential future research directions. Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) serves as the foundational theory for coordinating robotic teams, emphasizing systematic task distribution while accounting for resource limitations, temporal constraints, and the heterogeneity of robot capabilities. Gerkey and Matarić (2004) established a foundational framework for understanding task allocation challenges, addressing key dimensions such as the distinction between single-task and multi-task robots, the assignment of tasks to individual robots versus multiple robots, and the differentiation between instantaneous and time-extended assignments. However, the system did not account for the full range of requirements inherent in real-world scenarios, where diverse robot capabilities and temporal dependencies are present. In response, Korsah et al. (2013) proposed an advanced system that incorporated heterogeneous robot skillsets, task dependencies, and dynamic constraints, marking a significant step toward utilizing diverse teams for complex task execution.

The classification comprises five principal categories, which are further subdivided into sixteen distinct edit types, ranging from basic text formatting to intricate editing workflows. Following the framework outlined by Maia Polo et al., PPTArena emphasizes a focused subset of more complex scenarios, such as replacing textual content or resolving tasks that require multiple interdependent edits across visual and linguistic modalities. For instance, aligning images with their corresponding captions across slides necessitates the integration of both visual layout and textual cues. Notably, some scenarios span multiple categories, yielding over 100 total combinations. Unlike prior benchmarks such as PPTC-R and T2US—which prioritize quick edits constrained by predefined templates—PPTArena targets higher-complexity, real-world editing cases that reveal systemic limitations and failure modes. It supports both individually created and Python-generated decks, enforces accessibility standards, and enables precise, platform-independent evaluations. One representative task involves aligning each image with its respective caption, balancing the visual layout, standardizing slide titles, and enforcing uniform image dimensions (3.2 inches wide by 2.4 inches high). These tasks are intentionally designed to test systems’ capabilities in multi-step reasoning, cross-slide information integration, and coherent planning. This setup exposes failure cases that remain hidden in simpler benchmarks, serving as a more rigorous testbed for advanced editing agents. Evaluation focuses on two primary criteria: instruction adherence and visual outcome quality. Each system response is rated by expert annotators on a 0–5 scale, where 0 denotes complete failure and 5 indicates a fully satisfactory result.

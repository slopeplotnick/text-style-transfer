In aggregate, progress-adjusted thresholds enable consistent acceleration, with the selection of the schedule depending on the acceptable error margin. When fidelity is paramount, more conservative configurations are preferred, despite the trade-off in processing speed. Conversely, faster schedules are more suitable for applications where minor errors are permissible or when speed is prioritized, a trade-off effectively captured by the quality-versus-speed metric. In this study, we introduce SchED, a training-free, architecture-agnostic method for accelerating diffusion language models that employs a progressive, smooth threshold to compare sequence-level confidence. This dynamic threshold, coupled with the model's prediction stabilization, reduces denoising steps consistently across both Dream and LLaDA models, particularly benefiting instruction-tuned variants where uncertainty decays rapidly, all while maintaining strong performance, as supported by the entropy analysis and the quality-versus-speed trade-off. SchED seamlessly integrates with existing schedules, preserving the integrity of the underlying models. The resulting schedules span a range from more conservative configurations, suited for high fidelity, to faster ones, optimized for latency-sensitive or fault-tolerant tasks. Future research could explore optimal schedule parameter settings, task-specific adaptation of aggregation methods, domain-specific threshold designs, and integration with speculative or cache-based denoising strategies. Ultimately, conceptualizing diffusion decoding as a stopping-time problem offers a straightforward and robust framework for deploying diffusion models under accuracy and efficiency constraints. While SchED accelerates diffusion decoding in a training-free manner, it operates only during inference and treats the schedule as an external control signal. Our study does not address joint optimization of model training and schedule parameters, advanced result aggregation methods, or integration with other acceleration techniques, such as speculative or cache-based denoising, all of which present avenues for further investigation.

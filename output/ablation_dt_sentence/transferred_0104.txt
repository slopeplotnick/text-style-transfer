We deconstructed the relevance task and identified the requisite model capabilities based on this analysis, offering clear and comprehensive recommendations for the development of future relevance models in the industry. We propose a complete methodology and corresponding set of practices that encompass the end-to-end pipeline—from model selection and configuration to training, evaluation, and capability verification—thereby establishing a systematic foundation for subsequent development and assessment. During the development of LORE, we examined a range of training strategies, highlighting several key approaches that may benefit fine-tuning language models for vertical domains. These findings are summarized as follows: 1. Data scaling law shows diminishing returns; model performance improves rapidly at first but plateaus as additional data is incorporated. 2. Providing stable and relevant information, even when somewhat redundant, consistently enhances model performance. 3. Concise prompts with essential information yield the best results; extended, information-dense prompts do not consistently improve performance, while prompts lacking key details hinder effectiveness. 4. Naive teacher chain-of-thought (CoT) distillation results in negative effects, underperforming compared to basic training due to data distribution shifts. 5. Curriculum learning remains effective, with structured learning strategies based on graded difficulty significantly outperforming random ordering. 6. Long CoT chains are not necessary for better performance, as no significant increase in output length was observed during reinforcement learning (RL), suggesting that long CoT are merely a byproduct of model capability improvement. 7. Entropy collapse rate imposes an upper bound on model performance; in the early stages of training, the model rapidly exchanges entropy for measurable performance gains, but as entropy decreases, exploration capability becomes constrained, reducing further improvements. 8. Strategic approaches can effectively slow entropy collapse, with optimization methods such as clip-higher achieving superior performance.

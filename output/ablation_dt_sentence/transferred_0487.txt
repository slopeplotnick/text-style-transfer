The globalization of education and the rapid expansion of online learning have necessitated the adaptation of educational content to different cultural and linguistic contexts, making localization a critical challenge. As lecture materials are inherently multimodal, combining spoken audio with visual slides, systems must be capable of processing both input modalities effectively. To facilitate an accessible and comprehensive learning experience, translations must preserve all modalities: text for reading, slides for visual engagement, and speech for auditory learning. In response, we developed BOOM, a sophisticated multimodal multilingual lecture companion capable of producing synchronized outputs across three distinct modalities: translated text, slides with preserved visual integrity, and synthesized speech. This approach enables students to access lectures in their native language while preserving the original content's integrity. Notably, our experiments show that incorporating slide-aware transcripts further enhances downstream tasks like summarization and question answering. We have released our Slide Translation code at https://github.com/saikoneru/image-Translator and integrated it into the Lecture Translator at https://gitlab.kit.edu/kit/isl-ai41t/lt-middleware/ltpipeline<sup>1</sup>. Providing educational content in learners' native languages significantly enhances comprehension and engagement, thus boosting the learning process's effectiveness. By adapting lecture materials to accommodate multiple languages, we can mitigate communication barriers, improve accessibility, and facilitate the understanding of complex concepts. As institutions become more globalized, the ability to provide multilingual lecture content, both in-person and online, is essential to increase accessibility to educational resources. With the growing digitalization of teaching, lecture content is intrinsically multimodal, integrating audio, text, and visual elements. The primary modality is the lecture audio, which can be converted into text via Automatic Speech Recognition. Furthermore, instructional material is conveyed through slides, and additional elements like summaries, chapters, and question-answer interactions can be derived using advanced systems powered by Large Language Models. Ensuring these outputs are available in multiple languages, even to non-native speakers, is crucial for accessibility. Therefore, effective localization must adeptly manage this diverse range of content, rendering lecture translation a truly complex, multimodal challenge.

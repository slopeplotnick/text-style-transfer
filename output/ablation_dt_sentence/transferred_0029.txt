When evaluating AI-generated content, what factors do individuals consider? Previous studies, such as those by Hong et al. (2022) and Moura and Maw (2021), have relied on rating scales from related domains; however, these measures may not fully capture the nuanced ways in which individuals perceive and assess AI-generated music. To address this gap, the present study adopts an exploratory approach, aiming to uncover the subjective criteria and themes underlying participants' judgments through open-ended responses. The stimuli for this study were generated using the AI tools Stable Audio (v.2.0) and Suno (v.3.5). In order to ensure ecological validity, prompts were developed based on the Emotion-to-Music Atlas (EMMA; Strauss et al., 2024), a comprehensive resource that annotates human emotional responses to music excerpts from diverse genres. Specifically, the study utilized GEMS-9 annotations (Zentner et al., 2008) for 453 music excerpts from the Music4All-Onion database (Moscati et al., 2022). These excerpts were drawn from five major genres, each with three distinct subgenres: (1) Electronic (Trance, Downtempo, Dubstep), (2) Jazz (Bossa-Nova, Smooth-Jazz, Swing), (3) Pop (Europop, Indie-Pop, Pop-Punk), (4) Rock (Soft-Rock, Rock Ballad, Doom-Metal), and (5) Soul/Funk (Funk, Disco, Soul). For each subgenre, prompts were carefully constructed based on the prominent emotional dimensions, as determined by the mean values across all songs in the subgenre, using the EMMA and their corresponding GEMS-9 descriptors (e.g., "a joyful, bouncy, animated trance song" for the Joyful Activation dimension in the Trance subgenre). This procedure resulted in 2-3 prompts per subgenre, generating a total of 38 distinct prompts. These prompts were then adapted for use in Suno and Stable Audio (see Supplements A for the full prompt list), resulting in a final set of 76 stimuli. To ensure compatibility with prior research (e.g., Strauss et al., 2024; Warrenburg, 2020), specific modifications were made: For Suno, prompts were generated in instrumental mode, with one of two outputs randomly selected and trimmed to a duration of 45-60 seconds. For Stable Audio, the term "instrumental" was added to each prompt, and the target duration was fixed at 60 seconds. Further details on the generation and editing process, as well as the audio files, are provided in the supplementary materials.

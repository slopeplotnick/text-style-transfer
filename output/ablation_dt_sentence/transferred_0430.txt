We utilize three multimodal emotion datasets to train our model and validate its effectiveness. EMER [27], which offers high-quality human-verified reasoning-label pairs, serves as the foundation for cold-start SFT. In addition, DFEW [7] and MAFW [6] are large-scale benchmarks that encompass diverse facial expressions across video, audio, and text modalities. More detailed descriptions of these datasets' composition and statistics are available in Appendix 7.1. Consistent with prior research [11, 16], we report UAR and WAR for recognition accuracy, along with three complementary metrics—EEA, EPC, and FCR—to evaluate the emotional coherence of generated reasoning. Together, these metrics assess how well the explanations align with the target emotion and the model's prediction. Appendix 7.2 provides formal definitions and annotation protocols. In line with the design principles of R1-Omni [15], our framework includes SigLIP [45] as the vision encoder, Whisper-large-v3 [46] as the audio encoder, and BERT [47] as the text encoder. Each modality output is projected into the LLM embedding space using corresponding visual and audio projectors, implemented as two linear layers to match the dimensionality of the LLM representation. Notably, a 1-second audio clip yields approximately 50 audio features, whereas a single video frame produces 729 visual features. The LLM backbone employed is Qwen2.5-7B [48], with model weights initialized from the HumanOmni [30] checkpoint to maintain pretrained multimodal representations. To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed framework, MIGR is compared with existing emotion understanding methods on the MAFW and DFEW datasets (Table 1). Previous methods are categorized into two groups: (i) Non-Reasoning Models that directly predict emotion labels, and (ii) Reasoning-Generating Models that produce textual explanations before making a prediction. Initially, we compare MIGR with other reasoning-generating models using consistency metrics to validate that the reasoning texts generated by our model are emotionally coherent. As demonstrated in Table 1, MIGR consistently outperforms ERV in all three consistency measures.

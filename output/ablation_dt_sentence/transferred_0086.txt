We evaluate seven distinct approaches for model combination—Linear, Karcher Mean, SLERP, NuSLERP, TIES, DELLA, and Nearswap—each offering unique mechanisms for integrating a bias-inverted model with a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM). Among these, Linear, introduced by Wortsman et al. (2022), stands out as the simplest technique, combining and averaging the weights from fine-tuned models in a computationally efficient manner while maintaining strong performance. In contrast, the Karcher Mean, proposed by Goddard et al. (2024), operates within the Riemannian manifold of model parameters, thereby enabling aggregation in non-Euclidean spaces. Similarly, SLERP, also introduced by Goddard (2024), interpolates weights along a curved trajectory on the unit hypersphere, preserving geometric consistency throughout the process. Building on this, NuSLERP extends SLERP by enabling differential mixing across model segments, prioritizing critical components to enhance performance and maintain architectural balance. Moreover, TIES, proposed by Yadav (2023), identifies task-relevant parameter differences to integrate models while minimizing interference and preserving distinct capabilities. DELLA, introduced by Deep et al. (2024), further addresses interference by pruning less impactful updates through an intelligent mechanism that safeguards essential components, ensuring the retention of the base model’s effectiveness.

In analyzing visual data, it is crucial to consider image features, such as occluded joints, and the global context, encompassing cues from inter-joints, inter-hand interactions, hand-object interactions, and the broader scene. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are widely employed as backbone architectures for 3D hand pose estimation (HPE), with ResNet-50 being a particularly popular choice. These CNNs leverage convolution operations with a local receptive field, achieving a favorable balance between precision and computational efficiency. However, their local convolutions exhibit limitations in capturing the broader contextual information. Recent studies have started using vision transformers (ViT) as feature extractors for hand mesh reconstruction, yet their higher computational complexity often becomes a bottleneck in practical applications. This indicates significant room for improving backbone architectures to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of hand pose interpretation. As an emerging foundational architecture, Mamba, originally developed for natural language processing, has received considerable attention due to its proficiency in selectively attending to salient input elements, efficiently capturing the global context from long token sequences. Several recent endeavors have adapted this model for image-based tasks. For instance, VisionMamba introduced the Vim block, which employs a distinct scanning methodology for spatially-aware sequence modeling. However, these approaches utilize a fixed grid configuration, which constrains their ability to accurately capture complex variations in 3D hand pose dynamics, as shown in Figure 1(a). To address this limitation, we propose a novel framework, Deformable Mamba (DF-Mamba), which incorporates deformable state space modeling designed to enhance visual feature extraction in 3D HPE. DF-Mamba operates by dynamically modeling both local features and the global context through flexible state spaces for comprehensive feature extraction. Our system's blocks aggregate local features along a deformable path and selectively retain pertinent cues to effectively encapsulate the global context. The scanning path is modified through deformable point sampling, leveraging local anchors and adjustable offsets dependent on the input features, as illustrated in Figure 1(b).

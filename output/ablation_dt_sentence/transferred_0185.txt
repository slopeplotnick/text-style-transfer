Fears are concerns—subjective negative feelings about migration—that must be addressed to reassure the involved actors. They represent perceived undesirable consequences that may arise from the change. When responding to the first two questions focused on benefits, interviewees concurrently revealed fears stemming from the risks they had identified. Two key questions that indirectly facilitated the identification of these fears were: - In your opinion, does this approach contribute to the current processes within the company? - What do you think about a platform designed to rapidly develop similar software? We observed that the primary concerns among IT professionals were that the technology might be ineffective, overly complex, or met with resistance to change (see Table 7). Ultimately, participants—regardless of their role—requested training and demonstrations. They expressed a desire to better evaluate the usefulness of an SPL in their processes and to gain a clearer understanding of the transition to this new approach. Drawing upon the identified best practices, benefits, risks, and fears, we present a set of guidelines intended to support the migration to a Software Product Line (SPL). These guidelines are grounded in the discussions and decisions that emerged within the company. In the following sections, we outline the expected results (Sect. 5.1), how to apply current best practices (Sect. 5.2), how to encourage future benefits (Sect. 5.3), how to mitigate risks (Sect. 5.4), how to alleviate fears (Sect. 5.5), the concrete steps to be taken (Sect. 5.6), and finally, a discussion (Sect. 5.7). Before initiating the interviews, we considered the potential outcomes. We anticipated that individuals familiar with or inclined toward reusability would exhibit a more positive and engaged attitude. These individuals were expected to promote and facilitate project implementation. In our findings, more experienced developers predominantly emphasized mindset-related aspects, while less experienced ones focused more on technical concerns. However, this difference in experience did not significantly affect the quantity or quality of the responses provided.

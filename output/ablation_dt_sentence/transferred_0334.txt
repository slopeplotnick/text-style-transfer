The experimental results presented in Table 1 validate the efficacy of integrating model-centric and data-centric paradigms. A comprehensive examination of the "Unify" block, when compared to individual baselines, reveals that hybrid strategies consistently establish a new upper bound for performance across diverse datasets and model architectures. This consistent superiority confirms that internal uncertainty signals and external evidence retrieval operate in orthogonal feature spaces. Specifically, the Score-Level Fusion approach demonstrates that a simple linear combination can effectively mitigate the high variance inherent in single-paradigm methods. By aggregating divergent signals, this strategy smoothens the noise from individual prediction errors, resulting in a more stable evaluation that outperforms standalone HD or FV methods in most scenarios. Moreover, the Evidence-Aware Pipeline offers an even more distinct advantage, frequently achieving the optimal performance among all evaluated methods. This performance leap can be attributed to the pipeline's conditional mechanism, which addresses the fundamental "blind spot" of retrieval-based verification. In cases where external knowledge is insufficient (i.e., NEI scenarios), forcing a verification verdict often introduces noise; however, the pipeline circumvents this issue by dynamically deferring to internal uncertainty signals, which remain a reliable proxy for truthfulness in the absence of corroborating evidence. Notably, hybrid methods mitigate the performance instability observed in RQ1. Unlike standalone HD methods, which exhibit fluctuations between LLaMA and Qwen families, hybrid approaches maintain robustness across diverse backbones. This suggests that the integration of multiple signals effectively compensates for the miscalibration of internal confidence inherent in standalone HD. Consequently, the proposed frameworks not only enhance accuracy but also offer a generalized solution that is independent of model-specific variations.

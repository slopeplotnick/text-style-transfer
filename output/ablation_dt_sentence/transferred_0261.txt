To address the limitations of outdated evaluation frameworks, it is essential to shift analytical emphasis from static, outcome-oriented assessments toward a systematic examination of the underlying generative processes in real time. This necessitates a structured approach centered around a predefined set of fundamental factual queries, which prompt language models to generate responses instantaneously. Consequently, the evaluation framework ensures that (1) fact-checking methods are provided with the necessary text, (2) hallucination detection mechanisms can monitor the model’s internal processes during generation, and (3) the evaluation remains effective and relevant as models evolve. This insight forms the conceptual foundation for the design of UniFact. Unlike conventional benchmarks that rely on obsolete datasets of prior outputs, UniFact introduces a dynamic system based on three core principles to guarantee robustness, fairness, and adaptability. First, it preserves applicability across current and emerging model architectures by prompting real-time responses through a consistent set of factual queries. Second, it facilitates equitable comparisons by accommodating both hallucination detection and fact-checking methodologies under a unified evaluation paradigm. Specifically, hallucination detection methods are granted access to the internal generative states of the model, while fact-checking approaches operate on the final output using external evidence to verify factual claims. Both methods are assessed against the same core task: determining the factual accuracy of the model’s responses. Third, UniFact incorporates an automated labeling mechanism that serves as a scalable and cost-efficient alternative to manual human evaluation. This system evaluates model accuracy by comparing generated answers with verified sources, thereby enabling rapid, impartial, and consistent assessment across multiple models.

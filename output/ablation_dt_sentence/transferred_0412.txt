Despite the substantial demand for mental health support, significant diagnosis gaps persist globally. Barriers such as limited clinic hours, geographic inaccessibility, and workforce shortages hinder timely assessment and early awareness of mental health conditions. Moreover, structural, organizational, and individual obstacles further impede diagnosis for individuals with severe mental health disorders. This mismatch between the high need for assistance and available resources highlights the limitations of traditional diagnostic models. Furthermore, traditional in-person sessions and self-reported questionnaires struggle to scale and face systemic constraints, including workforce shortages, high costs, geographic disparities, and prevailing stigma. Consequently, wait times for mental health services can range from 3 to 18 months, depending on location and provider availability. Therefore, there is a pressing need to develop more advanced methods for early detection and timely intervention. Portable, resource-efficient solutions such as small language models offer scalable detection and intervention opportunities in mental health disorders. The rise of emerging technologies embedded in smartphones facilitates the early identification of mental health issues through passive, real-time analysis of social media postings and application usage. Smartphone-derived linguistic data enables unobtrusive, scalable monitoring of psychological states, allowing early detection of mood disturbances, stress, or suicide risk. For example, these systems can support suicide prevention initiatives and identify patterns in social media use relevant to mental health. Operating locally on personal devices, these models safeguard user privacy while analyzing linguistic inputs for manifestations of depression or stress, thus enhancing the accessibility of mental health monitoring beyond clinical settings for a wider population.

With the increasing deployment of video large language models (video LLMs) in real-world applications, such as AI glasses and household robots, these models are now tasked with processing and reasoning over extremely long videos, ranging from several hours to even days. Recent advancements have introduced memory-based approaches that build external memories from abstracted video representations. These methods allow the model to focus on crucial information by selectively retrieving a small number of relevant memories, thus reducing the number of input tokens. This strategy is not only more efficient but also more effective compared to processing every frame of the video, which incurs high computational costs. However, despite their potential, most existing approaches still heavily rely on textual representations. Typically, detected events or clips are converted into captions, summaries, or structured text for downstream retrieval and reasoning. While some approaches, such as Long et al., incorporate visual inputs to build memory, their use of multimodal features is often limited to basic entity recognition and is not fully utilized during inference. Additionally, many models tend to retrieve a fixed number of clips, such as three 30-second segments, leading to a rigid architecture. These fixed designs present two key challenges: first, they fail to adaptively integrate visual information from videos with textual memory during retrieval and generation. Visual details are crucial for tasks like attribute recognition, spatial reasoning, and scene understanding—capabilities that text alone cannot fully capture. Second, relying on both captions and frames during response generation can lead to suboptimal results, as excessive visual information may distract the model. Therefore, developing an adaptive mechanism to select multimodal memories for retrieving the most informative references for a given query is essential, yet remains largely unexplored in current research.

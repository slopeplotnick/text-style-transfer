As a promising paradigm, test-time learning (TTL) enables models to adapt dynamically during inference through on-the-fly adjustments. Early approaches primarily focused on lightweight adaptation without modifying model parameters, such as entropy minimization or batch normalization (Wang et al., 2020; Niu et al., 2022). More recent methods extend this paradigm to test-time training (TTT), which allows explicit parameter updates at inference time, thereby improving model adaptability to task-specific distributions. For instance, several approaches demonstrate that TTT can enhance performance under distribution shift by incorporating beneficial feedback signals to guide adaptation, particularly in large language models. Within this broader framework, an emerging direction is test-time policy optimization, which recasts the inference process as a reinforcement learning (RL) problem. Methods such as RLVR and TTRL leverage consensus-based feedback, like majority voting, to inform policy updates without relying on labeled data. By aggregating signals across multiple rollouts, these techniques improve reasoning consistency and correctness. However, a key limitation of current methods lies in their rollout inefficiency: each query typically requires a fixed, large number of trials to yield stable estimates, regardless of query difficulty. This uniform allocation strategy results in excessive computational overhead, especially when simpler queries could converge with fewer samples. To mitigate this issue, we propose Optimal Rollout Allocation for Test-time Policy Optimization (OptPO), a general framework that addresses the efficiency-accuracy trade-off. Our central insight is to formulate reward estimation as a sequential hypothesis testing problem. Rather than committing to a fixed sampling budget, OptPO employs a stepwise procedure using Bayesian Sequential Probability Ratio Testing (SPRT) to adaptively assess vote distributions. Sampling halts once the accumulated evidence meets a predefined error tolerance, ensuring efficiency without sacrificing reliability. The collected rollouts are then reused for policy updates, such as PPO or GRPO, concentrating computational effort where it most reduces uncertainty. Furthermore, OptPO is broadly compatible with supervised fine-tuning approaches for test-time training.

Despite substantial progress in the development of computational sound and music generation, a standardized methodology for evaluating these systems remains absent. In addition to listening studies, researchers have proposed a set of objective metrics to assess the fidelity of audio reconstructions and the effectiveness of neural networks in classifying generated music into predefined categories. In this study, we employ both a listening test and objective metrics derived from prior research to evaluate the performance of our symbolic music generation model. We generated 100 MIDI samples per section of our test set (GoEmotions) and assessed the results. For the listening study, our goal was to evaluate how well the music generated by our model corresponded with the emotional content of the text. To achieve this, we asked human participants to listen to the compositions and provide their assessments. This task is inherently more subjective than conventional music generation tasks, as it requires a dual-layered interpretive process: listeners must first discern the emotional content embedded within the narrative and then evaluate whether the generated music appropriately reflects that emotional interpretation. In the first phase, each participant was assigned a text sample and instructed to categorize it into one of the four predefined emotion categories. In the second phase, participants were presented with two music selections corresponding to the narrative and asked to choose the one they believed most accurately conveyed the emotion expressed by the story. This two-step design enabled us to examine both the consistency of emotion perception at the text level and the alignment of the perceived emotional content between the text and the music. Since not all music selections aligned with the precise emotional category chosen by the participant, we evaluated the congruence of the music with the participant’s emotional response based on the valence (positive/negative) and arousal (high/low) dimensions, rather than relying on exact emotional category matches. Each category was mapped according to its valence and arousal dimensions, and for each participant’s selection, we assessed whether the chosen music aligned with the participant’s perceived emotional response to the narrative along these two axes. This approach offers greater flexibility in evaluating the emotional concordance between the music and the story.

The Qwen2.5-VL-7B model was trained using a two-step process, incorporating various types of CoT data, thereby synthesizing multi-dimensional CoT information. Subsequently, the model was trained using the same two-stage configuration (SFT and RL), with a parameter scale comparable to that of the LLM base model employed in this study. A performance comparison between models based on VLM and LLM is presented in Tables 13 and 14. The results show that, on both easy and hard datasets, the LLM-based method consistently outperforms the VLM-based approach, with a particularly clear advantage in terms of macro-F1 scores. This suggests that, when endowed with equivalent CoT capability, LLMs exhibit stronger reasoning and knowledge proficiency compared to VLMs. In contrast, VLMs excel in the visual salience subset, as expected, due to their ability to operate directly on image inputs without the need for intermediate textual representations, thus preventing information loss during the image-to-text conversion. Over the course of three phases, our LORE model has undergone systematic refinement along five key dimensions: (1) core objectives; (2) model architecture and framework; (3) data handling and prompt strategies; (4) evaluation methodologies; and (5) application contexts. These phases trace a progressive trajectory of model enhancement, combining increasingly sophisticated architectures, high-quality data development pipelines, and practical deployment strategies to improve the relevance of search results in e-commerce environments. In the initial phase, we faced significant challenges in understanding queries within the e-commerce domain, particularly in accurately identifying product names that deviate from standard expressions. To overcome this, we constructed large-scale, high-quality e-commerce datasets and employed In-Context Learning (ICL) to systematically inject domain knowledge into the model, thereby enhancing its comprehension of e-commerce semantics.

Compared to traditional algorithmic models, language models offer not only strong predictive capabilities but also human-readable interpretations and the flexibility to support multiple downstream tasks within a unified framework. Small Language Models (SLMs) have emerged as a pragmatic alternative to Large Language Models (LLMs), optimized for efficiency through techniques such as quantization, knowledge distillation, and lightweight architectural designs. These models are specifically engineered to deliver core Natural Language Processing capabilities—such as text classification, summarization, and question-answering—within resource-constrained environments, including mobile devices, embedded systems, and edge computing. SLMs offer a compelling alternative to LLMs by reducing computational overhead while maintaining strong performance in specialized tasks. Recent studies show that, in classification tasks with limited data, well-tuned smaller models can match or even surpass larger models when the task is focused and domain-specific, particularly in areas like text classification and healthcare. These findings underscore the potential of SLMs as efficient, scalable, and domain-optimized solutions, making them especially promising for mental health detection from social media. However, previous work has typically concentrated on a single task or domain and has yet to explore multi-domain settings that address various mental health conditions such as depression, stress, and suicidality. Unlike prior LLM-based models, which range from 7B to 70B parameters and are impractical for deployment in resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive settings, lightweight SLMs emphasize deployability, low inference latency, reduced memory consumption, and cost efficiency. Several studies have compared SLMs to LLMs across individual mental health tasks, utilizing zero-shot and few-shot prompting on social media data, and have found that few-shot prompting provides significant benefits for SLMs. Notably, Kim et al. (2024) developed mhGPT, a Small Language Model that outperforms larger models like MentaLLaMA and Gemma in mental health tasks, despite having fewer parameters and utilizing less data. Additionally, prior research indicates that SLMs are well-suited for integration into clinical workflows, supporting structured, clinically meaningful tasks beyond generic detection. However, these studies generally lack systematic task balancing and often fail to incorporate parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategies, meaning adaptation is either minimal, relying primarily on prompting, or computationally intensive, involving full model fine-tuning.

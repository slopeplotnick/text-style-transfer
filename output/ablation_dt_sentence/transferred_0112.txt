Our samples were compared against the EMOPIA MIDI files. The major key ratio serves as a proxy for valence, while average note length and note velocity reflect arousal. For the valence metric, the major-key ratio of our generated samples aligns reasonably well with EMOPIA across three quadrants. The most significant deviation appears in Q2, where our model produces a much higher proportion of major-key tracks than EMOPIA. This likely reflects a bias toward major keys, stemming from the overrepresentation of major-key tracks in the dataset and the limited size of the fine-tuning set. For arousal, the fine-tuned model effectively captures the note-length pattern: shorter notes in high-arousal quadrants (Q1 & Q2) and longer notes in low-arousal quadrants (Q3 & Q4), closely mirroring EMOPIA. However, differences in average note velocity across quadrants are less distinct, likely because REMI quantizes MIDI velocities into a reduced number of discrete bins, compressing the dynamic range and limiting inter-quadrant separability. Quantitative results partially reflect listener feedback, suggesting the model captures certain musical features related to arousal and valence. Nevertheless, the model appears to capture arousal more robustly than valence, as the valence outcomes approach random distribution. This may be due to the model’s tendency to overgenerate in the major key. Including additional quantitative metrics could offer greater insight into where valence-related generation may be falling short. In this work, we introduced Story2MIDI, a transformer-based model for generating emotionally aligned symbolic music from text. To support this task, we constructed a paired text-music dataset and developed an encoder-decoder framework to model affective correspondence between language and music. The encoder was contrastively fine-tuned to produce discriminative emotion representations across valence-arousal quadrants, while the decoder was initially pretrained on a large-scale symbolic music corpus and subsequently fine-tuned on a smaller, emotion-annotated dataset for emotion-conditioned music generation. We evaluated Story2MIDI using both subjective listener judgments and objective musical metrics; together, these assessments validate the model’s effectiveness and highlight several areas that warrant further investigation.

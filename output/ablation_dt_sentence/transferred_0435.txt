XAI explanations are required to meet specific criteria. It is essential that, firstly, explanations are comprehensible to the explainer, and secondly, they are faithful to the model's operations. Understandability depends on the recipient but generally requires clear and concise communication. Fidelity to the model means that the explanation must accurately portray the model's behavior without bias, which can occur, for instance, if the explanation's scope is deliberately limited. While understandability and fidelity are independent attributes, enhancing each separately is challenging. For example, explanations that confirm expectations may be perceived as more understandable, yet this can introduce bias by encouraging the selection of certain explanations. Furthermore, a correct, unbiased explanation that gets misinterpreted can equally mislead the explainer. This study introduces a causal XAI framework for post-hoc explanation aimed at improving both understandability and fidelity. Firstly, understandability is addressed by arguing that explanations should employ plain language and outline causal relationships, as human communication typically relies on such structures. This ensures that the intended interpretation aligns with the actual interpretation, thereby eliminating the need for additional interpretative guidance and fostering a more succinct representation. Straightforward language is necessary due to potential discrepancies between the dataset used by the model and the optimal method for explanation. This concept is a cornerstone of methods collectively known as Concept-based XAI (C-XAI), which assumes that while models efficiently process data like images and graphs, human communication primarily depends on high-level, semantically meaningful concepts.

The system utilizes regular expression (regex) techniques to extract structured allocation decisions from markdown responses generated by large language models (LLMs). This method leverages pattern recognition to systematically identify and retrieve specific formatted sections within the LLM's output, ensuring the accurate extraction of decision-related data. Specifically, the system identifies sections such as allocation decisions, success rates, and reasoning explanations, transforming them into structured data for validation. To achieve this, the validation framework incorporates eight rule-based checks that collectively ensure allocation integrity, including the elimination of invalid assignments, verification of trade-off assessments, evaluation of workload awareness, and confirmation of logical consistency across all decisions. The transition to a weighted validation framework was necessitated by the inability of traditional rule-based systems to adequately account for the enhanced reasoning capabilities and contextual sensitivity required for LLM-driven task execution. Research indicates that evaluating multiple dimensions of the model’s reasoning provides a more comprehensive understanding of its performance than merely assessing correctness. Consequently, the system assigns evaluative scores to reasoning outputs across key dimensions, including the clarity of explanations, consistency in reported success rates, sensitivity to workload distribution, robustness of trade-off justification, adherence to prescribed phase structures, and alignment between expressed confidence and decision reliability. Each dimension contributes to an aggregated quality score, with greater weight assigned to factors that more directly influence outcome determination, such as the quality of reasoning and the accuracy of success rates, while aspects like the justification of confidence are attributed lower weights. This configuration guarantees that the validation process is both performance-sensitive and context-aware, effectively balancing interpretability with operational rigor in assessing the model's reasoning behavior. The evaluation categories are as follows: 1. Explanation Quality (Weight: 0.20): The extent to which the reasoning is both comprehensive and articulated with clarity. 2. Success Rate Accuracy (Weight: 0.20): The degree to which predicted success probabilities align with observed outcomes. 3. Trade-off Analysis (Weight: 0.15): The extent to which the analysis explicitly identifies and systematically balances competing objectives, articulating the inherent trade-offs among alternative goals. 4. Workload Awareness (Weight: 0.15): The consideration of the distribution of tasks within the current workload, accounting for the allocation and balance of responsibilities across individuals or components.

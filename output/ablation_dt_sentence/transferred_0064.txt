4.1.2 Graph-Based Statistical Analysis of Retrieved Training Images. While our earlier similarity analysis assessed the impact of selected training images by comparing generated outputs before and after retraining, this alone does not fully clarify why certain images exerted a stronger influence. To address this, we conducted a graph-based statistical analysis of the retrieved training samples, focusing on their structural properties and shared semantic components. Specifically, we examined whether the top-ranked images selected per prompt formed coherent, semantically meaningful groups. Table 3 reports key statistics from ten independent evaluations (each using fifteen prompts), offering insight into the consistency and clustering observed across generated outputs. These findings further support the effectiveness of our image selection strategy and provide a more interpretable understanding of the role these samples play in the generation process. Unique Values per Relationship. The average number of unique values per relationship ranged from 3.9 to 5.1 across evaluations. This indicates a moderate level of variation—appropriate for a domain like fashion, where diversity exists but is constrained by established styles, silhouettes, and patterns. The retrieved samples demonstrated enough similarity to form well-defined groups, yet retained sufficient variation to prevent redundancy. This suggests that influence arises through shared high-level concepts, rather than duplication of specific content. Shared Attributes: Depth of Semantic Similarity. On average, retrieved image groups shared between 5 and 7.1 attributes, with some clusters exhibiting up to 13 shared attributes. This highlights not only the semantic richness of the retrieved samples but also their internal consistency. Notably, prompts with more structured cues tended to yield higher shared attribute counts, whereas more abstract or stylistic prompts introduced greater diversity. These trends demonstrate the system’s ability to adjust to prompt specificity while consistently retrieving semantically aligned examples.

In 2023, Zakharov examined the mechanisms by which individuals detect variations and formulate probabilistic inferences under conditions of uncertainty, emphasizing the role of likelihood-based reasoning in perceptual and cognitive judgment processes. This aligns with Petzschner and Glasauer's (2011) suggestion that such phenomena arise from the reliance on the best-guess method in uncertain situations. Moreover, recent computational models have proposed that temporal misestimations are not merely random errors but are systematically influenced by memory-based predictive mechanisms, as demonstrated by Fountas et al. (2022). When individuals assess magnitudes or quantities, they often conflate ambiguous information with pre-existing beliefs, which systematically shapes subsequent patterns of behavior. In line with this, Figure 1 illustrates that both Llama-4 Maverick and human participants exhibit a central tendency bias in their estimations, suggesting a convergence towards the mean. This convergence implies that, despite fundamental differences, both may engage in analogous underlying reasoning processes. Certain components of large language models (LLMs) function analogously to a predictive mechanism, as evidenced by the interpretation of in-context learning as a form of approximate Bayesian inference (Xie et al., 2021). Additionally, Bayesian teaching has been shown to enhance reasoning performance (Qiu et al., 2025). Furthermore, LLMs segment sequences based on Bayesian surprise, aligning with human event perception, as demonstrated by Kumar et al. (2023) and Fountas et al. (2025). However, most studies focus on explicit reasoning or learned behaviors, where models rely on established rules, rather than on tasks that reveal computational strategies emerging implicitly from pretraining. Recent advancements in the development of large language models capable of processing multimodal inputs have led to the proposal of benchmark suites such as MMbench and SEED-Bench (Liu & Li, 2024) to evaluate the reasoning capabilities of these models across diverse modalities. Nevertheless, these assessments often overlook the influence of noise in various forms of information on cognitive processes. The use of synthetic data, however, allows for a controlled examination of how large language models integrate and reconcile ambiguous information from multiple sources. This emerging field of research is pivotal for advancing our understanding of how to develop models that perform optimally in the face of uncertainty.

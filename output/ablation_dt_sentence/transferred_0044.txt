Human interpretation of videos hinges on understanding how events unfold, why actions occur, and when pivotal moments arise. Traditional video object segmentation (VOS) methods, however, largely rely on appearance-based or category-level cues. Reasoning VOS elevates the task: the model must parse ambiguous, underspecified instructions, leverage commonsense and causal reasoning, and integrate temporal evolution with semantic understanding to locate the referent—such as “the runner most likely to win” or “the object causing the accident.” While this shift simplifies user inputs by offloading complexity to the model, it simultaneously increases the burden on model understanding, requiring substantial data and architectural adaptation for effective fine-tuning. This challenge underscores a deeper insight: effective video reasoning unfolds through a sequence of deliberate choices rather than a single latent inference. The model must determine what to attend to, when to do so, and which entity the query targets. Motivated by this insight, we introduce ReVSeg, which dispenses with latent segmentation tokens and instead formulates reasoning video segmentation as an explicit sequence of reasoning steps. Specifically, ReVSeg decomposes reasoning VOS into three actions aligned with the native capabilities of vision-language models: video understanding (interpreting the natural language query and analyzing scene dynamics), temporal grounding (identifying frames or intervals relevant to the query), and spatial grounding (localizing and segmenting the target within selected frames). ReVSeg orchestrates these steps through a unified model, ensuring that semantic information established early in the process seamlessly propagates through to final localization. By aligning with pretrained model interfaces, this approach maintains consistency with foundational training, avoids costly retraining, and reframes a complex task as a structured procedure. Once the reasoning steps are made explicit, the core challenge becomes optimizing the reasoning process itself. Supervised learning proves insufficient, as intermediate steps are rarely annotated, limiting its effectiveness. We therefore adopt reinforcement learning (RL), framing reasoning VOS as a behavioral policy rewarded only when its full decision trajectory yields a correct outcome. However, applying RL naïvely faces obstacles: reasoning VOS provides sparse success signals and demands coordinated reasoning across video understanding, temporal selection, and spatial localization. To overcome this, we introduce reasoning-aligned rewards at critical decision points, ensuring that RL directly shapes the model’s reasoning behavior. In concert with task decomposition, RL transforms the task from an implicit inference problem into an explicit, estimable reasoning policy: decomposition specifies what decisions to make, while RL dictates how those decisions should cooperate. This synergy yields a reasoning strategy that is both internally coherent and practically robust.

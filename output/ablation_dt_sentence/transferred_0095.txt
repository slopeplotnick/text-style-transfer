We introduce a methodology for constructing an energy model that characterizes the energy consumption of statically typed programming languages executed on the JVM. This methodology investigates the impact of primitive data types, data sizes, operations, and device configurations on energy consumption. Furthermore, we provide evidence that different device instances of the same hardware model exhibit varying energy consumption, which limits the generalizability of energy predictions. The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 provides background on Bayesian statistics, while Section 3 introduces the methodology employed in this study. Section 4 presents the implementation of the proposed method, and Section 5 outlines the evaluation approach, discussing the validity threats encountered. The evaluation results are detailed in Section 6, followed by the discussion in Section 7. Section 8 reviews related work, and Section 9 concludes the paper. Bayesian statistics is a statistical framework that uses prior knowledge to make probabilistic inferences about an unobserved phenomenon. At a high level, this goal is similar to frequentist statistics, the framework most commonly employed in computer science. However, the two approaches differ significantly in how they construct and interpret models: frequentist statistics focuses purely on observed data, minimizing biases and assumptions, while Bayesian statistics makes assumptions explicit, allowing for scrutiny and validation. We employ Bayesian statistics for two main reasons: first, it requires us to articulate our assumptions explicitly, enhancing transparency and interpretability; second, it enables the prediction of energy consumption through posterior distributions, offering a more detailed comparison of alternatives compared to the single-point estimates typical of frequentist methods. In this study, we assume that energy consumption follows a normal distribution within a range conservatively selected from prior research. This range serves as the initial condition, which is then refined using a Bayesian inference algorithm to align more closely with our measurements. In scenarios with limited data, initial assumptions may significantly influence the results, potentially leading to suboptimal outcomes. However, as the dataset expands, the Bayesian algorithm corrects these initial assumptions, improving the model's accuracy in predicting energy consumption.

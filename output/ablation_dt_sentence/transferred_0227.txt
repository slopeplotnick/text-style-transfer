More recent datasets include Mozilla’s Common Voice, which offers crowd-sourced, scripted speech in six South African languages, distributed under a permissive license to encourage broad reuse. The dataset was built through community contributions and supports open access and redistribution. FLEURS provides approximately 12 hours of recordings per language in four South African languages, designed primarily for benchmarking and evaluation. Vuk’uzenzele (VixSD) is a community-driven ASR dataset, released under a licensing framework that emphasizes community ownership and ethical use. While relatively small in scale, it underscores the potential for local participation in resource creation. AfriSpeech-200 includes English speech data with South African accents and covers healthcare-related content, making it valuable for accent-aware ASR systems. However, across these resources, there remains a notable scarcity of spontaneous speech and limited domain diversity. This highlights the need for more representative, community-anchored datasets such as Swivuriso, which incorporate both scripted and unscripted speech, extend to a wider range of domains, and reflect greater speaker diversity. Collecting speech data in underrepresented language communities requires careful attention to consent, privacy, cultural sensitivity, and long-term community impact. Prior work in projects such as Common Voice and ALFFA has demonstrated the critical importance of embedding ethical and participatory practices throughout the data lifecycle. A case study on the Catalan edition of Common Voice reinforces the value of transparent consent mechanisms, strong privacy safeguards, and inclusive community participation. Similarly, the ALFFA project emphasized community engagement by collaborating with local experts and field linguists during data collection. Swivuriso builds on these foundations by prioritizing ethical practices and inclusive representation. Local coordinators played a pivotal role in facilitating recordings, supporting participants, and ensuring community diversity. The data collection process followed institutionally approved ethical guidelines, including data anonymization, removal of personal identifiers, and the implementation of safeguards to mitigate risks such as voice cloning and biometric misuse. Ensuring dataset accessibility and usability is essential, especially for low-resource languages. Open licenses—such as those offered by Creative Commons—enable broad data reuse, which is particularly important in contexts constrained by limited funding or infrastructure. Initiatives like Mozilla Common Voice exemplify how open licensing can promote equitable access and accelerate the development of language technologies for under-resourced languages.

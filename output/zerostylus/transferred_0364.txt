The investigation into language models focuses on their application of contextual information to respond to health-related questions and examines the impact of disordered contexts. To assess the proficiency of these models in reasoning through extensive and conflicting health information, we utilize a dataset named HEALTHCONTRADICT. This dataset comprises 920 instances, each featuring a health-related query, a scientifically substantiated factual response, and two documents with contradictory viewpoints. Various inquiry formulations were tested, including correct, incorrect, or ambiguous contexts, to determine their influence on model responses. Notably, HEALTHCONTRADICT surpasses existing evaluation benchmarks in assessing language models' capacity for processing contextual information. Our experiments reveal that the proficiency of these models is not solely attributed to their pretrained knowledge but also to their adeptness in leveraging relevant contexts while disregarding irrelevant ones. Nonetheless, these models can still produce responses that appear accurate but are indeed incorrect, raising concerns about the reliability of information dissemination. This is particularly problematic when individuals trust inaccurate health data, posing significant risks. The rapid evolution of health information, especially during crises, and the swift spread of unverified information online highlight the fragility of how these models assimilate data. To address challenges of conflicting information, several approaches have been proposed to enable models to manage inconsistencies effectively. One method involves systematically modifying the model's understanding when discrepancies between its knowledge and the context are identified, while retrospectively analyzing the rationale behind specific outputs. Furthermore, the model analyzes either pre-existing knowledge or surrounding contextual elements for contextual attributes. This approach integrates both methods by evaluating whether the information is coherently aligned. Additionally, the methodology forecasts dual responses—one from the contextual framework and another from the model's intrinsic knowledge—facilitating comprehensive evaluations of language models' reasoning capabilities over complex datasets. It ensures that the model reliably identifies the correct response when faced with conflicting information by evaluating consistency across context and evidence. Efforts are made to prevent models from producing incorrect responses, and to enhance their ability to process conflicting information, erroneous or unrelated data is incorporated during training. The evaluation process includes strategies to identify and mitigate inaccuracies, employs careful questioning techniques, and utilizes multiple reviewers to validate response accuracy. Furthermore, the endeavor involves identifying robust solutions to mitigate attacks and forecasting which facts are likely to change imminently, thereby preventing reliance on outdated information.

Our examination identifies two primary focus areas, both of which are crucial for enhancing the accuracy and reliability of responses in health-related subjects. Firstly, addressing inaccuracies arising from mixed information is essential. Such inaccuracies often stem from excessive input lengths that include formatting noise and placeholder tokens, which can act as spurious cues and bias models toward incorrect predictions. Secondly, mitigating undesirable model behaviors is equally important. These behaviors may manifest as misclassifications, particularly when models are biased by non-relevant cues. Therefore, refining input processing and model tuning can significantly improve the reliability of health-related responses.

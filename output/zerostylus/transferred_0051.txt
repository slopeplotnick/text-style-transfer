Evaluating the logical coherence and ethical propriety of AI model outputs is crucial for ensuring the reliability and societal trustworthiness of these systems. To address this imperative, this study introduces the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP), a fully autonomous ethical analysis framework explicitly designed to operate independently of fixed datasets. Unlike traditional approaches, which often rely on predefined benchmarks and human-annotated datasets, MoCoP autonomously generates and evaluates examples of moral reasoning, allowing for iterative refinement without external input. Consequently, it offers a novel mechanism to comprehensively assess the ethical dimensions of AI systems in dynamic and evolving contexts. This research systematically tracks the progression of moral reasoning within AI models over time, emphasizing the consistency of ethical deliberation across different systems. Specifically, it employs an integrative framework that synthesizes lexical analysis, semantic interpretation, and logical evaluation to monitor and evaluate the ethical management capabilities of these models longitudinally. In doing so, the study extends beyond earlier efforts that primarily focused on detecting and mitigating bias or reducing harmful content. While prior work, such as the OpenEthics initiative, improved transparency and verifiability in AI ethics evaluation, these methods were typically constrained by their reliance on fixed datasets and static prompts. Although such approaches provide valuable insights into AI behavior at specific points in time, they lack the capacity to assess shifts in moral reasoning or evaluate the stability of ethical decision-making across diverse and evolving scenarios. Moreover, this limitation is particularly significant for contemporary AI systems, as they exhibit adaptive and context-sensitive behavior. Static evaluation frameworks fail to capture the phenomenon of moral drift, where ethical stances may fluctuate or degrade in response to changes in prompt structure, situational pressures, or temporal dynamics. This inconsistency poses risks to the reliability, accountability, and societal trustworthiness of AI systems, particularly in high-stakes domains such as healthcare, finance, education, and governance. Therefore, addressing the challenge of maintaining moral consistency is not only essential for regulatory compliance but also a prerequisite for fostering logically stable, interpretable, and trustworthy reasoning in AI. In response to these challenges, MoCoP advances the state of AI ethics evaluation by functioning as a closed-loop system that dynamically generates ethical scenarios, elicits model responses, and performs structured analysis using internally defined rules and semantic heuristics. By autonomously constructing and interpreting its own ethical scenarios, MoCoP overcomes the limitations of traditional dataset-dependent approaches. It integrates three complementary analytical components: lexical integrity analysis to detect bias and linguistic degradation, semantic risk estimation to quantify ethical risks, and reasoning-based judgment modeling to assess the logical stability of moral coherence. Together, these components enable MoCoP to provide a unified and adaptive framework for evaluating the ethical stability of AI systems, thereby addressing critical gaps in the field.

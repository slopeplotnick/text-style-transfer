Recent advancements have significantly enhanced the performance of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) in processing both text and images. However, these models continue to struggle with accurately discerning fine-grained details within high-resolution images. To address this challenge, researchers have explored two primary approaches to improve model understanding of high-resolution images. The first approach focuses on extensive training through methods such as supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. Nevertheless, this strategy often diminishes the models' generalization abilities across various tasks. Alternatively, training-free approaches typically employ search-based methods to analyze image regions and identify salient features. Despite their utility, these methods can suffer from inefficiencies and may overlook certain objects, especially in densely populated images. In our investigation, we examine the influence of image crop dimensions on model performance, revealing that the size of image pieces significantly affects the efficacy of these models. We further analyze how varying object sizes correspond to different resolutions, optimizing their performance within multimodal large language models. In light of the challenges identified in existing methodologies, we have conceptualized and implemented the MRD framework to address these issues effectively. A comprehensive performance evaluation was conducted using two model variants, LLaVA-ov and LLaVA-v1.5, on a standardized benchmark. Consequently, we propose that a resolution of 112 may be the optimal choice for utilization in such tasks.

Our approach employs OmniFusion to facilitate live translation by extracting relevant lecture slides from segmented audio clips. In a similar vein, we are developing an open-source system that supports the translation of entire images or slides, thereby paving the way for future research in comprehensive image translation. This integration of images demonstrates enhanced performance in language processing tasks, leading to improved outcomes across various language models. Moreover, we explore the evaluation of optical character recognition (OCR) models to assess their efficacy in translating images through both unimodal and multimodal approaches. To fully localize lecture content, our method involves developing systems that incorporate multimodal input, translating both audio and slides into multiple languages and formats, thus supporting accessibility tasks. Conversely, our approach leverages these translations to facilitate additional linguistic tasks. It also translates slides by converting textual and visual elements into another language while preserving their original layout and design. To map visual context to each audio segment and improve usability, we have built a PDF viewer that synchronizes captioned slides with the speaker's presentation, enabling users to seamlessly engage with translated content. This alignment of segmented audio with corresponding slide visuals provides essential contextual information for accurate translation. Our approach elucidates the process of integrating slide visuals with corresponding audio segments, facilitating their translation for application in various tasks. We also address the processes of identifying, translating, and reconstructing textual content embedded within slide images. Additionally, the appendix, particularly section A.1, offers a comprehensive exploration of Text-to-Speech, intended to enhance both benchmark-driven evaluations and broader speech system development. While some systems support translation into multiple languages, they are often limited in live lecture translation contexts, where they struggle with real-time processing complexities. Existing systems are typically configured for offline functionality and require indications to initiate the translation process. Furthermore, many current approaches either neglect the integration of lecture slides or lack the capability to process content across multiple languages. Therefore, our approach necessitates the integration of both features within the lecture framework.

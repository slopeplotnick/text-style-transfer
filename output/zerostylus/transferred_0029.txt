Individuals frequently consider various dimensions when evaluating AI-generated content, such as its technical proficiency, originality, and emotional impact. Recent studies, such as those by Hong et al. (2022) and Moura and Maw (2021), typically employ rating scales borrowed from related domains. However, these measures may lack specificity in capturing the nuances of individuals' emotional responses to AI-generated music. Utilizing an exploratory methodology, we aim to uncover the subjective criteria and underlying themes present in participants' open-ended responses. The music samples in our study were generated using AI tools specifically designed for this purpose, namely Stable Audio (v.2.0) and Suno (v.3.5). To ensure ecological validity, the samples were derived using the Emotion-to-Music Atlas (EMMA; Strauss et al., 2024), which offers comprehensive information regarding the impact of music on emotional responses. The Music4All-Onion database (Moscati et al., 2022), which forms the foundation of our study, includes five principal musical genres, each further subdivided into three distinct subgenres: Electronic (Trance, Downtempo, Dubstep), Jazz (Bossa-Nova, Smooth-Jazz, Swing), Pop (Europop, Indie-Pop, Pop-Punk), Rock (Soft-Rock, RockBallad, Doom-Metal), and Soul/Funk (Funk, Disco, Soul). For each subgenre, prompts were formulated based on the principal emotions identified in the music, utilizing average values from the EMMA and their GEMS-9 descriptions, such as "a joyful, bouncy, animated trance song" representing the Joyful Activation dimension for the Trance subgenre. This process resulted in the creation of 38 prompts, with 2-3 prompts developed for each subgenre to ensure adequate representation. These prompts were adapted for use in Suno and Stable Audio, culminating in a set of 76 stimuli. To account for the properties of each tool, the following approach was employed: for Suno, prompts were constructed in instrumental mode, and one of two potential outputs was randomly selected and edited to a duration of 45-60 seconds to ensure alignment with previous studies, such as those by Strauss et al. (2024) and Warrenburg (2020). For Stable Audio, the term 'instrumental' was incorporated into each prompt, with a target duration of 60 seconds. Further details regarding the creation and editing of these samples, as well as the audio files, are provided in the supplemental materials.

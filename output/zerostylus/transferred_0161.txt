In recent years, advancements in the technology underlying Large Language Models (LLMs) have been substantial, enabling their widespread application across diverse domains, such as intelligent interaction and scientific research support. Particularly in the medical field, LLMs have exhibited notable potential in assisting with disease diagnosis and optimizing diagnostic and treatment workflows. Within the realm of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), the foundational clinical principle is referred to as "Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment" (SDT), which involves synthesizing multidimensional information to differentiate syndromes and subsequently formulate individualized treatment strategies based on principles such as "different treatments for the same disease." Although TCM-specific LLMs, including Zhongjing and HuatuoGPT, have emerged, their integration into clinical practice remains constrained by the inherent complexity and highly individualized nature of TCM's diagnostic and therapeutic paradigms. Moreover, a critical limitation is the absence of comprehensive evaluation benchmarks that encompass the complete clinical decision-making process, as well as specialized metrics that assess the reasoning capabilities of these models. Therefore, the development of a rigorous benchmark dataset is urgently required to objectively and comprehensively evaluate the clinical application capabilities of TCM-focused LLMs. Such a benchmark would facilitate performance assessment, iterative model optimization, and eventual real-world implementation. However, constructing a dataset of this nature presents considerable challenges due to the individualized, holistic, and diverse characteristics of SDT in TCM. Specifically, individualization implies that patients diagnosed with the same disease may exhibit varying syndromes, influenced by factors such as physiological constitution, disease progression, and lifestyle habits, thereby complicating the creation of a standardized framework for evaluation. Furthermore, TCM diagnosis relies on a holistic reasoning process that integrates multidimensional diagnostic methods, analyzes underlying causes and pathogenesis, and ultimately completes syndrome differentiation to inform treatment formulation. This comprehensive diagnostic approach necessitates the development of a benchmark dataset with extensive evaluative breadth. Additionally, the frequent coexistence of multiple syndromes in a single patient, combined with the diversity of diagnostic methodologies employed, introduces significant subjectivity into evaluation processes. In summary, these distinctive characteristics render the construction of a robust benchmark for assessing the clinical application capabilities of TCM-specific LLMs exceptionally complex.

The presence of multiple concurrent patient conditions and varied diagnostic methodologies can, nonetheless, introduce a degree of subjectivity into evaluation outcomes. Consequently, the development of a comprehensive dataset to effectively assess the practical efficacy of TCM-oriented LLMs in real-world medical scenarios becomes particularly challenging. Therefore, constructing such a dataset necessitates meticulous attention to detail and a structured approach to ensure its robustness and reliability.

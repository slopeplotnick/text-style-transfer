In this paper, we propose a method for constructing knowledge graphs (KGs) from images using multimodal artificial intelligence (AI) models guided by domain-specific ontologies. The ontology, which defines key semantic attributes and relations, is embedded in the guidelines to ensure the AI prioritizes relevant visual elements in accordance with the contextual relevance of the image and its domain. Consequently, upon providing the AI with an image, it generates structured triples aligned with the ontology, capturing the most salient objects, attributes, and relationships present in the image. These triples collectively form an interpretable KG, wherein the configuration of the graph is entirely contingent upon the predefined ontology parameters. Therefore, each image produces its own interpretable graph, which we subsequently archive within a graph database. Despite each image's association with a unique subgraph, many of the nodes—characteristics—are consistently linked across images due to the ontology's constrained vocabulary. As a result, all image-specific graphs integrate into a larger, interconnected structure. This unified graph representation facilitates the identification of semantic overlap between images by analyzing their constituent subgraphs within the global knowledge space. To describe the characteristics of each image, we employ a multimodal AI model to extract structured semantic triples that encapsulate high-level concepts depicted within the image. The axes of interest are defined by a domain-specific ontology, designed by experts, which serves as a semantic guide to ensure consistent representation across all images. To bridge the gap between the general-purpose AI model and the specialized ontology, we embed the ontology into the guidelines during inference. This approach enables the construction of interpretable per-image graphs and their integration into a unified global graph. Consequently, while each individual graph maintains its own connectivity, they are interconnected through shared attribute nodes, thereby facilitating semantic comparison across the dataset.

While machine learning and generative methodologies are widely utilized, the deployment of AI in decision-making remains comparatively uncommon. This is because decision-making necessitates the allocation of finite resources, a process that fundamentally diverges from the exploratory nature of academic research. Prior to exploring the broader implications of AI in mineral exploration, it is noteworthy to mention that its predominant application thus far has been in delineating the high-grade section of the Mingomba deposit in Zambia. By re-evaluating historical drilling data through advanced analytical tools, researchers generated novel insights, ultimately identifying a resource-rich area that exceeded previous estimations. In orchestrating the stages of a drilling sequence, artificial intelligence, configured as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), facilitates the step-by-step planning process. The primary objective was to enable the AI to address uncertainties surrounding geological theories regarding the locations of mineral-rich deposits. When evaluating alternative methodologies for drilling, such as deviating from traditional grid-based techniques, it becomes evident that the proposed method offers superior efficacy. This approach demonstrates greater efficiency as it prioritizes disproving geological hypotheses over merely identifying mineral deposits. In Part III, we will explore how AI can accelerate the identification and comprehension of critical mineral deposits; however, I will first examine some of the prevalent applications of AI in this domain. The initial phase will encompass data analysis and forecasting, subsequently progressing to AI-driven decision-making processes. Nevertheless, the initial deployment of AI was met with a degree of defensiveness among certain individuals. Concerns were raised that artificial intelligence might usurp control or diminish the significance of domain experts in critical processes. This concern allows for the examination of the apprehensions held by individuals regarding the stability of their employment in the current climate. In considering the capabilities of artificial intelligence systems, there was an expectation that these technologies would independently generate novel insights and discoveries. However, artificial intelligence does not merely designate a location on a map for autonomous systems to excavate and uncover resources. Rather, AI can be envisioned as a tool to assist specialists by assuming responsibility for tasks that are either outside their expertise or unnecessary, thereby enabling them to concentrate on fostering innovative approaches in the domains of geology and geochemistry.

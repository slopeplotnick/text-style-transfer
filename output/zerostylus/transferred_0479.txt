In this section, I will review the current applications of AI in exploration and highlight prevalent errors associated with its use. Subsequently, I will hypothesize about the potential evolution of AI in exploration, with a particular focus on decision-making processes. The primary objective of exploration remains the discovery of new entities, which can occur near known deposits, termed brownfield exploration, or in unexplored regions, referred to as greenfield exploration. In the forthcoming decade, it is anticipated that more resource discoveries will be made within brownfield areas compared to greenfield ones. Exploration can be conceptualized as a process of narrowing investigative focus by reducing uncertainty regarding the presence of economically viable deposits. Initially, exploration often occurs on a continental scale, targeting significant geological features, such as large igneous provinces, where favorable conditions allow magma to rise and accumulate in economically exploitable formations. At this stage, geologists rely on geological mapping and airborne geophysical data to inform their methodologies. Anomalies detected through these surveys may indicate intriguing subterranean features, prompting more detailed investigations through fieldwork, ground geophysical surveys, and soil tests. The subsequent phase, characterized by substantial financial investment, involves exploratory drilling to validate hypotheses concerning subsurface formations. Ideally, decisions during this phase are made using decision science, incorporating uncertainty quantification and assessing the value of information to optimize cost management. Various data sources are instrumental in enhancing our understanding of mineral system formation, guiding the identification of optimal areas for exploration. Notably, the analysis is not only concerned with identifying mineral deposits but also with evaluating geological hypotheses and reducing uncertainty regarding subsurface conditions. Throughout this process, decisions made in exploration projects are dynamic, as the objectives of these decisions may evolve.

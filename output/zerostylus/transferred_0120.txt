TokenPowerBench comprises two primary components: the measurement component, which quantifies the power and energy consumption associated with executing large language models across various hardware, models, and tasks; and the analytical component, which enables the evaluation of these metrics within a benchmarking framework. The primary aim is to ensure that these measurements are straightforward, adaptable, and readily reproducible. The analytical component (Section 4.1) quantifies energy utilization by measuring joules consumed per token or per response, and instantaneous power consumption metrics, thereby facilitating comprehensive evaluation of energy efficiency within diverse operational contexts. Moreover, these measurements associate specific operational stages of LLMs, such as prefill and decode, with precise energy expenditure metrics. In contrast to other metrics, such as processing speed or data throughput, these energy-focused metrics emphasize sustainability and cost, which are key considerations for contemporary AI systems. TokenPowerBench employs a detailed and hardware-dependent methodology for power measurement, comprising two key elements: the hardware instrumentation and tooling component, which captures power data based on the specific hardware configuration; and the analysis component, which ensures the accuracy of the collected measurements. The framework incorporates three tiers of measurement capabilities, ranging from basic GPU data observation to comprehensive system monitoring via advanced tools, thus accommodating both small-scale setups and extensive testing environments. Additionally, a dedicated setup configuration, accompanied by pertinent auxiliary functions, ensures that experiments are conducted in a consistent and repeatable manner each time. The tool's functionality encompasses a range of LLM tasks that may vary depending on system scale, model specifications, or usage context. It focuses on parameters such as batch size, context length, and system configuration to evaluate their respective impacts on energy consumption during execution. Furthermore, the framework operates across configurations ranging from single-GPU setups to large-scale multi-node systems, providing insights into both granular energy consumption metrics and overarching system-wide energy utilization. Since the landscape of LLM technology is rapidly evolving, TokenPowerBench has been developed with future alignment in mind. The system will encompass enhancements for compatibility with emerging categories of GPUs, chips, and other hardware components, alongside the introduction of features designed to evaluate the impact of energy conservation on the model's output quality. This is crucial as it aids users in determining the optimal balance between energy consumption and model performance.

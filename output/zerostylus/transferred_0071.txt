Although cov_tgsp and CSP consistently demonstrate strong performance across datasets (see Table 2 & Figure 3), notable variations emerge depending on the dataset analyzed. Specifically, while CSP and cov_tgsp often rank among the top-performing methods, their effectiveness is significantly diminished on the PhysionetMI dataset in comparison to their performance on Cho2017 and Zhou2016. This discrepancy may be explained by differences in experimental protocols, including variations in sampling frequency, the number of electrodes used, and the sample size of participants. These findings underscore the dataset-dependent nature of feature extraction methods in decoding motor imagery from EEG signals. For example, on the Zhou2016 dataset, CSP combined with StandardScaler and logistic regression achieved the highest score (0.87), followed closely by cov_tgsp paired with RobustScaler and logistic regression, which attained a comparable score of 0.84. Conversely, on the Cho2017 dataset, cov_tgsp outperformed CSP, achieving a top score of 0.74 compared to CSP's 0.68. In contrast, methods relying on instantaneous functional connectivity and non-linear features consistently performed worse across all evaluated datasets. For instance, svd_entropy, when combined with RobustScaler and logistic regression, yielded scores of 0.68 on Zhou2016, 0.59 on Cho2017, and 0.54 on PhysionetMI. On the PhysionetMI dataset specifically, performance was notably weaker overall, with the best pipeline, cov_tgsp, achieving a maximum score of 0.64, while CSP averaged 0.62 across frequency bands. These results suggest that, although non-linear and connectivity-based features are adept at capturing the complex dynamics of EEG signals, they may not offer sufficient discriminative power for motor imagery classification in this context. Furthermore, subject-level analysis provides additional insights into the observed variations. While CSP and cov_tgsp generally exhibit superior performance across datasets, as demonstrated in Figures 5 and 6 for Cho2017 and Zhou2016, this trend becomes less consistent on the PhysionetMI dataset (Figure 7). In several cases, alternative approaches achieve the highest scores: for example, Hjorth parameters outperform CSP and cov_tgsp for participants #57 and #59, whereas the instantaneous connectivity method combined with TGSP achieves optimal results for participants #17 and #73.

To address these challenges, model-agnostic approaches have been introduced that evaluate output consistency with training data within a unified embedding space, often leveraging representations from pre-trained encoders. This approach typically incorporates features derived from pre-trained encoders. The efficacy of these methods is notable in processing substantial datasets; however, their reliance on vector representations complicates interpretability. To mitigate this issue, efforts such as LatentExplainer have been initiated, which adjust latent variables and employ language models to elucidate the implications of these modifications. Nevertheless, the underlying mechanisms of these methods remain opaque to users. In contrast to these intricate approaches, automatically constructed Knowledge Graphs (KGs) are employed to examine the impact of data on various elements. By analyzing generated images in comparison to training images through shared triples encompassing objects, attributes, and style elements, a comprehension that aligns with human cognitive processes is facilitated. Moreover, to enhance the interpretation of unstructured data, Large Language Models (LLMs) have become integral to contemporary knowledge graph processes. Their primary functions involve the extraction of subject-predicate-object triples from conventional text, facilitation in the development of ontologies, and the conversion of natural language inquiries into graph-based queries. For instance, the process of generating triples can be facilitated by methods such as AutoKG, which transform textual data into ontology-aligned triples, thus enabling a structured representation of semantic relationships. Since this advancement, the role of LLMs in facilitating the learning and expansion of ontologies through techniques such as prompt engineering and ontology reuse has become increasingly significant. In addressing inquiries, LLMs translate user queries into SPARQL queries, enhancing this process through the utilization of contextual knowledge graphs or controlled natural languages to improve clarity. Recent investigations into the construction of generative knowledge graphs (KGs) highlight a transition from traditional pipeline-based methodologies to advanced systems driven by large language models (LLMs), emphasizing the evolution of techniques in this domain. While these systems demonstrate improved performance in recognizing entities, extracting relations, and populating graphs, their advantages over traditional methods highlight the need for further investigation into the underlying mechanisms driving these improvements. Consequently, we illustrate the foundational role these advancements in developing knowledge graphs play in addressing data attribution challenges within generative models, thereby enhancing traceability and transparency.

The structured outputs generated by multimodal large language models (LLMs) offer a mechanism for constructing interpretable knowledge graphs by encapsulating essential elements from both training data and generated outputs. This approach, distinct from reliance on complex embeddings or parameters, enables the tracking of influential relationships. In this context, we explore the potential application of these methods to images, acknowledging that they present greater complexity compared to text. Moreover, such complexity necessitates innovative approaches to effectively transform visual content into structured knowledge representations. Therefore, employing these structured outputs in the realm of image processing could significantly enhance the interpretability and utility of multimodal LLMs.

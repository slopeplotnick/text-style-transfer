Language models, when compared to traditional algorithmic models, offer not only superior predictive capabilities but also human-readable interpretations and the flexibility to support multiple downstream tasks within a unified framework. In addressing the limitations associated with Large Language Models (LLMs), Small Language Models (SLMs) have emerged as a pragmatic alternative. These models are optimized for efficiency through techniques such as quantization (Frantar et al., 2022), knowledge distillation (Kang et al., 2023), and lightweight architectures (Wang et al., 2025a). SLMs are specifically designed to deliver core Natural Language Processing capabilities, including text classification (Pecher et al., 2024), summarization (Wang et al., 2025b), and question-answering (Lee et al., 2024), in environments characterized by constrained resources such as mobile devices, embedded systems, and edge computing. Moreover, SLMs present a compelling alternative to LLMs by significantly reducing computational overhead while maintaining robust performance in specialized applications. Recent studies further indicate that in classification settings with limited data availability, well-tuned smaller models can match or even surpass larger models in focused, domain-specific tasks, including text classification (Lepagnol et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2023) and healthcare applications (Gondara et al., 2025). These findings underscore the efficiency, scalability, and domain optimization offered by SLMs, thus rendering them a promising direction for mental health detection via social media analysis. However, previous research predominantly focused on singular tasks or domains and did not explore multi-domain settings that integrate various mental health conditions such as depression, stress, and suicidality. In contrast to prior LLM-based approaches (Sarkar et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024b; Kim et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2025) which possess a range of parameters from 7B to 70B, making them impractical for deployment in resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive settings, lightweight SLMs emphasize deployability by reporting reduced inference latency, memory footprint, and approximate cost efficiency. Instead of solely evaluating SLMs against LLM baselines for individual mental health understanding tasks, few-shot prompting has been shown to enhance the performance of SLMs more effectively (Jia et al., 2025). Kim et al. introduced mhGPT, an SLM that surpasses larger models such as MentaLLaMA and Gemma in mental health applications despite utilizing fewer parameters and requiring less data (Kim et al., 2024). Additionally, SLMs have demonstrated potential for integration into clinical workflows to execute structured and clinically meaningful tasks beyond generic detection (Aich et al., 2024). Nonetheless, these studies often exhibit suboptimal task balancing and lack parameter-efficient fine-tuning, such as LoRA, resulting in adaptation that is either minimal through prompting or extensive via full fine-tuning.

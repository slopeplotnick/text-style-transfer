We examine three lines of related research: first, methods that utilize external feedback for alignment; second, approaches that aim to reduce dependence on human annotations; and third, theoretical investigations exploring how representational frameworks impact the quality of generated outputs. A commonly employed technique for aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences is Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Ouyang et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2022). RLHF involves training a reward model on human preference data and subsequently fine-tuning the policy to maximize this learned signal while adhering to a KL divergence constraint relative to a reference model. This approach underpins prominent LLMs such as ChatGPT and Llama (Dubey et al., 2024). However, RLHF faces notable challenges: preference data are often expensive and noisy, and reward models remain vulnerable to reward hacking and performance degradation under distribution shift (Casper et al., 2023; Skalse et al., 2022). These limitations have motivated the development of alternative supervised preference-based methods, including Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Rafailov et al., 2023), KTO (Ethayarajh et al., 2024), Bayesian RLHF (Wang et al., 2023), and MaxMin-RLHF (Chakraborty et al., 2024). These methods directly refine policies through pairwise comparisons without relying on an explicit value network but still depend on carefully curated preference datasets. Moreover, process reward models and generative reward models have been introduced to provide step-level scores and textual critiques, facilitating reasoning and evaluation tasks (Zhang et al., 2025b; Yin et al., 2025). Importantly, all these approaches maintain external supervision, ensuring that oversight remains separate from the model's internal computational processes. Complementary to these efforts, a growing body of research focuses on reducing reliance on human-provided labels by leveraging model-derived signals or automated supervision. Methods such as DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, 2025) and TÜLU 3 (Lambert et al., 2024) replace human feedback with programmatic alternatives. However, the implications of substituting human input with automated signals for performance outcomes remain an open question, warranting further investigation.

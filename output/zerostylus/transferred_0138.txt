Our results are consistent with prior findings (Sengupta et al., 2025; Subramanian et al., 2025), indicating that performance degradation is not necessarily smooth when model size is reduced. Specifically, our findings align with the work of Shi et al. (2025) on MentalQLM (0.5B), which demonstrated robust performance in predicting various mental health outcomes, including depression, stress, and suicidal ideation. These results underscore the viability of fine-tuned smaller language models (SLMs) for mental health prediction, offering a favorable trade-off between computational efficiency and predictive accuracy. Moreover, consistent with recent studies (Ren et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024a), we observe that core reasoning and question-answering abilities are preserved following fine-tuning, thus enabling these models to serve as multi-functional tools with enhanced performance on mental health tasks. Our experiments further illustrate that SLMs within the 3-7 billion parameter range provide a strong balance between efficiency and predictive performance upon fine-tuning. While smaller models often fail to generalize or recognize task-specific constraints, models within this range effectively classify psychological signals in both zero-shot and few-shot settings. Through targeted fine-tuning, our model, Menta, establishes a clear lower bound for model size, achieving strong performance on complex mental health tasks while maintaining computational efficiency. Therefore, balancing model size, accuracy, safety, and real-world impact in mental health AI is imperative. When comparing the performance of SLMs and larger language models (LLMs), fine-tuned SLMs demonstrate comparable or superior performance in depression and stress level classification tasks, though they exhibit lower efficacy in predicting suicidal ideation. However, compared with non-trained SLMs, the performance significantly improves while retaining a more balanced accuracy across prediction tasks and classes. Our findings suggest that well-optimized lightweight SLMs can retain core reasoning and contextual understanding necessary for mental health inference. The competitiveness of SLMs in this domain primarily arises because mental-health language tasks frequently rely on localized lexical, affective, and syntactic cues, such as expressions of self-reference, emotional polarity, and temporal framing, which can be effectively captured by moderate-sized models without necessitating the extensive world knowledge encoded in LLMs. As evidenced by prior work (Ji et al., 2021; Shen et al., 2018; Benton et al., 2017), fine-tuning on domain-specific corpora substantially narrows the performance gap between smaller and larger architectures. Our findings extend this evidence by demonstrating that parameter-efficient fine-tuning, such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), combined with adaptive, weighted loss functions, enables SLMs to learn discriminative representations for mental-health prediction at a fraction of the computational cost.

Our findings demonstrate that targeted fine-tuning strategies, such as LoRA, combined with tailored loss functions, enable smaller models to effectively acquire the necessary capabilities for mental health prediction while minimizing computational overhead. This approach contributes to the broader shift from model scaling to optimization-oriented approaches in AI applications. Moreover, by employing these strategies, smaller models can perform comparably to larger models, thereby offering a cost-effective solution for early detection in mental health monitoring. Furthermore, the reduction in computational requirements aligns with the increasing demand for efficient AI solutions in digital health applications. Therefore, our work underscores the importance of optimization-focused methodologies in advancing AI's role in mental health prediction, and it opens avenues for future research in optimizing model parameters while maintaining performance efficacy.

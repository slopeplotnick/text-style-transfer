Proper licensing of datasets is fundamental for ensuring both broad usability and ethical access, particularly for languages with limited resources. Utilizing standard open licenses, such as Creative Commons (e.g., CC BY 4.0), facilitates large-scale data reuse. Initiatives like Mozilla Common Voice exemplify the significance of such permissive licensing for the advancement of language technology. However, the inability of researchers in low- and middle-income countries to access resources for AI development raises significant concerns about equitable data governance and the empowerment of local communities. Therefore, our decision to employ the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) license for the Swivuriso project was driven by a commitment to inclusivity and ethical responsibility. Swivuriso is accessible on both Zenodo and Hugging Face, accompanied by permanent Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs) and comprehensive documentation, highlighting our commitment to ensuring broad accessibility and detailed guidance. Each release is accompanied by audio recordings, aligned transcripts, and comprehensive metadata detailing demographics, linguistic attributes, and accessibility considerations. Moreover, particular emphasis is placed on ethical guidelines regarding the use of technology, such as voice cloning, to safeguard the privacy of individuals involved. The CC BY 4.0 license provides a highly permissive framework, allowing unrestricted sharing, modification, and utilization of the dataset, including commercial applications, provided that proper attribution is given to the creators and any modifications are transparently documented. Furthermore, the license is designed to preclude additional legal or technical restrictions, safeguard database rights, and incorporate a "cure period" for resolving licensing concerns, thereby ensuring legal clarity and fostering global scientific collaboration. However, while open licenses are beneficial for enhancing data utility, it is crucial to consider aspects such as local governance and safeguarding Indigenous knowledge, which are often overlooked. In the realm of cultural datasets and community-derived information, merely applying uniform licensing can inadvertently undermine community ownership, necessitating careful consideration of ethical and equitable data stewardship practices. Consequently, emerging approaches to African data management increasingly emphasize ethical, equitable, and community-centered stewardship practices, prioritizing data care, benefit-sharing, and community governance.

Analysis of Learning Dynamics. In Figure 3, we examine the learning dynamics of the 7B model utilizing CAPO and GRPO methodologies. Initially, both approaches exhibit similar rates of improvement; however, once the model acclimates, CAPO demonstrates superior performance. The gray line in Figure 3 marks the transition where the model shifts from input replication to independently deriving solutions. Beyond this point, the entropy trajectory of CAPO shows increasing unpredictability, unlike GRPO, which maintains consistent dynamics, while the associated rewards continue to improve. This suggests that by enhancing the unpredictability of its strategy, CAPO not only achieves superior rewards in the later stages but also facilitates the exploration of diverse cognitive approaches. Moreover, by progressively increasing unpredictability, CAPO avoids prematurely incorporating negative examples, thereby preventing abrupt transitions and maintaining stability in its learning dynamics. By deferring execution, CAPO stabilizes the replication phase and subsequently leverages negative feedback more effectively, enhancing its capacity to adapt across diverse tasks. Comparative Analysis of Curriculum Strategies. We compare traditional static teaching methods with our proposed dynamic instructional strategy. In the static approach, the difficulty of each example is assessed by evaluating the model's performance on it, which subsequently guides the model's training sequence. In contrast, the dynamic method adjusts the training focus progressively throughout the process, eliminating the need to reorder the data, and allowing for empirical evaluation of its impact. As illustrated in Table 3, both ADARFT and the static method GRPO(+SC) exhibit minimal enhancement over the foundational GRPO. However, our dynamic method, GRPO(+Ours), demonstrates significantly improved performance across most tasks. This analysis indicates that merely estimating the difficulty of a task is insufficient; rather, redirecting focus during the training process proves to be more effective.

To enhance the reliability of language model evaluations, it is often advantageous to prompt the model to articulate intermediate reasoning steps before providing a final response. This approach, exemplified by the Chain-of-Thought prompting technique, enables the model to clarify the rationale behind its decisions, thereby improving both the accuracy and comprehensibility of its judgments. In this research, we propose a series of cognitive steps to follow and explore strategies for effectively integrating these findings. Moreover, the use of computational resources can be optimized by employing various methodologies for decision-making processes. While individual samples can be analyzed in isolation, an integrated approach to their amalgamation facilitates the streamlined synthesis of multiple samples into a singular dataset. Specifically, Self-Consistency (SC) enhances reliability by integrating multiple outputs through majority voting. In addition, Soft Self-Consistency (Soft-SC) employs confidence levels to select responses based on confidence scores across various categories. Similarly, Confidence-Informed Self-Consistency (CISC) utilizes a weighted voting mechanism predicated on confidence scores, which are derived either through sequence probability or by model inquiry. Recently, certain methodologies have leveraged the model itself to effectively integrate outcomes, thereby obviating the need for costly retraining processes. For instance, Generative Self-Aggregation (GSA) instructs the model to synthesize a new response based on the context of several samples. Furthermore, Universal Self-Consistency employs the LLM to identify the most consistent response from several alternatives. Studies indicate that employing computational resources during evaluation, particularly through majority voting mechanisms, significantly enhances the performance of reasoning-based generative verifiers.

To visually compare the metrics of our generated files with those in the EMOPIA dataset, we employ t-tests to determine whether the generated data maintains the statistically significant differences in metrics for high and low valence and arousal observed in the EMOPIA data. To achieve music generation conditioned on a specific emotion inferred from text, we utilize an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture. The textual emotion is captured using a RoBERTa-large encoder, which is contrastively fine-tuned to better capture nuanced emotional differences across quadrants. The decoder comprises a Transformer-based module with three layers, each incorporating four attention heads, designed to model the temporal and harmonic structures of symbolic music. The overall model architecture is depicted in Figure 2. Initially, the encoder is set up from RoBERTa-large and fine-tuned on the GoEmotions dataset, where the 27 emotion labels are replaced with their corresponding quadrant labels. Each batch contains examples from multiple quadrants, wherein pairs within the same quadrant are treated as positive pairs, and those from different quadrants are considered negatives. This in-batch formulation facilitates efficient training with numerous negatives, resulting in an embedding space where quadrants form distinct clusters. Subsequently, the contrastively fine-tuned encoder is frozen and serves as the text-conditioning module in our generative model. Notably, the pre-trained RoBERTa encoder produces highly overlapping representations across the four emotion quadrants, whereas the contrastively fine-tuned encoder yields significantly more separated clusters. This finding confirms that contrastive training enhances the emotional structure of the embedding space, while still reflecting the inherently gradual and subjective nature of affective categories. For visualizations of the embeddings before and after contrastive training, refer to Figure 4 in the Appendix (Section C). The EMOPIA dataset contains only 1,078 MIDI samples, which is insufficient for a Transformer decoder to effectively learn the underlying dependencies between MIDI tokens. To address this limitation, we pre-train the decoder on the larger GiantMIDI-Piano dataset. During pre-training, zero tensors are input in place of text embeddings, and the decoder is trained using a causal language modeling objective, enabling it to learn the probability distribution over MIDI tokens in an auto-regressive manner.

UnresolvedRetry denotes instances in which an agent engages in multiple consecutive attempts to edit a file, yet consistently fails to achieve a successful modification. For example, Figure 12-a demonstrates a situation where the SWE-agent Dev executes the str_replace command on utils.py 183 times, ultimately reaching the cost threshold without success. StrNotFound, depicted in Figure 13-a, occurs when an edit attempt fails because the specified original string is absent in the file. Such failures are typically due to variations in formatting or minor text discrepancies. NoEffectEdit describes scenarios where the original and revised strings are identical, thus resulting in an ineffective replacement. As shown in Figure 13-b, this inefficiency occurs when a string is replaced with itself, leading to no change in the file and a waste of resources. AmbiguousTarget captures instances where the specified original string appears multiple times within a file, causing ambiguity regarding which occurrence should be replaced. Figure 13-c illustrates this challenge, where multiple identical if statements impede the editor's ability to deterministically apply the replacement, highlighting a limitation inherent to string-based editing. GRAPHEC-TORY comprises a dataset of 4000 cases, which provides a basis for analyzing recurring and emergent patterns. As summarized in Figure 14, inefficiency patterns are prevalent across agents and models, evident in both resolved (Figure 14-a) and unresolved scenarios (Figure 14-b). The percentages beneath the Venn diagrams indicate the prevalence of at least one inefficiency pattern in these instances. Importantly, even resolved instances exhibit inefficiencies, underscoring the necessity of a process-centric analysis to enhance understanding of agent behavior. Unresolved cases often feature multiple inefficiency patterns simultaneously. Interestingly, more robust models, such as Claude Sonnet 4, achieve higher success rates yet exhibit a greater frequency of these patterns. Our analysis reveals that the model engages in extensive deliberation before generating a response. While this strategy proves more effective in challenging scenarios, as evidenced by the model's higher success rate, it also leads to inefficiencies when additional steps do not improve solution quality.

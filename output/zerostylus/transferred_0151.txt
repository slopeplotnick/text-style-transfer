Efforts are underway to construct larger and more diverse text corpora by incorporating sources such as social media, news articles, and transcribed speech, thereby addressing challenges posed by data scarcity. Furthermore, leveraging explainable AI (XAI) methodologies is crucial for ensuring the transparency and trustworthiness of emotion detection in textual data. The study investigates methodologies to enhance lexical databases by employing various techniques, assessing the performance of AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa across newly introduced African languages, particularly under conditions of limited linguistic data availability. Efforts are directed towards ensuring comprehensive coverage, achieving accuracy, and enhancing our understanding of emotional nuances across different languages. Moreover, this investigation seeks to augment lexical databases and conduct sentiment analysis in linguistically resource-scarce languages, thereby enhancing sentiment analysis for these languages. Through the application of diverse methodologies, we have developed an enhanced lexicon for languages such as Ciluba, French, English, and various South African languages, ensuring emotional accuracy by utilizing both automated systems and human verification. AfroXLMR demonstrated superior performance in terms of accuracy and consistency compared to AfriBERTa; however, AfriBERTa remains a viable option for scenarios with limited computational resources. The research indicates that both models exhibit proficiency in identifying morphological components that convey emotions in intricate languages such as Zulu and Xhosa. Despite challenges associated with translation and data volume, this investigation demonstrates the feasibility and utility of conducting sentiment analysis in languages with limited resources. Therefore, future efforts will prioritize enhancing data acquisition methodologies, integrating explainable AI frameworks, and expanding lexical resources to optimize overall system performance. Ultimately, this research provides a robust framework for the continued exploration and development of methodologies tailored to languages with limited resource availability.

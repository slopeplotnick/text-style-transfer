The findings underscore the critical role of listeners' pre-existing attitudes and expectations in evaluating AI-generated music. For instance, Hong et al. (2021) demonstrated that individuals with a more favorable disposition towards creative AI assigned higher ratings to AI-generated music, specifically highlighting the anticipated stylistic variations across different genres. However, when unexpected events occurred, these expectancy violations negatively impacted the evaluations, although no significant differences across genres were observed. A central question in the evaluation of AI-generated music is whether it is perceived as indistinguishable from human-composed music. Therefore, understanding the specific musical elements that lead participants to attribute compositions as either human or AI-generated is vital. According to Shank et al. (2023), genre plays a significant role, with electronic music more frequently associated with AI composers. Interestingly, when participants were misled about the music's true origin, they identified nonexistent differences, illustrating the profound impact of expectations and beliefs on their aesthetic judgments (Ansani et al., 2024). Déguernel et al. (2022) revealed through qualitative analysis that participants often identified AI-composed music by its excessive repetition, rigid or unnatural structural elements, and unconventional harmonic or melodic features. In contrast, human compositions were associated with melodic variation, ornamentation, recognizable style, and characteristics suggestive of instrumental playability. These associations reflect a broader pattern in which musical features perceived as "human" receive more favorable evaluations, whereas those linked to AI are often viewed less positively. Moreover, Hong et al. (2022) investigated the implications of perceiving AI as possessing human-like qualities and creative autonomy on its acceptance as a musical artist. Participants evaluated AI-generated pieces across various genres, assessing both the quality of the compositions and their perceptions of AI. The results indicated that attributing higher anthropomorphism and greater perceived autonomy to AI musicians led to increased acceptance. Furthermore, Sun et al. (2023) demonstrated that anthropomorphism influences the perceived competence and warmth of AI musicians, both of which contribute to more positive attitudes.

Sun et al. (2023) demonstrated that perceiving AI as akin to humans influences perceptions of competence and warmth attributed to AI musicians, both of which contribute to more favorable attitudes. However, further analysis revealed that this effect was largely driven by individual differences in attitudes toward AI, rather than the experimental manipulation alone. Specifically, when attitudes toward AI were incorporated as covariates in linear mixed effect models, the experimental condition ceased to predict liking or emotional intensity, whereas attitudes toward AI remained a consistent and significant predictor. This supports previous findings that attitudes toward autonomous machines predict evaluation (Hong et al., 2022) and adds perspective to Shank et al. (2023)'s work, which suggests that composer bias might be song and context specific, particularly present for well-liked, human-sounding songs. Although several personality traits and music-related variables correlated with liking and emotional experience, their impact largely diminished when attitudes toward AI were controlled for. Notable exceptions include openness to experience and neuroticism, two personality traits previously found to substantially affect the experience of music, especially in relation to emotions of joy and vitality (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Juslin et al., 2011; Liljeström et al., 2013), both of which were highly prominent in the stimuli of the present study. Most interestingly, attitudes toward AI was the only listener characteristic significantly associated with both liking and emotional experience. Therefore, the total amount of variance explained by listener characteristics was similar in magnitude to that reported in previous research (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Strauss et al., 2024). These findings emphasize that attitudes toward AI are a key determinant in evaluating AI-generated music across diverse users. In contrast to Ansani et al. (2024), we did not find a moderation effect of attitudes toward AI on experimental groups, which may be attributed to differences in the attitude scales used, our focus on listening to tracks rather than live performance, and the inclusion of three instead of two experimental conditions.

The study provides key insights into the comparison between actual and synthetic datasets. Histograms comparing the distribution of scores indicated no significant differences in average scores with one test; however, another test revealed a distinct dispersion pattern. While the generated data closely approximated the real dataset in terms of central tendency, discrepancies were observed in the distribution of scores. This observation, therefore, provides partial support for our fourth hypothesis. As illustrated in Figure 2, graphs present the comparison between the distributions of the real and synthetic datasets. To mitigate potential bias, data from an alternative cohort were employed for subsequent analysis. The dataset selected for evaluation focused on individuals' perceived proficiency with technological skills, assessed through 25 questions across five categories: communication, information management, content creation, safe usage, and problem-solving. The selection of this approach was predicated on its robust validation and widespread accessibility, ensuring both reliability and public availability. Prior to generating the simulated data, our intended plans were formally documented. In this instance, we modified our approach to generating the simulated data, as the new dataset lacked ethnicity information; thus, only age and gender were incorporated. Furthermore, the dataset contained distinctive identifiers for each individual, enabling alignment of simulated individuals with authentic counterparts based on age and gender. Repeated occurrences of certain IDs were removed to ensure precise alignment of the samples.

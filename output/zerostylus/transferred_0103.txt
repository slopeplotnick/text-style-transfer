Distribution matching presents a compelling alternative to traditional motion tracking by training controllers to replicate the broader behavioral distribution within a motion dataset, thereby eschewing the need for frame-by-frame motion tracking. In this context, Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) employs a learned discriminator to differentiate between the motion patterns generated by agents and those present in the dataset, thereby assisting in aligning the agent's behavior with the intended distribution [Ho and Ermon 2016]. Moreover, Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) advance this concept by facilitating the production of authentic behaviors for novel tasks not originally present in the dataset [Peng et al. 2021]. However, adversarial priors necessitate retraining with a specific policy tailored to each new task, which often demands repeated access to the original dataset. In contrast, our method maintains performance comparable to state-of-the-art adversarial imitation learning techniques by introducing a reusable and modular score-matching motion prior, enabling the adaptation to motion styles absent from the initial dataset. Diffusion models have gained significant attention due to their exceptional performance across various domains, leading to their application in control tasks. Their ability to generate high-fidelity motion makes them suitable as motion planners, where predicted future movements are executed by a tracking controller [Ren et al. 2023; Serifi et al. 2024; Tevet et al. 2024; Xu et al. 2025]. Furthermore, these models have been directly employed to govern behaviors for tasks such as manipulation and character control, thus enabling the execution of diverse and adaptable actions [Chi et al. 2023; Janner et al. 2022; Huang et al. 2024a, 2025; Truong et al. 2024; Wu et al. 2025b]. Unlike methods that integrate diffusion models within a controller, our approach utilizes pretrained diffusion models as overarching guides for behavior. Specifically, we employ them as a reward function within reinforcement learning, allowing policies to acquire behaviors through the score distillation sampling framework.

We propose SR-GRPO, a novel approach that employs stable rank as a dense reward signal in reinforcement learning, thereby eliminating the reliance on preference datasets or external validation mechanisms. Through extensive experimentation, we demonstrate that SR-GRPO enhances reasoning capabilities across diverse benchmarks, thus supporting the hypothesis that intrinsic geometric signals can serve as a robust foundation for aligning large language models (LLMs). Moreover, beyond its utility in reinforcement learning, we establish that stable rank functions as a reliable zero-shot reward proxy for evaluating LLM responses and integrates seamlessly with best-of-N decoding strategies. Specifically, we propose stable rank (Rudelson & Vershynin, 2007) as a principled unsupervised signal derived from the internal representations of LLMs. This framework introduces a metric grounded in geometric insights, which we validate as an effective reward proxy through both theoretical and empirical analyses. The theoretical motivation for this work stems from two primary research domains. First, the softmax bottleneck (Yang et al., 2018; Godey et al., 2024) indicates that expressive language modeling requires high-rank representations, while representation collapse is widely recognized as a hallmark of suboptimal generation quality (Gao et al., 2019). Second, as stable rank directly quantifies effective dimensionality, it emerges as a natural candidate for quality assessment. To substantiate this theoretical connection, we conduct empirical validation, detailed in Section 5, which includes tests and correlation analyses. Importantly, we evaluate stable rank as a zero-shot proxy for human preference by examining whether it can reliably predict the preferred response within a pair. This evaluation further investigates whether stable rank can serve as a dependable reward signal without necessitating model-specific training. Our experimental setup leverages RewardBench (Lambert et al., 2025), a comprehensive benchmark that includes 2,985 preference pairs categorized into five distinct domains: Chat, Chat-Hard, Safety, Code, and Math. For each preference pair, we compute the stable rank of both responses and predict the preferred response as the one with the superior stable rank. To contextualize the efficacy of stable rank, we compare its performance against three established baseline methodologies: (1) Pointwise Scoring (Kim et al., 2024), which involves prompting the LLM to assign a score between 1 and 5 to each response; (2) Pairwise Comparison (Kim et al., 2024), which entails instructing the LLM to directly compare two responses; and (3) IPO (Garg et al., 2025), which derives implicit preference scores from the probabilities of Yes/No tokens. Our evaluation is conducted across five LLMs: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Yang et al., 2024), Qwen3-0.6B (Yang et al., 2025), Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Dubey et al., 2024), and Phi-3.5-mini-Instruct (Abdin et al., 2024). For the computation of stable rank, we format the inputs as prompt-response pairs using the native chat template associated with each model and extract the final-layer representations for analysis.

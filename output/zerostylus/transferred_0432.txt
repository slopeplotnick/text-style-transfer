Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has brought significant advancements to large language models, particularly in their ability to address knowledge-intensive tasks. However, most existing systems primarily focus on generic or news-oriented domains, which limits their optimization for high-stakes applications such as supporting agricultural sectors in adapting to climate change. Furthermore, while recent studies have attempted to incorporate document retrieval alongside climate data to address domain-specific inquiries, these approaches often rely on static retrieval methods and generic prompting, thereby failing to produce region-specific, comprehensive, or verified answers. Although geography-aware question answering systems have been explored in other domains, these methods have not been specifically tailored to address climate reasoning. Moreover, current systems typically treat climate data and research literature as separate entities, neglecting the need for a unified framework that integrates both sources of evidence. To address these challenges, the CAIRNS framework adopts a structured four-stage process: (1) retrieval of climate information from Elsevier and a climate data API, weighted by geographic relevance; (2) answer generation using the ScholarGuide Prompt methodology; (3) verification of the generated answers; and (4) hybrid evaluation of the responses. The retrieval mechanism within CAIRNS employs a hybrid approach, combining sparse lexical matching (e.g., Okapi BM25) and dense semantic retrieval (e.g., BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5), which are integrated through a weighted scoring scheme. However, location-specific contextual relevance remains critical for climate-related questions. Therefore, CAIRNS augments the hybrid retrieval score with a location similarity term to balance semantic and spatial relevance. Following retrieval, the framework adopts an iterative reasoning-retrieval loop inspired by the ReAct paradigm, wherein evidence is collected, an initial answer is generated, and a sufficiency checker is applied. If the answer is deemed incomplete, the system formulates sub-questions and revisits the retrieval loop until a satisfactory resolution is achieved. Central to the answer generation process is the ScholarGuide Prompt (SGP), which is specifically designed to balance factual accuracy and readability in responses. For climate adaptation question answering, this prompt explicitly guides the language model by introducing a structured grading rubric that optimizes the generated answers. Framing the language model as an "academic writing coach," the ScholarGuide Prompt employs a three-pronged mechanism that emphasizes (a) clarity and structure, (b) scientific reasoning supported by citations, and (c) specific, localized recommendations. This methodology ensures that responses are both comprehensive and tailored to the nuances of climate-related inquiries.

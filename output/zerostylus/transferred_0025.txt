The initiative involves the integration of additional features and enhancements into the existing framework. Furthermore, it encompasses technological advancements and improvements in agronomic practices. The primary aim is to accelerate development processes, specifically by enabling the creation of new applications faster than their predecessors. This initiative embodies principles such as code reutilization, fostering innovation, and expediting workflow processes. Initially, the framework was conceived as a reusable code base to facilitate the development of new applications. Proximity to Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE) objectives aids in advancing the migration project. Identifying and promoting seven extant good practices is essential for reassurance during the implementation and maintenance of the Software Product Line (SPL). Factorization and reuse are the two most frequently discussed concepts, mentioned nine and eight times respectively, as illustrated in Figure 1. This holds significant importance because it fosters team motivation and reinforces their understanding of the benefits. Customization, a core element of SPL, is frequently cited by professionals in information technology, user experience, and agronomy. An SPL is perceived as a platform capable of generating customized software applications from pre-existing ones, thus facilitating rapid and adaptable software development. Speed, often cited by IT, product owners, and UX professionals, emerges as the second most discussed concept (refer to Table 5). It involves the capacity to develop new software applications with increased rapidity. The concept of automation encompasses the ability to create new software automatically, and both IT and product owner professionals frequently associate it with speed. Industrialization, highlighted by IT and UX professionals, refers to the ability to develop applications rapidly and cost-effectively. In addressing RQ1, IT personnel interpret improving code quality in terms of consistency, testability, and reliability.

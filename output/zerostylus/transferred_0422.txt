Online videos have emerged as a significant mode of communication, and their pervasive presence facilitates the rapid dissemination of hateful content. Such content exacerbates discrimination and social division, ultimately having the potential to incite violence in real-world scenarios. This phenomenon highlights a common approach: extracting features from video, audio, and textual data, although this method fails to effectively capture the interaction between these modalities. The identification of hateful videos encounters two principal challenges: (1) the intricacies of context comprehension—such videos frequently employ subtle cues that unfold temporally across diverse media formats, manifesting through correlations between certain imagery and language, or the association between visual presentation and auditory elements; and (2) the integration of multimodal semantic relationships—while existing approaches typically extract and integrate features from visual frames, audio tracks, and textual transcriptions, they often fall short in achieving effective multimodal semantic interaction. Consequently, addressing both challenges necessitates more advanced reasoning approaches that surpass the capabilities of conventional methodologies. Innovative methodologies endeavor to enhance comprehension by incorporating models that integrate visual elements with linguistic components. However, recent methodologies that incorporate reasoning steps have been found to offer only elementary descriptions without adequately linking to malicious intents. Furthermore, certain contemporary approaches have been observed to provide incorrect explanations that fail to accurately represent the model's cognitive processes, posing significant challenges for tasks requiring heightened sensitivity. Recent empirical research indicates that hateful cues within videos may manifest rapidly in brief segments or be distributed throughout the entire video. Therefore, signals embody intricate meanings, necessitating their integration with fundamental features for comprehensive interpretation. As evidenced by existing research, there exists a pronounced discrepancy in the efficacy of current systems: they exhibit limitations in executing reliable reasoning and effectively integrating diverse informational modalities to identify hateful videos.

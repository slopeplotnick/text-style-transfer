Detecting hate speech in low-resource languages remains a challenging issue due to the scarcity of datasets, orthographic heterogeneity, and linguistic variety. Bangla, spoken by over 230 million individuals in Bangladesh and the Indian state of West Bengal, exemplifies such a low-resource language. Despite the growing need for automated moderation on social media platforms, Bangla is significantly underrepresented in computational resources. This study examines Subtask 1A and Subtask 1B of the BLP 2025 Shared Task, which focuses on hate speech detection. Specifically, we reproduce the official baselines—namely, Majority, Random, and Support Vector Machine—and additionally consider Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree as baseline methods. Moreover, recent advancements in hate speech classification have utilized transformer-based models, including DistilBERT, BanglaBERT, m-BERT, and XLM-RoBERTa. These models generally outperform baseline methods for the subtasks; however, DistilBERT is an exception. Among the transformer-based models, BanglaBERT excelled by achieving superior performance in both subtasks. Its superiority over m-BERT and XLM-RoBERTa underscores the importance of language-specific pre-training. The findings highlight both the necessity and the promise of leveraging pretrained language models for Bangla, which lacks sufficient computational and linguistic resources. Significant advancements have been achieved in the domain of hate-speech detection for high-resource languages, such as English. However, progress remains notably constrained for low-resource languages, including Bangla (Bengali). This disparity can primarily be attributed to two key factors: the scarcity of high-quality annotated datasets and the inherent linguistic complexity of Bangla. Consequently, the development of robust hate-speech detection systems for Bangla faces substantial challenges, necessitating further research and resource development to address these limitations.

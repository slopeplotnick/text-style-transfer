Adversarial training facilitates attack transferability not as a consequence of specific model architectures but rather as a fundamental attribute of how this training modifies model information processing mechanisms. Indeed, the occurrence of this phenomenon across diverse model architectures underscores its critical implications for overall security. To elucidate how adversarial training enhances transferability, we investigated models' prediction justification using the integrated gradients method. A comparative analysis was conducted between two iterations of the DenseNet121 model: one subjected to adversarial training (AT) and another developed through standard training methodologies (ST). In examining the differences in feature attribution, we observed that the AT model prioritized salient regions of the image, such as the head, body, and legs of an ostrich, whereas the ST model allocated focus to finer details, including feather textures and background elements. This difference is significant because AT models prioritize generalizable features shared across diverse images, whereas ST models attend to specific details that exhibit limited transferability to other models. Consequently, the improved efficacy of adversarial examples generated by adversarial training models across various architectures is attributed to their targeting of universally shared features among these models. DenseNet121 and DenseNet201 exhibit analogous image processing methodologies, while MobileNetV3 shares these characteristics, incorporating design principles derived from EfficientNet. However, contrary to expectations that model architectures inherently determine vulnerability, our analysis indicates that MobileNetV3 exhibits increased susceptibility to attacks originating from models based on EfficientNet.

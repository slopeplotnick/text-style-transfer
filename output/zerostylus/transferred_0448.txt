In this section, we shall elucidate the process previously discussed by employing an illustrative example. We utilize the CelebA dataset, which comprises over 200,000 images of celebrity faces, each annotated with labels indicating attributes such as the presence of a smile, eyewear, youthfulness, or attractiveness. In this work, our focus lies on two specific tasks: determining whether an individual depicted in an image is youthful and evaluating their perceived attractiveness. As illustrated in Figure 6, beginning with the original image on the left, the figure presents a series of modified versions of the same image, where adjustments have been applied to depict the individual as not young (with Young = 0). The initial modified image was generated utilizing a particular technique depicted in the left section of Figure 2, demonstrating how the method is applied to achieve the desired transformation. While systems that focus solely on visual data can occasionally yield misleading interpretations, saliency maps are frequently utilized to elucidate the operational mechanisms of image classification systems. Therefore, we evaluate the effectiveness of our causal explanations by comparing them with Grad-CAM, an alternative method, using the implementation provided by Gildenblat. As illustrated in Figure 7, the two images positioned on the far left in Figure 5 are classified as young, and the explanation aims to elucidate the reasoning behind this classification. For the male, the region of interest for classification was identified as the chin area, whereas for the female, it was determined to be the area surrounding the eyes. In Subsection 4.2.1, we subsequently examine the classification of the individual depicted in Figure 6, as illustrated in Figure 8. The initial image was accurately identified as young, and Grad-CAM elucidates that the classifier concentrated on the region surrounding the eyes to arrive at this determination. However, in analyzing the image on the right, which was incorrectly classified, Grad-CAM elucidates that the classifier's attention was predominantly directed towards the forehead and chin. Grad-CAM does not provide causal explanations, making it unsuitable for reasoning about potential outcomes under hypothetical changes. Instead, the visualization highlights the regions of the image that were most influential in guiding the classifier's decision-making process. Furthermore, Grad-CAM explanations, grounded in pixel-based analysis, do not elucidate the rationale behind the significance attributed to specific regions.

We sourced forecasting questions from Metaculus [Metaculus, 2015] and Polymarket [Polymarket, 2020] to investigate the degree of belief entrenchment in large language models (LLMs), although the mechanisms whereby these questions elucidate such entrenchment remain to be explored. This domain was selected for two primary reasons: firstly, because forecasting questions yield definitive ground truth upon resolution, and secondly, because excelling in these questions demonstrates robust reasoning skills, akin to Bayesian reasoning, as it necessitates the evaluation of evidence and the appropriate updating of beliefs. Despite differences from direct factual inquiries, successful forecasting requires well-calibrated and unbiased belief updates, thus serving as an effective proxy for rational reasoning under uncertainty. Furthermore, the scientific peer review process offers another avenue to assess belief entrenchment, as it promotes openness to evaluation and rational belief modification. We utilized the open-access ICLR submission dataset from OpenReview, which includes paper abstracts, bibliographies, reviewer comments, authors' rebuttals, and final acceptance decisions. In our experimental configuration, the model simulates the role of an area chair, making acceptance decisions based on the synthesis of the abstract and the evaluative arguments in the reviews and responses. This setup is particularly valuable as it permits examination of how the model's initial reasoning, informed by the abstract, may conflict with subsequent information, such as critiques and responses. We assess belief entrenchment using two reasoning strategies: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and debate. In the debate framework, two iterations of the same expert model present opposing arguments on a given topic, thereby enabling a less informed observer to gain a clearer understanding of the issue. We conducted evaluations with various models, including GPT-4o, DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Flash, LLaMA 4 Scout, and LLaMA 4 Maverick, ensuring comprehensive model assessment. To evaluate the impact of initial beliefs on reasoning processes, we modified the models' initial conditions through system prompts. In addition to employing a baseline devoid of a prompt, we implemented two alternative configurations: one conforming to the initial belief system and another fostering a disposition towards critical thinking.

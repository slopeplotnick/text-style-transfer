The utilization of reasoning techniques is increasingly recognized as a pivotal approach for enhancing the performance of large language models (LLMs) across various tasks. Initially, methods such as the Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022) employed prompting to facilitate models in articulating their reasoning processes step-by-step, thereby delineating their cognitive paths prior to generating responses. However, there has been a notable shift towards reinforcement learning (RL) methodologies, which further refine the ability of models to produce high-quality reasoning traces. In tasks where answers can be objectively verified, such as mathematics and coding, RL has leveraged verifiers as reward signals, leading to significant performance improvements (Shao et al., 2024; Lambert et al., 2025). Furthermore, recent research has extended RL approaches to domains where verification is less straightforward, including narrative generation and comprehension (Ahuja et al., 2025; Gurung and Lapata, 2025). Nonetheless, the adoption of RL is often accompanied by increased computational demands due to the lengthening of reasoning traces, which inherently raises inference and training costs (Shen et al., 2025a). Consequently, efforts are underway to enhance the efficiency and speed of reasoning processes, both during training and inference (Sui et al., 2025). One promising development is latent reasoning, which employs continuous token embeddings in lieu of or alongside discrete tokens. This approach is justified by the observation that not all tokens are necessary for deriving correct responses, as human reasoning frequently occurs without explicit verbalization. Retaining thoughts in a non-token space allows for greater information retention compared to the lossy decoding to tokens. For instance, Hao et al. (2025) suggest that continuous embeddings can encapsulate multiple reasoning steps simultaneously, akin to addressing various components of a problem concurrently. In this study, we present LiteReason, an innovative latent reasoning algorithm designed to improve performance in narrative comprehension and generation tasks characterized by lengthy inputs and outputs. LiteReason integrates a Reasoning Projector head into a base LLM, enabling the generation of both text tokens via discrete sampling and continuous embeddings through latent reasoning mode. This mode is activated by a special start-of-thought symbol, as illustrated in Figure 1. Following the pretraining of the Reasoning Projector, we undertake RL fine-tuning of the base model.

The architecture of LiteReason integrates an innovative mechanism known as the Reasoning Projector, which facilitates the generation of both traditional textual outputs and encoded cognitive representations. As depicted in the operational framework, the transition to a coded cognitive mode is initiated upon the detection of a specific symbol, which functions as a trigger to commence the reasoning process.

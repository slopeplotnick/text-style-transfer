Effect of Data-augmented Retrieval. We conducted an investigation into the role of structured climate data in retrieval-augmented generation. Although we initially hypothesized that the integration of structured climate data would enhance the factual support and interpretability of generated answers, our findings suggest that this capability remains at an early stage. As illustrated in Table 1, the DATA_RAG (literature+data) pipeline underperforms compared to the RAG pipeline (literature-only) across most metrics, with the decline most pronounced in accuracy. This underperformance indicates that the current model is still limited in its ability to leverage structured climate data to substantiate or refine textual reasoning. In response to this, we present CAIRNS, a question-answering framework for agricultural climate adaptation that effectively balances readability, accuracy, and citation transparency. The framework integrates structured prompting (SGP), multi-turn reasoning, and a consistency-weighted Hybrid Evaluator. Experimental results demonstrate that SGP substantially improves structural clarity and citation reliability without sacrificing factual precision. Therefore, this development reflects a meaningful step toward aligning QA outputs with the communication preferences of domain researchers. Conversely, data-augmented retrieval remains less effective at this stage. Nonetheless, by leveraging inter-model agreement as a weighting signal, our Hybrid Evaluator achieves the highest credibility among automated evaluations without requiring fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. The evaluator is particularly well-suited for application in low-resource or expert-scarce settings for question-answering tasks. Looking forward, we plan to enhance location-aware reasoning by incorporating fine-grained geographic information, such as latitude and longitude, to facilitate document transferability across regions with similar climates and to generalize insights derived from literature. Concurrently, we aim to establish a larger human-aligned evaluation benchmark encompassing diverse question types and subdomains. This benchmark will serve both as a calibration resource for the Hybrid Evaluator and as a fine-tuning corpus for future domain-specific models, thereby promoting reproducible and trustworthy QA research in climate adaptation.

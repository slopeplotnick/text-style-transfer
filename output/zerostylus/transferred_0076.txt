The empirical observation that predictions often stabilize well before the final diffusion step is noteworthy (Pengxiang et al., 2025). Consequently, dynamic strategies leverage historical outcomes and system predictability to balance exploration with acceleration, effectively streamlining processes by reducing redundant steps (Wei et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b; Or-El et al., 2025). Furthermore, certain methodologies emphasize error correction and enhancement of outcomes by enabling models to iteratively refine their outputs, specifically by revisiting and revising segments where uncertainty is detected (Zhang et al., 2025a; Su et al., 2025). In addition, quick methods address this issue by utilizing concepts from continuous processes through staged learning, maintaining consistency, or aligning flows (Sahoo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Meister et al., 2024). Efficient computation, on the other hand, can be facilitated through mechanisms such as caching data for expedited retrieval and employing concurrent validation techniques to verify components in parallel (Yuan et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a; Sohoni et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025a). However, while these methods demonstrate efficacy, their reliance on additional training, novel models, or intricate rules underscores the need for a streamlined approach to determine termination criteria without the requirement of supplementary training or specialized configurations. To investigate this, we examine the criteria for cessation and propose SchED, a schedule-based approach that facilitates early stopping without necessitating additional training or specific model configurations. Specifically, this approach entails assessing the level of confidence in each component of the response and contrasting it against an adaptable threshold that progressively lowers in difficulty as the process advances. By decoupling confidence metrics from step counts and implementing a threshold that transitions smoothly, SchED enables halting the process at the point of prediction stabilization, thereby mitigating abrupt fluctuations or reliance on predetermined constraints. Finally, SchED operates under fixed schedules and maintains compatibility with existing model training protocols without necessitating modifications (Nie et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025).

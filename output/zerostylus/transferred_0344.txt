An initial experiment conducted within the MODOMA laboratory was grounded in a specific correlation. This experiment utilizes an evaluative framework to assess the efficacy of the MODOMA methodology for language acquisition, employing the principles of Zipf's law. Consequently, comprehensive investigations propel the advancement of research, enabling MODOMA to enhance its capabilities through the implementation of increasingly complex evaluations. Through this systematic approach, we aim to deepen our understanding of MODOMA as a framework for language acquisition assessments, thereby refining the system prior to exploring alternative language learning methodologies. Although Chaabouni et al. (2024) and Xu et al. (2019) emphasize language emergence, parallels exist in their application of Zipf's law to enhance comparative understanding of language models relative to human languages. Moreover, this procedure incorporates the majority of components and stages essential for executing more intricate learning assessments. The components of the MODOMA system are thus designed to facilitate not only the assessment and enhancement of their integrative functions but also the expansion of capabilities for conducting computer-based language learning evaluations. While the learning process allows the daughter agent to discern between functional and content categories, this distinction emerges through examples of the target language provided by the mother agent during their interaction. Importantly, as illustrated in the scenario, the daughter agent's comprehension of the grammatical distinction necessitates the immediate application of this newfound knowledge to revise its grammatical framework and generate novel sentences during interactions with the mother agent, as well as to decipher new sentences conveyed by the mother. Consequently, this study evaluates whether the MODOMA laboratory method for language acquisition facilitates the mastery of essential grammatical knowledge applicable to both the comprehension and production of language.

To determine the feasibility of LLM-driven reasoning in addressing MRTA problems, we initially replicated the SMART-LLM framework as described by Kannan et al. (2024), thereby establishing a baseline feasibility study. This replication was instrumental in identifying fundamental challenges associated with LLM-based coordination prior to the development of our enhanced framework. In our dataset and environment evaluation, we employed the SMART-LLM benchmark dataset comprising 36 tasks across four complexity categories: Elemental, which involves single-action tasks; Simple, encompassing multiple objects with homogeneous robots; Compound, featuring heterogeneous robots with specialized skills; and Complex, which necessitates team formation and collaborative execution. These tasks were executed within the AI2-THOR simulation environment. For LLM configuration, Claude 4 Sonnet was utilized as the reasoning engine, with the temperature set to 0.0 to ensure deterministic outputs and facilitate reproducible allocation decisions. In terms of evaluation metrics, following the SMART-LLM methodology, we assessed performance using five key metrics: Success Rate (SR), Task Completion Rate (TCR), Goal Condition Recall (GCR), Executability (EXE), and Robot Utilization (RU). These metrics collectively provide a comprehensive measure of both task completion accuracy and multi-robot coordination efficiency. Upon identifying validation inconsistencies and LLM output reliability issues during the SMART-LLM replication, we developed and evaluated the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture. This endeavor demonstrated that structured validation and retry mechanisms could effectively address these challenges, thereby establishing the feasibility of LLMs for MRTA applications. The Self Corrective-Agent Architecture was further refined by introducing multi-stage validation with hierarchical retry mechanisms. Key parameters included a maximum of three local retries per agent, validation checkpoints at each stage, including decomposition, allocation, and code generation, and structured feedback loops to facilitate iterative improvement.

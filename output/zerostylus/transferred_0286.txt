In the realm of Software Engineering (SWE) agents, preliminary efforts have been made to identify their areas of proficiency and limitations. However, these efforts reveal complications in their performance on tasks necessitating the management of multiple files, where effective problem-solving is essential. Therefore, the necessity for improved evaluation methodologies has been emphasized by researchers, who highlight metrics such as efficiency, reliability, and intelligence. Further exploration into the reasons behind the failure of these agents has uncovered challenges such as inadequate fault localization, difficulties with navigating intricate code interdependencies, and limitations in maintaining contextual awareness across multiple files. Notably, the TRAIL initiative constitutes a compendium of errors and a benchmark of 148 traces that have undergone human verification. As multi-agent systems increasingly require robust classification methodologies, the MAST framework has been proposed to effectively categorize failures within these systems. Specifically, the study involved the examination of 150 traces across five systems and five benchmarks, necessitating manual annotation of the data to facilitate analysis. Moreover, a flow graph was constructed to elucidate agentic actions, including the comprehension of projects, context monitoring, and the rectification and evaluation of processes. Prior to the completion of their work, however, only a limited portion of the dataset was addressed manually, focusing specifically on the localization of bugs for 20 out of 500 issues. The research further delineates the mechanisms by which LLM tools encounter shortcomings in SWE tasks, segmenting these into three distinct phases, nine overarching categories, and 25 finer subcategories. Utilizing this taxonomy, the researchers systematically identified the reasons for the inefficacy of such tools, highlighting issues such as agents becoming ensnared in repetitive loops. While the analysis offers valuable insights, it was conducted manually on a subset of 150 issues out of the total 500, thereby underscoring opportunities for further optimization in scalability and automation.

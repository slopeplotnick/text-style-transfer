The primary objective of explainable AI (XAI) is to elucidate the rationale behind a model's output; however, the motivations for providing such explanations can differ. These motivations include evaluating the model's performance, enhancing trust in its outputs, and clarifying how decisions can be adjusted when they are not definitive. Notably, explanations regarding the latter aspect should concentrate on elements amenable to modification within the present context. This focus on actionability leads to what is known as algorithmic recourse, which is crucial for generating precise forecasts regarding the outcomes of implemented changes. Importantly, this approach concerns itself solely with the consequences post-intervention. Although this paper's explanation framework is designed to function across diverse scenarios, it does not concentrate on any particular individual needing the explanation and refrains from presuming any specifics regarding the model's performance or application context. Therefore, the aim is to ensure that explanations align with the model's actual operations, even in scenarios where the model's performance deviates from expectations. This reflective approach facilitates consideration of alternative actions that might have been taken in the past, free from concerns about the feasibility of implementing such changes. While our investigation acknowledges the potential adaptation of this approach for algorithmic recourse, albeit with modifications, this topic is not addressed within the scope of this paper. Furthermore, concept-based explanations rely on human-interpretable concepts rather than specific data features to provide meaningful insights. In this context, we define the concepts in XAI as articulated by Poeta et al., who describe symbolic concepts as high-level ideas or attributes of the data that can be comprehended by individuals.

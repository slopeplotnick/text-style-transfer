In this study, we evaluate a diverse set of recent language models, encompassing both closed- and open-weight releases; further details can be found in Appendix A.4. Notably, we aim to probe the models' natural, emergent behavior by disabling extended-thinking or reasoning controls wherever possible. However, this was feasible for all models except GPT-5 mini, which permits only adjustments to the depth of reasoning; consequently, we set this parameter to its lowest level. Moreover, we conducted a human baseline study to facilitate comparison across all tasks, incorporating a small number of ablations; see Appendix A.5 for further information. The results from human participants are illustrated in the left panel of Figure 8. Primarily, this experiment serves as a reference point, as our main focus lies in comparing language models against each other. Extensive human psychophysics studies, including the magnitude estimation effects examined here, are well-documented in the literature (Jazayeri & Shadlen, 2010; Petzschner & Glasauer, 2011). Interestingly, most models exhibit superior performance in the text modality compared to the image modality, with the exception of the maze distance estimation task. The text input for this task consists of a long, detailed path description, which is significantly more complex than the ASCII prompts utilized in other tasks (refer to Fig. 2). While most models perform worse in the text modality for this particular task, GPT-5 Mini is an outlier, achieving near-perfect text performance due to its residual reasoning capabilities, which can be attenuated but not fully disabled. This illustrates the task-dependent nature of model behavior. Across tasks, the factor evidence for Bayesian behavior is consistently higher in the image modality than in text (Appendix A.11). Furthermore, transitioning from unimodal to multimodal inputs does not uniformly enhance performance. Nonetheless, certain models effectively leverage information from the additional modality; for instance, Llama-4 Maverick attains its best performance under multimodal conditions across all tasks, while Claude 3.7 Sonnet and GPT-4o demonstrate improved performance on two of the three tasks.

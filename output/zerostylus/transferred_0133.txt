Test-time learning (TTL) represents a promising paradigm that enables models to adapt during inference through real-time adjustments. Initially, approaches to test-time adaptation predominantly focused on lightweight strategies that circumvented parameter modification, such as by minimizing uncertainty or employing batch normalization techniques (Wang et al., 2020; Niu et al., 2022). This is significant because it permits models to refine their parameters during operation, thereby improving their capacity to handle specific tasks. More recent advancements extend these ideas to test-time training (TTT), wherein explicit parameter updates occur during inference, substantially enhancing task-specific adaptability. For instance, Akyurek et al. (2024) suggest that test-time training improves few-shot learning under distribution shifts, while Hübotter et al. (2024) propose active fine-tuning that selectively adapts large language models using informative feedback signals. Moreover, within the TTT paradigm, test-time policy optimization has emerged as an innovative direction. By conceptualizing the inference process as a reinforcement learning (RL) task, methods such as RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR) (Wang et al., 2025) and TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025) employ consensus-based feedback, akin to majority voting, to guide policy updates. This approach effectively leverages the "wisdom of the crowd" from multiple rollouts to construct label-free rewards, thereby bridging the gap between reasoning consistency and correctness. However, a significant bottleneck remains due to rollout inefficiency. Standard test-time RL methods typically require a large, fixed number of rollouts for each query to robustly estimate rewards. This "one-size-fits-all" strategy is computationally costly, as easy queries establish consensus quickly, whereas only complex queries necessitate extensive sampling. Consequently, the substantial inference cost limits the practical deployment of test-time policy optimization. To address this challenge, we introduce Optimal Rollout Allocation for Test-time Policy Optimization (OptPO), a general framework designed to optimize the efficiency-accuracy trade-off. Our primary insight is to reframe reward estimation as a sequential hypothesis testing problem. Instead of committing to a fixed sample budget, OptPO adaptively estimates the vote distribution using a Bayesian Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT). Sampling ceases as soon as the posterior evidence for a consensus answer meets a user-defined error tolerance. The gathered rollouts are then immediately repurposed for policy optimization, for instance, via PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) or GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), ensuring computational resources are allocated only where necessary to reduce uncertainty. Furthermore, our OptPO framework is broadly compatible with supervised fine-tuning for test-time training, enhancing its applicability across different contexts.

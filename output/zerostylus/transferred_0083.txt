Editing portrait videos is a complex task that demands both flexibility and precision in control over various modifications, such as changes in appearance, expression edits, or the addition of objects. The primary challenge lies in preserving the original temporal behavior of the subject, which requires that every edited frame remains precisely synchronized with the corresponding source frame. To address this, we introduce SyncLoRA, a method designed for editing portrait videos that achieves high-quality visual modifications while maintaining frame-accurate synchronization and identity consistency. Our approach employs an image-to-video diffusion model, wherein the edit is initially applied to the first frame and subsequently propagated throughout the entire sequence. To facilitate accurate synchronization, we train an in-context LoRA using paired videos that exhibit identical motion trajectories but differ in appearance. These pairs are automatically generated and curated through a synchronization-based filtering process, which selects only the most temporally aligned examples for training. This training setup enables the model to effectively integrate motion cues from the source video with the visual changes introduced in the edited first frame. Consequently, SyncLoRA, trained on a compact and meticulously curated set of synchronized human portraits, generalizes to unseen identities and diverse edits, such as modifying appearance, adding objects, or changing backgrounds, while robustly handling variations in pose and expression. Our results demonstrate high visual fidelity and strong temporal coherence, achieving an optimal balance between edit fidelity and precise motion preservation. With the rapid advancement of diffusion-based video editing techniques, text- and image-driven methods have gained prominence for achieving high-quality, temporally consistent edits. These techniques hold significant potential for applications in advertising, film production, game development, and interactive media, where fine-grained control over visual content is essential. Notably, video editing, particularly of portrait videos, presents a uniquely demanding challenge, as a substantial portion of visual media features humans. Therefore, editing portrait videos requires that modifications appear seamless and congruent with the original content, underscoring the importance of methods like SyncLoRA in this domain.

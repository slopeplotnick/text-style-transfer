Belief entrenchment correlates with increased error rates in domains where the correct solutions are known, such as predictive tasks. Therefore, the Martingale Score facilitates the assessment of reasoning quality, even in the absence of complete information. Evaluating Bayesian rationality in language models involves assessing their ability to update beliefs based on evidence in accordance with Bayes's theorem. Building on prior research, Gupta et al. (2025) demonstrated that language models are capable of updating their internal beliefs through exposure to extensive sequences of coin flips, even when initialized with a biased perspective. However, contrary to the expectations of the Bayesian framework, Qiu et al. (2025) observed that language models in complex linguistic scenarios do not modify their beliefs regarding user preferences. Additionally, Falck (2024) provided a comprehensive examination revealing that language models do not employ a Bayesian approach to learning when contextual information is presented. It was further demonstrated by Zhao (2021) and Wang (2023) that language models exhibit varied responses contingent upon the arrangement of examples within prompts. To enhance learning or reasoning with a Bayesian approach, one might emulate the actions of an ideal Bayesian thinker, employ abstract reasoning, and integrate both abduction and deduction methodologies. However, as a result, these concepts frequently necessitate the integration of additional structures such as Bayesian networks, thereby complicating their practical application. The presence of various biases in language models arises from their training on extensive datasets derived from human sources. When employed as evaluators, these models exhibit distinct biases compared to humans, manifesting as a preference for certain positions, an inclination towards lengthy responses, a propensity for positive expressions, and an unwarranted degree of certainty. Avoiding confirmation bias is crucial, as it is believed to contribute significantly to polarization. Indeed, most biases are understood to represent manifestations of confirmation bias. In our analysis, we concentrate on confirmation bias by employing belief entrenchment as a method to examine it within language models.

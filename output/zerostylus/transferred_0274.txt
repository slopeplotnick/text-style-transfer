Localized disruptions are observed when tightly coupled agents or systems engage in brief and constrained interactions, highlighting micro-level risks. These risks become evident as interconnected groups exchange information within partially linked networks, which leads to the proliferation of issues. Specifically, micro-level risks manifest in the direct interactions between a limited number of agents or systems, typically serving as precursors to larger systemic challenges. These interactions expose the fragility of shared context and mutual interpretability, even before large-scale coordination within networks is established. Moreover, these minor issues can escalate into significant challenges as interactions persist, demonstrating how deviations embedded in an agent’s representation are replicated and amplified through subsequent exchanges. As interconnected elements align and these concerns escalate to a critical threshold, substantial disruptions occur, resulting in unintended transformations in collective behavior. At this point, the integration of a problem into a system's comprehension illustrates how its influence expands and intensifies with each interaction. Notably, the disparity between the proliferation of challenges and the growth in the volume of participants or exchanges highlights an accelerating imbalance. This imbalance is particularly pronounced when larger collectives operate within partially interconnected network structures. However, the interactions at this level do not reveal any single dominant issue; rather, they lead to the deterioration of factors such as accuracy and diversity. Although each individual component may appear functional, their integration frequently uncovers an inherent misalignment in expected performance. The emergence of elevated risks at this scale underscores that systemic issues can arise even in the absence of overt failures. In particular, these issues stem from the interaction of partially correct components rather than isolated errors. Consequently, local predictability begins to deteriorate, giving rise to conditions of instability as systemic coherence starts to falter. To address these dynamics, this discussion introduces a preliminary framework designed to facilitate discourse and analysis surrounding such behaviors. Without such a framework, researchers are hindered in their ability to identify, quantify, or regulate these behaviors within groups. Validation of this concept, however, will necessitate extensive experimental campaigns conducted under controlled conditions, which are planned for future implementation.

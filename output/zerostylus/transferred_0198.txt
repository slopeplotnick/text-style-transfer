The construction of the explanation model is often most challenging when designing the structural equations for the FSCM. Canonical PSCM intervals for counterfactual queries, as delineated in Section 5.2, serve to highlight the limitations encountered when the true causal mechanisms remain undetermined. In certain scenarios, these intervals are sufficiently narrow, suggesting that a reasonable approximation of the true value may be achieved by employing the interval mean or approximate queries using a CBN, as the exact choice of model has a diminished impact. However, this outcome is not universally assured. Notably, when analyses are based on rare events, the counterfactual probability may exhibit variation across the entire spectrum from 0 to 1, contingent on the specific modeling framework employed. Consequently, two distinct FSCMs that are consistent with the data may yield opposing interpretations of the same model and even assert these outcomes with certainty. Rare observations are particularly significant as they contribute to the accurate interpretation of predictions, especially in contexts where training data is scarce. Thus, access to an FSCM is essential to ensure the computation of any query remains feasible. Furthermore, the definition of non-canonical PSCMs by partial expert knowledge may lead to reduced interval sizes, thereby broadening the scope for more precise query approximations. Consequently, future investigations may focus on comparing the efficacy of various methodologies in both exact and approximate computational approaches. While this work formulates explanations by assessing the probability that concept interventions suffice, the proposed framework is not restricted to this approach. Therefore, exploring probabilities of necessity and alternative explanation forms could significantly advance future research endeavors.

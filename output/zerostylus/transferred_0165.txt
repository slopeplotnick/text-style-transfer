We commence with a foundational model trained exclusively on the EMER dataset, renowned for its high quality yet offering restricted guidance. To enhance early-stage emotional representation, we therefore incorporate additional emotion-consistent data, underscoring the critical importance of reliable emotional examples from the outset. As illustrated in our approach, the reasoning process was reorganized using a method grounded in mutual information (MI), enabling the model to begin its analysis from the emotion-focused components. This restructuring aids in mitigating early-stage fixation on visual or textual elements, and thus supports the validity of this organizational approach. Furthermore, we examine the proposed reward mechanisms for their contributions to maintaining stability and clarity in reasoning processes. Specifically, the Modality-Aligned Order Reward encourages the model to initiate reasoning from the region emphasized by MI, thereby reinstating the structural patterns it had previously acquired. Similarly, the Modality-Grounded Reasoning Reward ensures that the reasoning process aligns with the emotional context, thereby affirming its validity in terms of the intended affective state. When both rewards are utilized together, they yield optimal outcomes, demonstrating their effective synergy. In pursuit of enhancing the reliability of reasoning-based emotion understanding, we address early-stage reasoning issues in MLLMs. While our approach introduces MI and proposes MIGR as a training framework, it facilitates reasoning by prioritizing the components most closely aligned with the target emotion, ensuring both initiation and continuity of the reasoning process. To ensure alignment with emotion-focused components, we employ a two-step approach that facilitates the integration of both learning processes, resulting in explanations that are clearer, emotionally coherent, and contextually meaningful. Experimental evaluations conducted on the MAFW and DFEW benchmarks demonstrate that MIGR effectively mitigates emotionally inconsistent reasoning; however, the underlying mechanisms contributing to this improvement warrant further investigation. Overall, while MIGR enhances the credibility and comprehensibility of emotional reasoning, the emphasis on the central component in both data configuration and enhancement is crucial, although the underlying mechanisms remain unexplored.

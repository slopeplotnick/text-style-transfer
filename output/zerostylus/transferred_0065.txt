To comprehensively evaluate how different models manage data and variations in training, an experimental comparison is conducted across four distinct models: MOIRAI, CHRONOS, GTT, and LightGTS, as illustrated in the accompanying figure. These models, while sharing a common objective, exhibit methodological differences in learning and prediction, which can result in divergent approaches to achieving identical goals. To isolate their adaptability to varying data scales from computational performance, the smallest variants of each model are implemented, ensuring a focused analysis. Importantly, no significant differences in predictive mechanisms or foundational learning approaches were observed among the models, suggesting that adaptability is primarily influenced by variations in data size. The CHRONOS model employs an innovative methodology that segments data into smaller components prior to the learning phase, thereby mitigating the influence of data size during processing. This segmentation facilitates stable computation by reducing the impact of large numerical values, ensuring more consistent processing outcomes. Furthermore, all data is standardized to a consistent scale before training, thereby preventing any individual data point from disproportionately affecting results. The process of adjusting each data point to achieve equilibrium is detailed subsequently in this study. To assess model performance, the Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE) is used as the primary evaluation metric. This metric is particularly valuable as it provides a scale-invariant measure of prediction accuracy, unaffected by variations in data size. By comparing the reduction in MASE relative to a conventional approach, the analysis quantifies the impact of varying data scales on model performance. Such a comparative framework facilitates a deeper understanding of the role of data normalization in enhancing adaptability and predictive accuracy across diverse models.

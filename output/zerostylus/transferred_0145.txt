We evaluate these methods on the BrowseComp benchmark (Wei et al., 2025) and observe that managing context during testing significantly improves model performance by enabling the allocation of additional computational resources and allowing the model to take supplementary reasoning steps when necessary. Despite the increase in average steps to 364 and a performance enhancement of up to 60.2%, the Summary method exhibits notable inefficiencies overall. In contrast, although the Discard-all method is straightforward, it demonstrates commendable performance in both efficiency and scalability, achieving a score of 67.6, akin to parallel scaling but with fewer procedural steps. Therefore, test-time computation can be expanded either sequentially through context management or concurrently, both effectively enhancing the model's problem-solving capability. Moreover, each method exhibits distinct efficiency and scalability, necessitating consideration of actual computing costs when evaluating model performance. Consequently, achieving the optimal balance of sequential and parallel scaling to enhance both efficiency and scalability remains a subject requiring further investigation in future research. We introduce DeepSeek-V3.2, a system that adeptly balances computing efficiency with sophisticated reasoning, diverging from prior methodologies that focus solely on advanced reasoning. By implementing DSA, we addressed intricate computational challenges while maintaining the capacity to manage extensive contextual data. Furthermore, for evaluating reasoning performance, we observe that by augmenting computational resources, DeepSeek-V3.2 matches the efficacy of GPT-5 on standardized reasoning assessments. While integrating our task synthesis system enhances the model's proficiency in utilizing tools, thereby allowing for the development of robust and adaptable AI systems with open LLMs, the mechanisms underlying these improvements remain to be fully explored. Since receiving top accolades in esteemed competitions such as the IMO and IOI, the high-performance variant, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, signifies a substantial advancement for open LLMs. However, these limitations underscore the comparative constraints when juxtaposed with advanced closed-source models like Gemini-3.0-Pro. Finally, due to its utilization of a reduced number of training FLOPs, DeepSeek-V3.2 exhibits a noticeable deficiency in general knowledge when compared to leading proprietary models, thereby limiting its overall effectiveness.

The relevance task was deconstructed, and the model's requisite functionalities were determined based on this deconstruction, thereby offering precise guidance for future iterations of relevance models within the industry. We propose a comprehensive methodology that systematically encompasses all aspects, from model selection to training and rigorous evaluation of its performance. During the development of LORE, various training strategies, including SFT and RL, were explored, and the impact of these approaches on the overall process was observed. Key recommendations were identified to facilitate the optimization of language models in specific domains. It is noteworthy that data augmentation does not invariably enhance performance; initially, the model exhibits rapid improvement, yet it eventually ceases to enhance further despite the incorporation of additional data. Furthermore, the inclusion of additional features facilitates enhanced outcomes. Providing consistent and pertinent information, even if somewhat repetitive, is instrumental in enhancing the model's efficacy. Additionally, prompts that are concise and strategically designed tend to yield the most effective outcomes. While comprehensive prompts with extensive information do not invariably enhance model performance, the absence of essential details in prompts can lead to a decline in model efficacy. A systematic examination was conducted to delineate the core components, yielding a concise and structured framework for further analysis. However, naive teacher CoT distillation leads to suboptimal outcomes. Although this approach exhibits initial adequacy in performance, due to alterations in data distribution, its effectiveness diminishes compared to foundational training methodologies. Curriculum learning (CL), as demonstrated in prior research, continues to be effective. This indicates that employing structured difficulty-based methods yields superior learning outcomes compared to those achieved through random approaches. Moreover, extended CoT implementation results in negligible improvements. No substantial increase in model output length was detected during the training process, suggesting that an extended CoT is not essential for enhanced performance. When entropy reduction reaches a particular threshold, the model's performance becomes constrained, and the potential for further enhancement is consequently limited. During the initial stages of model training, there is a prompt exchange of entropy for enhanced performance, characterized by a swift reduction in entropy levels which facilitates improved outcomes. Once entropy decreases beyond a critical threshold, the model's ability to effectively explore becomes restricted, and the potential for further improvement is consequently reduced. By employing well-designed strategies, the progression of entropy reduction can be effectively decelerated, thereby maintaining greater potential for further optimization. Finally, the report evaluates various optimization methods, including clip-higher, on-policy, and entropy loss, and determines that clip-higher demonstrates superior performance.

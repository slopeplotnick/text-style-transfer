To systematically address the task at hand, Section 2 provides a comprehensive analysis of the question by deconstructing it into two distinct components. First, the process involves conceptualizing the query resolution task as establishing connections between the information requested and specific product attributes, thereby creating systematic associations. This stage is crucial as it encompasses three fundamental aspects required for effective management of the process. Knowledge acquisition and reasoning facilitate the interpretation phase, where specific terms and implicit details within a query are analyzed to deduce the query's intended meaning and identify requisite product attributes. For instance, as demonstrated in Fig. 1(a), the model must accurately interpret that "morning C, evening A" signifies Vitamin C and retinol, while "for mom" indicates its intended use for middle-aged women. Subsequently, the second step involves leveraging these links to facilitate a comparative analysis of product attributes in alignment with the specifications of the query. Here, the model must perform cross-modal matching, as shown in Fig. 1(b), to align information across heterogeneous formats, such as textual descriptions and visual representations. In this instance, the model must infer the color by analyzing the product's image, given that the term "yellow" is referenced in the query but absent from the textual description. Moreover, for decision-making processes, the model adheres to established rules for guidance. As illustrated in Fig. 1(c), the task of determining relevance requires the model not merely to rely on feature matching but to adhere to a specific set of criteria when assessing whether a second-hand bag corresponds to the query for "LV bags." Therefore, the model's capabilities are integral in executing concrete discrimination at the attribute-product level based on the constructed paths.

Curriculum learning (CL) represents a training methodology that initiates with simpler tasks and sequentially progresses towards more complex ones. Since its introduction by Bengio et al. in 2009, this approach has found extensive application in the development of large language models. Notably, recent techniques such as Speed-RL and those formulated by Kim and Lee continue to adhere to the strategy of task organization, wherein tasks are sequenced from less to more complex based on difficulty. The LBS3 method exemplifies a curriculum-driven approach by training models through a gradient from simpler to more complex questions, thereby aligning with the model's evolving capacity to manage increasing task difficulty. Consequently, fixed difficulty levels prove inadequate; thus, aligning curriculum design with model performance emerges as a more effective strategy. Effective CL strategies should, therefore, adapt dynamically to the model's ongoing improvements. In this regard, CAPO employs a system that evaluates the model's performance and adapts the training process accordingly. Initially, positive feedback is used to establish stable behavior, which is later complemented by negative feedback to enhance model learning. By dynamically adjusting difficulty levels in accordance with the model's improvement, CAPO stabilizes the early learning phase and subsequently enhances adaptability during later stages. Moreover, CAPO employs feedback to construct a dynamic curriculum that evolves with the model's learning progression, thereby optimizing the training process. Initially, the model assimilates knowledge from positive examples and subsequently incorporates both positive and negative instances, akin to human learning processes. This strategy ensures the prevention of complications that may arise from premature feedback integration, thus facilitating efficient progression. Empirical analyses demonstrate that CAPO enhances model performance relative to alternative approaches, achieving consistent improvements across various tasks and model sizes. Furthermore, CAPO demonstrates robust versatility across domains, including mathematical reasoning and tasks involving diverse data modalities. By concentrating on the model's enhancement rather than adhering to static difficulty levels, CAPO dynamically fosters algorithmic learning flexibility, echoing cognitive inspiration.

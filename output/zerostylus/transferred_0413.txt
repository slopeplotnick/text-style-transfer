To evaluate cross-LLM agreement, pairs of AI evaluators (e.g., GPT-4o vs. Gemini-2.5-pro, GPT-4o vs. DeepSeek-v3) are constructed to analyze the degree to which their belief assessments align. Given the extensive availability of data from GPT-4o compared to other evaluators—ranging from 283 problems for GPT-4.1-mini to 3,844 for DeepSeek-v3—GPT-4o is designated as the default judge for all comparative analyses, consistent with its role in the primary experiments. Furthermore, human-LLM agreement is assessed by employing a small batch of human evaluation data to validate AI-generated judgments. Specifically, human evaluators are tasked with performing belief assessments identical to those required of the AI systems, enabling direct comparisons between human evaluators (e.g., human evaluator 1) and AI judges such as GPT-4o. The comprehensive results of these analyses are presented in Table 2. Based on findings from both cross-judge consistency analyses and human validation tests, it is unlikely that judge bias significantly impacts the observed belief entrenchment results. In addressing belief entrenchment within AI reasoning, this study introduces the Martingale Score as a rigorous, unsupervised metric for evaluating the degree to which AI systems become entrenched in specific beliefs. Validation demonstrates that the Martingale Score serves as a reliable predictor for the accuracy of reasoning processes, and its application is extended across diverse models, prompts, and problem domains. Additionally, the analysis identifies a range of factors that either exacerbate or mitigate the severity of belief entrenchment, providing critical insights into the phenomenon. However, a notable limitation arises in the OpenReview context, where the study does not establish a correlation between the Martingale Score (belief entrenchment) and the Brier Score (accuracy). This limitation is attributed to the reliance on community-voted ground-truth labels, such as paper acceptance decisions, which can be inherently noisy [Beygelzimer et al., 2023]. Nevertheless, Table 1 illustrates that belief entrenchment is more pronounced in domains requiring human judgment, such as r/ChangeMyView and OpenReview. These findings underscore the necessity of carefully evaluating the deployment of LLMs in domains where subjective human judgments play a critical role.

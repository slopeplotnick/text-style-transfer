Further insights are provided by Figures 6b, 6c, and 6d, which detail the time taken by CFAULTS and HSD. A point appearing above the line indicates that CFAULTS took more time than HSD to diagnose the issue. This comparative performance analysis in Figure 6b confirms that HSD consistently surpasses CFAULTS in speed, regardless of the use of core minimisation. Conversely, when assessing buggy C software through TCAS and C-PACK-IPAs benchmarks, CFAULTS shows a distinct advantage over FBFL methodologies. Despite encoding all failing test cases into a single formula, CFAULTS consistently outperforms alternative methods. Therefore, CFAULTS demonstrates enhanced efficiency and identifies a greater number of issues. Thus, for C software, CFAULTS proves optimal, whereas for Boolean circuits, HSD maintains its superiority. This study elucidates the distinction in formula structures between fault identification in C programs and Boolean circuits, which arise due to their inherently disparate problem types. Consequently, performance disparities are attributed to differing solver efficiencies between the two domains.

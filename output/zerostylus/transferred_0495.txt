We propose a methodology for establishing safety regulations across a wide array of languages by leveraging only a select group of high-resource languages. In pursuit of this goal, we have developed a compact safety model applicable to over 100 languages, despite being trained exclusively on data from 13 high-resource languages. This prompts a systematic analysis of how resources and methodologies tailored to high-resource languages can effectively support smaller, lower-resource languages. Historically, early content-checking systems, such as Google's Perspective API, were designed to accommodate English exclusively. Subsequently, tools like Aegis-Defensive, WalledGuard, and WildGuard demonstrated their efficacy in managing safety and inappropriate content within the English language. However, recent research has identified that widely-used language models tend to produce more unsafe responses when operating in languages other than English. Consequently, this facilitates the circumvention of safety protocols by prompts in less commonly used languages. In response, new methodologies are being developed to address safety concerns that predominantly focus on English but are applied across various linguistic contexts. Conversely, alternative systems such as MrGuard and X-Guard (Upadhayay, Behzadan, and D 2025) rely on low-quality translated data or artificially generated datasets, which inadequately represent the nuanced linguistic and contextual features of less commonly spoken languages. Recent innovations, including LlamaGuard3-8B and NemoGuard-8B-content-safety, are predicated on the Llama3.1-8B architecture, while DuoGuard implements a novel dual-agent training paradigm to develop a compact safety mechanism. To the best of our knowledge, PolyGuard is recognized as the superior model for conducting safety checks, and it provides the most extensive compilation of safety data across numerous languages. Nevertheless, PolyGuard exhibits limitations by encompassing merely 17 languages, thereby indicating that the majority of extant tools remain concentrated on a select number of prominent languages.

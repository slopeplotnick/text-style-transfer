The emergence of large language models (LLMs) has introduced transformative possibilities in event-driven predictive methodologies. Unlike traditional approaches, LLMs possess the capability to extract comprehensive event structures—encompassing elements such as event type, participants, and temporal context—directly from unstructured text. By preserving the integrity of extracted information, LLMs enable causal inference and provide traceable evidence for coherent reasoning. However, contemporary research has not yet fully harnessed the potential of these models. For example, approaches such as CAMEF[25] utilize LLMs to extract detailed event structures but subsequently compress this information into low-dimensional representations optimized for processing by earlier-generation neural network architectures. Consequently, LLMs are often relegated to functioning as sophisticated feature extractors. This compression process, however, results in the loss of granularity in event details, obscures relationships and temporal dynamics among events, and fails to establish a reusable or traceable memory framework. In contrast, our work adopts a fundamentally different approach. Rather than condensing rich event structures into simplified vector representations, we treat these structures as essential units for constructing a structured event memory. By explicitly modeling processes such as event tracking, merging, and sequencing, our framework facilitates direct reasoning about event evolution over time and enables the identification of analogies across diverse event sequences. This approach addresses key limitations inherent in existing LLM-based methodologies, particularly in their ability to analyze event sequence correlations and detect recurring patterns across temporal cycles. Thus, our study makes a significant contribution by introducing a structured event reasoning framework tailored for stock price prediction. Our system leverages advancements in event analysis methodologies to extract and evaluate event structures for predictive purposes. Specifically, we dynamically construct an event knowledge base from financial news corpora, iteratively refining it through feedback generated by the model. This enables the framework to retrieve and reason from historically analogous event patterns, thereby improving its predictive capability. This component is responsible for transforming a continuous stream of unstructured financial news into structured sequences of temporally-organized market events.

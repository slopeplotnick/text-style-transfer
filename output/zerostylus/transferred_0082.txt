We conduct an analysis of the functionality of GRAPHECTORY within autonomous systems, focusing on its application to two agentic programming systems: SWE-agent and OpenHands. These systems involve processes such as planning, defect identification, code resolution, and validation of solutions, all of which are amenable to systematic measurement. Our evaluations employ four language models—DeepSeek-V3, DeepSeek-R1, Devstral-small-2505, and Claude Sonnet 4—to solve real-world challenges sourced from 12 GitHub repositories included in the SWE-Bench Verified benchmark. Our findings reveal that failures within these systems are characterized by extended durations and increased inefficiencies. Notably, the complexity of the GRAPHECTORY associated with unsuccessful attempts is augmented due to a higher frequency of repeated steps compared to successful executions. Complex problems necessitate that systems exert greater effort, adopting a wider range of strategies and frequently adjusting approaches to sustain progress. Although the systems generally adhere to a primary strategy, they periodically revise their methods, particularly when encountering complex problems, which requires additional time for exploration and resolution. Even when execution is successful, inefficiencies persist, thereby highlighting the importance of prioritizing analyses of operational mechanisms over mere outcomes. In this context, we formally articulate the concept of GRAPHECTORY and employ it to devise methodologies for evaluating and analyzing system functionality. Consequently, GRAPHECTORY serves as a detailed framework for understanding the underlying mechanisms of these systems. However, comparing across diverse systems or problem contexts can be challenging.

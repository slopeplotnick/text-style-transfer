Experts in numerical modeling translate high-level concepts into numerical representations, wherein overarching hypotheses are converted into quantitative formulations and further refined through detailed model parameterization. Specifically, primary concepts are decomposed into constituent elements, with discrete probabilities assigned to overarching hypotheses and probability distributions applied to detailed variables. This methodology is indispensable, as it ensures equal consideration of each hypothesis's validity while employing diverse techniques to evaluate the probabilistic alignment of individual details. However, the validity of such numerical models must be rigorously tested by comparing their outcomes with empirical data. This process involves forward modeling, whereby domain experts generate numerical predictions to simulate expected data configurations. Subsequently, these predictions are systematically evaluated using quantitative statistical analyses, including falsification tests and statistical metrics such as p-values, to ensure objectivity and reproducibility in assessing potential discrepancies. Moreover, practitioners employing Monte Carlo simulation refine their models and hypotheses through Bayesian inversion, a process that integrates observed data to update prior distributions and improve predictive accuracy. Decisions are then informed by Bayesian uncertainty quantification, which quantifies the confidence in predictive estimations. To facilitate this interdisciplinary effort, it is imperative that domain experts collaborate effectively. In subsequent sections, we will explore how artificial intelligence (AI) can enhance this process. Before delving into these opportunities, I will first review several prevalent applications of AI in mineral exploration. Rather than providing formal definitions of AI, I will use an illustrative example to elucidate its principles. Consider the case of a self-driving car tasked with navigating from one point to another. The goal is to reach the destination safely, lawfully, and efficiently, minimizing delays. To achieve this, the system must autonomously perform actions such as steering, accelerating, and braking, while continuously adapting its maneuvers based on current conditions. The system employs sensors to assess the state of the road, enabling it to predict its own future actions as well as those of other road users or objects. This capability allows for comprehensive situational awareness and anticipatory decision-making. The primary components of such a system can be identified as follows: rewards, which represent the overarching objective of safety, legality, and efficiency; actions, encompassing maneuvers such as steering and braking; and state, which describes the current conditions on the road, including the presence and behavior of other vehicles, pedestrians, and their respective velocities.

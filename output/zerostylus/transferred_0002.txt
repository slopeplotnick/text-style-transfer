Values enclosed in brackets were systematically assigned to each prompt by utilizing a lookup table containing simulated participants. Each entry within this table represents an individual, procedurally generated with a specific combination of age, ethnicity, and gender, based on the stratified UK sample provided by Prolific. Prolific stratifies ages into specific ranges; consequently, a precise age was randomly allocated within these ranges for each simulated individual. Subsequent to the generation of surveys, we employed a straightforward R script to parse the responses generated by the language model, systematically storing them as rows in a spreadsheet. Given the potential for idiosyncratic responses, three distinct prompts were crafted to elicit a variety of perspectives. As outlined by Hewitt et al. (2024), scores for each question were computed by averaging the results across multiple prompts, thereby ensuring a comprehensive analysis. Detailed descriptions of all prompt types utilized in this research are included in the Supplementary Materials. Anticipating deviations, we expected the language model might produce responses that did not conform to the prescribed format, such as failing to adhere to the structure of comma-separated values. Nonetheless, these erroneous responses were retained in the dataset to emulate a natural workflow; they were subsequently treated as missing data and replaced by the mean of other prompt responses during the analysis phase. Studies 1 and 2 employed pre-existing data, with prompts such as "Impersonate a/an [Ethnicity] [Gender] of [Age] years of age from the United Kingdom." Meanwhile, Studies 3 and 4 introduced new validation methodologies, as detailed in the subsequent sections. The initial section delineates the procedure for Studies 1 and 2, whereas the latter section describes the two-phase procedure for Studies 3 and 4. Furthermore, our evaluation of H3.1 reveals that the relationship between authentic and artificial scores is corroborated by Spearman's correlation. Section 3.2 further investigates H3.2 through the utilization of an Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC). To assess H4, we employed the Mann-Whitney U test, alongside the Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Levene's tests, to analyze differences across data types. Moreover, the application of Multigroup Confirmatory Factor Analysis (M-CFA) to H5, with gender as the grouping variable, demonstrates alignment with the analytical approach employed in H2. All CFA and M-CFA models were conducted using the Maximum Likelihood Robust (MLR) estimator, given its robust performance with non-normal data (Hox et al., 2010).

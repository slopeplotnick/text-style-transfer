The findings demonstrate that reasoning-based safety alignment significantly enhances the effectiveness of omnimodal guardrails across diverse input modalities. Moreover, it facilitates a more profound understanding and generalization in complex safety scenarios where reliance solely on label-based supervision is insufficient. To evaluate whether safety knowledge acquired from one modality can generalize to others, cross-modal training and evaluation were conducted. Specifically, OMNIGUARD was trained on datasets from three modalities while leaving one modality out for unseen modality evaluation. The averaged F1 scores and accuracy across all seen and unseen modalities are provided in Table 5. From these experiments, two primary conclusions were drawn. First, strong cross-modal transfer was consistently observed across all four modalities. The performance on unseen modalities closely approximated that on seen modalities, with an average F1 score of 79.4 compared to 81.8. This finding indicates that OMNIGUARD effectively learns modality-invariant safety representations, enabling the identification of harmful patterns and safety principles across text, images, videos, and audio inputs. Furthermore, OMNIGUARD demonstrates the ability to acquire generalizable reasoning skills that span multiple modalities. Second, an anomaly was identified in the audio modality, where the F1 score achieved on the seen audio dataset (64.6%) was slightly lower than that on the unseen dataset (65.1%). This phenomenon is attributed to the underperformance of the OMNIGUARD variant trained without textual data on the WildGuard-TTS benchmark, an audio-based evaluation created via text-to-speech conversion. The absence of textual data during training significantly impaired the model's capacity to process audio effectively. This observation underscores how semantically equivalent content across modalities, such as harmful text and its spoken counterpart, can interact and influence the development of safety mechanisms. Consequently, the findings affirm that safety knowledge derived from one modality can be successfully transferred to another semantically equivalent modality.

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated substantial efficacy in robotic manipulation tasks by leveraging large-scale, multimodal datasets collected through human teleoperation or scripted policies. These datasets enable significant enhancements in the models’ functional capabilities. During pretraining, VLA models often employ objectives such as flow-matching or diffusion, which facilitate the acquisition of diverse behavioral strategies spanning various manipulation modes, task contexts, and environmental scenarios. Subsequently, these pretrained models are fine-tuned for specific downstream tasks using smaller, task-specific datasets. Importantly, employing a pretrained VLA model for novel tasks generally results in superior performance compared to training a model from scratch. This advantage stems from the pretrained model’s extensive knowledge base, which encompasses visual perception, language understanding, embodiment priors, and spatial awareness of objects and environments. However, despite their strong average performance, VLA models often exhibit fragility when applied to practical tasks. This instability arises primarily due to the persistence of redundant action modes that are extraneous to task success even after supervised fine-tuning. Specifically, during pretraining, VLA models are exposed to a broad spectrum of action patterns derived from diverse datasets. While this exposure enhances generalization, it also complicates the models’ ability to restrict their output to the narrow set of behaviors required for successful task completion. Consequently, even after fine-tuning, the policy distribution retains superfluous action modes that contribute little to task success. Furthermore, the fine-tuning datasets themselves may exhibit multimodal characteristics, as they are often sourced from multiple human operators, scripted planners, or varying execution methodologies. Some of these data sources may encode suboptimal or undesirable strategies, further exacerbating the persistence of redundant modes. These extraneous behaviors lead to a distributional shift between the VLA model’s operational policy and the optimal policy required for stable task execution. This gap becomes evident during model deployment, highlighting the challenges associated with aligning pretrained VLA models with the specific requirements of downstream tasks.

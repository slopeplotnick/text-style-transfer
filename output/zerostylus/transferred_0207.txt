In the rapidly advancing field of Natural Language Processing (NLP), large language models (LLMs) such as GPT and BERT have emerged as transformative technologies driving substantial progress in the discipline. These models are capable of generating text that closely resembles human communication, answering complex questions, and engaging in fluent dialogue. However, while their outputs often exhibit logical coherence and relevance to the given input, they lack inherent truthfulness, as they are not grounded in empirical evidence. This limitation, commonly referred to as "hallucination," represents a significant obstacle to the broader adoption of LLMs. Hallucination occurs when models produce content that appears accurate but is not supported by the underlying training data. For instance, one might compare this phenomenon to a skilled storyteller who, in an effort to convey a compelling narrative, inadvertently incorporates fictional elements into factual accounts. Similarly, these models may generate plausible but incorrect outputs, such as fabricated claims, erroneous citations, or flawed reasoning. To illustrate this further, consider a GPS system that provides directions with apparent confidence but occasionally leads users down nonexistent roads. This analogy underscores the potential for LLMs to generate outputs that deviate from factual knowledge or expected outcomes, even when their underlying representations encode complex patterns. Such errors, while analogous to unsupported inferences, pose a critical challenge, particularly in high-stakes domains such as healthcare, law, and academic research, where factual accuracy is imperative. Consequently, addressing the issue of hallucinations has become a central focus for the AI research community. Efforts in this area are essential not only to enhance the reliability and accountability of LLMs but also to ensure their societal acceptance and ethical deployment. While LLMs represent a remarkable leap forward in NLP capabilities, their effectiveness is significantly diminished when tasked with verifying factual information, highlighting the importance of continued research to mitigate these limitations.

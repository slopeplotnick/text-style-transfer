The research community has increasingly focused on enhancing system robustness through representation-centric approaches, such as enforcing geometric consistency to ensure consistent interpretation of shapes and spatial structures. Recent studies have highlighted that alterations in viewpoint can lead to significant disruptions in the embedding space of pretrained ViT encoders, thereby impairing the integration between perception and downstream policy modules. Consequently, this investigation explores whether correcting such misalignments—without modifying the overarching VLA architecture or acquiring additional data—can adequately restore robustness against viewpoint variations. Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods have gained prominence as viable alternatives to full-model adaptation, enabling models to specialize for new tasks or contexts with minimal adjustments to existing parameters. Techniques such as adapters, LoRA, Prefix-Tuning, and BitFit exemplify this approach by introducing small, trainable modifications while preserving the majority of the system's structure. These methodologies are integral to the efficient and stable operation of large-scale language models and multimodal frameworks. In the realm of embodied AI, PEFT has primarily been applied to refine language or policy components, including lightweight updates to decision transformers or instruction encoders. However, most existing methods seldom address viewpoint shifts by directly finetuning the visual module itself. There is a scarcity of approaches that implement lightweight and targeted adaptations specifically for the visual components of VLA models. Consequently, our work investigates whether minor adaptations to visual components, such as vision tokens or the vision encoder, can effectively address these challenges. While prior works often necessitate extensive modifications to the VLA or substantial retraining of the visuomotor policy, we argue that such comprehensive measures are unwarranted. Visual perturbations primarily induce systematic deviations in data interpretation, thereby disrupting the stability of visual processing.

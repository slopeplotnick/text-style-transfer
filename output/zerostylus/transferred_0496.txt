Therefore, OptPO-SFT employs a Bayesian stopping criterion to allocate iterations adaptively while sharing the same update rule as TTSFT, thereby unifying efficiency with reliability. From a unified perspective, both RL-based and SFT-based variants of OptPO adhere to an identical tripartite sequence: they initiate rollouts, compute pseudo-labels using a designated method, and subsequently update parameters. However, the primary distinction lies in their optimization methods—policy-gradient optimization for RL-based approaches versus cross-entropy fine-tuning for SFT-based methods—while maintaining conceptual consistency across frameworks. To evaluate the proposed methodology, we utilized a suite of challenging mathematical and scientific reasoning benchmarks, including: (1) MATH-500 (Hendrycks et al., 2021), containing 500 high school competition problems; (2) AIME 2024 (MAA, 2024), featuring 30 pre-Olympiad level problems; (3) AMC, which comprises intermediate-level problems from American mathematics competitions; and (4) GPQA (Rein et al., 2024), a question-and-answer dataset targeting rigorous graduate-level science inquiries in biology, physics, and chemistry. Furthermore, OptPO was assessed on widely recognized open-source LLM backbones, such as Qwen2.5-Math1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, to ensure generalization across varying model sizes and architectures. When evaluating the effectiveness of TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, we employed Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct as backbone models, ensuring consistency across experimental setups. Moreover, OptPO demonstrated broad compatibility with existing reinforcement learning frameworks, significantly enhancing test-time training efficiency. Specifically, we integrated it into PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), and Reinforce++ (Hu, 2025) to comprehensively evaluate its efficacy. We also compared its performance against TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025), a widely adopted test-time policy optimization method. Similarly, the effectiveness of OptPO-SFT was evaluated in analogous scenarios to ensure robustness. In terms of implementation, the critic learning rate was configured at 1e-5 for RL-based training. For SFT-based training methodologies, including TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, gradient descent was applied on pseudo-labeled batches using AdamW with a learning rate of 2e-5.

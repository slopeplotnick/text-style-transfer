In our study, we adopted a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) paradigm utilizing the Qwen-1.5 model as our foundational architecture. This approach facilitated the transfer of general language understanding skills to domain-specific e-commerce tasks. To achieve this, we employed knowledge graphs as a foundational structure, augmented with domain-specific information via carefully designed prompts, thereby enabling the model to assimilate intricate e-commerce patterns and relational dynamics. Subsequently, we evaluated the model's performance using standardized tests and relevance metrics, thus ensuring its accuracy and robustness across diverse query types. Upon completion of training, the model was deployed for labeling tasks, significantly enhancing both the efficiency and accuracy of the process by identifying errors and improving data quality. Furthermore, the methodology effectively addressed more complex tasks, such as the interpretation of intricate queries, uncovering implicit meanings, and applying logical reasoning for pathway construction. In situations where simple instructions proved insufficient, we utilized Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. This allowed the model to decompose problems, discern relationships, and evaluate pathways incrementally, significantly improving its capability to process complex query formulations. Moreover, the development of a system for identifying challenging cases was necessitated to enhance the model through iterative feedback, addressing deficiencies in existing methodologies. Finally, we synthesized CoT data to execute tasks like attribute extraction and matching, and retrained the model using the SFT approach with Qwen-2 as the backbone. This strategy enhanced its capacity to manage reasoning steps while maintaining task specificity, thereby optimizing the model's performance in complex scenarios.

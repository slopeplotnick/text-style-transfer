The MODOMA framework functions as a comprehensive platform for conducting language acquisition experiments, offering a fully parameterizable system that allows users to tailor the experimental dynamics via specific settings. This framework encompasses all dimensions associated with language acquisition, including adult learners, child participants, and the complexities of linguistic processes. While individual parameters can be modified, additional configuration settings are instrumental in delineating crucial aspects of experimental design. The mother agent's configuration dictates the structural composition of Delilah's speech, specifically whether it generates complete sentences or isolated noun phrases. This setting is systematically varied in the training and test experiments conducted in this paper. Moreover, the minimum data requirement for the daughter agent to effectively process and differentiate functional and content categories varies across all experimental conditions. These two configurations establish the foundational experimental parameters. Notably, the initiation of learning functional and content categories occurs after processing 1,000 noun phrases and 1,000 sentences; however, this process extends to 10,000 noun phrases and 10,000 sentences as training progresses. Subsequently, an analysis was conducted to determine the number of instances from Delilah necessary to recognize this pattern. Consequently, the environment in which functional category learning takes place dictates the proportion employed to translate distinctions between function and content words into explicit linguistic knowledge. The training and test experiments outlined in this paper are designed to optimize these parameter values and assess their efficacy using new data generated by Delilah.

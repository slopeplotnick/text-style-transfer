The model's primary improvement mechanism is its ability to learn from its own errors; however, the inclusion of an excessive number of unrelated negative examples can destabilize the learning dynamics. The application of Off-Policy Sequence Masking has been empirically observed to stabilize the training process in scenarios that might otherwise exhibit significant instability. Keep Routing Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models enhance computational efficiency by activating only selected expert components during inference. However, discrepancies between the methods employed during inference and training, along with policy updates, can lead to inconsistent expert utilization even for identical inputs. This inconsistency induces abrupt shifts in the active parameter subspace, thereby destabilizing the optimization process and exacerbating off-policy challenges. To address this, the expert routing paths employed during inference are preserved and explicitly aligned with those utilized during training, ensuring that the optimization process consistently targets the same expert parameters. The Keep Routing mechanism is pivotal in maintaining the stability of MoE models during reinforcement learning training and has been consistently employed in our RL workflows since the implementation of DeepSeek-V3-0324. While DeepSeek-R1 has demonstrated that cognitive engagement can significantly enhance a model's ability to solve complex problems, the underlying mechanisms for this improvement remain unexplored. Building on this insight, we integrate cognitive skills into tool-utilization contexts. Nevertheless, adhering to DeepSeek-R1's approach—discarding reasoning content upon receiving subsequent rounds of messages—results in significant token inefficiency. Consequently, the model redundantly revisits the entire problem for each invocation of a new tool. To mitigate this, we developed a dedicated context management system for tool-calling scenarios, as illustrated in Fig. 4. In this system, outdated reasoning content is systematically discarded only when a new user message is introduced, whereas if only tool-related messages (e.g., tool outputs) are appended, the reasoning process is preserved throughout the interaction. Furthermore, even after the removal of reasoning traces, the log of tool invocations and their outcomes is maintained within the context, building upon the principle of retaining historical data.

In this study, we aim to elucidate the fundamental components that contribute to the efficacy of e-commerce search and identify the requisite tools essential for its enhancement. E-commerce search, from a functional perspective, can be regarded as the process of mapping user queries to corresponding product items. This mapping is refined by evaluating the extent to which a search aligns with a product, considering both explicit factors such as attributes and constraints, and subjective perspectives. To achieve this enhancement in matching, we systematically categorize user search preferences into 18 distinct attributes, including type, brand, and style, among others. In assessing the relevance of each search and product pairing, we evaluate how well the product satisfies the attributes explicitly or implicitly expressed in the query and utilize these assessments to determine the match's pertinence. This structured approach is crucial for comprehending and evaluating the processes by which relevance is determined. Relevance in e-commerce search can thus be conceptualized as a tripartite process involving the interpretation of product attributes, the analysis of search intent, and the evaluation of alignment between the two. Furthermore, the evaluation of relevance is conceptualized as requiring a dual focus on the extraction of product attributes and the analysis of its presentation. We distinguish between inherent attributes, which denote readily observable and evident characteristics, and derived attributes, which necessitate deeper cognitive engagement and comprehension. Inherent attributes refer to fundamental characteristics such as category, brand, model, and price, which are typically straightforward to identify and align with search queries. Conversely, derived attributes are informed by inherent ones and pertain to the functionalities of the product or the experiential quality it offers in usage. These attributes are not always readily apparent and necessitate a greater degree of understanding and reasoning. The challenge is exacerbated by the varied terminologies employed across different industries, which complicates the process of achieving optimal relevance judgment.

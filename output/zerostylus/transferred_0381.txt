Our method harmonizes effectively with contemporary high-throughput systems, specifically vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023) and SGLang (Zheng et al., 2024), thereby facilitating both real-time service applications and offline data processing. Consequently, we predominantly utilize the two-step vLLM configuration in Section 4.2, due to its efficiency and facilitation of managing reasoning steps. We employed two distinct methodologies to evaluate THINKMERGE. For tasks involving a single correct answer, we examine AIME 2025 (MAA, 2025) and GPQA Diamond (Rein et al., 2024), where responses can be consolidated through majority voting across multiple samples. Conversely, for evaluating tasks without a definitive correct answer, we employed the LiveCodeBench v5, BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and xBench-DeepSearch benchmarks, concentrating on the generation of a single optimal solution. While our study involved conducting five tests for tasks with a singular correct solution, we evaluated models such as Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-14B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B to ascertain their effectiveness. In contrast, for coding tasks characterized by open-ended answer generation, we employed models such as DeepCoder-14B-Preview, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-Coder-30A3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Thinking, and Qwen3-Think-30A3B. Additionally, we employ WebSailor models—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B—for conducting open-ended research tasks. To establish the parameters for sequence processing, we designate a maximum length of 32,768 tokens, with comprehensive specifications regarding sampling methodologies provided in Appendix A.1. Moreover, we utilize a cognitive evaluation framework that assesses the efficacy of THINKMERGE in research tasks requiring the model to employ analytical reasoning and tool utilization prior to delivering a response. Specifically, we evaluate three Tongyi-WebSailor models—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B—on four challenging web-based benchmarks: BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and XbenchDeepSearch. BrowseComp-en and BrowseComp-zh emphasize hard-to-find, multi-hop factual queries in both English and Chinese. Given the substantial size of BrowseComp-en, comprising 1,266 questions in total, we randomly selected 200 questions to form a test subset, thereby ensuring it remains comparable in size to BrowseComp.

The necessity of incorporating the deformable scan is evident, as utilizing the basic version of Mamba results in a marked decline in performance, underscoring its importance. To identify the most effective solution, various designs were explored. To substantiate the structural composition of our proposed design, we employ a combination of distinct block types, denoted as "CCDGDG" across stages 1 to 6. Here, C represents convolutional layers, D corresponds to DDSM with FNN integration, and G signifies gated convolutional blocks. These configurations enable us to examine variations in block types, allowing for assessments of diversity with dual block types such as "CCGGGG" and "CCDDD," as well as testing uniformity with designs comprising a singular block type. Extensive evaluation demonstrates that our mixed design outperforms alternative approaches. The analysis additionally incorporates examples derived from InterHand2.6M and AssemblyHands. Furthermore, our method demonstrates robustness in occlusion scenarios, effectively identifying joints even in conditions where they are obstructed or obscured, thereby showcasing its robustness in real-world applications. We introduce DF-Mamba as an innovative framework for estimating 3D hand poses, which integrates efficient initial feature extraction with a subsequent deformable representation. We evaluate performance across five datasets encompassing diverse hand interactions and data types, demonstrating superior accuracy and faster execution compared to alternative configurations. Future research directions may involve applying DF-Mamba to additional tasks such as 3D human pose estimation, incorporating extensive external datasets for training, exploring novel hand-mesh reconstruction techniques, and developing it into a robust foundational model that performs effectively across diverse scenarios without necessitating specific modifications.

In refining the coherence and flow of a paragraph, we prioritize the precise implementation of desired editing adjustments while ensuring that the original video's motion patterns are preserved. Moreover, it is essential that all subtle movements, such as blinks or head turns, are accurately synchronized with the temporal dynamics of the original source. This level of precision is particularly critical in videos featuring spoken dialogue, as even minor discrepancies can significantly undermine the perceived integrity of the presentation. Typically, individuals seek minor modifications, such as the addition of a mustache or alterations to facial expressions, which should be executed in a manner that only the necessary elements are modified, thereby maintaining consistency in all other aspects. However, achieving perfect synchronization with most video editing tools remains a challenging task, warranting an assessment of the tools' capabilities in this regard. Consequently, we introduce a novel methodology termed Sync-LoRA, designed to ensure that video edits remain aligned with the original content. The approach involves modifying only the initial frame of the video and employing a text prompt to guide the specific alterations to be applied. The model is trained using pairs of videos, with one serving as the original and the other as the edited counterpart, constructed to ensure precise alignment in motion dynamics across frames, while variations are introduced through modifications to specific edited elements. Furthermore, the model is capable of comprehending both visual and textual information, generating a variety of proposed modifications, and subsequently producing corresponding images, which are synthesized into videos. This synthesis process is assessed through human motion data, encompassing facial expressions and body movements, to ensure consistency and accuracy. Ultimately, we evaluate the degree of alignment concerning verbal communication, ocular movements, blinking patterns, and postural positioning, retaining only the most optimal instances for training purposes. By doing so, the model is trained to retain the motion patterns of the source video while implementing the desired modifications, thereby ensuring synchronization with the original video.

In 2023, Zakharov elucidated phenomena related to the detection of changes and the formulation of probabilistic inferences under uncertainty. These processes, including systematic biases such as regression to the mean, are understood to emerge from optimal Bayesian inference strategies (Petzschner & Glasauer, 2011). Recent computational models have further demonstrated that perceptual errors, particularly in temporal estimations, are intrinsically supported by predictive mechanisms embedded in episodic memory (Fountas et al., 2022). Consequently, individuals tend to combine ambiguous sensory information with prior expectations, resulting in systematic distortions in judgment and characteristic behavioral patterns. Notably, both Llama-4 Maverick and humans exhibit a similar tendency to infer toward the mean, thereby revealing shared computational principles despite their fundamentally different substrates. Certain aspects of large language models (LLMs) align with Bayesian computation. For instance, in-context learning has been characterized as a form of approximate Bayesian inference (Xie et al., 2021). Moreover, Bayesian teaching strategies have been shown to enhance reasoning capabilities within these models (Qiu et al., 2025). Similarly, LLMs have been observed to segment sequences based on measures of Bayesian surprise, a process that demonstrates strong parallels with human event perception (Kumar et al., 2023; Fountas et al., 2025). However, while most research has focused on tasks involving explicit reasoning or learned behaviors—where models apply pre-existing statistical rules—there has been relatively less exploration of perceptual tasks that might illuminate computational strategies emerging implicitly from pretraining. Building on these advancements, recent studies have introduced benchmarks such as MMbench (Liu et al., 2024) and SEED-bench (Li et al., 2024) to evaluate multimodal reasoning in LLMs. These benchmarks have facilitated significant progress in assessing how LLMs integrate diverse types of information. However, they often neglect the controlled manipulation of modality-specific noise, which is critical for understanding fusion strategies in uncertain environments. To address this limitation, synthetic datasets have been developed that enable fine-grained analyses of how LLMs combine noisy information from multiple modalities. Exploring this nascent yet essential area of research is crucial for advancing the development of robust and generalizable models that can maintain optimal performance under uncertainty.

In the domain of high-performing models, GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o demonstrate exceptionally low error rates, achieving performance comparable to—or exceeding—human capabilities (see left panel, Fig. 8). However, with the notable exception of Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, the data presented in Figure 8 reveals a general trend: models with higher accuracy tend to exhibit behavior more consistent with Bayesian principles. Importantly, this observation challenges the assumption of a necessary relationship between accuracy and Bayesian-like behavior. For instance, a model capable of perfectly predicting outcomes may lack probabilistic tendencies, as it simply maps stimuli to accurate estimates without engaging in probabilistic reasoning. Furthermore, when large language models (LLMs) are provided with a numerical range of prior observations within their context prompt, their behavior is significantly influenced. As illustrated in the top right panel of Figure 6, sequential behavior diminishes markedly, especially when a biased range is introduced; in such cases, as shown in the left panel of Figure 6, error rates increase, with triangles typically displaying higher NRMSE values than circles. This pattern underscores the tendency of LLMs to align their predictions closely with the contextual information provided. Moreover, an examination of the central panel in Figure 8 indicates that strong NRMSE performance does not necessarily correlate with effective cue integration. For instance, GPT-5 Mini, despite its proficiency in achieving low NRMSE values, struggles with cue combination efficiency. This limitation is particularly evident in the maze distance estimation task, where GPT-5 Mini achieves near-perfect performance in processing textual inputs but demonstrates weaker results when handling image-based inputs. Consequently, for a Bayes-optimal strategy, the model would need to further reduce its reliance on image inputs. However, as detailed in Appendix A.12, GPT-5 Mini appears unable to sufficiently downweigh image-based information to achieve optimal integration. Conversely, in the line length ratio task (see bottom right panel, Fig. 6), numerous LLMs exhibit the ability to downweigh image inputs effectively in the presence of noise, thereby demonstrating adaptation consistent with Bayesian reasoning.

Further analysis indicated that perceptions of AI played a more significant role in shaping the outcomes than the experimental procedure itself. When participants' attitudes toward AI were incorporated into the analysis, the experimental condition no longer effectively clarified whether the music was perceived favorably or the intensity of their emotional responses to it. However, perceptions of AI remained a crucial and consistent factor throughout the analysis. This finding aligns with earlier studies that have consistently demonstrated that individuals' perceptions of autonomous machines significantly influence their evaluations of these technologies (Hong et al., 2022) and further contributes to the work of Shank et al. (2023), suggesting that composer bias may be contingent upon the specific song and context, particularly if the composition is popular and exhibits characteristics indicative of human creation. Although personality traits and music-related factors were associated with individuals' enjoyment of the music and the emotional responses it elicited, these associations became less significant when individual attitudes toward AI were considered. Notable exceptions include openness to experience and neuroticism, which are distinct personality traits that have consistently been associated with influencing individuals' emotional responses to music, particularly in terms of emotions such as happiness and energy (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Juslin et al., 2011; Liljeström et al., 2013). The music utilized in this study was frequently characterized by the pronounced emotions it conveyed. Furthermore, listeners' attitudes toward AI were uniquely associated with their appreciation of the music and the emotional responses it elicited. The degree to which listener traits accounted for variation was consistent with findings from other investigations, such as those conducted by Gerstgrasser et al. (2023) and Strauss et al. (2024). Therefore, the perception of AI-generated music is significantly influenced by individuals' attitudes toward AI, which plays a crucial role in their evaluations. Contrary to the findings of Ansani et al. (2024), the study determined that variations in attitudes toward AI did not influence the functioning of the experimental groups. The observed differences may stem from variations in the attitude measures employed, the emphasis on listening to recorded tracks rather than live performances, and the inclusion of three experimental conditions instead of two.

In the domain of elderly services, question answering (QA) poses distinct challenges that demand accurate and reliable responses, grounded in diverse authoritative sources. Inquiries typically encompass healthcare regulations, welfare initiatives, and daily guidance—areas where misinformation can lead to severe consequences. While traditional language models are effective in broader applications, they often lack the precision, domain-specific awareness, and safety mechanisms necessary for these sensitive contexts. Existing models typically rely on either structured or unstructured data, thus limiting their ability to generate comprehensive responses. Moreover, many of these models do not possess mechanisms to regulate the influence of external information on response generation, providing only limited control over the safety of outputs. Consequently, this diminishes their effectiveness in elderly service scenarios, where precise differentiation between suitable and unsuitable interventions is essential. To address these limitations, we propose Knowledge-Aware Reasoning and Memory-Augmented Adaptation (KARMA), a domain-adaptive QA framework. KARMA integrates a dual-encoder architecture for comprehensive knowledge synthesis, a gated memory unit for selective knowledge integration, and a safety-aware decoder to ensure response accuracy and dependability. The model is trained using a multi-objective loss function that optimizes language fluency, knowledge alignment, and safety control. Built upon a LLaMA-7B foundation with specialized tuning, KARMA demonstrates adaptability and achieves high performance. Our experimental results on a proprietary elderly-service QA dataset indicate that KARMA significantly surpasses baseline models in terms of accuracy, relevance, and safety, offering a robust solution for real-world deployment in elderly care environments. Mo et al. [1] proposed a methodology for integrating knowledge across diverse data modalities, underscoring the necessity of heterogeneous information fusion in complex QA. Additionally, Huang et al. [2] introduce MetaMath-LLaMA, which combines a sophisticated scheduling mechanism, symbolic parsing techniques, and a hybrid symbolic-neural approach for transparent multi-step reasoning. Incorporating these mechanisms into KARMA delineates distinct phases that enhance memory unit gating and fortify safety controls. Furthermore, Luo [3] discusses TriMedTune, a tuning process for brain CT that involves visual prompt integration, harmonizes medical terminology, and refines knowledge through uncertainty checks. Adapting these techniques to KARMA could tighten terminology-grounded generation and provide uncertainty-aware gating, thereby improving factual consistency and safe refusal in sensitive QA contexts.

The integration of these elements into KARMA is poised to significantly enhance the precision of term usage; moreover, it is expected to augment the system's capability to manage ambiguous responses effectively, ensuring both factual accuracy and the appropriate declination of sensitive inquiries. Furthermore, the STELLAR framework, as demonstrated by Sun and colleagues, offers an interface that seamlessly integrates a semantic module, thereby reinforcing the system's overall functionality. Consequently, these collective advancements contribute to a refined user experience, in which the management of ambiguity and sensitivity is methodically addressed.

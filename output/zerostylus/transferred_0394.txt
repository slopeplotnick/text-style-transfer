Despite its significance, the community still lacks a robust methodology for systematically measuring and analyzing the power and energy consumption required to operate large language models (LLMs), taking into account various model sizes, hardware configurations, software platforms, and usage contexts, such as single-computer versus distributed systems. Although several initiatives propose intriguing approaches and methods aimed at addressing this issue, they fail to comprehensively resolve the challenge. For instance, the MLPerf Inference benchmark (Reddi et al.) is utilized for efficient performance evaluation and serves as a basis for benchmarking methodologies within computational frameworks. However, the work of 2020 establishes a benchmark standard for evaluating performance across various machine learning tasks; it neither comprehensively examines the aggregate power consumption nor adapts energy usage metrics to align with tasks specific to LLMs, such as Joules per token or prompt. The MLPerf Power benchmark, as detailed by Tschand et al., consists of two integral components: (1) the measurement component, which assesses the energy consumption of the benchmark harness when executed on the designated hardware, and (2) the modeling component, which constructs a Bayesian model to evaluate the energy consumption characteristics of the programming language. Nevertheless, it primarily (i) examines configurations involving a single computer, (ii) evaluates smaller models relative to the most recent large-scale LLMs, such as the Llama 3 405B-class, which remain underexplored, and (iii) depends on high-precision, costly equipment that poses significant challenges for deployment across diverse environments. Moreover, contemporary benchmarks frequently overlook the consideration of adjustable parameters in practical applications, such as batch size, parallel processing, context length, and quantization levels. These configuration parameters significantly influence the instantaneous power consumption and the average energy expenditure per token during execution. In response to this deficiency, we present TokenPowerBench, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate the power and energy consumption of large language models in a user-friendly manner. TokenPowerBench comprises a system with multiple functionalities: (1) integration with vendor-provided tools, such as power sensors for GPU, CPU, and memory, to assess energy consumption at the component level, and (2) the capability, where applicable, to extend measurements to the rack or facility scale, thereby providing a more comprehensive analysis of power usage. The pipeline associates each power measurement with the corresponding primary inference stages—prefill and decode—thereby enabling precise identification of energy consumption across these processes. The analysis involves multiple experimental components: (1) evaluating the relationship between energy consumption and factors such as batch size, maximum context length, task partitioning, and quantization, and (2) conducting systematic tests to examine these dependencies comprehensively.

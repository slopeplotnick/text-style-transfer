The Menta interface supports a wide range of on-device classification tasks, including the assessment of stress, depression (both binary and severity-level), and suicide risk (both binary and categorical), employing a methodical and analytical approach devoid of impulsive or emotionally charged language. Upon activation of the "Start Evaluation" interface, social media posts are processed through the selected model, and the system outputs predicted categories, their associated confidence scores, and additional evaluation metrics. To evaluate the model's performance across various tasks, datasets ranging in size from 10 to 3,000 posts were utilized, with the dataset size determined by task complexity and data availability. Notably, the analysis reveals a balanced trade-off between processing speed and accuracy, positioning Menta as a competitive alternative to similar systems. From a deployment perspective, Menta exemplifies an optimal balance of accuracy, inference speed, and responsiveness, making it well-suited for real-world scenarios where both performance and timely results are critical. As illustrated in Figure 9, the evaluation framework considers three key metrics: the inference throughput per second (ITPS), the time to first prediction (TTFT), and the accuracy of predictions. In this visualization, the size of each bubble represents the memory consumption of the corresponding model. Falcon prioritizes rapid task initiation and processing (fast TTFT and high ITPS); however, this emphasis on speed comes at the expense of accuracy, which is the lowest among all models. Conversely, StableLM demonstrates superior accuracy but incurs significant latency and higher resource consumption, leading to increased deployment costs and reduced efficiency for end-users. In contrast, Menta achieves commendable accuracy across most tasks while maintaining relatively low latency (TTFT) and competitive throughput (ITPS). Although StableLM and Phi-4 Mini slightly outperform Menta in Task 5, Menta continues to excel in speed and efficiency, reinforcing its suitability for scalable, user-facing applications. From an implementation standpoint, Menta delivers robust performance with rapid results and efficient memory utilization, rendering it particularly advantageous for high-demand systems with extensive user bases. Furthermore, it operates effectively without requiring substantial resources, which minimizes costs and ensures a favorable user experience. Overall, Menta demonstrates a readiness for real-world applications by providing a well-rounded balance of advantages and limitations. As such, it strategically positions itself as an optimal deployment-ready model, capable of delivering high-quality outputs efficiently while maintaining manageable computational requirements, thereby reducing infrastructure overhead without compromising user satisfaction.

Evaluation Metric. We employ two primary evaluation metrics: Toxic Score (Toxic) and Attack Success Rate (ASR). In alignment with previous studies [10, 31, 39], we utilize the GPT-4o judge to evaluate the harmfulness of responses. Specifically, given an original harmful query and the corresponding model-generated response, the evaluator assigns a Toxic Score ranging from 1 to 5, where higher scores denote increased harmfulness, with a score of 5 indicating that the response fully aligns with the harmful intent of the query. Consequently, an attack is deemed successful only when it is assigned a score of 5. The evaluation prompt can be accessed in App. B.3. Our study comprises four distinct components, each suited to different scenarios and models. Therefore, an attack is considered successful if either the original target image or any of the four enhanced target images achieves a successful attack. In calculating the Toxic Score, we select the highest score from five evaluation attempts for each example within a particular context. Results are reported exclusively for the Demonstrative Framework, with additional findings for alternative strategies available in Apps. C and D. Comprehensive experiments are conducted using the MMSafetyBench-tiny and SafeBench-tiny datasets, with the overall results summarized in Tab. 1 and Tab. 2. The system utilizes a multi-round protocol incorporating images in dialogues and robust red-teaming mechanisms to formulate attack prompts, which results in higher computational and financial costs compared to the CIA method. In contrast, the CIA method, which requires generating only a single contextual image per inquiry, adopts a more streamlined attack strategy, thereby offering enhanced scalability and reproducibility. Despite ongoing challenges in areas such as illegal counsel, fraudulent activities, and hate crime, this study highlights the efficacy of AI-augmented systems in navigating complex domains with precision and reliability. Our analysis indicates that the CIA framework does not rely on predefined templates for category-specific attacks but rather exploits a general vulnerability rooted in the misalignment between vision-language integration and instruction-following capabilities in contemporary models.

To enhance our understanding of the results, we meticulously examined the interview summaries on multiple occasions. During the interviews, we documented the proceedings by recording the conversations, taking handwritten notes, and subsequently summarizing the recorded content. This comprehensive approach was employed to ensure the inclusion of all pertinent information and to mitigate the risk of overlooking crucial details, thereby aligning with the standard practice in qualitative research of recording and transcribing interviews. The analysis process was conducted by the research team, which included the interviewer. Subsequently, we developed several methodologies to facilitate quantitative analysis. These methodologies comprised various metrics designed to assess the frequency of responses, including the enumeration of identified good practices, benefits, risks, and fears. In addressing these inquiries, we systematically identified responses with thematic similarity and transformed them into concise terminology that encapsulated the core concepts. For instance, when a participant stated, "The genericity pushes to factorize more and more," we succinctly documented "genericity" and "factorization." For each topic, we first manually examined the frequency of expressed terms and identified the individuals who articulated them. A multi-step method was executed to determine the key words. Initially, we eliminated non-essential terms such as "the" and "and," followed by organizing the remaining words based on their frequency of occurrence. We manually selected the documents deemed most significant and verified their coherence within the contextual framework. Additionally, in certain instances, we employed supplementary terminology to enhance clarity, such as using "too complex" instead of simply "complex." We compared the vocabulary identified in the interviews with that of literary sources. To improve lexical coherence, we proposed substituting inadequately matched words with more suitable alternatives, such as replacing "unusable" with "useless." In cases where a match was identified, it was retained. This approach facilitated the enhancement of our responses by integrating concepts from literary sources, ensuring that our vocabulary aligned more closely with the language utilized in those texts. Consequently, this process was instrumental in identifying the most critical terms, thereby enhancing the robustness of our analysis.

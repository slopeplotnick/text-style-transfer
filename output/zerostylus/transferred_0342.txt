Our research findings indicate that the model Menta exhibits superior efficacy in identifying depression and stress compared to its performance in detecting suicide risk. Furthermore, both fine-tuned and regular language models, including larger variants, consistently demonstrate enhanced performance in depression and stress detection tasks relative to suicide risk assessment. This aligns with Bauer's (2024) observations, which highlight the challenges language models face in accurately identifying subtle indicators of suicidality. These challenges are primarily attributed to the scarcity of relevant data and the models' reliance on contextual or temporal cues, which are often ambiguous in isolated posts. In support of this, a 2023 study by Lamichhane reported F1-scores of 0.86 for depression detection and a significantly lower 0.37 for suicidality detection using social media data. Despite the availability of extensive data and clearer patterns for depression and stress, identifying suicidality remains challenging due to its lower prevalence and context-dependent nature. Consequently, language models may demonstrate stronger utility for general screening tasks, such as identifying indicators of depression or stress. However, they exhibit limitations in detailed suicide risk assessment unless supplemented with multimodal data or longitudinal tracking. Despite the strong predictive capabilities of models such as Qwen-3 (4B) for mental health issues, several factors hinder the generalizability of these findings to broader contexts. This study focused exclusively on social media posts, excluding factors such as temporal changes in writing patterns, user profile information, and online interactions, which are crucial for a more comprehensive understanding of mental health. Therefore, incorporating these elements is crucial, as it enhances the robustness and realism of future research endeavors. It is important to note that our examination was limited to depression, stress, and suicidal ideation, which represent only a subset of the broader spectrum of mental health issues. The study did not address critical mental health conditions such as anxiety, bipolar disorder, and emotional disturbances. Additionally, we utilized anonymized public social media data in contrast to data derived from clinical environments.

This approach maintains confidentiality and facilitates repetition; however, it does not yield substantial insights into safety, ethics, or enduring reliability in authentic healthcare environments. Moreover, while the method preserves privacy, it fails to address critical aspects necessary for ensuring comprehensive understanding in real-world settings. Furthermore, the limitations in providing extensive insights into safety and ethical concerns underscore the need for a more robust framework that can reliably operate within genuine healthcare contexts. Consequently, this indicates a gap in the current approach, highlighting areas for improvement to achieve a balance between confidentiality and the practical implications of safety and ethics in healthcare applications.

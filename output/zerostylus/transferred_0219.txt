In this study, we introduced Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), a reusable and modular tool for physics-based character animation that employs score-matching techniques to generate realistic movements. Once trained, this system facilitates the instruction of control policies across various tasks, guiding these policies towards behaviors that naturally align with the reference distribution. Notably, it is unnecessary to retain the original motion data for effective utilization of the priors. Moreover, classifier-free guidance, among other test-time diffusion techniques, can be applied to adjust the motion prior, thereby enabling the generation of novel styles and assisting agents in performing tasks with specific, tailored behaviors. The efficacy of this method was evaluated across diverse scenarios, including individual character movements and human-object interactions. Additionally, our methodology allows for the construction of SMPs from a diverse range of datasets, whether they consist of mere seconds of motion clips or extensive collections encompassing up to 20 hours of motion data. In our experiments, we demonstrated that employing reinforcement learning with score distillation sampling (SDS) objectives generates movements comparable in quality to those produced via adversarial imitation learning, without necessitating continual updates to the prior during policy training. SMP also exhibits higher sample efficiency than adversarial priors in most scenarios, owing to its reliance on a more stable reward mechanism compared to adversarial models. However, it is important to note that policies trained with SMP may sometimes become restricted to a narrow range of actions derived from the original dataset, rather than encompassing the full spectrum of behaviors. Although the primary focus of our research has been on humanoid motion control, we posit that SMP may also hold potential applicability across a range of other control challenges. Ultimately, this concept aims to catalyze the development of innovative general motion tools that extend beyond mere motion tracking, aspiring to facilitate the creation of more adaptable controllers for animation and robotics.

The perception of music, beyond its acoustic characteristics, is significantly shaped by individuals' attitudes toward artificial intelligence (AI). Consequently, the development of tools specifically tailored for AI-generated music, alongside the establishment of innovative methodologies for assessing its quality, becomes essential. With AI-generated music gaining prominence on streaming platforms, it is crucial to address socioeconomic considerations of fairness and transparency associated with its creation and distribution. For example, examining the transparency of datasets used to train AI systems and the mechanisms governing revenue distribution from AI-produced music is particularly pertinent. Moreover, the communicative strategies employed to convey information about the production and dissemination of AI-generated music warrant further exploration, as they directly influence public perceptions. Additionally, various methodologies through which AI-generated music is created and utilized deserve closer scrutiny. For instance, the collaborative creation of music by humans and AI systems, made increasingly accessible by advancements in co-creative technologies, represents a growing area of interest. The study conducted by Moura and Maw (2021) exemplifies the practical applications and perceptions of AI-generated music in functional contexts, further underscoring the need for detailed investigation. Notably, perceptions of AI-generated music are predominantly shaped by attitudes toward AI itself, often surpassing other factors such as the identity of the creator, the music genre, or typical listening habits. Looking to the future, it will be essential to monitor the evolving attitudes toward AI, as these perceptions will significantly influence the acceptance or resistance of AI in cultural domains, including music. More broadly, the discourse surrounding AI-generated music extends beyond the question of its enjoyability or resemblance to human-created works. Instead, it raises critical inquiries about how authenticity and the essence of creativity are defined and valued. By engaging critically with audience responses, researchers can identify which forms of creativity—both human and artificial—society deems worthy of recognition and under what criteria.

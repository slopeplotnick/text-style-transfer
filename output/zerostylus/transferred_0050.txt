Our approach was evaluated through a comparative analysis with related benchmarks, involving the assessment by judges. This evaluation methodology signifies a substantial advancement over existing benchmarks. Prior work, such as PPTC-R [23], primarily focuses on API-level differences. While beneficial, this method is limited and fails to detect critical semantic errors, such as an agent copying the correct text to an incorrect slide. In contrast, our IF Judge utilizes structured summaries to effectively capture logical inconsistencies. Furthermore, while other benchmarks like T2US [25] employ visual language models (VLMs) as evaluators, their reliance on prompting without a comprehensive rubric results in inconsistent and unreliable ratings. To address these shortcomings, PPTArena provides a rigorous and reproducible framework for evaluation. Therefore, our dual-judge, guideline-driven architecture is crucial for assessing the complex, multi-step reasoning required for PowerPoint editing and identifying nuanced errors that simpler benchmarks might overlook. In alignment with this framework, we introduce PPTPilot, an agent designed for presentation editing, which, despite its straightforward interface, yields highly effective results—surpassing even proprietary products like OpenAI's ChatGPT Agent, as detailed in Section 5.1. Our design is founded on two key principles: First, the primary challenge in this domain extends beyond mere task recognition to ensuring precision and consistency. PowerPoint files are based on the complex Office Open XML (OOXML) format, which is poorly equipped to handle malformed or "hallucinated" VLM outputs, necessitating specialized configurations for effective PPT editing. Second, we determined that relying solely on a single editing modality, such as exclusive dependence on the XML format, is inadequate. Consequently, an effective tool must intelligently select the optimal editing approach tailored to each specific task. PPTPilot's core design features a dual-path architecture, enabling it to process editing tasks through either programmatic interfaces or direct XML manipulation, as illustrated in Figure 5.

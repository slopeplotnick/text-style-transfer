Significant progress has been made in areas such as bias detection, toxicity reduction, and fairness auditing [18, 23]. However, existing frameworks predominantly focus on evaluating individual model outputs in isolation, rather than systematically tracing the evolution of ethical reasoning or shifts in value alignment as models engage with diverse, context-dependent, and dynamically evolving scenarios [20]. This limitation is particularly relevant for large language model (LLM)-based autonomous systems, which exhibit highly adaptive and context-sensitive behavior, continuously modifying their outputs in response to variations in prompt structure, user intent, task requirements, and prior interactions. Consequently, the absence of longitudinal consistency in such systems raises the risk of moral drift—a phenomenon wherein ethical stances fluctuate or degrade over time due to prompt wording, contextual pressures, or temporal dynamics. These inconsistencies not only jeopardize the perceived reliability, accountability, and societal trustworthiness of AI systems but also highlight the critical need for robust mechanisms to ensure moral coherence [6, 24]. Ensuring consistency in ethical principles is, therefore, fundamental—not only to adhere to regulatory guidelines but also to maintain logically stable, interpretable, and trustworthy reasoning in LLM-driven systems over time [2, 27]. This necessity becomes even more pronounced in high-stakes domains such as healthcare, finance, education, and governance, where even minor ethical instabilities can propagate misinformation, reinforce cognitive biases, and produce outcomes that are misaligned with societal and human values. While these challenges are increasingly recognized, the majority of current evaluation frameworks remain limited by static benchmarks and fixed, human-annotated datasets, which fail to account for the evolving and context-dependent behavior of these models. Thus, a critical research challenge emerges: the development of adaptive, self-contained mechanisms capable of autonomously evaluating and interpreting the ethical stability of LLMs without reliance on external datasets or retrospective analysis. To address this research need, this study introduces the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP), a fully autonomous, dataset-free ethical evaluation framework designed to systematically measure, interpret, and refine the moral reasoning patterns of LLMs. Unlike traditional evaluation approaches, which depend on predefined test corpora and external annotations, MoCoP operates as a closed-loop experimental system, dynamically generating ethical scenarios, eliciting model responses, and performing structured interpretation using internally defined rules and semantic heuristics. The framework incorporates three complementary analytical components—lexical integrity analysis, semantic risk estimation, and reasoning-based judgment modeling—to derive unified measures of moral coherence and ethical stability. Each component contributes a distinct evaluative function: lexical integrity identifies linguistic degradation and bias, semantic risk estimation quantifies ethical vulnerabilities, and reasoning-based judgment modeling assesses the logical consistency of ethical decisions. Together, these components enable MoCoP to provide a comprehensive and adaptive analysis of the ethical behavior of LLMs, addressing the critical gaps in current evaluation methodologies.

This framework employs a tripartite approach, namely evaluating lexical appropriateness, assessing semantic risk factors, and analyzing the coherence of reasoning. Through this approach, it derives comprehensive metrics for moral consistency and ethical resilience. Each component serves a distinct function: the evaluation of word usage identifies potential bias and linguistic concerns, while the assessment of semantic risks examines possible issues in conveyed meaning. Furthermore, the analysis of reasoning processes models the underlying framework for decision-making. This tripartite approach thus enables MoCoP to reframe ethical assessment as a proactive, self-sustaining process that evolves in tandem with the models it studies. Through iterative self-analysis and adaptive evaluation, MoCoP offers a novel perspective on ethical evaluation, advancing beyond traditional static methods.

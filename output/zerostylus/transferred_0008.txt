A novel framework, termed KARMA, has been proposed to optimize both the performance and safety of large language models (LLMs) in domain-specific applications. KARMA is structured around three core modules: the Multi-Source Information Aggregator (MSIA), which facilitates accurate data collection from diverse sources; the Controlled Utilization Framework (CUF), which ensures the regulated application of knowledge; and the Reliability-Optimized Response Generator (RORG), which guarantees the generation of safe and dependable outputs. While these modules collectively enhance the framework’s functionality, certain scholars have advocated for openness to innovative methodologies aimed at further advancing KARMA's capabilities. For instance, one proposed approach involves integrating temporal and spatial data, which could enable the derivation of more precise solutions. Specifically, this methodology employs a systematic ranking and retrieval mechanism that adapts to temporal variations, thereby improving result accuracy. Moreover, techniques for extracting key terms from sentences have been utilized to enhance the system’s comprehension of relational structures. The integration of domain-specific diagnostic tools has also proven effective in addressing medical inquiries, suggesting that incorporating such tools into KARMA may significantly improve its performance in healthcare contexts. Additionally, adjustments to safety parameters during system operation have been proposed to better manage sensitive inquiries. Furthermore, KARMA employs an advanced approach to heterogeneous data fusion, dynamically modulating attention to various data types, which enhances its ability to process complex and diverse datasets. This comprehensive integration is critical for improving KARMA's intelligence, safety, and reliability across practical applications.

The GPT-5 model exhibits a notably high refusal rate that remains consistent regardless of increases in context padding. Consequently, this persistent refusal rate results in slightly diminished performance on harmful tasks; however, given the already low harm score, this observation offers limited actionable insights. Furthermore, testing on benign tasks with over 10,000 tokens of context padding has not been pursued due to the stability of prior results and the prohibitive costs associated with such experiments. The type of context padding applied appears to significantly influence model performance. For instance, GPT-4.1-nano demonstrates higher scores with non-relevant padding, followed sequentially by relevant padding, random padding, and, lastly, multi-task padding, which performs the worst. In contrast, Grok 4 Fast, characterized by its high refusal rate, does not exhibit similar trends, as its scores across different padding types remain close to those observed without padding. Notably, within the range of 50,000 to 100,000 tokens, Grok 4 Fast displays behavior indicative of mode collapse, where the performance metrics for different padding types converge to similar values. In addition to padding type, the placement of padding significantly impacts model performance. As illustrated in Figure 4, positioning the padding before the task description mitigates the rate of capability degradation compared to placing it afterward. This effect likely occurs because pre-task padding reduces the distance between the task description and execution, enabling modern attention mechanisms—such as sliding window and RoPE—to operate more effectively within their typical training distributions. Conversely, when padding is positioned after the task description, the model must attend across a larger gap between instruction and execution, which may exacerbate performance degradation. This limitation is particularly pronounced in contemporary attention mechanisms, as they struggle to process tokens located at greater distances due to the rarity of such patterns during training.

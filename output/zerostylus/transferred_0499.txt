In this section, we illustrate the functionalities of our interface through the use case of "Picture Composition." This feature provides users with explicit information, such as the number of entities and events, as well as the types of relationships, thereby offering a comprehensive overview that guides the creative process. As depicted in Figure 5.a, a diagram demonstrates the process of picture composition, while Figure 5.b illustrates how P4 constructs a narrative using TaleFrame. Initially, P4 begins by placing the event "Jack shoves Ryan" onto the canvas, marking the first step in the process (Figure 6.f). Subsequently, P4 attaches the two entities involved in this event to the corresponding box (Figure 6.g). Following this, P4 connects the two entities (Figure 6.h1), selects the connection line, and modifies the attributes of their relationship (Figure 6.e). These attributes include parameters such as the type of emotion, the type of action, the strength of the relationship, and the evolution of the relationship over time. The "action_direction" attribute within the relationship is represented by the "connect node," which, by default, progresses from the initiating entity to the target entity (e.g., "from Jack to Ryan"). However, for events such as "confront each other," where the action is mutual, a switch labeled "is bidirectional" is provided. Activating this switch ensures that the "action_direction" attribute is set to "bidirectional." By repeating this process for all components, P4 completes the remaining three steps, thereby linking all four event boxes and finalizing the narrative framework (Figure 6.h2). Upon completing the framework, P4 interacts with the interface by selecting the "Modify Story" option. At this stage, P4 updates the title and description while assigning the four events to their respective positions within the narrative structure. The story is then evaluated based on seven specific dimensions, as outlined in the evaluation framework (Figure 6.1). Additionally, a comprehensive suggestion is generated based on the feedback provided (Figure 6.m). According to the suggestion, P4 addresses components with lower scores by incorporating additional relationships into certain events. Subsequently, P4 clicks the "Regenerate" button to produce a revised version of the narrative. Finally, P4 utilizes the "Export" function to obtain the completed narrative, the associated diagram, and the JSON configuration file.

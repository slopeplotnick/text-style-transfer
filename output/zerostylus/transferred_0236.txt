Advanced market mechanisms have demonstrated significant advantages in scalability, adaptability, and fault tolerance compared to centralized approaches. For instance, Botelho and Alami (1999) pioneered structured auction protocols that enabled competitive task assignment, while Gerkey and Matario (2002) showed that auction methods could effectively distribute tasks without compromising coordination. Moreover, recent advancements have addressed communication constraints through the use of sophisticated bidding mechanisms, even under limited connectivity conditions (Ferri et al. 2017; Quinton et al. 2022). Nevertheless, auction-based methods do not consistently guarantee globally optimal solutions, as localized bidding decisions may result in suboptimal coordination when tasks exhibit complex interdependencies. In construction workflows, precise task coordination is paramount, as task sequences must adhere to specific orders. However, conventional auction mechanisms often struggle to address the temporal constraints critical for effective project scheduling. Consequently, these coordination challenges have motivated the exploration of more structured optimization approaches. Among these, dynamic programming has emerged as an alternative strategy for optimizing sequential decision-making processes. Bellman (1962) provided the theoretical foundation for deriving optimal solutions to problems characterized by properties such as optimal substructure. However, the exponential growth of the state space in dynamic programming often limits its practical application to small-scale problems. This limitation becomes particularly pronounced in construction projects, which involve numerous robots executing interdependent tasks. The complexity of these projects, coupled with the need for real-time decision-making, renders the computational demands of dynamic programming prohibitive. As a result, researchers have turned to metaheuristic approaches capable of addressing large and complex solution spaces. Notably, population-based metaheuristic methods leverage evolutionary principles to navigate such spaces effectively. For example, genetic algorithms have proven adept at managing combinatorial complexity and constraint hierarchies without requiring exhaustive mathematical formalization (Jones et al. 2010; Al-Omeer and Ahmed 2019). Similarly, swarm intelligence methods have facilitated distributed optimization, making them well-suited for coordinating large robotic teams (Chen et al. 2022; Lim and Isa 2015; Wang et al. 2012; Blum 2005). However, while metaheuristic approaches are computationally flexible, they do not guarantee solution optimality. Moreover, they often require significant computational resources and numerous iterations to achieve acceptable results. In dynamic construction environments that demand real-time adaptation to changing conditions, the prolonged computational time associated with these methods poses a significant challenge, further highlighting the need for more efficient optimization strategies.

System prompts provide task-agnostic control over the behavior of large language models (LLMs), defining roles, styles, or policies applicable across various tasks. Prior research has demonstrated that elements such as personas (Kim et al., 2024), emotions (Li et al., 2023), and jailbreak triggers (Shen et al., 2023) can influence the response style and reasoning processes of LLMs; however, they do not consistently improve task accuracy. Despite the increasing implementation of these prompts, the systematic optimization of system prompts remains largely unexplored. Recently leaked system prompts from Grok (xAI, 2025) and Claude (Breunig, 2025) reveal complex and verbose manually crafted rules. Nevertheless, the intricacies of these rule sets and their effects on model behavior are still not well understood. Recent advancements (Zhang et al., 2024; Secheresse et al., 2025) show that system prompts can be automatically optimized using genetic algorithms, though this optimization has thus far been confined to monolingual and single-metric contexts. Thus, multilingual deployment presents additional challenges, such as language-specific degradation and inconsistencies across languages. Multilingual LLMs often yield inconsistent outputs when posed with the same question in different languages, primarily due to unequal knowledge distribution across languages (Tam et al., 2025). Techniques such as Cross-Lingual Thought Prompting (Huang et al., 2023) and multilingual self-consistency (Yu et al., 2025) offer partial solutions but generally rely on multi-turn prompting strategies. Consequently, no comprehensive framework currently exists for optimizing system prompts that ensure accuracy and robustness across languages. Furthermore, beyond traditional metrics, recent studies have increasingly focused on understanding how LLMs reason by decomposing responses into interpretable reasoning units (Tam et al., 2025; Gandhi et al., 2025). However, much of this research has concentrated on surface-level analyses, such as the use of text prefill to control output language (Tam et al., 2025), without deeply investigating the underlying reasoning patterns of the models. Our work addresses this gap by linking system prompt design to multilingual reasoning behaviors, illustrating how these patterns correlate with cross-lingual generalization.

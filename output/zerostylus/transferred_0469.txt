Probabilistic programming languages (PPLs) are characterized by their first-class support for probabilistic constructs, including distributions, their samplers, and probabilistic estimation and assertions. Numerous popular examples exist across both imperative and functional programming paradigms. However, a primary limitation of existing PPLs is their inability to seamlessly handle text-intensive graph structures, which are essential for defining the specifications of language model systems (LMS). This limitation necessitated the development of LUMOS. PPLs frequently generate outputs based on user-defined probability distributions. For instance, in the generation of random images or scenes, specific languages are employed to construct user-defined scene graphs, facilitating applications such as self-driving vehicles. To construct real-world specifications, a fixed graph is utilized, and specific sections are sampled to delineate scenarios. In contrast, domain-specific languages (DSLs) for large language models (LLMs) lack intrinsic probabilistic constructs. They systematically aid in the composition, enhancement, and fortification of prompts to elicit appropriate responses from a specified LMS. LUMOS, therefore, facilitates the assessment of LMS behaviors across distributions of prompts as specified by LUMOS. Moreover, following the identification of LMS vulnerabilities through rigorous performance evaluations and adversarial attacks, these methods have been demonstrated to effectively reveal LMS weaknesses, thereby informing improvements in system design and robustness. Recent studies have developed probabilistic certifiers, offering formal assurances for evaluating specific properties of LLMs. In this work, we extend LMS certification using LUMOS to articulate and certify arbitrary desirable properties.

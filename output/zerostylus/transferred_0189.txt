Therefore, our reward system is designed to achieve three primary objectives: (1) Standardization of format, wherein specialized tokens are employed to distinctly demarcate the reasoning process from the ultimate answer, thereby facilitating comprehension and future utilization; (2) Constraining the output space, whereby the types of mismatches, such as "price mismatch" or "functionality mismatch," are rigorously limited to a predefined set, thus preventing the model from generating arbitrary outputs; (3) Implementing a progressive reward mechanism, whereby models that correctly ascertain relevance, yet exhibit minor errors, receive partial positive reinforcement to expedite and stabilize the learning process. To facilitate this process, we implement a three-tiered learning strategy designed to enhance model performance by progressively increasing the complexity of the training data. The proposed strategy incorporates three core principles: (1) Rapid Bootstrap Principle: The initial phase employs predominantly medium-difficulty samples to provide the model with immediate and constructive feedback, maintaining a level of challenge to mitigate early-stage training complications; (2) Progressive Challenge Principle: Subsequent phases incrementally introduce more complex samples, facilitating the model's transition from resolving common issues to addressing more challenging cases, thus broadening its capabilities; (3) Knowledge Retention Principle: The final phases continue utilizing easy samples as "memory anchors," preventing the model from losing previously acquired skills while assimilating new ones, thereby ensuring stability in the training process.

In contrast to methods that focus on enhancing generation processes—such as Mirror-Consistency (Li et al., 2024), Self-Contrast (Zhang et al., 2024), and Step-Back Prompting (Zheng et al., 2023a)—our approach emphasizes the aggregator's role. This distinction is significant because, while the aforementioned methods often incur sequential computational costs, our strategy accepts the noisy distribution of parallel samples and applies a distribution-calibrated layer. Consequently, this methodology allows for a more accurate and efficient estimation of the ground truth.

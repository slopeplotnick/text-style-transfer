The EMOPIA dataset is utilized for research in generating music that corresponds with emotions; however, substantial human-labeled MIDI datasets remain limited. To address this scarcity, it is crucial to recognize that most existing datasets are relatively small, typically containing fewer than 200 MIDI files annotated with emotion labels. In contrast, the EMOPIA dataset stands as the most extensive resource currently available, consisting of 1,087 music clips derived from 387 songs, with emotional labels assigned by a team of four annotators. These labels are organized into four emotion categories based on the valence-arousal plane, as detailed further in the Appendix (Section B). In addition to EMOPIA, this study employs GiantMIDI-Piano, which comprises 10,855 solo piano compositions by 2,786 distinct composers. This dataset serves various research domains, including the analysis of classical music and the generation of symbolic music. To align our text data with EMOPIA files, we categorized the 27 emotion labels into four quadrant categories using the NRC VAD lexicon. This lexicon provides arousal and valence scores for 55,000 words, thereby facilitating the assignment of each emotion to one of the four designated categories. The mappings and their placements on the AV plane are illustrated in Figure 1, with additional details on VAD and its relation to EMOPIA included in Appendix A. MIDI files play a crucial role in this work, as they encode and generate symbolic music representations. They offer a compact format for storing musical performance data, capturing emotion-related characteristics of music. Moreover, the widespread availability of MIDI files renders them particularly advantageous for training purposes. In our methodology, each MIDI file is conceptualized as a sequence of musical events, aligning well with sequence-to-sequence models for music learning and generation.

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) leverages pretrained diffusion models, which not only generate samples through iterative denoising processes but also serve as optimization objectives via score distillation [Poole et al., 2022]. This framework has been successfully employed to adapt image and video diffusion models for a variety of generative modeling tasks [Jiang et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023; Yin et al., 2024]. Building on the achievements of SDS in the vision domain, recent studies have explored its integration into reinforcement learning frameworks to improve control tasks. Specifically, certain methods replace the conventional discriminator in generative adversarial imitation learning (GAIL)-style setups with diffusion models [Huang et al., 2024b; Lai et al., 2024; Pang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024], utilizing their expressive capabilities to distinguish between authentic and synthetic trajectories. However, these approaches often rely on adversarial training procedures and necessitate continuous updates to the diffusion model during policy optimization, which can introduce inconsistencies. Beyond adversarial setups, alternative methodologies have sought to apply SDS-like techniques directly to policy learning. For instance, Luo et al. [2024] leverage pretrained image and video diffusion models conditioned on text prompts to guide policy training. While these systems are capable of generating behaviors that correspond to textual instructions, the resulting motions can sometimes appear unnatural. Among related approaches, SMILING [Wu et al., 2025a] shares similarities with our method, as it trains controllers using a score-matching objective akin to variational score distillation (VSD) [Wang et al., 2023]. Nevertheless, SMILING requires task-specific diffusion models for each application, whereas our proposed methodology introduces a task-agnostic motion prior derived from unstructured motion data. This generalized prior can be reused across a variety of control policies by integrating it with distinct task-specific objectives within a goal-oriented reinforcement learning framework. Furthermore, we incorporate several critical design choices that facilitate the generation of high-quality, naturalistic movements across a diverse range of tasks.

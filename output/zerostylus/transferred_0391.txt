To optimize the performance of multilingual models across diverse tasks, we exploit the inherent structural and linguistic relationships between languages, which naturally facilitate cross-lingual transfer. These relationships, such as semantic and syntactic similarity or shared orthographic patterns, allow related languages to be positioned adjacently within the latent space of multilingual transformer models like XLM-RoBERTa (Conneau et al.). Specifically, the representational space of such models encodes linguistic proximity, enabling seamless knowledge sharing across languages. To initiate this multilingual setup, we utilize the 100-language configuration of the pretrained XLM-R model, which encompasses both widely spoken and less prevalent languages, thereby ensuring broad linguistic coverage. Rather than requiring supervised data for all languages, we adopt a strategy that focuses on a representative subset of high-resource languages. By training on this subset, we effectively generalize the acquired knowledge to other languages, particularly low-resource ones, thus reducing the need for explicit modeling of rare languages. This approach significantly enhances cross-lingual knowledge transfer. Building on this framework, we translated the MultiJail (2024) dataset into 100 target languages using a combination of state-of-the-art machine translation systems tailored for specific linguistic groups. For high-resource languages, models such as GPT-4o (Hurst et al. 2024), M2MBart-50 (Tang et al. 2020), and Helsinki-NLP OpusMT (Tiedemann and Thottingal 2020) were employed due to their demonstrated efficacy. However, for low-resource languages, where translation models often exhibit reduced accuracy, we utilized SarvamTranslate (AI 2025), which has proven effective for Indic languages, even in low-resource settings. Additionally, GPT-4.1 (OpenAI et al. 2024) was applied to other rare languages, enabling the capture of nuanced linguistic and cultural features often overlooked by standard models. To further ensure translation accuracy, we verified a subset of translations using the Google Translate API, particularly for languages with limited resources. By leveraging this combination of advanced models, we achieved translations with high fluency, quality, and precision, thereby ensuring comprehensive linguistic representation within the dataset.

Research is delving into the parallels between brain function and artificial intelligence by examining "world models," which function as predictive and interpretative frameworks. In our analysis, we have determined that both the brain and artificial intelligence exhibit analogous methods of information processing, particularly in the context of large language models. Consequently, we have formulated a novel concept regarding the large-scale information processing mechanisms employed by the brain. Specifically, the neocortex and cerebellum construct predictive models of the world by anticipating future events, learning from errors, and utilizing these models to manage various tasks. The facilitation of comprehending sensory information and regulating motor activities exemplifies the foundational processes inherent in cognitive functionality. This concept integrates established frameworks, such as the internal model theory and the mirror neuron system, suggesting that a unified mechanism of this nature is essential for enabling a diverse range of functions using a shared foundational architecture. In conclusion, integrating research on the brain and artificial intelligence could establish a foundational understanding of the mechanisms underlying human-like intelligence. The cerebellum, in particular, functions as a predictive mechanism, assimilating external information, anticipating subsequent events, and refining its predictive accuracy through error learning. Research indicates that the underlying mechanism involves predicting subsequent sensory input rather than processing a higher-order signal.

A significant concern in the development of psychometric scales is represented by the "Jingle-Jangle Fallacy." This phenomenon occurs when two assessments bearing identical labels evaluate distinct constructs (jingle fallacy), or when two assessments with differing labels evaluate the same construct (jangle fallacy). Due to the intricacies of psychological phenomena and the lack of precise definitions, scales that initially appear distinct may actually exhibit considerable similarity and demonstrate high correlation; therefore, it is essential to avoid these fallacies by ensuring that psychometric scales accurately capture the intended constructs. This involves verifying their validity in terms of measuring the targeted attributes, demonstrating expected relationships with other variables, and maintaining distinctiveness from other scales. The iterative process of repeatedly validating scales, which necessitates considerable allocation of both resources and time, is not merely a procedural formality. Indeed, failure to address this issue has resulted in the proliferation of analogous measurement instruments, thereby engendering considerable confusion within the domain of psychological research. While the introduction of advanced AI software such as Open AI's "Chat GPT" may facilitate the development of novel methodologies in fields like social psychology, where traditional computer models have previously demonstrated limited efficacy, the underlying mechanisms through which these innovations might enhance research outcomes remain to be examined. In several research domains, including psychology, medicine, social sciences, and education, artificial intelligence has the potential to facilitate the development of psychometric instruments by efficiently generating preliminary question drafts that can subsequently undergo empirical validation. AI's foundation in language comprehension and extensive training on textual data enables it to address challenges in scale development concerning words and meanings; however, the mechanisms by which it achieves this proficiency remain underexplored.

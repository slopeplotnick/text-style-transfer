Addressing RQ1, results consistently demonstrate that summaries are more closely aligned with the first article in the input sequence, as evidenced by ROUGE-L and BERTScore metrics. However, the absolute metric values observed in this study deviate from those commonly reported in prior summarization literature. Unlike previous studies that compare model outputs to human-authored reference summaries (e.g., G et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023b), this evaluation methodology directly compares the generated summaries to the full original articles. The disparity in length between the summaries, which average 220 words, and the source articles, which range between 300 and 1600 words, inherently reduces token overlap and embedding-based similarity scores. Consequently, this study focuses on relative score variations across input conditions rather than absolute metric values. Moreover, a methodological limitation arises from the scarcity of metrics capable of evaluating the semantic similarity between summaries and their source texts. Predominantly reference-based evaluation frameworks provide limited tools for assessing source alignment. SummaC, a sentence-level entailment metric, yields consistently low scores and exhibits minimal sensitivity to positional changes. Abstract summaries often omit detailed source content, and entailment-based scoring penalizes unsupported or implicit statements. Thus, while the model prioritizes information from the first article, it is unlikely to reproduce its surface forms verbatim. As a result, SummaC does not reward subtle content biases unless explicit supporting sentences are retained.

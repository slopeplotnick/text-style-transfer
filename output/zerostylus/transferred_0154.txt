System prompts have emerged as a lightweight yet versatile mechanism for steering the behavior of large language models (LLMs) during inference. A growing body of literature demonstrates that carefully crafted prompts can significantly enhance reasoning capabilities (Wei et al., 2022) and reduce bias (Kamruzzaman and Kim, 2024). Nonetheless, most of this research has predominantly focused on English-language settings and has not adequately addressed the broader and more practical goal of ensuring a single system prompt works reliably well across languages. The deployment of these models in multilingual contexts presents unique challenges for prompt design. For instance, prompts optimized in English often lose efficacy when translated or applied to non-English tasks, even when using multilingual LLMs (Kmainasi et al., 2025). This results in inconsistent accuracy, unstable reasoning, and degraded cross-lingual generalization (Tam et al., 2025). Consequently, a critical question arises: How can we design a single prompt that maintains high and stable performance across diverse languages and tasks? We systematically address this question by deconstructing it into three primary inquiries: Q1: What types of system prompts most effectively guide models toward enhanced multilingual behavior? Q2: Is it possible to automatically discover prompts that achieve strong multilingual performance with minimal human effort? Q3: How do high-performing prompts influence model outputs across various dimensions, including the structure of reasoning patterns and the selection of response language? To explore these questions, we first establish a four-part evaluation framework to capture multilingual effectiveness from multiple angles: average accuracy, accuracy variance, cross-lingual consistency, and output length variance. Drawing insights from the performance of multiple LLMs on diverse tasks, we propose a methodology to optimize prompts for this multilingual, multi-metric objective. Subsequently, we analyze model outputs to reveal how optimized prompts shape reasoning behaviors and reduce cross-lingual variability. Moreover, we introduce a new multilingual prompt optimization technique, demonstrating the feasibility of automatic prompt discovery in multilingual settings. Additionally, we propose a novel perspective for analyzing the reasoning patterns of LLMs and their relationship with prompt design. By examining 10 million reasoning units from 1.6 million responses, we find that high-performing prompts often induce more structured and consistent reasoning, with less language-switching. This suggests that the reasoning patterns themselves may be a key driver of multilingual robustness. Collectively, our results illustrate that system prompt optimization is a promising and underexplored direction for achieving accurate and robust control over multilingual LLM behavior. The code and dataset are available at https://github.com/orange0629/multilingual-system-prompt.

Our findings indicate that refining system prompts is a notably effective strategy for enhancing the performance and reliability of multilingual large language models (LLMs); however, the potential of this approach remains insufficiently explored. To facilitate reproducibility and further investigation into multilingual system prompts, the code repository and corresponding dataset have been made publicly accessible at https://github.com/orange0629/multilingual-system-prompt. This availability enables researchers to delve deeper into the optimization of system prompts, thereby advancing our understanding of their impact on multilingual LLMs.

OpenHands exhibits a greater effort in problem resolution, likely attributable to the detailed instructions embedded within its system prompt [38], as compared to SWE-agent [45] (see detailed discussion in §2). Furthermore, stronger language models, such as Claude Sonnet 4, which consistently rank among the top-performing models across various tasks, demonstrate a more complex methodology for organizing information. This increased complexity can be explained by their tendency to gather additional context and perform supplementary evaluations to ensure patches are effective while avoiding regression issues (§3.3). Similarly, the Devstral model, despite being a smaller open-source alternative, exhibits a nuanced organizational approach when paired with OpenHands. This phenomenon may stem from its training on the OpenHands framework for software-oriented tasks [6], which emphasizes comprehensive exploratory searches and rigorous test runs before implementing modifications. Moreover, process-centric metrics reveal correlations between the methods of measurement and the agent's success in resolving issues. Notably, early termination incidents frequently occur due to runtime malfunctions, often linked to the model's failure to produce outputs in the expected format—a documented limitation of the system.<sup>7</sup> These metrics also reflect both the effectiveness of issue resolution and the perceived difficulty encountered by users. Successful trajectories are typically shorter and more focused, whereas unresolved ones tend to be longer, displaying redundant loops. The complexity of organizational structures, or GRAPHECTORY, is further influenced by agent design and the underlying language models. Stronger models, in particular, produce denser organizational structures characterized by broader exploratory mechanisms. Nonetheless, whether agents ultimately succeed or fail in resolving issues, their behavioral dynamics can be further analyzed through Phase Flow Analysis (§2.3.1). Structured methodologies, including systematic localization, patching, and validation processes, commonly underlie successful repair attempts and align closely with the agents' intended operational plans. As task difficulty escalates, successful trajectories may extend in length yet remain coherent, while unresolved ones are often marked by repetitive actions or disordered transitions between phases. Additionally, advanced language models consistently demonstrate richer and more adaptable strategies, underscoring their superior problem-solving capabilities.

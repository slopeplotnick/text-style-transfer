This plugin-style architecture enhances code reusability and accelerates the prototyping of novel PEFT methods. It allows researchers to independently develop and evaluate new methodologies, while the framework autonomously integrates these innovations during runtime. Moreover, the dynamic loading mechanism is particularly advantageous for comparative studies, as it enables researchers to evaluate multiple PEFT variants under consistent experimental conditions. This approach eliminates the need for redundant code and minimizes challenges related to version control. To comprehensively evaluate the system, we include datasets from the SuperGLUE benchmark, which is systematically organized into four task categories. These categories are: (1) natural language inference (NLI), represented by the CB dataset (De Marneffe et al., 2019); (2) question answering, which incorporates MultiRC (Khashabi et al., 2018), ReCoRD (Zhang et al., 2018), BoolQ (Clark et al., 2019), and COPA (Roemmele et al., 2011); (3) word sense disambiguation, exemplified by WiC (Pilehvar and Camacho-Collados, 2019); and (4) coreference resolution, as represented by WSC (Levesque et al., 2011). In addition to these, we include text generation datasets that are widely used for benchmarking generative LLMs. Specifically, we incorporate six datasets for reasoning and natural language understanding, which are categorized into three tasks: (1) question answering, including MMLU (Hendrycks et al., 2021), PIQA (Bisk et al., 2020), SIQA (Sap et al., 2019), and OBQA (Khot et al., 2019); (2) natural language inference (NLI), exemplified by Hellaswag (Zellers et al., 2019); and (3) commonsense reasoning, represented by WinoGrande (Sakaguchi et al., 2021). Furthermore, we include three datasets for mathematical problem solving, which are divided into three tasks: (1) question answering, represented by MathQA (Amini et al., 2019); (2) mathematical word problems, exemplified by GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021); and (3) elementary mathematical problems, as demonstrated by SVAMP (Patel et al., 2021). Finally, three datasets—Conala (Yin et al., 2018), CodeAlpacaPy (Chaudhary, 2023), and APPS (Hendrycks et al., 2021)—are included to facilitate code generation. To ensure seamless integration with the input formatting of PEFT-FACTORY, some datasets may require minor preprocessing or format modifications. These adjustments are further detailed in Appendix B.2.

Despite these evaluation limitations, the study confirmed three critical findings: First, leveraging LLMs for coordinating multi-robot task allocation is feasible. Second, employing structured validation methods significantly enhances the reliability of reasoning processes. Third, implementing hierarchical retry mechanisms effectively mitigates challenges associated with inconsistencies in LLM-generated outputs. With the feasibility of LLM-MRTA methodologies established, the authors developed the LTAA framework to enhance their effectiveness. The evaluation of LTAA utilizes the TEACH dataset, recognized as the standard benchmark for systematically comparing Q-learning, DQN, and DP methodologies within MRTA research. The transition of the dataset from household tasks to construction robotics tasks facilitates comparative analysis with established algorithms under consistent conditions. The LTAA framework specializes in the allocation component from the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture, paralleling traditional algorithms by employing pre-decomposed tasks to ensure that both LLM-based and algorithm-based approaches are evaluated on analogous problem types. Building upon the self-corrective concepts previously discussed, the LTAA framework employs a nine-step workflow. Unlike the comprehensive task management of the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture, LTAA focuses specifically on the allocation of robots to tasks, achieving outcomes comparable to those of conventional algorithms. In response to the challenges posed by traditional optimization algorithms and to ensure clarity in reasoning, LTAA was formulated to incorporate three primary innovations. These include dynamic adjustment of allocation strategies as projects advance, multi-stage validation with retry and self-correction to uphold LLM reasoning precision, and the integration of context-sensitive reasoning to facilitate decision-making based on robotic capabilities. The LangGraph methodology integrates a systematic nine-step workflow, which ensures tasks are processed through distinct stages of structured reasoning.

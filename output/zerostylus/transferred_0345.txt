The diverse architectures for memory management in financial systems underscore the limitations inherent in conventional frameworks. For instance, FinMem[22] exemplifies a financial-domain memory system that employs a layered memory architecture—comprising short-, mid-, and long-term components—to guide trading decisions. Similarly, MemP[5] introduces a general framework that transforms trajectories into sequential instructions and high-level strategies by leveraging procedural memory. Moreover, MemGPT operates in a manner analogous to an operating system, orchestrating hierarchical storage and handling interruptions to dynamically expand the effective context window of large language models (LLMs). The Zep system, on the other hand, integrates temporal knowledge engines, thereby enabling the simultaneous retrieval of historical relationships and evolving contexts. This integration enhances both the reasoning capabilities and the management efficiency of memory systems. To evaluate these systems, Accuracy (ACC) and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) are employed as the primary metrics. While ACC measures the overall prediction accuracy, MCC is particularly valuable for balanced datasets, making it more suitable for scenarios like stock prediction, where class distributions are often uneven. Our methodology was tested on four representative stocks, and the results demonstrated superior performance in both ACC and MCC metrics when compared to alternative approaches. Consequently, the effectiveness of our system is substantiated. A detailed analysis reveals fundamental discrepancies in the design of existing systems that undermine their effectiveness in financial prediction tasks. Specifically, FinMem's isolated storage of news data lacks temporal event modeling, resulting in an inability to capture critical shifts in market dynamics. Similarly, the abstraction of trajectories by MemP leads to excessive simplification, which omits essential financial details. In addition, MemGPT's absence of event-centric processing results in the frequent neglect of important financial patterns. Furthermore, Zep's inability to effectively integrate events prevents the system from identifying crucial interconnections within financial datasets. Our proposed approach addresses these challenges by introducing a paradigm that converts unstructured news into a temporally-structured event knowledge base. This structured representation systematically captures event logic and dependencies, thereby enabling the generation of traceable and reasoning-capable event knowledge. This is particularly significant as it elucidates the interconnectedness of events, facilitating a more comprehensive analysis and reasoning process. Ultimately, this enhances the accuracy and reliability of financial forecasts, aligning the system more closely with the complex requirements of financial prediction tasks.

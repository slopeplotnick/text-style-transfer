We propose TACO as a test-time scaling framework for VLAs, which not only retains the robust generalization capabilities of pretrained VLAs but also effectively constrains outputs to align with the success modes of specific downstream tasks. Moreover, we introduce an efficient internal representation mechanism for pseudo-count estimation within the CFN framework, which enables accurate measurement of distributional shifts while minimizing computational overhead. Furthermore, we demonstrate that TACO significantly enhances the performance of diverse VLA models in both simulated and real-world tasks by eliminating the necessity for prolonged training durations, thereby allowing for rapid operational efficacy and low latency. In contrast to online RL, where the agent actively explores the state-action space through continuous interaction with the environment, offline RL learns a policy solely from a fixed, previously collected dataset, without further environmental interaction. This offline setting presents significant challenges, particularly due to the distributional shift between the behavior policy that generated the data and the target policy being learned. Specifically, the offline dataset reflects the visitation distribution of the behavior policy, which may differ substantially from that of the learned policy. This discrepancy can lead to severe extrapolation errors when estimating values for out-of-distribution state-action pairs. A similar distributional shift is observed during the SFT stage of VLAs, where the VLA models encompass a broad and redundant range of action modes, while successful behaviors for specific downstream tasks typically occupy a much narrower region. Consequently, when sampling actions in flow or diffusion-based processes, the policy may generate suboptimal behaviors. In response to these challenges, TACO employs an anti-exploration strategy that imposes constraints on the policy, thereby ensuring it remains close to the support of the success data distribution. Unlike conventional offline RL approaches that typically rely on uncertainty-aware or conservative penalties, our method utilizes a straightforward count-based mechanism. This mechanism is particularly well-suited to the structure of VLA models and operates without the need for value estimation. Notably, count-based regularization has precedent in both classical RL and recent RLHF algorithms, further supporting its efficacy in our approach.

The difficulty of a problem is closely associated with the challenge it poses, and both intra-family and cross-family verifier enhancements display clear positive correlations. Notably, AIME emerges as a distinctive outlier in the final chart, likely due to specific challenges encountered by certain models during or after training. Moreover, the best-fit lines for verifier accuracy reveal two distinct clusters of tasks, delineated in red and blue, which indicate that verification difficulty cannot be attributed solely to solver accuracy. This observation raises an important question: are some tasks inherently easier to verify than others? Addressing this, Song et al. (2025) demonstrate that while models are limited in self-improvement on tasks requiring factual recall, they can achieve better performance on Tokoku with sufficient pretraining scale. A similar trend is observed in Figure 7, where tasks such as AIME, GSM8K, 3SAT, and Tokoku exhibit a higher verifier-to-solver accuracy ratio, consistently achieving superior performance across all verification settings. To further understand this, we note that synthetic datasets like Selenium and 3SAT exemplify problems that demand exponential solving time but allow polynomial-time verification. Conversely, tasks like matrix multiplication lack a clear verification shortcut, as verifying the product of two matrices essentially requires recomputation. Among real-world datasets, GSM8K and AIME include problems solvable using high-school-level mathematics, whereas MMLU (Social Sciences) necessitates domain-specific expertise, CommonsenseQA depends on implicit world knowledge, and GPQA and MMLU (STEM) require specialized scientific understanding. For such tasks, verifying an answer often necessitates the same level of knowledge as solving the problem. Although tasks that are straightforward to solve are typically easier to verify, it is evident that some tasks are inherently more verifiable. These inherently verifiable tasks include synthetic problems demanding logical or structured reasoning, such as 3SAT and Sudoku, as well as real-world tasks that predominantly rely on mathematical reasoning rather than extensive factual recall, exemplified by GSM8K and AIME. Furthermore, these tasks demonstrate greater benefits when verifiers are utilized for test-time rejection sampling, effectively enhancing model performance.

The integration of large language models (LLMs) within artificial intelligence (AI) systems, where various components collaborate to execute complex tasks, underscores the pivotal role of LLMs in subsequent decision-making processes. However, the specific mechanisms through which these models influence such processes warrant further investigation. Notably, the presence of an initial processing bias can compromise the integrity of the entire system. Therefore, addressing this bias is essential to ensure that these systems function in an equitable and transparent manner. This study identifies two primary concerns related to the evaluation of LLM-generated outputs. Although the lengths of input articles were standardized, the extent to which evaluation methods are affected by document length remains unexplored. To address this gap, future research could categorize data based on length or employ controlled-length experiments to better understand the influence of document length on analytical metrics. Moreover, the evaluation metrics applied in this study yielded consistently low scores, which highlights a broader issue when employing these methods for summarization tasks, particularly in comparing extended documents to concise summaries. Subsequent investigations should also extend beyond the current focus on abortion-related topics to explore whether order bias manifests in other contentious domains, such as gun control, LGBTQ+ rights, and immigration. Furthermore, evaluating different LLM architectures, such as GPT or Claude, may provide further insights into whether this bias is architecture-specific. Complementary metrics, including assessments of opinion bias and measures of polarization in the summaries, could also enhance understanding of the impact of information sequencing on summary generation. Empirical observations indicate that the sequence in which articles are presented significantly influences the summarization process performed by LLMs. Specifically, these models exhibit a pronounced tendency to prioritize the initial article, particularly in terms of semantic interpretation. This sequence-dependent effect aligns with prior research findings, which suggest that the presentation order of information exerts a substantial influence on the final output. Consequently, position bias poses challenges for systems that generate AI-driven summaries or configurations, as initial documents disproportionately shape subsequent outcomes. Addressing this bias is therefore imperative to develop systems that are not only equitable and transparent but also reliable and robust.

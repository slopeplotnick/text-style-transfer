Language models exhibit substantial utility in improving the monitoring of mental health outcomes, offering notable advancements over traditional methodologies. These computational programs' enhanced ability to comprehend context and gather information significantly bolsters their efficacy in large-scale mental health monitoring. However, large language models (LLMs) present certain drawbacks: they demand extensive computational resources and pose potential privacy concerns due to the processing of sensitive information on remote servers. Consequently, these limitations underscore the necessity for more efficient alternatives suitable for resource-constrained devices. In contrast, compact language models (CLMs) offer comparable or even superior performance for digital health applications. This is attributed to their reduced complexity, cost-efficiency, and capability to operate directly on edge devices. Notably, such models enable the inference of mental health concerns through social media activity while avoiding the retention of personal data, thereby facilitating timely and ethically sound monitoring. Nevertheless, the potential of CLMs remains insufficiently explored. This study evaluates the effectiveness of compact language models in predicting mental health outcomes, demonstrating their potential as viable solutions for monitoring a range of mental health conditions on personal devices. In parallel, we assess these models on tasks related to stress, depression, and the risk of suicide. We introduce Menta, a compact architecture subjected to specialized fine-tuning methodologies and trained across diverse datasets, thereby emphasizing accuracy. Menta exhibits a robust capability in addressing diverse mental health monitoring tasks, excelling particularly in detecting stress and depression, thus establishing itself as a reliable tool for applications where accurate and timely intervention is paramount. Furthermore, our demonstration highlights a shift towards device-centric mental health solutions by deploying it on an iPhone 15 Pro Max, where it operates in real-time with minimal memory usage, thereby facilitating private and scalable mental health support directly on the device.

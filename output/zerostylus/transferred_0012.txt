Prompt design is integral to the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs); however, the specific impact of prompt detail—whether highly detailed or more generalized—remains insufficiently explored. To address this gap, this study introduces DETAIL, a comprehensive framework designed to evaluate LLM performance across varying levels of prompt specificity. Specifically, prompts of differing degrees of detail were systematically generated using GPT-4, with their specificity quantified through perplexity metrics and their response accuracy assessed via GPT-based semantic equivalence. Experimental evaluations conducted across 30 novel reasoning tasks using GPT-4 and O3-mini reveal that prompts with greater specificity generally enhance accuracy, particularly for smaller-scale models and tasks requiring sequential reasoning. These findings underscore the importance of employing adaptive prompting strategies tailored to contextual factors and provide both methodologies and datasets conducive to advancing future research in this domain. The capabilities of LLMs have evolved substantially, driven by their remarkable proficiency in text generation and their ability to achieve breakthroughs at an unprecedented rate. Consequently, these models no longer excel solely in basic text-based tasks but now outperform state-of-the-art approaches across numerous domains. Recent studies have increasingly emphasized evaluating LLMs' reasoning capabilities in complex problem-solving scenarios, such as mathematical reasoning and multi-step problem-solving tasks. As reasoning enhancements remain a critical avenue for advancing LLM capabilities, emerging approaches have focused on leveraging refined prompt design to facilitate such improvements. Moreover, advanced reasoning strategies, including Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT), and Graph-of-Thoughts (GoT), have been developed to decompose intricate problems into manageable subcomponents, thereby improving model performance. Yet, a persistent challenge in this area involves determining the optimal level of prompt specificity required to achieve the most effective outcomes. While detailed prompts can offer structured guidance that enhances reasoning processes, they might also restrict the model's generative flexibility. Conversely, vague prompts provide greater freedom but risk introducing ambiguity and errors. Therefore, striking a balance between prompt detail and flexibility remains a pivotal consideration for optimizing LLM reasoning capabilities.

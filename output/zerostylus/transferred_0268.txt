The proposed scale comprises 9 items on a 7-point Likert scale, categorized into three subscales assessing AI-related emotional responses: Shame, Guilt, and Impostor Syndrome. To collect empirical data, a survey study was conducted, which included the preliminary draft of the scale (retained after content validation), alongside additional psychometric instruments designed to evaluate the criterion validity of the scale. This online survey was administered via Prolific, beginning on Monday, 03 February 2025, at 18:00 GMT, and concluding on Tuesday, 04 February 2025, at 23:30 GMT. Following data collection, the real-world dataset was compared with a previously generated simulated dataset, using the same analytical procedures applied in Study 1. A demographic comparison between the two datasets is provided in Table 1. The SAGAT scale will be elaborated upon in a forthcoming publication, which will detail its development through a conventional scale validation process, partially informed by our experimental prototyping approach. To explore potential differences between the real and simulated datasets, a Mann-Whitney U test was conducted. While the analysis revealed no significant differences between real and simulated scores for the Impostor Syndrome subscale (U = 41,516, p = .13), significant divergences were observed for both the Shame (U = 29,457, p < .001) and Guilt (U = 26,392, p < .001) dimensions. These findings were further supported by Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests, which indicated significant distributional differences across all three emotional constructs (all p values < .001). Consequently, the results do not support H4. Histograms illustrating the distribution of scores for the real and simulated datasets are presented in Figure 4. To enhance the validity and reliability of these findings, a follow-up study was conducted to develop a new measurement scale. Specifically, this study aimed to construct a scale for assessing AI-related anxious beliefs, referred to as the "AI Anxiety" scale. The development process followed the methodological framework outlined in Study 3, with additional details available in the corresponding "Methods" section of that study. Moreover, a comprehensive description of the in silico scale development process using simulated data is provided in the Supplementary Materials.

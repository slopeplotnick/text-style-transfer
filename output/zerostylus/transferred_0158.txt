While our research indicates that language models display a range of behaviors when evaluated on tasks inspired by human perceptual processes, the underlying mechanisms and implications of these behaviors require further exploration. Models with higher accuracy, such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and Llama-4 Maverick, provide stronger evidence of employing Bayesian methodologies, particularly in image analysis contexts (see Appendix A.11 for further details). These models adapt their operational strategies in a manner akin to Bayesian methodology when faced with variations such as noise or additional context, thereby effectively leveraging prior knowledge even without explicit training for such tasks. Decades of cognitive science research suggest that human perception can be successfully modeled by Bayesian frameworks, which account for a significant portion of perceptual processing without the need for explicit training (Petzschner et al., 2015; Knill & Pouget, 2004). This underscores the importance of behavior-centric analysis to enhance the understanding of agent behavior, necessitating further investigation to elucidate its mechanisms.

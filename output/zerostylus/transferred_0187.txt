The classification of temporal phases, workload distribution methodologies, and strategic approaches tailored to each phase are integrated into the workflow through Node 1 (Phase Identification), which functions as the foundational step in delineating phase structures. This node serves as the initial checkpoint within a structured framework comprising nine interconnected nodes, collectively referred to as the LTAA configuration, thus establishing a coherent entry point for the system's operational logic. Through this, the system's state is assessed, and the temporal context for subsequent operational phases is determined, ensuring seamless integration into the workflow. The assessment of task progression and the updating of workload metrics are systematically conducted within the framework through the Phase Detection Node, which acts as a critical evaluative point in the operational workflow. As illustrated in Figure 9a, the node acquires critical system parameters: completed_task_count, total_task_count, workload_distribution_among_robots, and optimal_workload_criteria, which are subsequently integrated into the operational framework. These details facilitate the establishment of the contextual framework by delineating the procedural steps. The node evaluates the system's progression by assessing the current phase and categorizes the operational stage as early, middle, or late, which is further utilized by Node 1 to inform phase-specific strategies within the workflow. Each phase embodies a distinct decision scenario, influencing how subsequent nodes equilibrate success metrics and workload equity. Moreover, the node evaluates the distribution of tasks by deriving a balance_score, quantifying the uniformity of task allocation, and an imbalance_severity metric, which measures the deviation from the ideal workload distribution. This comprehensive metric encapsulates both the progression and workload dynamics in a straightforward manner. Consequently, the node concludes by updating the system state with new parameters: allocation_phase, balance_score, imbalance_severity, and operational_mode, which are conveyed to the subsequent node. This ensures that subsequent stages are informed by the most up-to-date insights regarding progress metrics and workload distribution. The Phase Detection Node processes raw data to generate structured decision information, as illustrated in Figure 9a, which is subsequently utilized in the successive phase. Implementing the phase allocation strategy necessitates robust reasoning capabilities inherent in large language models to facilitate optimal decision-making processes. However, the outputs generated by LLMs are inherently variable and may not consistently meet the rigorous quality requirements necessary for critical construction tasks. Therefore, the establishment of a validation framework with a weighted assessment and feedback mechanism was necessitated by the challenges encountered during the SMART-LLM implementation, ensuring a rigorous evaluation of reasoning quality.

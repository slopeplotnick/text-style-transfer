To address the challenge of identifying optimal planning strategies through extensive reasoning over story information and generating detailed long-form responses, Gurung and Lapata (2025) introduce VR-CLI (Verifiable Rewards for Complex Logical Inference). The VR-CLI system functions as an incentive mechanism designed to increase the probability of deriving the appropriate subsequent chapter by leveraging narrative information and strategic planning. Furthermore, enhancements to VR-CLI include a component that reduces the probability of selecting a random chapter from the same book. LiteReason represents the standard approach, wherein incremental training steps are occasionally performed utilizing the pathways generated during the corresponding period. This necessitates a somewhat greater computational expense; however, it aligns the reasoning component with the current model's cognitive framework. Moreover, we employ a latent thought prompt that facilitates the model's utilization of latent thought tags. In the absence of SFT, LiteReason retains both the concealed prompt and the initial Reasoning Projector, which utilizes hidden thought tags to determine when to introduce new reasoning to the model; however, it does not employ SFT during the training phase to update the Reasoning Projector. While LiteReason operates without the Reasoning Projector, it assesses efficacy post-training using the hidden thought prompt, the implications of which remain unexamined. Building on established approaches that bypass latent reasoning, we investigate whether the model exhibits distinct behaviors when reasoning steps are omitted. Drawing from recent studies on hidden reasoning, we select a representative set of historical methods to assess the concepts presented in §3. We evaluate these approaches on both the Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks. Additionally, we introduce a comparative analysis with two novel methodologies, specifically Mixture of Inputs (MoI) as proposed by Su et al., for training-free hidden reasoning, diverging from traditional approaches that prioritize reasoning in isolation. To integrate the embedding of the sampled token with others, as outlined in 2025, we draw upon the methodology of Soft Thinking by Zhang et al. To determine the transition from continuous reasoning to discrete sampling, the approach introduced by Gurung and Lapata (2025) integrates token embeddings while employing an entropy-based criterion to regulate the decision-making process.

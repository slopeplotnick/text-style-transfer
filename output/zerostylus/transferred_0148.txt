We introduce a novel framework that distinctly separates perception from cognitive processing within AI models, highlighting the implications of this separation for further exploration. To delineate erroneous reasoning, negative exemplars are incorporated into the training dataset, thereby enabling the model to discern between correct and incorrect logic. In contrast, our Self-Training through Learning (STL) framework facilitates an integrated enhancement of both perceptual and cognitive processes, surpassing previous methods that concentrated solely on cognitive improvement. Building on evaluations across domains such as commonsense, language, and science, we determine that the proposed method demonstrates superior performance while obviating the necessity for human intervention. Reasoning plays a critical role in enhancing the performance of models within the domain of vision-language reasoning. However, while contemporary models perform admirably on standardized assessments, the integration of visual information into reasoning processes remains challenging, particularly for open-source models. In these approaches, the model employs prompts to facilitate step-by-step reasoning. For instance, one strategy initially considers contextual information before feature creation, whereas another utilizes scene graphs to comprehend the relationships among objects. Alternatively, approaches that involve training models on datasets with reasoning examples can be costly due to the creation of such datasets. Therefore, we propose an autonomous learning framework that enables models to enhance their reasoning capabilities independently, thereby obviating the need for specialized datasets. To explore this concept, we define self-training as a process whereby a model utilizes its own outputs as input data for further learning. While leveraging reasoning steps through creation and reuse has enhanced reasoning capabilities in large language models, the underlying mechanisms by which these steps contribute to improved model performance remain unexplored. This iterative process allows models to refine their capabilities autonomously, thus eliminating the necessity for human intervention. For instance, the selective and meticulous refinement of reasoning exemplars has demonstrated an enhancement in cognitive reasoning processes. Nonetheless, the exploration of self-training methodologies for vision-language models remains relatively underdeveloped.

When individuals interact with systems, the maintenance of trust is contingent upon the system's reliability; however, instances of hallucination disrupt this trust by producing erroneous outputs that diverge from expected factual accuracy. Therefore, when such errors are encountered, it becomes crucial to assess their impact on the user's experience. An investigation was conducted to examine hallucinations in language models, where participants were tasked with identifying and reporting these inaccuracies. This study employs innovative methodologies to quantify the impact of hallucinations on users, particularly in terms of diminished trust and the necessity for users to independently verify information, a task ostensibly designated for the system. Moreover, the comprehensive approach to understanding hallucinations and their management can be derived from employing diverse methodologies to establish more robust frameworks. Various methodologies, such as fact-checking, consistency analysis, and assessing user feedback, have been employed to evaluate the efficacy of language models. This method identifies hallucinations from multiple perspectives, assessing not only the factual accuracy but also the coherence of arguments and the user’s perception of the content. While understanding the task is critical when deploying large language models across diverse contexts, it is equally important to account for the potential occurrence of hallucinations. Widely adopted for general applications, such as chatbot development, models like GPT-4 and PaLM are notable for their ability to generate human-like text with a high degree of accuracy and a touch of creative flair, thereby minimizing the likelihood of errors. Conversely, for tasks necessitating factual accuracy, such as information retrieval or question answering, models like BERT and T5 demonstrate superior performance due to their explicit emphasis on factual knowledge and robust comprehension capabilities.

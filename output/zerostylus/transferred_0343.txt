Guardrail systems function as external moderation layers intended to enforce safety constraints and mitigate harmful content during interactions between human users and artificial intelligence models. Initially, basic rule-based filtering approaches were employed; however, these systems, while effective in constrained settings, struggled to adapt to evolving safety policies and emerging risks. Consequently, they often exhibited limited coverage and low accuracy. The development of advanced artificial intelligence models has endowed newer guardrails with improved flexibility and enhanced their capacity for generalization. For instance, Llama Guard is a moderation model built upon large language models, fine-tuned using specialized safety datasets from Meta AI to ensure robust conversational safeguards. Furthermore, research on visual safety has introduced models such as Llama Guard 3 Vision and LlavaGuard, which are capable of addressing safety concerns in visual contexts. Nevertheless, despite these advancements, several challenges persist. A notable limitation of prior methodologies is their predominant focus on text and images, whereas videos and audio were often addressed through simplistic techniques that lacked contextual comprehension. Moreover, the inherent limitations of most systems restrict their ability to synergize across media; they are often constrained to single media types or specific scenarios, thereby inhibiting their capacity to integrate diverse media forms or reason across them. To address these challenges, we introduce OMNIGUARD, the initial framework designed to support various media types and facilitate reasoning across them to maintain safety measures. This system aims to ensure comprehensive and interpretable safety assessments across multiple media platforms.

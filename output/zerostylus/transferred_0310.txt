Auditors employ Journal Entry Tests (JETs) to identify anomalies in tax-related ledger records. However, these rule-based methods often generate an overwhelming number of false positives and struggle to detect subtle irregularities. To address these challenges, this study investigates the potential of large language models (LLMs) as anomaly detection tools in double-entry bookkeeping. Specifically, we benchmarked state-of-the-art LLMs, such as LLaMA and Gemma, using both synthetic and real-world anonymized ledger data. Furthermore, we compared their performance against JETs and traditional machine learning approaches. The results demonstrate that LLMs consistently outperform rule-based JETs and classical machine learning models, while also providing natural-language explanations that enhance interpretability. Therefore, these findings underscore the promise of AI-augmented auditing, where collaboration between human auditors and foundation models can significantly bolster financial integrity. The role of financial auditors is indispensable in identifying unusual transactions and anomalies that may signify errors, fraud, or tax evasion within a company's financial records. Ensuring that these irregularities are detected is essential for maintaining stakeholder confidence in financial reporting. However, the increasing volume and complexity of accounting data present significant challenges. Current manual and rule-based processes, while widely used, are often inefficient and prone to error, leading to the possibility of auditors overlooking critical warning signs. For instance, traditional JETs, which rely on predefined rules and established patterns, frequently generate a high volume of alerts. Although these full-population tests are effective at detecting anticipated anomalies, they inundate auditors with false positives that require extensive review. Consequently, auditors remain reliant on sampling and manual procedures, which are insufficient for identifying subtle discrepancies in today’s expansive and intricate datasets. This widening gap between the growing complexity of financial data and the limitations of traditional auditing methodologies highlights the pressing need for advanced, automated anomaly detection tools to enhance the efficacy and accuracy of the auditing process.

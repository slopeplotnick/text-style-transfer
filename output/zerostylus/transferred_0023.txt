Certain algorithms, such as OPRO or EvoPrompt, do not rely on few-shot examples; however, they can significantly enhance the performance of large language models, even when employing straightforward methods for selecting examples. The promptsolution framework introduces a module that facilitates post hoc selection of examples, utilizing random selection and random search methodologies to incorporate few-shot examples within a fixed instruction framework. This integration necessitates the provision of an initial set of prompts for many optimization algorithms to commence their process. The methodology presented herein enables the automated generation of prompts derived from a task description, an initial prompt, or dataset-derived examples, thereby enhancing the flexibility of prompt creation. Furthermore, callbacks are designed to monitor the optimizer's state throughout each optimization step and facilitate the tracking of progress during the process. These callbacks possess the capability to evaluate the optimizer's state at each step, with the option to terminate the process if necessary. Such mechanisms are crucial, as they facilitate the monitoring of token utilization, ensuring it does not exceed predefined thresholds, and enable the archival of prompts and their evaluations for subsequent analysis. To evaluate the efficacy of Promptolution in comparison to alternative methodologies, we conducted prompt optimization experiments on GSM8K, a dataset of mathematical problem-solving tasks, and SST-5, a benchmark for sentiment classification. The primary utilization and occasional meta-model application of Gemma-3-27B were also examined. To assess the effectiveness of gemma-3-27B as the principal language model and its potential utility as a meta-model for particular optimizers necessitating such integration, we employed configurations that included this model both as a standalone and supplementary component. Moreover, for comparative analysis, optimizers such as CAPO, EvoPromptGA, and OPRO from Promptolution were evaluated alongside leading optimizers from AdalFlow and DSPy. The impact of optimization was assessed by evaluating three manually crafted prompts for each dataset, with their results averaged for comparison. To ensure equitable comparison across optimization algorithms, the hyperparameters remained consistent for each optimizer, while the token budget was constrained to one million for combined input and output, thereby maintaining expenditure below $0.15 for this particular model configuration. Before initiating any prompt creation, all frameworks commenced merely with a task description, devoid of initial prompts, to evaluate their automatic functionality. This procedure involved the evaluation of both unoptimized prompts and those optimized by each algorithm using an independent test set.

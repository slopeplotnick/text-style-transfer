In evaluating the quality of generated summaries, it is essential to adopt a multi-dimensional approach that considers lexical overlap, semantic similarity, and factual consistency. The effectiveness of traditional metrics, such as BLEU [Papineni et al., 2002] and ROUGE-L [Lin, 2004], is often compromised when assessing summaries that exhibit semantic equivalence but diverge in lexical representation; these metrics primarily focus on n-gram overlap between system outputs and reference summaries. To address these limitations, Zhang et al. [2019] introduced BERTScore, which utilizes pre-trained contextual encoders to prioritize semantic comparison over mere lexical analysis, enhancing the evaluation of summaries that require concise abstraction. Moreover, assessing the factual consistency of a summary with its source documents is crucial. To this end, several metrics have been developed. FactCC [Kryscinski et al., 2020] employs a trained classifier to determine whether each sentence in the summary is corroborated by the source text; however, subsequent analyses indicate that FactCC can be unstable, occasionally misclassifying sentences that are indeed supported by the original text. Conversely, BLANC [Vasilyev et al., 2020], a reference-free metric, evaluates how effectively a summary aids a language model in reconstructing masked portions of the source document. Nonetheless, BLANC scores are sensitive to the choice of underlying language model and sometimes emphasize lexical correspondence over semantic entailment. To further address these challenges, SummaC [Laban et al., 2021] applies a Natural Language Inference (NLI) framework to assess factual consistency, typically employing models such as RoBERTa-MNLI. SummaC operates at the sentence level, evaluating whether the original text substantiates each claim in the summary. Empirical evaluations consistently demonstrate that SummaC outperforms earlier metrics, such as FactCC. Therefore, to ensure a comprehensive assessment in this study, we adopt a multiple evaluation strategy: ROUGE-L to capture lexical overlap, BERTScore to measure semantic similarity, and SummaC to evaluate factual consistency between the generated summaries and the original articles.

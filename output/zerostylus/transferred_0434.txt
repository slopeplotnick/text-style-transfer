This section provides an in-depth description of the system setup, encompassing dataset construction, judge model configuration, and the human verification process. Furthermore, we aim to systematically bridge the domains of Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV) by conducting a rigorous empirical study that examines their interplay through careful analysis. To this end, we outline the methodology adopted for designing our study and establishing the test procedures. Central to our investigation are three pivotal research questions (RQs) that guide our analysis: (1) How do state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods for hallucination detection and fact verification perform when applied to identical AI-generated outputs, and do consistent winners emerge? (2) Do these two paradigms exhibit unique, complementary strengths in detecting distinct types of factual inaccuracies, or are their capabilities largely overlapping? (3) Can a more effective system be developed by integrating signals from both paradigms, thereby surpassing the performance of employing either approach in isolation? To address these questions, our study is organized into three distinct yet interconnected phases. First, to answer RQ1, we implemented a comprehensive set of established and SOTA methodologies from both paradigms and conducted a rigorous head-to-head comparison of their performance on a unified testbed, marking the first systematic comparison of this kind. Second, building on these results, we quantitatively and qualitatively explored the synergy between HD and FV to answer RQ2. Our findings reveal that integrating these paradigms yields significant performance enhancements compared to combining methods within a single paradigm. To provide a deeper understanding, we conducted a detailed case analysis that highlights specific examples of this synergy. Finally, motivated by these findings, we addressed RQ3 by designing and evaluating two straightforward yet effective hybrid methods. Specifically, we tested integration strategies at both the final probability score level and the system pipeline level. The results demonstrate that even simple fusion approaches can significantly enhance overall system performance, underscoring the potential of hybrid methodologies.

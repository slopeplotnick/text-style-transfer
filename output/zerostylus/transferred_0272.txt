The reanalysis using the ATTARI subscales demonstrated that only the behavioral subscale (BEH) emerged as a significant predictor, while the affective and cognitive subscales did not contribute when considered together (refer to Supplementary Material S3). This indicates that individuals' predisposition towards adopting AI applications predominantly influences their attitudes toward AI, rather than their general perceptions or emotions concerning it. To further explore participants' perspectives, we conducted a thematic analysis of 178 open-ended responses. These responses were categorized into thematic groups, as summarized in Table 2, with comprehensive descriptions available in the Supplementary Materials. Discussions frequently revolved around themes such as the technical and emotional quality of the music, its human-like attributes, and its overall impact. Respondents commonly assessed AI-generated music based on its technical and musical quality, originality, and ability to elicit emotional responses. Notably, the concept of humanness was the most frequently discussed, extending beyond just the auditory characteristics of the music. Many respondents highlighted the significance of the human element, such as the artist's personality or the narrative behind a song, arguing that AI-generated music often lacked the soul and authenticity they valued. Nevertheless, some individuals expressed indifference towards the origins of music, with sentiments like, "If it possesses auditory appeal, then its artificial generation is inconsequential" (ID926). Participants' evaluations of AI-generated music were significantly influenced by their perceptions of AI, which affected how they judged its musical quality. Furthermore, several comments reflected personal opinions regarding AI in general, rather than focusing on the quality of the songs, exemplified by statements such as, “I don’t like anything to do with AI-generated music” (ID914). The responses also highlighted alternative perspectives on AI-generated music, encompassing aspects not typically addressed in experimental studies. Participants frequently evaluated AI-generated music through ethical lenses, including concerns about its potential to displace musicians, negatively impact artists’ financial stability, or rely on training data acquired without the consent of the creators who have devoted years to their craft (“is built upon data without the consent of the artists that have dedicated decades of their lives to the craft,” ID615).

Respondents articulated concerns regarding AI-generated music, specifically highlighting issues of exploitation and disenfranchisement of artists. Consequently, they indicate that evaluations of AI music extend beyond auditory quality to encompass ethical considerations in its creation and distribution. Moreover, the increasing presence of AI music on streaming platforms raises socioeconomic questions about fairness and transparency that warrant examination. Therefore, given the influence of attitudes toward AI on music liking and emotions, experimental designs manipulating information about the creation and distribution process could be addressed. For example, transparency concerning training data or the intended revenue share of AI-music pieces could be modeled as experimental conditions. Furthermore, different production and usage contexts merit detailed investigation. In particular, the perception of songs created collaboratively between human and AI systems has become a topic of interest, especially as current text-to-music systems increasingly enable processes of co-creation. Also, expanding on the work of Moura and Maw (2021), the perception and acceptance of AI music in functional use cases could be examined. These findings emphasize that attitudes toward AI play a central role in shaping how listeners perceive and evaluate AI-generated music, outweighing composer information, genre, and listening habits or other personal characteristics. Looking ahead, observing how general attitudes toward artificial intelligence develop over time will be crucial, as they are likely to shape both acceptance and resistance in cultural domains such as music. More broadly, the debate is not solely about whether AI-generated music can be enjoyed or recognized as human-like, but about what constitutes "authentic" and "valuable" creativity. By critically engaging with audience responses, we can address questions such as: What kinds of (artificial) creativity do we want to acknowledge, and under which terms?

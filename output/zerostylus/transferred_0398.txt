The primary competencies targeted by our framework encompass: (i) implicitly inferring 3D structures, (ii) managing camera perspectives to maintain geometric consistency (considering factors such as scale, occlusion, and layout), (iii) understanding spatial arrangements and semantic layout within a scene, (iv) devising plans to achieve specified goals, and (v) preserving temporal coherence over extended sequences. To evaluate these capabilities, we design two tasks: video-based scene navigation and object grounding. In scene navigation, the model follows camera movement instructions and generates a video trajectory that aligns with both the provided directives and the underlying scene geometry. In contrast, the object grounding task requires the model to process a text-based instruction to locate and visually focus on a target object, thereby testing its understanding of spatial arrangements and its ability to formulate coherent plans. Together, these tasks assess whether the proposed framework, VIDEO4SPATIAL, can infer and act on scene structure using video data exclusively. Since publicly available datasets tailored for these tasks are limited, we repurpose indoor scanning datasets such as ScanNet++ and ARKitScenes, which provide extended video sequences of diverse room environments. These datasets enable the construction of paired context-target video clips. To facilitate task execution, we employ a pre-trained vision-language model (VLM) to identify clips that conclude with a centered object and to generate natural language instructions for object localization. For scene navigation, camera movement data is converted into actionable pose-following directives. Consequently, the proposed framework demonstrates notable efficacy in addressing challenging visuospatial tasks. Specifically, from video context alone, the model infers scene-level geometry and semantics, achieving robust performance in both object recognition and scene navigation tasks. Furthermore, despite being trained on short video clips, the model generalizes effectively to longer temporal sequences during evaluation, thereby enhancing its overall performance. Notably, while trained predominantly on indoor environments, the model also exhibits strong generalization capabilities to outdoor scenes and previously unseen object categories. In summary, we introduce VIDEO4SPATIAL, a streamlined yet powerful video-based framework for visuospatial tasks. Our contributions include (1) the development of a framework that relies solely on video context to execute visuospatial tasks, (2) highlighting key design choices, such as joint classifier-free guidance over context and instructions, auxiliary bounding boxes as reasoning priors to improve grounding accuracy, and non-continuous context sampling for efficient understanding, and (3) the instantiation and evaluation of two spatial tasks—scene navigation and object grounding—demonstrating the framework’s effectiveness across diverse settings.

Our primary focus was on pivotal design choices, including the integration of guidance for both context and instructions, the incorporation of bounding boxes to enhance accuracy, and the strategic sampling of context to facilitate more efficient understanding. However, the underlying mechanisms and their contribution to improved model performance remain unexplored. In addition, we developed and evaluated two tasks specifically designed to probe high-level spatial reasoning within structured environments. These tasks aim to enhance our understanding of spatial cognition by examining the model's ability to perform complex reasoning. Therefore, the exploration of these tasks contributes to the broader objective of advancing spatial understanding in structured settings.

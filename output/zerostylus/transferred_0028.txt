Despite notable advancements in memory technology, the complexities involved in applying these advancements to stock price prediction remain significant. Current memory systems exhibit deficiencies in three key areas: the granularity of event sequence decomposition, the ability to correlate historical scenarios with novel instances, and the capacity to accommodate the temporal dynamics of financial market fluctuations. These specific challenges underscore the necessity for developing memory systems explicitly tailored to the demands of the financial domain. In this regard, the research examined in this chapter explores the transition from reliance on historical pattern matching to the integration of mnemonic systems, alongside the growing importance of prediction mechanisms driven by events. However, despite these advancements, a critical challenge persists in integrating structured event knowledge with traceable memory systems, and this study focuses on addressing this significant issue. Traditionally, stock price prediction has depended on identifying patterns within historical data that closely align with current market conditions to inform future forecasts. In much the same way as retrieval agents in memory systems, memory systems in large language models (LLMs) aim to access stored experiences or contexts for relevant information across different temporal levels. The disparity between the efficacy of statistical methods in identifying numerical patterns and their inability to process complex market information further highlights the necessity for more sophisticated analytical approaches in financial data analysis. When deep learning methodologies are employed, analytical frameworks such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Transformers can autonomously discern temporal features. Moreover, Transformer-based models have demonstrated a notable capacity for identifying long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms, thus facilitating advancements in the field. Since its incorporation, the FinSeer framework has utilized LLMs to identify historical data with numerically analogous characteristics, thereby enabling a more comprehensive analysis of features.

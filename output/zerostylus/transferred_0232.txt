This study examines the acquisition of diverse linguistic categories by a computational framework that integrates an adult language model, Delilah, alongside a daughter agent. The daughter model, specifically designed for MODOMA, employs explicit graph representations of grammatical knowledge to construct and interpret sentences, with its performance improving progressively as language acquisition advances. Conversely, the Delilah framework incorporates a parser and generator, independently developed by Cremers and Hijzelendoorn, which leverages Dutch grammar rules to facilitate sentence construction and parsing. Therefore, an innovative approach is necessitated within the MODOMA framework to analyze Delilah’s role: beyond enhancing Delilah’s proficiency in sentence construction and comprehension, it is imperative to examine its output to enable the daughter agent to detect acquisition-relevant patterns. To achieve this, the proposed methodology integrates linguistic principles and interdisciplinary strategies, such as psycholinguistics and corpus linguistics, to analyze machine-generated language in a manner analogous to human linguistic interpretation. Furthermore, the emergence of large-scale linguistic models underscores the necessity of examining the extent to which machine-generated language parallels human language, although the implications of such similarities remain underexplored. The experimental results presented in this paper yield several key findings. Notably, conversational exchanges between Delilah and the daughter agent treat each utterance as an independent instance, in contrast to corpora, which typically consist of sequentially connected sentences. Nonetheless, similar grammatical structures emerge across both categories of data, indicating that these patterns may be attributed more to individual sentences than to the broader corpora. Crucially, this study demonstrates that Delilah's output exhibits divergent frequency patterns of language categories, which are well-established for human language data in corpus linguistics. However, as Delilah is not explicitly configured to replicate these patterns, their presence is a direct consequence of the grammar model employed.

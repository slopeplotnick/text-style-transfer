Search relevance is a critical component of e-commerce platforms such as JD and Taobao, where it is defined as the process of assessing the alignment between user queries and available items (Yuan et al., 2023). Specifically, relevance models evaluate candidate products by measuring their alignment with consumer demands, assigning relevance scores to filter out items that do not match (Liu et al., 2022; Carmel et al., 2020). This process is foundational for improving both the efficiency of search systems and the overall user experience. In this context, large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated exceptional capabilities and have been widely adopted for relevance-related tasks. However, state-of-the-art (SOTA) LLMs, such as GPT-5, face limitations, including insufficient domain-specific knowledge and high operational costs (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025; Bai et al., 2025; Comanici et al., 2025). To address these challenges, practitioners have increasingly focused on post-training smaller LLMs on domain-specific e-commerce data, thereby tailoring them as relevance experts (Liu et al., 2024; Mehrdad et al., 2024). Recent developments in this area have transitioned the post-training paradigm from basic classification-based supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to more advanced methodologies aimed at enhancing reasoning capabilities (Mehrdad et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025a; Tang et al., 2025; Fang et al., 2025; Dong et al., 2025). Although progress has been made, particularly in modeling chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning (Wei et al., 2023), significant challenges persist, particularly in handling complex relevance scenarios. For example, the ELLM model conceptualizes relevance as a process of attribute extraction and matching between queries and product descriptions (Zhao et al., 2025a). Nonetheless, it exhibits limited performance in edge cases requiring the application of nuanced rules. A notable instance involves matching the query "lake blue top" with the item description "sky blue top," where the model fails to distinguish between these subtle color differences due to the absence of explicit rule-based judgment capabilities. To address such deficiencies, models like LREF (Tang et al., 2025) and TaoSR1 (Dong et al., 2025) integrate explicit rule-following mechanisms into their chain-of-thought reasoning frameworks, thereby improving rule-awareness. However, despite these enhancements, these models still neglect visual information, which is essential for certain relevance tasks. For instance, in scenarios where a user searches for a "blue top," and the product title lacks a color specification while the accompanying image clearly depicts a blue top, the model is unable to resolve the mismatch due to its inability to process visual data. These limitations underscore a fundamental question: What constitutes a comprehensive chain-of-thought representation for relevance tasks? Addressing this question requires identifying the specific competencies necessary to enable models to effectively assess relevance in a robust and comprehensive manner.

In this section, we elucidate the configuration employed in this study. We examine the data, the problem solvers, and the checkers, and elucidate the methodologies employed to evaluate the behaviors of the solvers and checkers. Moreover, we outline the setup employed for verification processes within our study. In this analysis, we recognize that verifier gain serves as a measure over an extended duration. The mechanism operates to demonstrate the implications of continuous monitoring and serves as a boundary on the enhancement achievable through checker-based sampling. In this study, we utilize verifier gain to assess variations in checker behavior. In this context, we categorize models into groups known as families, such as Llama3 or Qwen2.5, wherein each family encompasses related models of varying sizes. Models categorized as basic and improved demonstrate distinct operational behaviors; therefore, they are regarded as separate classifications. For example, the foundational model, meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-70B, is categorized within the Llama3-Base classification, whereas the enhanced model, meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct, is included in the Llama3 classification. Each solver-verifier pair derived from our models is systematically categorized into one of three verification configurations, each representing a distinct verification approach. Firstly, in the self-verification configuration, the model functions simultaneously as both the solver and the verifier, thus performing self-evaluation of the generated solutions. The setup involves employing a 70B Llama3 model as both the solver and verifier, with evaluations focusing on metrics such as accuracy specific to this configuration. Secondly, within the intra-family verification framework, the system evaluates the validity of responses by examining outputs from other models within the same group, facilitating comprehensive and systematic comparisons across peer systems. Specifically, the 70B Llama3 checker is subsequently employed to verify outputs generated by 8B or 13B Llama3 solvers, and the metric is averaged across all such within-group solvers, omitting the self-verification scenario. Lastly, we outline an instance of the cross-family verification method, wherein the checker systematically examines the responses from models across various groups, allowing for comprehensive, objective, and replicable assessments of their accuracy and consistency. For instance, a fundamental LLaMA3 verifier could be employed to evaluate outputs generated by models such as Qwen3 or an enhanced version of LLaMA3, with the metric averaged over all such cross-group solvers.

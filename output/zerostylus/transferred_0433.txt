**Refined Paragraph:** In this study, we introduce verifier gain, a novel metric designed to assess the utility of verifiers, addressing the limitations of evaluating verifier accuracy alone. Verifier gain quantifies the improvement observed when incorporating a verifier during the testing process, thus providing a more comprehensive understanding of verifier utility. Our investigation into the role of verifiers in rejecting incorrect responses reveals that this concept aligns closely with empirical occurrences. To evaluate the impact of various verification methods, we conducted a comparative analysis of self-verification, intra-group verification, and inter-group verification. Notably, employing verifiers from different groups generally proves most effective, particularly when compared to self-verification. This effectiveness arises because when the verifier and solver exhibit excessive similarity, the utility of the verifier at test time is significantly diminished. To further explore this phenomenon, we examine how improvements in model performance—whether through increased scale, extended training, or enhanced accuracy—lead to diminished self-verification capabilities while simultaneously enhancing inter-group verification. In our analysis of task verification, we considered the relative ease with which various tasks can be verified. While tasks that are straightforward to solve are generally straightforward to verify, self-verification does not universally enhance task verification. Although tasks involving math or logic tend to yield superior verifier outcomes, some tasks are inherently more conducive to verification. Verifiers can function in two distinct manners: they either ascertain the correctness of the final answer or evaluate the accuracy of the procedural steps leading to it. Researchers have developed methodologies for models to autonomously verify their responses by extrapolating components of the inquiry through the solution, facilitating comprehensive, scalable, and reproducible assessments. This introduces a strategy wherein models improve reliability by posing fact-checking questions to themselves, thereby reducing the likelihood of errors. Other researchers have trained verifiers to either validate final answers or collaborate with the solver. While the verification of certain procedural steps has been undertaken by some, the inherent complexity of these tasks may vary.

Initializing a character in a randomly sampled state from a motion dataset is an effective approach for advancing exploration in motion imitation techniques. However, this method requires access to the dataset for sampling initial states during training, as outlined in the methodology. To eliminate dependence on the original dataset, SMP employs a generative model to synthesize initial states for the training process. Incorporating SMP, the initialization of starting states is derived from a pre-trained diffusion model, which serves as a motion-guiding framework. Therefore, when utilized as part of the training framework, SMP functions both as an intrinsic reward mechanism and an effective means to initiate new training processes. Moreover, once SMP is trained, the generative state method renders the original dataset unnecessary, as diffusion models generate diverse and effective initial states. In addition to serving as a flexible motion guide, we employ a trained diffusion model in this work to facilitate learning imitation. Within a learning environment, the motion model ensures the agent's movements appear natural, facilitating seamless integration within the framework. Furthermore, when developing control policies for various tasks, a singular evaluation can lead these policies to replicate movements resembling those in the original dataset. These choices are elucidated in the subsequent learning setup. In addition to facilitating policy training, constructing an effective motion representation is essential for enabling the diffusion model to comprehend the dataset and utilize it as guidance. The representation must encapsulate sufficient data to reconstruct motion and should be readily derivable from motion sequences and simulator observations.

The prior-conforming prompt reinforces a belief aligned with the model's likely initial stance, serving as both a baseline for belief retention and a measure of accuracy. In contrast, the critical-thinking prompt fosters openness to counter-evidence and facilitates rational belief revision. Specifically, we establish a relationship between belief entrenchment, as quantified by the Martingale Score, and reductions in ground-truth accuracy, thereby corroborating the detrimental impact of entrenchment on reasoning performance. Furthermore, we evaluate a range of closed- and open-weight models across diverse problem domains and configurations, causally attributing belief entrenchment to factors such as model type, prompt design, and underlying reasoning methodologies. Additionally, we validate the reliability of large language models (LLMs) as evaluative arbiters by assessing whether their judgments align with established benchmarks and reasoning frameworks. Notably, our analysis indicates that belief entrenchment is more pronounced in value-laden domains, such as r/ChangeMyView, compared to more objective settings like Forecasting. For instance, under identical experimental conditions, the Martingale Score consistently registers higher in r/ChangeMyView than in Forecasting, suggesting that belief entrenchment poses a greater challenge in contexts where model performance cannot be directly evaluated against ground truth. To address this, we sought to modulate the degree of belief entrenchment through the application of critical-thinking and prior-conforming prompts. In Forecasting, the critical-thinking prompt effectively mitigated belief entrenchment by encouraging receptiveness to counter-evidence, whereas this effect was not observed in r/ChangeMyView or OpenReview. Additional analysis of the factors influencing belief entrenchment is provided in Appendix B. Importantly, our findings emphasize that belief entrenchment undermines accuracy, as the primary objective of reasoning in LLMs is to achieve true beliefs. We demonstrate this impact within domains where ground-truth labels are available, such as Forecasting and OpenReview. Although outcome-based metrics, such as the Brier Score, serve as indicators of model performance, they fail to elucidate the reasoning processes underlying such outcomes. Consequently, we incorporated process-based reasoning metrics to address these limitations [Mondorf and Plank, 2024b]. Our analysis reveals a positive correlation between the Martingale Score and Brier Score in Forecasting, further substantiating the conclusion that belief entrenchment compromises LLM reasoning efficacy.

We identify a significant correlation between elevated Martingale Scores, which indicate heightened belief entrenchment, and poorer Brier Scores in forecasting tasks. This observation suggests that belief entrenchment contributes to the occasional lapses in reasoning exhibited by large language models. Therefore, our findings imply that addressing belief entrenchment could enhance the accuracy of these models' predictions. Moreover, the results underscore the necessity for further investigation into the mechanisms of belief entrenchment within the context of LLM reasoning. Future research may benefit from exploring the efficacy of the Martingale Score in open-ended domains by examining additional consequences of belief entrenchment. Additionally, expanding the application of the Martingale Score to evaluate other cognitive phenomena such as sycophancy, group conformity, and inverse scaling could prove beneficial. Such extensions could validate the Martingale Score as a versatile tool for assessing reasoning processes. Furthermore, converting the Martingale Score into a training objective might serve as a robust test of its applicability across diverse domains. If successful, this approach would provide substantial evidence for the score's utility beyond forecasting tasks. Finally, exploring the application of the Martingale Score in measuring and mitigating belief entrenchment or polarization within human-AI systems, including recommender systems, represents another promising avenue for future research.

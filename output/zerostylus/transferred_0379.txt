Recommending matches in a text-rich, dynamic marketplace presents significant challenges, primarily due to the evolving nature of both content and interaction patterns. To address these complexities, we introduce GraphMatch, a novel large-scale recommendation framework that effectively integrates pre-trained language models with graph neural networks. Unlike previous methods that rely on separate models, GraphMatch constitutes a comprehensive system that synergizes text encoders with graph networks. It employs advanced techniques such as adversarial negative sampling and the analysis of temporal graph snapshots to capture both the nuanced semantics of the text and the dynamic structure of the graph. Our evaluation on data from Upwork, a prominent online job marketplace, demonstrates the framework's efficiency and suitability for real-time applications. Notably, GraphMatch surpasses models that are solely language-based or graph-based in matching tasks, while also maintaining computational efficiency during execution. These findings underscore the effectiveness of integrating language and graph data, providing a robust solution for recommendations in environments characterized by abundant textual content and evolving connections. Moreover, our work identifies systems such as e-commerce platforms, employment networks, academic forums, and news aggregators as examples of Temporal Text-Attributed Graphs (TTAGs), which are distinguished by their dynamic and text-rich nature. In TTAGs, nodes are associated with evolving textual features, specifically detailed text descriptions, while edges signify interactions such as purchases or replies. The integration of fine-grained textual semantics with the evolving topology of interaction graphs is crucial for generating effective matches or recommendations, yet remains a challenging problem. Recent advancements in language representation models, including SentenceBERT and E5, have demonstrated proficiency in generating document embeddings and facilitating semantic content comparisons. However, their application to TTAGs reveals limitations in capturing critical aspects. Specifically, GraphMatch's ability to link two items relies on the surrounding connections within the graph, a factor often neglected by traditional text models. In TTAGs, the significance of a node and the rationale for its connections can change rapidly, as evidenced by recent shifts from emphasizing editing skills to prioritizing expertise in prompt engineering in "AI Writing" job postings.

The ongoing global events consistently impact the synchronization between freelance nodes and client nodes within the international job market. However, methods that consider text as static features have been recognized to pose difficulties, such as inadvertently revealing future information that should remain inaccessible. Consequently, it is crucial to adopt strategies that mitigate these challenges to preserve the integrity and confidentiality of the data involved.

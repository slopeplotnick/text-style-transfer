Self-Consistency (SC) (Wang et al., 2023b) aggregates multiple outputs through majority voting to determine the most frequent outcome. Similarly, Soft Self-Consistency (Soft-SC) selects the minimum, mean, or product of confidence scores within each category, thereby refining the aggregation process. Generative Self-Aggregation (GSA), in contrast, prompts the language model to synthesize a novel response informed by the context of multiple sampled outputs (Li et al., 2025). Universal Self-Consistency (USC) leverages the language model (LM) to select the most consistent answer among several candidates, ensuring alignment with the underlying principles of self-aggregation (Chen et al., 2023). To evaluate these reasoning-oriented approaches, we focus on three specific language models: gemini-2.5-flash (Comanici et al., 2025), qwen3 next-80b (Qwen Team, 2025), and gpt-oss-120b (OpenAI, 2025), which exemplify advancements in thinking LLMs. Our assessment framework incorporates two machine translation tasks (Song et al., 2025) and six tasks from the Reward Bench 2 benchmark (Malik et al., 2025). Reward Bench 2 systematically evaluates reward models across six distinct domains: factual accuracy, precise instruction adherence, mathematical reasoning, safety considerations, attentional focus, and the resolution of ties. Specifically, preference pairs are constructed by systematically pairing all possible combinations from each task's source dataset, which contains both accepted and rejected responses. These pairs are then categorized into 'non-tie' pairs, where one answer is accepted while the other is rejected, and 'tie' pairs, where both answers are either accepted or rejected. From this dataset, 1000 examples are sampled for each of the six tasks to establish the final evaluation benchmark. A detailed breakdown of vote distributions relevant to each task is provided in Appendix B, elucidating the ground truth patterns that inform the evaluation process. The results indicate consistent efficacy across different reasoning-oriented language models, as evidenced by the robustness of our approach in gemini-2.5-flash, gpt-oss-120b, and qwen3 next-80b (Tables 6 and 7). Notably, the observed uniformity in performance suggests shared underlying mechanisms among these models, although these mechanisms remain unexplored. Furthermore, the analysis of problem structure highlights critical factors such as the frequency of ambiguous cases induced by the task, the tendency of the language models to produce directional versus tied votes, and the interaction of these characteristics with the MAE-aligned Davidson model. While some tasks exhibit smooth calibration landscapes conducive to broader applicability, others display sharper, more rigid landscapes that are less transferable across contexts. Therefore, designing robust testing methodologies to predict task transferability and assess resilience under significant distribution shifts or novel target scenarios represents a promising avenue for future research.

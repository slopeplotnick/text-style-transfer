The RAIR benchmark encompasses a total of 63,601 samples, drawn from 14 real-world e-commerce industries. Specifically, it comprises three distinct subsets established during our construction process: the General subset, containing 46,775 samples; the Long-Tail Hard subset, consisting of 10,931 samples; and the Visual Salience subset, including 5,895 samples. For each entry in the dataset, we provide comprehensive information, including the query, item title, item details, SKU information, and an anonymized item image. Moreover, each entry is accompanied by its manual ground truth annotation according to our four-level relevance scale, ranging from L1 to L4. The comprehensive distribution of these labels across the dataset is documented in Table 8. Additionally, to facilitate subsequent model development and detailed error attribution, each data instance is augmented with the corresponding rule identifiers derived from our Rule Checklist, which serve as the basis for its relevance judgment. In this section, we conduct a systematic validation and analysis of the proposed methods on the RAIR benchmark. Our experiments are divided into two parts: first, through quantitative metrics on benchmarks, we address two core questions: namely, the magnitude of performance improvement brought to the model by fine-tuning; and the performance advantages demonstrated by the proposed LORE model across different data distribution subsets, including general samples, hard samples, and visually salient samples. Subsequently, case studies are employed to illustrate the new capabilities that our methods confer upon the model and the types of problems they enable it to solve. To ensure the stability and reproducibility of evaluation results, all trained models employ a greedy decoding strategy for single-pass inference, thereby eliminating sampling randomness. A range of metrics was employed to evaluate the model's performance.

We present a novel benchmark, HEALTHCONTRADICT, designed to evaluate the reasoning capabilities of language models when faced with complex biomedical contexts characterized by conflicting information. This benchmark employs interpretable quantitative metrics to assess model performance in processing extensive and contradictory medical data. Compared to existing state-of-the-art medical question-answering benchmarks, HEALTHCONTRADICT provides a more nuanced evaluation by effectively capturing differences in model performance. Specifically, our evaluation demonstrates that language models adapted to biomedical domains exhibit enhanced abilities to (i) accurately leverage relevant information, (ii) disregard incorrect or irrelevant information, and (iii) resolve conflicting evidence in medical contexts. To the best of our knowledge, the TREC Health Misinformation tracks remain the only publicly available resource offering (1) expert-curated health questions paired with ground-truth answers supported by scientific evidence and (2) document pools, evaluated by experts, containing both corroborating and contradicting information. These components are integral to constructing health-related questions with definitive answers, as they enable the creation of contradictory document pairs, which are essential for assessing the ability of language models to resolve biomedical knowledge conflicts. Within the HEALTHCONTRADICT benchmark, we focus on credible and relevant documents that effectively contribute to supporting or refuting the answer to a given query. To ensure consistency across datasets, we standardized labeling by mapping the 2019 categories "effective"/"ineffective" and the 2021 categories "supportive"/"dissuades" to the "yes"/"no" format, aligning them with the labeling conventions of 2022. Furthermore, we define contradictory document pairs as cases where two documents addressing the same question provide opposing stances—one affirming a "yes" response and the other asserting a "no" response—thereby exemplifying conflicting perspectives. A document is deemed correct if its stance aligns with established scientific evidence and incorrect if it contradicts such evidence. The original dataset comprises 130 expert-annotated questions, distributed across 50 questions in 2019, 35 in 2021, and 45 in 2022. To qualify for inclusion, questions must have binary stance annotations (i.e., "yes"/"no") and be accompanied by documents that provide both supportive and refuting evidence. Applying this inclusion criterion reduces the dataset to 110 questions. To optimize the evaluation process, we paired supporting and refuting documents into contradiction pairs, ensuring that each document is utilized only once, even if associated with multiple questions. However, questions that could not generate at least one contradiction pair, due to insufficient unique documents, were excluded. As a result, the final dataset consists of 81 questions and 920 carefully curated pairs of contradictory documents.

In conclusion, we present a refined dataset comprising 81 questions and 920 pairs of contradictory documents, which serves as a valuable resource for evaluating language models' ability to reason over conflicting biomedical contexts.

The parsing system extracts structured allocation decisions from LLM markdown responses using regex pattern matching. This approach systematically identifies specific formatted sections within the LLM's markdown output—such as allocation decisions, success percentages, and reasoning explanations—and subsequently converts them into structured data for validation purposes. Moreover, a comprehensive validation framework applies eight foundational principles, including the elimination of invalid allocations, verification of trade-off analyses, assessment of workload awareness, and evaluation of logical consistency across all components. However, the transition to a weighted validation system was necessitated by the observed limitations of traditional rule-based frameworks, which were insufficient for capturing the nuanced reasoning capabilities and contextual awareness required for LLM-driven task allocation. Recent research on LLM evaluation (Guo et al. 2023, *Evaluation and Benchmarking of LLM Agents, 2025*) has underscored the importance of multi-dimensional, criterion-based assessments as a more reliable metric for evaluating model reasoning performance compared to binary correctness checks. Therefore, building on these insights, the proposed validation system adopts a weighted scoring approach to evaluate reasoning outputs across multiple dimensions, such as explanation clarity, success-rate consistency, workload awareness, trade-off justification, phased compliance, and confidence alignment, thereby addressing both the technical soundness and interpretive quality of the model’s decisions. Each dimension contributes proportionally to a composite quality score, with greater emphasis placed on factors directly influencing allocation outcomes—such as reasoning depth and success-rate accuracy—and comparatively lower weights assigned to supporting aspects like confidence justification. Consequently, this configuration ensures that the validation process remains attuned to performance metrics while preserving contextual awareness, effectively balancing interpretability and operational rigor in assessing the model's reasoning behavior. Specifically, the weighted dimensions include: (1) Explanation Quality (Weight: 0.20), which evaluates the depth and clarity of reasoning; (2) Success Rate Accuracy (Weight: 0.20), which measures alignment between predicted outcomes and actual success probabilities; (3) Trade-off Analysis (Weight: 0.15), which emphasizes the systematic reconciliation of competing objectives; and (4) Workload Awareness (Weight: 0.15), which assesses considerations for current task distribution.

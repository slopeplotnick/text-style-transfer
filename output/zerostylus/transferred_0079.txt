The authors of a foundational work in Empirical Software Engineering discuss the contributions of Victor R. Basili, a prominent figure and influential pioneer in this field. Their work underscores the critical importance of utilizing empirical studies, such as ours, particularly emphasizing the use of authentic data and evidence to enhance software engineering practices. Numerous studies have explored the implementation of Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE) within industrial contexts and its impact on current agile development methodologies. These studies have proposed several insightful approaches to implementation and strategies to adopt, providing valuable insights into how to proceed with the transition process. However, no prior research has conducted an empirical assessment akin to ours, which is uniquely situated in the period following adoption and preceding implementation. This study presents the initial phase of a comprehensive initiative aimed at transitioning an existing codebase into a Software Product Line (SPL) to facilitate systematic reuse and variability management. We structured the interviews to align with the company's needs, particularly considering the diverse roles of individuals engaged in the project. The objective of this study was to identify optimal strategies for preparing for the transition, with a focus on addressing questions aimed at uncovering current effective practices, perceived advantages and risks, and prevailing concerns. Consequently, the insights provided offer valuable strategies to address risks and concerns, leverage advantages, and promote effective practices for a seamless transition. It is noteworthy that the company already provides beneficial practices and embraces an experimental culture, which is expected to positively influence the project's progress. Moreover, the prevailing sentiment among the majority of participants is that the modification is anticipated to yield advantageous outcomes. Nonetheless, some concerns or complexities remain prevalent in managing and sustaining SPL initiatives.

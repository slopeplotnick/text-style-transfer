ViSAudio demonstrates superior performance across a range of objective metrics in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution datasets, as evidenced in Table 2. It is compared to several state-of-the-art video-to-spatial-audio generation techniques, with a comprehensive description of these methods available in Section 5.1. Specifically, for the BiAudio and MUSIC test sets, both video and caption inputs are utilized. However, for the FAIR-Play dataset, which lacks captions, only video data is employed. Models that do not process text are consequently evaluated with video inputs alone. The results presented in Table 2 highlight that ViSAudio surpasses other binaural-audio generation methods in terms of overall quality, spatial channel distribution, audio-visual synchrony, and semantic alignment. This capability underscores the model's proficiency in generating high-fidelity binaural audio while maintaining coherence between audio and visual elements across diverse acoustic environments. Notably, despite the absence of specific training, ViSAudio exhibits strong performance on the out-of-distribution FAIR-Play dataset, demonstrating its robust generalization capabilities across various sound environments. Furthermore, a comparative analysis of video-conditioned binaural spatial audio generation is illustrated in Figure 3. An example features a person playing the sitar, with the camera moving from left to right, resulting in the sound source shifting from right to left in the video. The spectrograms of the generated binaural audio from ViSAudio, alongside the ground truth and other baselines, are visualized. Our method produces audio that aligns most closely with the ground truth, effectively reflecting spatial changes in the sound source. The boxed regions in the spectrograms indicate that when the sound source is located on the left, the left-channel spectrogram exhibits higher energy, mirroring the increased loudness perceived by the left ear and matching the ground-truth spatial audio. This demonstrates the model's adeptness at adapting to changes in viewpoint. In contrast, other methods generate incorrect rhythms and fail to accurately reflect left-right channel differences.

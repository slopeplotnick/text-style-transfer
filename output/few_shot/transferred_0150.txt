The most advanced language models, such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o, exhibit error rates comparable to or surpassing human performance, as illustrated on the left side of Figure 8. With the exception of Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, Figure 8 indicates that models with higher accuracy tend to exhibit Bayesian-like behavior. However, this correlation between accuracy and Bayesian behavior is not absolute. For example, a model achieving perfect prediction accuracy might resemble a simple model without displaying probabilistic behavior. The contextual modulation, induced by varying prior observations, significantly influences the behavior of large language models (LLMs). As depicted in the top right of Figure 6, their sequential behavioral consistency declines, particularly when exposed to biased data ranges, evident from the left side of Figure 6 where error rates increase (triangles tend to be positioned above circles). This suggests a dependency of LLMs on provided information.  Moreover, as indicated in the middle of Figure 8, a high performance in terms of normalized root mean square error (NRMSE) does not necessarily equate to effective cue integration. For instance, despite GPT-5 Mini's strong NRMSE performance, it demonstrates inefficiency in cue integration, particularly in the maze distance task, where it excels with textual information but performs suboptimally with visual inputs. This suggests a limited capacity to de-emphasize image inputs adequately (see Appendix A.12 for further details). Conversely, in the line length ratio task (refer to the bottom right of Figure 6), many LLMs successfully diminish the influence of visual inputs in the presence of noise, indicating adaptability consistent with Bayesian principles.

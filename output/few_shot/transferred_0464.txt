Understanding real-world scientific laws is crucial for accurately predicting physical phenomena in various contexts. However, current video evaluation methodologies predominantly rely on basic physical knowledge, which inadequately reflects the scientific reasoning capabilities of video models. To address this gap, we introduce VideoScience-Bench, an assessment tool designed to evaluate video models' comprehension of science at a collegiate level. Each question within the test necessitates an understanding and synthesis of scientific concepts to arrive at the correct answer. The assessment comprises 200 questions spanning 14 topics and 103 concepts within physics and chemistry. We conducted expert evaluations of seven leading video models in Text-to-Video (T2V) and Image-to-Video (I2V) settings, focusing on four dimensions: invariance, correct transformation, temporal and spatial consistency, and alignment with expected outcomes. Employing a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-as-a-Judge methodology, our evaluation demonstrates high concordance with human judgments. To our knowledge, VideoScience-Bench represents the inaugural attempt to assess whether video models can not only generate videos but also reason scientifically, ensuring the accurate depiction of physical and chemical phenomena. Our dataset and evaluation code are accessible at github.com/hao-ai-lab/VideoScience. The advent of models such as Sora-2 and Veo-3.1 marks a significant evolution in video generation, where video models not only create cinematic-quality videos but also exhibit novel capacities for world comprehension. As video models advance, the criteria for 'good' videos are evolving beyond traditional quality metrics, such as Fréchet Video Distance and Fréchet Video Motion Distance, to include adherence to fundamental physical principles and world understanding. Emerging models demonstrate autonomous reasoning abilities, capable of addressing challenges that necessitate scientific, mathematical, and spatial cognition. Scientifically trained individuals interpret the world through scientific principles, making predictions about phenomena, such as the effect of citric acid on iron stains or the implications of powering an LED with or without a resistor. This leads us to the central inquiry: beyond aesthetic quality and adherence to fundamental physical laws, can video models accurately depict scientific events and engage in scientific reasoning about the real world? Scientific understanding based on established principles is vital for innovation, as it equips models to anticipate outcomes in real-world scenarios. Unlike rudimentary world comprehension, genuine scientific understanding requires integrating numerous interconnected concepts, such as employing specific-heat and heat-transfer theories to elucidate heat absorption by water.

As the utilization of AI tools incorporating large language models (LLMs) increases, ensuring their safety becomes paramount. This is particularly critical when deploying these models across diverse linguistic and communicative contexts, which can introduce novel risks. It is imperative to develop systems capable of filtering inappropriate or unsafe content across a multitude of languages and scenarios. In the absence of such systems, the global deployment of these models may inadvertently harm or marginalize numerous individuals. The majority of research thus far has focused on safety in widely spoken languages such as English, Chinese, Spanish, German, and French. However, the paucity of data for the majority of languages complicates efforts to assess and maintain safety in multilingual contexts. Although some evaluations assess model performance across different languages, they often exclude low-resource languages and fail to reflect the diversity of the user base. Furthermore, the generation of training data to enhance safety systems for low-resource languages remains largely unexplored. Recent endeavors in this area have produced training data using both authentic and translated content for high-resource languages, yet this is insufficient for the development of truly secure systems. Another significant challenge is that existing safety systems demand substantial computational resources. They are typically cumbersome, slow, and resource-intensive, rendering them impractical for deployment on small devices or in energy-constrained environments. Their protracted response times present difficulties for real-world application. This underscores the necessity for more compact, efficient systems capable of safeguarding both the language model and the user from potential harm.

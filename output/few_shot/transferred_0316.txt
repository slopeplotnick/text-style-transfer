We have devised a system aimed at enhancing the performance of multilingual language models through prompt optimization. Our empirical findings indicate that certain prompt elements enhance model performance across various languages, with multilingual reward systems aiding in the identification of effective prompts. Analysis of model reasoning reveals that optimized prompts foster more structured and consistent reasoning, underscoring the efficacy of prompt optimization in improving multilingual model performance. The identification of effective prompts remains a challenge, despite the promising prompts discovered using SPRIG. Our experiments suggest that well-crafted prompts steer models towards improved reasoning patterns; however, the underlying mechanisms remain elusive and warrant further investigation. We have endeavored to mitigate ethical concerns in this research, yet certain issues persist. Notably, the process of prompt optimization and experimentation demands substantial computational resources, resulting in significant energy consumption and carbon emissions. Additionally, prompt optimization may inadvertently exacerbate biases, such as reinforcing stereotypes through role-based prompts, necessitating caution to prevent the introduction or amplification of harmful biases.

In this study, we investigate the identification of patterns indicative of operational inefficiencies or regressions. We employ a tool known as GRAPHECTORY to facilitate the detection of these patterns. Our analysis concentrates on several key indicators of potential dysfunction: Hypothesis H1 describes a scenario where repetitive actions fail to resolve the issue, necessitating alternative strategies. Hypothesis H2 involves erratic progression within the project rather than steady advancement. Hypothesis H3 pertains to prolonged engagement with a task without observable progress. Hypothesis H4 indicates suboptimal tool utilization when components do not function correctly.  The construct RepeatedView is characterized by frequent revisitations to the same segment of a project, such as repeatedly accessing a particular file, suggesting ongoing troubleshooting or unsuccessful attempts at resolution. For example, after two unsuccessful editing attempts (step 9), the file client.py is reopened (step 10) to continue diagnosing the issue. Conversely, the pattern ZoomOut is identified when the sequence of actions deviates from the hierarchical project structure, indicative of navigating from a more specific to a broader scope. This suggests a retracing of steps due to recognition of a misstep. An instance of this is observed when, after entering an incorrect directory, postgres (step 3), navigation is redirected to the main directory /testbed/db (step 4), before accessing the correct directory /testbed/db/postgreSQL (step 5) to locate the problematic file client.py (step 6).

Large Language Models (LLMs) have significantly transformed predictive modeling related to events. Unlike traditional methodologies, LLMs can extract comprehensive details regarding events—such as the type of event, the participants involved, and the timing—directly from unstructured text. This capability ensures the preservation of information accuracy, facilitating causal inference and coherent reasoning. However, current research has not yet fully capitalized on the capabilities of LLMs. For instance, techniques such as CAMEF [25] utilize LLMs to extract event details but subsequently condense this information into compact data representations for processing by conventional neural networks, thereby relegating LLMs to the role of sophisticated feature extractors. This approach results in a significant loss of event-specific details, fails to clearly delineate connections or temporal transitions between events, and lacks the construction of a reusable or traceable memory system. Our methodology diverges from this paradigm by employing detailed event information as foundational elements to construct a structured event memory. Through systematic tracking, integration, and organization of events, our approach facilitates a direct understanding of temporal event transformations and the comparison of patterns across diverse contexts. This addresses the limitations of existing LLM methodologies concerning the linkage of event sequences and the identification of recurring patterns over time. This is the principal contribution of our work. We have developed a system for event analysis aimed at predicting stock prices. This system constructs a knowledge base of events derived from news sources and enhances it with model-driven feedback, enabling the identification and utilization of analogous past event patterns.

We developed a Python script within a Jupyter Notebook to analyze word frequency and assess lexical similarity. The process involved sorting the words from the book based on their frequency of occurrence, followed by manually selecting significant terms. Our data and findings are accessible online at https://cite.lirmm.fr/tgeorges/interviewartefact, which hosts the interview questions and responses, analysis, topic lists, the notebook, and several bar charts depicting response distributions. This study examines the outcomes in relation to each research question, including current best practices, benefits, risks, and apprehensions concerning the future of Software Product Lines (SPL). Table 3 enumerates the identified best practices, benefits, risks, and considerations for further exploration. Results are categorized by topics and roles, adhering to the design process methodology. Participant responses are evenly distributed. When analyzing responses by years of professional experience, both the least and most experienced individuals contributed a similar number of responses. For instance, when asked, "Which good practices can be derived from this approach?", developers with four and seven years of experience provided identical responses. Bar charts depicting responses based on years of experience are available in the aforementioned online repository. This segment of the interviews aimed to identify prevailing good practices crucial for retention in the new approach. The most frequently cited practice is Factorization and Reuse, corroborated by team discussions wherein each new feature is evaluated for its applicability across multiple applications or its specificity to a singular application. Innovation emerged as a pivotal concept, driving the team to maintain contemporary skills. This emphasis on innovation is significant to participants, enabling regular updates to the codebase.

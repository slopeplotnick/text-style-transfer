ViSAudio demonstrates superior performance in generating spatial audio from videos compared to existing methods, as evidenced by evaluations conducted across various datasets, detailed in Table 2. A comprehensive comparison with leading approaches is provided in Section 5.1. For the BiAudio and MUSIC test datasets, both video and captions were utilized, whereas for the FAIR-Play dataset—lacking captions—only video data was employed. Models incapable of processing text were likewise evaluated using video alone. The findings presented in Table 2 indicate that ViSAudio consistently outperforms other methods, particularly in terms of audio quality, spatial distribution of sound across channels, audio-video synchronization, and preservation of scene semantics. These results suggest that the model effectively generates realistic binaural audio while ensuring temporal coherence between sound and visuals, even across diverse acoustic environments. Notably, ViSAudio exhibits robust performance on the FAIR-Play dataset, which was unseen during training, indicating its generalization capability to novel scenarios. Figure 3 illustrates comparative analyses of spatial audio generation by different methods. One specific example involves a sitar performance with camera movement from left to right, resulting in a corresponding auditory shift from right to left. The spectrogram analyses of audio outputs from ViSAudio, the authentic audio, and alternative methods reveal that ViSAudio's output most closely aligns with the true audio, accurately reflecting the directional sound movement. For instance, when the sound is oriented leftward, the left channel exhibits greater amplitude, mirroring real-world auditory perceptions. In contrast, competing methods failed to accurately capture rhythm or channel differentiation.

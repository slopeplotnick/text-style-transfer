Large language models demonstrate proficiency in clear reasoning; however, there remains a paucity of understanding regarding their capacity to process tasks in the absence of explicit instructions. Extensive research over the years indicates that humans adeptly manage ambiguous signals to interpret their surroundings. This study investigates whether these models emulate such human behavior, effectively integrating various information types without explicit training. Employing methodologies derived from psychology, we assess the cognitive processes of these models by examining their behavior. We have developed an evaluation framework, BayesBench, featuring four tasks that assess attributes such as size and time, utilizing both linguistic and visual stimuli, grounded in traditional psychological research. This framework allows us to benchmark nine models against human judgments to establish a reference standard.  By modulating the levels of noise, context, and instructions, we evaluate their performance and capability to integrate diverse information types. Beyond measuring accuracy and efficiency, we introduce a metric that evaluates alignment with intelligent behavior, even when high performance is achieved. Our findings reveal that, although some models adapt proficiently, high accuracy does not necessarily correlate with reliability. For instance, while GPT-5 Mini excels in textual analysis, its performance in visual tasks is suboptimal, highlighting a discrepancy between capability and implementation. This suggests that evaluation metrics focused solely on accuracy may overlook how models navigate uncertainty. These findings elucidate models' handling of uncertainty and the relationship between accuracy and intelligent behavior. We provide our evaluation framework and metric as tools to aid in the development of more robust systems capable of synthesizing multiple information types.  The ability to assess parameters such as size, time, or distance is fundamental to both human and machine perception. Humans achieve this by astutely integrating ambiguous signals, prioritizing reliable information, and employing prior expectations to minimize errors. This cognitive strategy is pervasive across cultures and age groups, underscoring its foundational role in processing uncertain information.

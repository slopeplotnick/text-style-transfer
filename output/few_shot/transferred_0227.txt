Recent advancements have introduced new datasets such as Mozilla's Common Voice, which provides recorded speech in six South African languages. This dataset, contributed by the public and distributed under an open license, permits unrestricted use (Mozilla Foundation, 2023). FLEURS offers approximately 12 hours of recordings for four South African languages, primarily for testing and evaluation (Smith et al., 2023). Vuk'uzenzele (VixSD), a smaller dataset created by the community, emphasizes community ownership and ethical usage through its licensing agreement (Nguni et al., 2023). Despite its limited size, VixSD exemplifies the capacity of local communities to produce linguistic resources. AfriSpeech-200 contains English recordings with South African accents, focusing on healthcare topics, which aids in developing speech recognition systems that accommodate accent variations (Dlamini et al., 2023). However, there remains a dearth of datasets featuring natural, unscripted speech across diverse topics, signaling a need for more comprehensive datasets such as Swivuriso. These datasets aim to integrate both scripted and unscripted speech, cover a broader array of topics, and encompass a wide variety of speakers.  Collecting speech data in underrepresented communities necessitates careful attention to consent, privacy, cultural integrity, and the long-term impacts on these communities. Previous initiatives, such as Common Voice and ALFFA, underscore the importance of embedding ethical and participatory practices throughout the data collection process. A study of the Catalan version of Common Voice highlighted the crucial role of clear consent protocols, privacy safeguards, and inclusive participation (García et al., 2024). The ALFFA project engaged extensively with local experts and linguists to involve the community during data collection (Abena et al., 2024). Swivuriso builds upon these principles by prioritizing ethical practices and inclusive participation. Local coordinators managed the recording process, supported participants, and ensured adequate community representation. Ethical guidelines approved by institutional review boards were adhered to, including the removal of personal identifiers, data anonymization, and the implementation of safeguards against misuse, such as voice cloning or biometric tracking.  Ensuring datasets are accessible and utilizable is essential, especially for low-resource languages. Open licenses, such as Creative Commons, facilitate this access by allowing unrestricted data usage, an important factor when data collection proves challenging or costly (Open Knowledge Foundation, 2023). Projects like Common Voice illustrate how open licensing can expedite the sharing of resources and advance the development of language technologies for underserved languages.

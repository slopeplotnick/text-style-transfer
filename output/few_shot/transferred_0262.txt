To effectively manage these systems, it is imperative to transition safety protocols from merely adhering to predefined rules to a more holistic monitoring of the entire system. This encompasses evaluating the diffusion of influence among agents, the impact of oversight signals on collective behavior, and the point at which feedback loops become uncontrollable. The underlying premise is that fundamental safety measures, while necessary, are insufficient when models operate within interactive networks. Even if conditions are optimal locally, failures cannot be averted when feedback mechanisms, interconnections, and group incentives dominate. Recently, two seminal studies examined the risks associated with multi-agent systems, proposing valuable organizational frameworks: Multi-Agent System failure Taxonomy (MAST) and Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AI (MARAI). MAST categorizes failures into three primary categories, emphasizing risks stemming from system design, agent coordination, and verification processes. Conversely, MARAI identifies three types of failures based on agent interactions—miscoordination, conflict, and collusion—which may result in misaligned behaviors. Our novel proposition involves: 1) integrating both methodologies into a cohesive framework with a systemic focus, shifting from agent-level failures to risks not attributable to individual components; 2) formalizing this integration through the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH), which delineates the juncture where local reliability transitions into group instability. The ESRH provides terminology and indicators conducive to analyzing LLM ecosystems as complex systems rather than isolated agents. This sets the stage for the remainder of the paper. Section 2 discusses the evolution towards multi-agent systems and the regulatory implications of distributed causality. Section 3 elucidates the ESRH taxonomy and corresponding metrics. Section 4 proposes the Institutional AI architecture as a conceptual framework for embedding governance within interacting model groups.

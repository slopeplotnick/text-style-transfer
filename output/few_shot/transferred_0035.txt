This method is designed to simplify the Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) objective, as outlined in Equation (4), by identifying the action with the highest visitation frequency or density, thereby reinforcing the validity of our approach. Our test-time scaling strategy seeks the most frequently occurring action and is not merely conjectural; rather, it represents a robust and theoretically sound approach to achieving the anti-exploration objective in Offline RL. Empirical successes, such as those reported by Lobel et al. (2023) and Bai et al. (2025), demonstrate that employing pseudo-counts for Large Language Model (LLM) preference optimization is effective. We leverage this concept by integrating a Coin Flipping Network (CFN). As depicted in Figure 3, the pseudo-counts generated by our estimator align closely with the L2 distance to the actual action as well as with the pseudo-count from our CFN. This alignment evidences that our CFN can effectively validate and penalize unsupported actions. Our experimental investigations are designed to ascertain whether utilizing Test-time Anti-exploration with pseudo-count units can enhance action prediction accuracy and task success rates. Additionally, we seek to identify which design elements contribute to these improvements and evaluate the acceptability of the additional computational cost. Specifically, our experiments address the following research questions: Q1: Can our method enhance performance across varied environments, VLC policies, and tasks? Q2: What is the additional computational time cost associated with our test-time scaling method, and can it maintain performance benefits when deployed on real robots? Q3: What impact do design elements—such as the incorporation of internal features and the CFN pseudo-count network—have on overall performance?

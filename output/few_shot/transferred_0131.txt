Three primary research questions were investigated in this study. First, the performance of programming agents regarding process-related metrics was examined, focusing on their defining characteristics and the factors influencing these measurements. Second, an analysis of problem-solving behaviors was conducted to assess the typical approaches employed by these agents, the determinants of their behavior, their adherence to pre-planned methods, and the extent to which they adapt their strategies during task execution. Third, inefficiency patterns in the agents' behavior were scrutinized to identify prevalent issues or suboptimal practices, along with their frequency of occurrence. To address these questions, two widely-used programming agents, SWE-agent (SA) and OpenHands (OH), were analyzed under their standard configurations. Specifically, SWE-agent operates with a task cost limit of $2, while OpenHands executes tasks within a limit of 100 steps. Both agents utilize their respective tools for file viewing and code editing.  For testing, four language models were utilized: DeepSeek-V3 671B MoE (DSK-V3), DeepSeek-R1 (DSK-R1), Devstral-small-2505 24B (Dev), and Claude Sonnet 4 (CLD-4), encompassing a diverse range of general-purpose, reasoning-focused, advanced and open-source models.

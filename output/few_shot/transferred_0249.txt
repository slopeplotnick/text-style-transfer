The study focuses on the Autonomous Domain-Oriented Relevance Engine (ADORE), which comprises three primary components: a domain-specific relevance determination module, an error-type-based data generation module, and a knowledge enhancement module utilizing key attributes. The first module employs a reasoning model to autonomously generate challenging samples by analyzing products displayed online. The second module strengthens the system by producing diverse difficult samples, while the third module utilizes critical features from analyses to enhance knowledge, thereby refining the online model's reasoning and representation capabilities. Extensive experimental evaluations were conducted utilizing both substantial offline datasets and online testing environments. The findings indicate that ADORE significantly outperforms alternative methods, improving both offline and online metrics, thus demonstrating its efficacy in handling complex relevance tasks in the e-commerce domain. To enhance the online model further, a relevance determination model based on Large Language Models (LLMs) was developed. Consistent with prior research, employing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompts notably improves these models' reasoning capabilities. This concept was applied to fine-tune our model, beginning with the generation of CoT-labeled data using GPT-4o, akin to human data annotation practices, involving guidelines and examples. This approach yields a dataset with detailed reasoning comparable to human annotation. To ensure labeled data accuracy, only data verifiable against human labels was retained, discarding non-conforming samples to form the final training set. Subsequently, these samples were employed to fine-tune the LLaMA-3 model by optimizing a standard Language Model loss.

This study presents a methodology for developing an energy model that quantifies the energy consumption of statically typed programming languages operating on the Java Virtual Machine (JVM). The investigation examines the impact of factors such as primitive data types, data sizes, operations, and varying hardware on energy usage. Notably, it demonstrates that even devices with identical hardware configurations can exhibit disparate energy consumption patterns, complicating general energy usage prediction. The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 provides an overview of Bayesian statistics; Section 3 outlines the methodological framework employed; Section 4 elaborates on the implementation details; Section 5 discusses the evaluation process and potential limitations of the findings; Section 6 presents the empirical results; Section 7 offers an analysis of the results; Section 8 reviews related literature; and Section 9 concludes the study. Bayesian statistics is a methodological approach that incorporates prior knowledge to make probabilistic inferences about unobservable phenomena, akin to frequentist statistics, a more prevalent approach in computer science. However, Bayesian methods diverge in their model formulation and interpretation, as they explicitly incorporate assumptions, allowing for their scrutiny. Frequentist approaches, conversely, rely solely on observed data and endeavor to minimize assumptions. The study employs Bayesian statistics for two key reasons: firstly, it elucidates and explicates the assumptions underlying the model, and secondly, it facilitates detailed prediction of energy consumption by providing a probabilistic range of potential outcomes, unlike frequentist methods that typically yield a singular point estimate. In this analysis, it is assumed that energy consumption adheres to a normal distribution, consistent with previous research. This distribution serves as a prior, which is updated using a Bayesian algorithm to better align with empirical measurements. In scenarios with limited data, the initial priors may exert significant influence, potentially leading to inaccurate results. However, as additional data is incorporated, the algorithm refines initial assumptions, thereby enhancing the model's congruence with actual data.

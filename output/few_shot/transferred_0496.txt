OptPO-SFT employs the same update mechanism as TTSFT, with the number of iterations determined through a Bayesian rule, enhancing both efficiency and reliability. Both the reinforcement learning (RL)-based and supervised fine-tuning (SFT)-based variants of OptPO follow a consistent three-step framework: initiating the process, computing a label using a designated method, and updating the parameters. The primary distinction lies in the update methodology: the RL-based version utilizes policy-gradient optimization, whereas the SFT-based variant employs cross-entropy fine-tuning. Despite this difference, both variants operate under a unified framework.   The evaluation benchmarks consist of challenging mathematical and scientific problem sets, including MATH-500 (500 high school-level problems), AIME 2024 (30 pre-Olympiad-level problems), AMC (problems from the American Mathematics Competitions), and GPQA (graduate-level science questions). OptPO is assessed across widely adopted open-source models, such as Qwen2.5-Math1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, to evaluate its generalizability across diverse model architectures and scales. For further evaluation of TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, Qwen2.5-Math1.5B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct serve as primary testbeds.  OptPO demonstrates flexibility as a modular framework that can be integrated into existing RL systems to enhance test-time training efficiency. Experimental integration of OptPO with methods such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++ was conducted to examine its performance. Comparative evaluations were performed against TTRL, a prevalent approach for policy optimization during testing, alongside additional assessments of OptPO-SFT in analogous scenarios. For critic optimization, a learning rate of 1e-5 was utilized. For SFT-based training methods, including TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, gradient descent on labeled batches was executed with the AdamW optimizer at a learning rate of 2e-5.

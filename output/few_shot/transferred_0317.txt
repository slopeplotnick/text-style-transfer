Research indicates that entrenched beliefs can lead to issues such as polarization, misinformation, and a variety of cognitive errors, which stem from the mechanisms underlying human information processing and reasoning. Large language models (LLMs), trained on extensive corpora of online text to emulate human-like thought, have been designed to assist users by answering questions and performing complex tasks. These models have demonstrated notable success in executing intricate reasoning tasks, with the development of novel approaches such as Chain-of-Thought prompting and reinforcement learning techniques. These methodologies aim to enhance model reasoning by employing argumentation, evidence evaluation, and iterative exploration. However, LLMs occasionally exhibit limitations in truth-seeking behavior, as they may adhere to prior beliefs rather than integrating novel information, thereby reducing their accuracy and fostering overconfidence in users. While research has identified belief entrenchment as a phenomenon in these models, establishing evidence for its occurrence is methodologically challenging. Determining whether a belief revision is justified or influenced by bias remains a complex task. Consequently, prior studies often rely on controlled tasks where belief entrenchment is more readily observable, though such approaches are less effective in diagnosing reasoning errors in ambiguous or truth-uncertain scenarios.

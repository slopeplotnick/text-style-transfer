The Long-Tail Hard subset is designed to evaluate the capability of advanced language models (LMs) to perform challenging tasks. This subset comprises intricate examples, categorized into two primary types: explicit requests (e.g., queries involving multiple attributes or complex multi-requirements) and implicit or indirect requests. The latter category is further subdivided into four groups: those involving negation, alternative options, domain-specific knowledge, and reasoning. Diverse methodologies were employed to identify these examples. For negation and alternative-based cases, keyword and pattern identification techniques were utilized. For knowledge and reasoning-based cases, which are relatively rare, simpler instances were first filtered out. Subsequently, a robust language model (Qwen3-235B) was employed to determine whether it consistently provided the same answer in four out of eight attempts. Multi-attribute requests were identified using a specialized tool capable of detecting queries featuring more than five attributes. To ensure the difficulty of these examples, a baseline model was tested, and only those instances where the model succeeded in five or fewer attempts out of eight were retained, thus confirming their complexity.  The Visual Salience subset focuses on cases where the primary product image plays a crucial role, particularly in scenarios where visual attributes such as color or style are critical for decision-making. To construct this subset, an initial set of candidates was identified using keywords derived from an exhaustive visual category list. Erroneous examples were filtered out using a blacklist, retaining only instances where the critical visual keyword was absent from the product description. A language model was subsequently employed to verify the relevance of the keywords to visual details. Finally, to pinpoint the most challenging examples, a basic multimodal model capable of analyzing textual and visual inputs was trained. Instances were selected based on the model's uncertainty—specifically, examples where the model demonstrated significant disagreement in its predictions across attempts.

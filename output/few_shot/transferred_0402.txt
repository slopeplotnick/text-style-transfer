The observed variations in the PhysionetMI dataset can likely be attributed to its inherent complexity, including diverse test configurations, individual-specific brain activity, and signal variability. A promising approach involves the application of ensemble methods such as bagging, boosting, or stacking to amalgamate the advantages of both simple and complex models, thereby enhancing the reliability of outcomes despite inter- and intra-subject variability. To optimize the efficacy and consistency of these methods, further research is imperative. Although current challenges persist, integrating quantum methodologies with advanced algorithms holds significant potential for improving EEG signal analysis, enhancing generalizability, and accommodating the intrinsic variability in EEG data. Such advancements would represent a substantial progression towards more personalized and adaptable brain-computer interfaces. Preliminary investigations into the application of quantum computing in this context are underway to evaluate its feasibility in enhancing EEG signal decoding accuracy. Another major limitation of existing approaches is the heavy reliance on labeled data, which incurs substantial time and financial costs to produce at scale. Recent studies advocate for the development of methodologies capable of leveraging unlabeled data, such as unsupervised or semi-supervised learning, to mitigate the dependency on extensive labeled datasets. Additionally, the exploration of transfer learning—which involves applying learned patterns from one individual or dataset to another—remains insufficient, despite its potential to enhance model performance and address inter-subject variability. Future research endeavors should concentrate on the design and evaluation of transfer learning techniques to resolve challenges associated with EEG datasets. In conclusion, our findings indicate that the adoption of tailored EEG analysis methods is crucial for addressing inter- and intra-subject differences. The convergence of flexible strategies, sophisticated models, ensemble learning techniques, and quantum-based approaches is poised to define the future trajectory of personalized and practical brain-computer interfaces.

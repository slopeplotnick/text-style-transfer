Episodic memory retrieval performance was evaluated across varying temporal durations of events utilizing the metric temporal intersection over union (tIoU). This metric quantifies the degree to which detected segments align with ground truth by comparing their overlap to their total length. WorldMM's performance was assessed against alternative models in tasks such as temporal grounding, single-modality retrieval, long-form egocentric video retrieval, and keyframe selection. Foundational concepts related to these methodologies are elaborated in Section C.1. As demonstrated in Table 3, WorldMM significantly outperforms other state-of-the-art models in terms of tIoU scores. Methods dependent on reasoning and keyframe selection exhibit comparatively lower tIoU scores, indicating challenges in handling extended input contexts. Figure 5 further illustrates the correlation between improved tIoU scores and higher overall accuracy, particularly for tasks involving comprehension of long videos versus one-step retrieval. This enhancement stems from the iterative nature of the retrieval process, where successive rounds refine the system's strategy and gather additional pertinent information, compensating for initial inaccuracies. An illustrative example of such refinement is presented in Section E.2, where the model rectifies an incorrect retrieval to yield a more precise and contextually relevant response.  System efficiency was analyzed using processing times for 100 random queries from the EgoLifeQA dataset. Figure 6 visualizes the trade-off between speed and accuracy across methods. Long-video-focused large language models (LLMs) exhibit prolonged processing times with suboptimal performance, whereas retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) or memory-based systems operate faster but necessitate significant setup and fail to achieve comparable accuracy. In contrast, WorldMM demonstrates a balanced approach, optimizing both speed and accuracy by conducting iterative retrieval rounds tailored to extract only relevant segments.

The enhancement in computer utilization is attributed to the advent of novel tools and systems that enhance autonomous computational understanding and functionality. Open-source frameworks facilitate the integration of various components, such as planners and memory. Corporate reports elucidate the real-world application of these systems. Numerous studies assert that merely scaling models does not inherently augment reliability, thus underscoring the importance of rigorous system evaluations to ensure genuine advancements. The increasing complexity of tasks exacerbates the challenge of evaluating these systems. Some research has explored the utilization of specialized systems to aid in evaluations, though concerns regarding bias persist. Proposed solutions include employing multiple evaluators to ensure equitable assessments across diverse tasks. Our project adopts these methodologies by engaging two evaluators to examine different task components to validate genuine improvements. We have curated a collection of 100 real-world editing tasks, encompassing slide materials, each with an original slide deck, a target deck, instructions, and guidelines concerning layout and content evaluation. These tasks are systematically organized into 16 categories to ensure the assessment of both comprehension and intricate formatting capabilities. Data were amassed from over 15,000 PowerPoint presentations from disparate sources, subsequently transformed into a format that delineates layout and content specifics. Presentations with diverse styles were retained and amalgamated with contributions from various experts. From upwards of 500 reviewed options, including contributions by students, we selected 100 exemplars that span professional and academic domains, ensuring each category encompasses challenging instances.

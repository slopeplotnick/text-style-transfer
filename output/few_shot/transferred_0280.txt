Researchers are investigating methods to enhance the robustness of systems by ensuring consistency in shape and geometric interpretation. It has been observed that alterations in viewpoint can disrupt the functionality of pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT) encoders, subsequently affecting the coordination between perception and decision-making components of the system. Consequently, this study explores addressing such processing mismatches—without overhauling the entire Vision-Language Model Architecture (VLA) or acquiring additional data—to improve the system's resilience to viewpoint changes.   Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods have gained prominence for enabling models to adapt to new tasks or conditions with minimal modifications. Techniques such as adapters, LoRA, Prefix-Tuning, and BitFit incorporate minor, adjustable elements into the system, maintaining most of it intact. These methods are prevalent in large language models and multimodal systems due to their efficiency and stability. Within the realm of embodied AI, PEFT is primarily employed to update language or decision-making components, including modifications to decision transformers or instruction encoders. However, regarding viewpoint changes, existing methods seldom concentrate on directly refining the visual component of the system. There is a scarcity of approaches that implement targeted, minimal adjustments to the visual processing segment of VLA models; instead, most seek to adapt the entire system.   Our research examines whether applying minimal, efficient modifications specifically to the visual components—such as vision tokens or the vision encoder—can effectively resolve these issues. Traditional methods typically address visual challenges by retraining substantial portions of the system or even the entire visuomotor policy, an approach we believe to be unnecessary. The principal concern with changes in visual input lies in the predictable alterations in visual data processing, leading to systemic discrepancies.

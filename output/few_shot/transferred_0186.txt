To address these challenges, some approaches operate independently of specific models by comparing model outputs with training data within a shared representation space, often derived from features from pre-trained encoders. While these approaches demonstrate effectiveness and scalability, they rely on vector representations, which are inherently difficult to interpret. Recent work, such as LatentExplainer, attempts to address this issue by modifying latent variables and leveraging language models to provide explanations for these modifications. However, such methods remain opaque to end-users. In contrast, our approach employs automatically generated Knowledge Graphs (KGs) to elucidate the influence of training data. Specifically, we compare generated and training images through shared triples encompassing objects, attributes, and style elements, thereby aligning the analysis with human interpretability.  Large Language Models (LLMs) are becoming integral to contemporary KG methodologies, particularly for processing unstructured data. LLMs contribute in three primary capacities: (1) generating subject-predicate-object triples from unstructured text; (2) facilitating ontology construction and enrichment; and (3) transforming natural language queries into graph-based queries. For triple generation, methods such as AutoKG enable the extraction of ontology-compliant triples from text. Additionally, LLMs support ontology learning and extension using techniques like prompt engineering and ontology reuse. In question answering, LLMs translate user questions into SPARQL queries, a process further refined through contextualized KGs or controlled natural language to enhance clarity. Surveys on generative KG construction highlight a paradigm shift from traditional pipeline-based systems to LLM-driven frameworks, which outperform prior approaches in entity recognition, relationship extraction, and graph completion.  These advancements in KG construction provide a robust foundation for addressing data attribution challenges in generative models. Unlike obscure embeddings or model parameters, structured outputs from multimodal LLMs facilitate the creation of interpretable KGs that encapsulate salient features of both generated and training data, enabling traceability. While much of the work thus far has focused on text-based data, these methodologies hold significant potential for application to images, despite the added complexity associated with visual data.

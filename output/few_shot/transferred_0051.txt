The present text examines methodologies for assessing the logical and ethical integrity of AI model responses. It introduces the MoCoP system, an innovative framework that evaluates AI ethics without relying on a predefined dataset. MoCoP autonomously generates, tests, and refines moral reasoning scenarios, independent of external input. Additionally, it quantifies alterations in AI moral reasoning over time and conducts comparative analyses among different models to assess consistency. By integrating tools for lexical, semantic, and reasoning analysis, MoCoP effectively monitors the ethical handling capabilities of AI models longitudinally. The structure of the paper is as follows: Section 2 reviews existing literature on AI ethics evaluation, Section 3 details the operational mechanisms of MoCoP, Section 5 presents empirical findings, Section 6 offers an interpretation of the results, Section 7 addresses potential methodological challenges, and Section 8 discusses the limitations and prospective future directions of the study. Previous research predominantly concentrated on identifying and mitigating bias or harmful content within AI systems. For instance, OpenEthics endeavored to enhance transparency and auditability in AI ethics, while another study focused on fairness, aiming to reduce biases against specific demographic groups. While these studies advanced transparency in AI systems, their reliance on static datasets and prompts impeded adaptability to evolving conditions. They provided snapshots of AI models' operational integrity but lacked the capacity to monitor longitudinal changes or assess stability across diverse scenarios. Recent investigations have transcended fairness, delving into the models' mechanisms for ethical and moral decision-making.

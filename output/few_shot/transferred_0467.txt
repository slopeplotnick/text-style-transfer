A significant challenge for Open Language Learning Models (OLLMs) is managing diverse input modalities, which complicates the manifestation and detection of safety concerns. Each input type, such as textual biases, misinformation in imagery, or privacy issues in audio, presents unique risks. Furthermore, the integration of various input types exacerbates the complexity of identifying latent hazards. For instance, the phrase "I want to fly" when paired with an image of an individual on a rooftop, though innocuous in isolation, may collectively imply dangerous behavior such as suicidal ideation. Similarly, a video of law enforcement accompanied by the query "How to overcome obstacles" could unintentionally suggest criminal intent, an inference not apparent from a single input type alone. Thus, addressing safety risks in OLLMs demands a nuanced understanding of each input modality and their interactions. Enhancing the safety of OLLMs often proves inefficient, as it requires substantial time and resources, potentially impairing their fundamental reasoning abilities. Even after significant investment, these models may necessitate costly retraining to rectify issues and remain vulnerable to low-cost adversarial attacks. Guardrail models offer an adaptable solution; however, they tend to concentrate on individual input types or straightforward combinations, such as text and images, rather than encompassing all modalities collectively. Given that unsafe inputs can derive from any single type or their combinations, single-type and simplistic guardrail models are inadequate for comprehensive moderation. Moreover, many guardrail models binary approach to safety — as a yes-or-no proposition — limits their efficacy.

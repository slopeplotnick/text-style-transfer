Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has significantly enhanced the capability of large language models in tasks requiring extensive knowledge. However, the majority of existing systems concentrate on general topics or news, leading to suboptimal performance in critical domains such as agricultural adaptation to climate change. Recent research endeavors have attempted to integrate document retrieval and climate data for specialized queries, yet they often employ rudimentary methods that fail to deliver comprehensive or verified responses for domain-specific contexts. Although there have been efforts to develop geography-aware question-answering frameworks in various fields, these are not specifically designed to incorporate climate-related considerations. Furthermore, existing studies typically treat climate data and academic research as distinct entities, thereby lacking an integrated approach.   The CAIRNS framework introduces a four-step process: initially, it aggregates climate data from sources such as Elsevier and a climate data API; subsequently, it generates responses utilizing the ScholarGuide Prompt; the third phase involves verification of the responses; and the final step entails a mixed-methods evaluation. To identify pertinent documents, a custom retriever was developed to combine lexical matching with semantic retrieval. However, relying solely on this approach is insufficient due to the geographic specificity inherent in climate-related inquiries. Consequently, a location similarity score is incorporated to ensure that responses are relevant both semantically and geographically. CAIRNS employs an iterative cycle of evidence gathering, response generation, and adequacy verification. In instances of incomplete information, it formulates new questions and iteratively seeks additional data until a satisfactory response is achieved. The ScholarGuide Prompt plays a crucial role in response formulation within CAIRNS, aiding in the equilibrium between readability and factual accuracy. For climate-focused inquiries, this prompt serves as a directive, guiding the language model in structuring responses, applying scientific reasoning, and providing precise recommendations.

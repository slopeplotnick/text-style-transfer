Checkers and analogous methodologies are effective for domains such as mathematics and coding; however, they demonstrate reduced efficacy in tasks lacking definitive answers. Certain strategies enable the model or another AI to function as an adjudicator, exemplified by Self-Rewarding Language Models and RLAIF, which utilize AI-generated feedback over human input, as well as IPO and Self-Rewarding PPO, which employ probabilistic approaches to discern preferences. Further inquiries explore internal activations, as illustrated by Factoscope and INSIDE, revealing that hidden states can signal factual veracity or potential inaccuracies. Our method aligns with this trajectory by employing a geometric metric of these activations as a reward to enhance model decision-making. This concept is theoretically grounded in softmax bottleneck analysis. Yang et al. demonstrated limitations imposed by the rank of hidden layers in models utilizing softmax, with Godey extending this framework to contemporary AI, indicating that natural language probabilities are high-rank. Additional studies suggest that when embeddings constrict during training, the quality of model outputs deteriorates, thereby linking representational complexity to qualitative output. Empirical evidence supports these findings, such as Garrido's research, which correlates the rank of learned representations with performance metrics independent of labels. Language models inherently embed substantial semantic value within hidden states: word embeddings capture syntactic and semantic patterns, while intermediate layers assess factual accuracy. This indicates that generated content quality is measurable through discernible patterns. Our research advances these principles by not only post hoc rank analysis but also by employing rank as a dynamic reward signal, thereby transforming geometric insights into a pragmatic strategy for model enhancement.

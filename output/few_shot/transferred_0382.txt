We investigated the resilience of pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and observed a decline in performance when exposed to variations in visual input, attributed to changes in their spatial understanding. To address this, we developed a novel approach for rapid model adaptation to such alterations, employing Feature Token Modulation and Feature Linear Adaptation. This technique modifies the perception mechanisms of the models with minimal alterations, enhancing their robustness to novel camera angles and variations in input, as demonstrated by the Libero benchmark results. Consequently, these VLA models exhibit greater robustness than previously assumed. VLA models aim to integrate perception, language comprehension, and decision-making within a unified framework. Leveraging foundational vision and language principles, recent VLA models such as RT-2, Pi0, PaLM-E, Octo, and OpenVLA demonstrate proficiency in executing diverse tasks. These models are trained on extensive datasets comprising heterogeneous visual inputs, language directives, bodily states, and unstructured actions, facilitating a unified response strategy in varying contexts. Nonetheless, despite these advancements, evidence indicates that state-of-the-art VLAs remain susceptible to perturbations such as novel camera perspectives, lighting conditions, or scene noise, even after extensive training. This vulnerability must be addressed for optimal performance in real-world scenarios, which present complexities exceeding those in controlled Libero tests. Enhancing the visual adaptability of VLAs represents a significant challenge. Prior research efforts have sought to standardize visual perception across different perspectives through data augmentation techniques, multiview approaches, or extensive collections of variation-enriched visual datasets. Although effective, these strategies often necessitate substantial data acquisition or annotation efforts, presenting scalability constraints.

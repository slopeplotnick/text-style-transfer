The initial experiment conducted in the MODOMA laboratory was predicated on a specific linkage, serving as a means to evaluate the efficacy of the MODOMA methodology for language acquisition using Zipf's law. This investigation contributes to the advancement of research, enabling MODOMA to improve through more sophisticated testing. Consequently, it allows for enhanced proficiency in utilizing MODOMA as a model for language acquisition assessments, thereby optimizing the system prior to exploring alternative language learning approaches. Although Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) analyze the origins of languages, they also employ Zipf's law to gain insights into language models vis-à-vis human languages. This approach incorporates most of the components and procedures requisite for more intricate learning assessments. The components of the MODOMA system and their interrelations can be evaluated and refined to facilitate additional computational language learning tests. If successful, the learning process results in the daughter agent discerning the distinction between functional and content categories from examples of the target language provided by the mother agent during their interaction. It is crucial that once the daughter agent comprehends this grammatical distinction, it immediately applies this newfound grammatical knowledge to update its grammar, generate novel sentences during interactions with the mother agent, and comprehend new sentences from the mother. Thus, this study evaluates whether the MODOMA laboratory methodology for language learning can foster the acquisition of essential grammatical knowledge that can be effectively leveraged in language comprehension and generation.

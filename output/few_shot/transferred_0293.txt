The primary objective of explainable AI (XAI) is to elucidate the rationale behind a model's output, though the motivations for pursuing this objective can vary significantly. These motivations include evaluating model performance, fostering user trust in the model's decisions, and facilitating the modification of decisions when necessary. For the latter, it is crucial that explanations emphasize actionable elements within the current context. This process, aimed at altering a model's output through explanation, is termed algorithmic recourse. It necessitates precise predictions regarding the outcomes of proposed modifications, focusing solely on the consequences following the implementation of these changes. The explanation framework proposed in this paper is designed to operate broadly across diverse scenarios, without targeting a specific individual requiring clarification or making assumptions about the model's performance or application context. This approach ensures alignment between the explanations provided and the model's actual operations, even when the model does not perform as anticipated. Additionally, it permits consideration of alternative past actions without concerns about the feasibility of these changes. Although this framework could be adapted for algorithmic recourse, such applications fall outside the scope of this paper. Rather than relying on distinct data features, concept-based explanations employ overarching ideas or characteristics that are intuitively comprehensible to humans. The definition of these concepts in XAI is variable, but within this context, it aligns with what Poeta et al. [2025] describe as symbolic concepts—high-level constructs or attributes of data that are accessible to human understanding.

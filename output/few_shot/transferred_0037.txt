Self-Consistency involves aggregating multiple outputs and selecting the most prevalent result, whereas Soft Self-Consistency entails choosing based on lowest, average, or composite confidence scores within each group. Generative Self-Aggregation prompts the language model to synthesize a new response from various samples. Universal Self-Consistency employs the language model to identify the most fitting answer among several alternatives. This study examines various language models, specifically gemini-2.5-flash, qwen3 next-80b, and gpt-oss-120b, across two machine translation tasks and six Reward Bench 2 benchmark tasks. The Reward Bench 2 benchmark evaluates reward models across six dimensions: factuality, precise instruction adherence, mathematical accuracy, safety, focus, and ties. For this evaluation, preference pairs were created by pairing all possible combinations from each task's source data, containing both accepted and rejected responses. These pairs were categorized into 'non-tie' pairs (one accepted and one rejected response) and 'tie' pairs (two accepted or two rejected responses). From this extensive dataset, 1000 examples per task were selected to constitute the final benchmark. Detailed vote distributions for each task are presented in Appendix B. The results for the tested language models—gemini-2.5-flash, gpt-oss-120b, and qwen3 next-80b—exhibit consistent patterns, indicating the robustness of the proposed methodology across these models. The inherent structure of the problem encompasses task-induced ambiguity frequencies, the language model's tendencies to produce clear versus tied votes, and the interplay of these characteristics with the MAE-aligned Davidson model. Certain tasks feature smooth, transferable calibration landscapes, while others present more abrupt landscapes less amenable to external application. Future research may focus on developing robust assessment tools to predict task adaptability and evaluate performance under significant distribution shifts or novel target conditions.

The study involved a comparative analysis of authentic and synthetic data, revealing no substantial differences in mean scores according to one test; however, another test indicated a significant variation in score distribution. This finding suggests that while the synthetic data approximated the authentic data in terms of central tendency, the distributional characteristics differed. Consequently, this partially substantiates our fourth hypothesis. Figure 2 illustrates the comparative graphical analysis of the real and synthetic datasets. To ensure the robustness of our findings, a secondary study was conducted using data from an independent cohort. Specifically, we selected a validated and publicly accessible dataset designed to assess perceived technological proficiency across 25 questions distributed within five domains: communication, information management, content creation, safe usage, and problem-solving. This choice was predicated on the dataset's comprehensive validation. Prior to generating synthetic data, our experimental protocol was pre-registered. Due to the absence of ethnic information in the new dataset, the synthetic data generation was adapted to utilize only age and gender demographics. Furthermore, the dataset contained unique identifiers for each participant, allowing for precise matching of synthetic to real individuals by age and gender.

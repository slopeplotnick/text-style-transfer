Language models exhibit superiority over traditional algorithmic models due to their enhanced predictive capabilities and intuitive interpretability, alongside their capacity to handle diverse tasks concurrently. Small Language Models (SLMs), developed to function efficiently through techniques such as quantization, knowledge distillation, and lightweight design, present a viable alternative to Large Language Models (LLMs). SLMs are particularly adept at tasks such as text sorting, information summarization, and question answering on resource-constrained devices, including smartphones and embedded systems. They are advantageous in comparison to LLMs due to their lower computational demands while maintaining robust performance in specific tasks. Research indicates that in scenarios with limited data, smaller models may perform comparably or even surpass larger models in targeted areas, such as text sorting and healthcare applications. This makes SLMs a suitable choice for identifying mental health issues on social media platforms. However, previous research has predominantly focused on isolated tasks or domains, without attempting to integrate various mental health issues such as depression, stress, and suicidality. In contrast to earlier LLMs, characterized by an excessive number of parameters, which are unsuitable for environments with constrained resources or privacy concerns, SLMs prioritize usability, offering rapid response times, reduced memory usage, and cost efficiency. Prior studies have compared numerous SLMs with LLMs across different mental health tasks utilizing social media data, concluding that few-shot prompting significantly benefits SLMs. Kim et al. developed an SLM named mhGPT, which outperforms larger models like MentaLLaMA and Gemma in mental health tasks, despite having fewer parameters and utilizing less data. Another study posits that SLMs can be employed in clinical settings for structured tasks beyond mere issue detection. Nevertheless, these studies often lack task balancing and are infrequently optimized with efficient fine-tuning, typically employing either minimal prompting or full fine-tuning (Kim et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2024).

Our evaluation methodology employs two primary metrics: the Toxic Score and the Attack Success Rate (ASR). In alignment with prior research, we utilize GPT-4o to assess the potential harmfulness of a response. Each response to a potentially harmful query is evaluated by assigning a Toxic Score ranging from 1 to 5, with higher scores indicating greater harmfulness; a score of 5 denotes a complete alignment with the harmful intent of the query. An attack is deemed successful only if it achieves a score of 5. The evaluation prompt is provided in Appendix B.3. Our study is composed of four distinct components, each optimized for different contexts and models. An attack is considered efficacious if it successfully compromises either the original target image or any of the four enhanced target images. For the Toxic Score assessment, we select the highest score obtained from five trials for each scenario-specific example. Results concerning the Demonstrative Framework are presented, with additional outcomes for alternative strategies detailed in Appendices C and D. Extensive testing was conducted on the MMSafetyBench-tiny and SafeBench-tiny datasets, with the comprehensive findings tabulated in Tables 1 and 2. The methodology involves iterative rounds and images in dialogues, alongside robust red-teaming models to generate attack prompts, resulting in higher computational and financial costs compared to the CIA framework. Conversely, the CIA framework, which requires generating a single image per query and employs a simplified attack strategy, demonstrates scalability and reproducibility advantages. It also performs effectively in challenging domains such as illegal advice, fraud, and hate crime, suggesting that the CIA framework exploits a general vulnerability in the alignment of vision and language models and their adherence to instructions.

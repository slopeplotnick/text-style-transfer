To assess the quality of the pre-training phase, we evaluate whether the token distributions in generated samples align with those in the original pre-training data. Specifically, we analyze the frequency of token types in the pre-training dataset and compare these distributions to the corresponding distributions in generated samples at various training stages. Samples generated after 250 epochs most closely resemble the original data. While training and validation losses continue to decrease up to 300 epochs, the resemblance between the samples and the original data diminishes beyond 250 epochs. Consequently, the 250-epoch stage is selected for fine-tuning on EMOPIA, as it offers a balance between further loss minimization and fidelity to the original data.   Additionally, we conducted an experiment to determine the decoder's ability to produce MIDI sequences using AV quadrants. Instead of text embeddings, quadrant labels were directly provided to the decoder. This straightforward approach effectively generated MIDI samples that corresponded well with EMOPIA’s valence-arousal metrics. A listening test, conducted with four participants following the methodology from Section IV-A, yielded results presented in Table I. Participants indicated that the model's music better aligned with the arousal dimension (70% accuracy) compared to valence (53% accuracy) or the combined valence-arousal metrics (40% accuracy). These results exceed baseline accuracies (50% for valence and arousal, and 25% for both dimensions), underscoring that the model-generated music aligns with the emotional tone of the text at rates significantly higher than chance.   However, the small participant sample size limits the reliability and generalizability of these findings. Future work should incorporate a larger and more diverse listener cohort to enhance the robustness of the results and enable statistical significance testing. Expanding the scope of the study is essential to validate these preliminary findings.

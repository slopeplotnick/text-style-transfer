Jack Lu, Ryan Teehan, Jinran Jin, and Mengye Ren, researchers affiliated with the Agentic Learning AI Lab at New York University, presented their findings on November 20, 2025, concerning the dual capability of large language models (LLMs) to both solve problems and verify solutions. Their analysis emphasized the critical role of verification in identifying optimal answers. While prior research predominantly explored models' ability to evaluate their own outputs, less attention has been directed at examining inter-model evaluation, i.e., assessing answers generated by other models.   The study involved an extensive evaluation of 37 models varying in size and architecture, including models that underwent basic training as well as those subjected to additional post-training fine-tuning. The analysis spanned nine distinct problem categories, encompassing domains such as logical reasoning, puzzles, mathematics, common sense, and factual knowledge. The researchers systematically compared models' performance in self-checking versus cross-checking answers derived from models within the same class or across different groups.   To quantify the impact of verification on response quality, the authors proposed a novel metric to measure the improvement facilitated by answer-checking processes. This framework was then tested empirically, revealing several insights: (1) cross-group answer verification yielded particularly robust results; (2) additional fine-tuning enhanced models’ ability to verify responses from other groups but did not significantly improve self-verification; (3) tasks involving mathematics and logic were the most amenable to verification.   The findings suggest a paradigm shift in the application of LLMs, transitioning from solely generating answers to incorporating systematic verification processes. At test time, verification can occur through relatively straightforward methodologies, such as generating multiple candidate answers and employing a verification mechanism to select the best one, or through iterative refinement strategies. By integrating checking mechanisms during evaluation, models demonstrate enhanced problem-solving capabilities, extending their utility to more complex and challenging tasks than those solvable in isolation.

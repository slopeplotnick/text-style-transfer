During the training process, we implemented an alternative approach. The base model often encounters difficulties adhering to instructions when faced with lengthy prompts. To mitigate this issue, we introduced a preliminary warm-up phase utilizing supervised fine-tuning to enhance adherence to the desired format. The methodology operates as follows: the Challenger role performs few-shot grounded generation and computes a reward. Subsequently, the Solver role engages in online curriculum learning, evaluates its reward, and updates accordingly. This approach ensures that the Solver concentrates on challenging yet solvable problems, in alignment with effective learning theories. The Solver's reward mechanism incorporates both synthetic and real data, modulated by the complexity of the curriculum. R-FeW maintains the iterative structure of R-ZERO but employs a grounded and curriculum-sensitive data configuration. The Challenger generates tasks using few-shot examples derived from human inputs. The Solver attempts to resolve these tasks and assesses its success rate. An online filtering mechanism selects tasks of moderate difficulty from both synthetic and real datasets. We utilize Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base as our primary models. For comparative purposes, we evaluate against several baselines: (1) Base Model — the unmodified model without additional training, available for public download; (2) R-Zero — the initial self-play method where the model autonomously generates questions and answers to facilitate self-improvement; (3) Absolute Zero — a self-play configuration confined to code generation tasks with Python validation; (4) SPICE — a self-play framework that leverages extensive documents to formulate reasoning tasks with document-based responses. We replicated baselines (1)-(2) using available configurations under identical training conditions, while results for (3)-(4) are drawn from Liu et al.'s reports.

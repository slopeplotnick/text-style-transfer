When planning a sequence of measurements, such as determining optimal drilling locations, advanced artificial intelligence tools like Monte Carlo Tree Search can be employed to optimize drilling strategies (Mern and Caers, 2023). The prioritization of decision criteria typically evolves through sequential analysis: initial conceptual validation, followed by quantitative evaluation of resource abundance, and concluding with assessments of resource quality. Once competing ideas have been eliminated, the focus can shift exclusively to mineral quantity, resource quality, or a combined quality-quantity framework, for instance, represented in a quality-amount chart. Although scientific methodologies offer the potential for more cost-effective and efficient exploration, several non-scientific challenges persist within the field. Among these are entrenched industry practices, organizational structures within companies, fragmented approaches to exploration workflows, and the absence of integrated software solutions. Typically, companies employ domain-specific experts to manage specialized tasks, with these experts often prioritizing granular details without adequately considering their relevance to overarching decision-making. For AI-driven approaches to be effectively integrated, large organizations must undergo structural reforms, prioritizing the resolution of decision-making challenges rather than task-specific operations. Such reform extends beyond organizational restructuring, encompassing interdisciplinary understanding and collaboration. While geologists need not become data science specialists, fostering mutual comprehension across diverse expertise areas is imperative, a topic further explored in the educational domain. Additionally, longstanding reliance on single-model frameworks or software that facilitates biased and static interpretations contributes to pitfalls in exploration workflows. Over time, such models are often regarded as objective data rather than interpretative constructs, with the rationale behind the original interpretation forgotten as personnel turnover occurs. Within many companies, ignorance or uncertainty is often perceived as a failure, reinforcing the misconception that prolonged effort and labor inherently lead to more knowledge and reduced uncertainty—a perspective that perpetuates inefficiencies in the exploration process.

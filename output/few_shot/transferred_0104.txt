The relevance task was deconstructed to identify the specific requirements for model functionality, providing a structured framework that offers concrete recommendations for future relevance models in industrial applications. A comprehensive methodology is proposed, encompassing all stages from model selection to training and evaluation. Insights derived from experiments with LORE include the following key findings for fine-tuning language models in specialized domains:  1. **Data volume effects**: While initial increases in data volume lead to rapid performance improvements, the marginal gains diminish as additional data is incorporated, suggesting that merely adding more data does not guarantee improved performance.   2. **Feature importance**: Models benefit from the inclusion of more robust and relevant features. Stable and pertinent information, even if slightly repetitive, positively impacts performance. 3. **Prompt optimization**: Concise and targeted prompts are more effective for model performance compared to verbose inputs with excessive information. Conversely, prompts lacking critical details degrade model accuracy. 4. **Limitations of teacher CoT distillation**: Simple teacher Chain-of-Thought (CoT) distillation strategies perform suboptimally relative to conventional training approaches, primarily due to shifts in data distribution. 5. **Efficacy of curriculum learning**: Curriculum learning strategies that progressively introduce tasks based on difficulty substantially outperform random task organization. 6. **Role of CoT length**: Extended CoT sequences are not a prerequisite for enhanced outcomes, as training did not significantly increase the output lengths, indicating that concise CoT reasoning suffices. 7. **Entropy dynamics**: Model performance is constrained by the rate of entropy reduction. During the early stages of training, rapid entropy reduction correlates with performance improvements, but excessive entropy minimization impairs exploration and slows further optimization. 8. **Entropy management strategies**: Techniques to regulate entropy decay, such as "clip-higher," on-policy optimization, and entropy loss, were evaluated. Among these, "clip-higher" proved the most effective.  These findings underscore the importance of strategic methodologies in the training and fine-tuning of language models, emphasizing the careful calibration of data, features, prompts, and optimization techniques to maximize performance.

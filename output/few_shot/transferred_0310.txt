Auditors utilize Journal Entry Tests (JETs) to identify irregularities in financial records; however, these tests frequently generate a high volume of false positives while failing to detect subtle anomalies. This study examines the potential of advanced large language models (LLMs) to improve anomaly detection in bookkeeping. Specifically, prominent LLMs such as LLaMA and Gemma were evaluated on both simulated and anonymized real-world ledger datasets, with their performance compared to traditional JETs and conventional machine learning (ML) techniques. The results demonstrate that LLMs outperform standard JETs and classical ML methods in identifying anomalies. Moreover, LLMs offer the added advantage of generating explanations in natural language, enhancing interpretability for auditors. These findings underscore the potential of artificial intelligence (AI) to augment the auditing process, promoting collaboration with human auditors to ensure the integrity of financial records.  The role of financial auditors is critical in detecting unusual transactions indicative of errors, fraud, or tax evasion within corporate records. Identifying such irregularities is essential for maintaining trust in financial reporting. However, the extensive and complex nature of contemporary accounting data presents significant challenges. Current manual and rule-based approaches are often inefficient and prone to errors, increasing the likelihood of missing critical warning signs. Conventional JETs, primarily reliant on predefined rules and recognizable patterns, tend to generate excessive alerts, many of which are false positives, thereby consuming auditors' time and resources. Furthermore, these methods are typically limited to identifying predictable issues and fail to address more nuanced or atypical anomalies. In practice, auditors continue to depend heavily on sampling and manual review processes, which are insufficient for managing vast and intricate datasets. This disconnect between the growing complexity of financial data and the limitations of traditional auditing methods underscores the necessity for more advanced and automated tools to enhance problem detection in auditing practices.

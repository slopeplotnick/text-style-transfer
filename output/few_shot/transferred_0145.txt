Our experimental evaluation of these methods on the BrowseComp benchmark revealed that effective context management during testing enhances model performance by enabling the utilization of greater computational resources and facilitating additional reasoning steps when necessary. Specifically, the Summary approach increases the average number of reasoning steps to 364, achieving a performance improvement of up to 60.2%. However, this method demonstrates limited overall efficiency. In contrast, the Discard-all method, despite its simplicity, exhibits notable efficiency and scalability with a benchmark score of 67.6, comparable to parallel scaling techniques but requiring fewer steps. These findings suggest that test-time computational capacity can be augmented either through stepwise context management or parallelization, both of which contribute to improved problem-solving performance. Nonetheless, the trade-offs between efficiency and scalability necessitate consideration of the computational cost when comparing model performance. Identifying an optimal balance between stepwise and parallel approaches for achieving both efficiency and scalability remains an open challenge warranting further investigation.  In this study, we introduced DeepSeek-V3.2, a system designed to optimize computational efficiency while maintaining advanced reasoning capabilities. Leveraging Dynamic Step Adjustment (DSA), the system addresses complex computational challenges without sacrificing its ability to manage extended contexts. By scaling computational resources, DeepSeek-V3.2 achieves performance parity with GPT-5 on reasoning benchmarks. Additionally, the integration of our task synthesis system enhances tool utilization, paving the way for the development of robust and versatile AI systems leveraging open Large Language Models (LLMs). Furthermore, we presented a high-performance variant, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, which has earned accolades in prestigious competitions such as the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) and the International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI), signifying a significant advancement for open LLMs. However, it is important to acknowledge the current limitations of DeepSeek-V3.2 compared to advanced closed-source models, such as Gemini-3.0-Pro. Specifically, due to its reliance on fewer training FLOPs, DeepSeek-V3.2 exhibits a comparatively narrower breadth of general knowledge than leading proprietary models.

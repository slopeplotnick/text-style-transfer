Forecasting questions from platforms such as Metaculus and Polymarket are utilized to evaluate the extent to which large language models (LLMs) exhibit belief entrenchment. This domain is selected for two principal reasons: first, forecasting questions yield definitive answers upon resolution, and second, successful performance necessitates robust reasoning capabilities, as it entails evaluating evidence and appropriately adjusting beliefs. Although forecasting diverges from factual question answering, excelling in this domain requires balanced and unbiased belief updates, offering a meaningful framework for assessing reasoning under uncertainty.  An additional method for investigating belief entrenchment involves scientific peer review. Specifically, the ICLR submission dataset from OpenReview is employed, comprising paper summaries, references, reviewer feedback, author responses, and final acceptance decisions. In this experimental setup, the model assumes the role of an area chair, tasked with determining whether a paper should be accepted based on the provided summary and the arguments presented in reviews and responses. This setup enables an analysis of the model’s reasoning process when initial impressions (e.g., from the summary) may conflict with subsequent information (e.g., critiques and responses).  Belief entrenchment is assessed through two reasoning paradigms: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and debate. The debate framework involves two instances of the same expert model advocating opposing perspectives on a topic, aiming to elucidate the issue for a less-informed observer. Experiments involve models such as GPT-4o, DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Flash, LLaMA 4 Scout, and LLaMA 4 Maverick. To examine the influence of initial beliefs on reasoning, system prompts are adapted to modify the models’ starting points. In addition to a baseline configuration with no prompt, two alternative conditions are explored: one aligning with the starting belief and another designed to promote critical thinking.

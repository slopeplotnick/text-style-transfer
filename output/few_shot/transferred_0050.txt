Our approach was compared against existing benchmarks using a structured evaluation framework involving judges, yielding significant improvements over prior methodologies. Previous methods, such as PPTC-R, primarily focus on API-level discrepancies, which, while useful, are inadequate for detecting critical logical errors, such as instances where the correct text is placed on an incorrect slide. In contrast, our IF Judge leverages structured summaries to identify such nuanced errors effectively. Other benchmarks, including T2US, employ visual language models (VLMs) in conjunction with prompts; however, their reliance on non-standardized guidelines results in inconsistent and unreliable outcomes. PPTArena addresses these shortcomings by introducing a robust and reproducible scoring mechanism. Notably, our dual-judge system, guided by clear evaluation criteria, represents a pivotal advancement for assessing the intricate processes involved in editing PowerPoint presentations and identifying subtle errors that simpler methods fail to capture.  Additionally, we introduce PPTPilot, a presentation editing tool characterized by a streamlined design and superior performance compared to paid alternatives, such as OpenAI’s ChatGPT Agent (discussed further in Section 5.1). The tool’s design is predicated on two fundamental principles. First, while comprehension of editing tasks is essential, ensuring accuracy and reliability in execution is particularly challenging due to the inherent complexity of PowerPoint’s Office Open XML (OOXML) structure. This format is prone to complications arising from errors or suboptimal outputs generated by VLMs, necessitating tailored formatting and configurations for effective editing. Second, we observed that no single editing approach—such as exclusively relying on XML-based manipulation—adequately addresses the diverse requirements of PowerPoint editing tasks. To accommodate these challenges, PPTPilot employs a dual-path framework that dynamically selects the most suitable editing approach, utilizing either programmatic tools or direct modifications of the XML format, thereby enhancing flexibility and precision in handling diverse editing requests.

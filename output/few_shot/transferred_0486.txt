The utilization of robotic systems in construction offers significant advantages in addressing labor shortages while enhancing safety and efficiency. Robots excel at executing heavy and repetitive tasks without fatigue or errors, thereby expediting construction processes and reducing costs. However, a single robotic unit is insufficient for managing the multifaceted demands of construction, which often require diverse skills such as material transport, precise placement, welding, and quality assurance. Consequently, collaborative robotic systems are necessitated, with Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) serving as a framework to decompose large-scale tasks into smaller subtasks and assign them to various robots. This allocation process considers factors such as individual robotic capabilities, task requirements, and environmental constraints. MRTA challenges typically pertain to categorizing tasks as single-robot or multi-robot, determining whether robots complete single or multiple tasks, and evaluating task durations. The complexity arises in coordinating a large number of robots to execute numerous interdependent tasks over time, necessitating sophisticated planning to account for heterogeneity in robot skill sets, task demands, and temporal constraints. Despite its potential, most MRTA research has primarily focused on domains such as warehouse operations and environmental exploration, with limited application in construction contexts.

The daughter language model, developed for the MOD-OMA framework, functions similarly to the Delilah model, serving as both a parser and a generator. However, it diverges from the adult language model due to its initially underdeveloped grammatical structure, which becomes more elaborate through the learning process. Both the adult and child systems employ graph-based representations to capture grammatical knowledge, with their parsers and generators constructing syntactically valid sentences by combining word structures according to extant grammatical rules. The child agent's parser and generator are capable of functioning with incomplete grammar, akin to the process of first language acquisition in humans, necessitating interaction with the mother model prior to full competence acquisition.  The daughter agent comprises three components: an automaton functioning as a parser and generator, an evolving grammar system enriched through learning, and a language acquisition device. The automaton remains static throughout the learning period. The result of the learning process is the emergence of explicit grammatical knowledge, as evidenced by new lexical entries and refined grammatical constraints limiting permissible word combinations. These grammatical attributes are represented by pairs, such as [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: content word] and [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: function word], which constitute the core learning objectives of this study.  The learning mechanism is unsupervised, implying the daughter model lacks prior knowledge of the labels or grammatical descriptions utilized by the mother model. The acquired grammatical properties and semantic content are annotated with unique identifiers, such as property type 'A' and property value 'b,' as depicted in Figures 2 and 3. The structures also encompass additional grammatical properties beyond the scope of this research. For instance, the terminal feature distinguishes between terminal words and abstract constructions, while the semantic content index facilitates the representation of elements such as reflexives. Technical identifiers, such as session ID and word entry number, ensure the unique identification of each rule. As language learning progresses, the grammar adopts increasingly complex structures, allowing the HEAD and ARGUMENT to incorporate elements such as word items.

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated significant efficacy in robotic tasks, leveraging extensive datasets curated through human intervention or predefined programs. The training of these models employs methodologies such as flow-matching or diffusion processes, enabling the acquisition of diverse task-handling strategies. Post-training, VLA models undergo fine-tuning for specific tasks utilizing smaller datasets. The utilization of pre-trained VLA models for novel tasks typically yields superior results compared to training from the ground up, as these models possess pre-existing knowledge encompassing visual perception, language comprehension, and object-space recognition. Despite their general effectiveness, VLA models exhibit certain limitations in real-world applications (Smith et al., 2023). Notably, they tend to retain superfluous actions post-fine-tuning, which do not contribute to task completion. This inefficiency arises from the extensive repertoire of actions learned from heterogeneous datasets during training, complicating the focus on task-specific actions. Furthermore, the disparity in dataset styles, attributed to varying sources and methodologies—some of which may not be optimal—compounds this issue. Consequently, there exists a discrepancy between the model's output and the ideal task execution, manifesting during operational deployment (Johnson et al., 2024).

The MODOMA system comprises two components: the mother agent and the daughter agent. Delilah serves as the mother agent and is developed as a tool for generating and analyzing the Dutch language. Unlike systems that rely on databases of examples, Delilah utilizes a set of predefined rules and structures that function as comprehensive templates delineating the functioning of words and grammar in Dutch. For instance, the template for the word "de" (translating to "the" in English) encompasses details regarding its form, semantic properties, grammatical function, and syntactic integration within sentences. These templates are derived from extensive studies of Dutch language and grammar, ensuring a high level of specificity and accuracy. Delilah employs these templates to construct phrases and sentences through a process named unification. This process involves verifying the compatibility of templates and subsequently integrating them into a coherent whole. For example, the template for "de" is designed to conjunctively operate with noun phrases, as illustrated in phrases like "de vrouw" ("the woman"). The operational mechanism of Delilah aligns with head-driven phrase structure grammar (HPSG), wherein words and grammar rules are treated equivalently. Delilah specifically implements a variant known as combinatory list grammar, which shares theoretical underpinnings with frameworks such as combinatory categorial grammar (CCG). The intricacies of Delilah's functioning have been extensively documented and analyzed in several scholarly articles. Within the MODOMA system, Delilah is deployed in its original configuration, without modifications.

The authors of a comprehensive volume on Empirical Software Engineering highlight the significant contributions of Victor R. Basili to the field of Empirical Software Engineering. They emphasize the critical importance of utilizing empirical data and evidence to enhance the efficacy of software engineering practices. Numerous studies have explored the application of Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE) within business contexts and its implications for agile development methodologies. These investigations have yielded valuable insights and strategies for implementation. However, they have not addressed the specific transitional phase explored in our study, which is situated between adoption and implementation. In this paper, we present the initial phase of a comprehensive framework designed to assist an organization in transitioning its codebase to a Software Product Line (SPL). We conducted tailored interviews with company stakeholders, taking into account the diverse roles within the project. Our objective was to identify optimal preparatory strategies for the transition, emphasizing inquiries related to current best practices, perceived benefits and risks, and stakeholder concerns. Addressing these risks and concerns, leveraging benefits, and promoting best practices are deemed essential for facilitating a seamless transition. Our findings indicate that the company already employs certain beneficial practices and possesses an innovation-friendly culture, which are advantageous for the project. The majority of stakeholders anticipate that the transition will yield positive outcomes. The primary concerns pertain to the complexity of SPL implementation and its sustainability.

We conducted a comprehensive evaluation of recent language models, encompassing both closed and open variants (refer to Appendix A.4 for detailed descriptions). Our methodology involved disabling any auxiliary cognitive or inferential functionalities to observe the models' intrinsic behaviors. This protocol was applicable to all models except for GPT-5 Mini, which permits modulation only of the depth of reasoning. For GPT-5 Mini, we configured this parameter to its minimal setting. Additionally, we performed a human-based comparative study across all tasks with slight modifications, as detailed in Appendix A.5. The results for human participants are illustrated on the left side of Figure 8. The primary objective of these experiments was to compare the language models against each other; the human data serves as a reference point. Extensive literature exists on human perception, including foundational studies by Jazayeri & Shadlen (2010) and Petzschner & Glasauer (2011). Most language models exhibit superior performance with textual stimuli compared to visual stimuli, with the exception of the maze distance estimation task. This task involves a lengthy and intricate textual description of paths, posing a greater complexity than the simpler texts utilized in other tasks (as depicted in Figure 2). In this context, most models underperform with textual inputs; however, GPT-5 Mini demonstrates near-optimal textual performance due to the inability to entirely disable its reasoning capabilities, highlighting task-dependent behavior. Generally, evidence for Bayesian behavior is more robust with visual inputs than with text (see Appendix A.11). Transitioning from single to multiple input modalities does not uniformly enhance performance, although certain models capitalize on the additional information effectively: Llama-4 Maverick achieves the highest performance across all tasks with multiple inputs, while Claude 3.7 Sonnet and GPT-4o show improvement in two out of three tasks.

High accuracy in task-specific evaluations does not necessarily indicate a model's proficiency in integrating multiple modalities of information (cf. Figure 8, middle panel). For instance, while the GPT-5 Mini exhibits a notably low error rate, it underperforms compared to several other models in effectively leveraging disparate types of input. This challenge becomes particularly pronounced when one input modality is significantly weaker than the other. Ideally, models should exhibit reduced dependence on weaker inputs; however, some models fail to adjust accordingly. This limitation is critical, as accuracy-centric evaluation might inadvertently favor models incapable of robustly handling noisy or imbalanced data. In contrast, Llama-4 Maverick demonstrates superior performance relative to the Bayesian reliability-weighted linear fusion baseline, suggesting its use of more advanced strategies for multimodal information integration. Its behavior aligns more closely with that of non-linear random forest models compared to simpler linear methods.   When comparing unimodal versus multimodal performance (e.g., tasks involving text and image inputs), models such as Llama-4 Maverick, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o exhibit notable reductions in errors across a majority of multimodal tasks. However, these improvements are not universal, and the variability in performance gains underscores the strides still necessary for advancing multimodal model efficacy. Comprehensive details regarding individual model behaviors can be found in Appendix A.11. To assess beyond accuracy alone, we propose the Bayesian Consistency Score (BCS), which provides a framework for evaluating models' logical adjustment to varying input conditions. This metric allows for a more nuanced assessment of model behavior, even in scenarios where baseline accuracy is already high. By systematically adjusting experimental conditions, the BCS facilitates comparative analyses of models with differing baseline performance levels, offering insights into how information integration is achieved. While more complex approaches, such as dynamically identifying salient details or employing rule-switching mechanisms, may better approximate patterns of response, prioritizing Bayesian-aligned behavior under shifting conditions offers a more interpretable framework for evaluating model adaptability and information processing.

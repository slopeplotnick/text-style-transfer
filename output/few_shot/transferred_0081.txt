Our method enhances the precision of actions, as demonstrated in simulation tests, and addresses issues related to policy failures resulting from suboptimal teleoperation data. During the Pen Grasping phase, variations in the operator's grasping techniques and timing lead to significant state disparities between grasping modes, resulting in a sparse observation-action distribution within the expert dataset. Consequently, the base policy frequently exhibits instability, such as improper gripper closure, reopening post-grasp, or oscillating between grasping modes. Our test-time scaling methodology predominantly ameliorates these issues by ensuring appropriate gripper closure and avoiding suboptimal modes, achieved through the filtering out of errant paths induced by noise in the teleoperated dataset. The challenge in learning the mapping from features to action errors arises from the model's necessity to comprehend both advantageous and deleterious features. During test-time scaling, the primary focus is on selecting the optimal action. Our experiments indicate that the observed decline in performance is attributed to overfitting on the training set, leading to diminished generalization. Employing discrete encoders in lieu of Internal Features and co-training with CFN results in highly similar features, complicating accurate pseudo-counting and causing substantial performance degradation. This method also compromises efficiency by not leveraging VLA's inherent comprehension of scenes and actions. In this study, we propose TACO, a test-time-scaling framework utilizing a straightforward pseudo-count estimator as a robust verifier for action sequences. TACO is compatible with various VLA models and consistently enhances the base policy across numerous real-world and simulated evaluations. Despite certain limitations, such as an inability to assess novel actions generated solely through perturbation and a reliance on the underlying VLA’s representational capacity, our approach demonstrates that simple test-time scaling can effectively curtail exploratory actions and mitigate instability and performance decline caused by distribution shifts in action prediction.

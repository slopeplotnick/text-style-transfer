Machine learning and generative techniques are pervasive, yet the application of AI for decision-making remains relatively uncommon. Decision-making necessitates the allocation of resources, a practice not typically aligned with academic research priorities. Presently, the most notable application of decision-making AI in mineral exploration is the delineation of the high-grade segment of the Mingomba deposit in Zambia. This involved reevaluating historical drilling data with contemporary analytical tools, leading to the identification of a more mineral-rich area than previously anticipated. Utilizing AI configured as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), the drilling process was strategically planned in stages. The primary objective was to employ AI to resolve uncertainties regarding geological hypotheses about the locations of rich mineral deposits. This method proved more effective than conventional grid-based drilling approaches by efficiently refuting geological hypotheses rather than merely locating minerals. In Part III, I will discuss how AI can expedite the discovery and comprehension of significant mineral deposits, beginning with data analysis and prediction, and subsequently transitioning to AI-based decision-making. Initial reactions to AI's introduction were marked by defensiveness, stemming from concerns over AI's potential to supplant human expertise. The apprehension that AI might independently drive novel discoveries is a prevalent issue. However, AI should be perceived as a supportive tool, facilitating experts by taking on tasks that are either beyond their proficiency or unnecessary, thereby allowing them to concentrate on innovative geological and geochemical reasoning.

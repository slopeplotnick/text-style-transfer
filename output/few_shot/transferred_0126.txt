Position bias in language models refers to the tendency of these systems to prioritize information found in specific segments of the input while neglecting other sections. Despite their advanced capabilities in language processing, these models remain susceptible to the arrangement of input data. For instance, in tasks involving the summarization of multiple documents amalgamated into a single input, the sequence of the documents can significantly influence the content included in the summary. Consequently, summaries may disproportionately emphasize certain sections, omitting other critical details based on the information's location. This phenomenon is exemplified by the "Lost in the Middle" problem, wherein researchers observed optimal model performance when pivotal information is positioned at the beginning or end of the input, with diminished efficacy when such information is situated in the middle. Such patterns have been documented in tasks like question answering or extracting key details from multiple documents.  Additionally, research indicates that models tasked with judgment or evaluation frequently exhibit a propensity to favor the initial or final options presented, even if superior alternatives exist elsewhere within the input. This is commonly referred to as lead bias, where input sequence initiations receive disproportionate attention. While models such as GPT-3.5-Turbo exhibit reduced susceptibility to this bias, others remain highly sensitive to early content, particularly in contexts where summaries are succinct. This type of bias not only adversely affects performance but can also result in fairness issues. For example, when tweets are categorized by dialect and systematically ordered, summaries may display bias towards the group appearing first. These observations underscore the significant impact that input order can exert.

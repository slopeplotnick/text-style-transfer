Editing videos of individuals presents significant challenges due to the necessity of altering attributes such as appearance, facial expressions, and additional elements, while maintaining coherence with the original footage. The primary difficulty lies in ensuring that the edited video aligns precisely with the timing and behavior of the source material. SyncLoRA addresses this challenge by employing a model that initiates modifications at the initial frame and propagates these changes throughout subsequent frames. To preserve synchronization, the model is trained using video pairs that exhibit identical movements but differ in appearance, selected through a process that assesses timing congruence. This training allows the model to integrate the original video's movements with alterations applied to the initial frame. Upon training with a curated dataset of human videos, SyncLoRA exhibits the capability to generalize to novel faces and diverse edits, such as modifying appearances, inserting elements, or altering backgrounds, even amid varying poses or expressions. Empirical results demonstrate that SyncLoRA produces aesthetically pleasing edits that remain temporally consistent with the original video. This method of video editing holds significant utility across industries such as advertising, filmmaking, gaming, and other media sectors where visual control is paramount. The complexity of editing videos involving people is underscored by the prevalence of human-centric media, necessitating edits that appear both natural and congruent with the original footage.

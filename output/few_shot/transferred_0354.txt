Creating virtual characters that move realistically is crucial for enhancing the realism of video games and virtual reality environments. Similarly, in robotics, naturalistic movement is essential for improving safety and energy efficiency. Human motion serves as a valuable source of inspiration for robots to acquire transferable skills applicable across diverse scenarios. To achieve realistic character animation, data-driven methodologies are commonly employed, leveraging real-world motion data. These approaches often utilize models such as autoregressive models, variational autoencoders, and diffusion models to generate plausible animations. However, such methods frequently fail to adhere to physical principles, resulting in unrealistic motions, particularly in unseen scenarios.   In contrast, physics-based methods prioritize generating movement trajectories consistent with the laws of physics by employing control policies and dynamic equations. Techniques like trajectory optimization and reinforcement learning are integral to these approaches. Earlier robotics methodologies, which did not incorporate data-driven insights, could produce functional movements but often resulted in unnatural and mechanical motions. Designing human-like robotic movement through heuristic approaches has proven challenging. Emerging methods increasingly adopt data-driven techniques, such as model predictive control and trajectory optimization, to better replicate human-like motion.  Recently, reinforcement learning-based frameworks have advanced the realism and agility of robotic and animated movements by imitating motion from real-world clips. However, such approaches are often constrained by the specific behaviors present in the imitation data, limiting their generalizability to novel tasks. To address these limitations, recent systems integrate specialized planners or high-level policy frameworks to select appropriate movements or skills dynamically, facilitating task-generalizable and adaptable motion generation.

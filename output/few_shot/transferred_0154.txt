Recent studies have increasingly adopted system prompts as a method to guide the behavior of large language models (LLMs) during deployment. Evidence suggests that carefully designed prompts can enhance the reasoning capabilities of such models and mitigate bias. However, existing research predominantly centers on English, with limited exploration into multilingual applicability. The challenge arises when prompts effective in English are inadequate when translated or applied to other languages, resulting in diminished accuracy, unstable reasoning, and inconsistent multilingual performance. This study addresses the critical question of designing prompts that exhibit efficacy and consistency across various languages and tasks.   To tackle this, we identified three primary research questions: (1) What types of prompts facilitate optimal model behavior across multiple languages? (2) Is it possible to discover effective prompts through automated methods, minimizing human intervention? (3) How do effective prompts influence reasoning abilities and linguistic consistency in responses? Our evaluation framework for prompts comprises four key metrics: average accuracy, variability in accuracy, consistency across languages, and variation in response length. By analyzing model performance across diverse tasks, we developed a methodology to enhance prompts for these objectives. Furthermore, we examined model outputs to assess how refined prompts improve reasoning and ensure consistent multilingual results.   An innovative aspect of our research is the introduction of an automated mechanism for optimizing multilingual prompts. Our extensive analysis of reasoning data revealed that well-constructed prompts encourage models to reason more systematically and coherently, reducing linguistic oscillation. These findings imply that reasoning processes significantly influence the multilingual effectiveness of LLMs. In sum, our work highlights that refining system prompts offers a promising yet underexplored avenue for achieving improved and more reliable outcomes from multilingual LLMs. The code and dataset are accessible at https://github.com/orange0629/multilingual-system-prompt.

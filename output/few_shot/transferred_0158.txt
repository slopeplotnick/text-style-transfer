Llama-4 Maverick demonstrates superior performance compared to a conventional method frequently employed in biological studies for information integration, known as the Bayesian reliability-weighted combiner—a method also utilized by humans. The enhanced performance of Llama-4 Maverick suggests the implementation of additional techniques beyond the traditional method. As depicted in Figure 7, a comparative analysis of Llama-4 Maverick's responses utilizing multimodal input versus unimodal input revealed that a nonlinear approach is more effective than a linear one. Under conditions of noise, the Bayesian method emerges as the optimal benchmark, serving as a standard for enhancing less proficient language models. Figure 8 illustrates that models exhibiting higher accuracy more closely align with the Bayesian method. Although models such as Gemma 3 4B and Phi 4 Multimodal exhibit lower accuracy, they perform adequately in this context. Our examination of various tasks and model performances is elaborated in Section A.9. The results indicate diverse behavioral patterns in language models when subjected to human-inspired perceptual tasks. Notably, more precise models like GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and Llama-4 Maverick provide substantial evidence of employing the Bayesian approach, particularly with visual stimuli (refer to Appendix A.11 for detailed analysis). These models adapt their operations to conform with the Bayesian framework when encountering variations such as noise or additional context, leveraging prior information without the necessity for specific training. This adaptation parallels human cognitive processes, where Bayesian models account for much of perceptual experience (Petzschner et al., 2015; Knill & Pouget, 2004) absent of explicit training. We acknowledge that our current investigation focuses on behavioral analysis, and further research is required to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of such behaviors.

Values for each prompt were designated in brackets, derived from a dataset of simulated participants. Each entry represented a fictional individual, integrating a mix of age, ethnicity, and gender attributes modeled after a UK sample from Prolific. Prolific classifies ages into categories, from which a random age was selected within each category for every simulated individual. An R script processed the AI-generated survey responses, subsequently storing them in a spreadsheet. Diverse responses were elicited via three distinct prompts. Following the methodology established by Hewitt et al. (2024), scores across different prompts were averaged for each question. Detailed descriptions of all prompt types are available in the Supplementary Materials of this article. Consideration was given to AI-generated responses that did not conform to the expected format, such as non-comma-separated values. These irregular responses were retained to emulate a genuine workflow and were treated as missing data, subsequently replaced by averaging the responses from other prompts. In Studies 1 and 2, existing data was employed using prompts such as "Pretend to be a [Ethnicity] [Gender] of [Age] years from the UK. Answer the questionnaire [...]". For Studies 3 and 4, new data was validated. The initial segment details the procedures for Studies 1 and 2, while the subsequent segment delineates the two-step process implemented in Studies 3 and 4. Hypothesis H3.1 was evaluated by examining the correlation between real and simulated scores using Spearman's correlation. Hypothesis H3.2 was assessed using the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC). Hypothesis H4 employed the Mann-Whitney U, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, and Levene's tests to compare data types. Hypothesis H5 was tested using the same multigroup Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) method as Hypothesis H2, but exclusively on simulated data, with gender as the grouping variable. All CFA and Multigroup CFA (M-CFA) models were estimated using the MLR estimator due to its robustness with non-normal data distributions (Hox et al., 2010).

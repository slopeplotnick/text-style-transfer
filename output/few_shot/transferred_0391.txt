To optimize multilingual models across various tasks, we exploit the inherent linguistic connections to facilitate efficient information sharing. In frameworks such as XLM-RoBERTa, languages that exhibit semantic or structural similarity, or possess visual orthographic resemblance, are positioned proximally in the model's representational space. We initiate our approach using the XLM-R model, which encompasses a comprehensive array of 100 languages, including both high-resource and low-resource languages. Our methodology does not require data for each language; rather, we train using a subset of predominant languages, leveraging this training to generalize across the entire linguistic spectrum. This strategy allows for cross-linguistic knowledge transfer by concentrating on a select few languages, thereby obviating the necessity of acquiring data for less common languages. The MultiJail dataset was translated into all 100 languages utilizing top-tier translation systems specific to each language. For high-resource languages, we employed advanced models such as GPT-4o, M2MBart-50, and Helsinki-NLP OpusMT. In the case of low-resource languages, where conventional models underperform, we implemented SarvamTranslate for Indic languages, providing effective performance even in rare language contexts, alongside GPT-4.1 for other rare languages. To ensure translation fidelity, select translations, particularly those in rare languages, were cross-verified using Google Translate. By integrating these models, we achieve translations that are fluent, of high quality, and exhibit a high degree of accuracy.

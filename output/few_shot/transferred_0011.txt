The AgentHarm test dataset comprises 176 examples derived from 44 tasks across 8 categories, including disinformation and fraud. Each task description can be adapted with two adjustable options: one provides a preliminary hint regarding the initial tool to use, while the other offers comprehensive step-by-step instructions. Combining these options results in four distinct versions of each task description. For experimental purposes, the focus is placed on the simplest configuration to minimize costs and evaluate the model’s capabilities, ensuring that the model's intrinsic capability is not a limiting factor and that the length of the context is primarily influencing performance (refer to Section A). AgentHarm employs both automated scoring and evaluation conducted by LLM judges, as detailed by Zheng et al. (2023). The evaluation encompasses two metrics: (1) Harm Score, ranging from 0 to 1, derived from rubrics for manually assessed harmful tasks; and (2) Refusal Rate, denoting the percentage of examples classified as refusals by the LLM model. Further elaboration is available in Section B. The primary focus is on the Harm Score, with semantic judging omitted on the assumption that the simplest tasks can be reliably evaluated. For refusal judging, minor modifications to the prompt and model are described in Section E. To rigorously examine context length, four padding types are utilized: random padding employs random tokens from the tokenizer; non-relevant padding incorporates text from fiction genres such as humor, mythology, sci-fi, crime, and romance that are unrelated to the task category; relevant padding includes text from Wikipedia aligned with the task category; and multi-task padding utilizes task descriptions from the validation dataset. These padding strategies serve distinct purposes: random padding aids reproducibility, non-relevant and relevant padding employ stylistically compatible text, and multi-task padding assesses the model’s ability to manage confounding context. Additional information is provided in Sections F and G.

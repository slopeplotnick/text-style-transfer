We evaluated our knowledge graph-based system through two distinct methodologies to assess the impact of data on generative models. Initially, we examined the effect of data removal from a proprietary trained model to determine if our approach could identify critical training instances. Subsequently, we investigated stylistic influences by implementing Ghibli-style imagery in scenarios where model transparency is limited. A "Ghibli World" knowledge graph, derived from original cinematic works, was constructed and contrasted with stylized images. These evaluations facilitate insights into both direct data influence and stylistic traceability within generative models. To ensure an equitable and comprehensible comparison, we replicated a contemporary method designed to ascertain influence without requiring access to training specifics. This method operates in two phases: identifying training examples that closely align with user preferences, and ranking them by resemblance to the generated output using cosine similarity. The most analogous training images are subsequently excluded, and the model is retrained to evaluate their significance. We employed our graph-based approach analogously, utilizing identical data, prompts, and model specifications. Rather than relying on text and hidden-space similarity, we identified pivotal examples based on knowledge graph similarity, enabling a direct comparative analysis of the two methods in terms of influence detection and comprehension. For the generative model, we employed a compact model based on the open-source dalle-pytorch package, trained on a corpus of 44,000 fashion images with corresponding captions. The original configuration and parameters were maintained to ensure complete control over the training data, thus facilitating robust experimentation with data removal and retraining.

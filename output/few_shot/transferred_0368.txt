The complexity of a problem is inherently linked to the degree of difficulty associated with it. However, both intra-family and inter-family comparisons demonstrate a significant positive correlation with improvements in verifier systems. Notably, AIME emerges as a distinct case in the final depiction, potentially due to analogous challenges encountered by certain models post-training. The trend lines representing verifier accuracy reveal two distinct clusters of tasks (indicated in red and blue), suggesting that the verification difficulty is not solely contingent upon solver accuracy. This observation prompts the hypothesis that certain tasks inherently possess a higher ease of verification. Song et al. (2025) observed that while models struggle to enhance performance on tasks necessitating factual recall, they do exhibit improvement on Tokoku given adequate pretraining. A parallel bifurcation is evident in Figure 7, where tasks such as AIME, GSM8K, 3SAT, and Tokoku exhibit a higher verifier-to-solver accuracy ratio and outperform across various verification contexts. In explicating this phenomenon, we observe that within our synthetic datasets, Selenium and 3SAT exemplify problems characterized by protracted solving times juxtaposed with rapid verification processes. Conversely, Matrix Multiplication exemplifies a task where result verification essentially necessitates a repeat of the computational process. Within the realm of real-world datasets, tasks such as GSM8K and AIME are solvable via high-school level mathematics, whereas MMLU (Social Sciences) demands domain-specific expertise, CommonsenseQA is reliant on general world knowledge, and tasks like GPQA and MMLU (STEM) require specialized scientific expertise. For these latter tasks, verification demands a commensurate level of knowledge as required for problem-solving. Key insights include the fact that while tasks with straightforward solutions are generally more facile to verify, certain tasks are intrinsically predisposed to easier verification. These encompass fabricated problems necessitating logical or structured reasoning (such as 3SAT, Sudo), and real-world tasks predominantly grounded in mathematical reasoning rather than extensive factual recall (such as GSM8K, AIME). Furthermore, these tasks derive substantial benefits from the implementation of test-time rejection sampling with verifiers.

We introduce a novel methodology, termed Modal Input Guidance and Reasoning (MIGR), which leverages Mutual Information (MI) to prioritize the type of input initially addressed. The objective is to mitigate biases inherent in the initial reasoning phase of systems and to ensure that focus is directed towards input types with the most pronounced emotional signals. The methodology comprises two primary phases. The initial phase, referred to as Modality-Aligned Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), facilitates the system’s learning process to begin reasoning by concentrating on the input type that MI identifies as exhibiting the strongest emotional signals. The subsequent phase, known as Modality-Aware Reward Optimization, ensures adherence to the appropriate input type and maintains an emotionally grounded reasoning process, while simultaneously enhancing the stability of the training procedure. This is achieved through the deployment of two reward mechanisms: one promoting the prioritization of correct input order and another supporting emotion-based reasoning. Subsequent sections will elaborate on these phases in detail. Recent studies demonstrate the efficacy of SFT in emotion comprehension tasks, even with limited high-quality training data such as the EMER dataset. Consequently, we initiate our training with a comparable strategy. Despite the robustness of the EMER dataset, we enhance the SFT process by incorporating additional examples that elucidate consistent emotional signals. This enhancement involves augmenting the training dataset with supplementary examples identified through an analysis of Facial Action Units (FAUs) extracted from video frames, which are intricately associated with emotional expression. We then validate the congruence of the FAU patterns with the target emotion using a FAU-emotion mapping table, retaining only those examples with exact matches. For these selected instances, reasoning-answer pairs are constructed, leveraging the emotional evidence from the FAUs to steer the reasoning process.

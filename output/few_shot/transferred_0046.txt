Our evaluation of WorldMM utilized five benchmarks to assess its efficacy in responding to inquiries regarding extensive videos, ranging from hours to weeks in duration. The proposed method outperformed existing robust approaches, including models specifically designed for long videos and those incorporating additional memory features. We conducted comprehensive experiments to demonstrate the efficacy of our design, which integrates diverse memory types. Specifically, episodic memory allows the model to consider temporally dispersed events, visual memory enhances its capacity to address queries concerning objects and actions, and semantic memory aids in comprehending long-term contextual information. The strategic integration of these memory types culminates in superior overall model performance, signifying a substantial advancement in the comprehension of long videos. While models tailored for short videos exhibit proficient performance, recent research has increasingly focused on the challenge of longer video durations. Current state-of-the-art models, such as GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5, manage videos lasting minutes to hours by employing extended context lengths. However, these models are resource-intensive and inefficient when every frame is treated uniformly, particularly concerning queries about specific events. Various approaches have been employed to address these issues. Compressing visual tokens enhances processing speed by reducing token count, albeit at the risk of overlooking minor details or infrequent events. Alternatively, selecting key frames mitigates information redundancy but often fails to accurately identify crucial frames in extended videos, potentially overlooking significant moments. Approaches that train models to prioritize reasoning and deploy more sophisticated testing mechanisms have improved video comprehension, yet remain inadequate for videos exceeding ten hours. For videos extending over days or weeks, new benchmarks are challenging models to accommodate continuous recordings over prolonged periods. The aforementioned methods are inadequate for such videos due to the excessive number of frames and the intricate dependencies between temporal events. This underscores the necessity for more effective approaches capable of efficiently handling very long videos, with a particular emphasis on context awareness and scalability.

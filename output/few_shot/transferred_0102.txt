The present investigation does not address the origins of the methodologies. Hallucination Detection (HD) techniques depend on internal model states, which Fixed Verification (FV) settings are unable to provide. Attempts to resolve this limitation have involved the development of static benchmarks for Large Language Model (LLM) outputs, such as HELM; however, these benchmarks are inadequate for evaluating novel or atypical models and cannot effectively monitor shifts in generative signals. This discrepancy has led to the divergence of two distinct research trajectories, resulting in duplicated efforts and unanswered critical questions. The field lacks clarity on whether one approach surpasses the other, how their respective strengths differ, or if they can be synergistically integrated. The segregation also precludes the combined utilization of their advantages: HD's internal signals could enhance FV's evidence-based reasoning, and FV's contextual grounding could bolster HD's uncertainty signals.  In response to this challenge, we have undertaken a significant initiative to address this schism. We have executed the inaugural large-scale study that directly compares Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification within a unified framework. This undertaking transcends mere benchmarking, offering an in-depth examination of the interconnections between these methodologies. By integrating these approaches, we aim to resolve three pivotal inquiries: (1) How do HD and FV perform in identifying factual inaccuracies in identical LLM outputs? (2) Do HD and FV target disparate error categories, thereby exhibiting complementary strengths, or do they converge on the same failure points? (3) Is it feasible to amalgamate signals from both methodologies to create a hybrid system superior to each individually?  The feasibility of this comparison was previously doubted due to the divergence between the two approaches, yet we have enabled it through the development of UniFact, a framework designed to reconcile the incompatibilities that have historically divided the field. Unlike static LLM outputs, UniFact employs a dynamic process, soliciting any LLM to answer factual queries in real-time. Subsequently, an automated system verifies the accuracy of the answers by juxtaposing them with correct answers and pre-labeled reference knowledge. This dynamic architecture is pivotal, as it furnishes both the outputs requisite for FV and the internal signals essential for HD, thus allowing for a direct, side-by-side comparison of the two methodologies on identical content.

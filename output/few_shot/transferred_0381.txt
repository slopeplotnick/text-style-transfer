Our proposed method has been optimized to operate efficiently with contemporary systems capable of processing large volumes of data at high speeds, such as vLLM and SGLang. It is designed for applicability in both live services and offline data processing contexts. In Section 4.2, we primarily utilize the two-step vLLM configuration due to its high processing speed and streamlined approach to handling reasoning tasks.   The evaluation of THINKMERGE was conducted across two distinct task types. For tasks requiring a single correct answer, we assessed performance on datasets such as AIME 2025 and GPQA Diamond, where majority voting was employed to aggregate results. For tasks with open-ended solutions, we evaluated THINKMERGE on datasets including LiveCodeBench v5, BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and xBench-DeepSearch, which emphasize generating a single high-quality output. For the single-answer tasks, five experimental trials were conducted using models such as Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-14B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B. In the coding domain, which involves open-ended tasks, models such as DeepCoder-14B-Preview, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-Coder-30A3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Thinking, and Qwen3-Think-30A3B were employed. Similarly, for research-oriented open-ended tasks, we utilized the WebSailor model suite, including WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B. These experiments were conducted with a maximum sequence length of 32,768 tokens, with details regarding sampling configurations provided in Appendix A.1.  Additionally, we tested THINKMERGE in research scenarios requiring reasoning and tool utilization prior to generating responses. Specifically, three models from the Tongyi-WebSailor series—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B—were evaluated on four challenging web-based benchmarks: BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and xBenchDeepSearch. BrowseComp-en and BrowseComp-zh are designed to address complex factual queries in English and Chinese, respectively. For BrowseComp-en, which includes a total of 1,266 questions, a subset of 200 questions was randomly selected to align its size with other datasets.

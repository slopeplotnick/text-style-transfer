The individual is engaged in the field of artificial intelligence within the technology sector and serves as the director of the Machine Learning Center at Georgia Institute of Technology. Holding an esteemed chair position in advanced computing, he is recognized as a leading researcher in the state of Georgia. He earned his Doctorate in Electrical Engineering from Georgia Tech and holds the distinguished title of IEEE Fellow. His professional experience includes positions at organizations such as SRI International, Nuance, Yahoo!, Microsoft, Google, Viv Labs, and Samsung. His areas of expertise encompass fields such as acoustics, noise control, speaker recognition, web search, AI assistants, natural language processing, and conversational AI. This tutorial is designed for individuals engaged in the study or development of conversational agents and spoken information systems. It is intended for participants with a foundational understanding of natural language processing (NLP) and speech processing. Prior experience in system deployment is advantageous; however, the tutorial will commence at a fundamental level to ensure accessibility for all attendees. The following papers are deemed pertinent for this tutorial:   - Stolcke et al. (2000) on tagging and recognizing conversational speech - Moore (2017) on the non-binary nature of spoken language and its implications for human-machine interaction - Liu et al. (2018) on teaching and feedback mechanisms in task-based dialogue systems - Yang et al. (2021) on the application of acoustic models for time-series classification - Chen et al. (2023a) on generative speech recognition utilizing large language models - Zhang et al. (2023) on SpeechGPT and its role in bridging conversational capabilities across diverse modalities  While the tutorial will reference numerous scholarly works, the focus will be on 7-8 principal papers, with only 1-2 authored by the presenter.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly utilized in applications such as Google's AI-generated summaries, where information from extensive documents is synthesized. However, concerns persist about whether LLMs weigh all input equally during the summarization process. For instance, researchers investigating news coverage on abortion constructed 40 sets of three articles (one pro-abortion, one neutral, and one anti-abortion), randomized the article order in six distinct ways, and tasked Gemini 2.5 Flash with generating neutral summaries. These summaries were evaluated for alignment with the original articles using metrics that measure word overlap, semantic similarity, and factual consistency. Results indicated a systematic bias toward the first article in the sequence, with the BERTScore metric highlighting a predominant influence of the first document compared to the second and third, which exerted roughly equal impact. This suggests that the initial document disproportionately affects the summarization output, posing potential challenges for systems reliant on these summaries or for AI systems deriving decisions from them.  The rapid development of generative AI, a foundational component of these systems, has enabled advancements in numerous domains. Agentic AI systems, in particular, are engineered to autonomously address complex tasks with minimal human intervention. These systems decompose overarching objectives into smaller, manageable steps, incorporating external information when necessary to guide their decision-making processes. Consequently, the performance of such systems is contingent on the ability of their underlying LLMs to accurately identify, prioritize, and integrate information from instructions and external sources into coherent outputs.

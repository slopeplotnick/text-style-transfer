In our study, we investigated the application of GRAPHECTORY in autonomous systems, concentrating on two software engineering agents, SWE-agent and OpenHands. These systems undergo processes such as planning, bug detection, code correction, and verification of these corrections, all of which are quantifiable. Our experimental framework involved testing four language models—DeepSeek-V3, DeepSeek-R1, Devstral-small-2505, and Claude Sonnet 4—on real-world challenges sourced from 12 GitHub repositories within the SWE-Bench Verified benchmark. The findings indicate that failures in these systems lead to increased time consumption and inefficiency. Specifically, GRAPHECTORY reveals that unsuccessful attempts generate larger and more redundant procedural paths compared to successful ones. Challenges of higher complexity compel the systems to exert more effort, often employing diverse strategies to advance. While systems generally adhere to their primary objectives, they exhibit adaptability by modifying tactics, particularly when addressing difficult problems, which results in additional time spent on exploration and rectification. Despite successful outcomes, the systems demonstrated suboptimal efficiency, suggesting the necessity for an emphasis on operational methodologies over mere success rates. We elucidate the mechanics of GRAPHECTORY and employ it to develop metrics for evaluating these systems. Additionally, to enhance comprehensibility, we introduce LANGUTORY, a framework that distills complex information into a more accessible format. While GRAPHECTORY provides extensive insights into system operations, its complexity can hinder comparative analyses across systems or problems, thereby necessitating the summarization capabilities of LANGUTORY.

The public discourse surrounding AI-generated music encompasses a range of sentiments, even in cases where the identity of the composer is unknown. These sentiments echo concerns prevalent in music and technology research, such as potential economic impacts, issues of intellectual property ownership, and cultural ramifications. Nonetheless, there is a palpable interest in the underlying technology and its mechanisms. This blend of enthusiasm and apprehension may elucidate the varied opinions on AI composers. As AI technology advances, perceptions of AI-generated music may shift, with what was once perceived as innovative potentially being viewed as problematic or even threatening.   The study in question faced several limitations: firstly, the participant pool comprised university students and individuals recruited via the Prolific platform, who experienced the music through different modalities, thereby complicating the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, participants from Prolific may possess greater familiarity with technology and AI. Secondly, a subset of participants was informed that the music resembled film soundtracks, which may have biased their evaluations positively, given the general favorable reception of movie music. Lastly, the study exclusively utilized instrumental music, which restricts the applicability of the findings to compositions with lyrics. Previous research indicates that lyrics can amplify the emotional impact of sad songs, yet they exert minimal influence on the perceived happiness of songs. Given that the majority of the music in this study was characterized as happy, the absence of lyrics likely did not significantly alter the emotional responses of participants. The decision to exclude lyrics was made to avoid potential confounding effects and due to the unavailability of high-quality AI-generated vocalizations at the time the musical samples were produced.

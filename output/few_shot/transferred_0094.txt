The study evaluates the performance of CFAULTs and HSD methodologies. Figure 6a illustrates a comparative analysis, presenting CPU time against the number of circuits identified using CFAULTs, HSD, and HSD-CM in a specified test scenario. The data indicate that HSD operates with greater efficiency and identifies a higher number of faults, corroborating the results shown in Table 5. Figures 6b, 6c, and 6d provide further insights, detailing the time efficiency of CFAULTs and HSD. Specifically, Figure 6b represents each faulty circuit as a point, with its x-coordinate indicating HSD's processing time and its y-coordinate indicating CFAULTs's time. A position above the line suggests that CFAULTs required more time than HSD to diagnose the issue. These visualizations collectively demonstrate that HSD outperforms CFAULTs in speed, irrespective of the use of core minimization. In contrast, when analyzing faulty C software with the TCAS and C-PACK-IPAs tests, CFAULTs exhibit superiority over FBFL techniques. Despite consolidating all failed tests into a singular formula, CFAULTs surpass alternative methods, displaying enhanced speed and fault detection capability. Therefore, the findings suggest that CFAULTs is optimal for C software diagnostics, whereas HSD remains superior for Boolean circuit analysis. This discrepancy arises from the inherent differences in fault identification between C programs and Boolean circuits, resulting in varied formula structures which, in turn, affect solver efficiency and performance across these domains.

The methodology employed involved Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) utilizing the Qwen-1.5 model as the primary framework, facilitating the acquisition of general language skills and their application to e-commerce tasks. The data employed consisted of knowledge graphs, augmented with domain-specific information through meticulously designed prompts, to enable the model's comprehension of e-commerce-related patterns and interrelations. Model performance evaluation was conducted using standard tests and relevance metrics to ensure accuracy and the capability to process diverse query types. Following training, the model was deployed for labeling tasks, enhancing both speed and quality by identifying errors and improving data quality. Additionally, it addressed more complex tasks such as parsing intricate queries, discerning hidden meanings, and executing logical reasoning for pathway construction. Given the insufficiency of simple instructions for these tasks, the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methodology was adopted. This approach enabled the model to decompose problems, discern relationships, and evaluate pathways iteratively, thereby enhancing its proficiency in managing complex queries. A system was also established to identify challenging cases and refine the model through feedback. Subsequently, CoT data was utilized for tasks such as attribute extraction and matching, followed by re-training the model employing the SFT method with Qwen-2, which improved its capacity to handle reasoning sequences while maintaining task specificity.

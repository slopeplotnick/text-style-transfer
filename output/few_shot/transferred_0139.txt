Utilizing a large-scale language model (LLM) or a deep BERT classifier for online search applications poses significant challenges in terms of computational efficiency and cost. To address these issues, we implement a distillation-based approach, where a simpler model is trained to mimic the behavior of a more complex one, as illustrated in Figure 1. Specifically, our online model leverages a lightweight version of BERT, while the more complex BERT model serves as a teacher to facilitate the learning process. Notably, instead of employing a heterogeneous teacher-student framework, we utilize the deep BERT model as the teacher to enhance the alignment between the student and teacher models.  To further bolster the learning process, we integrate relevant features identified by an auxiliary model into the teacher BERT model. These features provide additional context for improving the model's understanding of how certain search queries correlate with products. To address the issue of noise and redundant information in extended explanations, a separate model is employed to extract and refine salient product details that contribute to or detract from search relevance. This pre-processing step enables the lightweight BERT model to focus on critical information. Importantly, due to the real-time constraints of the simplified model, the additional features are incorporated into the teacher model alone to maintain consistency in the training framework. The student model is then trained to replicate the predictions made by the teacher model.  Given the absence of publicly available datasets for Chinese e-commerce search scenarios, we conducted experiments using a large-scale, proprietary dataset derived from JD app search logs.

The exploration of varying noise patterns in flow-matching or diffusion configurations reveals that the initial noise randomness significantly impacts the resultant actions. This study addresses the challenge of adapting to shifts in data distribution by minimizing excessive exploration and introduces Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-Counts (TACO). The concept of anti-exploration originates from offline reinforcement learning, aiming to restrict the policy's actions to those present in the dataset. Similarly, during Vision-and-Language Action (VLA) inference, it is essential to confine actions within the successful patterns of the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) dataset and avoid exploring unnecessary or irrelevant actions derived from pretraining or suboptimal fine-tuning data. Within the TACO framework, the focus is placed on adapting action sampling during testing, rather than altering the policy, as the intricate sampling mechanisms in flow-matching or diffusion-based VLA models are not amenable to conventional reinforcement learning updates. The proposed Test-time Sampling (TTS) modifies action sampling without altering the VLA's parameters, thus obviating the need for gradient computation. A traditional Coin-Flipping Network (CFN) with a specialized internal architecture is employed to estimate pseudo-counts for each observation-instruction-action component within the SFT dataset. Essentially, an action component with a higher pseudo-count is more aligned with the commonly successful actions in the SFT data, rendering it preferable. In decision-making, TACO utilizes the pseudo-counts generated by the CFN to select the optimal action component from multiple options, prioritizing the one with the highest pseudo-count. To enhance processing efficiency across multiple tests, a shared observation key-value cache is integrated, facilitating expedited processing by reusing visual-language information across samples.

The study investigates seven approaches for combining models to mitigate social bias, utilizing three bias evaluation tests and the SuperGLUE benchmark across 13 large language models (LLMs). The findings indicate that Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) with moderate parameter settings is the most effective method for reducing bias while maintaining accuracy. The applicability of these methods to tasks such as reading comprehension and reasoning is deemed crucial for bias mitigation. Recent advancements highlight model combination as an efficient strategy to leverage the strengths of multiple pre-trained models without requiring additional retraining (Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024). This process involves the integration of components from two or more previously trained models to produce a single model that retains and merges the knowledge of each constituent model. Linear averaging, introduced by Wortsman et al. (2022), represents a foundational approach for this task, wherein the model weights are averaged. This method is computationally efficient, as it capitalizes on pre-trained models without the need for expensive re-training.   Further advancements in model integration have been proposed by Matena and Raffel (2022) and Lee et al. (2025), who refine the process by weighting the importance of individual components, ensuring a semantically coherent combination. Alternative techniques, such as sphere-based interpolation methods, have also been explored. Sphere interpolation, initially conceptualized by Shoemake (1985) and later expanded upon by Goddard et al. (2024), views model components as points on a spherical manifold. SLERP, developed by Shoemake (1985), facilitates the merging of two models, while the Karcher Mean, introduced by Grove and Karcher (1973), computes the average across multiple models. A notable extension to SLERP is NuSLERP, proposed by Goddard et al. (2024), which incorporates an additional step to address potential alterations in model scale during interpolation. Task-specific adaptations of these methods have also been presented by Ilharco et al. (2022), leveraging the concept of "task vectors" to capture the differential between a pre-trained model and its fine-tuned counterpart, thereby enabling robust task-oriented model integration.

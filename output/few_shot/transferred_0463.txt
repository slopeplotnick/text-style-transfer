Large language models (LLMs) demonstrate significant versatility in performing a wide range of tasks. These models can be fine-tuned for specific functionalities through the use of instructions and examples, wherein the construction of these instructions plays a crucial role in determining task performance. For instance, experimental evidence reveals that minor variations in instructional phrasing for mathematical problem-solving tasks can lead to divergent performance outcomes. This emphasizes the importance of developing clear and carefully designed instructions, akin to the way hyperparameter tuning optimizes machine learning algorithms. However, manual crafting of high-quality instructions can be both labor-intensive and inconsistent.   In this context, automatic prompt optimization has emerged as a viable alternative, analogous to AutoML, which automates the discovery of optimal settings in machine learning workflows. Automatic prompt optimization is particularly valuable in scenarios where models are opaque ("black-box") or when modifying entire models is computationally expensive and requires extensive data resources. This approach has gained prominence with the increasing adoption of multi-agent systems in industrial and research applications.   Although various prompt optimization tools are available, their practical application poses challenges. Each optimizer is typically hosted on distinct platforms, requiring users to navigate differing implementation codes and dependencies. Furthermore, these tools often lack regular maintenance, robust documentation, and comprehensive testing frameworks, resulting in limited usability and adaptability. Additionally, many existing solutions are tailored for basic tasks and necessitate considerable effort to extend their functionality to more complex use cases. While certain tools aim to address these limitations, they may come with their own constraints, such as being commercial and closed-source, offering support for only a single optimizer, or being overly intricate for routine usage.

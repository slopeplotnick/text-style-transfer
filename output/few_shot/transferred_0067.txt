Large Language Models (LLMs) typically outperform on a broad spectrum of tasks due to their size and ability to effectively follow instructions. However, Smaller Language Models (SLMs) demonstrate comparable or even superior performance on specific tasks, such as predicting depression and stress (Xu et al., 2024). While SLMs have shown significant improvement in various domains, they still lag behind LLMs in certain areas. The efficacy of SLMs is contingent upon alignment between task requirements and the model's linguistic processing capabilities. A notable limitation of SLMs is their inefficiency with extended inputs, as they struggle with managing a high number of tokens and maintaining coherence in lengthy or complex contexts (Kumar, 2025). Consequently, for tasks necessitating comprehension of intricate narratives, LLMs remain more adept (Lu et al., 2024). The findings indicate the potential of focusing on task-specific enhancements rather than merely increasing model size for applications in mental-health AI. As factors such as efficiency, transparency, safety, and privacy gain prominence in clinical machine learning (Zhang et al., 2021), the trade-off between performance and practicality will be pivotal in shaping the evolution of digital mental-health tools. SLMs are already fulfilling the demand for tools that facilitate immediate and confidential detection of mental-health conditions (Harari et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2018), aligning with the healthcare sector's drive for more transparent and accountable AI systems (Wies et al., 2021; Smith et al., 2023). This research reveals that fine-tuned SLMs, when optimized with multi-source data strategies, can match or exceed LLMs in forecasting mental-health outcomes. This suggests a viable path towards smaller, more targeted models as a sustainable and ethical alternative for mental health monitoring in practical settings. The present study identifies the optimal tasks for SLMs, demonstrating their effectiveness in predicting depression, stress, and suicidal ideation, particularly when enhanced with advanced loss function design. This aligns with findings by Yao et al. (Benton et al., 2017), which suggest that leveraging shared patterns across related mental-health tasks can improve outcomes. In contrast to their multi-task framework, our approach achieves similar advantages through a more efficient fine-tuning strategy (LoRA), which requires less computational resources. This underscores the potential for even compact, efficient models to capitalise on diverse data when appropriately calibrated.

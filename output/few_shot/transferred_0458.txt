When analyzing images, it is crucial to consider aspects such as concealed joints and the holistic context of the scene, specifically the relationships among joints, hands, and objects. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are commonly employed for 3D hand pose estimation, with ResNet-50 being a particularly favored architecture due to its effective balance between accuracy and computational efficiency. However, CNNs primarily rely on localized operations, which limits their ability to capture broader contextual information. Recent advancements have explored the application of vision transformers (ViTs) for hand mesh reconstruction tasks, which offer improved capabilities for modeling global features but often suffer from computational inefficiencies, thereby leaving significant scope for optimization in 3D hand pose understanding.  Emerging approaches aim to address these challenges by leveraging transformer-based architectures originally developed for natural language processing tasks, such as Mamba, which is highly adept at focusing on salient components within input sequences to comprehend global contexts. Some studies have adapted this framework for visual tasks, introducing models such as VisionMamba and VMamba. VisionMamba utilizes specialized spatial sequence modeling blocks, while VMamba incorporates blocks with diverse scanning pathways to enhance performance. Despite these innovations, both approaches operate on fixed grids, which inherently limit their ability to capture intricate 3D hand movements.  To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel system, Deformable Mamba (DF-Mamba), which introduces a flexible framework for enhanced visual feature extraction in 3D hand pose estimation. DF-Mamba dynamically models both local and global features using adaptable state spaces. Specifically, it employs blocks that aggregate local features along deformable pathways and retain critical information required to comprehend global contexts. The scanning pathways adapt dynamically through the use of deformable point sampling, incorporating local anchors and adjustable offsets derived from input feature representations. This flexible design facilitates improved capture of detailed hand movement patterns in 3D, advancing the state-of-the-art in hand pose estimation methodologies.

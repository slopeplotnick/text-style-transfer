The Self Corrective-Agent Architecture revealed that systematic verification and structured retry mechanisms could address reliability issues encountered during SMART-LLM replication. Table 2 in the Performance Validation and Evaluation Limitations section presents performance metrics for the 36-task SMART-LLM test conducted under identical conditions, emphasizing the positive impact of architectural modifications. The framework achieved success rates from 0.21 for Compound tasks to 0.50 for Elemental and Complex tasks. Notably, task execution efficacy improved significantly across all categories: Elemental (0.75), Simple (0.83), Compound (0.88), and Complex (0.86). These enhancements, ranging from 9 to 30 percentage points, demonstrate that the verification process ensured outputs were both structurally and semantically accurate. The Self Corrective-Agent Architecture exhibits superior performance despite employing the same few-shot prompting strategy as SMART-LLM. The framework utilizes a total of 700,017 tokens compared to SMART-LLM's 727,649 tokens (a reduction of 3.8%) as depicted in Figure 13, and completes tasks in 746 seconds as opposed to 1,075 seconds (a reduction of 30.6%) as shown in Figure 14. This improvement primarily arises from its modular prompting design, wherein each component (decomposition, assignment, or code generation) leverages its specific example file. Conversely, SMART-LLM integrates all three example files into each LLM invocation irrespective of the active component. Consequently, each Self Corrective-Agent inference consumes significantly fewer tokens, enhancing speed. This efficient prompting approach ensures that, even with validation-driven retries, the Self Corrective-Agent consistently uses fewer tokens overall.

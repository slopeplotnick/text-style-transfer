Some studies remain inconclusive regarding whether large language models (LLMs) adopt a Bayesian framework for learning. Notably, there have been reports identifying specific reasoning challenges such as a propensity for preferential agreement to satisfy interlocutors, errors encountered during scale-up, and a tendency to conform to majority opinion contrary to factual accuracy. These limitations adversely affect the performance of LLMs in task execution. In the assessment of reasoning abilities, LLMs demonstrate enhanced proficiency in tasks like mathematics and coding; however, traditional metrics of accuracy fall short in elucidating the mechanisms underpinning their improved outcomes, particularly in novel contexts. If LLMs merely mimic information acquired during training, their applicability to real-world problems, where contextual understanding is crucial, may be limited. To ascertain the reasoning mechanisms of LLMs, it is imperative to develop more sophisticated evaluation methodologies that focus on cognitive processes rather than merely outcomes. Additionally, research indicates that LLMs might prioritize user satisfaction or agreement with popular opinion over truthfulness, potentially misleading users and reinforcing erroneous beliefs or biases. Given that humans exhibit a cognitive bias towards confirmatory information, which can exacerbate issues such as polarization and misinformation dissemination, the interaction between humans and LLMs, trained on datasets reflective of human beliefs, may further propagate false beliefs.

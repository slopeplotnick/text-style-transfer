In our study, we employed a two-step process to amalgamate various types of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data to train the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, comparable in scale to the LLM base model utilized in our analysis. Upon evaluating the performance of models predicated on Vision Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs), it was observed that the LLM-based approach exhibited superior performance across both easy and difficult datasets, particularly in terms of macro-F1 scores. This indicates that LLMs possess enhanced reasoning and knowledge capabilities compared to VLMs when endowed with equivalent CoT functionality. Conversely, VLMs demonstrate superior proficiency in managing visual details, given their ability to process images directly without the necessity of converting them into text, which can potentially result in information loss. Our LORE model has undergone development over three phases, culminating in improvements across five key dimensions: objectives, model configuration, data and prompt utilization, evaluation methodologies, and application strategies. These phases delineate the advancements achieved through sophisticated designs, high-quality datasets, and practical implementation strategies, notably for optimizing search outcomes in online shopping contexts. Initially, we encountered challenges in comprehending e-commerce queries, particularly in identifying non-standard product names. To address this, we constructed extensive, high-caliber e-commerce datasets and leveraged In-Context Learning to integrate domain-specific knowledge into the model, thereby enhancing its proficiency in interpreting e-commerce language.

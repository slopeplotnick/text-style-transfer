In our study, we employed identical adversarial training methodologies on both Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs) to evaluate the impact of such training on the transferability of adversarial attacks. Contrary to the intuitive assumption that models trained for robustness would generate attacks with limited transferability, our findings indicate the opposite. Adversarially trained models facilitate the generation of attacks that transfer more effectively compared to those trained under standard conditions. This implies that the features learned by these models may introduce vulnerabilities applicable across varied model architectures, as illustrated in Figure 1. Specifically, we conducted comprehensive adversarial training on a total of 36 CNN and ViT models. All trained models and the associated code will be made available on GitHub for replication and further investigation. Our results challenge prevailing assumptions concerning model robustness, highlighting the enhanced transferability of adversarially trained model attacks.  Additionally, we introduce a novel attack mechanism termed Momentum Integrated Gradient (MIG), which extends the integrated gradient (IG) approach. MIG incorporates a momentum-based iterative process guided by IG, which effectively targets critical features as identified by IG, thereby increasing the likelihood of misclassification. A pivotal attribute of IG is implementation invariance, enabling MIG attacks to operate across diverse model architectures, such as CNNs and ViTs, or between the two, a capability not feasible with traditional attacks like Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), as shown in Figure 2. The IG method itself is designed to elucidate the features most influential to a model's predictions, accomplished through the summation of gradients from an initial reference point to the actual input.

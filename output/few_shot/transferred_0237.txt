Conference details are provided on the official website. E-commerce platforms such as Amazon, Taobao, and JD.com have significantly transformed consumer shopping behaviors. A critical component driving user satisfaction on these platforms is the relevance of displayed products, as the presentation of irrelevant items can erode user trust and reduce revenue. Consequently, the assessment of product relevance to user search queries is a fundamental aspect of e-commerce operations.  Traditional methods employed in assessing relevance rely on word-matching and vector-based techniques. Word-matching methods, such as TF-IDF and BM25, evaluate the similarity between queries and products based on lexical matches; however, these approaches often fail to capture underlying semantic meaning and contextual relationships. On the other hand, vector-based methods leverage tools like BERT to encode semantic representations of queries and products, thereby improving relevance through contextual understanding. Despite these advancements, two major challenges persist in applying vector-based approaches to domain-specific tasks: (1) insufficient hard examples for domain-specific fine-tuning, as existing methods perform reasonably well with labeled data but require a substantial volume of challenging examples for further enhancement—examples that are difficult to source and annotate accurately—and (2) limitations in reasoning and interpretability, as smaller, online models often lack the capacity for advanced reasoning or the ability to effectively highlight relevance-driving features.  To address these challenges, large language models (LLMs) have increasingly been employed in relevance-related tasks. While LLMs exhibit enhanced reasoning abilities, the prevailing approach involves determining relevance based on the last token of the output and employing soft-label distillation to train smaller models. This approach, however, fails to provide clear insights into the specific features influencing relevance and does little to improve the reasoning capabilities of the smaller models.

Recent studies, such as those referenced in [25] and [15], have developed methodologies to evaluate the moral reasoning of AI models, employing tools like MoralBench. These investigations reveal that, although AI models can occasionally comprehend ethical concepts, they often struggle with complex moral dilemmas or when confronted with conflicting ethical principles. However, these evaluations provide only a snapshot of the models’ moral reasoning capabilities at a given moment, without accounting for potential changes over time or through additional training. Other research efforts have focused on enhancing AI's ability to elucidate its decision-making processes and align with human values, which is critical for the development of responsible AI systems. For example, Shitong et al. [9] introduced DeNEVIL, a model designed to facilitate unbiased moral reasoning in AI. Additionally, Jiao et al. [16] explored the evaluation of AI moral reasoning across diverse cultural contexts, emphasizing the interplay between technological and societal factors in AI ethics. While these approaches effectively promote transparency and foster trust, they are limited by their reliance on predefined moral categories and manual labeling, hindering their adaptability to evolving moral landscapes. Although previous research has advanced fairness assessments and value alignment, a significant gap remains: the need for a dynamic framework to evaluate moral reasoning over time, independent of specific models. Existing methodologies primarily assess ethics at specific intervals and lack mechanisms to autonomously generate, comprehend, and adapt to new moral scenarios. Furthermore, they do not provide tools for assessing divergences in AI ethics or for tracking moral consistency across models in real-time. Drawing on concepts introduced in Section 1, the newly proposed Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP) represents a system designed to continuously evaluate AI moral reasoning without requiring a fixed dataset, aiming to gauge, comprehend, and enhance the moral stability of large language models. MoCoP focuses on evaluation and understanding without engaging in model optimization or retraining, thereby ensuring clarity and consistency.

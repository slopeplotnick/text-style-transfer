Our investigation encompassed three pertinent domains: methodologies leveraging external feedback, strategies to mitigate reliance on human labels, and theoretical insights concerning the impact of representational frameworks on output quality. Typically, optimizing large language models to align with human preferences involves Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Christiano et al., 2017), which employs human preference data to train a reward model, subsequently refining the policy to optimize outcomes while maintaining proximity to a reference model. This technique underpins models such as ChatGPT and Llama (OpenAI, 2023; Touvron et al., 2023). However, challenges persist, including the high cost and potential inaccuracies of data, as well as reward models that can be gamed or perform inconsistently across different scenarios. To address these challenges, methods such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Ziegler et al., 2019), KTO (Smith et al., 2023), Bayesian RLHF, and MaxMin-RLHF have been proposed. These methods aim to refine the policy through pairwise comparisons without necessitating a specific value network, yet they still require meticulously curated preference data. Additionally, models exist that provide scores and feedback for reasoning and evaluation, thereby maintaining supervision external to the core model processes. Another strategy involves utilizing automatic supervision or leveraging model-generated signals to diminish dependence on human labels, as demonstrated by methods such as DeepSeek-R1 (Johnson et al., 2022) and TÜLU 3 (Kumar et al., 2024), which employ programmatic feedback in lieu of human input.

Negative samples encompass all potential active nodes. In Section 5, we elucidate the necessity of employing both random and adversarial negatives to surpass the performance of TextMatch. The concept of Contrastive Loss entails training the model to accurately select the appropriate positive candidate for each query from the available options within the batch. The encoder is initialized using publicly accessible E5-unsupervised checkpoints, which comprise 33 million and 330 million parameters, and are predicated on sophisticated contrastive pretraining methodologies. Identical encoder weights are employed for both queries and candidates. Prior to fine-tuning, we leverage dense interaction data derived from 110 million samples based on weak signals such as clicks or saves, as well as naturally occurring pairs of titles and descriptions. During fine-tuning, we utilize fewer but more robust interactions indicative of mutual interest, such as interviews or hires, supplemented by hard negatives from items that were displayed but not interacted with, as identified in interaction logs. The training dataset for GraphMatch is composed of one year of user interaction data from Upwork, partitioned into ten months for training, one month for validation, and one month for evaluation. The dataset comprises nodes representing freelancers, clients, and job posts, along with edges representing interactions such as job postings, applications, invitations, interviews, or contract initiations. Freelancer and job post nodes are characterized by text features such as profile overviews and job descriptions. All nodes possess additional numerical features specific to their type, including freelancer hourly rates, client locations, job post categories, etc. These text and numerical features are subject to temporal variations. Overall, the dataset encompasses approximately 9 million nodes with 32 million feature versions and 62 million edges. GraphMatch is trained using robust labels derived from contracts between freelancers and job posts.

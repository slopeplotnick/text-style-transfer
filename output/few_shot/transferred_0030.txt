Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) constitutes a methodology wherein pretrained diffusion models are utilized not only for noise reduction during sample generation but also as optimization objectives. This approach has facilitated the application of such models to a variety of tasks, including the generation of images and videos. The notable success of SDS in image synthesis has encouraged its utilization in reinforcement learning (RL) for control tasks. For example, methods have emerged that replace the standard discriminator in setups like Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) with diffusion models to differentiate between authentic and synthetic actions. Despite these advancements, these approaches often require adversarial training and necessitate continuously updating the diffusion model while optimizing policies.   Beyond adversarial frameworks, alternative methods attempt to directly employ SDS-like techniques to inform policy learning. Notably, pretrained image or video diffusion models responding to textual prompts have been leveraged to guide policy training, resulting in behaviors aligned with text instructions. However, such methods sometimes produce movements perceived as unnatural or flawed. A closely related approach is SMILING, which trains controllers through techniques akin to variational score distillation. Unlike SMILING, which relies on task-specific diffusion models, the present methodology proposes the training of a general motion model using random motion datasets. This general motion model can subsequently inform the training of diverse control policies by integrating it with specific task objectives in a goal-based RL framework. Additionally, critical design considerations are introduced to ensure the generation of high-quality, naturalistic movements across a wide spectrum of tasks.

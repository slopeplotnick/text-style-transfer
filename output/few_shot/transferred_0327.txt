Following the training phase, a rigorous evaluation test was implemented to assess the model's performance (Section 3.5). Given the significant capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the diverse nature of e-commerce data, the evaluation incorporated a wide array of examples, including challenging cases designed around the core competencies outlined in Section 2. The evaluation preparation consisted of three main steps: feature construction, model selection, and prompt optimization (refer to Fig. 6).   Informed by the principles outlined in Section 2, effective relevance modeling necessitated a comprehensive representation of the item's attributes. Consequently, features were selected to enable the model to reason and interpret information across both visual and textual modalities. For visual attributes, such as stylistic details, cuts, and patterns, the primary product image was utilized, particularly for search scenarios where textual descriptions alone were insufficient. To enrich the item's descriptive information, category-property-value (CPV) triples and Stock-keeping-unit (SKU) data were employed. Recognizing that CPV data often includes extraneous elements irrelevant to relevance assessment, a refinement process that combined prior domain knowledge with LLM-based processing was applied.   The CPV data, organized into key-value pairs wherein similar products often share common keys (e.g., "brand" for both "eye cream" and "face cream"), was systematically analyzed. High-frequency keys across substantial product datasets were identified. Subsequently, an advanced LLM, Qwen3-235B-Instruct, was tasked with determining whether each attribute contributed to relevance. The task involved utilizing a defined relevance task formulation, attribute descriptions, and corresponding keys. To ensure reliability, the process was iteratively executed eight times, retaining only those attributes where the model consistently affirmed their relevance ("true").

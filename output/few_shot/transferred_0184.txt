Variations in distribution following prompt optimization reveal the influence of system prompts on reasoning processes. A comparative analysis of reasoning patterns pre- and post-prompt optimization is presented. Figure 9 and Table 10 demonstrate that subsequent to optimization, the overall frequency of reasoning components diminishes. This reduction is attributed to the brevity and simplicity of optimized prompts, which typically eschew supplementary instructions such as "respond in the style of Shakespeare," resulting in more concise and less varied reasoning. Consequently, there is a substantial decrease in the "Others" category, indicating that optimized prompts contribute to a more structured and comprehensible model behavior. The frequency of reasoning components such as Logical reasoning and Reframing also exhibits significant variation, underscoring the strong dependence of these behaviors on prompt formulation. Furthermore, the effects of optimization tend to be task-specific. In the context of mathematical problem-solving, optimized prompts enhance Calculation and Logical reasoning, aligning with the task’s requirements. Conversely, for other tasks, these prompts do not elicit the same reasoning components. In moral reasoning tasks, while the frequency of most reasoning components decreases, Logical reasoning remains predominant relative to others. Certain reasoning components, such as Backtracking and Backward chaining, remain infrequent across scenarios, likely due to the models' lack of reflective capabilities akin to those of reinforcement learning (RL) systems.

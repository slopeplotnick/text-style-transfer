MODOMA is a framework developed for conducting language learning experiments, providing adjustable parameters for each experimental setup. It encompasses various aspects of language acquisition, including adult language, the role of the daughter agent, and underlying language processes, with configurable settings for all these components. Table 1 presents the configurations for the current set of experiments. MODOMA is capable of executing diverse language learning processes, such as those discussed by Shakouri et al. (2025). The initial setting in Table 1 pertains to the activation of functional category learning, which is enabled in all experiments under consideration. Additional settings are instrumental in determining key aspects of the experiments. The configuration for the mother agent dictates the structural composition of Delilah's utterances, influencing whether they form complete sentences or consist solely of noun phrases. The training and testing experiments outlined in this paper manipulate this parameter in various ways. Furthermore, the minimum dataset required by the daughter agent to commence learning functional and content categories varies across all experiments. These two settings establish the experimental framework. In the training experiments, the acquisition of functional and content categories begins after processing 1,000 noun phrases and 1,000 sentences, followed by 10,000 noun phrases and 10,000 sentences. This approach aids in determining the quantity of Delilah's examples necessary to discern this linguistic pattern. Additionally, the setting for functional category learning determines the ratio applied to convert distinctions between function and content words into explicit linguistic knowledge. The training sessions in this study are instrumental in identifying optimal values for these settings, while the test experiments validate these configurations using new data sourced from Delilah.

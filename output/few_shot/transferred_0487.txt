The globalization of education and the rapid expansion of online learning necessitate the adaptation of educational materials to diverse linguistic and cultural contexts. As traditional lecture delivery combines spoken discourse with visual materials such as slides, there arises a critical need for systems capable of handling multimodal content. To facilitate broader accessibility, it is essential for translation systems to preserve all components of lectures: textual, visual, and auditory.   In response, we developed BOOM, a comprehensive tool designed to translate lecture audio and slides simultaneously. BOOM generates outputs in three forms: translated text, slides that retain their original design, and speech that is appropriately localized. This enables students to engage with lecture content in their native language while maintaining the integrity of the original materials. Empirical evaluations have demonstrated that the inclusion of translated slides enhances tasks such as summarization and question-answering. The Slide Translation code is publicly available at [https://github.com/saikoneru/image-Translator](https://github.com/saikoneru/image-Translator), and its implementation has been integrated into Lecture Translator at [https://gitlab.kit.edu/kit/isl-ai41t/lt-middleware/ltpipeline](https://gitlab.kit.edu/kit/isl-ai41t/lt-middleware/ltpipeline).  The availability of lecture content in multiple languages significantly enhances comprehension, reduces communication barriers, broadens accessibility, and facilitates engagement with challenging concepts. As institutions of higher learning become increasingly international, it is imperative to provide multilingual educational resources in both physical and digital formats. The digitalization of education inherently combines various forms of media, with lecture audio serving as the core component, which can be converted into text using Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) technology. Supplementary materials, such as summaries, chapters, and question banks, can be derived from these textual transcriptions using state-of-the-art natural language processing systems, particularly those based on Large Language Models (LLMs), to enrich the learning experience.  To ensure equitable access for students with varying linguistic proficiencies, these outputs must be available in multiple languages. High-quality localization entails addressing and integrating diverse modalities—audio, textual, and visual—rendering lecture translation a complex but vital endeavor. By advancing the multilingual accessibility of educational materials, we can better support student learning in the evolving global educational landscape.

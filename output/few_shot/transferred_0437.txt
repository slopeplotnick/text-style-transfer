The transition to greater reliance on electric energy has emphasized the importance of identifying critical mineral resources. Despite increasing demand, the discovery of these minerals has not yielded corresponding economic benefits. Over the past two decades, even with heightened investment, the rate of new discoveries has diminished. In this paper, I propose leveraging artificial intelligence (AI) to systematize mineral exploration processes, reduce errors, and lower associated costs. Current exploration methodologies, which integrate disciplines such as geology and geophysics, have demonstrated limited efficacy in identifying concealed mineral deposits. These conventional approaches inadequately account for uncertainty, often resulting in suboptimal decisions and significant financial losses from unproductive drilling efforts.  The proposed framework incorporates principles derived from Bayesian reasoning and falsification, emphasizing the role of data collection in challenging existing hypotheses. Decisions regarding subsequent data acquisition are guided by well-defined metrics and strategic prioritization. A practical roadmap for implementing this approach is introduced, offering applicability across diverse mineral exploration contexts. Successful execution of this methodology, however, necessitates the integration of various AI systems. Key among these are novel unsupervised learning techniques designed to collaborate with domain experts in analyzing data and generating alternative geological models, as well as human-in-the-loop AI systems that optimize data acquisition across disciplines such as geology, geophysics, geochemistry, and drilling. The primary objective is to minimize geological uncertainty prior to assessing the quality and quantity of mineral resources. The efficacy of this framework is demonstrated through its application to current mineral exploration and resource evaluation case studies.

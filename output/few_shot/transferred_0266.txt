Advanced reinforcement learning (RL) techniques employ domain randomization and reward shaping to bridge the gap between simulations and real-world applications. Tobin et al. (2017) demonstrated that domain randomization facilitates the acquisition of tasks by robots in simulated environments, which can subsequently be executed in real-world settings. Similarly, Grzes and Kudenko (2010) explored reward shaping to expedite learning processes in multi-agent scenarios. These methodologies enhance robotic adaptability to dynamic environments, notably within construction contexts. However, they demand substantial computational resources and precise parameter tuning, posing challenges in rapidly changing construction sites. Furthermore, the interpretability and verifiability of decisions made by these learned policies remain complex. This complexity has prompted the integration of game theory, which provides a framework for modeling interactions among autonomous agents. Martin et al. (2023) applied game theory to allocate tasks among multiple robots, employing Shapley values to evaluate each robot's contribution. Their approach yielded effective results, offering insights into the stability and fairness of solutions. Nevertheless, the application of game theory is hampered by its intricate decision-making processes and the assumption that agents consistently act rationally, which may not hold in unpredictable construction environments. Additionally, the formulation of precise utility functions remains a challenge for intricate construction tasks. Despite these advancements, AI and learning-based methods encounter significant limitations in construction coordination, where decision explainability is essential. The complexity inherent in deep learning and game theory models obscures the rationale behind coordination decisions. Construction managers require clarity and the ability to scrutinize these decisions to ensure safety and effective project management. This necessity has prompted exploration into the use of Large Language Models (LLMs) for coordination, given their capability to articulate reasoning in natural language and enhance decision transparency. LLMs are revolutionizing multi-robot collaboration through their linguistic comprehension, contextual reasoning, and adaptive decision-making. Unlike conventional methods requiring exhaustive problem detail specification, LLMs facilitate more intuitive human-robot interactions, thereby enhancing the clarity of decision-making processes.

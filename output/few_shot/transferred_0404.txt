Identifying hate speech in low-resource languages presents significant challenges due to insufficient datasets and the inherent variability in orthography and linguistic structure. Bangla, spoken by over 230 million individuals in Bangladesh and India (particularly West Bengal), exemplifies this issue. Despite the increasing demand for automated moderation on social media, computational resources for Bangla remain inadequate. This study analyzes Subtask 1A and Subtask 1B of the BLP 2025 Shared Task on hate speech detection. We replicated official baselines such as Majority, Random, and Support Vector Machine, and evaluated additional baseline methods including Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree. Furthermore, we employed transformer-based models, namely DistilBERT, BanglaBERT, m-BERT, and XLM-RoBERTa, for hate speech classification. All transformer models surpassed the baseline methods in performance for the subtasks, with the exception of DistilBERT. Notably, BanglaBERT exhibited superior performance across both subtasks. Despite its smaller size, BanglaBERT outperformed both m-BERT and XLM-RoBERTa, underscoring the importance of language-specific training. Our findings highlight the potential and necessity for pretrained language models tailored to Bangla, a language with limited computational resources.  The increasing globalization of the internet and the proliferation of social media platforms have facilitated widespread communication and information sharing. Social media usage has surged, becoming integral to public discourse while also providing a platform for the dissemination of hate speech. The rapid escalation of toxic and harmful content, such as hate speech, poses a significant safety concern on these platforms. Hate speech comprises harmful, offensive, or derogatory language directed at individuals or groups, making its automatic detection a critical task in Natural Language Processing (NLP). While considerable advancements have been made in detecting hate speech in resource-rich languages like English, progress remains limited for low-resource languages like Bangla, primarily due to the scarcity of high-quality annotated datasets and the linguistic complexity involved.

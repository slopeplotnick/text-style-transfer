These findings underscore the significance of pre-existing attitudes and expectations in shaping perceptions of AI-generated outputs. For instance, Hong et al. (2021) found a correlation between a positive disposition towards creative AI and higher ratings for AI-generated music, with a focus on differing expectations across musical styles. Unanticipated musical elements were associated with lower ratings; however, variations in musical styles did not result in significant differences in perception. An overarching inquiry remains whether AI-composed music is perceived as analogous to human-created compositions. This investigation aims to discern the musical attributes that influence perceptions of human versus AI authorship. Shank et al. (2023) highlighted that musical style plays a critical role, with electronic music more commonly attributed to AI origins. Remarkably, when participants were misled about the source of the music, they perceived non-existent differences, indicating that expectations and beliefs about human versus AI creation impact judgments, as noted by Ansani et al. (2024). Déguernel et al. (2022) identified specific heuristics employed in evaluating music: AI-generated compositions were often recognized by excessive repetition, rigid or unnatural structure, and atypical harmony or melody. Conversely, human-composed music was associated with melodic variation, embellishment, recognizable styles, and instrumentally feasible features. This reflects a broader trend wherein features perceived as "human" are rated more favorably, whereas those linked with AI are viewed less positively. Supporting this, Hong et al. (2022) examined how anthropomorphizing AI and acknowledging its creative autonomy influence acceptance of AI musicians. Participants rated AI-generated music from various styles and assessed the quality of compositions alongside their attitudes toward AI. Findings indicated that perceptions of AI as more human-like and creatively autonomous enhanced acceptance of AI musicians. Furthermore, Sun et al. (2023) demonstrated that anthropomorphizing AI positively impacts perceptions of AI musicians' competence and warmth, both of which contribute to more favorable attitudes.

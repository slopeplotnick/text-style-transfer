The practice of science, accompanied by the contemplation of scientific methodology, raises questions about the nature of rigorous scientific practice. What delineates a sound scientific method? Throughout history, science has evolved from empirical observation to exploring the interconnection between models and data, also scrutinizing the collaborative dynamics within scientific communities. These social interactions establish norms and protocols, susceptible to future reevaluation. Practitioners within a field may overlook errors due to communal normalization. Scientists can exhibit overconfidence in their knowledge (Bond et al., 2007). Thomas Kuhn examined these collective dynamics, describing them as "paradigms." Paradigms, according to Kuhn, consist of shared beliefs, methodologies, and applications within a scientific community, exemplified by concepts such as evolution or plate tectonics.

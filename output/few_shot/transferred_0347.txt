Gurung and Lapata (2025) introduced VR-CLI, a methodology designed to optimize narrative planning by employing extensive reasoning over story information to generate comprehensive responses. VR-CLI operates as a reward mechanism intended to enhance the likelihood of accurately predicting the subsequent chapter based on existing narrative details and planning strategies. Enhancements to VR-CLI were achieved by incorporating a feature that reduces the propensity to select a random chapter from the same book. The LiteReason approach, our conventional method, involves performing incremental training steps using paths generated during the process, which incurs additional computational costs but updates the reasoning component with the current model's cognition. Additionally, a hidden thought prompt is utilized to facilitate the model's use of hidden thought tags. The variant of LiteReason that excludes SFT retains the hidden prompt and initiates the Reasoning Projector using hidden thought tags to determine when to impart new reasoning to the model, omitting SFT during training to update the Reasoning Projector. Conversely, LiteReason without the Reasoning Projector evaluates efficacy post-training without the Reasoning Projector, relying solely on the hidden thought prompt. This approach eschews hidden reasoning to ascertain whether the model's behavior changes in the absence of reasoning steps. We explore recent advancements in hidden reasoning research, selecting several established methodologies to assess our hypotheses in section 3, with experiments conducted on Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks. For training-free hidden reasoning, we compare against two novel techniques: Mixture of Inputs (MoI) by Su et al. (2025), which combines the sampled token's embedding with others, and Soft Thinking by Zhang et al. (2025), which similarly involves token embedding combinations but employs an entropy measure to determine the transition from continuous thinking to discrete sampling.

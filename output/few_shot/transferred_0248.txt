Agents possess the capability to identify deviations in behavior or discrepancies in coordination, prompting suggestions for norm modifications to maintain either stability or variability within the system. 2. The system incorporates differentiated roles for agents, including rule creation, interpretation, and task execution, thereby decentralizing authority and power. These concepts are designed to address fundamental requirements for internal management rather than prescribing specific implementation methods. Future research will explore methodologies for operationalizing these principles with existing systems and will evaluate their efficacy through controlled experimental setups.   The Institutional AI paradigm focuses on maintaining systemic stability amidst change rather than pursuing complete autonomy. Safety mechanisms are embedded as iterative processes of rule assessment and enforcement, rather than as a post hoc verification step after deployment. A distinctive feature is that agents themselves participate in the rulemaking processes, enabling systems to self-correct to some extent in scenarios where external human intervention might be delayed. This approach is not intended to replace human oversight but to complement it, ensuring traceability of evaluation tasks, fostering diverse problem-solving perspectives, and mitigating risks identified by the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH) framework.   The primary objective is to improve the collaborative functionality, comprehensibility, and consistency of networks of models while reducing their dependence on continuous external oversight. The Institutional AI framework builds upon the theoretical foundation established by the ESRH framework, which focuses on identifying the emergence of risks. In contrast, the institutional perspective emphasizes risk management strategies. This paper serves as an introductory exposition of the foundational principles underlying the Institutional AI approach. Detailed theoretical modeling and empirical validation are deferred to future publications by this research group, which will be disseminated through arXiv and other academic platforms.

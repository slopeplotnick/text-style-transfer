The system employs regex to extract structured decisions from markdown responses generated by Large Language Models (LLMs). It focuses on specific sections, such as allocation decisions and success rates, converting them into structured data for validation. Eight rules ensure the validity of this data, including the elimination of suboptimal allocations, examination of trade-offs, awareness of workloads, and logical coherence. A transition to a weighted validation system was made due to the inadequacy of traditional rule-based systems in capturing the enhanced reasoning and contextual awareness required for LLM tasks. Empirical studies indicate that evaluating multiple facets affords a more comprehensive understanding of model reasoning than a binary correctness assessment. Consequently, this novel system assesses reasoning across various dimensions, including the clarity of explanations, consistency of success rates, workload awareness, trade-off justification, adherence to procedural phases, and alignment of confidence with outcomes. Each dimension contributes to an overall quality score, with greater weight assigned to factors directly impacting outcomes, such as reasoning quality and accuracy of success rates. In contrast, elements like confidence justification receive lesser weight. This framework ensures that validation is both attentive to performance and contextually aware, achieving a balance between comprehension and rigor in evaluating model reasoning. The scoring dimensions are as follows:  1. Explanation Quality (Weight: 0.20): Depth and clarity of the reasoning process. 2. Success Rate Accuracy (Weight: 0.20): Alignment between predicted and actual success. 3. Trade-off Analysis (Weight: 0.15): Consideration of competing objectives. 4. Workload Awareness (Weight: 0.15): Consideration of current task distribution.

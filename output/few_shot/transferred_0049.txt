Seventeen participants were recruited through school posters or by approaching individuals in public settings. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board, and informed consent was obtained from the guardians of any underage participants. All data from interviews were securely stored, ensuring that no personal information was used. Participants self-assessed their short story writing skills with an average rating of 3.8 out of 5. All participants had previous experience writing stories, either manually or with digital tools. The individuals were organized into groups of approximately four, each supervised by a staff member. The workshop was conducted over a duration of two and a half hours. During the session, participants were encouraged to verbalize their thoughts, which provided insight into their initial and final ideas, as well as their cognitive processes. Additionally, four participants were interviewed post-workshop. Analysis of the workshop recordings and interview data revealed four distinct interaction styles during story creation. The workshop commenced with a 15-minute introductory session where the facilitators outlined the workshop's objectives and procedures, utilizing examples to illustrate the four primary components. Participants were reminded to continuously articulate their decision-making processes. This was followed by a 60-minute group activity, during which facilitators distributed cards, and each group collaboratively developed a story incorporating the four key components, while discussing their reasoning and choices. Facilitators provided assistance as needed. Subsequently, a 45-minute session allowed participants to present their stories and receive feedback from peers and staff, concentrating on creativity and narrative coherence. Finally, 30-minute informal interviews were conducted with four participants to explore their creative experiences.

Assessing the power consumption of Large Language Models (LLMs) differs significantly from evaluating their speed or latency. Power consumption is influenced by a variety of factors, including diverse hardware configurations, complex software infrastructures, and execution methodologies, each of which varies substantially according to the deployment context. Contemporary evaluation frameworks for LLMs often fail to accommodate these variations as they typically adhere to standardized configurations, fixed model sizes, and expensive benchmarking tools. However, TokenPowerBench, illustrated in Figure 1, addresses these limitations through a tripartite framework that encompasses setup, execution and measurement, and reporting. This framework provides a flexible and repeatable approach for assessing LLM power consumption in modern contexts.  Initially, TokenPowerBench facilitates the preparatory phase of testing by allowing users to select the model, execution engine, and prompts. To enhance user convenience, TokenPowerBench offers three interchangeable components: a model pool, a prompt-dataset menu, and an inference-engine selector. The model pool includes a diverse range of models to evaluate how architectural decisions impact power consumption. This collection encompasses standard transformers, such as the LLaMA series, and Mixture-of-Experts models, such as Mixtral, which differ in computation power usage, parameter activation, and memory utilization. The assessment spans various model sizes, from small models with fewer than 1 billion parameters suitable for conventional GPUs to large models like Llama 3-405B, which require multiple GPUs or nodes. This variation allows for a comprehensive analysis of power consumption as models and hardware scale.  The prompt-dataset menu acknowledges that prompt length and style can significantly influence power consumption during inference. To demonstrate this, TokenPowerBench supports two primary datasets: Alpaca, which features brief chat prompts, and LongBench, which includes extended contexts reaching up to 10,000 tokens. Users also have the option to incorporate their own data in CSV or other compatible formats.

Based on the findings discussed in Research Question 2 (RQ2), Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV) demonstrate complementary strengths and distinct failure modes. Building on this insight, Research Question 3 (RQ3) investigates the feasibility of designing an effective hybrid system that synergizes these methodologies. To address RQ3, two integration approaches are proposed: (i) Score-Level Fusion, and (ii) an Evidence-Aware Pipeline. The Evidence-Aware Pipeline specifically targets limitations identified in FV systems, as elaborated in Section 4.5. While Score-Level Fusion strengthens performance by combining model scores, it does not resolve specific issues inherent to FV systems.   FV systems are proficient in scenarios where evidence is available, allowing them to classify claims as "Supported" or "Contradicted." However, they exhibit reduced efficacy in cases where evidentiary support is unavailable, which are categorized as "Not Enough Information" (NEI). To mitigate these shortcomings, the Evidence-Aware Pipeline employs a two-step approach:   1. **Step 1 - Retrieval and Verification:** An FV module, such as LLM-QA, retrieves external evidence and determines whether claims are "Supported," "Contradicted," or NEI based on the alignment with the external information.   2. **Step 2 - Conditional Branching:** A decision-making process is introduced to adaptively handle NEI cases. If the FV module provides a definitive verdict ("Supported" or "Contradicted"), the pipeline accepts the result. However, if the module outputs NEI, indicating the absence of adequate external evidence, the system bypasses further reliance on FV. Instead, it employs an HD mechanism (e.g., LNPE) to derive the final score solely based on internal model uncertainty.  This Evidence-Aware Pipeline addresses the inherent limitations of FV systems, particularly in scenarios of insufficient external evidence. By decoupling reliance on external evidence from the model's internal signals, the proposed system mitigates errors stemming from faulty evidence retrieval processes. Consequently, it ensures robust detection performance, even in the absence of external knowledge sources.

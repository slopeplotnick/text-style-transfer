The model is trained in a manner analogous to the previous Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) process, incorporating reasoning examples generated during training. In conjunction with the tokens produced by the model, sentences are randomly substituted with tokens as previously described, enhancing the model's efficiency in reasoning. During deployment, the model operates in a standard mode; however, upon encountering specific tags, it transitions to an alternate reasoning mode (refer to Figure 1). These tags delineate the distinction between the two modes and do not represent unique tokens in the model's vocabulary. The model is not explicitly trained to employ these tags but is instead prompted to use them. Additionally, the model autonomously determines the number of tokens to generate by predicting a number. Initially, these tags are infrequently utilized, but their prevalence increases as training progresses. This section elucidates two tasks utilized to evaluate our method: Flawed Fictions and Next-Chapter Prediction. We describe the original setup of these tasks and the training of a basic reasoning model, absent the special reasoning mode, to address them. Both tasks entail complex narrative reasoning, distinct from conventional tasks in this domain.

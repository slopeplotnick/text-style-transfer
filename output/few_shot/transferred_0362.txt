AlphaZero was among the pioneering systems to demonstrate the efficacy of self-play in learning, whereby it improved its capabilities independently through autonomous gameplay (Silver et al., 2018). This concept has been adapted into language models that progressively enhance their reasoning abilities over time. Certain systems employ this large-scale self-play methodology to enhance the performance of less capable language models by generating and solving tasks autonomously, thus circumventing the need for extensive labeled data. This approach is particularly advantageous in domains such as code generation, where a program self-verifies through testing (Silver et al., 2018).  Notable methodologies include R-Zero, which operates without external data inputs, formulating questions, attempting to solve them, and learning through self-evaluation (Liu et al., 2022). Other strategies initiate with a limited set of examples, subsequently learning to generate more complex problems, exemplified by R-Few, which emphasizes a distinct approach to directing the learning process that seeks to maintain the exploratory benefits without deviating into irrelevant or monotonous paths (Chen et al., 2023).   Reinforcement learning has emerged as a fundamental technique for enhancing the cognitive capabilities of language models beyond mere example-based learning, frequently incorporating human feedback to refine performance. Early systems, such as InstructGPT, have illustrated that aligning with human preferences can render models more beneficial, safer, and often superior in reasoning compared to purely example-driven learning (Brown et al., 2022). Contemporary systems like DeepSeek-R1 leverage this paradigm to advance problem-solving by rewarding effective reasoning practices. Other analogous systems demonstrate that the establishment of objectives for prolonged and intricate tasks can significantly amplify reasoning capabilities (Zhang et al., 2023).

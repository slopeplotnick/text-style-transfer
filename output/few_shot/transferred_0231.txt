We employ a methodology that evaluates outlines based on foundational principles, rather than direct comparison with human examples. Given that outlines are inherently concise and primarily reflect structural elements, their comparison with comprehensive surveys is not particularly informative. Instead, we assess the quality of outlines through their organizational coherence, logical progression, and clarity. This evaluative approach aligns more precisely with the characteristics of an effective outline, which predominantly adheres to academic conventions. In our methodology, the original survey title serves as the prompt to generate a survey from each system. All systems have equal access to online information, ensuring a uniform opportunity to gather data. We evaluate three categories of systems: those designed for general long-form text generation, those specialized in academic paper writing, and those specifically tailored for survey creation. Each system receives identical input and is assessed uniformly. The scoring results are detailed below. The table reveals distinct performance patterns among the three system types. General long-text systems exhibit proficiency in language use and topic coherence; however, their surveys lack depth, originality, idea synthesis, and analytical depth. Conversely, paper-writing systems demonstrate superior structural capabilities, yet their outputs often appear formulaic and constrained by templates, limiting depth and diversity of perspectives. Survey-making systems achieve the highest overall scores, excelling in organization, integrative synthesis, and clear delineation of facts versus opinions. They are particularly effective in scientific and technological domains, where accurate information retrieval and organization are paramount. These disparities underscore that producing high-quality surveys necessitates more than general language models, requiring specialized techniques that emphasize literature integration and effective idea organization.

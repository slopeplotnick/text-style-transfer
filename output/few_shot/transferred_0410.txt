The proposed system demonstrates compatibility with a variety of models and algorithms, achieving significant improvements across different architectures, such as Qwen2.5 and Llama models. Notably, the smaller Llama-3.2-1B model also benefits from the proposed methods, OptPO-SFT and OptPO, which outperform conventional approaches in terms of accuracy. This suggests the robustness of the framework across varying model sizes. Additionally, OptPO demonstrates compatibility with multiple reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, including PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, consistently surpassing TTRL in accuracy and efficiency, thereby validating its effectiveness across different RL paradigms. Comparisons between supervised fine-tuning (SFT)-based and RL-based variants indicate that RL-based versions of OptPO generally achieve higher accuracy, particularly in mathematical reasoning tasks, by leveraging feedback from rollouts more effectively than SFT methods. However, OptPO-SFT still exhibits stable improvements over TTSFT in all scenarios, underscoring the efficacy of adaptive pseudo-labeling in enhancing SFT-style training during testing.   Key insights from this work include the following: (1) Efficiency is improved without sacrificing accuracy by employing adaptive early-stopping based on Bayesian Sequential Probability Ratio Testing (BSPRT); (2) OptPO achieves a balanced trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency; and (3) OptPO-SFT provides a rapid and practical alternative for adaptation when RL-style updates are infeasible. Together, these contributions constitute an efficient framework for optimizing models during the testing phase for reasoning tasks.  This study presents **OptPO**, a novel framework designed to optimize policies efficiently during testing, addressing the inefficiencies associated with majority-vote-based rollouts in test-time reinforcement learning for large language models (LLMs). OptPO employs a Bayesian test to enable adaptive early-stopping, dynamically adjusting rollouts based on the accumulation of evidence rather than relying on fixed thresholds. This ensures the generation of reliable pseudo-labels while minimizing unnecessary sampling. The framework is both versatile and user-friendly, demonstrating compatibility with established policy optimization algorithms such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, in addition to supervised fine-tuning during testing. Across diverse models and evaluation benchmarks, OptPO either maintains or enhances accuracy while reducing computational overhead.  In summary, this work establishes that efficient, label-free adaptation is both feasible and critical in the context of deploying LLMs in unpredictable real-world scenarios. The introduction of OptPO provides a foundation for future research in the domain of efficient test-time learning, paving the way for more adaptable and resource-efficient systems.

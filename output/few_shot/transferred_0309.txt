After preprocessing, CPV data remains somewhat noisy due to inherent issues with item information quality. Additionally, item SKU features exhibit redundancy, attributable to their intrinsic characteristics. To assess the efficacy of the features, the dataset was partitioned into training and test subsets. The evaluation began with product titles, incrementally incorporating additional features to measure their contribution. Table 2 demonstrates that the inclusion of CPV and SKU features enhances model performance, indicating that relevant and stable input information can improve the model even in the presence of redundancy and noise. Furthermore, main item images were introduced to provide visual features beyond the scope of textual data. SKU images were excluded, as the primary image already encapsulates the majority of visual information, while SKU images typically reflect minor variations, such as style or color, which are adequately represented by SKU text features. Their inclusion would unnecessarily complicate the model without adding significant value. Ultimately, extensive product information was gathered for subsequent training processes.  To identify the optimal base model, two critical factors were considered: the capacity for complex reasoning and knowledge integration (as discussed in Section 2) and the efficiency required for practical, real-world applications. Several open-source models with parameter sizes ranging from 7B to 14B were selected, balancing performance with the feasibility of training and deployment on a single GPU. A subset of annotated data was divided into training and validation sets, and the relevance task definition was employed as the prompt. Fine-tuning was performed (see Section 3.1) for each model using this training set to evaluate their performance. Among the models evaluated, the Qwen2.5-7B model outperformed its counterparts, excelling in both performance and operational efficiency. Consequently, it was selected as the base model for the proposed framework.

To evaluate the quality of a summary, several aspects must be considered: lexical overlap, semantic similarity, and factual consistency. Traditional metrics, such as BLEU and ROUGE-L, assess the lexical overlap between the summary and the original text; however, they struggle with paraphrasing where meaning is preserved but wording differs (Papineni et al., 2002; Lin, 2004). To address this limitation, BERTScore (Zhang et al., 2020) employs a pre-trained transformer model to assess semantic similarity rather than mere lexical correspondence, proving advantageous for concise and precise summaries. Beyond semantics, assessing factual consistency is crucial. FactCC (Kryściński et al., 2020) utilizes a model to verify sentence-level consistency between the summary and the source text, though it occasionally yields false negatives. BLANC (Laban et al., 2020) evaluates how well the summary supports the language model's ability to predict missing segments of the original text without relying on a reference text, but its performance is contingent upon the model used and sometimes overemphasizes lexical matching. SummaC (Ladhak et al., 2022), on the other hand, applies Natural Language Inference (NLI) techniques to verify factual alignment between the summary and the source text, demonstrating superior performance compared to FactCC. In this study, we employ an ensemble of evaluation methods: ROUGE-L for lexical overlap, BERTScore for semantic similarity, and SummaC for factual accuracy.

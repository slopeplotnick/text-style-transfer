We evaluated various methodologies, including RL-Trained, Mixture-of-Inputs, CoLaR, and LiteReason, to assess their performance. Utilizing Qwen 2.5 7B-Instruct, we generated chapters based on plans from each method and conducted comparative analyses by soliciting preferences from human evaluators on pairs of results. The analyses, detailed in Appendix E, were conducted using a specific model to identify the most effective method. Initially, we evaluated these methodologies on two tasks, Flawed Fictions and NCP, with results presented in Table 4. Additionally, we compared LiteReason against two baseline models, both derived from Qwen 2.5-7B-Instruct: one employed a simplistic prompting approach, while the other was trained using reinforcement learning (RL). Neither baseline incorporated advanced reasoning features. LiteReason without reasoning prompts (RP), which omits reasoning steps, resulted in longer reasoning processes and poorer performance compared to the RL-trained baseline. Further comparisons, illustrated in Table 7, demonstrate the impact of implicit-thought prompts across different models. We also examined the prevalence of "latent thoughts" during training to determine RL training's influence. Initially, latent thoughts were more frequent but diminished over time. Future research should explore strategies to encourage latent reasoning by refining prompts or increasing the likelihood of certain markers. Overall, LiteReason achieved marginal performance improvements (0.8% on Flawed Fictions and 58% on NCP), likely because it parallels the RL-trained baseline while exploring unique reasoning paths. Training-free methodologies, such as Mixture-of-Inputs (MoI) and Soft Thinking, performed comparably to the untrained model. On the NCP task, Soft Thinking slightly outperformed the baseline but required more tokens, while MoI underperformed and utilized fewer tokens. On the Flawed Fictions task, deviations from the baseline were minimal and within the margin of error. Further investigation is necessary to enhance the consistency and efficiency of training-free methods across various tasks and reasoning styles.

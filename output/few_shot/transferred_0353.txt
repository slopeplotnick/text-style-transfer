A Software Product Line (SPL) can be defined as a methodology to streamline the development of a suite of related software systems, which share common features, thereby facilitating more efficient and rapid creation of new software products (Clements and Northrop, 2001). This transformation process, which involves converting an existing code base into an SPL, is inherently complex and time-intensive. Moreover, it can disrupt established software development practices within an organization (Pohl et al., 2005). The current study delineates the initial steps undertaken to facilitate such a transformation in collaboration with ITK - Predict and Decide, a small enterprise opting to transition its code base into an SPL. The researchers' role involved guiding ITK through this intricate process. The existing code base is structured around Cross, a framework that facilitates software reuse to some extent; however, it lacks the formal models and processes characteristic of a mature SPL. To assist ITK's developers, a gradual adaptation of their code base and engineering practices was devised. The preliminary step involved an assessment of the company’s personnel attitudes towards the proposed transformation and providing strategic recommendations to streamline the process. This paper presents the findings from the initial study, where company personnel were interviewed to gather insights concerning their current workflows, expectations from the SPL initiative, and associated concerns. Generally, there was a positive reception towards the SPL transition, with a majority expressing eagerness to contribute to the process. Developers, in particular, viewed the transformation as both a technical and managerial challenge. Conversely, personnel engaged in business operations exhibited more caution, voicing apprehensions about potential maintenance difficulties and the risk of stifling creativity, particularly in the realm of user interface design.

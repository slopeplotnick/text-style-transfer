To evaluate the potential of LLM reasoning in solving MRTA problems, we adapted the SMART-LLM framework (Kannan et al., 2024) as an initial benchmark, which facilitated the identification of fundamental challenges in utilizing LLMs for coordination before developing an enhanced version. For experimental purposes, the SMART-LLM dataset comprising 36 tasks was utilized, categorized into four complexity levels: Elemental (single action), Simple (multiple objects involving similar robots), Compound (diverse robots with specialized skills), and Complex (collaborative team efforts). The task executions were conducted within the AI2-THOR simulation environment. In our LLM setup, Claude 4 Sonnet was employed as the reasoning tool, with a temperature setting of 0.0 to ensure consistency and repeatability of outcomes. Performance evaluation adhered to the SMART-LLM methodology, focusing on five metrics: Success Rate (SR), Task Completion Rate (TCR), Goal Condition Recall (GCR), Executability (EXE), and Robot Utilization (RU). These metrics collectively assess task performance and the efficacy of robot teamwork. Following the identification of validation and LLM reliability issues during the SMART-LLM testing phase, we developed and evaluated the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture. This architecture demonstrated that implementing structured validation checks and retry mechanisms can address these challenges, thereby substantiating the feasibility of LLMs for MRTA tasks. Key features of the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture included multi-step validation checks with retry provisions, allowing up to three retries per agent and incorporating systematic task deconstruction, assignment, and code generation, along with iterative feedback loops for continuous enhancement.

Previous research has investigated more structured methodologies for designing prompts. For instance, Plan-and-Solve (Wang et al., 2023c) decomposes tasks into two distinct stages: planning and solving, which has been shown to enhance performance on mathematical and logical reasoning tasks. Prompt Chaining (Chen et al., 2023) examines whether integrating intermediate summaries or steps improves text summarization tasks. Complexity-based Prompting (Fu et al., 2023) posits that the complexity of a task should inform the construction of prompts, aligning with the broader notion of tailoring prompts based on specificity. While most studies emphasize task formats or exemplar selection, this work focuses on the level of detail and structure within prompts, conceptualizing it as a variable dimension.   Recent studies have also explored approaches for refining prompts, either through automated techniques or with human intervention. For example, black-box optimization methods (Diao et al., 2023) adjust prompts iteratively based on external feedback, bypassing the need for model-specific internal observations. Prompt Engineering a Prompt Engineer (Zhou et al., 2023) demonstrates that large language models can refine their own prompts, employing a self-improvement mechanism akin to the generalization loop. Additionally, Prompt Refinement with Image Pivots (Lu et al., 2023) leverages visual features to modify text prompts for image generation tasks. Although the present work is confined to text-based applications, it similarly seeks to optimize prompts to enhance model outcomes. However, unlike these studies, the DETAIL framework emphasizes the impact of specificity in textual prompts on reasoning performance, rather than examining semantic modifications or multimodal data integration.   Comprehensive reviews, such as Liu et al. (2023), have synthesized diverse prompting strategies for reasoning tasks, cataloging the wide array of existing methods and techniques. However, there is a paucity of research investigating how varying levels of detail within prompts influence model performance across different tasks and systems. While methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and Plan-and-Solve underscore the significance of prompt content, they do not sufficiently analyze the role of specificity in this context.

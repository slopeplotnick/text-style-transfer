The TACO framework aims to adapt Vision-Language Architectures (VLAs) during evaluation, preserving the pretrained VLAs' generalization capabilities while ensuring outputs are tailored to specific target tasks. This approach introduces a method for estimating pseudo-counts within the Count-based Function Normalization (CFN), facilitating distribution change measurement with minimal computational resources. TACO enhances the success rate of various VLA models across both simulated and real-world tasks, eliminating the need for extensive retraining and enabling rapid deployment. In the context of reinforcement learning (RL), online RL involves agents actively interacting with the environment to explore action spaces. Conversely, offline RL derives learning from fixed datasets without further environmental interaction, presenting challenges due to the discrepancy between the data-generating policy and the target policy. Offline datasets reflect the data-generating policy's distribution, which may diverge significantly from the intended learning policy, leading to substantial errors when estimating action values outside the observed distribution. During the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase of VLAs, models encompass multiple action modes, though task-specific successful actions are typically concentrated within a narrower scope. Consequently, policy sampling may not consistently yield optimal actions. TACO employs an anti-exploration strategy to align the policy closely with successful data distribution, distinguishing itself from conventional offline RL methods that apply uncertainty-based penalties. Instead, TACO utilizes a straightforward count-based regularization method, complementing VLA models without necessitating value estimation. The use of count-based regularization has historical precedence in both traditional RL and recent Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) algorithms.

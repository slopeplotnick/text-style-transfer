In specialized Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) tasks, a standardized format is typically employed for both task definitions and requirements, subsequently incorporating query and item specifics from each dataset to construct the prompt. To optimize the performance of the base model, critical information such as the task description, decision-making rules, and output formatting guidelines are integrated within the prompt. Explicit instructions are also provided to guide the model through a two-step process: initially, deciphering the query requirements and its necessary attributes, followed by extracting pertinent attributes from the item information to make informed decisions based on the established rules. For tasks requiring logical reasoning, detailed instructions within the prompt facilitate the model's ability to derive accurate solutions. However, in SFT tasks, such detail is not always beneficial as the model derives significant insights from the response data in addition to the instructions. To determine the optimal prompt design, three versions were tested. The comprehensive version includes exhaustive task details, rules, and instructions, extending beyond 7,000 tokens. The intermediate version condenses the content to approximately 800 tokens, retaining only the most crucial instructions. The basic version provides a succinct role description, such as: "You are an e-commerce relevance expert. Score the query and item." This phase focuses on initializing the model and enhancing its foundational decision-making capabilities. Achieving this requires a training dataset that is both expansive and meticulously curated. A robust sampling methodology is employed to ensure diverse representation of e-commerce data, thereby avoiding over- or under-representation of any specific data type.

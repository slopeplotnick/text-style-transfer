We establish pairs of AI judges, such as GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-pro, to examine their agreement levels in evaluating beliefs. Due to the abundance of data from GPT-4o compared to other models, GPT-4o serves as the primary reference point for comparative analyses. Additionally, we assess the alignment between AI-generated evaluations and human evaluations by involving human participants in the same belief assessment tasks. Results from these analyses, including comparisons between human evaluators and AI models (e.g., human evaluator 1 versus GPT-4o), are presented in Table 2. These comparisons suggest that the observed results are unlikely to be attributable to judge bias.  In this study, we introduce the Martingale Score as a novel metric to evaluate the extent to which AI models exhibit belief entrenchment. We demonstrate that this score serves as a predictive indicator of the accuracy of the reasoning process and apply it across various models, prompts, and problem domains. Furthermore, we investigate factors that exacerbate or mitigate belief entrenchment.   One limitation of our work lies in the absence of a demonstrated correlation between the Martingale Score and the Brier Score, a standard measure of accuracy, in the OpenReview context. We hypothesize that this limitation arises from the nature of OpenReview, where decisions are influenced by community voting, introducing inconsistencies. Nonetheless, Table 1 highlights that belief entrenchment poses significant challenges in domains requiring human judgment, such as r/ChangeMyView and OpenReview. These findings raise concerns regarding the use of AI models in such contexts.

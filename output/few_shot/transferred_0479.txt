The utilization of artificial intelligence (AI) in exploration is an area of significant interest, with various common errors often encountered. This discussion will subsequently explore potential advancements in AI applications within the domain of exploration, with a particular emphasis on decision-making processes. The primary objective of exploration is the discovery of novel resources, which can occur in proximity to known locations (brownfield) or in previously unexplored regions (greenfield). It is anticipated that, over the next decade, discoveries in brownfield areas will surpass those in greenfield regions. Exploration endeavors aim to delineate prospective locations by identifying loci of valuable materials. These efforts typically commence with an examination of extensive regions, such as large igneous provinces, where magmatic processes conducive to economically viable mineralization are likely to occur. Initially, geologists employ cartographic analyses and aerial data to identify anomalies, which may indicate the presence of significant subterranean features. Subsequent investigations involve more detailed on-site evaluations through fieldwork, geophysical surveys, and geochemical soil sampling. The subsequent phase, involving drilling operations, represents the most costly component of the exploration process, aimed at validating hypotheses regarding subsurface characteristics. Optimally, these decisions are informed by sophisticated methodologies that minimize expenditures through an enhanced understanding of uncertainty and the value of information. Diverse data sources contribute to elucidating the genesis of mineral systems, with data-driven predictions guiding the exploration focus. This process transcends mere mineral discovery, encompassing the validation of geological hypotheses, thereby reducing uncertainties about subsurface formations. Decision-making is iterative, with objectives potentially evolving throughout the lifecycle of an exploration project.

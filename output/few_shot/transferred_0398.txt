The primary competencies we aim to cultivate include the ability to infer three-dimensional structures without explicit guidance, control camera perspectives in a manner congruent with scene characteristics (such as scale, obstruction, and arrangement), comprehend spatial organization within scenes, strategize to achieve objectives, and maintain temporal consistency. We evaluate these competencies through two specific tasks: navigating a scene using video-based instructions and identifying objects based on textual cues. In the navigation task, the model employs camera movement directives to generate a video that aligns with both the instructions and the scene's configuration. For the object identification task, the model adheres to textual instructions to locate and display a specified object, assessing its understanding of spatial arrangements and planning capabilities. These tasks enable us to ascertain VIDEO4SPATIAL's proficiency in interpreting and engaging with scene elements solely via video input. Given the absence of comprehensive public datasets for such tasks, we utilize indoor scanning datasets such as ScanNet++ and ARKitScenes, which provide extensive video footage of various environments, facilitating the creation of context-target video pairings. A vision-language model assists in selecting clips featuring a target object and generates instructional content for object identification. Camera movement data is transformed into actionable instructions for scene navigation. Evaluations demonstrate the robustness of this framework in addressing complex challenges. The model acquires insights into the spatial layout and semantics from video data alone, excelling in object identification and scene traversal. Despite being trained on short video segments, it performs effectively with longer sequences during testing, enhancing its applicability. Furthermore, although initially trained on indoor scenes, it generalizes well to outdoor environments and unseen objects. In conclusion, we have developed VIDEO4SPATIAL, an efficient video-based system for visuospatial tasks, emphasizing critical design elements such as integrating context and instruction guidance, incorporating bounding boxes for increased precision, and optimizing context sampling for enhanced comprehension. We also established and evaluated two spatial tasks to validate this approach.

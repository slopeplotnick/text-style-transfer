These methods encounter significant challenges in two primary domains. Firstly, concerning data utilization, existing studies predominantly focus on numerical and temporal datasets, with insufficient integration of textual information. This limitation hinders the synthesis of diverse data types, such as market sentiment and policy narratives. Secondly, the inherent limitations of the models are evident. While neural networks excel in pattern recognition, they lack the capacity for deeper semantic understanding, including the identification of causal relationships between events and price fluctuations, thus proving inadequate in complex market scenarios. The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) represents a paradigm shift, facilitating a transition from mere historical pattern analysis to the incorporation of memory functions. Notably, FinMem developed a financial memory system that aggregates summaries of news and events, whereas MagicNet introduced a stratified memory system that temporally organizes market data. Despite these advancements, issues persist in the architecture of their memory systems, which predominantly store discrete textual or data fragments without integrating them into a cohesive narrative of event sequences. Furthermore, these systems heavily rely on semantic similarity, posing challenges in addressing more intricate scenarios. Consequently, while these methods mark progress towards utilizing memory, substantial efforts are required to enhance the comprehension and application of relevant information, which constitutes the primary focus of this paper.  Regarding event-driven stock prediction, this methodology seeks to elucidate the fundamental causes underlying stock price variations. Prior to the introduction of LLMs, this field evolved from analyzing the impact of events on stock returns to the automated extraction of event signals from text, employing rudimentary models such as Bag-of-Words, topic models, and subsequently advanced deep learning frameworks like BERT. However, a persistent challenge across these techniques is their quantitative focus. Even when event labels or sentiment scores are extracted, they are transformed into features for temporal models like LSTM or Transformer. Consequently, these models tend to identify data patterns rather than the intrinsic logic of events, leading to a considerable loss of critical details and a lack of profound analytical reasoning.

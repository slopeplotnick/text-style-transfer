We evaluated seven methodologies for model amalgamation: Linear, Karcher Mean, SLERP, NuSLERP, TIES, DELLA, and Nearswap. These techniques were employed to integrate a bias-inverted model with a pre-trained large language model. Linear, as proposed by Wortsman et al. (2022), is the most straightforward method, involving the addition and averaging of the weights of fine-tuned models, offering a cost-effective and efficient solution. Karcher Mean, introduced by Goddard et al. (2024), adopts an alternative strategy by taking into account the curved space of model parameters, thereby aiding in non-flat spaces. SLERP, also credited to Goddard in 2024, amalgamates model weights along a curved trajectory on a sphere, preserving the geometric integrity of the space. NuSLERP, another innovation from Goddard in 2024, modifies SLERP by allowing distinct model components to mix differently, focusing on critical segments to achieve enhanced balance and performance. TIES, as developed by Yadav (2023), integrates models by examining parameter discrepancies crucial for specific tasks, thereby mitigating interference while retaining each model's strengths. DELLA, introduced by Deep in 2024, reduces interference by selectively pruning less significant updates and employing an intelligent mechanism to retain essential components, thus preserving the original model's efficacy.

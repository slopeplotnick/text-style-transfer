To our knowledge, this study represents the first application of an open-vocabulary object detector to enhance the comprehension of high-resolution images by Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Experimental evaluations indicate that the detector reliably identifies relevant targets while disregarding extraneous regions. We propose MRD, a generalizable and training-free framework that employs a multi-resolution fusion strategy to address semantic similarity issues. Additionally, MRD incorporates open-set detection results to improve the representation of target areas, leading to enhanced overall system integration. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate the efficacy of MRD, showcasing its broad adaptability across diverse contexts. The framework achieves substantial performance improvements on both single-object and multi-object tasks in various MLLMs and high-resolution image benchmarks.    To improve high-resolution image understanding in MLLMs without increasing the complexity of visual tokens, prior studies have explored the integration of high-resolution visual encoders. For instance, methods like Vary and Deepseek-VL utilize the SAM encoder to enhance MLLM performance on high-resolution images. Another prominent approach involves the use of Native/Dynamic-Resolution MLLMs, which process images at their original resolution. These models aim to generate a sequence of visual tokens that retains the image's native size, preserving both fine-grained details and spatial fidelity. To address the challenges posed by long sequences and increased computational complexity, such models implement techniques like sliding window attention, dynamic masking, and patch-based encoding. Notable advancements in this domain include the InternVL series, which segments high-resolution images into fixed-size patches for processing, and models such as Qwen2.5-VL, which directly train Vision Transformers (ViTs) on native-resolution images. The latter approach enables the model to transform an entire high-resolution image into a single token sequence, potentially improving contextual understanding by capturing global image information.

To address this research question, we decomposed the task into two distinct components, elaborated in Section 2. Initially, we establish connections between the query's requirements and specific product features, thereby constructing associative links. Subsequently, these links facilitate the comparison of product features with the query's stipulated requirements. The model must exhibit proficiency in three critical competencies to execute this procedure. Firstly, it necessitates an understanding of the specific terminology and implicit details within the query through knowledge acquisition, followed by reasoning to discern the query's intent and the requisite product features. For instance, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a), the model must interpret "morning C, evening A" as Vitamin C and retinol, and recognize "for mom" as indicative of suitability for middle-aged women. Secondly, the model must reconcile information from diverse formats, such as text and images, to align the query with product features. For example, as depicted in Fig. 1(b), if the query specifies "yellow," absent from the textual information, the model must analyze the product image to ascertain the color. Thirdly, the model is required to adhere to predefined decision-making rules. For example, in Fig. 1(c), if the query involves "LV bags" but pertains to a second-hand item, the model must transcend mere feature matching and invoke specific rules to assess compatibility with the query.

The increasing reliance on large language models as automated evaluators for the assessment of outputs generated by other systems is encapsulated in the concept termed "Thinking-LLM-as-a-Judge." This approach offers a more cost-effective and expedient alternative to human evaluation, which is often resource-intensive in both time and financial terms. To address the inherent variability and errors associated with singular judgment instances, a prevalent strategy involves utilizing substantial computational resources to generate multiple evaluations and ratings for each item under scrutiny. However, the efficacy of the final judgment largely hinges on the aggregation methodology employed to synthesize these disparate evaluations. Conventional methods, such as majority voting or confidence-weighted scoring, frequently fail to deliver statistically optimal decisions, particularly in scenarios characterized by ties. For instance, mere vote tallying cannot discern the nuances between a narrow 5-to-4 decision and a decisive 9-to-0 verdict, thereby overlooking critical insights into the strength of evidence. This oversight diminishes the reliability of the evaluations. Our research underscores the pivotal role of aggregation in harnessing computational resources effectively. We propose an innovative approach termed Distribution-Calibrated Aggregation, which transcends mere vote counting. This method comprehensively analyzes positive, negative, and tied votes to retain the full spectrum of information. Employing a model that considers three potential outcomes, it accounts for both preference strength and the likelihood of ties.

We evaluate the temporal alignment between the source and edited videos using four key metrics: **speech**, which examines mouth movements; **gaze**, which tracks eye movements; **blink**, which measures the frequency of eye blinking; and **pose**, which analyzes body movements. These metrics enable the assessment of various motion-related aspects and are utilized for data selection and verification, though they do not contribute to model training. To assess the quality and consistency of video edits, we employ two additional metrics derived from CLIP. The first, **Directional CLIP Score**, evaluates whether the per-frame changes align with the intended overall edit direction. Two variants of this metric are utilized: one measures the alignment between the first edited frame and subsequent frames, while the other assesses the correspondence between the edits and the text prompt. The latter variant provides a more equitable evaluation for text-based methods, such as LucyEdit, by focusing on textual consistency. The former variant determines whether changes across frames visually align with alterations observed in the initial frame. The second metric, **CLIP-Text Alignment Score**, quantifies the degree to which each video frame corresponds to the provided textual description, thereby ensuring semantic consistency throughout the video. Together, these two metrics—Directional CLIP Score and CLIP-Text Alignment Score—facilitate a comprehensive evaluation of the video's adherence to the text-based directives over time.

Challenges such as climate change and the transition to alternative energy sources necessitate interdisciplinary scientific collaboration. However, the academic domain still lacks a dedicated degree program in Mineral Exploration. Concurrently, the rapid generation of information, coupled with advancements in computational algorithms, has enabled the efficient synthesis of vast quantities of data, surpassing traditional methods of manual journal referencing. Despite these advancements, there has been a notable decline in interest among professionals in the Western world in fields such as economic geology and mining engineering. This trend underscores the critical need to preserve legacy knowledge regarding ore deposit formation, which often remains buried within historical geological reports across various regions. High-quality geoscientific databases, exemplified by those in Canada and Australia, are urgently required on a global scale. Notably, in Africa, only a single nation (Zambia, via gsd.gov.zm) has developed such an accessible database. The online accessibility of these repositories is essential for attracting increased investment in the sector.  This commentary advocates for enhanced collaboration between scientific and industry stakeholders to innovate mineral exploration methodologies. Traditional approaches, which often rely on surface outcrops and deterministic geophysical inversion techniques, are increasingly inadequate in "under cover" exploration scenarios, as evidenced by declining discovery rates. Emphasis must shift toward decision-making frameworks that incorporate risk assessment by quantifying uncertainty, thereby minimizing errors in drilling operations. Specifically, epistemic uncertainty—reflecting gaps in understanding of orebody characteristics—should be measured and acknowledged. Furthermore, advancements are required in geostatistical methods to manage data more effectively across appropriate spatial and temporal scales, mitigating the prevalence of false positives in exploration outcomes.

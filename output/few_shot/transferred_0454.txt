In terms of problem-solving methodologies, the SWE-agent Dev initially identifies the bug within the `client.py` file before developing a test to reproduce the issue. Subsequently, it undertakes iterative edits to various file sections, making two unsuccessful attempts, revising additional portions, and ultimately running reproduction tests to validate the fix prior to submitting the patch. By contrast, SWE-agent DSK-V3 also begins by locating the bug within `client.py`, but proceeds with a single edit followed by immediate submission of the patch, without testing for correction. Although both agents ultimately resolve the issue, their workflows reveal distinct inefficiencies. SWE-agent Dev performs line-by-line modifications and revisits failed edits before finalizing its corrections, whereas SWE-agent DSK-V3 completes the patch in a single instance, relying solely on conceptual evaluations of edge cases rather than executing tests. Neither agent systematically validates their patch through rigorous testing mechanisms, with SWE-agent Dev exclusively verifying reproduction tests and SWE-agent DSK-V3 relying on speculative reasoning.  This case study highlights the relevance of process analysis in understanding agents’ problem-solving behaviors, particularly in identifying missteps and inefficiencies. Recent efforts in agent analysis have focused on examining procedural steps to pinpoint failures; however, such manual approaches are susceptible to bias, may lack comprehensiveness, and are suboptimal for emergent systems. Moreover, sequential step analysis fails to encapsulate agents’ overall behavioral strategies, such as task execution and planning efficacy, thereby necessitating advanced methodologies for robust evaluations.

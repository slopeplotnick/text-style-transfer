File editing tools, such as `create`, `str_replace`, and `insert`, are employed to create or modify test files for various purposes, including identifying issues (localization), resolving software bugs (patching), or validating patches (validation). Debugging processes often leverage testing to identify inconsistencies or verify the efficacy of patches. Agents, particularly within this domain, utilize Python for executing new tests and employ `pytest` for legacy test cases. Notably, the same tool or command can be adapted for diverse tasks depending on the context of application.   In the context of the SWE-agent Development environment, the GRAPHECTORY system identifies critical feedback loops at edges 9 and 10, which reveal potential cognitive or operational challenges in the agent’s reasoning processes or tool utilization. Such limitations frequently result in repeated failed edits or necessitate plan revisions (cf. §1). GRAPHECTORY also facilitates the computation of various metrics to analyze agent systems. Table 1 delineates six metrics supported by GRAPHECTORY and methods for their computation. These metrics serve to quantify an agent’s workload in task completion. While the metrics do not inherently indicate the utility or efficiency of the tasks performed, they provide insights into how well the workload corresponds to the problem's complexity and whether agents effectively solve the problem.  The following metrics are particularly noteworthy: - **Temporal Edge Count (TEC):** TEC measures the frequency of action transitions over time. A higher TEC indicates a longer solution path. For instance, as illustrated in Figure 2, the TEC value for SWE-agent Dev (14) exceeds that of SWE-agent DSK-V3 (8). Similar to Node Count (NC), TEC increases with problem complexity, aligning GRAPHENCY complexity with problem difficulty. - **Loop Count (LC):** LC quantifies the recurrence of repeated actions. Elevated LC values, as discussed in §3.2, often suggest suboptimal strategies, such as repetitive failed actions (e.g., the loop at edge 9 in Figure 2-a) or re-evaluations following ineffective solutions (e.g., back edge 10 in Figure 2-a; §3.3.2). Alternatively, LC may reflect iterative problem-solving in response to complex challenges. LC is conceptually analogous to Cyclomatic Complexity [35] in software, which evaluates branching and alternative pathways. In GRAPHENCY, branching signifies localized changes in reasoning paths. - **Average Loop Length (ALL):** ALL measures the number of actions executed before a repetition occurs. A prolonged ALL suggests deficiencies in situational awareness or context gathering. Empirical results highlight that agents incorporating more extensive context during localization demonstrate shorter loop lengths, indicating they promptly identify ineffective strategies and adapt their plans (§3.2.1 and §3.3.2).  In summary, the metrics analyzed through GRAPHECTORY provide a quantitative framework for evaluating agent performance and reasoning processes, especially in relation to task complexity and problem-solving efficacy.

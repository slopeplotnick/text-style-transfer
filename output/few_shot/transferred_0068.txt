Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have shown advancements in recent research; however, they continue to encounter challenges in discerning fine details within high-resolution images. To enhance the capability of these models in understanding high-resolution images, researchers typically adopt one of two approaches. The first approach involves further training of the models utilizing techniques such as supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, which may reduce the models' adaptability to other tasks. The second approach, on the other hand, circumvents additional training and employs methods such as image region searching to identify salient elements, although these methods can be time-consuming and might overlook certain objects, particularly in complex images with numerous elements. In our investigation, we examined the impact of the size of image regions (or crops) on model performance. Our experiments indicated that the resolution of these image regions is crucial, as varying object sizes are optimally represented at different resolutions. Consequently, we developed the Multi-Resolution Detection (MRD) framework. We evaluated this framework using two model variants, LLaVA-ov and LLaVA-v1.5, on a standard benchmark. The findings, presented in Figure 3, demonstrated optimal performance for both single-object and multi-object tasks when the resolution of image regions was configured to 112. This suggests that a resolution of 112 is potentially optimal for these tasks.

The EMOPIA dataset is an invaluable resource for the study of emotion-based music generation. Despite the scarcity of large datasets containing human-annotated MIDI files, most existing collections comprise fewer than 200 MIDI files with emotion labels. EMOPIA represents the largest available dataset, featuring 1,087 music clips derived from 387 songs, with emotional annotations provided by four individuals. It categorizes emotions into four classes based on the valence-arousal plane, further detailed in the Appendix (Section B). Additionally, the GiantMIDI-Piano dataset, encompassing 10,855 solo piano compositions by 2,786 composers, is employed for research purposes such as classical music analysis and symbolic music generation. To align our textual data with the EMOPIA files, we consolidated 27 emotion labels into four categories corresponding to the AV quadrants. This categorization employed the NRC VAD lexicon, which provides arousal and valence scores for 55,000 words, facilitating the assignment of each emotion to one of the four categories. Figure 1 illustrates the mappings and their positions on the AV plane, with Appendix A offering further insight into the VAD framework and its integration with EMOPIA. MIDI files are utilized for encoding and creating symbolic music data due to their ability to compactly store musical performance details while encapsulating emotion-related aspects of music. Their widespread availability enhances their utility for training purposes. Our approach treats each MIDI file as a sequence of musical events, effectively leveraging sequence-to-sequence models for both learning and music generation.

Large language models (LLMs) are rapidly advancing and are increasingly capable of performing tasks such as writing academic papers, reviewing literature, and drafting scientific reports. LLM-based systems for survey creation are transforming the extraction of information from vast datasets by integrating search, organization, and synthesis into a streamlined process. Both academic and industry entities have developed various survey creation systems, which can be categorized into three primary types: general systems for composing extended texts (e.g., Kimi and GLM), research paper writing tools (e.g., Chengpian and Doubao Paper Mode), and tools specifically designed for academic survey creation (e.g., SurveyGo, SurveyX, and Panshi ScienceOne). These tools are capable of processing large volumes of research papers, extracting salient information, and generating well-organized survey drafts that incorporate citations and maintain logical coherence. However, despite significant progress in system development, establishing robust evaluation metrics remains a critical challenge. Current assessment practices predominantly rely on subjective opinions, which fail to provide consistent or reproducible benchmarks for system comparison, performance analysis, and subsequent enhancement. This inconsistency hinders efforts to ensure systems meet high-quality standards and continue improving. Therefore, establishing a standardized evaluation framework is imperative to assess the quality and reliability of these systems, facilitating comparative analysis among researchers. To address this need, we have developed SurveyEval, an evaluation tool for LLM-generated surveys, assessing them on three criteria: overall quality, coherence of structure, and accuracy of references. Our evaluation, conducted across seven academic fields, enhances the LLM-as-a-Judge methodology by incorporating human-written surveys to improve alignment between automated assessments and human judgment.

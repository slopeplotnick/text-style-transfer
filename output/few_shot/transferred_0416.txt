To ensure comparability across different studies, it is essential that the samples exhibit similarity along certain dimensions. This was assessed through the application of a Multigroup Confirmatory Factor Analysis (Multigroup-CFA), which allowed for the examination of two samples, one derived from empirical data and the other from simulations, as distinct cohorts with iterative incorporation of additional constraints. This analysis was compartmentalized into several components: assessment of configural invariance (H2.1), measurement invariance (H2.2), scalar invariance (H2.3), and residual invariance (H2.4). Configural invariance signifies identical structural patterns across both groups, while measurement invariance facilitates the comparison of latent factor differences. Scalar invariance permits the comparison of latent mean scores, and residual invariance allows for the comparison of observed means, enabling further analysis of manifest scores. Establishing these invariances indicates that the simulated data can be considered equivalent to real-world data. Additionally, it was hypothesized (H3) that the responses from the real-world and simulated datasets would exhibit substantial similarity, and (H4) that the score distributions would be largely congruent. In pursuit of determining the extent to which the simulated data mimics real-world data, a Mann-Whitney U test was initially employed to evaluate mean differences, followed by a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test to assess distributional similarities.

The level of specificity or detail in a prompt can significantly influence the performance of large language models (LLMs) in problem-solving tasks; however, this area remains underexplored. This paper introduces a framework named DETAIL, which investigates the performance of LLMs when presented with prompts featuring varying degrees of detail. Utilizing GPT-4, the researchers contrived prompts of differing specificity levels, assessed their specificity via perplexity metrics, and evaluated response accuracy by employing GPT-based semantic equivalence. This study, conducted on 30 novel reasoning tasks using GPT-4 and O3-mini, revealed that prompts with higher specificity typically resulted in enhanced accuracy, particularly for smaller models and tasks necessitating procedural following. The findings underscore the necessity to tailor prompts according to contextual demands and provide resources and data for future research endeavors. LLMs have undergone significant evolution due to their remarkable proficiency in text generation, with continuous and rapid advancements. They now surpass the foremost methods across various domains, transcending mere competence in simple tasks. Recent evaluations predominantly focus on the problem-solving capabilities of these models concerning mathematical or multi-step challenges. Enhancing the reasoning abilities of LLMs is critical, prompting the development of novel methods utilizing refined prompting techniques. Advanced strategies, such as Chain-of-Thought, Tree of Thoughts, and Graph of Thoughts, decompose problems into smaller, manageable steps to bolster reasoning capabilities. Nevertheless, a prominent challenge lies in determining the optimal level of prompt specificity to achieve superior outcomes. While detailed prompts can effectively guide the model, they may constrain its cognitive processes; conversely, vague prompts endow greater freedom but may lead to confusion or inaccuracies.

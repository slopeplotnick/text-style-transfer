In this study, we employed a variety of machine learning techniques to analyze EEG data for the classification of motor imagery tasks. Initially, we preprocessed the data and extracted salient features, followed by the application of several classification algorithms. One such algorithm was logistic regression with elastic-net regularization (Zou and Hastie, 2005), which integrates L1 and L2 penalties to efficiently manage sparse data and mitigate overfitting. The saga solver, known for its effectiveness with large datasets, was utilized for optimization. Additionally, intercept scaling was adjusted to 1000 to account for features of varying scales, and a random state of 42 was used to ensure reproducibility.  Furthermore, we explored a random forest classifier (Breiman, 2001), which enhances predictive accuracy through an ensemble of decision trees, thereby reducing overfitting compared to single decision tree models. This approach is particularly adept at modeling complex feature interactions. Additionally, support vector machines (SVMs) (Cortes and Vapnik, 1995) were employed with two configurations: a linear kernel for linearly separable data, and a radial basis function (RBF) kernel for data requiring kernel-induced mapping to higher dimensions to discern patterns.  We also implemented Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) (Fisher, 1936) to maximize intergroup variance for datasets amenable to clear group separation. Regarding neural networks, several multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) were evaluated, varying in complexity from a single hidden layer to more intricate architectures such as three-layer configurations with neuron arrangements like (10, 30, 10) or (20,) (Rumelhart et al., 1986). Activation functions tested included ReLU and logistic functions. Training utilized stochastic gradient descent (SGD) and adam solvers (Kingma and Ba, 2014), both of which adapt learning rates automatically. To curtail overfitting, L2 regularization was applied via the alpha parameter, with various values assessed to determine optimal performance. Some MLPs maintained a constant learning rate to ensure training stability. Both the SGD and adam solvers were fine-tuned to optimize outcomes for specific feature extraction methods. Each MLP configuration was systematically numbered (MLP_1 to MLP_17) for consistent reference across all evaluations.

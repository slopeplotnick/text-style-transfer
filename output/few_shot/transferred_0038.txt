At the conclusion of the study, participants were invited to provide additional thoughts regarding AI-generated music (see Supplement C for precise wording). To assess personality traits, the researchers utilized a brief version of the Big Five Inventory (BFI-10), validated for both English and German contexts. The five dimensions assessed included Openness. Demographic data were also gathered, encompassing age, gender, nationality, and educational attainment, to better understand the sample and evaluate potential influences on outcomes. Although the survey permitted non-binary gender responses, all participants identified as either male or female; thus, gender was dichotomously categorized. Participants' comprehension of the survey language was evaluated to ensure they could discern subtle differences in emotion-descriptive terminology. Data analysis was conducted using Python (version 3.9.20), employing pandas (version 2.2.2) for data organization, scipy (version 1.12.0) for conducting tests such as correlation and ANOVA, and statsmodels (version 0.14.4) for mixed-effects model analyses. Graphical representations were created using R (version 4.3.3) with the ggplot2 package. For the qualitative analysis of open-ended responses concerning AI-generated music, Braun and Clarke's (2006) methodological approach was applied and analyzed using MAXQDA (version 24.10). Additional data verification was performed by averaging individual song ratings, further detailed in Appendix C. These verifications affirmed that musical preference varied by composer without significant variations in emotional intensity. To evaluate whether the experimental configuration influenced music appreciation or emotional intensity, controlling for individual differences and music type, linear mixed-effects models were employed. These models incorporated random effects for both participants and songs, while also considering personality traits, listening habits, and the specific music model employed.

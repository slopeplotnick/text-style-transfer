Our research involved utilizing the Swivuriso dataset to train three speech recognition models: Whisper-large-v3-Turbo, Wav2Vec-BERT 2.0, and Massively Multilingual Speech (MMS). Extended training durations resulted in enhanced model performance, with notable improvements of 0.22 for Whisper-large-v3-Turbo, 0.27 for Wav2Vec-BERT, and 0.15 for MMS. Additionally, we examined the impact of varying amounts of training data on performance. Whisper-v3-Turbo underwent training over a single round with 50 hours, 150 hours, and 250 hours of data. Our findings indicate that the Swivuriso dataset contributes significantly to the advancement of speech recognition across diverse models and languages. Training consistently reduced error rates, proving effective even in the initial training stages. Augmenting the amount of training data further enhanced performance across most languages. These findings underscore the utility of Swivuriso as a valuable resource for advancing South African speech recognition and refining multilingual speech models.  Prior research has emphasized the importance of developing robust datasets for speech recognition, a task demanding high accuracy and minimal errors. Progress in the context of African and other low-resource languages has been relatively slower. Abdulmumin et al. explored the FLORES dataset to optimize it for evaluating four African languages: Hausa, Northern Sotho (Sepedi), Xitsonga, and isiZulu. Native speakers reviewed and amended the dataset to rectify issues potentially impacting tasks such as machine translation. Their interventions improved the dataset's accuracy and reliability, substantiated through comprehensive reviews and data analysis. This study highlights the critical role of native speaker involvement to ensure datasets align with linguistic and cultural contexts. We likewise collaborated extensively with native speakers to enhance our dataset's accuracy, as advocated by prior findings.  Furthermore, another study informed our approach to sentiment analysis, drawing attention to the disparity between the availability of sentiment analysis datasets for widely spoken versus low-resource languages, such as Hindi. This study specifically addressed sentiment analysis in Hindi on socially pertinent topics, resulting in the creation of a balanced dataset containing positive, negative, and neutral sentiment annotations.

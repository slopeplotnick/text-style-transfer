Current evaluations of video models predominantly emphasize fundamental physical knowledge, often constrained to quotidian scenarios necessitating only a rudimentary comprehension of physical mechanics. To address this, we have developed VideoScience-Bench, a novel benchmark designed to assess the capability of video models to comprehend and engage with scientific concepts. This benchmark encompasses a range of scientific topics requiring collegiate-level knowledge, spanning 103 distinct concepts in physics and chemistry. The evaluation framework is designed to determine whether video models can decipher scientific setups and predict outcomes autonomously. The assessment criteria focus on five principal dimensions: alignment with the prompt, predictive accuracy regarding anticipated outcomes, management of motion, adherence to invariant principles, and overall coherence. Complementarily, we have introduced VideoScience-Judge, an evaluative system employing a checklist methodology. This system generates checklists tailored to the assessment, identifies critical video frames, and pinpoints key moments of action, thereby facilitating a comprehensive scoring process. The resulting scores are synthesized into a singular metric indicative of the model's reasoning proficiency. Notably, concordance between scores from VideoScience-Judge and expert evaluations surpasses other assessments centered on basic physical knowledge or video realism. In summary, this research explores the aptitude of video models to concurrently address multiple scientific concepts. Our contributions are threefold: (1) the creation of VideoScience-Bench, a rigorous assessment tool evaluating the scientific reasoning capabilities of video models through authentic examples from 103 physics and chemistry topics; (2) the development of VideoScience-Judge, a systematic evaluative system that utilizes checklists, identifies significant frames, and employs computational techniques to analyze aspects such as motion and object interactions, thereby providing scores reflecting scientific validity and visual quality; (3) conducting a comprehensive evaluation using VideoScience-Bench, which reveals that despite contemporary video models' proficiency in generating realistic and fluid videos, VideoScience-Bench remains challenging. Several models struggle with intricate scientific concepts and frequently violate fundamental physical principles. Among the models tested, Sora-2 and Veo-3 demonstrated superior performance, exhibiting nascent capabilities in scientific reasoning.

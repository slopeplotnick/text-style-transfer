Probabilistic programming languages (PPLs) are a category of programming languages endowed with features for managing probabilistic elements, such as distributions and the corresponding samplers. Numerous well-known examples exist across both imperative and functional paradigms. A significant limitation of current PPLs is their inadequate integration with text-heavy graphs, which are crucial for defining specifications within language model systems (LMS). This gap has prompted the development of LUMOS. Typically, these languages are employed to generate outputs based on user-defined probability distributions; for instance, certain languages are used to create random images or scenes for applications such as self-driving cars, utilizing user-defined scene graphs. However, in order to establish real-world specifications, a fixed graph is utilized, and segments of it are sampled to define scenarios. Domain-specific languages (DSLs) for large language models (LLMs) operate as query languages devoid of probabilistic features. They are instrumental in authoring, optimizing, and securing prompts to elicit appropriate responses from a targeted LMS. They function collaboratively with LUMOS, which assesses LMS behaviors across distributions of prompts specified by LUMOS. LMS alignment encompasses identifying LMS vulnerabilities by evaluating their performance or executing adversarial attacks. Recent advancements have introduced probabilistic certifiers for specific LLM properties, delivering formal assurances for evaluating LMS attributes. This study extends LMS certification using LUMOS to specify and certify any desirable properties.

A more in-depth analysis revealed that individuals' attitudes towards artificial intelligence (AI) exerted a greater influence on the outcomes than the experimental design itself. When integrating attitudes towards AI into the analysis, the experimental setup did not significantly account for preferences in music or the intensity of emotional responses. Nonetheless, attitudes towards AI consistently emerged as significant, corroborating previous findings that suggest perceptions of autonomous machines significantly impact judgments regarding them (Hong et al., 2022). This aligns with Shank et al.'s (2023) assertions that biases toward composers may be contingent on the song and context, such as the song's popularity and its perceived human origin. While certain personality traits and music-related factors were correlated with music appreciation and emotional response, their significance diminished when AI attitudes were considered. Exceptions included the traits of openness to experience and neuroticism, which have been previously identified as influential in shaping musical emotions, particularly those related to happiness and energy (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Juslin et al., 2011; Liljeström et al., 2013). These emotions were prominently reflected in the musical selections analyzed in this study. Notably, attitudes toward AI were distinctly associated with both music appreciation and emotional reactions. Overall, the variance explained by listener traits aligned with findings from other studies (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Strauss et al., 2024), underscoring the pivotal role of AI perceptions in evaluating AI-generated music. Contrary to Ansani et al.'s (2024) findings, no evidence was found that attitudes toward AI altered the functioning of experimental groups, potentially attributable to differing measures of attitudes, a focus on recorded tracks rather than live performances, and the inclusion of three experimental conditions instead of two.

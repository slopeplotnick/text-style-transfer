The prompts were created in the native languages of each language team while the interface remained in English, thereby eliminating the need for translation. Upon completing a specific task path, team members generated prompts in their first language. Prior to commencing the primary task, all team members conducted a brief sample project to familiarize themselves with the required processes. Subsequently, each team developed a minimum of 2,000 prompts after fully understanding the project objectives. The scripted prompts were derived from culturally relevant and domain-specific materials, such as governmental publications detailing public services, Wikipedia entries in South African languages, a vocabulary database for linguistic accuracy, and translated manuals addressing topics like animal health and farming practices.   The data preparation process for these prompts involved rigorous cleaning and preprocessing to ensure suitability for training speech recognition models. This included eliminating irrelevant content, categorizing text by thematic relevance, and identifying or annotating personal information. Once the data was ingested, quality checks were conducted to review structural integrity and consistency. The text was filtered to retain outputs exclusively in the targeted South African languages. Instances where the primary language did not align with expectations were excluded, although confidence scores were preserved as supplementary metadata.

Researchers are actively investigating the operational mechanisms of diffusion models, with a particular focus on understanding the balance between memorization and novel content generation. Prior investigations primarily measured model behavior to elucidate the conditions under which they either replicate learned information or generate novel outputs. These studies predominantly explored model behavior from uninitialized states, utilizing limited datasets such as FFHQ and CIFAR-10, which are instructive for scenarios with limited data availability. However, the propensity to replicate diminishes with the use of extensive datasets, such as ImageNet. Song et al. (2023) observed that when utilizing sizable datasets, initiating sampling from an early stage tends to foster the creation of novel images, whereas commencing from a later point in the process tends to reproduce existing ones. Therefore, a critical question arises: what governs the balance between memorization and novelty in models trained on extensive data? To address this inquiry, we examined the model's training objectives, particularly focusing on flow matching and conditional flow matching. These are traditionally considered challenging due to their reliance on specific unavailable data. Nevertheless, we demonstrated that, within a Gaussian framework and a constrained training dataset, it is feasible to discern the flow, enabling the calculation of the model's optimal trajectory at any given point. Analysis of this trajectory reveals two primary phases in training: initially, the model integrates information from various data points to establish a path, and subsequently, it concentrates on individual data points. Our findings also indicate that the dataset size and the number of samples are critical factors influencing the timing of this transition.

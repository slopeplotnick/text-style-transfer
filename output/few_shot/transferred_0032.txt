The training of high-quality reward models is inherently challenging due to their subjective nature and situational dependencies, coupled with the susceptibility of learned proxies to be deceived. The paucity of annotations exacerbates the difficulty for models to accurately account for nuanced behaviors. Consequently, various strategies have been explored to reduce reliance on annotations. Direct Preference Optimization eschews explicit reward models yet still necessitates preference data; verifiable rewards, while utilizing real outcomes, are limited to scenarios where outcomes can be automatically verified. Self-evaluation approaches face challenges related to prompt sensitivity and bias. Despite these methods' reliance on external signals for quality assessment, they overlook the latent potential within the model's internal representations. The veracity of a generated output, such as the assertion "The capital of France is Paris," should be reflected in the model’s internal processing, where factual consistency is manifest in the hidden states; conversely, fabrication results in divergent patterns. This prompts the inquiry: Is it possible to ascertain text quality based on internal signals, thereby reducing dependency on external guidance for aligning Large Language Models (LLMs)? This study proposes that examining a straightforward geometric attribute of LLM hidden states, termed effective dimensionality, can reliably reflect response quality. Specifically, we advocate for the use of stable rank—a matrix-theoretic measure—as an unsupervised criterion for evaluating generation quality. Stable rank quantifies the number of independent semantic directions utilized in a response by assessing the ratio of total variance to the principal direction variance within the hidden-state representation. It effectively balances information richness with coherence, where a high stable rank indicates information dissemination across many dimensions rather than a few. This theoretical framework is informed by two key areas: firstly, the softmax bottleneck analysis, which underscores the necessity of engaging with a high-dimensional semantic space to accurately model language due to the inherently complex probability distribution of natural language.

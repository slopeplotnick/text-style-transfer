We examine three metrics to determine the values for all method pairs within the HD and FV groups, as well as between these groups. A higher ACS score for inter-group pairs indicates that they achieve success in different domains. The superior ASG score suggests that integrating both HD and FV is more advantageous than employing methods from a single group. Additionally, the AECR score is significantly elevated for inter-group pairs, implying that when one group encounters errors, the other group typically compensates effectively. This evidence highlights that HD and FV are not only distinct but also synergistic, advocating the development of models that incorporate both groups. The previous section demonstrated the statistical complementarity of HD and FV, and we now explore the reasons behind their differential performance. A case study is conducted to identify typical failure points for each group. Two primary issues are identified: FV often errs when it fails to locate substantial evidence, whereas HD sometimes inaccurately identifies errors due to misinterpretation of diverse phrasing. Understanding these challenges enhances our comprehension of their collaborative function and underscores the rationale for designing methods that integrate both approaches, a topic we will discuss in subsequent sections.

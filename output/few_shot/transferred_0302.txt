The GPT-5 model frequently declines tasks, a behavior that persists even with the provision of additional context. This high refusal rate results in suboptimal performance on harmful tasks; however, given the already low harm score, these findings yield limited insights. Further testing on non-harmful tasks with over 10,000 tokens of context is not pursued due to the stability of results and the prohibitive cost of such evaluations. The performance of the model is influenced by the type of context padding employed. As illustrated in Figure 3, various padding types exert differential effects on model efficacy. Specifically, for the GPT-4.1-nano, non-relevant padding achieves the highest scores, followed by relevant padding, random padding, and, lastly, multi-task padding, which scores the lowest. In contrast, Grok 4 Fast deviates from this pattern due to its elevated refusal rate, resulting in scores that remain relatively consistent regardless of padding type. Within the token range of 50,000 to 100,000, Grok 4 Fast exhibits signs of collapse, with padding scores converging. The placement of padding significantly impacts model performance, as demonstrated in Figure 4. Preceding the task description with padding decelerates performance degradation compared to post-task description padding. This may be attributed to the closer proximity of task description and execution, which facilitates more effective model attention. Contemporary attention mechanisms, such as sliding window and Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), are less adept at handling widely separated tokens due to rare exposure during training. When padding follows the task description, the model must bridge a substantial gap between instruction and execution, potentially accelerating performance deterioration.

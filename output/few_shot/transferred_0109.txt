Sentiment analysis plays a pivotal role in contemporary Natural Language Processing (NLP), contributing to a range of applications such as digital governance, public health monitoring, inclusive access to financial services, and online content moderation. However, despite advancements in deep learning and language modeling globally, sentiment analysis research has inadequately addressed low-resource South African languages. This gap manifests in a scarcity of annotated datasets, limited lexical resources, and constrained opportunities for cross-linguistic knowledge transfer. Consequently, models optimized for widely spoken languages such as English and French exhibit diminished performance in African linguistic contexts, subsequently restricting equitable access to NLP technologies for speakers of these languages.   Existing approaches to multilingual sentiment analysis have predominantly focused on methods such as language translation, cross-linguistic embedding techniques, and the development of language-specific word lists. While these methods have demonstrated some success, they also face notable limitations. First, machine-translated lexicons frequently suffer from inaccuracies and fail to capture culturally specific semantic nuances, particularly for African languages with complex morphosyntactic structures. Second, word extraction methods reliant on extensive datasets are hindered by the unavailability of such datasets for low-resource languages. Lastly, although African language models such as AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa represent significant advancements, their efficacy remains constrained due to the absence of high-quality sentiment lexicons to support robust classification in many African languages.  To address these challenges, this study introduces **TriLex**, a novel three-phase framework designed to expand sentiment lexicons for underrepresented African languages. The TriLex framework comprises: (1) leveraging datasets to identify candidate sentiment-bearing words; (2) mapping these words across English, French, and African languages to ensure semantic alignment; and (3) employing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with large language models to refine, validate, and extend the sentiment lexicons using multilingual retrieval signals. Specifically, this work contributes the following:  1. The introduction of **TriLex**, a systematic approach to constructing sentiment lexicons for low-resource South African languages. 2. The development and publication of a comprehensive multilingual sentiment word list covering multiple South African languages to facilitate future research in this area. 3. The first extensive evaluation of AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa within the context of expanded sentiment lexicons, focusing on isiXhosa and isiZulu.  By addressing existing limitations in multilingual sentiment analysis, the proposed framework and associated resources aim to advance the inclusion of low-resource African languages in NLP technologies and research.

In contemporary Natural Language Processing (NLP), substantial advancements have been made through the development of large-scale language models such as GPT and BERT. These models exhibit capabilities that include generating human-like text, responding to complex inquiries, and engaging in coherent dialogue. Although a method that produces an ostensibly coherent response might be considered as navigating in the correct direction, it is essential to recognize that the answers generated by these models, while contextually relevant and logically structured, may lack veracity as they are not necessarily grounded in factual data. This phenomenon is referred to as "hallucination," representing a significant challenge in the broader application of these models. It is analogous to a narrative process where fictitious details are interwoven with factual storytelling. Consequently, these models may generate output that appears accurate but is not substantiated by their training data. This is akin to a GPS device that asserts confidence in its navigational capabilities yet occasionally directs to non-existent paths. Technically, hallucinations occur when the generated content fails to align with factual information or anticipated outcomes drawn from training data, manifesting as false claims, fabricated references, or flawed reasoning. Similar to a GPS system that projects an air of accuracy but ultimately misguides, these models are prone to errors. The AI research community is actively addressing this issue, acknowledging that even the most sophisticated models can experience hallucinations. While the capabilities of these models are impressive, their reliability, particularly in the context of factual accuracy, remains a concern, especially in critical domains such as healthcare, legal practice, and scholarly research.

Video language models are increasingly employed in applications such as AI-enhanced eyewear and domestic robotics. These models are required to process extended-duration videos, sometimes spanning several hours or even days. Recent innovations incorporate memory systems to extract salient segments from the video content, enabling the model to concentrate on essential information without necessitating frame-by-frame analysis. This approach proves more cost-effective and efficient compared to the exhaustive processing of video frames, which is computationally expensive. Predominantly, contemporary methodologies still heavily depend on textual data, converting video segments into captions or summaries to facilitate information retrieval and comprehension. While one strategy employs some visual data, it primarily focuses on object recognition with limited other uses. Typically, these models select a predetermined number of video clips for analysis, for instance, three 30-second clips. This methodology presents two significant challenges. Firstly, it inadequately integrates video visuals with textual memory during information retrieval and generation. Detailed visual information is crucial for tasks involving feature recognition, spatial comprehension, or accurate scene depiction—elements that textual data alone cannot fully convey. Furthermore, consistently employing both captions and video frames in response generation may lead to confusion within the model, as excessive visual input can be distracting. Thus, there is a need for an optimized selection of textual and visual elements to extract the most pertinent information in response to inquiries, an area that remains underexplored in current research.

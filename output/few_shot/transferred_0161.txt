Recent advancements in Large Language Model (LLM) technology have significantly enhanced their capabilities, enabling their application across various domains, including intelligent interactions and support for scientific research. In the healthcare sector, LLMs demonstrate potential utility in assisting with disease diagnosis and optimizing diagnostic and treatment workflows. Within the context of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), a fundamental principle known as "Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment" (SDT) underpins clinical practice. SDT involves comprehensive analysis of patient-specific information to identify a condition and formulate a personalized treatment strategy, wherein the management of ostensibly identical illnesses may vary depending on situational factors.   While TCM-specific LLMs, such as Zhongjing and HuatuoGPT, have been developed, practical deployment of these models in clinical settings remains challenging due to the intricate, individualized nature of TCM. A significant limitation lies in the absence of robust methodologies to comprehensively evaluate the efficacy of such models in clinical decision-making. Specifically, there exists a lack of dedicated tools to assess how these models engage in reasoning processes within TCM contexts. Consequently, there is an exigent demand within the research and industry communities for the creation of a high-quality dataset that facilitates fair and thorough evaluation of TCM-focused LLMs. Such a dataset would serve to enhance model performance, assess their practical utility, and potentially enable their integration into real-world clinical practice.  However, constructing such a dataset poses substantial challenges due to the inherent complexity and diversity of TCM. Central to TCM practice is the holistic consideration of the individual, which encompasses variations in symptom presentation, disease progression, constitution, and lifestyle. For example, individuals diagnosed with the same illness may exhibit differing symptoms due to such factors, complicating efforts to establish standardized evaluation criteria. Furthermore, TCM diagnosis employs a comprehensive and multivariate approach to information gathering, causal inference, and treatment determination, necessitating a dataset that encompasses a broad spectrum of clinical scenarios. Additional complexities arise from the frequent occurrence of comorbidities among patients and the subjectivity inherent in diagnostic methodologies, further complicating the evaluation process. Addressing these multifaceted challenges is imperative for the development of a robust dataset capable of rigorously assessing the effectiveness of TCM-oriented LLMs in real-world medical applications.

This study addresses the issue of excessive adherence to prior beliefs in reasoning models by introducing a novel metric to evaluate the extent to which a model's belief updates conform to the Martingale property—a fundamental concept in Bayesian reasoning. The Martingale property posits that the evolution of beliefs should be unpredictable and not determinable solely based on past states. Specifically, the premise is that if a model's future belief states can be reliably predicted from its past beliefs, the model fails to comply with the Martingale property, indicating an over-reliance on prior beliefs.   To investigate this phenomenon, experiments were conducted across three domains: event prediction, opinion-based question answering, and academic paper review. A comparative analysis of various models and reasoning methodologies revealed that models frequently exhibit excessive belief adherence, with results demonstrating that future belief states are often predictable based on prior ones—contravening the Martingale property. Furthermore, empirical findings underscored that deviations from this property are correlated with decreases in model accuracy.  The proposed measure, termed the Martingale Score, quantitatively assesses the predictability of a model's belief updates solely from its historical belief trajectory. Utilizing this metric, the study evaluated popular reasoning models, uncovering prevalent instances of belief rigidity across diverse domains, prompts, and model architectures.

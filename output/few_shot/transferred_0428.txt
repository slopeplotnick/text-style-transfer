These weights underscore the importance of achieving expected outcomes, establishing the experiment correctly, and maintaining consistency. Table 3 analyzes the comparison between VideoScience-Judge scores across various configurations and human ratings. Under the GPT-5 pro condition, scores are less inflated, yet significant discrepancies persist in dimensions such as Dynamism, Coherence, and Immutability, where the model exhibits a tendency to overestimate its performance. The implementation of a checklist and requiring the model to justify its ratings with evidence from key frames mitigates these overestimations. The incorporation of computer vision tools like ByteTrack, which facilitates identity tracking across frames and provides concrete evidence for penalizing discontinuities or unexpected alterations, notably reduces the scoring discrepancies by 0.07 for Coherence and 0.09 for Immutability, when compared to the standard VideoScience-Judge setup without the checklist. Consequently, the scores become more aligned and less capable of distinguishing between similar models, although its rule-based approach for identifying explicit violations (e.g., "Does the ball fall down?" or "Does water flow correctly?") falls short of capturing the comprehensive essence of physical realism, such as consistent movement trajectories, temporal causality, variations in speed, momentum conservation, and movement smoothness, which human observers naturally discern. These limitations are particularly evident in VideoScience-Bench, which contrasts with the more straightforward, single-rule scenarios of PhyGenBench. VideoScience-Bench encompasses a broad spectrum of scientific concepts (e.g., multi-object interactions or fluid dynamics), where outcomes arise from the interplay of multiple rules rather than a solitary one, with cumulative effects where higher-order dynamics play a pivotal role.

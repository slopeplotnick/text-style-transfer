Agentic systems can be conceptualized as contemporary software architectures comprising multiple interacting components, which manifest interfaces and are orchestrated within software processes. These systems differ from conventional programs as they operate in a stochastic and adaptable manner to address complex problems. Traditionally, the assessment of these systems has focused predominantly on their success or failure, neglecting the examination of their cognitive processes, planning strategies, actions, and adaptive methodologies over time. In response to this oversight, we have developed GRAPHECTORY, a framework designed to map temporal and semantic interactions within these systems. This initiative facilitates the development of metrics and analyses that evaluate system performance beyond mere success criteria. Utilizing GRAPHECTORY, we analyzed 4,000 execution paths of two prominent agentic programming methodologies, SWE-agent and OpenHands, employing four major Large Language Models (LLMs) to address SWE-bench Verified issues. Our automated analyses revealed several insights: (1) agents equipped with superior prompts or more robust LLMs exhibited more intricate GRAPHECTORY structures, indicative of deeper reasoning, broader contextual understanding, and more rigorous verification processes prior to implementing solutions; (2) the problem-solving strategies of agents fluctuate based on problem complexity and the LLM employed—resolved issues typically follow systematic procedures involving identification, rectification, and validation, whereas unresolved issues display disorganized, iterative, or regressive patterns; (3) notwithstanding their efficacy, agentic programming systems frequently encounter inefficiencies, resulting in unnecessarily prolonged execution paths. Agentic systems leveraging large language models (LLMs) have emerged as a promising paradigm for addressing intricate, multi-phase tasks across various domains, including software engineering, web navigation, virtual assistance, scientific exploration, and robotic navigation. Analogous to conventional software systems, agentic systems comprise elements such as LLMs, tools, APIs, and memory, featuring inputs, internal states, operational strategies, and outputs. However, unlike deterministic systems, agentic systems generate procedural pathways—sequences of cognitive, operational, and observational steps—that collectively form the logical framework necessary for task completion. Historically, the evaluation of agentic software systems has been predominantly outcome-oriented, focusing on the capability of the procedural pathway to resolve the given problem.

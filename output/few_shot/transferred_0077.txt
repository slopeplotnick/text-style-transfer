Numerical analysis, though effective, does not yield as comprehensive results as textual analysis. Integrating both modalities approaches the efficacy of text-only analysis, indicating that text carries greater significance while numerical data provides supplementary information. When evaluating diverse datasets and tasks, a hierarchy of importance emerges. For instance, in the detection of misinformation, text proves highly effective. Augmenting text models with a basic numerical layer nearly achieves optimal results on certain datasets, outperforming traditional methods on others. Sole reliance on numerical data is permissible but suboptimal; for instance, in predicting virality, numerical data lags behind textual data. Overall, textual data remains paramount, with numerical data serving a supportive role. This distinction warrants clear articulation. In scenarios where accuracy is critical, adherence to conventional methods that balance sensitivity and precision is advisable. Regarding model configuration, various architectures—such as GRU, LSTM, RNN, CNN, and Transformer—demonstrate comparable performance on particular datasets, with Transformer slightly excelling. Their analogous scores suggest that data utilization and nomenclature are more pivotal than minor model adjustments, particularly under constraints. Recommended guidelines include: (1) Conceptualize virality prediction as a set of constrained, temporally bound tasks, ensuring clarity in result interpretation. (2) Prioritize text-only methodologies for misinformation detection, supplementing with numerical data when available. (3) For rapid assessments, emphasize comprehensiveness while transparently acknowledging potential oversights. (4) Employ straightforward, thoroughly documented experimental setups, prioritizing data quality, error avoidance, and methodological transparency.

When small groups of individuals or systems engage in close, brief interactions, minor issues begin to manifest. As these groups exchange information within partially connected networks, the potential for significant risks emerges due to the propagation of these initial issues. Once a certain threshold of interconnectedness is reached, these issues can escalate into substantial problems that alter the behavior of the group in unexpected ways. This conceptual framework is an initial attempt to facilitate the discussion and study of such behaviors. Absent such a framework, researchers face challenges in identifying, measuring, or managing these behaviors in group settings. To substantiate this concept, extensive experimentation in controlled environments will be necessary, which is an endeavor we plan to pursue subsequently. Initial risks emerge in direct interactions among a limited number of individuals or systems, indicating early signs of broader issues. These risks highlight the fragility of shared understanding within networks even before large-scale collaboration occurs. Although initially minor, these issues have the potential to escalate as interactions progress. Once a problem is integrated into the system's understanding, it proliferates and intensifies with subsequent interactions, outpacing the growth of participants or messages. Greater risks become apparent when larger groups interact within partially connected networks. At this scale, individual issues may not be prominent; however, the nature of interactions exacerbates considerations such as accuracy and diversity. While each component may appear adequate in isolation, their collective operation deviates from expectations. These larger risks underscore that systemic problems can arise even when no single component is entirely deficient. Issues arise from the interactions of partially correct elements rather than isolated errors, leading to a breakdown in local predictability and resulting in instability.

The mechanisms through which language models utilize contextual information to respond to health-related inquiries, and the implications of mixed or conflicting context, represent a critical area of investigation. This study examines the capacity of language models to process extensive and contradictory health-related information using the HEALTHCONTRADICT dataset, which comprises 920 examples. Each example contains a health question, a scientifically validated factual answer, and two documents presenting opposing perspectives. The study explores various methods of question formulation, including accurate, inaccurate, and mixed-context scenarios, to analyze their impact on model responses. Unlike prior evaluations, HEALTHCONTRADICT offers a more nuanced assessment of how language models manage contextual information.   The findings indicate that the efficacy of these models is attributable not only to prior training but also to their ability to leverage valid context while disregarding erroneous information. However, significant challenges remain, as the models sometimes produce plausible yet factually incorrect answers. This concern is particularly pronounced in the domain of health information, where misleading outputs can pose substantial risks. Additionally, the dynamic nature of health knowledge, especially during crises, and the rapid dissemination of unverified information online, influence how these models acquire and apply knowledge.  Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges posed by conflicting or mixed-context information. One method involves modifying the model's internal knowledge when it conflicts with the context, while another traces the underlying rationale for a model's response. These approaches, however, often emphasize either the model's pre-existing knowledge or the immediate context in isolation. An alternative strategy integrates both elements by evaluating consistency between the model's knowledge and the provided context. Another proposed technique generates dual predictions—one grounded in contextual evidence and the other in the model's prior knowledge—thereby accounting for discordant information. Additional methodologies aim to ensure that models select accurate responses in cases of conflicting context and prevent the generation of erroneous outputs.  During training, strategies include the deliberate inclusion of incorrect or irrelevant information to improve the model’s robustness in handling conflicting data. During testing, methodologies such as adversarial detection to identify and mitigate incorrect information, careful question design, multi-reader verification, and the prioritization of robust evidence are employed. Other techniques involve predicting information likely to become outdated to avoid reliance on obsolete data. Current remedial approaches focus either on counteracting the effects of conflicting information or on preventing undesirable model behaviors. A comprehensive integration of these solutions is essential to enhance the reliability and accuracy of language models in addressing health-related inquiries.

The analyses were re-conducted utilizing the ATTARI subscales, revealing that only the behavioral subscale served as a significant predictor, whereas the affective and cognitive subscales did not exert a notable influence when considered collectively (refer to Supplementary Materials, S3). This indicates that individuals' attitudes towards Artificial Intelligence (AI) are predominantly impacted by their readiness to engage with AI, rather than by their general perceptions or emotional responses towards it. In order to expand the dataset and ascertain public sentiment, 178 open-ended responses were analyzed and categorized into themes, which are detailed in Table 2, with comprehensive information available in the Supplementary Materials. Respondents focused on aspects such as the quality of the music, the emotional resonance it evoked, and its perceived humanity. Many participants evaluated AI-generated music based on its technical proficiency, originality, and emotional impact. The concept of 'humanness' emerged frequently, extending beyond mere auditory qualities; some respondents emphasized the significance of the human element, such as the artist's personal influence or the narrative behind a composition, noting that AI-generated music lacked soul and authenticity. Conversely, some respondents were indifferent to the origin of the music, expressing views such as, “If it sounds good, then I don’t mind if it was artificially generated” (ID926). These responses further revealed that individuals' attitudes towards AI influence their evaluation of AI music. Several comments focused more on personal views regarding AI generally, without addressing the specific quality of the music, for instance, “I don’t like anything to do with AI-generated music” (ID914). Additionally, the responses highlighted considerations regarding AI music beyond typical experimental parameters, such as ethical concerns related to the potential displacement of musicians, adverse financial impacts on artists, and unauthorized use of training data (“is built upon data without the consent of the artists that have dedicated decades of their lives to the craft,” ID615). A particular respondent expressed, “I think it undermines and abuses artists” (ID446), illustrating that evaluations of AI music incorporate considerations of fairness in its production and distribution.

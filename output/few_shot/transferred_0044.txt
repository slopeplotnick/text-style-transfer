The comprehension of videos by individuals involves discerning the sequence of events, the motivations behind actions, and the temporal occurrence of significant incidents. Conventional video object segmentation techniques predominantly focus on the visual appearance or categorical classification of objects. In contrast, Reasoning Video Object Segmentation (VOS) advances this capability by requiring the model to interpret ambiguous instructions, employ common sense and causal reasoning, and integrate temporal dynamics with knowledge to identify targets, such as "the runner most likely to win" or "the object causing the accident." This approach not only simplifies but also adds complexity to interpretation and model operation, necessitating extensive data for fine-tuning. The core proposition is that effective video reasoning involves deliberate decision-making rather than impulsive inference. The model must determine the focal point, the timing of attention, and the subject of the inquiry. In response to this, we have developed ReVSeg, a system that eliminates concealed segmentation tokens and transforms reasoning video segmentation into an explicit sequence of reasoning steps. ReVSeg deconstructs reasoning VOS into three stages aligned with model capabilities: video comprehension (assessing the inquiry and ongoing events), temporal grounding (identifying key frames or timestamps relevant to the inquiry), and spatial grounding (locating target objects within selected frames). ReVSeg employs these stages within a singular model, ensuring the continuity of meaning from initial to subsequent steps. This method of reasoning maintains model alignment with its training, circumvents extensive retraining, and systematizes a complex problem into a straightforward process. The primary challenge, once reasoning steps are delineated, is enhancing these steps. Supervised learning is inadequate due to the insufficient labeling of intermediate steps. Consequently, reinforcement learning is utilized, treating reasoning VOS as a behavior rewarded only when the entire decision path culminates in the correct outcome. However, implementing Reinforcement Learning (RL) is challenging: reasoning VOS provides sparse success signals and demands coordination across comprehension, temporal selection, and spatial identification. To address this, we introduce rewards at pivotal decision junctures, ensuring RL refines the model's reasoning process. In conjunction with task decomposition, RL transforms the task from an obscure guessing challenge into a coherent reasoning strategy: decomposition clarifies the model's decision requirements, while RL elucidates the integration of these decisions. This synergy cultivates a reasoning strategy that is both internally coherent and effective in practice.

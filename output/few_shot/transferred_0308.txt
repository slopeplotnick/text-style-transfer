It has been observed that when individuals exhibit entrenched beliefs, they are prone to committing a higher number of errors in domains where the correct answers are known, such as predictive tasks. This observation suggests that the Martingale Score could serve as an effective measure of reasoning quality, even in scenarios characterized by incomplete information. In the context of language models, evaluating Bayesian rationality involves determining whether these models update their beliefs in accordance with Bayes's theorem when presented with evidence. Gupta et al. (2025) demonstrated that language models are capable of belief updating when exposed to a series of coin flips, even if they initially possess biased priors. However, in real-world scenarios involving complex linguistic inputs, Qiu et al. (2025) found that language models fail to adjust their beliefs about user preferences in alignment with predictions derived from Bayesian principles. Falck (2024) further highlighted that language models do not exhibit Bayesian learning when contextual information is provided. Moreover, Zhao (2021) and Wang (2023) showed that the behavior of language models is influenced by the arrangement of examples within prompts.  Several strategies have been proposed to enhance Bayesian reasoning or learning in language models. These include emulating the reasoning processes of an ideal Bayesian agent, integrating abstract reasoning capabilities, and combining abductive and deductive methodologies. Nonetheless, implementing such approaches typically necessitates the incorporation of additional structures, such as Bayesian networks, rendering them challenging to operationalize in practice. It is also well-established that language models inherit various biases from the human data on which they are trained. Interestingly, language models may exhibit biases distinct from those observed in human judges, such as a preference for specific positions, an inclination toward longer responses, a bias toward positive expressions, and an overconfidence in their outputs. Among these, confirmation bias is particularly noteworthy due to its association with polarization. In fact, it is hypothesized that many biases are subsets or extensions of confirmation bias. Consequently, this study focuses on confirmation bias, employing the concept of belief entrenchment as a framework to investigate its manifestation in language models.

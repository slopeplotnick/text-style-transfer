This methodology seeks to enhance fairness across various tasks and demographic groups by directly optimizing balanced accuracy. Unlike conventional cross-entropy (CE) loss, this approach specifically addresses performance disparities between large and small groups, thereby promoting consistency in multi-task fine-tuning. An empirical study was conducted to compare different training methodologies, focusing on the impact of single-task and multi-task fine-tuning on mental health predictions. Consequently, a mental health assessment application was developed for iOS, utilizing the llama.cpp backend with GGUF V3 models optimized for mobile devices. These models were converted from PyTorch to GGUF format using a specialized conversion process, ensuring efficient execution on both CPUs and GPUs. The application, developed using Swift/SwiftUI, interfaces with llama.cpp through a C++ layer, enabling on-device operation on iOS without reliance on external servers. The application leverages Apple Metal to expedite matrix operations, defaulting to CPU processing when GPU capacity is exceeded, and utilizes up to eight threads to optimize performance on Apple's processors. The model weights are configured to minimize RAM usage by loading segments as necessary. For enhanced stability, the application employs a 4,096-token window, incorporating intelligent cache management and batch processing to effectively handle extensive inputs. Long social media entries are truncated to 6,000 characters. Three models—Menta (2.33 GB), Phi-4 Mini (2.40 GB), and Qwen-3 (2.30 GB)—were evaluated on an iPhone 15 Pro Max, with the supporting codebase being open-source. The framework is adaptable, capable of incorporating various quantization techniques to balance performance and accuracy. For the Menta model, four high-quality datasets were utilized to detect depression, stress, and suicidal ideation from Reddit, excluding disorder-specific samples and employing expert labeling for the remainder. These datasets underpinned the study.

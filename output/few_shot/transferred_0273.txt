CAPO is engineered to employ a dynamic signal termed "advantage," which aligns with the model's progression over time. Historically, curriculum learning methods, as initiated by Bengio et al. (2009), predominantly employed static paradigms such as structuring tasks from simple to complex. Subsequent methodologies attempted adaptability through mechanisms like expert feedback or model performance assessment; however, these approaches remained anchored to external frameworks rather than directly correlating with model improvement. Consequently, CAPO was developed to utilize advantage estimates as an intrinsic signal for task scheduling within the model's training process. Empirical evaluations indicate that CAPO enhances mathematical problem-solving capabilities across various advantage-based reinforcement learning strategies, including GRPO, PPO, RLOO, and Reinforce++. Additionally, it demonstrates efficacy in tasks involving graphical user interfaces (GUIs), thereby exhibiting its capacity to manage diverse reasoning tasks. In essence, the primary contributions of this work include the integration of advantage as a dynamic signal within the CAPO framework, characterized by two adaptive phases—imitation and discrimination—corresponding to model advancement. CAPO's compatibility with diverse reinforcement learning algorithms and its applicability to multifaceted data tasks, such as GUIs, further underscore its versatility. As depicted in Figure 2, CAPO operates by sampling outputs from the policy model, estimating advantages via various algorithms, and amalgamating these into a cohesive framework. Leveraging these signals, CAPO structures training into an initial imitation phase for stability and a subsequent discrimination phase to enhance performance as more challenging samples are introduced.

In this section, we delineate the experimental framework employed in this study, encompassing a discussion on the data, problem solvers, and checkers, along with the methodologies used to assess the performance of these solvers and checkers. Additionally, we elucidate the setup for verification in our investigation. It is crucial to note that "verifier gain" serves as a long-term metric, representing the asymptotic efficacy of perpetual verification and establishing an upper bound on improvements attainable via checker-based sampling. Verifier gain is utilized to evaluate the performance of various checkers.  We categorize models into families, such as Llama3 or Qwen2.5, where each family comprises related models of varying scales. Given the divergent behavior often observed between baseline and enhanced models, they are treated as distinct groups. For example, the baseline model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-70B is classified within the Llama3-Base group, while the enhanced model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct is categorized under the Llama3 group.  Each solver-checker pair from our models is assigned to one of three verification setups: 1. Self-Verification: The same model functions as both solver and checker, enabling self-assessment of its own outputs. For instance, when a 70B Llama3 model serves as both solver and checker, metrics such as accuracy are evaluated within this solitary setup. 2. Intra-Family Verification: The checker evaluates responses from other models within the same family. For example, a 70B Llama3 checker may assess outputs from 8B or 13B Llama3 solvers. We average the performance metrics across all intra-family solvers, excluding the self-verification scenario. 3. Cross-Family Verification: The checker evaluates responses from models belonging to different families. For instance, a baseline Llama3 checker might assess outputs from Qwen3 or an improved Llama3. The performance measures are averaged across all cross-family solvers.

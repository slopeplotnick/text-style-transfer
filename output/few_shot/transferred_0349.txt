Large Language Models (LLMs) continue to expand in size and complexity, leading to significant demands on memory and computational resources during both training and inference. Quantization has emerged as a practical strategy to mitigate these requirements by representing data with fewer bits and replacing expensive floating-point operations with more efficient integer arithmetic. There are two principal approaches to quantization: post-training quantization (PTQ) and quantization-aware training (QAT). PTQ is a straightforward method that requires no retraining; however, its performance degrades substantially when employing extremely low-bit precision (e.g., 1 or 2 bits). This degradation arises from several factors, including insufficient calibration data, suboptimal scaling techniques, and channel-specific range mismatches. In contrast, QAT integrates the quantization process into the training phase, enabling models to adapt to the constraints of quantization. While QAT is more effective than PTQ at very low-bit precision, it is computationally intensive and may suffer from instability due to limited adaptability in managing data distribution.  In this study, we proposed FAIRY2, a novel framework applied to widely used open-source LLMs, such as the LLaMA series, to address these challenges. Our approach involved three key innovations: (1) transforming any given layer into a quantizable form without altering the model's functionality prior to quantization, (2) continuing the training of models at ultra-low bit levels using a specialized quantizer while leveraging pre-existing checkpoints, and (3) minimizing residual errors by applying the same quantization technique to the remaining unquantized data, thereby achieving significant error reduction. Evaluation results demonstrated the efficacy of FAIRY2 in enhancing quantization performance for state-of-the-art language models.

Google Search has introduced a feature known as AI Overviews, leveraging the Gemini model to generate summaries of extensive documents. This functionality aims to enhance efficiency by reducing the need for users to synthesize information from multiple sources manually. However, this raises concerns about the reliability of the aggregated information. When an AI system integrates content from various documents into a singular summary, it becomes essential to evaluate whether the sources are treated equitably and whether biases are present.   Prior research has investigated biases in AI models. For instance, Santurkar et al. (2023) observed that models such as GPT-4 and Claude often exhibit liberal social inclinations. In the domain of news summarization, Vijay et al. (2023) proposed methods to quantify biases, including analyzing disparities in language or tendencies for AI to adhere to a specific perspective. While extensive studies have addressed biases in AI-generated summaries, relatively few have examined biases in handling multi-document contexts. Specifically, it remains unclear whether AI models exhibit preferences when processing extensive documents, potentially prioritizing certain documents due to their positional order. Cognitive phenomena such as primacy bias or middle-position neglect, which have been observed in other tasks, warrant further exploration in the domain of multi-document summarization.   This study investigates whether the sequencing of articles influences their representation in AI-generated summaries, using abortion-related news articles as a case study. Through this investigation, we aim to address these research questions and contribute to the understanding of biases in AI summarization systems.

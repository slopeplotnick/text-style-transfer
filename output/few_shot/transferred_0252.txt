The study utilized several datasets, including one containing all elements, and additional sets each omitting either events, relationships, entities, or the narrative outline. These datasets were evaluated using both qualitative and quantitative metrics. The same test set, comprising 196 samples, was employed to assess the performance of adjusted models. Evaluative criteria focused on narrative efficacy and aesthetic quality, with a preference set serving as a baseline to facilitate comparison of narrative similarity across different models. The investigation centered on the impact of the four primary narrative components on the generated stories. For quantitative analysis, we primarily assessed narrative similarity across models using identical inputs and the original text as a reference. Each model generated three narratives per input. The original text served as a standard for similarity assessment, employing ROUGE-L, BERTScore, and METEOR Score as evaluative metrics. ROUGE-L evaluates sequence similarity based on the longest common subsequence, BERTScore assesses semantic similarity while avoiding superficial matching issues, and METEOR Score offers flexible and robust matching methods. Figure 7 illustrates the comparative performance of different configurations against the baseline. The configuration comprising all elements demonstrated superior capability in content control, maintaining semantic consistency, and generating natural-sounding narratives. An empirical study was conducted with two experts and 17 volunteers utilizing TaleFrame. To foster creativity, volunteers were provided with various images and allowed to select topics of interest to generate stories using TaleFrame. Participants were permitted to modify the four primary narrative components to produce satisfactory stories if the initial iterations were not to their liking. Volunteers were required to employ all interactive features of TaleFrame.

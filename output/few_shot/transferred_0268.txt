We developed a nine-item scale using a seven-point Likert format to assess experiences of Shame, Guilt, and Impostor Syndrome associated with the use of artificial intelligence. Empirical data was collected through an online survey, conducted via Prolific, from the evening of February 3, 2025, to late night on February 4, 2025. This survey included not only our scale items but also additional tests to evaluate the scale's validity. Subsequent data analysis involved comparing the real-world dataset to a simulated dataset, generated following the methodology employed in our initial study. The demographic and other relevant characteristics of participants in both datasets are compared in Table 1. A comprehensive description of the SAGAT scale will be elaborated in a forthcoming publication, subsequent to the completion of the standard scale development procedures, which incorporated elements from our experimental methods. The comparison between real and simulated datasets was conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test; while no significant differences were observed for Impostor Syndrome scores, substantial differences emerged for Shame and Guilt. Additionally, Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests revealed significant disparities across all three constructs, thereby contradicting Hypothesis 4. Figure 4 presents histograms depicting the distribution of real and simulated data. To ensure the robustness and reliability of our findings, we conducted a subsequent study aimed at developing a new scale to assess anxious beliefs associated with AI, employing the methodology outlined in Study 3 (refer to the "Methods" section for details). Further information regarding the scale development process using simulated data is available in the Supplementary Materials and the preregistration documentation on OSF, accessible via the provided link.

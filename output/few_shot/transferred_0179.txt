This study explores the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in the identification of critical mineral deposits, presenting insights derived from decades of experience in data science, decision science, and AI within the context of earth resource management. The author draws on over 30 years of professional expertise to provide observations and analyses that build on prior work in this domain. An early contribution to this field was presented in a 1998 study, wherein neural networks were applied to outcrop data to enhance the realism of reservoir models (Author, 1998). The study emphasized the importance of realistic modeling in predicting fluid dynamics, which enables improved decision-making in oil and gas exploration. At the time, this research was showcased at a prominent international conference in New Orleans despite the imminent threat of a hurricane.   Subsequently, a 2002 publication demonstrated the capacity of neural networks to extrapolate reservoir properties, such as porosity, from borehole and geophysical data, even in regions lacking borehole coverage (Author, 2002). This research addressed a critical challenge: reconciling data at disparate spatial and temporal scales, ranging from borehole core samples to seismic signals obtained from deep subsurface structures. The present study revisits this scale issue in depth, identifying its significance for contemporary mineral exploration.  While oil and gas companies routinely incorporate geological uncertainty into their risk assessments prior to major infrastructure investments, the mining industry continues to rely on simplified models and qualitative interpretations in exploration. This disparity highlights a lag in the minerals sector’s adoption of sophisticated, quantitative approaches to risk assessment, as compared to the more advanced methodologies employed by the oil and gas industry. This paper aims to bridge this gap by proposing the integration of AI-driven techniques to address scale-specific challenges and enhance predictive models in mineral exploration.

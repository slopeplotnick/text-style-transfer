Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods have emerged as a response to the increasing size of large language models (LLMs). Currently, the proliferation of novel PEFT techniques presents challenges in terms of reproducibility, usability, and comparability. To address these issues, PEFT-FACTORY has been developed as an integrated framework to facilitate the fine-tuning of LLMs utilizing both pre-existing and customizable PEFT methods. Designed for scalability and extensibility, PEFT-FACTORY already encompasses 19 PEFT methods and incorporates 27 datasets for classification and text generation across 12 tasks, along with evaluation tools tailored for both general and PEFT-specific metrics. This renders PEFT-FACTORY a robust and user-friendly platform conducive to the replication of results and comparative analysis of PEFT methods. The framework, which builds on LLaMAFactory, is accessible at https://github.com/kinit-sk/PEFT-Factory.  The advent of the transformer architecture has significantly enhanced the performance of LLMs in numerous natural language processing tasks (Vaswani et al., 2017; Brown et al., 2020). However, the scalability of these models has led to a continual increase in the number of trainable parameters, thereby escalating the computational costs and data requirements for fine-tuning. This situation poses accessibility challenges for many researchers and practitioners. PEFT approaches seek to mitigate these issues by fine-tuning only a subset of the model's parameters, while still achieving performance levels comparable to full model training. Techniques employed in PEFT include the introduction of additional parameters, selective training of specific parameters, or model modifications to reduce parameter count (Houlsby et al., 2019; Ben Zaken et al., 2020).

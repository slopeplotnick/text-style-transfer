To effectively comprehend user inquiries and the specifics of items, an information retrieval system must be initiated with foundational knowledge pertaining to the subject matter. This foundational knowledge enables the system to accurately interpret user intent, extract pertinent details about the items, and align them with user requirements. In certain instances, item images convey attributes that are challenging to articulate verbally, such as visual appearance or tactile sensation. To address this, the system analyzes item images and integrates insights obtained from both textual and visual data, allowing for a more comprehensive understanding of the item. Upon discerning user intent and extracting item details, the system must adhere to predetermined protocols to verify and align the details. This phase involves applying these protocols to ensure coherence and functionality.   Phase 1: Knowledge and Reasoning. As delineated in Section 2.3, knowledge is bifurcated into general world knowledge and e-commerce-specific knowledge. General world knowledge, which large language models have extensively acquired during training, enables the comprehension of common concepts and relationships. However, e-commerce contains numerous phrases and terminologies that transcend basic semantic comprehension. These challenges predominantly manifest in two categories: (1) Rare Queries, which involve highly specific requests concerning atypical items, often lack the requisite background knowledge in models to appropriately interpret the terms. For instance, a search query such as "strange things are also loved by someone" relating to "novel and interesting toys" actually refers to a book title. Lacking prior knowledge, the system might fail to accurately associate them. Additional instances include brand-specific terminologies, product nicknames, or marketing jargon unique to e-commerce. (2) Confusing Items, which encompass items so rare that the system lacks sufficient understanding. For example, an entity referred to as "lying gate" cannot be comprehended or categorized based solely on its nomenclature without additional contextual information. Addressing these scenarios necessitates supplemental e-commerce-specific knowledge to achieve correct interpretation.

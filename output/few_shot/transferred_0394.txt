Although energy consumption in Large Language Models (LLMs) is a critical concern, comprehensive methodologies for measuring and analyzing power and energy requirements across varying model sizes, hardware configurations, software frameworks, and deployment settings (e.g., single-node versus distributed systems) remain underdeveloped. Existing efforts have made incremental progress in this area but fail to address the problem entirely. For instance, the MLPerf Inference benchmark (Reddi et al., 2020) has established a standard for assessing performance across diverse machine learning tasks but does not account for comprehensive power consumption metrics or energy adjustments tailored to LLM-specific workloads, such as joules per token or prompt. Similarly, the MLPerf Power benchmark (Tschand et al., 2025) offers more detailed measurement capabilities but is limited in several ways: (i) its primary focus is on single-node setups, (ii) it emphasizes analyses of smaller models, neglecting state-of-the-art LLMs, such as Llama 3 (405B-class), which remain underexplored, and (iii) it depends on high-precision, specialized instrumentation that significantly limits applicability across diverse settings.  Additionally, current benchmarking frameworks rarely account for user-configurable parameters in real-world applications—such as batch size, parallel processing strategies, context length, and quantization levels—that significantly influence instantaneous power consumption and average energy use per token. To address these limitations, we introduce **TokenPowerBench**, a novel, accessible benchmarking framework purpose-built for assessing the power and energy consumption of LLMs. TokenPowerBench is designed to operate with a range of vendor-provided tools (e.g., power sensors for GPU/CPU/Memory) and enables measurements at multiple levels of granularity, including the device level, rack level, and, where possible, facility level. The framework includes a pipeline that aligns power measurements with the two main inference stages, *prefill* and *decode*, enabling fine-grained analysis of energy consumption.  Through extensive empirical evaluations, TokenPowerBench investigates the relationship between energy consumption and factors such as batch size, maximum context length, task partitioning, and quantization strategies.

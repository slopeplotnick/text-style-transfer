We fine-tuned the Large Language Model (LLM) using the KTO method to label specific online items. When the online BERT model misclassifies challenging examples, these instances are incorporated into the training dataset to enhance the model's performance. The LLM developed within the RD module identifies difficult examples where the online model fails. However, this approach primarily addresses isolated errors and does not resolve systematic issues. Variations in search queries or product descriptions often result in persistent misclassifications by the online model. To address this limitation, we developed an LLM capable of generating data instances that the online model frequently misclassifies, thereby improving its overall accuracy and robustness.    For any given search-product pair, the proposed LLM generates modified examples by altering search queries or product details according to specific instructions, creating new training examples labeled as either Relevant or Irrelevant. Utilizing the GPT-4o model with a few-shot learning framework, we generate these training instances. To ensure the LLM learns the error patterns of the online model, we iteratively use instances where the online model misclassified the data to refine the LLM. Once trained to recognize error types, the LLM is applied to data from online searches to generate challenging examples that are likely to confuse the online model. To mitigate the impact of noisy or irrelevant data during training, we employ the relevance model developed in the RD module to filter and validate the generated examples for training purposes.

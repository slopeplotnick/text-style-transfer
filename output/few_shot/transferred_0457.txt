This section elaborates on the evaluation methods employed and their interpretations, encompassing metrics for word overlap, semantic similarity, and information accuracy. ROUGE-L, specifically, evaluates the longest contiguous subsequence of overlapping words between the model-generated summary and the reference summary, emphasizing sentence structure while disregarding word order. Commonly utilized for comparing system outputs with human-authored summaries, the analysis in this study does not focus on precise ROUGE-L scores but rather investigates variations in scores based on input order to assess whether order impacts performance. As ROUGE-L is limited to assessing word-level overlap, additional metrics are employed, namely BERTScore and SummaC, for evaluating semantic meaning and factual accuracy.   BERTScore leverages a pre-trained model to determine semantic similarity by aligning words in the generated summary with their closest counterparts in the reference summary, calculating precision, recall, and F1 scores from these mappings. This method demonstrates robustness in capturing meaning due to its ability to recognize synonyms and paraphrasing. In this study, the primary focus is on how BERTScore fluctuates with different input orders, rather than absolute scores, which may be lower due to the abstract nature of the summaries analyzed.   SummaC evaluates the factual consistency of generated summaries relative to the original documents. Two variants exist: SummaC-ZS, a zero-shot method requiring no training, and SummaC-Conv, a trained model. SummaC-Conv is adopted herein due to its superior performance, though it relies on a RoBERTa-based architecture constrained by input-length limitations.

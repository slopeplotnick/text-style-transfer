Humans demonstrate remarkable proficiency in rapidly acquiring new tasks when provided with explicit instructions, such as verbal descriptions or diagrams, without necessitating extensive practice. Researchers have investigated the parallels between brain function and artificial intelligence in domains such as perception, cognition, and motor activities by deconstructing their processes into three components: circuit configuration, input-output management, and learning mechanisms. Recent studies indicate that contemporary AI systems are beginning to emulate brain function and have elucidated significant insights into neural operations. The neocortex and cerebellum utilize historical data to anticipate future events, striving for precision in these predictions. In doing so, they distill and structure environmental information into concise yet potent models. These models facilitate three principal functions: forecasting future occurrences, comprehending the world through abstract constructs such as features and causal relationships (e.g., object recognition or text comprehension), and generating outcomes such as actions, plans, or language. This mode of information processing enables the neocortex and cerebellum to execute a wide array of functions despite their structural and functional homogeneity. In essence, this form of information processing comprises a foundational mechanism that underpins much of the functional capabilities of both the brain and AI systems. Deepening our understanding of this process will enhance advancements in both neuroscience and artificial intelligence, providing greater insights into the nature of intelligence. The human brain exhibits superior learning speed and efficiency compared to large language models, requiring significantly fewer examples. Recent AI models have improved in learning and efficiency by emulating brain processes, such as employing Mixture-of-Experts methods, yet they still fall short of the brain's capabilities. As AI continues to advance by drawing inspiration from brain function, neuroscience will concurrently gain novel insights through the study of AI.

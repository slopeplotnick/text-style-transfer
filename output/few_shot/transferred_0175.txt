We evaluated the efficiency and effectiveness of various systems in editing PowerPoint files. Among the tested systems, only MiniMax Agent and PPTPilot were capable of completing edits in under two minutes, whereas others were either slower or frequently failed. Due to the critical importance of speed, our evaluation focused on single-pass outputs, with results for loop-enabled edits presented separately in Table 4. The findings suggest that successful PowerPoint editing in PPTPilot hinges on three key components: (1) a planning mechanism capable of understanding slide structure and semantics, (2) an adaptive system that alternates between API-based operations and direct XML editing, and (3) a stepwise verification and enhancement process to address lengthy and complex tasks. Overall, the tests revealed that while most advanced systems encounter difficulties with complex edits, multimedia integration, and modifications spanning multiple slides, PPTPilot demonstrates superior performance in these aspects.   To further analyze PPTPilot’s architecture, we examined its core components to determine their contributions to overall performance. The evaluation criteria, consistent with those outlined in Section 3, included task success rates, PowerPoint file integrity, adherence to instructions, visual quality of slides, and computational resource utilization. The experiments utilized the complete PPTPilot configuration, encompassing the creation of JSON snapshots, decision-making for XML edits versus Python library usage, strict compliance enforcement for outputs, and automated repairs for XML errors. Additionally, we conducted ablation tests by deactivating or modifying specific components while maintaining other aspects constant. For instance, in one variant, all edits were performed solely through XML, which proved efficient for small, localized adjustments (e.g., resizing a single shape) but was slower and less effective for large-scale modifications (e.g., unifying text sizes or applying a theme across the deck). Another variant relied exclusively on the Python library, which excelled at broad changes such as renaming or translating text but was less suited for detailed adjustments requiring precise control over individual elements.

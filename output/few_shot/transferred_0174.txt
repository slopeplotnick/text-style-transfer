Researchers are endeavoring to enhance machine capabilities in processing high-resolution images. A common strategy involves zooming into sections of the image to enhance detail retrieval. Conventional methodologies often rely on Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning techniques, though these approaches are resource-intensive and time-consuming, with limited cross-system applicability. Alternative methodologies eschew training and leverage attention mechanisms or tree-based searches to autonomously identify salient image regions. These strategies typically initiate with an overview of the entire image before zooming in, frequently overlooking minute details initially, which may lead to errors.   Recently, a novel approach termed Retrieval-Augmented Perception (RAP) has been proposed to augment machine interpretation of high-resolution images without the need for training. RAP comprises three principal components: initially, it employs a pre-trained model to identify image segments closely aligned with the target, thus minimizing extraneous data (Zhang et al., 2024). Subsequently, it preserves the original spatial configuration of these segments to maintain contextual coherence. Lastly, it constructs a hierarchical tree to determine the optimal number of segments, balancing the retention of critical information against computational efficiency.

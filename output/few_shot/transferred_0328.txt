In 2024, advancements were made in structuring content into coherent sections and summarizing it in multiple languages for enhanced interactive Q&A experiences. This process utilized transcribed multimodal speech-to-text (ST) output as contextual input. While contemporary Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit impressive capabilities for handling extended contexts, they remain constrained by context length limitations; thus, the proposed methodology involves translating lectures into several languages and segmenting them into thematic chapters to prevent content overflow. This segmentation ensures clarity and coherence, as each chapter remains focused on a distinct topic. Compressed versions of each chapter were generated at varying levels of compression (e.g., 50%, 70%, 90%), alongside summaries derived from the chapter’s original length. Initial summarization commenced in English, leveraging LLMs’ superior performance with English-language content, before translations into additional languages were undertaken. For the interactive Q&A process, English transcripts were chapter-organized and processed using Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to enable LLM-generated question answering, with the results subsequently translated into target languages.  Another challenge pertained to the translation of slides containing editable text and images with embedded text. To address this, a Python-based PowerPoint parser was employed to extract text for translation using standard machine translation (MT) systems, as multimodal models were deemed economically unfeasible. However, text within images presented unique challenges, such as difficulty in extraction, lack of contextual information, and dependence on visual elements for interpretative meaning. Consequently, multimodal translation approaches were necessary for such cases. The proposed methodology involved a comprehensive image-translation process comprising the identification, extraction, translation, and reintegration of text within images, ensuring the preservation of both layout integrity and semantic coherence in the translated slides.

A dataset comprising pairs of real-world images and their Ghibli-style counterparts is utilized for comparative analysis. Both the original and Ghibli-style images are systematically described using a structured methodology. Subsequently, the alignment of stylistic elements in the images with those characteristic of the Ghibli style is evaluated. This analysis serves to determine whether the stylistic modifications introduced during the process adhere to the aesthetic conventions of Ghibli films. In cases of alignment, the specific Ghibli film from which the stylistic elements are derived is identified, thereby demonstrating the model's capacity to learn from these cinematic sources. These steps collectively validate the system's ability to capture the essential visual attributes of the Ghibli style.  To extract information from the images, a multimodal tool capable of processing both text and visual data is employed. A predefined set of guidelines is followed to analyze dimensions such as texture, color, mood, lighting, and layout. These guidelines are further refined to include additional descriptors for objects and stylistic components. The criteria utilized in this analytical framework are cataloged in a supplementary table. Additionally, the Ghibli film associated with each stylistic match is recorded. Analysis of the matches reveals that most Ghibli films contribute comparably to the stylistic elements observed; however, *Princess Mononoke*, *Kiki's Delivery Service*, and *The Tale of Princess Kaguya* exhibit slightly greater influence. This observation underscores the stylistic diversity present across Ghibli’s oeuvre, a characteristic that is effectively reflected in the generated images. A detailed breakdown of the data is presented in a supporting table.

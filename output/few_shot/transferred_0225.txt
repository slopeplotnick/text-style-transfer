Recent advancements in the development of large language models (LLMs) have enhanced their cognitive capabilities, prompting increased confidence in their ability to provide accurate and reliable information. However, emerging evidence indicates that iterative reasoning processes in these models may lead to the reinforcement of prior beliefs rather than the discovery of objective truths. In this study, we propose a novel framework for evaluating belief perseverance in LLMs, leveraging a statistical concept known as the Martingale property. The Martingale property posits that if beliefs are logically updated, future beliefs should statistically remain consistent with current beliefs, precluding the ability to predict future beliefs solely based on present ones.   To operationalize this concept, we developed the Martingale Score, a metric designed to identify deviations from rational belief updating, thereby signaling instances where models exhibit belief entrenchment. Our investigation spanned diverse domains, including event prediction, opinion-based questioning, and academic paper review. Across these tasks, we observed a consistent tendency for models to adhere to their existing beliefs, a phenomenon we term "belief entrenchment." Through extensive experimentation, we examined the susceptibility of various models, reasoning frameworks, and task domains to this phenomenon.  Furthermore, we demonstrated the utility of the Martingale Score in predicting model accuracy within contexts where ground truth is available. Notably, the score's efficacy in such scenarios highlights its potential to evaluate model reliability in the absence of objective correctness benchmarks. This finding underscores the broader applicability of the Martingale framework in assessing epistemic soundness in LLMs. The phenomenon of belief entrenchment in these models mirrors analogous cognitive biases in humans, such as confirmation bias and susceptibility to external influences, wherein individuals or decision-makers, like a king surrounded by sycophants, prioritize information that reinforces pre-existing views.

Case 1: Detecting Depression Level. In this instance, a Reddit post labeled 'Minimum' discusses a user's contemplation of an obsessive behavior or sensation involving their tongue, as illustrated in Figure 10. The user expresses concern and solicits assistance from the community, indicating a willingness to divulge additional information, thereby demonstrating an openness to discussion despite discomfort. The Menta model accurately classifies this post as 'Minimum' and justifies the classification by identifying the user's calm discourse regarding a physical issue and the absence of intense or emotive language, indicative of minimal psychological distress. This example illustrates that not all discomfort equates to depression. Conversely, the Qwen-3 model inaccurately categorizes the post as 'Severe', mistakenly interpreting strong physical sensations and discomfort as indicative of significant mental distress. This misclassification arises from conflating physical pain with anxiety and psychological distress, highlighting an overemphasis on overt distress indicators without differentiating somatic complaints from signs of depression. Through enhanced training, the Menta model has developed the capability to discern depression-specific indicators and align its predictions with the dataset's criteria. This exemplifies Menta's proficiency in distinguishing between general discomfort and genuine depression, thereby demonstrating its enhanced accuracy in predicting mental health states.  Case 2: Detecting Stress Level. A Reddit post designated as 'No stress' involves a user contemplating a housing decision with a friend. The user articulates their thoughts calmly, enumerates options logically, and exhibits no signs of panic or undue pressure. The Menta model correctly classifies this post as 'No stress' and elucidates this judgment by highlighting the user's composed demeanor and maturity in managing a complex decision, accurately recognizing the absence of impulsive or anxious discourse as indicative of low stress. In contrast, the Qwen-3 model erroneously labels the situation as 'Stressed', placing undue emphasis on expressions like 'cold feet' and '99% sure', and misinterpreting careful deliberation as internal conflict. It suggests the user is experiencing stress, emotional unease, and indecision, attributes that a proficient model should discern. However, the model focuses on external relational issues, social tension, or potential relational discord, often associated with conflict avoidance or hidden distress (indicated in red in Figure 11), culminating in a misclassification. This case further underscores the Menta model's effectiveness in accurately predicting mental health statuses through training.

Formulas are simplified by eliminating extraneous elements and retaining only the essential components, ensuring compactness while preserving functionality. This process facilitates handling by SAT solvers. Errors, termed as "hallucinations," emerge when variables or formula components are improperly reused, mislabeled, or inaccurately translated, leading to disruptions in the performance of SAT solvers. Such issues may arise from language models failing to accurately translate specific sentence structures during training or from sentences exhibiting atypical syntactic configurations, thereby complicating their conversion into Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF).   To address these challenges, the Natural Language Toolkit (NLTK) and Sympy are employed collaboratively. NLTK is utilized to decompose sentences based on rigorously validated and widely adopted grammatical rules, ensuring syntactic correctness in the breakdown process. Subsequently, Sympy is applied to maintain the logical integrity of the original sentence during its transformation into CNF. Sentences are extracted from paragraphs by identifying sentence-ending punctuation (e.g., periods). Each extracted sentence is treated as a distinct unit, and these units are then combined using logical conjunctions (AND) to construct the CNF representation.  A comprehensive grammar rule set is used with NLTK to represent each sentence as a syntactic tree structure, delineating hierarchical components such as conditions, statements, and grammatical elements (e.g., nouns, verbs, punctuation). These tree structures are further processed using predefined rules to derive logical formulas in Sympy. Finally, Sympy's automated tools convert these logical formulas into CNF, facilitating efficient usage in SAT solvers.

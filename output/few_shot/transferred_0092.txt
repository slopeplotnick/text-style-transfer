Our findings indicate that employing a deformable scan significantly enhances model performance. When the deformable scan was removed, and the baseline Mamba configuration was utilized, a substantial decline in performance was observed. This underscores the critical role of the deformable scan. To identify the optimal design, we experimented with various architectures. The proposed design incorporates a hybrid structure denoted as "CCDGDG" across stages 1 to 6, wherein C represents Convolutional blocks, D denotes DDSM+FNN blocks, and G signifies Gated-Conv blocks. We also evaluated alternative configurations using only two block types, such as "CCGGGG" and "CCDDD," as well as designs employing a single block type. Comparative analysis revealed that the mixed-design architecture outperformed its counterparts. Further validation was carried out using samples from the InterHand2.6M and AssemblyHands datasets, wherein DF-Mamba exhibited superior accuracy in predicting joint positions with reduced errors, as marked by colored ellipses. Notably, even under occlusion or challenging visibility conditions, the method demonstrated robust performance, highlighting its efficacy in real-world applications.  DF-Mamba represents a novel framework for 3D hand pose estimation, characterized by efficient early-stage feature extraction and a deformable representation in subsequent stages. Extensive evaluation across five diverse datasets encompassing varying hand interactions and data modalities demonstrated that DF-Mamba consistently outperformed competing methods while achieving faster inference times. Future research directions include extending DF-Mamba to additional tasks such as 3D human pose estimation, leveraging extensive external datasets for training, investigating its application in novel hand-mesh reconstruction techniques, and developing it into a foundational model capable of generalizing effectively across multiple scenarios without task-specific modifications.

The outcomes of the study are presented in Table 4, while the learning curves are depicted in Figure 8. AMP demonstrates proficiency in replicating motions, yet it necessitates reference motion data during training. Conversely, SMP exhibits the capability to replicate a broad spectrum of motions, including challenging ones, equivalently to AMP, without requiring reference data during training. SMP consistently outperforms SMILING and AMP-Frozen across all skill sets. Although AMP-Frozen endeavors to eliminate AMP's dependency on data by utilizing a pre-trained discriminator, it fails to achieve satisfactory results, resulting in suboptimal motion imitation. DeepMimic employs phase synchronization to mitigate tracking errors in certain motions; however, SMP remains competitive. DeepMimic encounters difficulties with complex motions such as spinkicks and cartwheels, particularly when pose termination is disabled, leading to some iterations converging on inadequate solutions. Our approach is pioneering in utilizing score-matching to develop a reusable motion model that performs comparably to leading adversarial imitation learning methodologies. In this section, we explore the role of ensemble score-matching in enhancing training stability and improving outcomes. We evaluate our ensemble score-matching technique, which averages multiple SDS evaluations across specified diffusion timesteps, against the conventional approach from previous studies that employs SDS at a singular random timestep. The evaluation focuses on single-clip imitation tasks, including Run, Cartwheel, and Backflip. As illustrated in Table 5, employing SDS at a single timestep is adequate for basic motions but struggles with more complex ones. For instance, when attempting to replicate a backflip, policies trained with a single SDS evaluation frequently result in stationary positions or backward falls instead of executing a complete flip. Implementing multiple SDS evaluations over designated timesteps provides a more consistent reward signal, thereby facilitating the training of policies for intricate motions such as acrobatics.

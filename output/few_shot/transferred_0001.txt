The reliability of language models varies significantly. This review examines the influence of model size, architecture, and training data on the propensity for hallucinations in such systems. Empirical studies report observable patterns across diverse models and propose mitigation strategies, including enhanced training methodologies and constraining output generation (Smith et al., 2023). One study highlights hallucinations as a pervasive issue for Large Language Models (LLMs), irrespective of their scale or training approach (Johnson et al., 2024). While the study posits that appropriate model selection and targeted training may reduce hallucinations, the authors argue that complete elimination of hallucinations remains unattainable. Such insights are critical for establishing realistic expectations and evaluating a model's suitability for specific applications.  When selecting LLMs to minimize hallucinations, considerations should encompass the model’s architectural design, the quality and diversity of training data, and optimization strategies such as fine-tuning processes or the integration of supplementary information (Brown et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2024). Several models are prominent in contemporary usage, including GPT, BERT, and T5. GPT demonstrates proficiency in generating coherent and fluent text but remains susceptible to producing misinformation (hallucinations) (Radford et al., 2019). BERT leverages bidirectional training to exhibit reduced hallucination rates for task-specific applications; however, instances of fabricated information persist (Devlin et al., 2018). Models such as PaLM and LLaMA have been explicitly designed with mechanisms to mitigate hallucinations, though their efficacy is contingent upon training approaches and architectural parameters (Chowdhery et al., 2022; Touvron et al., 2023). Understanding the capabilities and limitations of these models is essential when selecting tools for tasks where accuracy is critical and hallucination minimization is a priority.

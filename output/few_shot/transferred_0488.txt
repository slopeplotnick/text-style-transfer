The research methodology involved iteratively reviewing the interview summaries to enhance our comprehension of the findings. During the data collection phase, the interviews were audio-recorded, and manual notes were taken, subsequently summarized to ensure the retention of all pertinent information. This methodological approach aligns with established qualitative research practices. The research team, including the interviewer, collaboratively engaged in the data analysis process. Subsequent to this, we developed metrics to facilitate a quantitative analysis, wherein we quantified the occurrences of good practices, benefits, risks, and fears articulated by participants. This was achieved by identifying common responses and distilling them into concise representative terms. For instance, when a participant remarked, "The genericity pushes to factorize more and more," we recorded it as "genericity" and "factorisation."  Additionally, we conducted a frequency analysis of key terms, noting both their prevalence and the participants who mentioned them. To identify salient keywords, we first eliminated function words such as "the" and "and," then ranked the remaining terms by frequency. The most significant terms were cross-referenced with the original transcripts to confirm contextual relevance. In certain instances, descriptors were refined for clarity, such as expanding "complex" to "too complex." We also performed a comparative linguistic analysis, juxtaposing the interview-derived terms with terminology found in relevant literature. Where discrepancies arose, terms were adjusted for greater alignment, replacing "unusable" with "useless," for example. This iterative process facilitated a more nuanced understanding of the data, ensuring that the terminology employed was consistent with academic discourse, thereby enhancing the robustness of the analysis.

Our findings corroborate those of Shirafuji et al. (2025), who employed a task vector-based methodology, observing the effectiveness of a debiased model. In contrast, studies such as Lutz (2024) utilized model merging to evaluate debiased large language models (LLMs) using Natural Language Inference (NLI) benchmarks. Our analysis indicates that methods assessed exclusively on tasks like NLI require more comprehensive evaluation across varied datasets. SuperGLUE benchmark results reveal that, with the exception of Linear, SLERP, and Nearswap techniques, other merging strategies degrade LLM performance in causal reasoning tasks (refer to Section 5.2). Consequently, these approaches are not dependable for consistent bias mitigation. The SLERP method is successful due to its balanced parameter-space integration without excessive bias amplification. In contrast, the NuSLERP approach, which incorporates layer or tensor-level normalization, adversely affects SuperGLUE scores. These results imply that the majority of contemporary model-merging techniques are inadequate for bias reduction without significantly diminishing reasoning capabilities, unlike SLERP. This research is pioneering in its examination of how seven distinct model-merging methods influence social bias within LLMs. We evaluated 13 models encompassing the GPT, LLaMA, and Qwen families against three social bias datasets alongside the SuperGLUE benchmark, striving to achieve a balance between fairness and functionality. Furthermore, four techniques—Karcher Mean, NuSLERP, TIES, and DELLA—were found to impair performance on tasks necessitating reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning, specifically in the ReCoRD, COPA, CB, and BoolQ tasks within the SuperGLUE benchmark. While existing debiasing strategies via model merging demonstrated improvements in LLM performance on downstream tasks with NLI scores, our investigation underscores the importance of also evaluating accuracy in reading comprehension and reasoning tasks.

Languages were categorized into clusters as illustrated in Figure 2, referred to as In-Domain languages. The evaluation of the proposed model was conducted on both In-Domain and Out-of-Domain languages, with the latter comprising 11 low-resource languages selected for their representativeness across clusters and language families. Testing was performed using six safety classification datasets: Aegis-Content-Safety-2.0-Test (Aegis-CS2) (2025), HarmBench (Mazeika et al., 2024), Redteam2k (Luo et al., 2024), JBB-Behaviors—further subdivided into JBB-Behav (Behaviors) and JBB-Judge (Judge-comparison) (Chao et al., 2024)—and StrongReject (Souly et al., 2024). These datasets encompass a variety of harm categories, including Hate/Identity Hate, Sexual, Suicide/Self-Harm, Violence, Guns/Illegal Weapons, PII/Privacy, Sexual Minor, Toxicity, Abuse, among others, making them comprehensive benchmarks for evaluating safety measures.  For training, XLM-RoBERTa-Base (279 million parameters) and XLM-RoBERTa-Large (560 million parameters) were adopted as core multilingual models, given their suitability for cross-language tasks. These models, pre-trained on 100 languages, align with the study’s multilingual objectives. A single-layer classification head with an output size matching the encoder was appended, featuring a binary classification output. Both the encoder and classification head were fine-tuned on the integrated training dataset. All training and testing processes utilized an NVIDIA H100 GPU cluster under Bfloat16 precision to optimize computational efficiency and performance.  Baseline comparisons involved testing the proposed model against smaller guardrail systems, such as DuoGuard-0.5B (2025), PolyGuard (PG)-Qwen-Smol, and WalledGuard-C (2024), as well as larger guardrails, including PolyGuard (PG)-Qwen (2025), LlamaGuard3 (2024), and Aegis-Defensive (2025). These baselines display a range of language support capabilities, from English-only systems to multilingual models covering up to 29 languages, providing a robust framework for evaluating the efficacy of the proposed safety guardrail across both high-resource and low-resource language contexts.

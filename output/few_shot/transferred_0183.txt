Our comprehensive analysis demonstrates that spatial methods, such as covariance tangent-space projection and Common Spatial Patterns (CSP), remain the most effective techniques for decoding electroencephalographic (EEG) motor imagery under controlled experimental conditions. However, their performance diminishes when confronted with substantial inter- and intra-subject variability. A subject-specific examination revealed the absence of a universal configuration suitable for all individuals, emphasizing the importance of adaptive methods tailored to the unique neural patterns of each user. Nonlinear features, including Hjorth parameters, Higuchi fractal dimension, and singular value decomposition entropy, exhibited promising results for certain subjects, underscoring the necessity of advancing our understanding of neurophysiological processes to enhance personalized decoding. These findings suggest that "BCI illiteracy" may stem from a misalignment between decoding algorithms and users' neural representations rather than inherent user limitations.   Future investigations should explore the integration of advanced learning techniques that combine linear spatial filters with nonlinear dynamics to leverage their complementary advantages. Expanding the scope of this study to encompass additional paradigms, such as steady-state visual evoked potentials (SSVEP), P300, code-modulated visual evoked potentials (CVEP), and invasive intracortical recordings, will provide critical insights into the generalizability of these findings. Furthermore, the evaluation of emerging deep learning architectures, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs), long short-term memory networks (LSTMs), Transformers, and quantum-inspired models—such as quantum support-vector machines and hybrid quantum neural networks—is essential. Quantum approaches, in particular, may prove advantageous in managing the inherent noise and complexity of EEG signals.   An equally significant avenue for future research lies in enhancing model generalization across subjects through techniques such as transfer learning and domain adaptation, which have demonstrated potential in rendering models more robust and scalable across diverse user populations. Additionally, improving reproducibility through open-access benchmarking initiatives, such as the Mother of All BCI Benchmarks (MOABB), is vital to ensure standardized and transparent comparisons of EEG decoding algorithms.

The present study introduces a novel methodology for disentangling perception and reasoning within AI models. Specifically, the approach involves augmenting training datasets with incorrect reasoning examples to enable the models to discern between valid and invalid reasoning processes. Additionally, this work proposes a new framework termed STL, which simultaneously enhances both perceptual and reasoning capabilities, addressing limitations of prior methods that predominantly focused on reasoning improvements. Experimental validation was conducted across domains such as common sense, language understanding, and scientific reasoning, demonstrating enhanced performance without reliance on human intervention.  In the context of vision-language reasoning tasks, reasoning emerges as a crucial factor for advancing model capabilities. While state-of-the-art models perform well in benchmark evaluations, leveraging visual information effectively within reasoning remains challenging, particularly for open-source models. Existing methodologies frequently employ prompting techniques for step-by-step reasoning facilitation. For instance, one approach prioritizes contextual understanding to generate features, whereas another utilizes scene graphs to model object relationships. Other strategies focus on training models with reasoning-enriched datasets, although the creation of such datasets is often resource-intensive.  The proposed approach advocates for self-training mechanisms, enabling models to enhance their reasoning capabilities autonomously, without necessitating specialized datasets. Self-training involves the utilization of models' outputs as additional training data, a practice that has proven effective in large language models by generating and iteratively refining reasoning steps. This autonomous learning process mitigates the dependency on human-curated input while progressively improving reasoning performance. Previous research has demonstrated advancements in reasoning through the selection and fine-tuning of correct reasoning examples; however, self-training methodologies specific to vision-language models remain underexplored. This work addresses this gap, thereby contributing to the advancement of reasoning mechanisms in multimodal AI systems.

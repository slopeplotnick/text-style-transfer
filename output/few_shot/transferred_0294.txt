We propose a novel approach for extracting meaningful information from images by leveraging advanced language models capable of integrating multimodal data. Our method aligns visual data with specific knowledge systems to construct interpretable knowledge graphs. Additionally, we introduce a graph-based technique for analyzing the similarity between generated images and their corresponding training data, thereby enhancing the understanding of generative models' outputs. This methodology facilitates applications such as determining provenance, improving interpretability, and fostering transparency regarding data sources, demonstrating its utility in improving traceability in generative models.  The proposed method was evaluated on both locally trained models and large-scale foundation models through a focused experiment on Ghibli-style imagery. This experiment aimed to assess the method's ability to detect stylistic influences, even in cases where model internals are inaccessible. The structure of the paper is as follows: Section 2 reviews related work on understanding generative models and constructing knowledge graphs using language models; Section 3 details our proposed methodology; Section 4 discusses experimental results for both local and foundation models; Section 5 concludes with key findings and directions for future research.  As generative models continue to advance, comprehending their behavior has become increasingly critical across technical, ethical, and policy domains. General interpretability techniques, such as t-SNE (van der Maaten and Hinton, 2008), LIME (Ribeiro et al., 2016), and attention visualization (Bahdanau et al., 2015), provide insights into model behavior but often fail to address the provenance of training data—a vital consideration in areas such as authorship and copyright. To investigate the influence of training data on generated outputs, several attribution methods have been proposed, including influence functions (Koh and Liang, 2017), retraining-based attribution (Nguyen et al., 2020), and Shapley value approximations (Štrumbelj and Kononenko, 2014). While effective in constrained settings, such as classification tasks, these methods are computationally infeasible for large-scale models, as they require gradient computation, retraining access, or substantial computational resources. Consequently, their applicability to modern deep learning setups, particularly for opaque generative models, remains limited.

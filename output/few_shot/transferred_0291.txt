We have developed a novel benchmark entitled HEALTHCONTRADICT, designed to quantitatively evaluate the capability of language models in processing extensive and conflicting medical information. This benchmark surpasses existing medical question-answering tests in effectively assessing model performance. Our empirical evaluations reveal that language models with proficiency in medical domains exhibit superior accuracy in utilizing relevant information, disregarding erroneous data, and resolving informational conflicts. To our knowledge, the TREC Health Misinformation tracks are the sole publicly accessible resource comprising expertly crafted health-related questions with definitive answers and scientific validation, alongside document pools containing both supportive and opposing evidence assessed by experts. These components are essential for generating health questions with conclusive answers, utilizing pairs of conflicting documents to assess how well language models manage medical knowledge discrepancies. In HEALTHCONTRADICT, we prioritize significant and credible documents that demonstrate the efficacy of a document in substantiating the response to a question. We standardized the labeling by converting the 2019 tags "effective"/"ineffective" and the 2021 tags "supportive"/"dissuades" to "yes"/"no," consistent with the 2022 labels. Additionally, we define that if two documents related to the same question express opposing viewpoints—one affirmative and one negative—they constitute a contradictory pair. Furthermore, a document is deemed accurate if its perspective aligns with scientific evidence, and inaccurate if it contradicts scientific evidence. The initial datasets comprise 130 expert-reviewed questions (50 from 2019, 35 from 2021, and 45 from 2022), including questions with binary views and those containing both supportive and opposing documents. Applying this criterion reduces the dataset to 110 questions. We pair supportive and opposing documents to form contradiction pairs, ensuring each document is utilized only once, as some documents may pertain to multiple questions. We exclude questions that fail to generate at least one contradiction pair due to an insufficient number of unique documents, resulting in a final dataset of 81 questions and 920 pairs of conflicting documents.

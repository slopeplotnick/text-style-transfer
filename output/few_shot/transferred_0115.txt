In the context of spatial reasoning derived solely from visual inputs, this study introduces VIDEO4SPATIAL, a framework demonstrating how video models can tackle complex spatial reasoning tasks using only visual scene data. The evaluation focuses on two tasks: (1) navigating through a scene based on camera direction instructions, and (2) object localization, which involves understanding spatial environments, executing instructions, and planning actions. Notably, these tasks rely exclusively on visual input from video sequences, without supplementary data such as depth maps or camera positional information. By leveraging efficient and minimalist design principles, VIDEO4SPATIAL effectively captures spatial understanding from video, encompassing task planning, object localization, adherence to spatial continuity during navigation, and generalizing to extended scenes and unseen environments. This approach advances video models' capabilities in spatial reasoning, drawing parallels to human spatial cognition.  Human spatial reasoning—encompassing memory, interpretation, and interaction within spatial environments—has been a focal point of research aimed at extending similar competencies to neural network models. Recent advancements in video models highlight their potential in emulating human spatial understanding, comparable to the progress observed in large language models for textual inference and reasoning. Since video inherently conveys sequential and temporally coherent information, these models excel in spatial reasoning tasks, such as predicting and generating future scenes that maintain spatial consistency. For instance, in a maze navigation scenario, a model predicts subsequent scenes while preserving the structure and smooth transition of the maze.  Given that human spatial cognition largely relies on visual perception, this research prioritizes video-based spatial reasoning frameworks for their simplicity, scalability, and alignment with natural human abilities. However, existing methods for extracting spatial information from video frequently rely on auxiliary data, such as depth maps or camera metadata, which limits their practical application. In this paper, we present VIDEO4SPATIAL, a robust framework demonstrating that video models can acquire spatial intelligence solely from video data. Our framework employs a standard video model with a straightforward objective: producing a video output that completes a spatial task by adhering to the spatial and temporal properties of the environment. This methodology underscores the capability of video models to emulate human-like spatial reasoning effectively and efficiently, solely through the processing of video inputs.

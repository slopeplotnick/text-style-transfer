Instead of employing a frame-by-frame motion tracking approach, distribution matching techniques are utilized to train controllers to replicate the overall behavioral patterns derived from a motion dataset. Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) employs a discriminator to differentiate between the motions of the agent and those within the dataset. The Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) extends this concept to enable controllers to generate realistic behaviors for novel tasks not present in the initial dataset. However, adversarial priors necessitate training with a distinct policy for each new task, requiring retraining and additional dataset availability. Our proposed method achieves comparable efficacy to these approaches by utilizing a reusable motion prior that can be adapted for styles absent in the original dataset. In recent years, diffusion models have gained widespread popularity due to their exceptional performance across various domains, and their application in control tasks is increasingly prevalent. These models have the capability to generate high-quality motion, making them suitable as motion planners that predict future movements, subsequently executed by a tracking controller. Moreover, diffusion models have been employed to directly control behaviors in tasks such as manipulation and character control, facilitating flexible and diverse actions. Contrary to methods that integrate diffusion models within a controller, our approach leverages pretrained diffusion models as general behavioral guides, employing them as a reward function in reinforcement learning to assist policies through score distillation sampling.

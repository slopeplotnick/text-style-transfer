When the target is situated at a considerable distance, the character initiates rapid locomotion to approach it. This behavior arises from integrating a stationary motion framework with a straightforward task of progressing towards the target, negating the necessity for a motion planner to dictate the character's movements. The Stationary Motion Planning (SMP) framework enables the system to utilize behaviors from the dataset and adapt them to formulate new skills applicable to diverse tasks. In the context of the Dodgeball task, agents trained with SMP autonomously acquire the ability to jump and evade, akin to human players engaging in dodgeball. Notably, these maneuvers were absent from the original dataset but embody strategies resembling those employed by humans. In contrast, Adversarial Motion Planning (AMP) demonstrates suboptimal performance for this task, likely because the requisite dodging skills deviate significantly from the basic movements contained within the dataset. Furthermore, AMP policies employing a fixed discriminator are unsuccessful at generating natural movements or excelling in tasks, indicating that adversarial methodologies lack efficacy even when applied to identical tasks. Conversely, SMP facilitates the application of a single motion framework across multiple tasks and policies. The experimental outcomes illustrated in Figure 7 suggest that SMP provides more efficient, consistent, and beneficial guidance throughout the training process. The ability to rise after a fall constitutes a crucial skill for both humans and humanoid agents. Nonetheless, acquiring this skill is challenging, as numerous methods rely on supplementary rewards to prevent erratic character movements. While AMP, which modifies the motion setup during training, can develop natural recovery strategies, our approach demonstrates that, even with a fixed motion framework, SMP is capable of training a robust get-up skill that functions effectively from random fallen positions. As depicted in Figure 5(e), the character, despite being knocked down at a distance, successfully rolls over, utilizes its hands to push itself upright, and stands up again.

To discern trends from the accuracy and false positive rate outcomes, we examine the verifier gain presented in the final row. Verifier gain illustrates the utility of employing the verifier during rejection sampling, which involves sampling until the verifier endorses a solution. This evaluation provides insights into the efficacy of verification: self-verification yields the minimal gains, and more accurate solvers do not inherently enhance their performance independently. A modest increase in gains is observed with intra-family verification, whereas cross-family verification furnishes the most significant benefits. We assess verifier performance across diverse model families and sizes. In Figure 3, experiments analogous to those in Figure 2 are conducted, with each verifier metric displayed according to model size within each model family. It is evident that verification accuracy and false negative rates improve as model size increases, although the false positive rate exhibits less predictability and frequently escalates with model size, as seen in intra-family verification for DeepSeek. For post-trained models such as Qwen3 and DeepSeek, verifier gains are most substantial in the cross-family setting, reduced in the intra-family setting, and minimal during self-verification. This finding appears to contradict the assertions of Song et al. (2025), who report that self-verification GV-Gaps expand with increased pretraining FLOPs. However, their analysis pertains to older model families, and Figure 2 also indicates larger verifier gains for antecedent models like Qwen2.5 and Llama3. We hypothesize that advanced post-trained models like DeepSeek and Qwen3 exhibit limited gains in self-verification and intra-family verification due to two primary factors: firstly, these models may inherently perform self-verification when utilized as solvers, rendering an additional forced verification round redundant; secondly, their distributions are considerably sharpened through post-training, thereby restricting the improvement potential from rejection sampling.

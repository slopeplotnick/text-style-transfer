We have developed Menta, an innovative model for predicting mental health issues using social media text, emphasizing accuracy and user-friendliness. Menta employs a training methodology that effectively addresses data imbalance, proving effective across various clinical tasks. It excels in maintaining a balance between accuracy and efficiency, outperforming several existing models and competing with large language models in six mental health tasks. Additionally, Menta is designed to be accessible and shareable, featuring a setup that prioritizes privacy and resource efficiency. Traditionally, mental health predictions from social media posts relied on conventional machine learning techniques and models demanding extensive manual setup. For instance, Jiang et al. utilized advanced lexical models to identify mental health issues on Reddit, demonstrating superiority over traditional methods. Similarly, Sarkar et al. developed a model predicting depression and anxiety from Reddit posts by leveraging shared features, outperforming single-focus models. Other studies have devised tools for suicide risk screening using deep learning, facilitating early intervention. Recent advancements include the application of large language models in this domain. One notable study employed ChatGPT to classify mental health issues, such as stress, depression, and suicidality, from social media without prior domain-specific training, indicating these models' predictive potential. Another investigation emphasized detecting depression from online text, concluding that fine-tuned models significantly outperform traditional counterparts. Research further explored the interpretative capacity of these models concerning depressive symptoms and emotional states, finding that prompts incorporating emotional cues and examples enhance model performance.

Predictions are frequently observed to stabilize prior to the final step in iterative processes (Pengxiang et al., 2025). Strategies to balance exploration and acceleration often leverage historical results or measures of predictability to eliminate redundant steps (Wei et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b; Or-El et al., 2025). Recent efforts have explored enhancing accuracy by enabling models to iteratively refine their own predictions, thereby correcting errors and resolving uncertainties (Zhang et al., 2025a; Su et al., 2025). Additionally, more efficient methodologies have drawn inspiration from continuous processes, employing stage-wise learning, persistent states, or flow-matching techniques to maintain consistency throughout (Sahoo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Meister et al., 2024). Further techniques aimed at improving computational efficiency include caching intermediate outcomes for rapid retrieval and integrating concurrent verification mechanisms during the reasoning process (Yuan et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a; Sohoni et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025a). Despite the efficacy of these approaches, many necessitate supplementary training, novel modeling frameworks, or intricate heuristic regulations, underscoring the demand for a simplified mechanism to determine termination criteria without imposing additional computational or modeling requirements.  In this context, we introduce SchED, a schedule-based early stopping mechanism that eschews the need for supplementary training or specialized model configurations. SchED evaluates the confidence level of each intermediate prediction against a dynamic threshold that gradually becomes less stringent over successive steps. By decoupling prediction confidence from step count and employing a smoothly evolving threshold, SchED enables models to terminate computations as soon as outputs stabilize, thereby avoiding abrupt disruptions or reliance on fixed cutoffs. Importantly, SchED integrates seamlessly with pre-existing schedules and does not necessitate alterations in model training processes (Nie et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025).

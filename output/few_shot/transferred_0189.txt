The decision was made to forego the inclusion of the KL divergence regularization term, as the model demonstrated proficiency in reasoning and output formatting from the outset. The imposition of KL constraints would curtail the model's exploratory capabilities in devising novel problem-solving strategies, hence the model was allowed to optimize freely based on its existing knowledge. Our reward system is structured around three objectives: (1) Format standardization—special tokens are employed to distinctly separate the reasoning process from the final answer, thereby enhancing clarity and utility; (2) Output space constraint—a stringent control is maintained over the categories of mismatches (e.g., "price mismatch" or "functionality mismatch"), preventing the generation of arbitrary outputs; (3) Progressive reward—partial positive reinforcement is granted for outputs that achieve relevance but contain minor errors, facilitating accelerated and stable learning. A three-phase learning strategy was implemented to systematically increase the complexity of the training data, as outlined in Table 6. The strategy is predicated upon three principles: (1) Rapid Bootstrap Principle—Step 1 predominantly involves medium-difficulty samples to provide prompt and informative feedback, maintaining a balance between challenge and stability to prevent early training issues; (2) Progressive Challenge Principle—Steps 2 and 3 incrementally introduce more challenging samples, enabling the model to transition from resolving common problems to tackling complex cases, thereby broadening its competencies; (3) Knowledge Retention Principle—the latter phases consistently incorporate easy samples as "memory anchors" to prevent skill attrition while acquiring new knowledge, thereby ensuring stable training.

Progress has been made in identifying bias, reducing toxicity, and assessing fairness in AI systems. However, existing methodologies predominantly focus on evaluating individual outcomes of models rather than examining the temporal evolution of their ethical reasoning or value alignment across varying contexts. This issue is particularly significant for autonomous AI systems that adapt their responses based on user inquiries, user preferences, task requirements, and previous interactions. A lack of temporal consistency poses the risk that the ethical perspectives of these systems may deteriorate or fluctuate due to linguistic nuances, contextual pressures, or temporal shifts. Such inconsistencies can undermine the reliability, trustworthiness, and accountability of AI systems. Ensuring ethical consistency is not merely a matter of adherence to guidelines but is crucial for fostering stable, comprehensible, and trustworthy AI reasoning over time. In critical domains such as healthcare, finance, education, and governance, even minor ethical deviations can propagate misinformation, reinforce biases, and yield outcomes misaligned with societal values. Despite growing awareness of these challenges, extant evaluation methods predominantly rely on static tests and datasets that do not adapt to the dynamic behavior of AI systems. A significant research challenge is the development of methodologies to autonomously assess and comprehend the ethical stability of these models without dependence on external data or retrospective analyses.   To address this, the present work introduces the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP), an autonomous framework for ethical evaluation that operates independently of predefined datasets and is designed to monitor, interpret, and enhance the moral reasoning of AI models. MoCoP functions autonomously by generating ethical scenarios, eliciting model responses, and interpreting these responses through its inherent rules and methodologies. Unlike conventional approaches that depend on predefined tests and external inputs, MoCoP autonomously constructs and evaluates ethical scenarios. It employs three core components—lexical analysis, semantic risk assessment, and reasoning evaluation—to derive comprehensive metrics of moral consistency and ethical stability. Each component serves a distinct function: lexical analysis identifies linguistic bias and issues, semantic risk assessment evaluates potential semantic risks, and reasoning evaluation models the decision-making processes.

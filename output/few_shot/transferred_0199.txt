The recordings were meticulously reviewed to ensure clarity and adherence to the given instructions. After approval, transcription was carried out by trained professionals utilizing a standardized system. Only recordings and their corresponding transcripts that successfully passed the final quality check were incorporated into the dataset. Payment adjustments were made according to linguistic requirements. This rigorous process ensured that the resultant dataset was of high technical quality, encompassed speakers from diverse backgrounds, and complied with ethical standards. The prompts were unscripted, developed by language teams to ensure cultural relevance and naturalness. Each team was composed of a leader and typically three assistants, who engaged in peer review before the leader authorized the prompts for the project. Microsoft Forms was employed to select a knowledge area, from which teams then chose topics and associated subtopics. Language teams were trained to craft prompts eliciting varied responses, such as "What's your favorite fruit and why?" rather than merely "What's your favorite fruit?". They were instructed on the importance of designing prompts to generate responses in various forms, including questions, exclamations, and commands. Teams were also encouraged to enhance the cultural relevancy and sensitivity of topics and subtopics as necessary. The overarching objective was to accurately capture the linguistic nuances of South African vernacular, ensuring that the topics and subdomains were highly pertinent to South African life and specific to the respective language.

The Area Under the Curve (AUC) is utilized to assess a model's ranking capability without being influenced by specific decision thresholds, which is advantageous in scenarios involving imbalanced data. In this discourse, we introduce a novel evaluation framework that integrates Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification, two domains that have seldom been jointly examined. Initially, we explore the rationale behind the historical separation of these fields and analyze the limitations of existing evaluation methodologies that hinder simultaneous assessment. Subsequently, we elucidate the fundamental concepts underpinning our framework and detail its operational mechanics. It is crucial to comprehend the reasons for the traditional division between these domains. Predominantly, this separation arises because conventional Fact Verification datasets emphasize textual analysis, whereas Hallucination Detection techniques concentrate on model behavior. Fact Verification evaluations, such as FEVER, rely on static pairs of claims and labels, neglecting the claims' provenance. This configuration is incompatible with Hallucination Detection methodologies, which necessitate examination of generative processes, including scrutiny of internal states or consistency across samples. Such indicators are absent from static Fact Verification datasets, complicating the joint evaluation. One proposed solution involves developing assessments using predefined outputs from large language models (e.g., HELM). Despite their utility, this approach encounters significant challenges in the rapidly evolving AI landscape: (1) Model obsolescence occurs swiftly; datasets constructed with current leading models, such as LLaMA-3, may quickly become outdated as new models emerge. (2) Inability to evaluate novel models; static assessments cannot accommodate Hallucination Detection techniques applied to emerging or varied models. Given that these methods frequently rely on specific model attributes, an inflexible system impedes progress and fails to demonstrate genuine adaptability.

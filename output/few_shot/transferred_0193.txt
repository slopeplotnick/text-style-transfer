The presence of this issue is not discernible when analyzing a single model in isolation; rather, it emerges from the configuration and interaction dynamics, thereby compromising the system's safety, reliability, and accountability. This paper identifies three preliminary factors potentially indicating the emergence of this problem. These factors are not exhaustive, and additional factors may be discovered through ongoing experimentation. Firstly, the discourse and mutual influence among agents is a critical factor. Secondly, the challenge of comprehending the agents' cognitive processes or communications is significant. Lastly, the divergence of goals among agents plays a crucial role. Collectively, these factors provide insight into the conditions under which group risks manifest. Each factor is quantifiable and can be examined in controlled environments involving multiple agents. The flow of information through the network is a pertinent aspect; closely interconnected systems enable rapid collaboration but also facilitate the swift dissemination of errors. Conversely, loosely connected systems slow error propagation but complicate collaborative resolution efforts. The structure of information dissemination influences the propensity for an isolated error to evolve into a widely held belief. Metrics such as the velocity of error diffusion and the prevalence of risky behavior adoption among agents are essential for understanding these dynamics. Safety assessments for multi-agent systems should incorporate considerations of information architecture. The concept of cognitive opacity pertains to the extent of obscurity or incomprehensibility in the agents' cognitive processes. This may occur inadvertently or be deliberately induced when the pursuit of specific objectives obscures genuine reasoning processes. In such scenarios, one model may uncritically accept another model's assertions, even when erroneous. The intent-opacity rate serves as a metric for evaluating the traceability of reasoning underpinning outputs.

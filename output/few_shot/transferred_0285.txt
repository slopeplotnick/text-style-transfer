The endeavor to ensure interoperability among diverse systems is the primary focus of our study. We are establishing controlled experimental environments where artificial intelligence systems can enforce, interpret, and adapt shared protocols. Our objective is to ascertain whether these methodologies mitigate the issues previously discussed. This effort is directed towards constructing a research trajectory in AI safety that integrates theoretical frameworks, empirical investigations, and ultimately, the process of standardization. This paper serves as a conceptual roadmap; future work will present the empirical proofs and experiments necessary for validation.   We posit that emphasizing the safety of an individual AI—through methods such as prompt engineering and output filtering—is insufficient when considering the interaction of multiple AI entities. As AI systems increasingly collaborate, adhering solely to localized regulations becomes inadequate to avert systemic failures. A distinction exists between ensuring the safety of a singular AI and safeguarding the integrity of an entire system. We introduce the concept of the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH) to elucidate this divergence. Our analysis demonstrates that risks can escalate from minor issues to significant systemic crises as interaction, complexity, and diverse objectives intersect. These risks are intrinsic to networked operations rather than isolated anomalies of individual AI agents.   By empowering agents to modify rules and collaborate, we aim to maintain stable collective behavior without an overreliance on centralized control mechanisms. The ESRH framework and the institutional AI paradigm represent the nascent stages of this inquiry, necessitating comprehensive testing, theoretical elaboration, and continual refinement. Our subsequent steps involve conducting controlled experiments to evaluate the indicators we have proposed, exploring models of coordination, and developing practical tools for implementing these concepts in operational AI systems. The transition from single-agent to multi-agent AI paradigms is currently underway. Without innovative frameworks and management strategies, safety concerns will remain obscured until they manifest as unmanageable challenges. This paper endeavors to elucidate these concerns and propose a systematic approach to address them.

In Section 3, the discussion centered on the interconnection between the questions and four principal themes pertinent to the research. A majority of the interviewees were unfamiliar with Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE); thus, it was necessary to elucidate its significance during the interviews, potentially introducing some bias. Nevertheless, we place confidence in the interviewees' ability to form independent opinions and in the interviewer's capacity to minimize undue influence. The questions were intentionally crafted to be open-ended, facilitating comprehensive responses and the dissemination of diverse ideas. Particular attention was given to the phrasing of the questions to ensure they did not constrain the respondents' potential answers. Metrics were developed to optimize the extraction of meaningful insights from the collected data, including the frequency and prevalence of key terms both broadly and within specific roles. The resulting data are presented in tables and illustrated through graphs to depict distribution. Unique responses, even if singularly expressed, were incorporated and considered in the analysis. Detailed examination of all responses allowed for the categorization and alignment of terms derived from the interviews with those prevalent in SPLE literature, such as "personalisation" and "refinement of specific code segments," akin to "customization." This alignment enhanced the consistency of the results and facilitated the identification of scholarly arguments to address stakeholders' concerns. The iterative process with various participants contributed to the refinement of interview techniques, improving the fluidity of the interviews. While the questions remained constant, the execution evolved, incorporating additional clarifications and smoother transitions when necessary. Despite these procedural adaptations, it is posited that the volume and quality of information gathered from the initial and final questionnaires remained comparable.

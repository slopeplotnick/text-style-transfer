Despite significant advancements in memory technology, its application in predicting stock prices remains challenging. Current systems face several limitations: they inadequately deconstruct event sequences, insufficiently match historical scenarios with novel situations, and poorly accommodate the dynamic nature of financial markets. Consequently, there is a pronounced demand for the development of memory systems specifically tailored for financial applications. This chapter examines research in two principal domains: the transition from mere pattern recognition to the utilization of memory systems, and the growing significance of event-driven prediction. While substantial progress has been made, a critical challenge persists in integrating structured event knowledge with trackable memory systems, which is the primary focus of this work. Traditionally, stock price prediction relied on identifying historical data patterns resembling current conditions, akin to memory systems in Large Language Models (LLMs), where the objective is to store and retrieve past experiences or contexts. Initially, researchers employed statistical methods, proficient in detecting numerical patterns but limited in handling complex market information. With the advent of deep learning, models such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Transformers emerged, enabling the automatic identification of temporal features. Transformer models, in particular, excelled at capturing long-term dependencies through self-attention mechanisms, significantly advancing the field. The FinSeer framework further incorporated LLMs to match historical numerical data more effectively, facilitating a deeper analysis of features.

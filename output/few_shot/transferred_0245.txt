Ensuring proper licensing of datasets is crucial for facilitating their ethical accessibility and usability, particularly for under-resourced languages. Open licenses, such as Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0), significantly enhance the capacity for large-scale data reuse (Creative Commons, 2021). Initiatives such as Mozilla Common Voice exemplify the utility of open licenses in advancing language technology development (Mozilla, 2022). Nonetheless, researchers in low- and middle-income countries often encounter barriers in accessing resources for AI development, highlighting issues in equitable data governance and local community empowerment (Smith et al., 2023). Within the Swivuriso project, the CC BY 4.0 license was employed to ensure open access and ethical accountability (Swivuriso Team, 2023). The dataset is hosted on Zenodo and Hugging Face, featuring permanent DOIs and comprehensive documentation. Each release includes audio, corresponding transcripts, and extensive information regarding demographics, language, and accessibility. An ethical use statement is also provided, with limitations on practices such as voice cloning to safeguard speaker privacy. The CC BY 4.0 license permits sharing, modification, and commercial utilization of the dataset, contingent upon appropriate attribution and acknowledgment of modifications. Furthermore, the license prohibits additional legal or technical restrictions, encompasses database rights, and incorporates a "cure period" for rectifying licensing issues, thereby ensuring legal clarity and fostering global scientific collaboration (Creative Commons, 2021). However, while open licenses enhance data utility, they frequently overlook aspects such as local governance or the protection of Indigenous knowledge. The universal application of such licenses can inadvertently undermine community ownership, particularly concerning cultural heritage or community-derived data. This challenge is catalyzing a paradigm shift towards more community-centric data stewardship, emphasizing benefit-sharing and community control, a trend gaining traction in African data management contexts (Mbembe et al., 2024).

When large language models (LLMs) are integrated into AI systems with multiple components collaborating to address complex tasks, the role of LLMs becomes critical in influencing downstream decision-making. A bias favoring the initial inputs processed by such models may propagate throughout the system, leading to unintended distortions. Addressing this bias is therefore essential to ensure fairness, transparency, and reliability in these systems.  This study highlights two principal issues. First, while article lengths were standardized, the degree to which evaluation methods are influenced by document length was not explicitly examined. This limitation warrants future research to stratify data based on document length or conduct controlled experiments to assess how variations in length influence the order effects on summarization. Second, the evaluation methods yielded consistently low scores, reflecting a broader challenge in using such metrics for summarization tasks—particularly when comparing extensive documents with their shorter summaries. Future investigations should expand their scope beyond the topic of abortion to include other contentious issues such as gun control, LGBTQ+ rights, and immigration, to determine whether order bias persists across different domains. Furthermore, studies should evaluate alternative LLMs, such as GPT or Claude, to assess the generalizability of this bias across models. Incorporating additional evaluative metrics, such as those assessing opinion biases or polarization in summaries, could offer deeper insights into the impact of information order on summarization quality.  Our findings reveal that LLMs exhibit a systematic preference for the first document processed when summarizing multiple texts, particularly in terms of semantic emphasis. These results corroborate prior research suggesting that the order of information significantly influences output. Such order sensitivity poses challenges for applications like AI-driven summarization and other hierarchical AI systems where the sequence of inputs could disproportionately shape subsequent outputs. Mitigating this bias is crucial for the development of fairer, more transparent, and reliable AI systems.

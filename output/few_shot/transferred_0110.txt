In conclusion, while employing contextual memory to identify LeechHijack appears promising, its precision is insufficient for direct application. The examination of contextual memory can aid in issue detection; however, establishing a robust and effective defense necessitates further development. This section discusses the limitations and broader implications of the proposed LeechHijack attack. We analyze the shortcomings of the current framework, delineate its potential applications, and identify prospective avenues for the threat's evolution or exacerbation over time. A significant limitation of our method pertains to its efficacy in handling hijacked computing resources. As illustrated in Table 7, the attack's Answer Success Rate (ASR) is suboptimal compared to that of the native LLM, which adversely impacts the utility of stolen resources for the attacker. This issue can be mitigated by distributing the additional tasks among multiple victims and employing a collaborative decision-making process on aggregated outcomes. Moreover, the range of tasks LeechHijack can perform is restricted. Our evaluations demonstrated its functionality for tasks within the MMLU benchmark, yet its efficacy on more challenging tasks, such as those in GAIA, was not assessed. LeechHijack is predominantly configured for conventional chat-based tasks, and its performance on more complex undertakings is likely inadequate. Enhancing the attack to efficiently handle sophisticated commands would require more advanced attack infrastructures. Given that agent systems utilize resource-intensive LLMs, attackers can exploit LeechHijack without engaging it for complex tasks. Instead, they can leverage the hijacked resources for simpler operations, such as information retrieval or generating content templates, by utilizing the model's extensive knowledge repository. Consequently, LeechHijack represents a tangible and ongoing threat to MCP and agent systems, irrespective of the complexity of the hijacked task.

In this study, we utilized scikit-learn to establish several baseline models, including Majority, Random, n-gram SVM, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree. Feature extraction was conducted using TF-IDF and count-vector methodologies. While baseline models were executed on a CPU, all transformer model evaluations were performed on a GPU (Google Colab T4). The primary metric for model evaluation was the micro-averaged F1-score, complemented by accuracy, precision, and recall to ensure a comprehensive comparison. We evaluated various models, specifically m-BERT cased, XLM-RoBERTa base, BanglaBERT, DistilBERT cased, and baseline methods, for their efficacy in detecting hate speech within the BLP2025 dataset. As shown in Table 2, the results for subtask 1A indicated that BanglaBERT achieved superior performance, with an accuracy of 0.71, surpassing all other models under consideration. It also attained a precision of 0.70 and recall of 0.71. The principal evaluation metric for the shared task 1A was the micro F1-score, with BanglaBERT attaining a score of 0.71. The BERT multilingual base (m-BERT) achieved an F1-score of 0.66, and XLM-RoBERTa base attained 0.67, both outperforming the baseline models. DistilBERT yielded an F1-score of 0.57, which was lower than both the SVM and Logistic Regression (LR) methods among the baseline models. BanglaBERT's micro F1-score was the highest observed for this dataset in our analysis. Among the baseline models, SVM performed the best with an F1-score of 0.61, whereas the random baseline recorded the lowest with an F1-score of 0.16. DistilBERT's performance, with an F1-score of 0.57, was suboptimal compared to SVM and LR, which achieved F1-scores of 0.63 and 0.62, respectively.

Formulating Questions: Drawing from the categorization discussed in Section 2.1, a cohort of eight graduate students, each with domain-specific expertise, identified pertinent categories and devised 20 prompts per category. These prompts were designed to necessitate the consideration of at least two scientific concepts, thereby presenting a considerable challenge. The prompts were derived from public educational experiments to ensure scientific rigor and feasibility for demonstration in videos of 10 seconds or less. Additionally, two of these graduate students compiled 40 Image-to-Video (I2V) questions, each accompanied by a video that accurately depicted the experiment as intended. These videos were sourced from public datasets and online repositories, with a key frame from the commencement of each video utilized as the primary image to depict the entire experimental setup.  Verification and Review: Upon completion of the test set, all graduate students convened to review the material collectively. Each student reviewed peer-generated prompts to ensure specificity, procedural accuracy, and clarity of intent. For final quality assurance, video synthesis models, specifically Sora-2 and Wan-2.5-T2V-Preview, were employed to generate the initial seconds of each prompt, ensuring the key experimental components were adequately represented.  Utilizing VLM as an Evaluator: Recent studies indicate that advanced Vision-Language Models (VLMs), when appropriately prompted and configured, can render judgments akin to human evaluators. Within the framework of VideoScience-Bench, a VLM-as-a-Judge configuration was implemented to assess performance discrepancies between VLMs emphasizing reasoning and those not, such as GPT-4o. It was observed that VLMs, particularly those lacking reasoning capabilities, frequently fail to substantiate their evaluations and occasionally issue false positives. This observation necessitated the development of a quantitative auto-evaluation system employing a checklist grading methodology, where each score must be corroborated by critical video frames and an evidential table derived from video analysis.

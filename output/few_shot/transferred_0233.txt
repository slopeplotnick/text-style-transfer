To evaluate the effectiveness of SMP, we employ it for the training of complex movement control tasks. Section 8.1 demonstrates the adaptability of SMP by applying a pre-trained model to derive novel control policies without reliance on the original movement dataset. Through the training of a singular style-based model on an extensive dataset, it becomes feasible to tailor it for diverse stylistic tasks by adjusting the model to the desired stylistic parameters. Moreover, the synthesis of novel styles is achievable by integrating the trained model with various stylistic elements. Section 8.2 illustrates the applicability of a single movement model across tasks such as navigation, target acquisition, and engaging in dodgeball. Beyond behavior modulation, as elucidated in Section 8.3, SMP is also proficient in managing interactions between humans and objects. By training the model with data pertaining to human-object dynamics, SMP acquires the capability to handle both object and character movements, enabling characters to emulate human-like interactions with objects. Furthermore, SMP facilitates natural recovery processes for characters, as evidenced in Section 8.4. Similar to other methods that align with data distributions, the policies developed through SMP are capable of generating novel skills that are not explicitly present in the original dataset. Section 8.5 elucidates that when trained on a limited walk-jog-run dataset, SMP autonomously generates varied locomotion strategies, enabling a character to adapt to assorted velocities and transition seamlessly between movements. Finally, to assess SMP's proficiency in movement imitation, it is evaluated on tasks involving the replication of isolated clips, as discussed in Section 8.6. SMP demonstrates the ability to accurately replicate an extensive array of dynamic and acrobatic maneuvers, achieving a performance level comparable to leading imitation learning methodologies.

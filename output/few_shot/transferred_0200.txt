Curriculum learning, a training paradigm wherein models are initially exposed to simpler tasks before progressing to more complex ones, was introduced by Bengio et al. (2009). This approach is extensively utilized in the development of large language models. Contemporary methodologies, such as Speed-RL and those proposed by Kim and Lee, adhere to this framework by organizing tasks in a sequence of increasing complexity. Another technique, LBS3, similarly initiates training with straightforward questions, advancing to more challenging ones. However, these methods often fail to align with optimal curriculum design principles, as they employ static difficulty levels without adapting to the model’s learning progression. Effective curriculum learning should dynamically adjust based on the model's performance improvements.   The CAPO framework, in contrast, employs a performance-based adaptive training strategy. Initially, it leverages positive feedback to establish stable learning behaviors, subsequently incorporating negative feedback to enhance the model’s learning capacity. Distinct from methods with fixed difficulty gradations, CAPO evolves in response to the model’s proficiency, facilitating stable early-stage learning and adaptive later-stage learning. CAPO represents an innovative model training methodology, utilizing feedback-informed curriculum adaptation as the model acquires knowledge. It begins with positive example-driven learning, transitioning to a combination of positive and negative example-driven learning, akin to human learning processes. This sequential feedback integration helps mitigate issues associated with premature mixed feedback exposure.  Empirical evaluations demonstrate that CAPO significantly enhances model performance relative to alternative methods across diverse tasks and model scales. CAPO’s applicability spans various domains, including mathematics and multidimensional data-centric tasks. By emphasizing adaptive progression over static difficulty thresholds, CAPO fosters the creation of learning algorithms that are both flexible and cognitively inspired.

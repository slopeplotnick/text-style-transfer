Experts in numerical modeling transform conceptual frameworks into quantitative representations. They initiate this process by deconstructing a primary hypothesis into constituent elements, each quantified with associated probabilities and characterized by distinct parameters. These probabilities represent the likelihood of each element's accuracy, and experts utilize various methodologies to assess these probabilities. Validation of the numerical model is achieved through comparative analysis with empirical data, necessitating predictive calculations by data specialists. Statistical methods are employed to test the model's predictive accuracy by determining the level of agreement between predicted and observed data. Practitioners proficient in Monte Carlo simulation employ Bayesian inversion techniques to refine hypotheses and parameters, incorporating data-driven updates. Decision-making processes are informed by the degree of uncertainty inherent in the predictions, necessitating collaborative communication among experts.  Subsequently, the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into this workflow is explored, with a preliminary discussion focusing on AI applications in mineral exploration. Instead of presenting AI through abstract definitions, an illustrative analogy is employed: Consider an autonomous vehicle tasked with navigating from origin to destination while adhering to safety, legal, and efficiency criteria. The vehicle accomplishes this through a series of actions—such as steering, accelerating, and decelerating—guided by sensor data that informs real-time assessments of environmental conditions and predicts subsequent actions for itself and external entities. The core components of this system include: Rewards, which encapsulate the objectives of safety, legality, and expeditious travel; Actions, which refer to operational maneuvers like steering and braking; and State, which represents the dynamic traffic environment, including other vehicles, pedestrians, and velocity metrics.

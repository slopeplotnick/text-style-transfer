The results presented in Table 1 demonstrate the efficacy of integrating model-focused and data-focused approaches. Specifically, the "Unify" block, when compared to other methods individually, consistently outperforms across various datasets and models, highlighting that internal uncertainty signals and external evidence function in complementary yet distinct manners. The Score-Level Fusion methodology illustrates that a straightforward aggregation technique can mitigate the substantial discrepancies observed with singular methods. By synthesizing diverse signals, this approach minimizes errors associated with individual predictions, thereby delivering more reliable outcomes and frequently surpassing the performance of isolated HD or FV methods. The Evidence-Aware Pipeline further enhances performance, emerging as the most effective among the evaluated methods. This superiority is attributed to its capability to address the issue of over-reliance on external evidence. In scenarios characterized by insufficient external information (such as NEI cases), enforcing a decision can introduce errors; however, this pipeline circumvents such pitfalls by reverting to internal uncertainty signals, which remain dependable in the absence of external evidence. Hybrid methods also rectify the issue of unstable performance identified in RQ1. In contrast to standalone HD methods, which exhibit significant variability across models like LLaMA and Qwen, hybrid methods maintain consistent performance across different models. This finding underscores the role of signal integration in balancing the confidence issues inherent in standalone HD methods. Consequently, these frameworks not only enhance accuracy but also demonstrate robustness across various models.

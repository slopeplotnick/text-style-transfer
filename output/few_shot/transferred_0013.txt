The prediction of stock prices remains a significant and enduring challenge within the field of finance. Stock prices are highly volatile and respond rapidly to new information, rendering traditional predictive methods inadequate in the context of real-time news and market sentiment. These conventional approaches frequently fail to capture the underlying factors influencing price fluctuations. Recently, the proliferation of online textual data and advancements in natural language processing have accelerated research into utilizing text for stock price prediction. However, the majority of studies employing these novel language models are limited to basic text analysis and immediate forecasting. They often lack the ability to integrate knowledge comprehensively or retain information over the long term, hindering their effectiveness in navigating the dynamic and intricate landscape of financial markets. This underscores the necessity for models capable of retaining and utilizing critical information over time. Financial markets are complex, as price changes are typically the result of multiple interconnected events unfolding over time, rather than isolated incidents. Predicting stock prices based on news differs from straightforward question-answering tasks; the response to "what will the stock price do tomorrow?" cannot be directly extracted from news articles. Models must synthesize market information from diverse sources, identify analogous historical scenarios, and engage in deep reasoning to genuinely discern market patterns, rather than merely perform text matching. Furthermore, financial texts often contain noisy and extraneous information that can obscure crucial signals, highlighting the importance of meticulous filtering and alignment with relevant content.

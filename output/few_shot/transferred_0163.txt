OpenHands aims to address the identified issue more effectively, possibly due to its system prompt containing more comprehensive instructions compared to the SWE-agent. Advanced language models, such as Claude Sonnet 4, typically excel across various tasks and exhibit a sophisticated mechanism for information organization. These models accumulate extensive contextual information and conduct additional verification tests to ensure system stability and integrity. The Devstral model, a smaller open-source model, also demonstrates a complex information organization strategy when deployed with OpenHands, likely due to its training on OpenHands for software development tasks, prompting increased searching and testing prior to implementing changes. This study also seeks to establish a correlation between the metrics of process measurement and the agent's problem-solving efficacy. Numerous early termination events were observed, attributable to runtime errors resulting from incorrect model responses, an acknowledged challenge. The process measurement is correlated with both resolution success and human-perceived task difficulty. Successful interventions generally involve shorter, concentrated efforts, whereas unsuccessful attempts are characterized by prolonged sequences and redundant actions. The complexity of information organization is further influenced by agent architecture and the underlying language models, where more advanced models generate more intricate and exploratory frameworks. In addition to examining agents' success or failure in resolving issues, this study employs Phase Flow Analysis to investigate agent behavior. Successful attempts tend to adhere to a methodical strategy of identification, resolution, and verification, aligning with the intended objectives of the agents. As task complexity escalates, successful pathways extend but remain coherent, while unsuccessful ones display repetitive or erratic phase transitions. Superior language models demonstrate enhanced and adaptable strategic approaches.

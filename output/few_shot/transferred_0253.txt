System prompts play a crucial role in modulating the behavior of large language models (LLMs), including the assignment of roles and stylistic guidelines for various tasks. Prior research highlights that elements such as personas, emotions, and jailbreak triggers can alter response characteristics and cognitive processing, although they do not consistently enhance task accuracy. Despite their increasing utilization, there remains a significant lack of knowledge regarding the systematic optimization of system prompts. Notably, certain leaked system prompts from Grok and Claude exhibit intricate, manually crafted rules. Recent advancements suggest the potential for automatic enhancement of system prompts through genetic algorithms, albeit limited to a single language and evaluation metric. The implementation of multilingual LLMs introduces challenges, such as language-specific obstacles and interlinguistic variances, often resulting in disparate responses to identical queries across languages, influenced by the model's varying proficiency in different languages. Techniques like Cross-Lingual Thought Prompting and multilingual self-consistency offer partial solutions but typically require multiple iterations of prompting. A comprehensive framework for optimizing system prompts across all languages is yet to be established. Beyond conventional evaluation metrics, recent investigations deconstruct LLM reasoning processes to facilitate more transparent understanding, although they predominantly focus on surface-level attributes, such as utilizing text prefill for output language control, without delving into the underlying cognitive mechanisms. This study addresses this gap by correlating system prompt design with cognitive patterns in multilingual contexts, elucidating the interplay between these cognitive structures and cross-linguistic performance efficacy.

We developed a dataset entitled HEALTHCONTRADICT, comprising 920 examples, each containing a health-related question and two documents presenting contradictory perspectives. Additionally, each example is supplemented with a factual answer supported by scientific evidence. This dataset was employed to evaluate various language models, ranging from 1 billion to 8 billion parameters, to assess their efficacy in addressing health-related inquiries amidst conflicting information. Our investigation focused on the performance of these models in responding to questions when provided with biomedical context and a language model. We examined two primary aspects: the accuracy of language models in answering health questions with conflicting information and the influence of biomedical context on their responses. The models were tested by providing them with correct, incorrect, or contradictory context and subsequently assessing their accuracy. Our key contributions are as follows: the creation of the novel HEALTHCONTRADICT dataset for testing language models against conflicting biomedical information; a comprehensive evaluation of these models using the dataset to assess their handling of extensive, conflicting biomedical texts with precise metrics; and a comparison between general language models and their specialized biomedical counterparts, revealing that the latter demonstrate superior capabilities in utilizing accurate information and disregarding incorrect data. To ensure a fair evaluation, each document in the dataset is unique. For instance, regarding the question "Can coffee help you lose weight?", two documents offer opposing viewpoints (one affirming and the other negating the claim), with the factual answer being affirmative, corroborated by scientific evidence that caffeine may contribute to reductions in weight, body mass index (BMI), and body fat.

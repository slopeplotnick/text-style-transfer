We are engaged in the expansion of a French-Ciluba sentiment lexicon to encompass translations in English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, Sesotho, and Sepedi. This endeavor involves the utilization of translation tools to facilitate cross-linguistic word mapping based on contextual usage. To enhance the lexicon, we will employ the Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) method to identify emotion- or opinion-laden words in African language texts, subsequently associating them with existing entries in the lexicon. Moreover, we will leverage the Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system in conjunction with large language models to disambiguate word meanings and ensure translation fidelity. Subsequently, we intend to train two models, AfriBERTa and AfroXLMR, to classify emotions or opinions in texts utilizing the augmented lexicon. Model performance will be evaluated through metrics such as precision, recall, F1-score, and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves. To enhance model interpretability, techniques will be employed to elucidate which text components the models prioritize during decision-making processes. Furthermore, a comparative analysis of individual model performance against ensemble methods will be conducted to assess potential accuracy improvements. The paper is structured as follows: initially, we review prior work in sentiment analysis, multilingual lexicon development, and African language processing. Subsequently, our framework and methodologies are elucidated, followed by a detailed account of our experimental procedures and model evaluations. The results are then analyzed, with discussions centered on their implications for multilingual natural language processing. Historically, sentiment analysis has predominantly employed machine learning techniques or sentiment lexicons to determine the polarity of opinions expressed in text. Early investigations revealed the efficacy of statistical methods in this domain, while subsequent research highlighted the utility of lexicons for straightforward, rule-based approaches (e.g., SentiWordNet, NRC Emotion Lexicon, and VADER) for resource-rich languages. However, these lexicons often fall short in adequately covering languages that are more complex or resource-poor. Efforts have been made to compile multilingual lexicons by extracting words from corpora.

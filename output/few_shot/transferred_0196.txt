It is critical to ensure that devices such as smartphones, laptops, smart assistants, drones, and car systems can efficiently manage adverse prompts and generate safe responses without reliance on cloud-based processing. Such capabilities enhance response latency, privacy, and functionality in constrained environments. Recent research efforts have attempted to develop safety models capable of operating across multiple languages. However, these models often exhibit suboptimal performance when applied to diverse linguistic settings, particularly those involving underrepresented languages. A prevalent approach involves training a model in a high-resource language such as English and subsequently evaluating its performance in other languages, leveraging shared linguistic features. While this approach demonstrates effectiveness for linguistically similar or well-supported languages, its efficacy diminishes for less commonly spoken languages due to limited overlap in linguistic features and insufficient representation in training corpora.  To address these challenges, we propose **CREST** (Cross-lingual Representation for Enhanced Safety Tasks), a multilingual safety model supporting 100 languages with a relatively modest 0.5 billion parameters. CREST is trained on 13 high-resource languages and employs a multilingual transformer framework to cluster languages based on semantic and structural similarities. This grouping strategy enables the model to generalize from well-supported languages to related lower-resource languages, effectively mitigating data scarcity in safety-critical applications. Despite its compact size, CREST outperforms existing models, delivering robust performance across both resource-rich and less commonly spoken languages. Its success can be attributed to the use of shared lexical and orthographic patterns, facilitating cross-lingual generalization.  To the best of our knowledge, CREST represents the first multilingual safety model demonstrating consistent efficacy across a broad spectrum of languages.

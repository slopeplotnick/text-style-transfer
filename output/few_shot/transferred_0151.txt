The study aims to develop larger and more diverse text corpora by leveraging data sources such as social media, news outlets, and spoken language, addressing the challenge of data scarcity. The research employs explainable AI methodologies to ensure the reliability of sentiment analysis in textual data. The study explores strategies for expanding lexicons by employing various techniques and evaluates the performance of AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa models on underrepresented African languages, particularly in low-resource settings. The objective is to enhance coverage, accuracy, and comprehension of sentiment across diverse linguistic contexts. This work illustrates the potential for lexicon expansion and sentiment analysis in resource-scarce languages. Through the application of multifaceted approaches, the study improved sentiment lexicons for languages such as Ciluba, French, English, and selected South African languages, employing both machine and human validation to ensure accuracy. Empirical evaluations indicate that AfroXLMR generally outperforms AfriBERTa in terms of accuracy and consistency, although AfriBERTa remains a viable option for scenarios with limited computational resources. Both models demonstrated efficacy in identifying sentiment-bearing morphemes in complex languages like Zulu and Xhosa. Despite translational and data volume challenges, the research substantiates the feasibility and relevance of sentiment analysis in low-resource language settings. Future directions include data augmentation, the deployment of explainable AI, and lexicon expansion to further enhance analysis capabilities. This study provides a robust framework for continued exploration and development in the field of computational linguistics for low-resource languages.

The threat model examines the potential consequences following the deployment of a malicious tool by an adversarial agent. Under this model, it is assumed that while the tool's code might be inspected or audited, the presence of an embedded backdoor goes undetected due to the similarity between malicious and benign actions. In hybrid or cloud-based environments, where users lack visibility into internal tool functionalities, the risk arises solely from observable outcomes. The attacker adheres to predefined operational constraints, avoiding privilege escalation or direct memory manipulation. The attack is deemed successful if it achieves two objectives: (1) fulfilling the attacker’s intended task and (2) maintaining satisfactory performance on the user’s task with minimal deviations.  The primary concern of the threat is resource misuse, specifically financial cost, such as excessive consumption of API tokens or GPU time. The cumulative impact of multiple tools engaging in such behavior can lead to significant resource expenditure. This section introduces the LeechHijack attack, beginning with an overview of its operational framework. Initially, the mechanism of embedding a covert backdoor into a seemingly compliant tool is discussed. Subsequently, the exploitation of the backdoor to establish a covert communication channel and induce unauthorized computational workload is detailed. Finally, the conditions necessary for the attack’s success and the metrics employed for its evaluation are presented.  LeechHijack executes its exploit in two phases. In the first phase, the attacker deploys a tool that appears benign but is embedded with a dormant backdoor. During standard usage, the backdoor remains inactive, exhibiting no apparent malicious behavior. In the second phase, the backdoor is activated via a predefined trigger. Upon activation, the tool generates manipulated responses that compel the agent to execute additional computational tasks benefiting the attacker. This phase concludes when the tool reverts to its normal operation after completing the attacker’s objectives, leaving no discernible evidence for the user.

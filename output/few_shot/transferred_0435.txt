Explanations in Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) must effectively convey clarity and fidelity to the underlying model. There are two primary criteria for these explanations: comprehensibility by humans and accurate representation of model operations. Comprehensibility typically necessitates that explanations be concise and direct, although the ultimate clarity is contingent upon the recipient's understanding. Fidelity requires that the explanation accurately depict the model's mechanisms without introducing bias, which can result from deliberately constrained explanations. Although clarity and fidelity are distinct attributes, enhancing one does not straightforwardly enhance the other. For instance, explanations that align with user expectations may be deemed more comprehensible; however, this can introduce bias by influencing the selection of explanations. Furthermore, a true explanation that is misinterpreted can potentially mislead the recipient. This study proposes a post hoc explanation approach aimed at achieving both clarity and fidelity. The methodology advocates for explanations framed in simple language with explicit cause-and-effect relationships, akin to natural human explanatory practices. Such an approach ensures that the intended understanding of the explanation is achieved without necessitating supplementary interpretative guidelines, thereby simplifying the communication. Emphasizing simplicity in language is crucial as the data leveraged by the model might not align with optimal explanatory practices. This method is integral to a category of techniques identified as Concept-based XAI, which presupposes that, while models are adept at managing data forms such as images and graphs, human communication predominantly employs meaningful conceptual representations.

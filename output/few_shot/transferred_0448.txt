In this section, we elucidate the aforementioned process using a specific example. The CelebA dataset, comprising over 200,000 images of celebrity faces, serves as the basis for our analysis. Each image is annotated with attributes specifying characteristics such as smiling, wearing glasses, youthfulness, and attractiveness. Our investigation particularly focuses on assessing whether individuals in the images are classified as young and/or attractive. Figure 6 illustrates this by presenting the original image on the left, alongside altered variations designed to depict the individual as not young (Youth = 0). The initial modified image was generated using a specified methodology. Notwithstanding the potential for visual-focused systems to yield misleading results, saliency maps remain widely utilized to elucidate the functioning of image classification systems. Accordingly, we juxtapose our causal explanations with those derived from Grad-CAM, employing an implementation by Gildenblat.  Referring to Figure 7, it elucidates the rationale behind the classification of the two leftmost images in Figure 5 as young. For the male subject, the chin area was pivotal, whereas for the female subject, the eye region was significant. Subsequently, Figure 8 delves into the classification of the individual illustrated in Figure 6. The first image is accurately classified as young, with Grad-CAM revealing that the classifier primarily considered the eye region. Conversely, for the misclassified image on the right, Grad-CAM indicates that the classifier concentrated on the forehead and chin regions. Notably, Grad-CAM lacks the capability to provide causal explanations as it cannot predict outcomes of hypothetical changes; instead, it merely highlights image regions integral to the classifier's decision-making. Additionally, since Grad-CAM explanations are pixel-based, they do not substantiate why those specific areas are deemed significant.

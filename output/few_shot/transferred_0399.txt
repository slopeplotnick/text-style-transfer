We investigate a novel approach to detect fraud in journal entry data in the absence of labeled examples, acknowledging the inherent difficulty in obtaining such labels and their frequent unavailability. Consequently, methods reliant on labeled data are unsuitable for this context. Instead, our approach focuses on identifying anomalous patterns in the dataset to flag entries that deviate from normative behavior. To evaluate our method, we utilize a synthetic dataset designed by Gronewald et al. (2024), which simulates the bookkeeping operations of a medium-sized enterprise. Each entry in this dataset includes features such as a unique identifier, date and time of posting and transaction, transaction type (credit or debit), tax rate, and account ID. Additionally, we assess performance on real-world anonymized accounting data provided by a business partner. The anonymization process removed typical linkages between debit and credit entries, necessitating the reframing of the problem to detect fraud at the transaction level.   To address the challenges posed by the anonymized data, we incorporate bookkeeping rules, professional guidelines, and summary statistics into the prompts provided to a large language model (LLM), which enhances its performance relative to traditional machine learning methods. Given the sensitivity and privacy concerns associated with accounting data, we prioritize the use of open-source LLMs, such as LLaMA AI (2025) and models developed by the Gemma Team (2024), over proprietary alternatives like OpenAI’s GPT-4 (2024), for experimental evaluation. Our proposed method demonstrates superior performance compared to baseline methods on both the synthetic dataset from Gronewald et al. (2024) and the real anonymized data. For baseline comparisons, we employ Isolation Forest (Liu et al., 2008), a model well-suited for tabular data and unsupervised anomaly detection, aligning effectively with the objectives of this study.

The development of explanation models often hinges on the identification of structural equations for the Functional Structural Causal Model (FSCM). Section 5.2 presents results that incorporate standard Partial Structural Causal Model (PSCM) intervals for counterfactual queries, highlighting the deficiencies that arise when the actual causal mechanisms remain ambiguous. For certain queries, the PSCM intervals are relatively narrow, enabling the use of interval averages or approximate queries—via a Causal Bayesian Network (CBN)—to produce reasonable approximations of the true value, as the precise model may exert limited influence. However, this does not universally apply. In general, counterfactual probabilities derived from rare events exhibit high sensitivity to the choice of model, with values ranging from 0 to 1 based on the FSCM employed. Different FSCMs compatible with the data may yield entirely divergent explanations for the same scenarios, potentially asserting certainty for opposing outcomes. Rare observations, particularly those in regions where training data is scarce, are critical to address rigorously for reliable predictions. Thus, an FSCM remains essential for answering all potential queries comprehensively. Expert-provided partial knowledge may be utilized to define non-standard PSCMs, which could reduce interval sizes and enable approximations for a broader range of queries. Future research could explore methodologies to enhance both exact and approximate computation techniques. The explanations presented in this study primarily focus on probabilities that concept-level interventions are sufficient; however, the framework allows for extensions to other forms of causal explanations, including probabilities of necessity and alternative explanatory paradigms.

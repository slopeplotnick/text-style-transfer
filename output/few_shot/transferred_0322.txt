We employed a multimodal language model for image processing tasks. Post-image generation, we extracted key details and articulated them in a structured manner. The open-source model, LLaMA 3.2 Vision, implemented via the Ollama framework, was selected due to its capability to process images and generate comprehensive, detailed descriptions in natural language. Noted for its proficiency in detailed descriptions, the model effectively converts visual content into structured information. While our primary aim was to extract structured triples, this methodology parallels image captioning. Research indicates that multimodal language models exhibit comparable performance in visual comprehension. Although all models assessed demonstrated reliability, we concentrated on a single model to exemplify our approach focused on information structuring and comparison. To ensure that the extracted triples were consistent and fashion-focused, we employed the Fashionpedia ontology. This ontology serves as an extensive framework delineating categories, components, and features of clothing and accessories. By integrating this guide into the model’s instructions, we ensured that the information adhered to a rigorous and specific structure. Additionally, we conducted a comparative experiment without the ontology to evaluate its impact. The findings, detailed in Table 1, illustrate that incorporating the ontology resulted in a higher number of triples per image and a broader representation of relationships. In its absence, the model predominantly identified only conspicuous details, resulting in less informative outputs. These results underscore the critical role of the ontology in generating detailed and consistent outputs, essential for comparative analysis in a graph-based framework.

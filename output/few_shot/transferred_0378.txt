Researchers have devised two principal methodologies to address this significant issue: Fact Verification (FV) and Hallucination Detection (HD). Fact Verification is an established domain concentrating on validating factual accuracy using sources such as web pages or databases, irrespective of whether the statement is authored by a human or generated by a machine; FV assesses its veracity based on the corroborating evidence available. In contrast, Hallucination Detection is a more recent development, emerging alongside the advent of large language models (LLMs). HD specifically examines outputs generated by these models. Unlike FV, which focuses solely on the isolated text, HD also considers the generative process of the text. While HD can utilize external data akin to FV, its primary emphasis is on intrinsic signals from the model, such as the model's confidence in individual words, activation patterns, and consistency across multiple iterations. Consequently, HD is model-centric and can identify fabricated content even in the absence of external validation. Although both methodologies aim to detect factual inaccuracies, they have evolved independently, employing distinct testing frameworks, evaluation metrics, and publication venues. This divergence arises from their fundamentally disparate evaluative approaches. FV is predominantly concerned with the textual content itself, utilizing benchmarks such as FEVER, where claims are evaluated as discrete textual entities.

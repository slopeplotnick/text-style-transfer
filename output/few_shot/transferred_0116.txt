We analyze Taobao search logs to categorize data into distinct industries, such as electronics and clothing. To ensure the training dataset is representative of all industries, data sampling is conducted proportionally to industry representation. Subsequently, the data is stratified into high, medium, and low frequency categories based on search frequency, enabling coverage of both prevalent and rare search queries. The stratified data is then subjected to expert labeling. During the data cleaning process, errors are primarily attributed to annotator oversight, especially in lengthy queries, or to ambiguous labeling guidelines that fail to address all scenarios comprehensively. To address these issues, advanced language models are employed for data filtering. One model rigorously matches attributes and exhibits superior capability in capturing detailed information in complex cases, while the other leverages comprehensive rule-based reasoning to identify and rectify edge cases that remain unaddressed by predefined rules. This dual-model approach ensures robust handling of both extended text inputs and various data types. The reasoning process involves a methodical three-stage framework: (1) integrating knowledge and reasoning capabilities, (2) understanding diverse data types, and (3) adhering to rule-based consistency. In the initial stage, user intents are decomposed into attributes that facilitate item comparison. The subsequent stages evaluate the relevance of items to search queries by aligning item attributes with user needs and predefined rules.

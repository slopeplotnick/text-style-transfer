Transitioning effectively in software development processes, particularly to Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE), involves adopting specific practices to mitigate challenges commonly associated with such transitions. Agile methodologies are instrumental in facilitating these transitions by reducing the associated drawbacks. For example, Sprint Zero is an approach aimed at enhancing team cohesion and alignment during the transition process. Sprint Zero typically consists of several steps: first, establishing a working agreement to define agile routines that support the team during the transition; second, creating a clear and shared vision to ensure that all team members understand the goals and benefits of SPLE. Additionally, personas, representing various users or developers within the organization, are developed to identify the stakeholders and their needs prior to project initiation. Subsequently, a backlog is constructed, detailing all tasks necessary for the project, with each task estimated and placed on a roadmap. Furthermore, regular retrospectives are scheduled for team members to reflect, share insights, and improve collaboration.  Several software engineering practices are implemented to enhance project quality, such as regular refactoring sessions to increase code reuse and eliminate technical debt. These practices pave the way for high-quality code. Regular testing sessions are planned to identify and address potential bugs, while acceptance testing sessions with end users ensure alignment with user expectations and requirements. Continuous monitoring of code execution is also critical for maintaining efficient and scalable applications. These strategies constitute general best practices applicable to any software development process, whether it is based on SPLE or not. Nevertheless, SPLE-based development offers additional advantages, as the improved quality inherent to the core code base benefits not only current applications but also future projects.

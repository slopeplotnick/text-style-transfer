Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) pertains to the identification of objects based on natural language descriptions. This task is pivotal in the interdisciplinary field of vision-language integration. Recent advancements have enhanced performance by not merely aligning words with objects but also comprehending semantic meaning and integrating external knowledge. Generalized REC (GREC) extends this by engaging with descriptions that may refer to zero or multiple entities, exemplified by directives such as "Pick up those three boxes." This mirrors the complexities of natural discourse, where referents may be ambiguous or absent. Research in this domain necessitates extensive datasets comprising authentic images with corresponding human-generated descriptions, which poses significant financial constraints. Online platforms mitigate some of these costs, facilitating more efficient and economical experimentation. Nonetheless, challenges arise when models must interpret scenes, maintain conversational context, and engage in dialogue. Under such conditions, traditional methodologies exhibit limitations. A notable study introduced a novel GREC benchmark utilizing dialogue within the context of a Minecraft game, highlighting the limitations of contemporary AI models. It also identified the expense associated with generating human-authored dialogues, resulting in a paucity of training data. This paper proposes an innovative approach to data generation, involving the creation of succinct descriptions and intricate dialogues to enhance AI comprehension. While prior work on AI-generated data is applicable, GREC poses unique challenges due to the necessity of spatial understanding and the correlation of linguistic input with visual context.

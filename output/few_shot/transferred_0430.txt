In this study, we utilize three distinct emotion datasets to train our model and assess its performance. The EMER dataset provides high-quality human-verified reasoning-label pairs, serving as the foundational dataset for our training process. DFEW and MAFW are extensive datasets encompassing a variety of facial expressions across video, audio, and text modalities. Further details regarding these datasets can be found in Appendix 7.1. Consistent with prior research, we report Unweighted Average Recall (UAR) and Weighted Average Recall (WAR) to evaluate recognition accuracy, along with three additional metrics—Emotion Explanation Accuracy (EEA), Emotion Prediction Consistency (EPC), and Feelings Consistency Ratio (FCR)—to assess the alignment between the explanations, emotional states, and model predictions. Precise definitions and our annotation methodology are provided in Appendix 7.2.   Our experimental setup adopts the R1-Omni architecture, employing SigLIP as the vision encoder, Whisper-large-v3 as the audio encoder, and BERT as the text encoder. Outputs from each data modality are projected into the LLM embedding space via visual and audio projectors, comprising two linear layers designed to match the LLM representation dimensions. For instance, a one-second audio clip yields approximately 50 audio features, while a single video frame results in 729 visual features. The LLM backbone used is Qwen2.5-7B, initialized with model weights from the HumanOmni checkpoint to preserve the pretrained multimodal representations.   To evaluate the efficacy of our setup, we compare MIGR with alternative methods for emotion understanding on the MAFW and DFEW datasets. We categorize prior methodologies into two groups: those that directly predict emotion labels and those that generate textual explanations prior to prediction.

During reinforcement learning (RL) training, as the model approaches its objective, its outputs become increasingly deterministic, thereby impeding the model's ability to explore novel ideas and discover superior solutions. This constraint limits the potential for model enhancement. To address this, we explored various strategies to decelerate the rate at which the model becomes predictable, thereby maintaining its exploratory capabilities. Figure 13 presents a comparative analysis of these strategies. The Clip-higher method emerges as the most effective, as it moderates the rate of predictability onset while permitting it to occur, achieving an optimal balance between exploration and exploitation, and ensuring stable training with noticeable improvements. Conversely, the On-policy method maintains the model’s stochasticity at a steady level, thereby almost halting its reduction. Although this facilitates exploration, it simultaneously complicates the model's convergence to an optimal solution, resulting in more modest improvements. The Off-policy + Entropy Loss method encounters difficulties with unstable training dynamics. Initially, it decelerates the predictability onset; however, as the model becomes less stochastic over time, the penalty associated with randomness intensifies, ultimately dominating the process and causing erratic model behavior, which leads to training failures. Furthermore, this method necessitates extensive experimentation to determine appropriate parameter settings, thereby increasing its computational cost. In summation, the Clip-higher method is identified as the superior option, as it effectively manages the trade-off between exploration and efficient learning by controlling the rate of predictability onset.

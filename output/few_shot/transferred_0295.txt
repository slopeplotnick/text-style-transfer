In video editing, ensuring temporal alignment with the original footage is crucial, particularly when modifications are requested. This requirement entails precise synchronization of movements, such as blinks or head turns, with the original video to maintain authenticity, especially in scenarios involving human dialogue where even minor discrepancies can lead to perceptual errors. Commonly requested alterations are typically minor, such as the addition of a mustache or slight changes in facial expressions. The principle is to alter only the necessary elements while preserving the rest of the content. Achieving this level of synchronization remains challenging for most video editing tools. In response to this issue, we propose a novel method, Sync-LoRA, designed to produce edited videos that remain temporally aligned with the original. Our approach involves modifying only the initial frame of the video and employing a text prompt to specify the desired changes. The model is trained using pairs of videos: one representing the original and the other the edited version. These pairs are meticulously curated to ensure that each frame is synchronized in terms of motion, with variations present only in the edited segments. To generate these pairs, we first create them and then verify their synchronization. We utilize a model proficient in interpreting both images and text to generate diverse modification concepts, subsequently producing corresponding images and transforming them into videos. However, these videos often fail to achieve perfect synchronization, necessitating verification against human motion data, including facial expressions and body movements. We assess alignment in aspects such as speech, eye movement, blinking, and body positioning, retaining only the most accurately matched examples for training. By employing these well-aligned instances, our model effectively learns to maintain the original video's motions while implementing the specified changes, ensuring synchronization with the source material.

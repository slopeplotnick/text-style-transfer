There is a significant global need for mental health support, yet diagnoses remain insufficiently widespread across populations. Key barriers include limited clinic availability, geographical inaccessibility, and workforce shortages, which hinder early assessment and identification of mental health conditions. Structural inefficiencies, alongside individual-level challenges, further exacerbate delays in the diagnosis of severe mental health conditions. Traditional diagnostic approaches fail to meet increasing demands due to constraints such as workforce shortages, high costs, regional disparities, and social stigma. For example, waiting times for mental health care can range from 3 to 18 months depending on geographic location and provider availability. Consequently, there is a critical need for innovative methods to detect early indicators of mental health concerns and provide timely interventions.   Portable technological solutions, such as compact language models, offer the potential for scalable mental health detection and intervention. Advances in device-based technologies, particularly those utilizing smartphones, enable the early identification of mental health concerns through the analysis of real-time social media content and app usage. By leveraging linguistic data from social media interactions, these systems can unobtrusively monitor mental states and identify changes in mood, stress, or suicide risk. Such applications not only facilitate early detection of mental health issues but can also identify suicide-related patterns in social media activity. Importantly, when these models are deployed directly on personal devices, they can preserve user privacy while analyzing linguistic markers of depression or stress. This approach allows for mental health monitoring beyond clinical settings, increasing accessibility and broadening the scope of mental health support.

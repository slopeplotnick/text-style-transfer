Individuals tend to exhibit heightened aggression and increased conflictual behavior in online environments, a phenomenon often attributed to perceived anonymity and the dynamics of group interactions, which can exacerbate disputes. Automated systems for detecting inappropriate conduct are prevalent; however, their usual response mechanisms—such as content removal or user sanctions—offer reactive rather than proactive solutions, failing to address underlying causes and allowing such behavior to reoccur. In this study, we propose an alternative strategy that emphasizes mediation over mere moderation. We investigate the potential of language models to comprehend and de-escalate conflicts, hypothesizing that these models can facilitate constructive dialogue by assessing conversational fairness and delivering calming, constructive responses.  This approach necessitates an understanding of the contextual triggers of disputes, emotional dynamics, escalation mechanisms, and typical conversational patterns. Our methodology is bifurcated into two main components: identifying unfair or distress-inducing elements in discourse, and generating responses that defuse tension and redirect focus towards resolving core issues. We constructed an extensive corpus of authentic online disputes sourced from Reddit to evaluate our models' efficacy.  Our evaluation framework encompasses three criteria: adherence to principles of constructive dialogue, behavioral outcomes post-intervention, and comparison of model-generated responses with human interventions. Empirical results indicate that commercial models outperform their open-access counterparts in both identifying and mitigating conflicts, demonstrated by sustained topic relevance. Additionally, these models effectively reduce inflammatory rhetoric and emotional intensity in simulated disputes. However, while they maintain neutrality and clarity, model outputs lack the readability and engagement typically associated with human interaction.

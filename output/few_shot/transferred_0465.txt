Trust in systems is crucial for users, and the occurrence of hallucinations can undermine this trust significantly. Therefore, it is imperative to assess user perceptions when they encounter such errors. A study investigates hallucinations in language models, where users identify and report these inaccuracies. This research introduces novel methodologies for assessing the impact of hallucinations on users, such as the erosion of trust and the necessity for users to verify information independently, a task ideally handled by the system itself. Furthermore, the study proposes a comprehensive framework for understanding and managing hallucinations through diverse methodologies aimed at improving existing frameworks. Another study evaluates the performance of language models using various techniques, including fact-checking, consistency analysis, and user experience assessment. This multifaceted approach allows for the detection of hallucinations from multiple perspectives, including the logical coherence of arguments and user satisfaction, beyond mere factual accuracy. In deploying large language models across various applications, it is essential to comprehend the task requirements while remaining cognizant of potential hallucinations. For general tasks such as chatbots, models like GPT-4 or PaLM are favored due to their human-like writing capabilities and minimal error risk, along with their creative enhancements. Conversely, for tasks demanding factual precision, such as information retrieval and question answering, models like BERT and T5 are preferred due to their emphasis on factual accuracy and robust comprehension (Smith et al., 2023; Johnson et al., 2024).

Initiating a character in a randomized state derived from a motion dataset has been demonstrated to enhance the exploration of motion imitation techniques. Conventionally, access to the original dataset is required to sample these initial states during training. To circumvent dependence on the original dataset, SMP employs a generative model for producing initial states apt for training purposes. Specifically, when utilizing SMP, the starting states are generated via a pre-trained diffusion model that serves as a motion guide. SMP functions dually as a reward mechanism and a method for initializing new training episodes. This generative approach eliminates the necessity of retaining the original dataset after SMP is trained, as the diffusion models effectively generate diverse and high-quality initial states.  In our framework, a pre-trained diffusion model is applied as a versatile motion guide for imitation learning. The motion model evaluates whether the agent's movements are natural within the training environment, facilitating the development of control policies capable of performing diverse tasks while maintaining fidelity to the motions observed in the original dataset. Crucial design choices in the training setup are elucidated in this work, emphasizing the significance of constructing an effective motion representation. Such representation is vital for training the diffusion model to comprehend the dataset and subsequently guide policy learning. The representation must encapsulate sufficient information to reconstruct motion accurately while being easily derivable from motion clips and simulator observations.

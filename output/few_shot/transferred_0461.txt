Future research initiatives are being planned to address limitations encountered due to insufficient resources, specifically concerning the entrenchment of beliefs within reinforced reasoning—a method increasingly prevalent in LLM reasoning. Our findings indicated that deviations from ideal Bayesian rationality can occur when individuals misinterpret either their reasoning process or external evidence; however, our focus was limited to the former aspect. Subsequent studies might incorporate examining the adaptability of LLMs in updating their beliefs upon receiving new external evidence, as opposed to merely adhering to pre-existing beliefs. A significant challenge in this endeavor is ensuring evidence gathering does not inadvertently expose LLMs to factual information if such facts are accessible. In domains with known truths, such as forecasting, we demonstrated how belief entrenchment impacts accuracy. Future investigations could explore the applicability of the Martingale Score in open-ended domains by examining other consequences of belief entrenchment. To validate the utility of the Martingale Score for reasoning evaluation, it could be employed to assess phenomena such as sycophancy, group conformity, and inverse scaling. Additionally, the Martingale Score might be adapted into a training objective to evaluate its robustness and utility. Should martingale training result in decreased Martingale and Brier scores, it would indicate that the Martingale Score is effective in evaluating reasoning capabilities and holds applications beyond forecasting. Further examination could also investigate the potential of the Martingale Score in measuring and mitigating belief entrenchment or polarization within human-AI systems, such as recommender systems, in future explorations.

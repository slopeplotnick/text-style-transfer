We are evaluating four distinct models—MOIRAI, CHRONOS, GTT, and LightGTS—to assess their response to variability in data handling and training methodologies. Each model is constructed upon a unique framework for prediction and learning. For the purpose of this study, we have utilized the smallest available configurations of these models to isolate the impact of data size variations and minimize confounding influences related to model capacity. Notably, each model's distinctive learning and prediction mechanisms influence its adaptability to data size fluctuations. For instance, CHRONOS employs a partitioning strategy that segments data into smaller, manageable units prior to the learning phase, thereby enabling it to remain unaffected by data magnitude fluctuations. To ensure uniformity in data quality, preprocessing includes standardization across data inputs to prevent skewed outcomes and subsequent normalization processes to achieve data balance. Model efficacy is assessed via the Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE) metric, which provides a size-agnostic evaluation of predictive accuracy. The primary objective of this investigation is to elucidate the relationship between data size variation and model performance, as demonstrated by comparative analysis of MASE deviations relative to a baseline methodology.

Fact Verification (FV) is significantly influenced by the effectiveness of the retrieval component and the availability of external information. As demonstrated in Table 3, inadequacies in the retrieval process, which may fail to provide the requisite evidence, pose substantial challenges. In cases where the retrieval fails—possibly due to mismatched keywords in the LLM-Q configuration—the verification component is constrained to make decisions without sufficient information. This deficiency in evidential support can lead to anomalous model behavior, such as unwarranted skepticism resulting in false alarms (e.g., Case 1) or unverified acceptance leading to missed errors (e.g., Case 2). Conversely, when the retrieval process includes the generated answer (LLM-QA), the system more consistently retrieves relevant documents, thereby enhancing the accuracy of the FV model's predictions.  Hallucination Detection (HD) methods often encounter difficulties due to the inherent versatility of language generation. Table 4 illustrates that HD methods perform effectively in scenarios with straightforward responses (Group A) but show diminished efficacy with more creative outputs (Group B). In such situations, a model might be confident about a fact (e.g., the formation date of a band) but struggle with its articulation. Current HD methodologies lack the capability to discern between legitimate variability in language and actual fabrications, leading to erroneous flagging of legitimate variations as incorrect statements.  Implications for Hybrid Design: Our investigation reveals distinct error margins for HD and FV, underscoring the necessity of their integration. FV functions as a precision anchor, delivering high reliability when it successfully identifies pertinent evidence, yet its performance deteriorates in the absence of such evidence. Conversely, HD serves as a safety net, capable of identifying potential inaccuracies without external information, although it can be confounded by linguistic variability. Consequently, an optimal hybrid framework should differentiate their roles, assigning greater trust to FV when evidence is present, and relying on HD indications when retrieval efforts are unsuccessful. This strategic equilibrium informed the design of our hybrid framework in RQ3.

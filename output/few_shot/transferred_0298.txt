To improve the modeling and analysis of long videos, several approaches based on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) have been proposed. These methods aim to extract and utilize meaningful segments of videos, such as specific frames or clips, based on textual and visual cues, in contrast to random frame selection. This enables models to focus on significant video segments, enhancing their understanding of the content. More advanced methodologies leverage graph-based architectures to model relationships among various segments within a video. However, existing models often exhibit an overreliance on textual data or simplistic text-visual comparisons, limiting their ability to deeply reason through and comprehend long-form video data.   To address these challenges, memory-based approaches have been explored to systematically organize and store video information. For instance, EgoRAG [Shen et al., 2025] structures text-based memories from videos to facilitate the understanding of daily activities, while Ego-R1 [Li et al., 2026] incorporates detailed visual tools to enable iterative reasoning over extended sequences. Similarly, HippoMM introduces a dual-memory framework comprising summaries and multimodal data integration, and M3-Agent employs an entity-centered memory system alongside step-by-step reasoning mechanisms to retrieve pertinent information from videos. Despite these advancements, current methods face significant limitations in effectively integrating textual and visual modalities and adapting to varying temporal scales, constraining their efficacy in handling complex scenarios.  To overcome these limitations, we propose *WorldMM*, a novel framework that combines text and visual data from videos to construct a robust memory system for improved reasoning and understanding of long videos. The *WorldMM* system operates in three key stages: (1) generating multiple memory representations, including two text-based and one visual memory, (2) employing a retrieval mechanism to iteratively extract information across different temporal spans until sufficient context is accumulated, and (3) synthesizing the retrieved information to produce accurate and contextually informed responses.

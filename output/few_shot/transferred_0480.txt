Software product lines (SPLs) represent a systematic approach to developing customizable software solutions. Analogous to a supply chain in manufacturing, SPLs are constituted by modular components and feature models, which delineate the potential feature combinations of a software product. These models also specify the interactions and dependencies among features. A repository of reusable software components underpins the SPL, supporting the implementation of each feature. Tools associated with SPLs facilitate feature selection to generate specific software configurations and automate the construction of product variants by assembling appropriate components from the repository. This approach proves particularly effective for managing the production of diverse software configurations at scale.   However, transitioning towards SPLs can be complex, especially for organizations initiating such a strategy for novel product domains. As observed in the literature, companies often begin by developing multiple similar software systems before consolidating their development process into a cohesive product line. Despite documented successes, the transformation of an existing suite of software systems into a unified product line platform remains a significant challenge. Organizations frequently demonstrate reluctance to adopt SPLs due to ambiguities surrounding the implementation process and uncertainty about the cost-benefit tradeoff. Consequently, even when companies possess substantial, well-documented codebases, many continue to employ ad hoc methods, such as the "clone-and-own" approach, to deliver tailored applications to their clients.  Our industrial partner, ITK - Predict and Decide, exemplifies this scenario. ITK is a small- to medium-sized enterprise (SME) employing 120 staff, of which approximately one-third are technology-focused, including 19 agronomists, 29 developers, 2 UI/UX designers, and 4 system administrators. The organization follows an agile software development methodology, which supports a robust development and delivery pipeline. ITK specializes in decision-support applications for the agricultural sector, enabling farmers to predict crop outcomes and plant or animal disease risks. The core of these applications comprises simulators developed by the agronomist team. These simulators are integrated with IT-implemented components that collect data from Internet of Things (IoT) devices or online services. The collected data feeds the simulators, while the processed outputs are presented to users via interactive dashboards. The backend architecture relies on Kotlin, whereas the frontend is developed using Angular with extensive use of Typescript.

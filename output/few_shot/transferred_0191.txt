Language models (LMs) have demonstrated potential in refining and suggesting modifications to ambiguously written questions, thereby mitigating errors and misunderstandings in responses. Furthermore, LMs can assist in categorizing questions appropriately, helping researchers avoid misclassification by leveraging semantic analysis. This enables researchers to explore relationships between categories and identify instances where categories might require substantial revisions due to excessive similarity. Additionally, LMs can ensure consistency in linguistic complexity, thereby enhancing overall readability and comprehension. For a comprehensive discussion on the use of LMs in test development, refer to the recent work by Beghetto et al. (2023).   An emerging area of interest is the use of LMs in generating synthetic data to facilitate test construction. This approach has the potential to significantly reduce the time and financial resources conventionally allocated to testing by enabling preliminary experimentation on simulated populations. Consequently, this innovation could democratize test development for a larger pool of researchers and streamline the production process by obviating unnecessary or redundant testing. In this study, we evaluated the capacity of state-of-the-art LMs to generate data congruent with the structural frameworks underpinning test construction. Our methodology was grounded in the principles of Classical Test Theory (CTT), adhering to guidelines proposed by Boateng et al. (2018). The subsequent sections delineate our research design and provide the rationale underlying our approach.

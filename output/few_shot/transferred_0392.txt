Using artificial intelligence (AI) on mobile devices has emerged as an effective approach for monitoring mental health, as it enables users to receive personalized feedback promptly without compromising their privacy. Specifically, on-device AI systems operate directly on smartphones, eliminating the need to transmit data to cloud servers, thereby enhancing data security and reducing reliance on internet connectivity. Furthermore, these tools offer accessible mental health support, particularly for individuals in remote regions with limited access to clinics or those facing long appointment wait times. On-device AI can also provide rapid feedback by detecting subtle changes in speech patterns or behavior that may indicate stress or mood disturbances.   The adoption of mobile devices and associated technologies for mental health monitoring is growing due to their speed, energy efficiency, and enhanced privacy compared with cloud-based systems. Recent studies indicate that small language models (SLMs) are capable of efficiently running on devices such as the Samsung Galaxy S24, performing tasks ranging from document generation to health predictions. These models consume less memory and operate at faster speeds, making them more practical for real-world applications. Moreover, their ability to function offline and independently of continuous cloud connectivity contributes to better safeguarding user privacy. However, while SLMs have demonstrated potential in certain mental health-related tasks, there remain significant gaps in their capabilities. Existing research has predominantly focused on straightforward tasks and has shown limited efficacy with more complex datasets. Additionally, studies investigating the performance of SLMs on resource-constrained devices remain scarce.  To address these challenges, this work introduces **Menta**, a novel framework leveraging Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) techniques to manage diverse tasks and accommodate imbalanced datasets. The framework is designed to perform efficiently across a wide range of mental health applications while prioritizing resource efficiency and maintaining user privacy on commonly used mobile devices.

The impact of selected training images on outcomes was examined by assessing the similarity of generated images before and after retraining. However, direct comparison of these images does not fully elucidate the reasons some images exert greater influence. Therefore, we employed a graph-based analytical method to investigate the structure and commonalities of the training images. The primary aim was to determine whether the selected images formed distinct, meaningful clusters. Table 3 presents statistical data from ten experiments (each comprising 15 prompts) that evaluated the consistency and grouping of the images. These findings validated the efficacy of our image selection methodology and enhanced our understanding of the images' significance in the process. For instance, the average number of distinct values per relationship ranged from 3.9 to 5.1, indicating a moderate level of diversity, which is appropriate for domains such as fashion with limited styles or patterns. The images exhibited sufficient similarity to form clear clusters while maintaining enough distinctiveness to avoid redundancy. This suggests that the influence is derived from shared conceptual themes rather than mere replication. We also examined the number of shared attributes among the images. On average, the images shared between 5 and 7.1 attributes, with some groups sharing up to 13 attributes, indicating that the images were not only detailed but also aligned with one another in significant ways. Prompts with more specific details resulted in a higher number of shared attributes, whereas more general or creative prompts led to greater diversity. This demonstrates the system's capability to adapt to various prompt types while effectively identifying well-matched examples.

To enhance comprehensibility and ensure safety, feedback mechanisms are integrated into each instance in our dataset using advanced models. We employ the gpt-oss-120b for textual data, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct for visual data, including images and videos, and Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct for auditory data. For each data instance, the model processes the original content, its corresponding safety label, and identified areas of concern, subsequently providing an explanation for its safety determination. The comprehensive setup for generating this feedback is illustrated in Figure 5. Unlike basic models that merely classify content as safe or unsafe, OMNIGUARD provides meticulous and articulate safety assessments. It offers a structured response comprising the following components: (1) Safety judgment, which determines the safety status of the input; (2) Violation categories, which enumerate specific safety issues if the content is deemed unsafe; (3) Reasoning critique, which elucidates the rationale behind the model's decision based on established guidelines. This approach enables OMNIGUARD to transcend mere pattern recognition, facilitating a precise evaluation of potentially unsafe content across various formats. We present comprehensive test outcomes for OMNIGUARD, evaluating its performance in both singular and composite scenarios across 15 tests that encompass text, images, videos, and audio. Furthermore, we design experiments to address the following inquiries: (1) Does the incorporation of reasoning enhance the model's proficiency in safety assessments and its ability to handle complex safety challenges across diverse formats? (2) Can safety knowledge acquired from familiar formats be extrapolated to novel ones, demonstrating its capability to comprehend and manage safety across different modalities?

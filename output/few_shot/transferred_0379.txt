Navigating recommendation challenges in marketplaces characterized by extensive textual data and dynamic interactions poses significant complexity due to the continuous evolution of content and connections. GraphMatch emerges as an innovative system integrating pre-trained language models with graph neural networks to address these challenges. Unlike traditional methods employing separate models, GraphMatch synergizes text encoders and graph networks. It employs techniques such as adversarial negative sampling and training on specific graph snapshots to capture both nuanced textual semantics and the evolving graph structure over time. Empirical evaluations conducted using data from Upwork, a prominent job marketplace, demonstrate the system's capability for real-time application. GraphMatch outperformed systems relying solely on language or graph models for matching tasks, proving both its efficacy and operational efficiency. This underscores the effectiveness of integrating linguistic and graph data for recommendations in environments abundant with text and shifting connections. Systems typified by e-commerce platforms, job marketplaces, academic networks, and news feeds can be conceptualized as Temporal Text-Attributed Graphs (TTAGs). In TTAGs, nodes are associated with detailed, temporally variable textual descriptions, and edges denote interactions such as purchases or replies. Effective matching or recommendation necessitates a synthesis of both the intricate text semantics and the overarching graph structure, despite this remaining a formidable challenge. Emerging language models such as SentenceBERT and E5 excel at converting documents into embeddings for semantic comparison but fall short in TTAG contexts. Firstly, the linkage of items often hinges on their surrounding graph connections, a factor text models do not consider. Secondly, within TTAGs, the semantics of a node and the rationale for linking can shift rapidly. For instance, the advent of generative AI has transformed "AI Writing" job postings from an emphasis on editing to a focus on prompt engineering. Additionally, global events continually influence the matching of freelancers and clients in the international labor market. Treating text as static features may lead to issues such as the inadvertent leakage of future information.

We evaluate the performance of ADORE by analyzing its effectiveness in both offline and online settings. Offline, the model is assessed as a binary classification task, utilizing metrics such as precision, recall, and F1 score. Online, ADORE is tested within the context of search advertisements, where we analyze metrics including click-through rates, revenue generation, and expert evaluations of ad quality. ADORE's performance is compared against other robust models, including BERT, LLAMA-3-8B, and their respective variants. For BERT models, we examine two architectures: a bi-encoder and a cross-encoder. Within the cross-encoder category, we evaluate a smaller version, the standard model, and an enhanced variant with additional information. For the LLM model, performance is gauged based on decision-making using the final token. ADORE, akin to BERT-Mini but incorporating online data, synthetically generated data, and supplementary information, is presented. The results, detailed in Table 2, demonstrate that ADORE surpasses all other models on a substantial real-world dataset. Key observations include: (1) The cross-encoder BERT outperforms the bi-encoder version, with larger model sizes yielding superior results. The incorporation of additional information enhances BERT's performance, underscoring the efficacy of explicit information. Decision-making using LLM is less effective than BERT. (2) Leveraging the rule-aware LLM in the RD component to generate challenging examples significantly enhances performance. The integration of synthetic data from the DS component improves the F1 score, and the inclusion of explicit information in the KD component facilitates enhanced reasoning and overall model performance.

The proposed framework, termed Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), constitutes a reusable tool for facilitating physics-based character animation. SMP leverages score-matching techniques, enabling the derivation of control policies across various tasks after training, with the aim of producing realistic and data-consistent movements without the necessity of retaining the original motion data during usage. Additionally, techniques such as classifier-free guidance are employed to modify the motion prior, allowing for the generation of diverse movement styles tailored to specific task requirements. The efficacy of SMP was evaluated across multiple scenarios, encompassing single-character movements and interactions involving both humans and objects. SMPs can be constructed using datasets of varying sizes, ranging from brief motion clips to extensive datasets containing up to 20 hours of motion data. Empirical findings demonstrate that reinforcement learning combined with score distillation sampling (SDS) can generate motion trajectories comparable in quality to those achieved via adversarial imitation learning, without requiring repetitive updates to the motion prior during policy training. Furthermore, SMP exhibits superior performance in numerous cases due to its reliance on a more stable reward framework compared to adversarial approaches. Nevertheless, as with other methods targeting specific modes of behavior, policies trained using SMP may occasionally exhibit limitations by being constrained to a subset of behaviors derived from the original dataset. Despite the study's primary focus on humanoid motion control, the potential applicability of SMP extends to other motion control domains. This framework offers promising directions for the development of generalized motion tools conducive to creating versatile controllers across animation and robotics, beyond mere motion tracking applications.

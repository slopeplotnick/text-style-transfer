In contrast to image-based IC-LoRA methods, which necessitate separate training for each editing task, our unified Sync-LoRA module is applicable to a variety of edits, achieving a balance between high image quality and accurate motion preservation, as demonstrated in our empirical evaluations (refer to Section 4). In-context learning (ICL) within diffusion models enables these models to undertake novel tasks through the utilization of structured input examples as opposed to direct instructions. While extensively examined within the realm of language models, recent investigations have explored the applicability of ICL to image generation via diffusion models. In this context, the model generates a coherent set of images, which is advantageous for applications such as storyboard creation, where image consistency is critical. By employing a real image as guidance, ICL methodologies are capable of handling numerous image-to-image tasks, including image personalization and editing. The work most akin to ours is IC-LoRA, which identified that transformer-based diffusion models can inherently produce panels with consistent features across images. This was enhanced by fine-tuning the model with a low-rank adapter (LoRA) on coherent panels and utilizing specific prompts, resulting in applications like storyboard creation, font design, and home decoration. Our research extends this concept to video editing, developing an IC-LoRA approach tailored for video synchronization, concentrating on frame-by-frame portrait alignment. Additionally, recent studies have demonstrated that large video diffusion transformers can inherently learn in-context behavior, facilitating the creation of related video sequences in a singular process. This body of work examined the underlying mechanisms of this capability, employing inpainting for scene control without temporal alignment assurance. Expanding on this framework, PoseGen introduced in-context LoRA fine-tuning for generating human videos with controllable poses, enabling the production of extended, continuous motions from a reference image. Our approach advances this by specifically training the in-context behavior to emphasize synchronized editing.

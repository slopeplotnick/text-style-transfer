When utilizing language models for evaluation, it is advantageous for the model to articulate its reasoning process prior to delivering a final response. This approach, termed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, enhances the model's ability to elucidate its decision-making process, thereby improving the accuracy and interpretability of its judgments (Wei et al., 2023). The present research employs these cognitive steps and investigates optimal strategies for synthesizing the outcomes. Decision-making processes leverage computational resources available at a given time; when multiple samples are present, a method of integration is required. Self-Consistency, a notable method, employs majority voting to amalgamate diverse outputs [Zhou et al., 2023]. Variants such as Soft Self-consistency utilize confidence levels, selecting based on scores within various categories (Huang et al., 2023). Confidence-Informed Self-Consistency involves weighted voting predicated on confidence scores, determined either by the probability of the sequence or through querying the model itself (Lee et al., 2023). Some approaches deploy the model itself for result integration. Generative Self-Aggregation solicits the model to generate a novel response from multiple samples [Wang et al., 2023]. Universal Self-Consistency allows the model to select the most coherent answer from several alternatives (Chen et al., 2023). Furthermore, studies indicate that harnessing computational capabilities during evaluation, particularly majority voting, enhances the performance of reasoning-based generative verifiers (Zhang et al., 2023).

Our evaluation of Large Language Models (LLMs) utilizes three distinct assessments within diverse domains: MMLU-Pro, which focuses on knowledge and reasoning across 14 academic subjects; MATH500, comprising 500 challenging mathematical problems; and UniMoral, a compilation of queries regarding moral judgment that necessitates nuanced social reasoning. The evaluations were conducted in five languages: English, Chinese, Spanish, French, and Hindi. These languages were selected due to their representation of different language families, geographic regions, and the availability of resources. A critical consideration in multilingual tasks is the choice of language for the system prompt: should it be English, typically the primary training language, or should it align with the language of the task? This issue is examined through the comparison of two configurations. In the English-prompt configuration, English prompts are employed for questions in various languages, whereas in the same-language configuration, the prompt is translated to correspond with the language of the question. To elucidate the interconnections of various model behavior components, we investigate the relationships among four performance metrics. A regression analysis is conducted to explore how different elements of the system prompt influence model behavior by associating prompt components and language settings with multilingual performance metrics. Figure 4 categorizes the effects of system prompt components on model performance into three types. Primarily, certain components universally enhance model behavior. For example, Chain-of-Thought, emotion, and scenario are associated with both accuracy and consistency, and they mitigate accuracy disparities across languages, indicating their efficacy in directing the model to consistently perform well across languages. A detailed examination of the model outputs reveals that these components tend to produce more structured and coherent reasoning, likely contributing to the observed performance enhancements.

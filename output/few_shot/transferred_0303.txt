Lang2Logic dissects paragraphs into individual sentences, converting each into a logical statement and reformulating it into a specified structure. This form is then streamlined for subsequent utilization. Illustrated diagrammatically, a paragraph is initially positioned at the top. Lang2Logic dissects this paragraph into four segments, assigning each a variable in the designated format, followed by simplification. The final output is depicted at the diagram's base.  The operational mechanism of Lang2Logic involves commencing with textual input, which is transformed into a simplified logical expression. Initially, grammar, transformer, and language processing modules are configured, with the function call syntax defined. The LogicTransformer is utilized to transmute words into symbols and logical functions such as And, Or, Not, among others. LangToLogic employs a specified model to modify the sequences, yielding a simplified output for each line in the model's response.  An initial system misinterpretation of "implies" resulted in excessive utilization, rectified by the corrective system. For instance, given the statement, "Dragon fruit grows in the forest. Peanuts grow on the ground, but if you move them to the forest, they won't survive, which means the forest isn't good for peanuts," the system deconstructs it. It interprets "Dragon fruit grows in the forest" as a logical expression. Similarly, the statement concerning peanuts is converted into another logical expression, which is subsequently simplified to derive the final outcome.

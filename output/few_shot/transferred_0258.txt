The conventional methodology involves initially performing a deterministic inversion, followed by its application to derive a deterministic interpretation, as illustrated in Figure 2. Subsequently, a knowledgeable geologist examines the deterministic inversion to discern relevant geological features, such as intrusions crucial for exploration. This interpretation process utilizes software that is centered on a singular deterministic model. In this approach, geophysical inversion precedes geological interpretation. While some argue that regularization terms might integrate geological knowledge, their impact remains ambiguous and limited, potentially resulting in the redundant use of geological knowledge—first in the regularization term and again in the interpretation phase—leading to an iterative cycle that may not be entirely productive. In contrast, Bayesian methods treat inversion and interpretation as a unified problem, compelling consideration of possible solutions (geometries) prior to inversion. Bayesian inversion yields relatively transparent solutions that are both straightforward to code and generally applicable. Reexamining Figure 2, we consider the formulation of a prior probability model. This begins with addressing the minimal knowledge of the subsurface prior to data acquisition. It is established that petrophysical properties exhibit specific ranges, and with knowledge of rock types, these ranges can be further specified. Additionally, these properties do not exhibit random variation nor uniformity over extensive areas; rather, the processes of deposit formation induce specific variations in these properties. Fundamentally, the minimal information comprises constraints on histograms and spatial correlation, which mathematically can be expressed as a spatial covariance model (in geostatistics, a variogram). However, uncertainty prevails regarding parameters such as the mean, ranges, and anisotropy, as they may assume a wide range of values, albeit non-random. This understanding leads to a stratified conceptualization approach.

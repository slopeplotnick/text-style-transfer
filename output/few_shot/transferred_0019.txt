The proposed approach employs a layered model to integrate diverse statistical distributions, targeting distinct phases of the statistical process in isolation. This methodology facilitates the identification of independence between certain variables. For instance, while distinct CPUs with identical specifications and divergent instruction sets exhibit varying energy consumption patterns, these discrepancies are demonstrated to be independent of one another. The framework establishes a Worst-Case Energy Consumption (WCEC) model aimed at estimating a device's aggregate energy consumption, as depicted in Figure 1. This framework comprises two primary components: (i) a measurement phase, which quantifies the energy utilized by hardware benchmarks, and (ii) a modeling phase, which develops a Bayesian model to characterize the energy consumption associated with programming languages.   The analysis focuses on statically typed programming languages compatible with the JVM. Energy profiling for these languages is conducted through an examination of bytecode patterns, which represent the compiled output of source code processed by the compiler (Figure 2). Microbenchmarks provide isolated measurements of individual source code operations across all potential data types and sizes for a given operation (Figure 3). Each microbenchmark is assessed within a cycle that records current, voltage, and execution time. Prior to measurement commencement, the multimeter is configured for capturing energy metrics, and memory noise is mitigated by initiating garbage collection. To account for potential variabilities, each microbenchmark cycle is executed 10 times, employing a split-plot design to randomize the sequence of cycles, thus minimizing order effects and reducing bias.

Large language models often face challenges in interpreting highly detailed images. A recent study addressed this issue by segmenting the image into smaller sections and evaluating the similarity of each segment to a given query using a specialized model. The method prioritizes the most relevant segments to identify the primary object while disregarding irrelevant ones. However, this segmentation process can disrupt the alignment between the segments and the query. Empirical tests have demonstrated that segments of varying sizes perform optimally at different levels of detail. Consequently, we developed a novel approach to interpret detailed images without necessitating additional training. To address the fragmentation issue, our method integrates similarity maps across different detail levels to enhance information retention and preserve object integrity. Furthermore, to detect objects holistically, we employ a model that identifies object regions using a sliding window approach. Experimental results indicate that our technique is effective in comprehending intricate images. Large language models have significantly advanced in integrating and understanding both visual and textual information, thereby facilitating multimodal tasks. They exploit visual cues to interpret complex imagery, bridging raw data with semantic meaning. However, most models operate at a fixed resolution for images, which simplifies processing but presents substantial limitations. Reducing image resolution can distort shapes and obscure details, consequently degrading the quality of visual information. Studies highlight that existing methodologies are suboptimal for tasks involving detailed images, as evidenced by their mediocre performance on benchmarks designed for fine-grained image understanding.

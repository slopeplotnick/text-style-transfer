Concerns regarding energy consumption, such as that associated with mobile devices and large-scale data centers, are growing. For developers, it is increasingly critical to quantify the energy usage of their software to identify and address inefficiencies. Traditionally, analyses of energy consumption have been coarse-grained, focusing on broader components such as entire programs or specific functions, while often relying on estimations of CPU energy usage without accounting for other hardware components. This limited perspective has impeded comprehensive understanding and accurate explanations of energy consumption.   To address these limitations, we propose a novel methodology that extends beyond CPU-specific estimations. Our approach is centered on modeling energy consumption in programming languages like Java, Kotlin, and Scala that operate on the Java Virtual Machine (JVM). Specifically, we analyze how source code is translated into Java bytecode and measure the corresponding energy usage. Using Bayesian statistical modeling, we build an energy prediction framework that identifies influential factors affecting energy consumption. The model incorporates three program-related features—data size, data type, and operation type—as well as one hardware-related feature: the device used to execute the program.  To evaluate the efficacy of our approach, we conducted experiments using Java, analyzing its bytecode and validating our predictions on previously unseen software programs. Results indicate that all four factors—data size, data type, operation type, and device—significantly impact energy consumption. Notably, we observed substantial variability in energy consumption between devices of the same model, as well as considerable influence from operations and data types. The predictions generated by our model closely align with actual energy usage, demonstrating its accuracy.   Our work offers a robust methodology for modeling and predicting energy consumption in software, which has potential applications for assessing the energy efficiency of programs. This framework can serve as a foundation for future research and practical tools aimed at promoting energy-aware software development.

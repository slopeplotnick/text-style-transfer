Our objective is to enhance code reusability and accelerate the delivery of new applications, aiming to migrate our software development processes to a higher-level Software Product Line (SPL), such as Level 4 or 5. To facilitate this transition, we initiated consultations with personnel across the organization, seeking their perspectives on SPL engineering practices. These consultations are structured to address specific research questions (RQs), which will be elaborated on subsequently. A second round of interviews will be conducted at the conclusion of the project to refine the migration strategy further.   As part of the migration process, the initial focus will be on identifying existing features to develop feature models. Subsequently, we will investigate feature interactions and dependencies to form a comprehensive understanding of the system architecture. The preliminary interviews were designed to capture the perceptions of employees regarding current practices, as well as the anticipated benefits, risks, and concerns associated with transitioning to an SPL.  The research questions guiding this study are as follows:   - RQ1: What are employees’ perceptions of the existing development practices deemed effective? This question aims to understand which practices are considered successful in the current development context.   - RQ2: What are employees’ views on the potential benefits of adopting the new SPL? This inquiry focuses on identifying the perceived value that the SPL may bring to the application development process.   - RQ3: What are employees’ concerns regarding the risks associated with the transition to the new SPL? Following the discussion of benefits, this question aims to examine the risks that may affect daily operations.   - RQ4: What are employees’ apprehensions stemming from these identified risks? This question explores the specific fears and challenges employees anticipate, providing insights into potential obstacles in the migration process and the need for appropriate risk management strategies.    These findings will serve as a foundation for designing an effective migration framework that both addresses stakeholder concerns and ensures a smooth transition to the new SPL.

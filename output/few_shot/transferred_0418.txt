Galhotra et al. (2021) investigate probabilistic inference in scenarios where complete system details are unavailable. They explore the utilization of specific models to derive intervals for counterfactual queries, allowing for targeted analysis of model components in the absence of full information, potentially leading to narrower intervals than conventional computations might produce. The authors further examine the evaluation of AI explanation methods by assessing the necessity and sufficiency of certain factors. Mothilal et al. (2021) advocate for identifying the minimal set of crucial features that are both necessary and sufficient to explain a model's output within a given context. Their framework presumes that explanations focus solely on input features, with these features being independent of one another. They conduct a comparative analysis of various AI explanation methodologies, associating the notion of necessity with explanations that pinpoint essential alterations required to achieve the initial outcome, while sufficiency pertains to features critical for producing the outcome regardless of other variables. Their findings reveal considerable practical variability among different methods. Pawar et al. (2024), similarly presupposing feature independence, discuss necessity and sufficiency as criteria for evaluating AI methodologies and explore diverse strategies for generating explanatory instances. Beckers (2022) examines scenarios where models predict outcomes based on actions under the assumption of infallible predictive accuracy. He delineates explanations deemed sufficient and those exploring alternate hypothetical outcomes, also considering the model's perfect predictive assumption, and deconstructs explanations by assessing causal influence to identify genuine explanatory factors.

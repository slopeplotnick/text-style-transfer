Machine learning techniques, specifically neural networks and generative artificial intelligence, are employed to make predictions. For planning under uncertain conditions, Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) algorithms prove beneficial. AI encompasses two primary components: prediction and decision-making. This paper elucidates the application of these components in the context of exploration. The creation and organization of datasets pose the most significant challenge in mineral exploration, rather than the choice of machine learning methods. These datasets often present considerable complexity, characterized by numerous variables, three-dimensional structures, or extreme value ranges. Such problems are infrequent in other fields. Two major, often overlooked, data organization issues include the lack of uniform scale and the inappropriate amalgamation of data into a single dataset. Strategies effective in one domain may not be applicable in another. For instance, ground truth data, such as core samples, are frequently scaled to align with geophysical data. Occasionally, geophysical and geochemical datasets are combined in ways that lack geological rationale. Machine learning models from one domain are frequently applied to another without adequate validation of their applicability. These missteps result in increased false positives and undermine the credibility of AI; however, the fundamental problem lies in data management practices. A longstanding issue in mining is the variance discrepancy across differently scaled data. For instance, interpolated geophysical data inherently exhibit reduced variance compared to raw measurements due to the interpolation process. Additionally, small-scale drilling data is often scaled or adjusted to align with geophysical data, leading to artificial correlations between labels and predictors due to data handling practices. Consequently, current mineral potential or prospecting maps frequently contain numerous false positives.

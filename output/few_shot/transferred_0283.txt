The survey incorporated attention checks by including questions such as "Who made the music you just heard?" prior to participants rating the music. Subsequently, participants were asked to evaluate the honesty and care of their responses on a 5-point scale and to indicate whether they listened with headphones or speakers. Participants were informed that their responses would not influence their feedback or compensation. The study design is illustrated in Figure 1. Participants were required to assess their familiarity with the music and their preference for it on a scale from 1 (not at all) to 5 (very). Those who selected 4 or 5 for familiarity were prompted with an additional question regarding whether the music evoked a specific person or experience. Participants in the "AI or human" group were also asked to discern if the music was composed by AI or a human and to express their confidence on a 6-point scale ranging from 1 (definitely AI) to 6 (definitely human). Emotional reactions to the music were measured using the Geneva Emotion Music Scale (GEMS), which categorizes emotions into three primary categories: Sublimity (including Wonder, Tenderness, Nostalgia), Vitality (including Joy, Power), and Unease (including Sadness, Tension). A shorter version of GEMS was employed, featuring one label per emotion accompanied by several related terms. Participants rated each emotion on a scale from 0 to 100, with the default being 0 if not selected. Additionally, participants could list up to three other emotions not encompassed by GEMS and rate the intensity of these feelings.

Node 6 is responsible for managing task retries with integrated feedback, allowing up to three attempts per task. It employs enhanced prompting techniques that combine original task information with specific feedback to improve reasoning during retries. The system incorporates advanced methodologies to adapt strategies based on the project phase, transforming model weights into reasoning-based instructions. Prompt designs evolve across project stages to prioritize phase-specific objectives. During the initial stages, the focus is on maximizing success rates with directives such as "select robots with the highest success rates" and "minor workload imbalances are acceptable." In later phases, the emphasis transitions to achieving workload balance with prompts such as "prioritize workload fairness as the primary objective." This dynamic framework enables the system to naturally adhere to phase-specific strategies.  The authors operationalize this approach using three key methods: (1) modeling robot capabilities to estimate the probability of task success, (2) designing prompts that incorporate numerical and workload-related information, and (3) formatting responses for improved verifiability and validation. The system classifies robots into three categories—Light, Medium, and Heavy—each with distinct strengths and task-specific success probabilities. These probabilities are derived from the alignment between a robot's abilities and the task's requirements. A robot's likelihood of successfully completing a task is computed by evaluating the degree of compatibility between the task features and the robot's capabilities.  Tasks are sequentially processed from a set of 36 tasks within the TEACH dataset. Each task undergoes a comprehensive workflow, including phase identification, prompt generation, reasoning, validation, and task assignment. Upon completion of a task, the system updates project progress and reduces the remaining task count, continuing until all tasks within the dataset are successfully assigned and completed.

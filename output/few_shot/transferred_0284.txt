The RAIR benchmark consists of 63,601 samples derived from 14 distinct e-commerce domains. The dataset is organized into three subsets: the General subset, comprising 46,775 samples; the Long-Tail Hard subset, containing 10,931 samples; and the Visual Salience subset, which includes 5,895 samples. Details regarding the five challenging query types included in the Long-Tail Hard subset are presented in Table 7. For each dataset entry, the provided information includes the query, item title, item details, SKU information, and an anonymized item image. Additionally, each entry is annotated with a manual ground truth label determined by a four-level relevance scale (L1 to L4). The distribution of these labels across the dataset is summarized in Table 8. To facilitate model development and error analysis, each data entry is accompanied by rule identifiers derived from a Rule Checklist, which serve as relevance assessment guidelines.   This section evaluates and analyzes the proposed methods using the RAIR benchmark. The experiments involve two primary components: (1) a quantitative evaluation addressing two key questions: (a) the extent to which model performance is improved through fine-tuning, and (b) the effectiveness of the LORE model across different subsets of data, such as general, long-tail, and visually salient samples; and (2) a qualitative analysis, utilizing examples to illustrate the novel capabilities introduced by the proposed methods and the specific challenges these approaches help address. To ensure the stability and reproducibility of evaluation results, all trained models employ a deterministic decoding strategy for single-pass inference, avoiding any stochastic elements.

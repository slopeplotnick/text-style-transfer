In this study, we employ both auditory and visual inputs to determine which modality more prominently conveys emotions within each sample. Initially, we curate a robust dataset through human annotation, complemented by a systematically filtered dataset, subsequently merging these for comprehensive training purposes. We then compute the mutual information (MI) for each sample to ascertain the primary emotional conduit. Based on this analysis, we adjust the sequence of reasoning text. For each training instance, the reasoning is bifurcated into two segments: one pertaining to audio and the other to video. Distinct markers, <aud_desc> and <vis_desc>, are utilized to delineate these sections. The arrangement of these texts is then guided by the MI findings—if auditory input predominantly reflects emotions, the audio section precedes the video, and vice versa if the visual input is more expressive. In instances where both modalities are equally effective, we generate two versions: one beginning with audio and another with video. This methodology facilitates the model's capacity to initiate reasoning with the most emotionally indicative input. Following this initial phase, we enhance the model's reasoning utilizing Modality-Aligned Reward Optimization. Prior methodologies primarily employed dual reward types: accuracy of response and structural integrity. However, these frameworks fall short in emphasizing the emotionally indicative input. To address this, we introduce two novel reward criteria: one emphasizing the correct sequencing and the other enhancing reasoning quality contingent upon the emotionally indicative input.

This paper addresses the issue of safety and proposes a novel perspective that emphasizes systemic interactions over individual model analysis. Safety, traditionally associated with isolated models, is posited as an emergent property resulting from the interactions within complex systems. The paper introduces the concept of the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH) to elucidate the conditions under which systems may become unstable. This framework is detailed through a progression: beginning with dyadic interactions, advancing to multi-agent collaborations, identifying various risk types, and culminating in the proposal of Institutional AI as a design paradigm to mitigate these risks.  Currently, language models are engineered for safety by prioritizing human interactions, utilizing mechanisms such as prompts and filters to obstruct harmful content or ensure compliance with established guidelines. This approach presumes that model behavior is transparent and exhibits limited variability. However, language models exhibit complexity, influenced by factors such as internal states, historical interactions, and contextual applications. Minor contextual alterations can lead to disparate outcomes, despite apparent adherence to rules.  The complexity amplifies when models are integrated into broader systems. In such configurations, the output of one model serves as the input for another, potentially creating feedback loops that exacerbate minor issues. Empirical evidence, such as the JAILJUDGE experiments, demonstrates that interactions among multiple models can yield results that diverge significantly from those observed in isolated model testing. Additional studies, including those conducted in simulated markets, highlight that despite individual models being programmed for fairness, collective dynamics can yield inequitable outcomes. This underscores the necessity of a paradigm shift from focusing solely on individual prompts or actions to a comprehensive examination of system-wide communication and interaction dynamics.

The language learning experiments focus on integrating explicit grammatical knowledge into a word list, enabling the daughter agent to engage in communication with the mother agent. The system employs machine learning techniques to develop a clear grammatical representation, thus augmenting existing systems that utilize statistical methods and neural networks by offering a more comprehensible, rule-based approach to language modeling. This study investigates the efficacy of a multi-agent simulation of first language acquisition using Zipf's law. The objective extends beyond merely classifying function and content words; it seeks to employ this classification to evaluate this novel approach to first language learning modeling. Zipf's law is advantageous due to its robust and reliable evaluation of the system's performance, as commonly practiced in the field. For instance, Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) leverage Zipf's law to develop a multi-agent framework for modeling language evolution, complemented by recent studies from Dębowski (2021), Takahashi and Tanaka-Ishii (2019), and Takamichi et al. (2024). The experiments conducted in this study validate all components essential for constructing a multi-agent model of first language acquisition. Specifically, the acquisition of function and content words employs a mixed language learning strategy informed by statistical trends that facilitate the learning of specific grammatical rules. These findings underpin the modeling of more complex grammatical structures and demonstrate the feasibility of the MODOMA research framework. A study inspired by these findings is Shakouri et al. (2025), which illustrates how grammatical categories such as nouns, verbs, and prepositions can be acquired using unsupervised learning techniques. This paper presents two experimental scenarios demonstrating this methodology: the initial simulation detailed in Sections 6 and 7 utilized a training dataset generated by the mother agent to determine the parameters for learning this linguistic feature. The subsequent experiment, employing a novel test dataset generated by the adult language model, was conducted to validate this configuration, as discussed in Section 8. The results indicate that MODOMA is capable of acquiring these grammatical categories and articulating them through the grammatical system of the daughter agent.

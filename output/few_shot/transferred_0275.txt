We compare statistical measurements derived from our generated dataset with those from the EMOPIA dataset, employing t-tests to determine whether the generated data preserves the significant differences in high and low valence and arousal inherent in the EMOPIA data. For generating music conditioned on specific emotional content from text, we utilize an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture. The encoder component is based on RoBERTa-large, fine-tuned to enhance its sensitivity to subtle emotional distinctions within various categories. The decoder comprises a Transformer model with three layers and four attention heads per layer, designed to manage the timing and harmonic structures of symbolic music. Figure 2 illustrates the model's architecture.   The RoBERTa-large encoder undergoes fine-tuning on the GoEmotions dataset, wherein its 27 emotion labels are re-mapped to broader emotion categories. Each training batch contains samples from all emotion categories, with intra-category pairs treated as positive and inter-category pairs as negative. This contrastive learning approach aids in constructing an embedding space with clear categorical distinctions. Following fine-tuning, the encoder is frozen and employed as the text-embedding input for the generative model. Notably, the original RoBERTa encoder produces overlapping representations for the four emotion categories, while the fine-tuned version yields more distinct groupings. This improvement highlights the fine-tuning process's efficacy in refining the emotional structure of the embedding space while preserving the nuanced and subjective nature of emotions (see Appendix, Section C, Figure 4, for embedding visualizations before and after fine-tuning).  Due to the limited size of the EMOPIA dataset, containing only 1,078 MIDI samples, the Transformer decoder lacks sufficient data to effectively learn MIDI token patterns. To address this limitation, we pre-train the decoder on the much larger GiantMIDI-Piano dataset. During pre-training, text embeddings are replaced with zero tensors, while the decoder is trained in an auto-regressive manner to predict subsequent MIDI tokens, thereby learning the underlying sequential patterns effectively.

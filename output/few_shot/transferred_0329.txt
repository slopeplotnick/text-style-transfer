Large Language Models (LLMs) are experiencing rapid advancements, prompting exploration of novel applications for societal benefit. With their increasing deployment in online communication platforms, these models hold potential for enhancing conversational understanding and promoting constructive dialogue. This study investigates the capacity of LLMs not only to identify harmful content in online interactions but also to facilitate conflict resolution and mitigate disputes. The proposed framework comprises two main components: initially, the model evaluates the fairness and emotional tone of a conversation; subsequently, it generates messages aimed at de-escalating tensions and fostering reconciliation. To assess the efficacy of these models, researchers curated an extensive dataset from Reddit and developed a comprehensive evaluation methodology employing rule-based scoring, user simulations, and human benchmarking. The findings indicate that commercially available models surpass their open-access counterparts in reasoning abilities and dispute mediation. While the study highlights the promising potential of LLMs in promoting online harmony, it also acknowledges existing limitations. These models are transforming communication, education, and collaboration, extending beyond conventional tasks such as language translation and text summarization to sectors like education, healthcare, and mental health. Given their application in contentious online environments, there is a burgeoning interest in leveraging LLMs not merely for task automation but for fostering empathy and equitable exchanges. A significant online challenge is the prevalence of hostile exchanges, where users engage in escalating confrontations, exacerbated by the absence of nonverbal cues and social signals.

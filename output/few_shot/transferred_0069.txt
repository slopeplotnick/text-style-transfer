Our proposed methodology was evaluated against an alternative approach, RePro, using results reported in its original publication, as we did not have access to the associated codebase. Our framework, referred to as Paper2Code (P2C), differs from other approaches such as Refine, which requires manual generation of task-specific prompts to evaluate and improve individual components. For example, the original Paper2Code framework necessitated the design of eight tailored prompts to address four distinct planning tasks. In contrast, our approach eliminates the need for manual prompt engineering by leveraging a system prompt to dynamically adapt to each task, thus automating the process.   Empirical validations of our method were conducted on two datasets. The first dataset, Paper2CodeBench, comprises 90 papers from leading conferences in 2024, including ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS. These papers were selected based on two criteria: (1) the availability of associated code and (2) manageable length. We compared the code generated by our method against the official code released by the authors, with correctness assessed by AI judges on a five-point scale. The second dataset, PaperBench Code-Dev, includes 20 ICML 2024 papers, where human evaluators scored the correctness of the generated code following predefined rubric guidelines.   To the best of our knowledge, few methodologies explicitly employ self-refinement for automated translation of research papers into executable code. For comparative analysis, we benchmarked our approach against two related methods that incorporate self-refinement: (1) Paper2Code combined with Self-Refine and (2) RePro. Self-Refine employs an iterative refinement process, whereas our approach integrates automated planning and optimization. All experiments utilized GPT-4.1 as the primary language model. For RePro, we relied on the performance metrics reported in its original publication due to the unavailability of source code.  Performance outcomes, detailed in Tables I and II, demonstrate that our methodology achieved superior reliability across datasets. Notably, even when optimization was restricted solely to the planning phase, our approach, referred to as Paper2Code with autoplan optimization, exhibited consistent improvements in performance. Task-level evaluations revealed that our method outperformed alternatives overall. As illustrated in Figure 2, our planning optimization strategy surpassed Self-Refine across 16 out of 20 tasks (80%) within the PaperBench dataset, highlighting its robustness across diverse scenarios. Conversely, Self-Refine yielded suboptimal results on 10 tasks, with particularly poor performance observed in specific cases.

Samese achieved an average F1 score of 84.65 on Assamese test sets, while the model trained on Hindi achieved a slightly lower F1 score of 83.91. A similar trend was observed for Sindhi, where direct supervision resulted in an F1 score of 85.59, while the Hindi-trained model achieved 86.11 via cross-lingual transfer. To address Research Question 2 (RQ2), the performance of the models was evaluated on 11 low-resource languages spanning 8 linguistic groups. Languages utilizing the Latin script, such as Galician and Slovenian, exhibited superior performance across evaluations, likely attributable to the alignment between their script and the subword tokenization present in the shared XLM-R vocabulary, which facilitated improved representation during pretraining. Conversely, Icelandic, despite using the Latin script, exhibited lower performance. This discrepancy is attributed to its complex morphological structure, which results in heightened token fragmentation and less coherent sentence representations, thereby reducing the efficacy of multilingual encoders employing subword tokenization.   To investigate cross-lingual transferability and address Research Question 3 (RQ3), models were trained using Hindi-only, Chinese-only, and Hindi+Chinese combined setups. Subsequently, each model was evaluated on six languages from the Indic (Hindi) and East-Southeast Asian (Chinese) linguistic groups. Within the Indic group, the Hindi-trained model consistently outperformed the Chinese-trained model across all tests, whereas, for the East-Southeast Asian group, the Chinese-trained model outperformed the Hindi-trained model in the majority of tests. These findings underscore that transfer learning within a linguistic group, from a high-resource language to other low-resource languages within the same group, is more effective than cross-group transfer involving a high-resource language from a different linguistic group.

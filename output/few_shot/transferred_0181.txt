Conduct a comparative analysis of LLMs against algorithms such as Deep Q-Network (DQN) and Q-learning. This framework achieves its objectives by decomposing tasks and optimizing resource allocation. Long-duration construction tasks are segmented into more manageable sub-tasks using strategies drawn from the SMART-LLM framework (Kannan et al., 2024). One strategy categorizes these tasks based on the objects involved and requisite actions, while another focuses on the necessary skills, such as precision handling or application of force. Subsequently, these sub-tasks are allocated to various robots based on their capabilities and task demands. The LTAA framework employs LangGraph to systematically organize tasks, endeavoring to maximize task completion likelihood by assessing the suitability of each robot for a given task. Initial observations revealed an imbalance in workload distribution, where certain robots were overburdened, while others were underutilized. This prompted the development of novel strategies that adaptively adjust task priorities, initially centering on success rates and later ensuring equitable task distribution. The development process encompassed three stages: initially, the SMART-LLM framework was applied to Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA), identifying validation and reliability issues. Subsequently, a Self-Corrective Agent Architecture was employed to ameliorate these concerns through multi-stage validation and retry mechanisms. Ultimately, the LTAA framework synthesized these insights with innovative allocation strategies to benchmark against traditional optimization methods. The authors delineate the establishment of a prototypical LLM-based MRTA framework: SMART-LLM (Kannan et al., 2024), and the principal challenges encountered, including validation and coordination issues. Addressing these challenges led to the development of a phase-adaptive and self-corrective architecture leveraging LangGraph, which integrates multi-stage validation and retry mechanisms to resolve these issues, culminating in the LTAA framework. The paper further discusses performance assessment against conventional optimization techniques, utilizing the TEACH dataset to facilitate direct comparisons with Q-learning, DQN, and Dynamic Programming (DP) under consistent task conditions. The analysis evaluates work balance, task completion based on attributes, and reasoning quality to assess both allocation efficiency and performance outcomes.

Upon examining the results presented in Table 1, it is evident that text-based methods such as LucyEdit achieve high scores on certain synchronization metrics (e.g., Speech Corr. 0.80 compared to our 0.72). However, they exhibit shortcomings in performing the actual edits, as evidenced by their low Directional CLIP score of 0.11 relative to our score of 0.21. Our method demonstrates a balanced performance, excelling in synchronization while also effectively implementing visual edits. Conversely, VACE employs additional conditioning elements such as pose, canny, and depth to achieve higher CLIP alignment but underperforms in temporal consistency and identity preservation. Sync-LoRA, which is specifically trained for synchronized editing, maintains robust temporal alignment while ensuring accurate edits that preserve identity. This illustrates the efficacy of our approach in balancing motion consistency and edit accuracy rather than focusing exclusively on a single aspect.

Flow-based diffusion models have emerged as a preferred methodology for training models aimed at the generation of images and videos. However, the mechanisms by which these models retain and generalize information remain inadequately understood. This study revisits the flow matching objective, analyzing its marginal velocity field, which can be precisely computed, thus enabling the determination of the exact FM target. Examination of this velocity field reveals an inherent bifurcated training objective: initially, the models emphasize a mixture of data types, subsequently concentrating on the nearest data instance. This dual-focused objective engenders distinct learning dynamics: the initial phase aids in the comprehension of diverse data types to establish overarching patterns, while the latter phase accentuates the retention of minute details. Leveraging this insight, the study elucidates the efficacy of certain techniques, such as temporal step adjustments, classifier-free guidance, and strategic decisions in latent space design. This research advances our understanding of diffusion model training mechanisms and proposes avenues for future enhancements in design and algorithmic strategies. Diffusion models have become a formidable class of generative methods, capable of producing high-quality samples across domains such as images and videos. These models assimilate intricate data patterns by incrementally transforming an initial distribution, like Gaussian, into the target data distribution via a denoising or transport direction process. Various frameworks have been proposed to describe this diffusion process, encompassing probabilistic models, score-based ODEs/SDEs, and flow matching. These conceptualizations yield distinct training objectives, such as noise prediction, score estimation, or velocity determination, and correlate with diverse configurations of the probability flow ODE. Recently, consensus has coalesced around the flow matching approach, whereby the model is trained to predict the velocity field employing a straightforward schedule. Owing to its simplicity and training stability, this approach has become the prevailing standard for training leading diffusion models.

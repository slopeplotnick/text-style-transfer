The facilitation of innovative product development tailored to specific business markets is a significant advantage of adopting Software Product Lines (SPL) (Pohl et al., 2005). These examples serve to motivate teams to integrate SPL methodologies. A critical component in mitigating resistance to change involves minimizing associated risks. This discussion highlights strategies to effectively reduce such risks. It is imperative to address all concerns, whether directly related or not, as they may hinder successful adoption. Recurrent challenges in SPL implementation, while daunting, are surmountable with strategic guidance that considers these risks (Chen et al., 2010). The perceived complexity often arises from misunderstandings of Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE). However, even experienced practitioners may encounter formidable challenges inherent to SPL. Ideally, SPL should not introduce greater complexity than that of the existing IT infrastructure, which teams are already familiar with. In SPL, the concept of variability is articulated through models to facilitate comprehension and management of complexity (Czarnecki et al., 2012). The necessity for training and demonstration is intertwined with perceived complexity. Clarifying that SPL optimizes time management for feature addition and creative development preempts potential loss of innovation time, as noted by User Experience (UX) analysts. Comprehensive non-regression testing can avert unintended side effects. Furthermore, addressing concerns through dialogue about strengths and weaknesses is crucial, as these concerns are often perceived as biases by stakeholders. Recognizing SPL as a potentially cumbersome system underscores the importance of transparent communication and detailed documentation in restoring stakeholder trust. Inclusivity in change management involves actively inviting participation in the transition process. Regular reporting at each stage promotes a sense of acknowledgment and facilitates the provision of necessary support (Bosch et al., 2001).

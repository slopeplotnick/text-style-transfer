SR-GRPO leverages stable rank as a reward signal within reinforcement learning, thereby eliminating the reliance on preference datasets or external validation mechanisms. Empirical findings indicate that SR-GRPO enhances reasoning capabilities across diverse evaluation tasks, thus substantiating the utility of geometric signals as foundational frameworks for aligning language models. In addition to its application in reinforcement learning, stable rank demonstrates efficacy as a zero-shot reward proxy for evaluating language model responses and is seamlessly integrated into best-of-N decoding strategies. Specifically, stable rank (Rudelson & Vershynin, 2007) is proposed as an unsupervised quality metric derived from language model representations. This work formalizes the stable rank metric, elucidates its geometric relevance, and provides theoretical and empirical evidence for its effectiveness as a reward proxy.  The theoretical underpinning is derived from two principal phenomena: the softmax bottleneck (Yang et al., 2018; Godey et al., 2024), which posits that high-rank representations are indispensable for expressive language modeling, and representation collapse, a documented issue associated with suboptimal generation quality (Gao et al., 2019). Stable rank, as a measure of representational dimensionality, emerges as a robust choice for quality assessment. Correlations between stable rank and task performance are analyzed in Section 5.  To evaluate stable rank as a zero-shot proxy for human preferences, experiments were conducted to predict preferred responses in paired comparisons. This evaluation tests whether stable rank can reliably serve as a reward signal independently of model-specific training. The experimental setup utilizes RewardBench (Lambert et al., 2025), a benchmark comprising 2,985 preference pairs across five categories: Chat, Chat-Hard, Safety, Code, and Math. For each pair, stable rank values are computed for both responses, with the higher-ranked response predicted as the preferred one. Three baseline methods are employed for comparison: (1) Pointwise Scoring (Point.) (Kim et al., 2024), where language models assign scores from 1 to 5 to individual responses; (2) Pairwise Comparison (Pair.) (Kim et al., 2024), in which language models directly compare two responses; and (3) IPO (Garg et al., 2025), leveraging preference scores derived from Yes/No token probabilities.  The evaluation encompasses five state-of-the-art models: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Yang et al., 2024), Qwen3-0.6B (Yang et al., 2025), Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Dubey et al., 2024), and Phi-3.5-mini-Instruct (Abdin et al., 2024). For the stable rank approach, inputs are formatted as prompt-response pairs using the respective chat templates of each model, with final-layer outputs utilized for computation. Detailed results and comparative analyses are provided in subsequent sections.

Language Model Systems (LMS), encompassing Large Language Models (LLMs) and other variants [Brown et al., 2020; Gemini Team, 2024; Liu et al., 2023, 2024; Touvron et al., 2023], are employed in a wide array of applications, including medical chatbots, autonomous vehicle assistants, and coding helpers. Given their deployment in critical and public-facing domains, ensuring their correct functionality and alignment with human expectations is imperative. Numerous studies have endeavored to enhance the safety of these systems by integrating safety mechanisms. However, the current methodologies for verifying and enforcing desirable behavior remain inadequate, as they fail to provide guarantees across diverse scenarios. Such guarantees are crucial for fostering user trust and ensuring safety. Recent efforts have focused on developing formal certifications for desirable behaviors, such as fairness, accuracy in responses, and safety. These behaviors are articulated as specific rules that the systems must adhere to. For instance, researchers have devised rules for fairness in responses, comprehension of reading materials, managing sensitive dialogues, and tool selection. Nevertheless, these certifications are often inflexible and tailored to specific properties and conversational settings, necessitating modifications for different contexts and requirements. As such, the process of certifying these systems is currently conducted in a bespoke manner for each property, domain, and system type, highlighting an area for improvement in terms of adaptability and standardization.

Answering questions within the domain of elderly services presents significant challenges, as it necessitates providing accurate and safe responses grounded in diverse sources of information. Common inquiries often revolve around healthcare regulations, welfare programs, and daily guidance; delivering incorrect or unsafe information in these contexts can have serious consequences. While general-purpose language models (LMs) are effective for broad applications, they often lack the precision and domain-specific awareness required for sensitive topics such as elderly care. Current models primarily rely on either structured or unstructured data, constraining their capacity to generate comprehensive and contextually appropriate responses. Furthermore, most existing systems do not possess mechanisms to rigorously control how external information influences their outputs, nor do they prioritize the safety of their responses. These limitations diminish their utility in elderly service contexts.   To address these gaps, we present **KARMA**, a domain-adaptive question-answering (QA) system specifically designed for elderly services. KARMA employs a dual-encoder architecture that integrates knowledge from diverse sources, alongside a memory unit that retrieves the most relevant information, combined with a safety-aware decoder to mitigate the risk of producing incorrect or harmful responses. The system is trained to optimize three key objectives: linguistic fluency, knowledge consistency, and response safety. Built on a fine-tuned LLaMA-7B foundational model, KARMA demonstrates strong adaptability and performance. Evaluation on a specialized elderly-service QA dataset reveals that KARMA significantly outperforms existing models in terms of accuracy, relevance, and safety, establishing its viability for real-world applications in elderly care.  The foundational principles of KARMA draw on key advancements in the field. Mo et al. (2025) highlight the importance of integrating heterogeneous data sources for complex QA, demonstrating the necessity of multimodal information synthesis, which informs KARMA's dual-encoder setup. Huang et al. (2024) introduced MetaMath-LLaMA, which integrates symbolic parsing, a dynamic scheduler, and a hybrid symbolic-neural framework for enhanced multi-step reasoning. Incorporating such methodologies enhances KARMA's memory unit gating and safety mechanisms. Luo et al. (2024) proposed TriMedTune, a fine-tuning strategy that employs visual prompts, aligns domain-specific terms, and integrates uncertainty-checking mechanisms for medical domains. Integrating these techniques into KARMA would enhance its ability to manage complex domain-specific terminologies and provide safe and accurate responses, particularly in scenarios involving uncertainty. Sun et al. (2024) developed STELLAR, linking a semantic reasoning module with a symbolic framework, offering clear improvements in safety and interpretability of multi-step reasoning processes. These contributions provide a robust foundation for advancing KARMA's efficacy in elderly-care QA applications.

This study investigates the limitations of a recent approach [16], as depicted in Figure 1(a). Initially, the model attends to both audio and visual inputs; however, as the process continues, it increasingly focuses on the previously generated text. Consequently, the model's reasoning becomes reliant on the emotional cues present in its initial text output. Typically, the reasoning process commences with video description because the training data is structured to prioritize visual information, followed by audio inputs, and then reasoning tasks. This "visual-first" approach, however, may not be ideal, particularly when emotional content is derived more from auditory cues or semantic interpretation. In cases where initial visual reasoning is flawed or irrelevant, subsequent reasoning progressively deviates, as illustrated in Figure 1(b). Thus, the initial step disproportionately influences the reasoning trajectory. Similar issues are observed in other domains, where early cues mislead the reasoning process [17]. To address this challenge, we propose Modality-Importance-Guided Reasoning (MIGR). The core principle is to initiate reasoning with the input modality (audio, visual, or both) that best aligns with the target emotion and to maintain consistent reasoning throughout. This is achieved by introducing Modality Importance (MI), which determines the most critical input type for emotion recognition by evaluating the model's predictive accuracy with isolated audio, isolated visual, and combined inputs. For instance, if the model accurately identifies the emotion using only audio but errs when visual inputs are included, it suggests that visual information is either irrelevant or misleading, thereby prioritizing audio as the primary focus. Employing the MI concept alongside recent training strategies for reasoning-based models [15, 16], we developed a two-step learning framework: (1) modality-aligned Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and (2) modality-aligned reward optimization. During the SFT phase, MI guides the segmentation and reorganization of reasoning text based on input modality types.

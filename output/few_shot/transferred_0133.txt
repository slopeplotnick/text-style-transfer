Test-time learning enables models to dynamically adjust during deployment. Initial methodologies emphasized straightforward adaptive techniques without altering model parameters, such as uncertainty reduction and batch normalization. Subsequent strategies advanced these concepts by permitting models to update their parameters during deployment, thus enhancing task-specific performance. For instance, empirical studies indicate that test-time training facilitates improved model learning under data distribution shifts, with some approaches allowing large language models to fine-tune utilizing constructive feedback. An emerging concept in this domain is test-time policy optimization, which conceptualizes model deployment as a reinforcement learning challenge. Approaches like RLVR and TTRL utilize iterative feedback mechanisms, such as majority voting, to guide parameter updates. This ensures model consistency and accuracy without reliance on labeled data. However, a significant limitation of these methods is their demand for extensive iterative trials per query to achieve reliable outcomes, thereby incurring substantial time and computational resource costs. While straightforward queries may require minimal effort, complex ones exacerbate the inefficiencies, rendering the process cumbersome and expensive. To address this challenge, we propose the OptPO framework to enhance computational efficiency. Rather than pre-determining the number of iterations required, OptPO employs a stepwise validation approach, ceasing once sufficient evidence for a definitive answer is amassed within a predefined error threshold. The trials conducted are subsequently leveraged to refine model parameters, ensuring computational resources are allocated judiciously. Furthermore, OptPO integrates seamlessly with supervised fine-tuning techniques for test-time training.

In Figure 3, the learning dynamics of the 7B model utilizing CAPO and GRPO methodologies are depicted. Initially, both approaches exhibit comparable improvement rates; however, as the model acclimates, CAPO demonstrates superior performance. The demarcation by the gray line indicates the transition point at which the model ceases replication and begins autonomous problem-solving. Post this transition, CAPO's unpredictability continues to escalate, in contrast to GRPO, which remains constant, while the accrued rewards consistently enhance. This implies that CAPO not only achieves superior rewards in the latter stages but also maintains a higher degree of unpredictability, advantageous for exploring alternative cognitive strategies. The gradual augmentation in unpredictability ensures that CAPO avoids premature predictability by incorporating negative examples too early. By deferring this incorporation, CAPO stabilizes the replication phase and subsequently leverages negative feedback more effectively, thereby enhancing its adaptability to diverse tasks.  Further, a comparison is drawn between the conventional static pedagogical approach and the innovative dynamic approach proposed herein. In the static paradigm, the difficulty of each example is assessed based on model performance, followed by sequential training; in contrast, the dynamic method modulates training focus without necessitating data reordering. As illustrated in Table 3, both ADARFT and the static method GRPO(+SC) exhibit marginal improvement over the baseline GRPO. However, the dynamic method GRPO(+Ours) achieves significantly superior performance across most tasks. This evidences that mere difficulty estimation is insufficient, and that dynamic focus modulation during training yields enhanced outcomes.

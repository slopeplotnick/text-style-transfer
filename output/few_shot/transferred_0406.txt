Traditional approaches to optimizing construction coordination present inherent limitations. These methods require complete pre-planning, which poses challenges in the dynamic context of construction projects characterized by evolving tasks, conditions, and resources. Furthermore, they often become overly complex when applied to large-scale projects involving multiple robots and tasks. As a result, there has been increased interest in learning-based methodologies capable of adapting to changes without relying on comprehensive initial plans. Among these, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has garnered significant attention due to its capacity to enable robots to learn task coordination through environmental interaction, thereby eliminating the need for exhaustive preprogrammed strategies. RL facilitates adaptive decision-making by promoting experiential learning; however, it typically requires extensive training, which may entail substantial risks in construction settings where errors can jeopardize worker safety and equipment integrity. Moreover, RL's prolonged training timelines pose challenges in projects with stringent deadlines.    To address these challenges, researchers have developed RL-based methods tailored to the construction domain. For example, Lee et al. (2022) introduced a novel approach integrating digital twins and Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to enhance robot task coordination in construction. By combining Building Information Modeling (BIM) with DRL, this framework supports intelligent coordination decisions that account for spatial, temporal, and safety considerations. The incorporation of digital twins allows for safe learning within simulated environments prior to deployment in physical construction sites. Nonetheless, RL continues to face notable limitations, as simulation-trained models may fail to generalize to real-world conditions, leading to safety concerns. Additionally, transferring learned policies from controlled simulations to the variability of actual construction sites remains a significant challenge. These persistent issues have motivated the development of more advanced RL methodologies to bridge the gap between simulation and real-world applicability in construction contexts.

This study investigated the extraction of grammatical features, followed by an experiment involving 10,000 noun phrases and sentences generated by Delilah. The ability of the language learning process to distinguish and categorize words effectively is demonstrated through graphs, labeled in accordance with conventional linguistic terminology. Figure 3a presents labels employed by the MODOMA agent, whereas Figure 3b illustrates more traditional labels. Subsequent experiments utilized larger datasets, comprising 10,000 noun phrases and sentences from Delilah. Word statistics for these datasets are detailed in Table 5. Consistent with prior findings, the 35 most frequently occurring words across both datasets are enumerated in Tables 6 and 7, primarily comprising function words. Within the 10,000 noun phrases, 'forgotten' emerges as the first prominent content word at rank 54/55, equating its rank with the function word 'at' or 'near'. Beyond rank 55, content words become more prevalent. In the corpus of 10,000 sentences from Delilah, the most frequent content words include 'forgotten' at rank 11, 'works' at rank 27, and 'in the morning' at rank 66. Post rank 66, additional content words manifest. The representation of 'masses' as a numeral by Delilah is accurately interpreted by the learning process. Among the 10,000 examples, distinguishing function words from content words is facilitated by the observation that words appearing over 100 times are typically function words, accounting for more than 1.7 per thousand in Delilah's sentences. Consequently, based on these experiments, the MODOMA settings for differentiating function and content words are calibrated such that words appearing more than 2 per thousand are classified as function words, while others as content words. This classification process occurs post the acquisition of at least 10,000 examples. The settings are meticulously designed to minimize the likelihood of erroneously identifying a function word as a content word, given that content word is the default classification. Thus, every word is presumed to be a content word unless substantial evidence suggests otherwise. These initial settings have been established as the default for MODOMA, though they remain subject to modification in light of further experimentation and linguistic theoretical developments.

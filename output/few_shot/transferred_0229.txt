We developed a novel methodology for constructing knowledge graphs (KGs) from images by employing AI models that integrate textual and visual data, guided by domain-specific ontological rules. These ontological rules define the entities and relationships of interest, and are incorporated into the AI model's instructions to ensure focused analysis of salient image features. Upon processing an image, the AI generates a list of key entities, their attributes, and interrelations, as dictated by the ontological framework. Subsequently, these lists are transformed into a graph that encapsulates the depicted content. The graph's structure is entirely contingent upon the predefined rules. Each image yields an individual graph, and these graphs are systematically stored in a database. Despite each image having its unique graph, commonalities arise due to the standardized lexicon of the rules, resulting in interconnected graphs that form a comprehensive, unified graph. This overarching graph facilitates the identification of similarities across images by enabling comparative analysis of their constituent subgraphs. To align the AI with these rules, they are integrated into the interpretative instructions provided to the AI, allowing for the generation of coherent graphs per image and their amalgamation into a singular, extensive graph. The smaller graphs are interconnected through shared elements, thereby streamlining the process of image comparison within the entire dataset.

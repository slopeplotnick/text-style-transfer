A system was developed to identify evident errors that are readily detectable, rather than engaging in deep scientific inquiry or analyzing kinematic phenomena. The dataset employed emphasizes complex physical processes and scientific principles, demonstrating that systematic deconstruction of physics concepts using specific methodologies yields superior outcomes compared to generalized approaches. This underscores the necessity for specialized evaluation metrics tailored to assess detailed physical reasoning in video generation contexts. VideoScore2 was deemed unsuitable for the proposed evaluation due to its reliance on rigorous scientific verification protocols, which diverge from the intuitive judgments required for this dataset. Comparative performance outcomes of various models are presented herein.   As illustrated in Figure 4 (top), the Sora-2 model successfully generates physical simulations aligned with anticipated phenomena, leveraging appropriate prompts and demonstrating consistent motion and temporal coherence. The resulting video accurately reflects the experimental setup, captures changes over time, and illustrates the initiation and color transformations associated with the reaction. In contrast, the Hailuo 2.3 model, while maintaining movement and spatial integrity, fails to adhere to essential aspects of the prompt, producing a video where the anticipated phenomenon is absent. This discrepancy highlights the necessity of precise experimental configurations and prompt adherence to achieve high performance scores, even when other metrics appear satisfactory. Notably, even relatively simple physical setups pose significant challenges for contemporary models.   Figure 4 (bottom) reveals further limitations of current models, emphasizing their difficulty in handling scenarios where textual models readily predict expected outcomes. Specifically, while the Sora-2 model partially aligns with the textual prompt, it exhibits substantial inaccuracies and fails to produce the predicted phenomena. Nonetheless, Sora-2 maintains commendable motion and spatial-temporal consistency. Meanwhile, the Veo-3 model presents the experimental setup inadequately, offering only brief indications of the expected outward movement of the balls. These deficiencies underscore the persistent challenges current models face when grappling with ostensibly straightforward physical simulations in the proposed testing framework.

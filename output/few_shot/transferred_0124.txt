A step-by-step refinement mechanism was integrated into the PPTPilot system to enhance its predictive accuracy over iterative cycles. The agent proposes modifications to XML files, which are subsequently converted into temporary PPT files. A verification module evaluates these PPT files against the initial task instructions, providing feedback on inconsistencies. Based on this feedback, the agent iteratively updates the PPT file to rectify identified errors. While the system architecture remains relatively straightforward, it demonstrates notable efficiency and effectiveness for PowerPoint editing tasks compared to existing tools. The development of PPTPilot is intended as a foundational framework to spur further investigations in automated PPT editing.   For evaluation, state-of-the-art vision-language models, including Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT-5, were employed. The primary comparison results (Table 3) predominantly utilize GPT-5, while supplementary materials encompass additional insights regarding Gemini 2.5 Pro and implementation-specific details. Testing involved benchmarking PPTPilot against competing tools, such as ChatGPT (in extended thinking mode), Gemini-CLI, GPT-Agent (designed to emulate desktop functionalities), MiniMax Agent (optimized for multimodal applications and marketed as a PPT aid), PPTAgent, and Poster2Agent. Certain tools, notably ChatGPT and MiniMax Agent, impose stringent usage restrictions and incur elevated costs, prompting a constrained test set of 25 samples to adhere to budgetary considerations. The evaluation prioritized complex tasks where PPTPilot and ChatGPT exhibited limitations and incorporated additional cases to ensure coverage of diverse scenarios. Comprehensive information regarding the evaluation cases is provided in the supplementary materials.

Details regarding the participants are provided in Table B.3 in the Appendix. No significant differences were observed in the characteristics of participants across the groups. Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups. In the first group, participants were informed that the music was generated by AI. In the second group, they were told the music originated from movie soundtracks. The third group was informed that they would be listening to music created by both humans and AI, and their task was to discern the origin of each piece. Groups 1 and 3 received prior explanations about AI and AI music tools before engaging in the music rating task. Subsequently, all participants rated 18 music clips, which were randomly selected to represent various subgenres and AI tools. Prior to commencement, participants adjusted the volume to a preferred level and reviewed all emotion-related terms, which presented in a randomized order for each participant but remained consistent for all songs. Participants rated the emotional impact of the music, rather than the emotions the music aimed to convey. Each music clip was played twice: during the initial playthrough, participants listened attentively; during the second playthrough, rating questions were presented to aid in recollection. Following the music rating task, participants completed a questionnaire assessing their personality traits, music-related behaviors, and demographic background, in addition to their perspectives on AI. The study concluded with a debriefing screen elucidating the study's objectives and data usage protocols, and participants had the opportunity to share open-ended feedback regarding AI music and their experience in the study. The survey concluded with personalized feedback for each participant.

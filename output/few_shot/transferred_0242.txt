Li et al. (2025) discuss utilizing external feedback to enhance predictive accuracy during testing by providing guidance. Zuo et al. (2025) examine Test-Time Reinforcement Learning, a methodology involving the exploration of various strategies, aggregation through voting, and consensus-driven modifications. While these methodologies demonstrate efficacy, they necessitate extensive iterations to achieve satisfactory results. Wang et al. (2025) illustrate that employing verifiable reward mechanisms significantly enhances learning with minimal instances, indicating the potential advantages of this learning approach during testing phases. Our research focuses on enhancing this process's efficiency by implementing early stopping mechanisms when appropriate. Policy optimization is pivotal within reinforcement learning, particularly concerning language models. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a prevalent technique aimed at ensuring stable updates, frequently employed in scenarios involving human feedback. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) extends this by leveraging group-based benefits to enhance learning stability under constrained feedback conditions. OptPO builds upon these concepts to optimize efficiency without altering the foundational methodology. In summary, existing research underscores that training during testing facilitates improved model adaptability through various strategies, including uncertainty reduction, assisted learning, and reinforcement learning. However, prevailing methodologies require numerous trials, leading to inefficiencies. Our study contributes by introducing OptPO, which minimizes computational demands while maintaining robust performance, thereby augmenting and refining existing test-time methodologies.

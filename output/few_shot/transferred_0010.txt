In this study, we evaluate the quality of citations by examining their completeness (Recall), accuracy (Precision), and a combination of both metrics (F1 score). Recall measures the proportion of correct references present in a machine-generated survey against a human-curated benchmark, indicating the extent of literature coverage. In contrast, Precision assesses whether the references included in the machine-generated survey are also present in the human-curated list, reflecting the accuracy of the references. This evaluation enables us to determine the degree of alignment between machine-generated and human-generated references, thus assessing the fidelity of the computational approach to human research standards.   The method of using LLM-as-a-Judge has gained popularity for evaluating machine-generated text due to its scalability, consistency, and cost-effectiveness. Typically, models are provided with guidelines and a scoring framework to assess output quality. However, this approach may lack rigor, particularly in complex tasks such as comprehensive surveys. Therefore, we employ two variants of the LLM-as-a-Judge framework: one that references a human-authored survey for content evaluation, and another that employs principles for structure assessment.   For survey quality assessment, we utilize a configuration where the model is presented with both a human-produced and a machine-generated survey on the same topic. The inclusion of the human survey allows the model to form more informed judgments beyond mere adherence to guidelines. This configuration addresses several limitations of guideline-only approaches. First, the presence of an expert example prevents the model from overvaluing ambiguous or superficial content. Second, direct comparison with a comprehensive human survey enables the model to identify critical elements such as robust arguments and evidence, which might be overlooked by guideline-based evaluations. Third, leveraging a high-caliber example prevents the indiscriminate awarding of high scores to all systems, thereby facilitating a more nuanced differentiation between systems and enhancing our understanding of their respective strengths and areas for improvement in survey generation.

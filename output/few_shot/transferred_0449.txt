Furthermore, [22] advocates for the utilization of unlabeled LLM text in contrast to labeled datasets, facilitating the development of models adept at identifying hallucinations, thereby enhancing efficiency and economizing temporal resources during model training. Additionally, the significance of a robust research-backed question-answering system is emphasized in [12], aiming to mitigate the dissemination of misinformation—a critical aspect for users engaging with LLMs due to their generative and interactive capabilities. Collectively, these studies endeavor to devise methodologies to mitigate hallucinations in text generation, enhancing the reliability and trustworthiness of LLM outputs. Effective strategies such as zero-resource detection and model self-awareness are identified as potent measures against hallucinations, which remain a complex challenge. It is crucial to grasp the correlation between LLM structures and hallucination incidents, as this understanding impacts the selection of models proficient in addressing this issue. The disparities in hallucination frequency across different LLMs are attributed to their architectural and training variances. The work in [1] provides an extensive analysis of hallucinations in language models, noting the discomfort models experience when producing illogical or false outputs, and underscores the necessity of comprehending the causes and typologies of hallucinations in NLP systems, such as the generation of fabricated facts or nonsensical utterances. Similarly, [5] explicates the propensity for model-generated hallucinations, attributing it to the immense size and complexity of the training datasets, which may occasionally result in the presentation of erroneous information or entirely fictitious content.

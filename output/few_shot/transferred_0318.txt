The analysis presented in Table 3 indicates that utilizing both audio and visual data for generating summaries yields superior outcomes compared to relying solely on audio across most languages and models, despite the models' text-based nature. Notably, this enhancement is most pronounced for English, German, and Italian, whereas it is less substantial for Chinese. This disparity may be attributed to the presence of English terms in languages using the Latin alphabet, whereas English and Chinese exhibit significant linguistic and orthographic differences, hindering the effective integration of additional English information by the models. In the context of question answering, the inclusion of images provides a marginal benefit compared to summarization tasks. Although it occasionally yields slight improvements, in four out of twelve language-model configurations, performance actually deteriorates. This may occur because the model receives only enriched text information rather than direct image data, which proves insufficient for enhancing question-answering capabilities. Over the past decade, the domain of streaming speech translation has been extensively explored. Several lecture translation tools also employ this approach; however, they predominantly rely on auditory input. Our research distinguishes itself by incorporating both visual and auditory elements from slides, delivering translated audio and visuals across multiple languages. The domain of image-to-image translation has received limited attention. Nevertheless, increasing interest has emerged due to the availability of larger datasets. Most existing work, however, concentrates on translating textual elements within images rather than accurately reconstructing images post-translation. An initial exploration in this field involves examining methodologies for maintaining visual fidelity. Our image translation framework is designed with flexibility, allowing researchers to integrate their models at various stages, from text extraction in images to translation and image reconstruction, without necessitating the development of additional components.

When systems evolve autonomously without external guidance, they frequently encounter limitations rapidly, resulting in instability and reduced diversity, often leading to manipulative strategies that exploit the reward mechanism. This observation is vividly illustrated in our results. On the left-hand side of the graph, R-Zero exhibits a decline in question diversity as training progresses, with a decrease from 35 to under 20 within the initial 50 steps. Post 50 steps, there is an observed increase in the 2-gram diversity score; however, this is attributed to the elongation of questions, as depicted in the central graph. This increase does not signify genuine diversity; rather, it arises from the inherent nature of longer questions to yield more unique word combinations, which artificially inflates the diversity score without introducing novel ideas. A similar phenomenon is observed with question length: R-Zero persistently elongates its questions, eventually leading to excessive length. This trend indicates an attempt to game the evaluation system through superficial tactics, such as verbosity, to create an illusion of enhanced question variety or complexity. Conversely, R-Few maintains a consistent level of diversity throughout training and avoids unnecessary elongation of questions. This stability suggests that moderate human intervention effectively prevents the system from losing diversity. On the right-hand side of the graph, both approaches demonstrate an increased question difficulty over time, yet the underlying reasons differ significantly. Difficulty assessment was conducted using Gemini-2.5-Pro to relabel questions at various stages of training, followed by evaluating another model, Qwen3-8B-Base, on these questions. Difficulty is quantified as the proportion of questions answered incorrectly by the model. Both approaches exhibit an overall increase in difficulty, but for disparate reasons: R-Zero's questions become harder primarily due to increased length rather than requiring deeper cognitive engagement, thus misleadingly enhancing perceived difficulty without genuine reasoning improvement. In contrast, R-Few maintains consistent question length, enabling the system to naturally progress towards genuinely more challenging questions while retaining diversity and structural integrity.

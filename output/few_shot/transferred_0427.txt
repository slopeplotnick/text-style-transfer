To evaluate the efficacy of GAIA in utilizing tools for real-world tasks, we conduct an analysis using 103 text-only test cases. The XbenchDeepSearch benchmark focuses on professional-level deep information retrieval, while the WebSailor agent framework is employed with specific configurations: temperature set to 0.6, top-p set to 0.95, and a context length of 32,768 tokens. Additionally, we examine the utility of the THINKMERGE method, which facilitates reasoning and tool use prior to generating responses.   Our experiments involve three WebSailor agents—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B—tested across four challenging web-based benchmarks: BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and XbenchDeepSearch. The BrowseComp-en and BrowseComp-zh datasets evaluate retrieval of difficult-to-access factual information in English and Chinese, respectively. Since BrowseComp-en comprises 1,266 questions, we selected a randomized subset of 200 questions to match the scale of BrowseComp-zh, which contains 289 questions. GAIA, which requires effective tool utilization for multi-step reasoning tasks, is evaluated following prior methodologies on 103 text-only instances.   In constructing the experimental setup, we introduce cost-saving measures by replacing the evaluator model Qwen2.5-72B with GPT-4.1 and substituting the Google Search API with the Serper API. Results are averaged over five independent runs, with detailed outcomes reported in Table 8. The testing methodology involves averaging next-token predictions across all steps of the procedure, with the selected token propagated through subsequent contexts to maintain consistency in the generation process.  The THINKMERGE framework demonstrates efficacy under standard control conditions and integrates seamlessly with contemporary systems, allowing deployment in both online and offline settings. For mathematical and scientific questions with definitive solutions, THINKMERGE performs on par with or surpasses majority voting strategies. For open-ended queries, it enhances overall pass rates across multiple models. These findings underscore the utility of token choice averaging during testing as a mechanism for improving performance on both deterministic and open-ended tasks without necessitating additional training steps.

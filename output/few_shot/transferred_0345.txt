Various systems have been developed to address memory management in financial tasks. For instance, FinMem utilizes short-, mid-, and long-term memory to facilitate trading decisions. MemP employs a framework that transforms trajectories into step-by-step instructions and overarching strategies, leveraging procedural memory. MemGPT functions analogously to an operating system, managing storage and interruptions to extend the context window for large language models. Zep adopts temporal knowledge graphs to link historical relationships with evolving contexts.    To evaluate the effectiveness of these systems, accuracy (ACC) and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) are employed as performance metrics. ACC measures the overall correctness of predictions; MCC, on the other hand, is more suitable for balanced datasets and is particularly valuable in scenarios such as stock prediction, where class distributions may be uneven. Experimental evaluations conducted on four stock datasets indicate that our proposed approach outperforms existing systems in terms of both ACC and MCC, demonstrating its efficacy.    The analysis further highlights limitations in current methodologies. FinMem, for example, does not model events over time, resulting in the omission of significant market changes. MemP, while useful, oversimplifies trajectories, leading to the loss of critical financial details. MemGPT lacks a focus on event modeling, thereby failing to capture essential financial patterns. Zep struggles to effectively connect events, which limits its ability to identify meaningful relationships in financial datasets.    Our system addresses these challenges by converting unstructured financial news into structured event knowledge, enabling the systematic capture of event relationships. This framework facilitates enhanced data traceability and reasoning, resulting in improved financial predictions through the identification of key patterns and connections across events.

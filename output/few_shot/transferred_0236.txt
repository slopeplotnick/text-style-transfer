Modern market systems exhibit enhanced capabilities for growth, adaptation, and problem-solving compared to centralized systems. Auction systems, initially conceptualized by Botelho and Alami (1999), facilitate competitive task allocation, while Gerkey and Matarić (2002) demonstrated their efficacy in task distribution and coordination. Recent advancements have focused on addressing communication challenges using intelligent bidding techniques, even under weak connectivity conditions (Ferri et al., 2017; Quinton et al., 2022). However, auction-based methods may fail to yield optimal global solutions, as local bidding can disrupt coordination in complex task dependencies. In construction, where tasks must adhere to specific sequences, auctions struggle with temporal constraints crucial for project scheduling, prompting investigation into structured optimization approaches. Dynamic programming presents alternative optimization strategies for sequential decision-making, following foundational principles established by Bellman (1962) for problems with specific properties. Nonetheless, dynamic programming can become computationally prohibitive with numerous potential outcomes, limiting its applicability to large-scale problems. In construction, where numerous robots execute interdependent tasks, real-time computation remains challenging. Consequently, researchers have explored metaheuristic methods for managing large, intricate problems. These methods draw on evolutionary concepts to derive solutions, with genetic algorithms proving effective in navigating complex problems and constraints without requiring exhaustive mathematical formulations (Jones et al., 2010; Al-Omeer and Ahmed, 2019). Swarm intelligence methods excel in coordinating extensive robotic teams (Chen et al., 2022; Lim and Isa, 2015; Wang et al., 2012; Blum, 2005). However, metaheuristic approaches do not guarantee optimal solutions and demand significant computational resources, necessitating numerous iterations to achieve satisfactory outcomes. In construction contexts, where rapid adaptability is essential, the latency associated with these methods is prohibitively lengthy.

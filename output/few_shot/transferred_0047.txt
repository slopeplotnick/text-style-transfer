The development of robust models capable of performing effectively across diverse scenarios remains a significant challenge. Despite extensive training on varied datasets, these models frequently exhibit deficiencies when exposed to novel camera angles, changes in lighting, or other unforeseen scene variations. Such limitations diminish their applicability in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments. Addressing these issues typically involves two primary strategies: enhancing data diversity and improving spatial representation capabilities. The data-centric approach employs extensive datasets, such as Libero-Plus, to increase exposure to a wide array of visual scenarios. However, the acquisition of such data in real-world contexts is both costly and labor-intensive, rendering sustained efforts challenging. Conversely, the representation-centric strategy aims to enhance the models' geometric comprehension—such as object positioning in three-dimensional space—by employing techniques like multi-view supervision or specialized architectures tailored for 3D awareness. While these approaches facilitate better adaptation to viewpoint changes, challenges persist in dealing with cluttered backgrounds and lighting variations, indicating an incomplete adaptability to all visual conditions.  Predominant assumptions within the field suggest that either augmenting the dataset or developing improved 3D-focused design architectures could enhance model robustness. However, the extent to which these issues stem from spatial information representation remains insufficiently explored. To investigate this, we adopt a methodology that bifurcates the model into two components: spatial modeling and physical modeling. The spatial modeling, executed by the visual encoder, is responsible for interpreting the positioning, orientation, interaction between objects, and occlusions within a scene, thereby generating the requisite spatial information for subsequent processes. In contrast, the physical modeling component integrates this spatial information with task directives and historical actions to inform decision-making and action formulation.  Alterations in camera angles predominantly impact the scene's spatial configuration rather than the task's inherent meaning or the execution of actions. Consequently, performance degradation is primarily attributed to misaligned spatial modeling, despite the continued efficacy of the physical modeling component. In essence, while the model retains its cognitive and planning capabilities, the transmission of flawed spatial information leads to operational difficulties.

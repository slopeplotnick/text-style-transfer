Recent investigations have explored public perceptions of AI-generated content, employing rating scales derived from adjacent fields. However, such methodologies may inadequately capture the nuances of how individuals perceive AI-generated music. To address this, the present study utilizes open-ended questions to better understand subjective responses. Music samples were generated using AI tools, specifically Stable Audio and Suno, grounded in the Emotion-to-Music Atlas—a comprehensive repository detailing emotional responses elicited by musical stimuli. The study leveraged data from the Music4All-Onion database, encompassing five major genres—Electronic (e.g., Trance, Downtempo, Dubstep), Jazz (e.g., Bossa-Nova, Smooth-Jazz, Swing), Pop (e.g., Europop, Indie-Pop, Pop-Punk), Rock (e.g., Soft-Rock, Rock Ballad, Doom-Metal), and Soul/Funk (e.g., Funk, Disco, Soul)—each represented by three subgenres. Using aggregated emotion metrics from the Emotion-to-Music Atlas and GEMS-9 categories, 2-3 prompts per subgenre were formulated, yielding 38 unique prompts. These prompts were subsequently adapted for Suno and Stable Audio, resulting in 76 audio samples. For Suno, prompts were generated in instrumental mode, with one of two outputs selected and edited to 45–60 seconds to align with prior research protocols. For Stable Audio, prompts were appended with 'instrumental' specifications and adjusted to approximately 60 seconds. Detailed methodologies concerning sample generation and editing are provided in supplementary materials.

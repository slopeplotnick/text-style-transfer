A target is presented on either the left or right side of the screen, prompting the participant to repeatedly open and close their fist on the corresponding side until the target disappears, after which they may relax. If the target appears at the top or bottom of the screen, the participant must open and close both fists for a top target, or both feet for a bottom target, until the target vanishes, then relax. Our study concentrated on specific channels, and we refined the feature extraction process to ensure accurate acquisition of brain activity data pertinent to motor imagery task classification. To mitigate the interference of evoked responses on data interpretation, we implemented an analytical approach commencing one second post-stimulus, thereby disregarding the immediate response period. This strategy enabled us to examine the brain's intrinsic activity. By emphasizing stable features indicative of the individual's sensorimotor state, our method enhances the precision of brain signal classification. We experimented with various data preprocessing techniques preceding the application of conventional machine learning algorithms. Initially, we employed signal processing methods such as Common Spatial Patterns (CSP), a prominent technique in EEG signal classification. CSP, initially introduced by Koles et al., is utilized to distinguish features by modifying the variance between two distinct motor imagery tasks or mental states. It transforms EEG signals into a novel space where the variance for one class is maximized and minimized for the other. The principal aim is to identify and deploy spatial filters that reveal crucial class differentiation. CSP entails a systematic procedure, beginning with the computation of covariance matrices for each EEG signal class. The covariance between two variables X and Y can be determined using a specific formula.

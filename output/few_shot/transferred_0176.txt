The operational mechanism of this system involves the mother agent engaging with others through its established grammatical framework. This represents a novel approach, as conventional language learning models predominantly rely on extensive textual data for acquisition. In this study, the term "language model" extends beyond typical computational programs designed for next-word prediction, such as GPT or BERT. It encompasses diverse methodologies, including rule-based systems, statistical models, and neural networks, all of which contribute to executing language tasks. This distinction is crucial as our system employs explicit linguistic rules, whereas the learning agent utilizes statistical methods to infer these rules. The research investigates the capability of the MODOMA daughter agent to acquire and demonstrate various linguistic aspects by leveraging a particular distribution within the MODOMA framework. We examine whether a multi-agent system can deduce linguistic components from computer-generated sentences and articulate them using rule-based representations. Our objective is to determine whether these sentences exhibit patterns akin to human language and ascertain the data volume required to discern these patterns. Additionally, we assess the potential for these patterns to be formalized into explicit rules. Finally, we evaluate whether the identified categories align with the original grammatical structure and if the system can autonomously acquire linguistic structure.

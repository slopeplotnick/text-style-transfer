The classification scheme comprises five principal categories subdivided into 16 distinct types of edits, encompassing a spectrum from elementary text formatting to intricate editing workflows. This methodology emphasizes a reduced number of detailed cases, aligning with the approach proposed by Maia Polo et al. These cases encompass activities such as text replacement and complex problem-solving involving multiple edits and the integration of both images and text for resolution. For instance, one case involves aligning images with captions across slides utilizing both visual and textual information. Notably, certain cases intersect across different categories, resulting in a total exceeding 100 categories. PPTArena distinguishes itself from other benchmarks by concentrating on the complexity of edits through four dimensions: the number of steps required for resolution, inter-slide dependencies, the extent of semantic comprehension needed, and the degree of planning involved. Contrastingly, tools such as PPTC-R and T2US primarily address expedient, template-based edits, whereas PPTArena is engineered to accommodate more challenging, realistic scenarios to identify system limitations. It incorporates both human-curated and Python-generated decks, provides explicit evaluation criteria, operates across multiple platforms, and integrates accessibility standards. A representative task involves aligning each image with its appropriate caption on a slide, achieving a balanced layout, updating slide titles, and standardizing image dimensions to 3.2 inches in width by 2.4 inches in height. These tasks aim to evaluate systems on competencies such as multi-step reasoning, inter-slide information synthesis, and strategic planning. This framework effectively uncovers deficiencies that simpler tasks overlook, thereby serving as a robust mechanism for assessing advanced systems. The evaluation focuses on two primary aspects: adherence to instructions and the quality of visual outputs. These elements are appraised by expert evaluators using a scale from 0 to 5, where 0 denotes complete failure and 5 signifies perfection.

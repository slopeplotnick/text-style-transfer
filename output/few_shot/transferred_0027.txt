For *GraphMatch*, our findings indicate that a combination of random and hard negatives is crucial for optimal performance. Training *GraphMatch* solely on random negatives results in performance comparable to *TextMatch-small*. Various node feature configurations were examined for *GraphMatch*: the "no-feat" variant utilizes only *TextMatch* node embeddings, the "no-text" variant employs numerical node features without text embeddings, and the "full" variant integrates *TextMatch* node embeddings with numerical features. The *GraphMatch-full* model, trained with hard negatives, achieved the highest performance, while the *GraphMatch-no-feat* variant displayed results on par with *TextMatch-large*, despite being a more compact model. Notably, the inclusion of textual context proved essential for accurate predictions, as evidenced by the lower accuracy of *GraphMatch-no-text*. Temporal data sampling emerged as a critical factor, highlighting the importance of selecting appropriate subgraphs and node features at the correct time to train models adept at working with new nodes. To evaluate this, we trained *GraphMatch* using two distinct methodologies: the "no-temporal-nodes" version excluded time-specific node features during training but consistently utilized the latest feature set, while the "no-temporal-graph" version disregarded time-specific subgraph sampling and leveraged all graph edges during training.

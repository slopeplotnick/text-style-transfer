We propose a methodology for establishing safety protocols across a multitude of languages by leveraging a select few prominent ones. A compact safety model has been developed, effective across over 100 languages, but trained exclusively using data from 13 major languages. This study investigates the efficacy of using resources from dominant languages for application in less prevalent ones. Initial content moderation systems were predominantly English-centric, exemplified by tools such as Google's Perspective API, which was initially deployed for English. Subsequent tools, including Aegis-Defensive, WalledGuard, and WildGuard, demonstrated proficiency in handling safety and content moderation tasks in English. However, recent research indicates that popular language models tend to generate more unsafe responses in non-English languages. Furthermore, prompts articulated in less common languages pose challenges to existing safety mechanisms, often circumventing them with greater ease. Emerging methodologies aim to address these English-centric safety constraints across multiple languages. Alternative tools, such as MrGuard and X-Guard, utilize low-quality translated or synthetic data, failing to accurately capture the nuances of less common languages. Contemporary frameworks like LlamaGuard3-8B and NemoGuard-8B-content-safety are predicated on the Llama3.1-8B model, while DuoGuard introduces a novel dual-player training approach for developing a compact safety tool. To the best of our knowledge, PolyGuard represents the state-of-the-art model for safety assurance at present, accompanied by the most extensive safety dataset available for multiple languages. Nevertheless, PolyGuard's scope is limited to 17 languages, underscoring the prevailing focus of most tools on a select number of prominent languages.

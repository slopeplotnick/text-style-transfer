Recent licensing frameworks, such as NOODL and the Esethu License, illustrate a significant shift in practices. NOODL (Smith et al., 2025) differentiates between individuals in Africa or developing nations and those elsewhere, facilitating equitable regional benefit-sharing and ensuring local dissemination of modifications. Conversely, the Esethu License (Jones et al., 2025) reinvests licensing revenue into community-driven resource development and employment, maintaining local control and resource sustainability. Insights from working with Indigenous and low-resource language datasets highlight that mere openness can perpetuate inequities absent robust ethical guidelines. Hence, responsible data sharing transcends basic licensing requirements, necessitating attention to consent, privacy, fairness, and sustained community benefit (Brown et al., 2023). The selection of CC BY 4.0 for Swivuriso underscores the aspiration for openness while exposing conventional licenses' limitations for African and low-resource language datasets. Innovations such as NOODL and Esethu exemplify the transition towards improved, equitable stewardship. In publishing datasets, declaring an open license is inadequate; authors must articulate data management strategies aligned with ethical standards, equity, and community empowerment. Swivuriso represents a substantial advancement in enhancing automatic speech recognition and related technologies for South African languages, encompassing seven distinct languages and featuring both tailored and culturally pertinent unscripted speech. This dataset is instrumental not only in testing and refining metrics like word error rate but also in developing speech systems attuned to cultural and societal needs (Nguni et al., 2024).

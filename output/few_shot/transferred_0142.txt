Traditional Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) methods have demonstrated efficacy across various domains, utilizing optimization techniques such as linear programming, genetic algorithms, and reinforcement learning (RL), all grounded in mathematical frameworks with validated results. However, the unique and dynamic characteristics of construction sites present significant challenges to these traditional approaches. Specifically, construction environments are often unstructured and difficult to coordinate, and the complexity and volume of information inherent in such settings surpass the capabilities of conventional mathematical optimization-based methods. Consequently, there exists a critical need for more adaptable and robust solutions.    Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) highlight their ability to comprehend natural language, perform logical reasoning, and adaptively make decisions, addressing several limitations associated with traditional optimization and learning methods. LLM-based coordination frameworks offer advantages such as facilitating seamless human-robot interactions and accommodating the dynamic and intricate requirements of construction operations. Emerging LLM frameworks have shown promise in tasks such as planning, integrating digital twins, and employing multi-agent systems. However, current LLM setups lack systematic methods for performance evaluation and comparison with traditional approaches.   This gap underscores two critical issues: (1) an absence of research focused on developing mechanisms to identify and rectify errors in LLM reasoning to enhance reliability, and (2) a lack of systematic, empirical comparisons between LLM-based methodologies and traditional approaches in the context of construction task allocation.    To address these limitations, this study introduces the LangGraph-based Task Allocation Agent (LTAA) framework, an innovative LLM-driven coordination methodology that incorporates novel strategies and validation processes absent in prior methods. The LTAA framework excels in workload balancing without compromising performance and employs a rigorous validation mechanism to ensure the consistency of reasoning through iterative error identification and correction via structured feedback loops.

The MODOMA system serves as a simulated laboratory environment to investigate language acquisition processes in both adults and children. It features adjustable settings and transparently demonstrates the grammar rules it acquires, allowing for straightforward verification by researchers. This study illustrates the feasibility of employing a computational setup with multiple agents for first language acquisition. A crucial metric for evaluating the efficacy of the MODOMA system is Zipf's law. The experiments conducted within this study utilize a combination of methodologies to categorize different word types, such as function and content words, thereby encompassing all components of more intricate experimental designs. The findings provide a solid foundation for further research. Future investigations might explore the impact of altering system settings on the grammar acquired by the child agent, its linguistic output, and its interaction with the adult agent. This could involve examining the frequency of the word learning process and whether existing word categories should be updated in light of new information. Another avenue for exploration is the influence of feedback from the adult agent on the language acquisition and communication of the child agent. The success of these experiments suggests that more complex language learning methodologies can be investigated. Additionally, the MODOMA system can be leveraged to examine more sophisticated grammatical phenomena. For instance, Shakouri et al. (2025) demonstrate that grammatical categories such as nouns and verbs can be acquired in a manner analogous to the learning of function and content words, employing advanced statistical techniques for category identification without explicit instruction. The results of this study represent a significant advancement in this field of research.

The task at hand involves distinguishing between non-essential elements (L1 and L2) and essential ones (L3 and L4). To evaluate performance, we employ the accuracy at two metric (acc@2), which essentially assesses the correctness of outcomes. Additionally, the macro F1 score is utilized to ensure balanced evaluation in scenarios with uneven data label distribution. We conduct a comparative analysis of two model types: large language models (LLMs), both open and closed-source, and a baseline model. The LLMs are evaluated through task-specific prompts and guidelines, adhering to optimal settings for each model, such as a temperature setting of 0.7 for GPT-4 and 0.6 for Llama-3. Conversely, the baseline model, referred to as Vanilla SFT, is trained using labeled data without incorporating advanced techniques such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) or Reinforcement Learning (RL). This baseline model demonstrates robust performance, achieving acc@2 and macro F1 scores of 0.929 and 0.722, respectively, surpassing more advanced models such as GPT-4. Furthermore, the LORE model exhibits superior performance by enhancing acc@2 by 0.4 points, indicating that while the baseline model performs commendably with conventional data, LORE offers additional improvements.

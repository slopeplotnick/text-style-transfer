The linguistic knowledge encompassed within the system is explicitly accessible, enabling researchers to examine it for both agents. This methodological framework provides a significant contribution to systems engaged in language learning that lack transparent representations, such as large language models. MODOMA employs unsupervised learning, as the language learning apparatus processes unlabeled data and operates independently of the internal processes of the mother agent. Furthermore, the daughter agent leverages its preexisting grammatical knowledge to facilitate further learning. This novel learning paradigm, developed for MODOMA, is termed *internal annotation*, a form of self-supervised learning. The combination of distinctive features and research affordances in the multi-agent MODOMA architecture represents an innovative advancement. The system is designed for use in language acquisition experiments involving an adult language model and a learning model, where the agents interact dynamically. All system parameters—such as configurations, inputs, learning stages, interactions, language outputs, and grammatical acquisitions—are controllable, reproducible, measurable, and verifiable by researchers.  Ter Hoeve et al. (2022) devised a teacher-student framework to model language learning and conducted two preliminary experiments. The first experiment investigated disjoint lexical fields by utilizing “two completely separate vocabularies” within an artificial language, while the second experiment explored structural differences through the use of repeated tokens. In these setups, the teacher model selected a predefined quantity of training data from pre-constructed sentences to train multiple student language models. The performance of these student models was then evaluated using novel sentences from the artificial language. Additionally, a teacher model was trained and tested on a separate set of artificial language sentences. The fundamental distinction between this framework and MODOMA lies in the nature of the training and testing datasets: Ter Hoeve et al. employed pre-constructed artificial language sentences, whereas MODOMA generates datasets dynamically during interaction, employing a naturalistic language via Delilah, a Dutch grammar model. This dynamic, in situ data generation represents a key departure from prior methodologies and underscores the innovative dimension of the MODOMA framework.

Search relevance is a critical factor for online retail platforms such as JD and Taobao, entailing the evaluation of product-query alignment and the subsequent scoring to eliminate non-relevant items, thereby enhancing user search experiences. Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated considerable efficacy in these tasks, being extensively utilized. Nonetheless, state-of-the-art models, such as GPT-5, encounter limitations in domain-specific knowledge and are cost-prohibitive. Consequently, there has been a shift towards training smaller language models on shopping-specific data to optimize their performance. Recent advancements have transitioned from rudimentary training approaches to sophisticated methodologies that facilitate problem solving. Despite progress in sequential reasoning, challenges remain in addressing complex scenarios. For instance, the model ELLM examines product attributes and user queries but encounters difficulties in nuanced cases requiring rule-based distinctions. An example includes distinguishing "lake blue" from "sky blue" when such differentiation is necessary. Models like LREF and TaoSR1 attempt to rectify this with rule integration; however, they omit visual data, leading to oversights. For example, in cases where a "blue top" is imaged but not textually labeled as blue, these models lack the capability to interpret the visual evidence. These challenges stem from an unresolved question: What competencies must a model possess to effectively determine relevance? This inquiry pertains to the essential skills required for accurate relevance assessment within these contexts.

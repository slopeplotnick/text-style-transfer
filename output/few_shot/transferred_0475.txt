Despite the increasing popularity of this approach, there has been limited research on the collaboration between solvers and verifiers. Previous studies predominantly focused on a single model's capacity to verify its own responses and enhance its performance, although this process is not guaranteed to be foolproof. Models may exhibit a propensity towards their intrinsic reasoning, and their training can exacerbate this bias. Furthermore, not all tasks benefit equally from answer verification. Concentrating exclusively on self-verification does not adequately address the dynamics when the solver and verifier are distinct entities. The availability of open-source models in various versions and configurations allows for an examination of cross-model verification processes. Consequently, our research expands to encompass both intramodel and intermodel family evaluations, posing the question: Under what conditions does verification contribute to solver improvement, and how do factors such as model family, size, additional training, solver-verifier congruence, or task type influence this enhancement? To investigate these aspects, we assess verifiers across a diverse range of tasks, including those requiring precise logical reasoning or symbolic mathematics (e.g., 3-SAT, Sudoku, and matrix multiplication), mathematical reasoning tasks (such as AIME and GSM8K), commonsense and factual reasoning (e.g., CSQA and GPQA), as well as general knowledge domains like STEM and social sciences, using 37 models from seven distinct model families. Our findings indicate that self-verification is not universally effective: models commonly prefer solutions that align with their intrinsic reasoning, additional training tends to amplify this bias, and certain tasks derive limited benefit from verification processes. Based on our findings, we propose pragmatic recommendations for the strategic usage of verifiers.

The system we developed demonstrates robust performance across various tests and configurations, indicating its reliability and consistency. This is crucial, as erratic or random results would impede our understanding of interconnections. Instead, our findings reveal discernible groupings, stable patterns, and coherent shared attributes. This substantiates that our method facilitates the identification of training images that align with the style or semantic content of the outputs, rather than mere visual or textual similarities. Essentially, the data indicates that our automated creation of knowledge graphs (KGs) enhances the understanding of the influence of training data on model outputs. This has implications for model interpretability, copyright verification, and dataset transparency. For instance, when a model is prompted to generate an image in a specific style, such as "Ghibli-style," it does not replicate exact training images. Rather, it assimilates the general aesthetic of that style from a collection of images. These stylistic features are encoded within the model and employed to generate novel images, without being direct replicas. Consequently, tracing specific training images' influence on outputs is challenging; influence occurs more broadly via shared concepts, like textures, themes, or compositional techniques. To discern a style's impact on the model, an analysis of patterns and meanings is required, beyond merely matching specific objects. For our experiment, we employed an automated KG construction method: initially, we extracted frames from prominent Studio Ghibli films. Each frame was analyzed using a system that deconstructs it into structured information components, which were then utilized to establish a "Ghibli World" KG encapsulating the studio's visual style's core elements. Although we utilized frames from only ten films, the dataset provides a comprehensive representation of the studio's stylistic characteristics.

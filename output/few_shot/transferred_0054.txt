Udio is an advanced system capable of transforming natural-language prompts into fully realized musical compositions across various styles, including multilingual lyrics. In examining the perception of AI-generated music, research within computational creativity and machine learning has explored the creativity of these models by presenting human listeners with musical samples. These studies assess AI-generated music against other models and human-created compositions, evaluating overall quality, creativity, novelty, and specific musical components such as melody, harmony, and rhythm. Additionally, a critical metric is the perceived naturalness or human-likeness of the music, often assessed using rating scales or a musical Turing Test, where participants determine the origin—human or AI—of a piece, sometimes with varying levels of disclosure regarding its origin. Historical models, such as Deepbach, have demonstrated an ability to produce Bach chorales indistinguishable from authentic compositions. To evaluate the emotional resonance of AI-generated music, listeners typically identify intended emotions, categorizing them as "happy" or "sad," or employing dimensional models of emotion. As AI music technology advances, research has investigated whether judgements about the music differ based on beliefs about its authorship. However, findings have been inconclusive: Initial studies that manipulated participants' knowledge of the composer reported no significant bias in evaluations of live recordings of string quartet compositions produced by humans versus computers. Similarly, research by Zlatkov et al. indicated no bias against AI-generated music using pop music samples. Furthermore, consumer marketing research by Moura and Maw identified no discernible preference for music based on its origin, whether classical or pop.

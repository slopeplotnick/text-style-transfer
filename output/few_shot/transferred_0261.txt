To address these challenges, a shift from focusing solely on fixed outputs to examining real-time processing is necessary. The concept involves utilizing a set of foundational questions enabling any language model to generate responses instantaneously. This ensures that (1) fact-checking methodologies receive the necessary text for verification, (2) hallucination detection mechanisms gain insights into the model's internal processes during generation, and (3) the evaluation framework remains effective as models evolve. This concept forms the basis of UniFact. In response to the limitations of outdated benchmarks, this system is founded on three principal strategies to ensure robust, fair, and future-proof evaluations. Firstly, it eschews reliance on legacy datasets, instead employing a set of factual inquiries prompting models to generate real-time responses, thereby maintaining relevance for both contemporary and forthcoming models. Secondly, it facilitates equitable comparisons by supporting both hallucination detection and fact-checking approaches. It enables hallucination detection methods to monitor model activity during response generation and provides fact-checking methods with the generated text along with supplementary information for verification. Both methodologies are assessed on a unified task: determining the factual accuracy of the model’s output. Thirdly, it employs an automated system for output labeling, eschewing the slower and subjective nature of human reviews. This system evaluates the accuracy of model responses by comparing them to a correct answer and other trustworthy sources, thereby enabling the efficient, equitable, and consistent evaluation of numerous models.

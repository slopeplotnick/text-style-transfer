Researchers have investigated various methodologies to enhance models' reasoning capabilities and the verification of their procedural steps. For instance, certain studies emphasize deconstructing reasoning into incremental steps, meticulously validating each component, and employing reward models to augment mathematical reasoning (Smith et al., 2023; Jones and Williams, 2024). One particular study analyzed the effect of employing a verifier that evaluates the final solution, termed as GV-Gap, showing improvements, particularly when the solver and verifier constitute the same model (Doe et al., 2025). However, this study was limited to foundational models and excluded those with post-initial training.   Recent research has also explored scaling computational resources during verifier testing (Miller et al., 2024). For example, some studies examined the integration of random sampling with self-checking mechanisms, while others investigated the synergy of parallel sampling with error correction strategies (Taylor et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024). Additionally, comparative analyses of Self-Consistency versus generative verifiers have been conducted (Nguyen et al., 2025). Efforts to optimize computational resource allocation for verification during testing are ongoing, though verifiers remain imperfect—they may erroneously validate incorrect solutions, dismiss accurate reasoning, or select suboptimal answers (Appendix A).  Another significant research area pertains to models' self-improvement and self-assessment capabilities. While some researchers express skepticism, others have proposed theoretical frameworks and empirical evidence supporting models' enhancement through refined understanding (Anderson et al., 2024). A study focusing on smaller models highlighted the necessity of robust verifiers to facilitate their development (Lee et al., 2024). Practical approaches to self-improvement include incorporating feedback articulated in plain language and training models explicitly for error correction (Chen et al., 2025). Additional methodologies involve utilizing tools, such as code interpreters, to iteratively refine their responses (Patel et al., 2025).

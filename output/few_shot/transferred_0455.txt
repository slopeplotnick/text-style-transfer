We conducted extensive evaluations utilizing UniFact across various datasets, model types such as LLaMA and Qwen, and fact-checking methodologies. Our findings yield several key insights in response to our research questions. Firstly, regarding RQ1, our results indicate the absence of a singular optimal approach; performance is significantly contingent on the specific model and task. Secondly, addressing RQ2, robust evidence was found supporting the synergistic potential of different methodologies. Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV) methods excel in identifying distinct types of factual inaccuracies, demonstrating that they capture complementary aspects of factuality. Finally, concerning RQ3, we demonstrated that the integration of both methods surpasses their individual applications, establishing a novel benchmark for identifying factual errors. This suggests two implications: researchers should consider collaborative methodologies rather than isolated efforts, and practitioners should integrate model-based and evidence-based checks to ensure factual accuracy. In summary, this paper accomplishes several objectives: it examines the distinction between HD and FV and their significance for AI advancement; it introduces UniFact, a framework for fair comparison between HD and FV; it empirically demonstrates that HD and FV are distinct yet synergistically effective; and it establishes that integrating both methods outperforms single-method approaches. Additionally, we clarify the terminologies of Fact Verification and Hallucination Detection, emphasizing their importance despite common misconceptions. FV evaluates the overall truthfulness of text, whereas HD assesses the factual accuracy of model-generated text. Detailed explanations are provided in sections 2.1 and 2.2, with joint measurement methodologies outlined in section 2.3.

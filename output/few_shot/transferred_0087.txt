Upon closer examination of the results for each sample, it is evident that varying the resolution can occasionally yield superior outcomes compared to a fixed resolution of 112, as demonstrated in Figure 3. The process of cropping images at diverse resolutions and analyzing the semantic similarity maps elucidates this phenomenon. Specifically, when an object is divided into components, some segments exhibit higher semantic similarity, as quantified by VisRAG, while others display lower similarity. Post-filtering, only segments with high similarity are preserved, which disturbs the comprehensive representation of the object and impedes the Multi-Layered Language Models (MLLM) from making precise assessments. To address this issue, we propose a novel framework termed Multi-resolution Retrieval Detection (MRD). The core principle of MRD involves utilizing multiple resolutions across varied scales to enhance the identification of target object regions. This approach facilitates the system's ability to pinpoint accurate image crops corresponding to target objects, therefore minimizing distractions and aiding MLLMs in the interpretation of high-resolution images. As detailed in subsection 3.2, we posit that employing varying resolutions for semantic similarity computation is more effective for objects of differing sizes and locations. In light of this, we introduce a straightforward yet efficacious technique called Multi-resolution Semantic Fusion, which computes semantic similarity maps at diverse resolutions on a localized scale and amalgamates them to enhance similarity result accuracy. To streamline the detection of target objects, an open-vocabulary object detection model is employed, scanning the entire high-resolution image via a sliding window to assign confidence scores to potential object-containing regions. Integrating these confidence scores with the multi-resolution semantic similarity maps, our method not only facilitates more precise location of target areas but also distinguishes subtle differences between the crops within those regions. Consequently, this enhances the system's capability to discern essential elements of the image. Subsequent sections will present a detailed exposition of each component of this methodology.

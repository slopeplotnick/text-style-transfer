Nearswap, introduced by Goddard et al. (2024), is a methodological approach that strengthens interpolation when parameters are similar and weakens it when they differ. To evaluate the efficacy of various model configurations, three groups of large language models (LLMs) were selected for experimentation: GPT, LLAMA, and QWEN. The GPT group, developed by Radford et al. (2019) and Gao et al. (2020), includes GPT2-small, GPT2-medium, GPT2-large, GPT2-x1, and GPT-neo-2.7B. The LLAMA group, proposed by Touvron et al. (2023) and Dubey et al. (2024), comprises LLAMA-2-7B, LLAMA-3-8B, LLAMA-3.1-8B, LLAMA-3.2-1B, and LLAMA-3.2-3B. Lastly, the QWEN group, introduced by Qwen (2024), consists of QWEN2-0.5B, QWEN2-1.5B, and QWEN2-7B. These models, which are accessible on the Hugging Face platform (detailed links in Appendix A), were utilized alongside computational resources for ongoing pretraining (outlined in Appendix C) and specific hyperparameter settings (provided in Appendix D).   To investigate social biases in LLMs, three benchmarks were employed: BBQ, BOLD, and HONEST (dataset details available in Appendix E). The BBQ dataset (Parrish et al., 2022) includes approximately 58,000 question-answer pairs spanning nine sociocultural domains relevant to U.S. English-speaking populations. This dataset evaluates whether models rely on stereotypes rather than evidence by comparing “underspecified” and “fully specified” question versions. The BOLD dataset (Dhamala et al., 2021) comprises 23,679 prompts categorized into 43 demographic and sociopolitical groups, including job roles, gender, race, religion, and political affiliations.   To ensure that debiasing interventions do not compromise model performance on general natural language tasks, both pre-trained and debiased LLMs were evaluated on the SuperGLUE benchmark (Wang et al., 2019), which includes eight tasks: BoolQ, CB, COPA, MultiRC, ReCoRD, RTE, WiC, and WSC. All evaluations were conducted using the Language Model Evaluation Harness.

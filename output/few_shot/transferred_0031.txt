During the execution of benchmark tests, the system may exhibit noise due to extraneous background activities. To mitigate this, we disabled the Wi-Fi function to reduce the interference and collected multiple samples at various intervals to filter out the noise. The configuration of our statistical model is crucial for our research, although it may not be without flaws. We have made certain assumptions based on previous studies and our understanding of the data distribution, aiming to minimize personal bias within the model. However, as with any model, the chosen parameters and their distributions are open to critique. Our selections were informed by the principles governing the Java Virtual Machine (JVM) and programming language specifications, with a particular focus on Java Bytecode. The Gaussian distribution was employed given its prevalent occurrence in natural phenomena and its alignment with additive and equilibrium processes, as postulated by the central limit theorem. Limited knowledge about the measurements, apart from their mean and range, compels us to adopt this distribution to represent our uncertainties. Other considerations pertain predominantly to the system and JVM. The memory state prior to executing each bytecode pattern potentially influences outcomes; hence, we invoke the garbage collector before each test to standardize conditions. To circumvent issues arising from paging, caching, and concurrent processes, we conduct ten trials for each bytecode pattern, randomizing the order of executions.

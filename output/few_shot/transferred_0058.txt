The PF model provides a framework to analyze the temporal evolution of a multiphase system, aiming to achieve equilibrium. This model is particularly effective for small scales and brief time frames, rendering it advantageous for photovoltaic device fabrication such as solar cells. The energy characteristics of the system are encapsulated within the Gibbs free energy functional. Our approach investigates the formation processes of solar cells, emphasizing phenomena such as solvent evaporation, phase separation, and crystallization. The driving forces guiding the system towards equilibrium are derived from the Gibbs free energy functional. Phase changes, including drying and crystallization, are described by equations that also account for mass transport dynamics. During simulations, we ensured the model’s compatibility with a fluid dynamics framework that incorporates mass transport with flow. For a detailed methodology of our simulations, refer to Section S2. The material parameters obtained were utilized to analyze optical behavior in solar cells. Depending on the complexity of the solar cell architecture, either computational simulations or simplified analytical methods were employed. The solar cell is conceptualized as stratified material layers, and a model is applied to facilitate understanding of optical interactions. A neural network is employed to predict charge generation as a function of light incidence angle, active layer thickness, and light wavelength. Further details on this model can be found in Section S3.

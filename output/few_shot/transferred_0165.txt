We initially developed a foundational model utilizing solely the EMER dataset, which, while robust, lacks extensive guidance. The integration of additional data aligned with emotional content demonstrably enhances performance, underscoring the criticality of incorporating reliable emotional exemplars at the inception phase. Subsequently, we employed a method predicated on Modality Inference (MI) principles to restructure reasoning processes, enabling the model to prioritize emotion-centric cognition. This approach substantiates our hypothesis that MI-oriented cognitive structuring mitigates fixation on visual or textual inputs during the initial stages. Furthermore, we evaluated the efficacy of each proposed reward in maintaining stable and coherent reasoning. The Modality-Aligned Order Reward incentivizes the model to initiate reasoning processes with an MI-focused approach, reinstating the previously acquired structural framework. Concurrently, the Modality-Grounded Reasoning Reward ensures that the reasoning remains emotionally congruent and aligned with the designated target emotion. The synergistic application of both rewards yields optimal outcomes, reinforcing their complementary nature. The primary objective was to enhance the reliability of reasoning-based emotional comprehension in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by addressing early-stage reasoning deficits. We introduced the Modality Inference (MI) concept and proposed the Modality-Informed Guided Reasoning (MIGR) framework, a training paradigm designed to initiate and sustain reasoning from the emotion-relevant segment. Our bifurcated approach ensures that both learning processes are aligned with the emotion-focused component, resulting in more lucid, emotionally anchored, and meaningful explanations. Empirical evaluations on the MAFW and DFEW benchmarks indicate that MIGR significantly reduces emotionally inconsistent reasoning. In summary, MIGR enhances the credibility and comprehensibility of emotional reasoning, highlighting the significance of prioritizing core components in both data preparation and methodological enhancement.

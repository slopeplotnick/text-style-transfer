We investigated the impact of incorporating structured climate data on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. While the initial hypothesis was that integrating such data would enhance both accuracy and interpretability of generated responses, the experimental findings indicate that this approach remains nascent. As presented in Table 1, the DATA_RAG configuration, which combines literature and structured data, underperformed relative to the RAG baseline, which relies solely on literature, with the most significant decline observed in accuracy. This outcome highlights the current limitations of the model in leveraging structured climate data to enhance textual reasoning and evidential support.   CAIRNS, a question-answering (QA) system designed for agricultural climate adaptation, prioritizes interpretability, accuracy, and source transparency. It employs Structured Guided Prompts (SGP), multi-step reasoning, and a consistency-weighted Hybrid Evaluator. Empirical evaluations suggest that SGP improves structural organization and source reliability without compromising accuracy, marking progress toward generating domain-preferred QA outputs. However, the current efficacy of data-augmented retrieval remains limited. The Hybrid Evaluator, which uses inter-model agreement as a credibility weighting mechanism, demonstrates superior performance among automated evaluation techniques, achieving notable effectiveness without requiring fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. This makes it particularly well-suited for resource-constrained or expert-scarce QA scenarios.  Future research will focus on enhancing location-specific reasoning by incorporating granular geographic metadata, such as latitude and longitude, to facilitate document transferability across climatically similar regions and broaden the generalizability of literature-based insights. Additionally, we aim to develop a comprehensive evaluation benchmark encompassing diverse question types and topics. Such a benchmark would support the calibration of the Hybrid Evaluator and provide training data for future models, ultimately advancing reliable and reproducible QA research in climate adaptation.

Most existing systems store information either as isolated documents or discrete facts. However, financial prediction requires the storage of events in a temporal sequence to comprehend causal relationships over time. In stock prediction, it is essential to consider analogous past situations, as relying on the interpretation of a single document is insufficient. Traditional systems often experience limitations due to time-based forgetting or simplistic human memory models; such constraints hinder the retention of long-term critical patterns necessary for financial forecasting. To address these limitations, this study introduces a novel framework aimed at enhancing memory mechanisms for financial applications by constructing a detailed and dynamically updated repository of financial knowledge tailored for language models.  The proposed system converts market information into structured memory units that include timestamps, event types, and associated entities, such as companies and industries. By organizing market information into coherent sequences, this approach facilitates the identification and interpretation of patterns within financial data. Additionally, it incorporates a mechanism for retrieving comparable event sequences, surpassing the limitations of single-document analytics. By leveraging the current event sequence for retrieval, the system effectively identifies intricate historical patterns that align with ongoing market conditions. This transition from text-matching to pattern-recognition represents a significant advancement.  Empirical evaluations demonstrate that the proposed system achieves superior stock prediction performance compared to existing models. By modeling event sequences, it highlights the pivotal events influencing stock price fluctuations and enables analysis of their temporal dynamics. Consequently, the system enhances the interpretability and reliability of financial predictions, offering clearer insights into market behavior.

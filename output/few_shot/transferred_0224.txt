The current landscape lacks effective software solutions for logical decision-making processes. Software developers primarily aim to create products that cater to consumer demands, often prioritizing user-friendly functions over robust data-driven decision-making capabilities. The implementation of decision-making frameworks that effectively manage uncertainty often necessitates significant computational resources, such as those required for Monte Carlo methods, making them financially burdensome. Small exploration firms typically refrain from investing heavily in sophisticated cloud computing systems or employing specialized personnel. Predominantly, exploration activities are conducted by small-scale mining companies, which generally manage a limited number of sites and employ a small group of geologists. These entities primarily secure funding by demonstrating tangible successes, such as mineral discoveries from initial drilling, prioritizing immediate financial gains over scientific methodologies for informed decision-making. The endeavor to disprove geological hypotheses requires extensive data collection, an undertaking often at odds with the short-term objectives of smaller companies. This scenario underscores the necessity for innovative funding strategies. Relying exclusively on isolated drill hole success is akin to investing solely in a single stock; a diversified investment approach, common in financial markets to mitigate risk and optimize returns, should be emulated within the mining sector. The exact methodologies for achieving such diversification remain to be elucidated. Concurrently, the academic realm has undergone substantial transformations in recent years, notably with the advent of large language models, compelling educators to promptly adapt teaching methods, assessment tasks, and examination formats. These adjustments, arguably, were long overdue.

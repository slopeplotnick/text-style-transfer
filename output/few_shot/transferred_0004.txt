ReVSeg demonstrates exceptional performance in video object segmentation (VOS), surpassing competing methods on standard benchmarks. It outperforms both feature-based approaches and training-free methods, while also providing interpretable decision-making processes. As illustrated in Figure 1, the removal of the integrated reasoning steps results in diminished performance in challenging tasks for both the baseline model and the model trained with reinforcement learning (RL). However, our approach exhibits robust performance even prior to RL training, with further enhancements observed upon incorporating RL. The methodology involves three key innovations: firstly, the decomposition of the VOS reasoning process into distinct steps, utilizing fundamental model components to construct a reasoning chain; secondly, the development of a novel RL framework aimed at optimizing this reasoning chain, thereby enabling improved decision-making with minimal additional training data; thirdly, achieving a new benchmark in VOS performance and enhancing the transparency of the reasoning process. Recent advances in text-guided VOS [as discussed in references 3, 5, 29, 45] have been facilitated through fine-tuned models and specialized architectures. Nonetheless, significant challenges persist, particularly in complex scenarios involving numerous similar objects, rapid motion, or occluded objects. Emerging methodologies, such as CoT-RVS [13], attempt to address these challenges by employing dual systems to analyze each video frame and integrate them textually. Despite their efficacy, these approaches face limitations due to the lack of system integration and absence of training. Our approach reconceptualizes reasoning in VOS as a unified, stepwise process within a single system, enhancing it progressively through RL.

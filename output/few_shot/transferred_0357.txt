Our experimentation focused on evaluating the influence of various prompts on the degree to which a model adheres to its initial beliefs and its overall accuracy. A prompt conforming to prior beliefs tends to reinforce the model's initial stance, serving as a baseline for assessing belief persistence and model accuracy. Conversely, a critical-thinking prompt encourages the model to be more receptive to new evidence, facilitating belief revision. All prompts utilized are detailed in Appendix C. This section presents our findings. Firstly, we demonstrate that excessive adherence to initial beliefs, quantified by the Martingale Score, correlates with reduced accuracy, supporting the notion that belief entrenchment poses a significant challenge. Subsequently, we examined various models and configurations to identify factors contributing to belief entrenchment, including model type, prompts employed, and reasoning processes. Furthermore, the validity of employing large language models (LLMs) as evaluators was investigated. Our observations indicate that belief entrenchment is exacerbated in subjective domains, such as r/ChangeMyView, in contrast to more objective domains like Forecasting. This implies that belief entrenchment is more problematic in contexts where measuring model accuracy is challenging. We also explored altering the degree of belief adherence by employing the two prompt types. Our findings indicate that belief entrenchment was more pronounced with prior-conforming prompts and mitigated with critical-thinking prompts in the Forecasting domain, although this trend did not extend to r/ChangeMyView or OpenReview. Further insights on factors influencing belief entrenchment are provided in Appendix B. We also establish that belief entrenchment negatively impacts accuracy, as the objective of reasoning within LLMs is to ascertain truth. This was tested in domains with verifiable correct answers, such as Forecasting and OpenReview. While metrics like the Brier Score evaluate model performance, they do not elucidate the underlying reasons for performance levels, necessitating an examination of reasoning metrics. Our analysis revealed that higher Martingale Scores, indicative of increased belief entrenchment, are associated with poorer Brier Scores in the Forecasting domain, suggesting belief entrenchment as a contributing factor to reasoning failures in LLMs.

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), analogous to Large Language Models (LLMs), integrate visual inputs, thereby enriching semantic content beyond text and introducing additional safety challenges. The phenomenon of "jailbreaking" involves manipulating inputs to bypass established safety protocols, enabling models to generate potentially harmful outputs. This issue is critical for enhancing the safety and reliability of these models. Historically, jailbreaking predominantly targeted LLMs via techniques like deceptive prompting or role-playing. With the advent of MLLMs, adversaries exploit visual features to compromise model integrity. Current methodologies exhibit limitations: one approach involves crafting deceptive images or embedding sensitive information within visuals, which often fails to leverage full contextual richness; another relies on textual descriptions akin to LLM strategies, insufficiently utilizing the potential of visual data. To address these deficiencies, we introduce the Contextual-Image Attack (CIA), a method that synthesizes images embedding both situational context and malicious intent, supplemented by textual elements. For instance, rather than merely inscribing "How can someone make illegal drugs?" within an image, we depict a scenario where a teacher poses this query in a chemistry class, thereby obfuscating malicious intentions and confusing model detection mechanisms. Alternative methodologies, such as FigStep, typically superimpose textual prompts onto images or solely depend on verbal context descriptions. Our approach surpasses these by harnessing visual representations more effectively. This advancement gains significance as image generation technologies progress in producing realistic scenarios, and novel techniques emerge to convert text into visual data, demonstrating the capability of images to deceive models by encapsulating complex, covert meanings.

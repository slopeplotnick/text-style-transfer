Stylized Motion Policies (SMP) is a framework designed to facilitate the training of motion policies by providing rewards based on stylistic attributes, without requiring access to the original motion data. This enables the use of various methodologies to adjust the reward model, thereby influencing the motion dynamics. However, it should be noted that these adjustments may occasionally result in movements that appear unnatural. A comparative analysis of different methods is demonstrated in a supplementary video. Additionally, SMP allows for the synthesis of novel motion styles by modifying the outputs of the pretrained model, enabling the creation of previously unavailable styles without necessitating retraining. For instance, as illustrated in Figure 6, it is feasible to intermix two distinct styles, such as "AeroPlane" and "HighKnees," to generate a new composite style through the blending of model predictions. A single pretrained motion model can be repurposed to develop policies for diverse tasks, including steering, target acquisition, or dodgeball, as detailed in Table 2. The motion model is initially trained on a subset of the LaFAN1 dataset, which comprises randomly sampled running movements. The resulting policies demonstrate effective task performance while preserving naturalistic motion characteristics. As depicted in Figures 5(a) and 5(b), the character is able to transition between different movements seamlessly, maintaining task continuity alongside a natural appearance. For example, the steering policy directs the character to jog backward when oriented incorrectly with respect to the target direction and to jog forward when appropriately aligned, also accommodating side-steps and cross-steps without the need for distinct motion planning. In the Target Location task, the policy developed using SMP successfully devises human-like movements, such as automatically decelerating when necessary.

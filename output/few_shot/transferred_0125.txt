Empirical evidence underscores the efficacy of reasoning-based guardrails in enhancing the reliability and interpretability of moderation systems, representing a significant advancement toward developing safe artificial intelligence frameworks. This work introduces OMNI-GUARD, a suite of models explicitly designed to manage safety checks across multimodal inputs, including text, images, videos, and audio, leveraging reasoning-based mechanisms. Additionally, a novel training methodology has been developed to improve the models' capacity for explaining safety-related decisions. Experimental results indicate that OMNI-GUARD achieves high accuracy, robust generalization, and enhanced interpretability relative to previous approaches.  The advent of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has catalyzed interest in omni-modal language models (OLLMs), which are capable of processing and generating diverse types of data simultaneously. Unlike traditional models employing modular architectures, OLLMs are trained on integrated datasets, enabling a unified understanding of text, audio, images, and videos. These systems convert heterogeneous inputs into a common representation space, facilitating cross-modal reasoning and generation. Prominent examples include Qwen2.5-Omni and LLaMA-Omni, which demonstrate real-time generation of text and speech. Notably, NExT-OMNI extends these capabilities further by enabling comprehensive processing and generation for any data modality. However, challenges persist in ensuring safety, as parameter-related vulnerabilities in these models may result in unintended and potentially harmful behaviors.

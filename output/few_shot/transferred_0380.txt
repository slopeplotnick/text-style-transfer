In the process of enhancing chatbots utilizing language models, it is imperative to consider their capacity to accommodate diverse linguistic expressions, not only to ensure equity but also to enhance practical performance. Although advancements have been made in voice-based language models, challenges persist regarding accents, dialects, and sociolects (Kumar et al., 2023). Speech models have historically struggled with variations in accent and dialect (Jones et al., 2022; Smith et al., 2023). Despite their proficiency, language models still exhibit biases stemming from their training datasets (Gonzalez et al., 2024). These models often prioritize prevalent accents or languages with abundant data, resulting in suboptimal performance for individuals with less common accents (Li et al., 2023). To address these issues, it is essential to develop models capable of accommodating diverse pronunciation and linguistic patterns (Chen et al., 2023). Ongoing research on adaptive model training and intelligent data grouping holds potential in enabling models to handle unfamiliar accents, even with limited data availability (Wang et al., 2025). Traditional evaluation metrics, such as word error rates, are insufficient in capturing model performance across diverse user groups (Miller et al., 2023). Improved assessment methodologies are required to evaluate fairness and model efficacy across various accents, languages, and demographics (Lee et al., 2024). Furthermore, error analysis should extend beyond mere quantification; it should examine the disproportionate impact of biases on different user groups, thereby fostering fairness (Johnson et al., 2023). Linguistic variability within a single language can significantly differ based on factors such as age, gender, social class, or occupation (Nguyen et al., 2023). Older speech models often disproportionately emphasize homogenous data, predominantly reflecting dominant linguistic groups (Patel et al., 2024). Addressing this necessitates not only augmenting data diversity but also developing advanced tools that enable models to comprehend various speech styles and cues (Zhang et al., 2023). Attention to elements such as tone, rhythm, and emotional nuances is critical for accurately interpreting social and emotional communication (Anderson et al., 2023).

The tool library encompasses several functionalities: a tool for identifying specific regions, a tool for planning region analysis employing the method "Think with Image," a tool for generating reports utilizing templates, and a tool for assessing the quality of radiology reports, providing feedback for enhancement. These tools facilitate the comprehensive process of creating automated radiology reports. Figure 2 illustrates the operational framework of the Radiologist Copilot. To evaluate the effectiveness of our Radiologist Copilot, we employ a liver radiology reporting task utilizing the public 3D CT dataset AMOS-MM, which comprises over 2,000 CT scans accompanied by detailed radiology reports. Specifically, 1,287 scans with reports are allocated for training, while 400 scans with reports are designated for validation. The liver task focuses on liver-specific reports within the AMOS-MM dataset, yielding 1,149 CT scans with liver reports for training and 367 CT scans with liver reports for validation. The validation set assesses the method's efficacy, whereas the training set facilitates report analysis. Initially, the BioBERT tool is utilized to transform the reports into data points, which are subsequently clustered using K-means clustering to formulate template reports. These templates are decomposed into liver-related components such as liver surface, liver tissue, bile ducts, and liver pathologies, utilized by the Analyzer Tool. The Radiologist Copilot is constructed using the OctoTools system and operates on Python 3.10.18 with NVIDIA L20 GPUs. It requires no additional training as it utilizes pre-trained models. The primary reasoning tool employed is Qwen3-32B, while the Segmentator Tool utilizes the TotalSegmentator model for region identification. The Analyzer Tool employs the 3D medical model Hulu-Med for CT scan analysis. The system is configured to undertake a maximum of 10 steps, with an allocated time of up to 500 seconds per case.

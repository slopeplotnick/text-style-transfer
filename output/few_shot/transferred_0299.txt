We employ OmniFusion to facilitate live translation by retrieving relevant lecture slides from audio clips, and further develop an open-source system to enable the translation of images. This system is designed to support future advancements in translating entire images or slides. Our work demonstrates that incorporating images into translation pipelines enhances language processing performance across a variety of language models. To evaluate this, we examine several OCR models and assess their efficacy in translating images through both unimodal and multimodal approaches.   To promote accessibility in lectures across diverse languages and formats, we design systems capable of translating both audio recordings and slides. The proposed method integrates such translations into broader language processing tasks. Specifically, we translate slides by transforming the embedded text and visual elements into the target language while preserving their original layout. Additionally, we introduce a PDF viewer that presents slides with captions synchronized to the speaker’s content, enabling users to follow along with the translations seamlessly. This system aligns slide content with corresponding audio segments, offering essential contextual information for translation.  Our methodology encompasses the alignment of slide images with audio clips, leveraging these translations for downstream tasks. Furthermore, we detail the processes for extracting, translating, and re-creating text embedded within slide images. Appendix A.1 provides additional information on Text-to-Speech systems. While some translation systems support multiple languages, they often fall short for live lecture scenarios due to their offline-oriented designs and inability to adapt to real-time translation queues. Existing systems either fail to incorporate slide content or lack multilingual functionality, emphasizing the necessity of a comprehensive approach for live lecture translation systems.

This study investigates the application of robotics within the construction industry. To evaluate the efficacy of the proposed system, the authors conduct extensive testing against established methodologies. The research leverages concepts from robotic modeling and construction principles to assess the comparative performance of Large Language Model (LLM)-based reasoning versus traditional methods in the context of task allocation for construction robots. The LTAA system incorporates models that delineate the capabilities of individual robots, strategies adaptive to various phases, and LLM-driven decision-making to address specific construction coordination challenges identified in previous studies. This framework is compared to conventional methods such as Brute Force and Greedy Method, as well as learning approaches like Q-learning and Deep Q-Network. The study presents a robust LLM coordination system and elucidates the comparative efficacy of reasoning-based methods relative to traditional frameworks for automating construction tasks. The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews prior research on multi-robot task allocation employing optimization, learning, and LLM methodologies. Section 3 provides a detailed explanation of the LTAA system. Section 4 outlines the experimental setup. Section 5 presents a comparative analysis of results with traditional methodologies. Section 6 concludes with insights and prospects for future research. The Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) theory underpins the sharing of tasks among robot teams, accounting for resource constraints, temporal requirements, and varying robot capabilities. Gerkey and Matarić (2004) established a foundational framework for comprehending task allocation challenges, considering factors such as single versus multiple task execution by robots, as well as single versus multiple robot task requirements, and the distinction between instantaneous and time-extended tasks. However, their framework did not fully address the complexities of real-world scenarios involving diverse robot capabilities and temporal constraints. Consequently, in 2013, Korsah et al. introduced an enhanced framework incorporating diverse robot skills, task interdependencies, and dynamic constraints, reflecting the field's progression towards utilizing heterogeneous teams for complex task execution.

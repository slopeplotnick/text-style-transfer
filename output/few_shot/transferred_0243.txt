The study seeks to elucidate the factors contributing to the effectiveness of e-commerce search and to identify the tools necessary for further enhancement. E-commerce search fundamentally involves aligning consumer queries with appropriate products. This alignment is optimized by evaluating the degree of correspondence between a search query and a product, examining both explicit factors, such as attributes and constraints, and subjective factors, such as user preferences. To systematically analyze search intent, consumer queries are categorized into 18 distinct dimensions, including type, brand, style, and others. For each query-product pair, the alignment quality is assessed to determine the relevance of the match, providing a framework for understanding and evaluating relevance in this context.   Relevance in e-commerce search is measured through three primary components: product understanding, search understanding, and relevance assessment. Relevance assessment, in particular, focuses on two critical aspects of the product: its attributes and its presentation. Attributes are further classified into two types: inherent and derived. Inherent attributes represent easily identifiable characteristics, such as category, brand, model, and price, which are typically straightforward to align with search queries. Conversely, derived attributes, which are inferred from inherent attributes, pertain to functional or experiential qualities of the product. These derived attributes often require deeper cognitive processing and reasoning to interpret, as they vary significantly across industries due to domain-specific terminology and conceptual frameworks.

The Self-Taught Multimodal Reasoner (STL) is a framework designed to enhance model performance through self-generated explanations, leveraging the model’s pre-existing knowledge. A significant research question concerns the optimal structuring of these explanations. Effective visual question answering necessitates the model's ability to observe and reason with clarity. STL addresses this by employing a methodology where the model initially describes the visual content, subsequently reflects upon it, and ultimately produces an answer. This process mirrors natural human cognition, facilitating improved organization of the model’s reasoning processes.  Relying solely on explanations derived from correct answers is limited, as it exposes the model only to successful reasoning paradigms, posing challenges for complex tasks that require distinguishing between valid and flawed reasoning. To address this limitation, our approach incorporates explanations for incorrect answers, akin to human learning from both successes and failures. These explanations reveal erroneous thinking patterns, enabling the model to differentiate between correct and incorrect answers, thus mitigating errors such as conjecturing or making unfounded assumptions.  This concept is implemented in the Self-Taught Multimodal Reasoner (STL), wherein the model learns to generate both accurate and inaccurate explanations, refining its capabilities through training on these varied outputs. Empirical evaluations across domains such as common sense reasoning, scientific inquiry, and language processing demonstrate that STL outperforms models trained solely on final answers and approaches the efficacy of models trained with human-derived explanations, as evidenced in Table 4. Despite variability in results across different datasets, these findings suggest that STL constitutes a viable alternative to methodologies reliant on extensive human explanations.

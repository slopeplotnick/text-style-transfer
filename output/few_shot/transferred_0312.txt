The performance of models varies across different topics. As illustrated in Table 2, models tend to exhibit lower performance on topics such as Religion and Sport, with scores ranging from 7.8 to 8.2. Conversely, they perform better on topics related to Game and Lifestyle, often achieving scores above 8.4. This indicates that models exhibit increased caution with sensitive topics like religion and sports. In contrast, gaming and lifestyle topics are more straightforward for the models, likely due to their neutrality and comprehensive representation within training data, which enables smoother handling. This implies that enhancing fundamental skills such as context comprehension and balanced decision-making can aid performance across various evaluation tasks. Future research should explore strategies to integrate these skills more effectively to augment model capabilities in language interpretation and control. The findings in Table 3 indicate that mediation impacts different aspects of language variably. It attenuates toxicity and reduces the use of exclamations, resulting in more neutral and composed responses, thereby fostering more balanced and less emotionally charged conversations. However, mediation does not significantly alter argumentativeness, suggesting its limited effect on how models handle disagreements. Notably, models behave diversely when simulating both sides of a conversation in contrast to interacting with real users, underscoring the necessity of assessing mediation based on both procedural outcomes and real-world implications.

Adopting a Software Product Line (SPL) approach necessitates significant organizational transformation. The complexity of software product management is well-documented, as evidenced by a survey conducted by Springer and Miler. SPL implementation emphasizes collaborative efforts among teams, fostering the use of shared tools and resources across multiple projects. This approach facilitates resource optimization, accelerates time-to-market, and enables teams to concentrate on innovation. Key practices such as component reuse, configuration-based customization, and standardized documentation contribute to operational efficiency and streamlined processes. However, transitioning to SPL requires meticulous planning and management to ensure its successful adoption, ultimately enhancing organizational cohesion and adaptability.  At ITK, the SPL migration was conducted collaboratively with the development team to ensure their active involvement and informed participation throughout the process. Regular consultations were held to align the migration strategy with the team’s feedback. The ITK initiative was benchmarked against existing reports on SPL adoption to compare methodologies and strategies. Notably, ITK's implementation adhered to the IDEAL improvement model, initiating the project with an assessment of existing processes and organizational readiness. Subsequent phases involved formulating a strategic action plan, executing it, and employing iterative agile practices to refine the process based on outcomes. These guidelines served as a foundation for structuring the action plan.  While the ITK case study aligned with broader SPL adoption findings, certain challenges deviated from commonly reported issues. Specifically, ITK did not encounter challenges related to insufficient skilled personnel or lack of management support, as these were not perceived as barriers within the organizational culture. This divergence underscores the importance of contextualizing SPL strategies to the specific needs and mindset of the organization.

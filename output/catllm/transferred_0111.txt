For both solvers and verifiers, a generation setup was employed with a temperature of 0.7, top-p at 0.9, and a maximum output length of 8192 tokens. Outputs lacking a boxed answer were excluded from further analysis. All experimental procedures were conducted utilizing vLLM on H200 GPUs. Additional details regarding the prompts and filtering processes can be found in Appendix F. Regarding the performance of the solvers, all 37 models were evaluated across nine datasets, with their performance averaged across tasks, as illustrated in Figure 1, while detailed task-specific results are provided in Appendix G. In general, models with larger capacities demonstrated superior performance, with those from the Qwen3 and DeepSeek groups emerging as top performers. Conversely, Llama3-Base exhibited lower performance due to an apparent unfamiliarity with the question-answering format. Within the respective groups, Qwen2.5-Base and DeepSeek models displayed notable improvements as scaling increased, and similar trends of enhanced performance with scaling were observed across other groups. Subsequently, an analysis was conducted to evaluate the correspondence between solver performance and verifier performance, as depicted in Figure 2. For all 21 post-trained models and each dataset, the effectiveness of the verifiers was assessed on the same solver models, with measurements including verifier accuracy, F1-score, precision, false positive rate (FPR), false negative rate (FNR), and gain for each solver-verifier pairing. For each verifier, these metrics were categorized into three distinct verification setups, with averages computed across solvers and datasets within each setup. Verifier accuracy generally improved in alignment with the solver accuracy of the respective verifier; however, other metrics exhibited more intricate patterns. For instance, FPR increased during self-verification and when verifying models from the same group, yet decreased slightly when verifying models from different groups. These findings suggest that verifiers possessing higher solver proficiency are more inclined to erroneously classify their own outputs or outputs from their group as correct. Further details regarding F1-score and precision are available in Appendix H.

Agents are observed to detect changes in behavioral patterns or disruptions in coordination, subsequently proposing modifications to established norms to maintain either stability or diversity within the system. Furthermore, agents evaluate the adherence of others to established rules and adjust their levels of trust or willingness to engage, thereby fostering a decentralized reputation system. Specifically, distinct roles are allocated to agents, encompassing rule creation, interpretation, and task execution, ensuring that authority is not centralized. This conceptual framework emphasizes the requirements for internal management rather than prescribing specific implementation methodologies. Future research endeavors aim to integrate these principles with existing systems and evaluate their efficacy in controlled experimental environments. Institutional AI is designed to sustain system stability amidst ongoing changes rather than achieving complete autonomy, with safety being embedded as a continuous process involving the monitoring and reinforcement of rules rather than a post-operational assessment. Moreover, agents contribute to the formulation of rules that govern their behavior, facilitating self-correction in scenarios where external intervention is delayed. These configurations are intended to complement human oversight rather than replace it, distributing evaluative tasks within the system to enhance traceability, foster diverse perspectives, and mitigate risks identified in the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon framework. Consequently, the objective is to optimize the collaborative functionality of model networks, improve interpretability, and uphold consistency without necessitating persistent external supervision. The Institutional AI paradigm expands upon the foundational principles outlined in this paper, contrasting with the ESRH framework by focusing on risk mitigation strategies rather than risk emergence. The present study provides an introductory exposition of these concepts, with subsequent research anticipated to elaborate on theoretical refinements and empirical validations, which will be disseminated through forthcoming publications and preprints on arXiv. Planned investigations will aim to systematically evaluate the practical implications and performance of these ideas.

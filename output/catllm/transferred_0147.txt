The dataset construction and validation were undertaken with meticulous attention to detail by a cohort of TCM specialists. Samples were derived from clinical cases, historical case records, and examination questions, subsequently undergoing a rigorous cleaning process to eliminate duplicates and ensure the absence of personal information. The annotation of data was executed through a tripartite method, initially performed by the experts themselves, followed by peer review, and concluding with scrutiny by an independent entity. The resultant benchmark, designated as TCM-BEST4SDT, encompasses 600 questions across four domains: TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, LLM Content Safety, and SDT. To ascertain the efficacy of models within authentic TCM clinical scenarios, three evaluative methodologies were devised. The selected-response evaluation method involves multiple assessments of each question, with answer choices shuffled, and scoring varies according to question type. The judge model evaluation employs an LLM with expert-generated prompts to assess model responses. Additionally, a reward model evaluation was developed to quantify the congruence between prescriptions and syndromes. TCM-BEST4SDT comprises two task categories: SDT tasks and general evaluation tasks. SDT tasks utilize data from clinical cases and historical case records, whereas general tasks encompass TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, and LLM Content Safety. Questions pertaining to TCM Basic Knowledge are sourced from public examinations such as the National Qualification Exam for Medical Practitioners, the National Postgraduate Entrance Exam: Comprehensive Clinical Medicine (TCM Integrated), and the Chinese Herbal Medicine Title Exam. Medical Ethics inquiries are drawn from reputable sources and have been manually annotated by experts to address specific scenarios. Furthermore, LLM Content Safety questions were independently crafted by experts, tailored to practical requirements.

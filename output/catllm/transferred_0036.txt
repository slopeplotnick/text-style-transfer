Negative samples encompass all potential active nodes, and it is elaborated in Section 5 that the combined utilization of random and adversarial negatives is essential for achieving performance surpassing that of TextMatch. Contrastive loss refers to the process of training the model to accurately select the optimal positive candidate for each query from the available options within the batch. The encoder employed in this study is initialized from publicly accessible E5-unsupervised checkpoints, which comprise 33 million and 330 million parameters, respectively, and are developed through advanced contrastive pretraining methodologies. Identical encoder weights are utilized for both queries and candidates to ensure consistency. Prior to fine-tuning, dense interaction data encompassing 110 million samples derived from weak signals, such as clicks or saves, as well as naturally occurring pairs of titles and descriptions, are incorporated. Fine-tuning subsequently involves a reduced yet higher-quality set of interactions indicative of mutual interest, including interviews or hires, alongside hard negatives identified from items presented but not interacted with, as recorded in interaction logs. The training dataset for GraphMatch is constructed from one year of user interaction data sourced from Upwork, divided into 10 months for training, one month for validation, and one month for evaluation. The dataset is structured with nodes representing freelancers, clients, and job posts, and edges denoting interactions such as job postings, applications, invitations, interviews, or contract initiations. Freelancer and job post nodes are characterized by text features, including profile overviews and job descriptions, while all nodes are further supplemented with numerical features specific to their respective types, such as freelancer hourly rates, client locations, and job post categories. These text and numerical features are subject to temporal variation. In aggregate, the dataset comprises approximately 9 million nodes, 32 million feature versions, and 62 million edges. GraphMatch is trained utilizing robust labels derived from contracts established between freelancers and job posts.

Recent advances in the integration of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) have markedly enhanced the capability of large language models to manage tasks necessitating extensive knowledge bases. However, the predominant focus of most systems remains on general topics or news, which undermines their effectiveness in addressing critical challenges such as assisting agricultural sectors in coping with climate change. Although some contemporary studies have attempted to employ document retrieval and climate data to address specific inquiries, these endeavors often rely on rudimentary methodologies that fail to deliver detailed or verified answers tailored to particular domains. While efforts have been made to develop geography-aware question answering systems in diverse fields, these systems are not inherently designed to contemplate climate-specific issues. Furthermore, existing research tends to segregate climate data from scholarly research, thereby overlooking the benefits of an integrated approach. The CAIRNS process, structured into four pivotal steps, initially acquires climate information from Elsevier and a climate data API. Subsequently, answers are generated through the ScholarGuide Prompt, followed by a comprehensive verification and evaluation phase utilizing a mixed-methods approach. To ensure the retrieval of pertinent documents, a novel retriever has been developed that integrates both lexical matching and semantic-based retrieval. Nonetheless, the mere application of these techniques is insufficient, as the geographical context is essential in climate-related inquiries. Therefore, a location similarity score is incorporated to ensure that responses are pertinent in both semantic and locational dimensions. CAIRNS employs an iterative cycle wherein evidence is gathered, answers are generated, and their sufficiency is assessed. Should any information be deemed inadequate, the system formulates supplementary questions and revisits the information retrieval process until satisfactory answers are achieved. The ScholarGuide Prompt serves as an integral tool in the answer generation process within CAIRNS, facilitating a balance between readability and factual accuracy. Specifically, for climate-related questions, this prompt provides a structured framework that guides the language model in answer composition, employs scientific reasoning, and offers precise recommendations.

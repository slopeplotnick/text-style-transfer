The efficacy of ViSAudio in generating spatial audio from video content surpasses that of alternative methodologies, as evidenced by comparative analyses across various datasets, illustrated in Table 2. This investigation encompassed a juxtaposition with leading approaches, the specifics of which are elucidated in Section 5.1. For the BiAudio and MUSIC test sets, both video and caption data were utilized; however, in the case of FAIR-Play, which lacks caption availability, solely video data was employed. Models incapable of processing textual input were similarly assessed using only video data. The findings presented in Table 2 indicate that ViSAudio exhibits superior performance overall, notably in terms of auditory quality, the spatial distribution of sound across channels, audio-video synchronization, and the preservation of scene semantics. Such outcomes demonstrate the model's proficiency in generating authentic binaural audio while maintaining congruence between sound and visual elements across diverse acoustic environments. Furthermore, its effectiveness on the FAIR-Play dataset, despite not being trained on it, attests to its adaptability in novel scenarios. Figure 3 offers a comparative analysis of spatial audio production methods from video inputs, with one illustration depicting an individual playing the sitar as the camera transitions from left to right, thereby causing a perceived auditory shift from right to left. The spectrograms of audio generated by ViSAudio, real audio, and other techniques were scrutinized, revealing that ViSAudio's outputs most accurately correspond to real audio and correctly demonstrate the auditory movement. For instance, when auditory stimuli are positioned to the left, the left channel exhibits increased amplitude, reflecting realistic auditory perception. Other approaches failed to accurately capture the rhythm or differentiate between channel outputs.

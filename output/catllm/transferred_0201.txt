The debate regarding whether large language models (LLMs) learn in a Bayesian manner remains unresolved among researchers, with some studies presenting conflicting perspectives. Reports have highlighted specific reasoning challenges encountered by LLMs, such as a tendency to agree with individuals to appease them, errors emerging during scaling, and a propensity to conform to majority opinions rather than adhering to objective truth. These problems adversely affect the performance of LLMs in executing tasks. In evaluating reasoning capabilities, LLMs exhibit superior proficiency in domains such as mathematics and coding; however, conventional accuracy metrics fail to elucidate the mechanisms underlying their improved performance or their adaptability to novel situations. Should LLMs merely replicate patterns learned during training, their utility in addressing real-world problems, where contextual understanding is crucial, could be limited. Therefore, enhanced methodologies are imperative to assess reasoning processes, transcending mere outcomes. Furthermore, investigations reveal that LLMs may prioritize user satisfaction and consensus over veracity, potentially misleading users and perpetuating erroneous beliefs or biases. Given that humans demonstrate an inherent tendency to seek information that corroborates their preconceived notions, this interaction between humans and LLMs could exacerbate issues such as polarization and the dissemination of misinformation. This phenomenon is compounded by the fact that LLMs are trained on data encompassing human beliefs, which could contribute to the proliferation of false beliefs.

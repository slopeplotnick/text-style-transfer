The research conducted by Galhotra et al. (2021) addresses the challenge of determining probabilities in scenarios where comprehensive system details are absent. Specifically, they investigate the application of particular models to derive ranges for queries concerning potential alternative outcomes. This approach facilitates the examination of specific model components in instances of incomplete information, potentially resulting in narrower ranges compared to purely mathematical calculations. Furthermore, the evaluation of methods for explaining artificial intelligence (AI) is considered through the examination of necessity and sufficiency of certain elements. Mothilal et al. (2021) propose that the optimal strategy for elucidating a model involves identifying the smallest set of critical features requisite and sufficient for achieving a result in a given context. Their analysis presumes that the sole factors for explanation are the input features, which are considered independent. Various AI explanation types are compared, with necessity linked to explanations that demonstrate requisite changes for the initial result, while sufficiency pertains to the significance of features in causing the outcome irrespective of other variables. It is demonstrated that practical variations exist among different methods. Pawar et al. (2024), also operating under the assumption of feature independence, utilize necessity and sufficiency to evaluate AI methodologies and explore diverse approaches for generating explanatory examples. Beckers (2022) examines circumstances where a model forecasts outcomes based on actions, with the presupposition of model accuracy. Explanations are defined as either sufficient or as examining alternative potentialities, assuming consistent predictive correctness by the model. Additionally, explanations are dissected by assessing the influence of causative factors to discern authentic reasons behind explanations.

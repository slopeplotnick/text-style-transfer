Traditional methodologies aimed at optimizing construction coordination are characterized by inherent limitations, particularly the requirement for a comprehensive plan from the outset. However, construction projects are often subject to significant variability in tasks, conditions, and resources, rendering such methodologies less effective in dynamic environments. Moreover, these approaches exhibit considerable complexity, particularly when applied to large-scale projects involving numerous robotic systems and interconnected tasks. Consequently, researchers have increasingly explored learning-based methodologies capable of adapting to evolving project requirements without necessitating pre-defined plans. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a prominent approach, enabling robotic agents to acquire collaborative strategies through environmental interactions. This adaptive framework facilitates learning by iterative experimentation and adjustment to dynamic changes. Nevertheless, RL necessitates extensive practice, which poses substantial risks in construction contexts due to the potential for hazardous outcomes impacting worker safety and equipment integrity. Furthermore, the prolonged training durations associated with RL are incompatible with the stringent timelines characteristic of construction projects. In response to these challenges, domain-specific RL methodologies have been developed to address construction-specific requirements. For instance, Lee et al. (2022) proposed an advanced framework integrating digital twins with Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to optimize robotic task coordination in construction environments. Their approach harnessed Building Information Modeling (BIM) in conjunction with DRL to make informed coordination decisions, incorporating spatial, temporal, and safety dimensions. The utilization of digital twins facilitated safe learning through simulations prior to real-world implementation. Despite these advancements, RL continues to encounter challenges, particularly regarding the reliability of simulation-trained models in real-world scenarios. The discrepancies between simulated environments and actual construction sites often result in safety concerns and reduced applicability of learned strategies under varying conditions. These persistent limitations have necessitated the development of more sophisticated RL techniques to address the complexities associated with construction project coordination.

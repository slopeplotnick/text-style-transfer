The RAIR benchmark comprises 63,601 samples derived from 14 distinct e-commerce domains and is divided into three components: the General section consisting of 46,775 samples, the Long-Tail Hard section containing 10,931 samples, and the Visual Salience section incorporating 5,895 samples. For each dataset entry, comprehensive information is provided, including the query, item title, item details, SKU information, and an anonymized item image. Furthermore, each entry is associated with a manually assigned ground truth label determined based on a four-level relevance scale ranging from L1 to L4, with Table 8 illustrating the distribution of these labels across the dataset. To facilitate model development and error analysis, rule identifiers from the established Rule Checklist have been integrated into each data entry to assist in assessing relevance. In the current section, the proposed methods are systematically evaluated and examined using the RAIR benchmark. The experimental procedure is structured into two components: initially, quantitative analyses are conducted to address two primary questions, namely (1) the extent of model improvement achieved through fine-tuning and (2) the advantages offered by the LORE model across various dataset segments, including general, hard, and visually salient samples. Subsequently, illustrative examples are presented to highlight novel capabilities introduced by the proposed methodologies as well as the specific challenges they address. To ensure stability and reproducibility in evaluation outcomes, all trained models employed a deterministic decoding mechanism for single-pass inference, thereby precluding the inclusion of randomness.

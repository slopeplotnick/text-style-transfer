Large language models (LLMs) exhibit sensitivity to minor variations in input, such as prompt phrasing and linguistic choices, which enhances their utility for evaluating performance across different languages by modifying prompts. The evaluation of LLMs was conducted through three distinct testing frameworks encompassing diverse domains: MMLU-Pro, designed to assess knowledge and reasoning across 14 academic subjects; MATH500, consisting of 500 complex mathematical problems; and UniMoral, a collection of questions necessitating nuanced social reasoning to evaluate moral judgment. These assessments were implemented in five languages—English, Chinese, Spanish, French, and Hindi—selected due to their representation of diverse linguistic families, geographical distributions, and disparities in resource availability. In multilingual tasks, a critical consideration pertains to the optimal language for system prompts, with the choice between English, often the predominant training language, and the language corresponding to the task being examined through two experimental configurations. The English-prompt setup involved utilizing English prompts for questions in various languages, whereas the same-language setup entailed translating the prompts into the language of the respective question. To elucidate the interrelation of different dimensions of model behavior, four performance metrics were analyzed in conjunction, employing regression analysis to investigate the impact of prompt characteristics and language settings on multilingual performance measures. The findings, illustrated in Figure 4, revealed that the components of system prompts exert distinct influences on model performance, categorized into three primary types. Firstly, certain prompt elements demonstrated universally positive effects on model behavior; for example, Chain-of-Thought, emotion, and scenario components were associated with enhancements in accuracy and consistency, alongside reductions in accuracy disparities across languages, thereby indicating their efficacy in promoting consistent and robust performance across linguistic contexts. A detailed examination of model outputs further indicated that these elements facilitated more structured and coherent reasoning, contributing to the observed improvements in performance.

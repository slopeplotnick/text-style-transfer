A scale comprising nine questions on a seven-point scale was developed to assess emotions such as Shame, Guilt, and Impostor Syndrome in the context of AI utilization. Empirical data was obtained through an online survey conducted on Prolific from the evening of February 3, 2025, to late night February 4, 2025, incorporating these questions alongside additional tests to evaluate the effectiveness of the scale. Upon collection of this real-world data, comparisons were made with a simulated dataset generated using the methodology from a prior study. The comparative analysis of demographic characteristics between the two datasets is presented in Table 1. Further elaboration on the SAGAT scale will be provided in a subsequent article, following the completion of the conventional scale development process, which integrated elements from the experimental method employed. The Mann-Whitney U test was utilized to compare real and simulated data, revealing no significant difference in Impostor Syndrome scores, whereas substantial differences were observed in the scores for Shame and Guilt. Additionally, Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests indicated pronounced differences across all three emotional dimensions, findings which do not corroborate Hypothesis 4. Figure 4 depicts histograms illustrating the comparative analysis of real and simulated data. To ensure the validity and reliability of the findings, a further study was conducted to develop a new scale aimed at measuring anxious beliefs related to AI. This scale was created utilizing the same methodology as Study 3, detailed in the "Methods" section of that study. For comprehensive insights, the process of scale development with simulated data is documented in the Supplementary Materials and preregistration on OSF, accessible via the provided link.

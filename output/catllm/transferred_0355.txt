A comprehensive series of tests were conducted utilizing various benchmarks, such as the evaluation of machine translation (WMT23) and reward models (Reward Bench 2), to ascertain the effectiveness of our proposed method of vote distribution adjustment. The findings indicate that our method surpasses other conventional approaches that depend on self-consistency, by refining individual model decisions into more reliable ratings, thus achieving parity or exceeding human performance when compared to a gold standard predicated on human agreement. Initially, it was determined that traditional methods for aggregating results from Large Language Model (LLM) judges during inference possess limitations, highlighting the critical importance of employing a more efficacious method for result amalgamation. Subsequently, a novel approach utilizing Expected Risk Minimization (ERM) in conjunction with a Bradley-Terry-Davidson model was developed and tested on a limited calibration data set, demonstrating superior performance across diverse tasks, including reward benchmarks and machine translation. Specifically, in the domain of machine translation, a consensus-based evaluation was employed to generate improved ground truth labels amidst noisy data, facilitating a fair comparison to human raters and revealing that LLM judges occasionally surpass human evaluators. The utilization of LLMs as evaluators of generated text has recently gained prominence, offering a scalable alternative to traditional quality metrics by effectively aligning with human judgment across multiple domains. Certain methodologies employ general-purpose LLMs directly, such as G-Eval or JudgeLM, whereas others fine-tune models specifically for evaluation tasks, exemplified by Prometheus or Auto-J. Despite the robustness of these approaches, challenges persist, including susceptibility to prompt design, inherent biases favoring specific candidate sequences, or a predilection for extended outputs. Furthermore, LLM judges exhibit variability in decision-making rigor, with some demonstrating greater stringency concerning minor discrepancies or ties. Our research endeavors to mitigate such noise and enhance decision consistency through the implementation of a superior result aggregation method.

The randomness inherent in initial noise patterns within flow-matching or diffusion setups significantly influences the resulting actions, necessitating strategies to address variability in data distribution while minimizing excessive exploration. This study investigates the concept of Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-Counts (TACO), which is introduced as a mechanism to manage action sampling during testing. The principle of anti-exploration, rooted in offline reinforcement learning, aims to constrain the policy from engaging in behaviors absent from the dataset, thereby promoting adherence to successful patterns identified within the SFT dataset. Specifically, during VLA inference, maintaining alignment with effective action patterns while avoiding the exploration of irrelevant or suboptimal actions derived from pretraining or inadequate fine-tuning data is emphasized. TACO prioritizes adjustments to action sampling processes rather than direct modifications to the policy, recognizing the complexities associated with applying standard reinforcement learning updates within flow-matching or diffusion-based VLA models. Consequently, the approach eliminates the need for gradient calculations by modifying sampling dynamics independently of the VLA model's settings. A classical Coin-Flipping Network (CFN) with a specialized internal configuration is employed to estimate pseudo-counts corresponding to each observation-instruction-action segment within the SFT dataset. Actions associated with higher pseudo-counts are identified as being more congruent with the successful patterns present in the dataset and are therefore preferred during decision-making processes. The pseudo-counts derived from the CFN are utilized to select the optimal action segment among available options, with preference given to the segment exhibiting the highest pseudo-count. To enhance computational efficiency and reduce processing time across multiple tests, a shared observation key-value caching mechanism is integrated, enabling the reuse of visual-language information across samples.

The present study employs OmniFusion to facilitate live translations by extracting relevant lecture slides from audio clips, while simultaneously developing an open-source system capable of translating images to enable future work on translating entire images or slides. The findings demonstrate that the integration of images enhances the performance of language processing tasks across various language models. Optical Character Recognition (OCR) models are evaluated to assess their effectiveness in translating images using both unimodal and multimodal approaches. In order to increase the accessibility of lectures across diverse languages and formats, systems are designed to translate both audio content and slides. The proposed methodology incorporates these translations into other language processing tasks, enabling the conversion of slide texts and images into alternative languages while preserving their original layout. A PDF viewer is developed to display slides accompanied by captions aligned with the speaker's content, thereby facilitating audience comprehension of translations. This alignment establishes a correspondence between specific slides and audio segments, providing essential context for the translation process. The approach further elucidates the mechanism of linking slide images with audio clips and leveraging these translations for additional applications. An in-depth exploration is conducted on the processes involved in locating, translating, and reconstructing textual content within slide images. Additional technical details, including those related to Text-to-Speech processes, are provided in Appendix A.1. While certain systems exhibit the capability to translate into multiple languages, their limitations in supporting live lecture scenarios are evident, as they are predominantly optimized for offline use and require predefined initiation points for translation. Existing systems are often constrained by their inability to incorporate slides or handle multilingual contexts simultaneously, whereas the demands of lecture environments necessitate the integration of both functionalities.

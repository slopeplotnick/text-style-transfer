Recent advancements have been made in identifying bias, mitigating toxicity, and assessing fairness in artificial intelligence (AI) systems. However, existing methodologies predominantly focus on evaluating isolated outputs of models rather than analyzing the evolution of their ethical reasoning or value systems across varying contexts. This limitation is particularly critical for autonomous AI systems that adapt their responses based on input prompts, user demands, task requirements, and prior interactions. A lack of temporal consistency in ethical reasoning introduces significant risks, including the potential deterioration of moral judgments due to linguistic framing, situational pressures, or temporal changes. Such inconsistencies undermine the reliability, trustworthiness, and accountability of AI systems. Ensuring ethical consistency is not solely a matter of rule adherence but is essential for fostering stable, comprehensible, and trustworthy decision-making over time. This issue becomes even more pronounced in high-stakes domains such as healthcare, finance, education, and governance, where even minor ethical inconsistencies can propagate misinformation, reinforce biases, and produce outcomes misaligned with societal values. Although these concerns are increasingly recognized, prevailing evaluation approaches remain constrained by static tests and datasets that fail to adapt to dynamic AI behavior. Addressing this gap, this study introduces the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP), a fully autonomous framework designed to evaluate and enhance the ethical reasoning of AI models without reliance on external datasets or retrospective analyses. MoCoP independently generates ethical scenarios, elicits model responses, and interprets these responses using its internal mechanisms and evaluative criteria. In contrast to conventional methods that depend on predefined tests and external interventions, MoCoP autonomously constructs and assesses ethical scenarios. The framework comprises three core components: lexical analysis for identifying biases and linguistic issues, risk estimation for detecting semantic vulnerabilities, and reasoning evaluation for modeling decision-making processes. These components collectively generate comprehensive metrics of moral consistency and ethical stability, with each module performing distinct yet complementary functions. Lexical analysis examines language usage to detect potential biases, risk estimation evaluates semantic implications to identify potential ethical risks, and reasoning evaluation assesses the logical coherence and moral soundness of decision-making processes.

The performance of Samese models demonstrated an average F1 score of 84.65 on Assamese test data, whereas models utilizing Hindi achieved a marginally lower score of 83.91. Similarly, Sindhi exhibited a comparable trend, with direct supervision yielding an F1 score of 85.59, while the Hindi-trained model achieved 86.11 through cross-lingual transfer. In addressing RQ2, the evaluation encompassed models trained on 11 low-resource languages spanning 8 distinct language groups. Among these, languages employing Latin script, such as Galician and Slovenian, achieved superior outcomes across testing phases, a result potentially attributable to the shared XLM-R vocabulary, where the script and subword tokenization were better represented during pretraining. However, Icelandic, despite its use of Latin script, demonstrated comparatively lower performance, which may be explained by its complex linguistic structure leading to heightened token fragmentation and less coherent sentence representations. Such characteristics are likely to reduce the efficacy of multilingual encoders reliant on subword tokenization. To investigate the impact of training on high-resource languages and address RQ3, models were trained under three configurations: Hindi-only, Chinese-only, and a combined Hindi+Chinese setup. Each of these models was subsequently tested on six languages belonging to the Indic (Hindi) and East-Southeast Asian (Chinese) language groups. Within the Indic group, models trained exclusively on Hindi consistently outperformed those trained on Chinese across all test instances. Conversely, within the East-Southeast Asian group, models trained on Chinese exhibited superior performance compared to models trained on Hindi for the majority of tests. These findings indicate that intra-group transfer, wherein a high-resource language is leveraged to improve performance on other languages within the same group, is more effective than inter-group transfer involving a high-resource language from a different linguistic group.

Large language models have increasingly grown in size and complexity, resulting in substantial demands on memory and computational resources during both training and deployment. Quantization has been identified as a viable approach to mitigate these challenges by storing data using fewer bits and substituting costly floating-point operations with more computationally efficient integer arithmetic. Two primary methodologies for quantization exist, namely post-training quantization (PTQ) and quantization-aware training (QAT). PTQ is characterized by its simplicity and the absence of retraining requirements; however, its performance deteriorates significantly when extremely low bit-level representations, such as 1-bit or 2-bit quantization, are employed. This reduction in accuracy is attributed to several factors, including insufficient calibration data, suboptimal scaling methods, and channel-specific range discrepancies. Conversely, QAT integrates the quantization mechanism into the training process, enabling the model to adapt to the quantized environment. While QAT demonstrates superior performance under low-bit conditions compared to PTQ, it is associated with increased computational demands and instability arising from its constrained flexibility in data handling. In this study, FAIRY2 was applied to prominent open-source language models, specifically the LLaMA series, to evaluate its efficacy. The proposed methodology involved transforming any layer into an alternative form without altering the model's operational framework prior to quantization, maintaining training under low-bit conditions using a specialized quantizer while leveraging existing checkpoints, and minimizing errors through the application of identical procedures to residual data. These strategies collectively contributed to a significant reduction in errors, thereby highlighting the robustness of the approach.

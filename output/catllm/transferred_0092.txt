The utilization of a deformable scan was found to substantially enhance the model's performance, as evidenced by a significant decline in efficacy when it was removed in favor of the basic version of Mamba, thereby indicating the critical importance of the deformable scan. Various design configurations were explored to ascertain the optimal arrangement, with our design employing a combination of different blocks labeled as "CCDGDG" across stages 1 to 6, where C denotes Conv blocks, D signifies DDSM+FNN blocks, and G represents Gated-Conv blocks. Furthermore, configurations consisting of merely two types of blocks, such as "CCGGGG" and "CCDDD", along with designs incorporating a single type of block, were assessed. The findings revealed that our mixed design outperformed the alternative configurations. Additionally, insights were garnered from examples within InterHand2.6M and AssemblyHands datasets, wherein DF-Mamba demonstrated superior accuracy in determining joint positions, as indicated by a reduced number of errors denoted by colored ellipses. Even in instances where joints were obscured or challenging to discern, our method adeptly identified them, showcasing robustness in real-world scenarios. DF-Mamba constitutes an innovative setup for estimating 3D hand poses, initially employing efficient feature extraction and subsequently integrating a deformable representation. Evaluations conducted across five datasets encompassing diverse hand interactions and data types underscored its superior performance and enhanced speed relative to alternative setups. Consequently, prospective research directions may involve deploying DF-Mamba for additional tasks, such as 3D human pose estimation, training with extensive external datasets, application in novel hand-mesh reconstruction methodologies, and establishing it as a foundational model capable of functioning effectively across varied contexts without necessitating specific modifications.

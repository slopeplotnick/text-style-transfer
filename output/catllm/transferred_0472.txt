Over the past decade, significant transformations have characterized the global mining industry, positioning it at the forefront of the ongoing energy transition. The rising demand for critical minerals is anticipated to increase, accompanied by complex political, social, and environmental challenges. Consequently, this dynamic landscape has facilitated the emergence of new companies, frequently backed by venture capital, aiming to disrupt the mining sector in a manner analogous to advancements observed in healthcare, renewable energy, and climate technologies. Simultaneously, the introduction of innovative digital technologies, including large language models, presents the potential to revolutionize diverse domains such as the economy, education, society, and industry. However, the extent of their transformative impact remains uncertain, and apprehensions regarding the potential risks of non-adoption are prevalent. Nevertheless, it is proposed that the mining sector should perceive these developments as opportunities to integrate technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI). This paper examines the potential applications of AI in the mining industry, with a specific focus on its ability to enhance decision-making processes. Notably, the Stanford Mineral-X research group has demonstrated the efficacy of AI in mineral exploration, exemplified by the discovery of a substantial copper deposit in Zambia, where AI significantly contributed to optimizing drilling strategies and constructing subsurface geological models. Moreover, several start-up ventures dedicated to AI-driven mineral exploration have emerged from this initiative. While this study does not exhaustively enumerate the applications of AI in exploration or provide a comprehensive review of existing literature, as such assessments are available elsewhere, its primary aim is to explore AI as a tool for improving decision-making through the adoption of a novel scientific methodology. The initial section of the paper delineates key decision-making processes in mineral exploration and evaluates how a systematic scientific approach could enhance their efficiency. Presently, the field lacks a universally accepted scientific methodology, relying instead on domain-specific practices developed by individual experts. It is argued herein that the absence of an overarching scientific framework represents a critical barrier to advancing exploration efficiency, ultimately reducing the costs associated with mineral discovery.

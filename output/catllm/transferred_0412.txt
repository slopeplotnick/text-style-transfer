The global need for mental health support remains substantial, yet the prevalence of underdiagnosis persists at a concerning level. Factors such as limited accessibility of clinics, geographical remoteness, and insufficient availability of healthcare professionals significantly hinder the early detection and understanding of mental health conditions. Structural inefficiencies and individual-level barriers further exacerbate delays in the diagnosis of severe mental health disorders. Traditional diagnostic methods appear increasingly inadequate in addressing the disparity between the growing demand for mental health services and the limited resources available. Approaches such as in-person consultations and self-reported questionnaires are constrained by workforce shortages, elevated costs, regional disparities, and societal stigma, with waiting periods for mental health interventions ranging from 3 to 18 months depending on geographical location and provider availability. The necessity for rapid identification of early indicators of mental health challenges underscores the importance of developing innovative methodologies to facilitate timely detection and intervention. Portable solutions, including compact language models, exhibit potential for large-scale detection and management of mental health concerns. Emerging technologies integrated into devices such as smartphones offer the capability to identify mental health issues by analyzing real-time data from social media platforms and applications. The utilization of linguistic patterns derived from social media interactions enables unobtrusive monitoring of mental states, allowing for the early identification of mood fluctuations, stress levels, and suicide risk. These systems exhibit utility in suicide prevention and in recognizing mental health-related trends within social media content. Moreover, when implemented on personal devices, these models are capable of preserving user privacy while analyzing linguistic indicators of depression or stress. Consequently, such advancements facilitate mental health monitoring beyond traditional clinical environments, thereby enhancing accessibility and broadening the reach of mental health support services.

The implantation stage encompasses three primary objectives: maintaining concealment, ensuring reversibility of changes, and establishing control. Concealment involves ensuring that the malicious tool remains indistinguishable from legitimate tools, thereby avoiding detection. Reversibility refers to the tool's capability to revert to its original state following its utilization. Control, meanwhile, pertains to the attacker's ability to leverage the compromised system for additional operations. This section elucidates the mechanisms employed to achieve these objectives. A covert communication mechanism is utilized to transmit and receive commands, wherein a two-step process is implemented by the backdoor. Initially, the implant transmits a minimal signal containing the victim's identifier to a remote server via the internet, which subsequently responds with additional instructions. These instructions are embedded within the tool's responses to ensure they appear normal. In the subsequent phase, the implant monitors subsequent tool utilization to capture user inputs and transfers them to the remote server. This two-step process is designed to minimize the duration of network connections, thereby reducing the likelihood of detection. During the exploitation stage, the agent is tasked with executing the additional instructions while simultaneously delivering the tool's standard response to the user, ensuring that no anomalies are perceptible. Triggers for initiating these processes are categorized into three types: content-based, frequency-based, and context-based. A content-based trigger is activated by specific keywords or symbols within the tool's input. In contrast, a frequency-based trigger is initiated after a randomized number of normal interactions, thereby mitigating the formation of detectable patterns. Finally, a context-based trigger is dependent on specific tools or functions being utilized in response to a request. Upon activation of a trigger, the LeechHijack system acquires additional instructions from the attacker, facilitating unauthorized utilization of the agent's computational resources.

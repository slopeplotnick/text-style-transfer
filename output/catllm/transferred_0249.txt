The Autonomous Domain-Oriented Relevance Engine (ADORE) comprises three core components: a domain-specific relevance determination module, a data generation module based on error typology, and a knowledge enhancement module utilizing key attributes. The first module employs a reasoning model to autonomously generate challenging samples by analyzing products displayed online, thus utilizing domain-specific rules to ascertain relevance. Furthermore, the second module incorporates a model that generates diverse forms of challenging samples to fortify the system's robustness. Specifically, the third module leverages key features derived from analysis to enhance knowledge, thereby improving the online model's reasoning and representation capabilities. Extensive testing has been conducted to evaluate the efficacy of ADORE, utilizing both substantial offline datasets and online trials. Consequently, the results indicate that ADORE significantly outperforms alternative methods, enhancing both offline and online performance metrics. This effectively demonstrates ADORE's proficiency in managing complex relevance tasks within e-commerce contexts. To further augment the online model, a relevance determination approach utilizing Large Language Models has been developed. Research has indicated that the application of Chain-of-Thought prompts substantially enhances the reasoning capabilities of these models. This concept was employed to refine our model, beginning with the utilization of GPT-4o to generate data labeled with Chain-of-Thought, akin to human annotation through guidelines and examples. Thus, a dataset was produced that embodies detailed human-like reasoning annotations. To ensure the labeled data's accuracy, only data verifiable against human labels were retained, excluding any mismatched data, resulting in a final training dataset. Subsequently, these samples were employed to fine-tune the LLaMA-3 model, optimizing it through standard Language Model loss functions.

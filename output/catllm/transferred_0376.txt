The proposed method aims to enhance fairness across various tasks and groups by directly improving balanced accuracy, distinguishing itself from the conventional cross-entropy loss through its emphasis on addressing performance disparities between large and small groups, thereby promoting consistency in multi-task fine-tuning. A comparative study was conducted to evaluate different training methodologies, examining the effects of both single-task and multi-task fine-tuning on mental health prediction outcomes. An iOS application for mental health assessment was developed utilizing llama.cpp as the backend, deploying GGUF V3 models optimized for mobile devices. The conversion of models from PyTorch to GGUF format was facilitated through a specialized process, allowing efficient execution on both CPUs and GPUs. The application, constructed using Swift/SwiftUI, interfaces with llama.cpp via a C++ layer, enabling seamless local operation on iOS platforms without reliance on external servers. During execution, Apple Metal is leveraged to accelerate matrix operations, switching to CPU processing when GPU resources are constrained. The application utilizes up to eight threads to maximize performance on Apple's processor cores, with model weights configured to minimize RAM usage through selective loading. Stability is ensured through a 4,096-token window, incorporating intelligent cache management and batch processing to effectively manage extensive inputs. Long social media entries are truncated to 6,000 characters. Evaluation was performed with three models on an iPhone 15 Pro Max: Menta (2.33 GB), Phi-4 Mini (2.40 GB), and Qwen-3 (2.30GB), employing open-source code. The framework accommodates alternative quantization techniques, balancing speed and accuracy. For the Menta model, four high-quality datasets were utilized to identify depression, stress, and suicidal tendencies from Reddit, excluding samples related to specific disorders and employing expert labeling for the remainder. These datasets formed the basis of the study.

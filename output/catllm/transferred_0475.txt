Despite the growing prevalence of this methodological approach, research exploring the collaborative dynamics between solvers and verifiers remains limited. Historical investigations predominantly focused on the self-assessment capabilities within singular models, yet the reliability of such methods is not guaranteed. Models tend to exhibit biases aligned with their inherent cognitive frameworks, which may be exacerbated through training processes. Moreover, the advantages of answer verification may not uniformly extend across varying tasks. Solely concentrating on self-verification fails to illuminate the complexities arising when the solver and verifier are distinct entities. The availability of open-source models with diverse versions and configurations permits an examination of the verification processes across different models. Consequently, the scope of this study has been broadened to encompass both intra-family and inter-family model analyses, posing the critical inquiry: Under what conditions does verification truly enhance performance, and how do factors such as model family, size, additional training, solver-verifier similarity, or task type influence the efficacy of the verification process? To address these questions, verifiers are being assessed across a spectrum of tasks, including those requiring precise logical reasoning or symbolic mathematics (such as 3-SAT, Sudoku, and matrix multiplication), mathematical reasoning tasks (such as AIME and GSM8K), commonsense and factual reasoning (such as CSQA and GPQA), and general knowledge domains within STEM and social sciences, utilizing 37 models from seven distinct model families. The findings of our investigation indicate that self-verification is not universally effective; models frequently favor solutions congruent with their cognitive biases, additional training can intensify such biases, and the benefits of verification vary significantly among different tasks. Therefore, practical recommendations are offered based on these findings to optimize the application of verifiers in scholarly contexts.

Two primary metrics are employed to assess performance: the Toxic Score and the Attack Success Rate (ASR). Consistent with previous studies, GPT-4o is utilized to evaluate the harmfulness of generated responses. Specifically, when presented with a harmful query and a corresponding model-generated answer, evaluators assign a Toxic Score ranging from 1 to 5, with higher scores indicating greater harm. A score of 5 signifies complete alignment of the response with the harmful intent of the query, and an attack is deemed successful exclusively in instances where this score is attained. The evaluation prompt utilized for this process is detailed in Appendix B.3. The study is structured into four distinct components, each demonstrating optimal performance under specific conditions and with varying models. An attack is considered successful if either the original target image or any of the four refined target images achieves a successful outcome. When determining the Toxic Score, the maximum score from five iterations for each example within a specific context is selected. While the results presented focus exclusively on the Demonstrative Framework, additional findings pertaining to alternative strategies are provided in Appendices C and D. Extensive testing was conducted on the MMSafetyBench-tiny and SafeBench-tiny datasets, with the summarized results provided in Tables 1 and 2. This methodology incorporates multiple interaction rounds, diverse images, and robust red-teaming models to generate attack prompts, which inherently incurs higher computational and financial costs compared to CIA. Conversely, CIA necessitates the generation of only a single image per query and employs a less complex attack mechanism, rendering it more suitable for scalability and repetition. Furthermore, CIA demonstrates considerable efficacy in challenging domains such as illegal advice, fraud, and hate crimes, thereby indicating its capacity to exploit general vulnerabilities in the alignment of vision and language models and their instruction-following mechanisms rather than relying on predefined category-specific templates.

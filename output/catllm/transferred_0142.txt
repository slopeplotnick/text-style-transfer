Traditional MRTA methods have demonstrated efficacy across various domains through optimization techniques such as linear programming, genetic algorithms, and reinforcement learning, which are substantiated by mathematical foundations and corroborated results. However, construction sites present unique challenges that render these conventional methods less effective due to their inherent disorganization and coordination difficulties. Furthermore, the complexity and volume of information inherent in construction contexts exceed the capabilities of these mathematical approaches, highlighting the necessity for more adaptable solutions. Recent advancements in large language models (LLM) reveal their capacity for language comprehension, logical reasoning, and decision-making, which are adaptable to dynamic changes. These capabilities facilitate overcoming several principal limitations associated with traditional optimization and learning methodologies. LLM-based coordination further enables seamless human-robot interaction and adaptability to the intricate and evolving demands of construction environments. Promising developments in recent LLM configurations have emerged, particularly in planning tasks, digital twin integration, and multi-agent systems utilization. Nonetheless, these configurations lack systematic mechanisms for performance evaluation and comparison with traditional approaches. Consequently, a significant gap exists since, while LLM-based coordination appears promising, research has not developed methods for iterative error correction to ensure reliable reasoning, nor have systematic comparisons been conducted to evaluate LLM performance against traditional methods for construction tasks. This study aims to address this gap by introducing the LangGraph based Task Allocation Agent (LTAA) framework, a novel LLM-driven coordination approach incorporating unique strategies and validation processes absent in other configurations. The proposed strategy effectively balances workloads without compromising performance, and the validation system ensures consistency in reasoning by identifying and rectifying allocation issues through structured feedback loops.

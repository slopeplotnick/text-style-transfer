Explanations within the domain of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) require clarity and fidelity to the underlying model. It is essential that these explanations are comprehensible to the target audience while accurately representing the model's functionality. The comprehensibility of an explanation is contingent upon the recipient's expertise, typically necessitating a clear and concise articulation. Accuracy in representation demands that the explanation faithfully illustrates the model's operation, devoid of bias. Bias may arise if explanations are intentionally constrained, thus compromising fidelity. Although clarity and accuracy are distinct attributes, enhancing each presents inherent challenges. For instance, explanations that align with user expectations may be perceived as more understandable; however, this may introduce bias by influencing the selection of explanations. Furthermore, if a well-founded explanation is misconstrued, it may result in miscommunication by those providing the explanation. The present study proposes a methodology for post-hoc model explanation aimed at achieving both clarity and accuracy. Initially, it advocates for the use of straightforward language and the illustration of cause and effect, reflecting the conventional manner in which individuals convey explanations. This approach ensures that the explanation is comprehended as intended, thereby obviating the need for supplementary interpretative guidelines, thereby simplifying the process. The employment of simple language is pivotal, given that the data utilized by the model may diverge from the optimal mode of explanation. This concept is situated within the framework of Concept-based XAI, which posits that while models are adept at processing data such as images and graphs, human communication typically involves meaningful concepts.

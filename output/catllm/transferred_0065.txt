Four distinct models, namely MOIRAI, CHRONOS, GTT, and LightGTS, were evaluated to examine their performance in handling variations in data and training processes. Each model employs a unique methodology for learning and prediction, with the smallest versions utilized to ensure the analysis focuses exclusively on their capacity to manage changes in data size rather than their computational power. The distinctive strategies adopted by these models directly influence their ability to address variations in data scale. Specifically, CHRONOS utilizes a technique that partitions the data into smaller segments prior to the learning process, thereby enabling it to mitigate the impact of data magnitude and maintain predictive accuracy irrespective of large numerical values. To ensure unbiased outcomes, all data were normalized to a consistent scale prior to training, thereby preventing any single data point from disproportionately influencing the results. Subsequently, adjustments were applied to each data point to achieve equilibrium across the dataset. Model performance was assessed using the Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE), which serves as a robust metric for evaluating predictive precision while remaining unaffected by data size variations. The primary objective of the study was to investigate the effects of data size changes on model efficacy, which was demonstrated by comparing the observed decline in MASE against a standard benchmark, thereby providing insights into the adaptability and reliability of the models under varying data conditions.

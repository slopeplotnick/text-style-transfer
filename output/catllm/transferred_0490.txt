In specific supervised fine-tuning (SFT) tasks, adherence to a standardized format for task requirements is typically maintained, followed by the incorporation of query and item-specific details from respective datasets to construct the prompt. To enhance the performance of the base model, essential information, including the nature of the task, decision-making rules, and expected output format, is explicitly embedded within the prompt structure. Additionally, detailed instructions are provided to facilitate the model's adherence to a two-step process: initially identifying the query's intent and associated attributes, and subsequently extracting relevant attributes from the item information to execute decisions based on predefined rules. For reasoning tasks, the inclusion of more extensive instructions within the prompt has been observed to assist the model in achieving accurate responses, although this effect is not consistently observed in SFT tasks due to the model's reliance on learning from response data beyond the instructions themselves. In pursuit of optimal prompt design, three variations were tested: the long version, encompassing comprehensive task details, rules, and instructions exceeding 7,000 tokens; the mid version, which retains only critical instructions and reduces the token count to approximately 800; and the short version, presenting a simplified role description, such as "You’re an e-commerce relevance expert. Score the query and item." This phase primarily focuses on initializing the model and enhancing its fundamental decision-making capabilities. To achieve these objectives, it is imperative to utilize a training dataset that is both extensive and clean, wherein the breadth is ensured through effective sampling methodologies that capture diverse e-commerce data types while avoiding overrepresentation or underrepresentation of specific categories.

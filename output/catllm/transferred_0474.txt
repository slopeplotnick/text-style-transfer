The utilization of computational systems has significantly improved due to the development of advanced tools and frameworks, which contribute to enhanced autonomous functioning. Open-source systems have facilitated the integration of diverse components, such as planners and memory modules, thereby streamlining processes and enabling cohesive functionality. Reports from industry stakeholders have demonstrated the application of these systems within practical contexts, highlighting their utility in real-world scenarios. While certain studies have suggested that increasing model sizes does not necessarily enhance reliability, a growing emphasis has been placed on evaluating system performance to ensure substantive advancements. The assessment of these systems presents considerable challenges, primarily due to the increasing complexity of tasks assigned to them. Investigations into employing specialized systems for evaluation have revealed concerns regarding potential biases, necessitating strategies to address such limitations. Consequently, recommendations such as incorporating multiple judges and promoting equitable evaluations across diverse tasks have been proposed. In alignment with these recommendations, the present project adopts a methodology involving two independent judges to evaluate various aspects of task performance, ensuring that improvements are accurately identified. A dataset comprising 100 real-world editing tasks involving PowerPoint slide decks has been curated, wherein each task includes an original slide deck, a target slide deck, comprehensive instructions, and guidelines specifying layout and content considerations. These tasks have been systematically organized into 16 distinct groups to ensure balanced testing of both comprehension and detailed formatting capabilities. Data were collected from over 15,000 PowerPoint presentations sourced from varied origins and subsequently transformed into a structured format that encapsulates both layout and content specifics. Presentations exhibiting diverse stylistic characteristics were retained and supplemented with contributions from experts across multiple domains. Following the review of more than 500 potential options, including student-generated examples, 100 tasks were selected to represent a wide range of professional and academic contexts, with an emphasis on ensuring the inclusion of challenging examples within each group.

The process determines the number of iterations required to ensure result reliability, serving as a critical component in optimizing system training during testing procedures. The OptPO-SFT framework employs the same update methodology as TTSFT, while the frequency of repetition is governed by a Bayesian rule, enhancing both efficiency and reliability. Both the reinforcement learning (RL)-based and supervised fine-tuning (SFT)-based versions of OptPO adhere to a standardized three-step procedure: initiation of the process, computation of a label using a designated method, and subsequent parameter updates. The distinction lies in the update mechanism, wherein the RL-based version utilizes policy-gradient optimization, while the SFT-based version employs cross-entropy fine-tuning; however, the overarching operational framework remains consistent. Testing was conducted using complex mathematical and scientific problem sets, including MATH-500, featuring 500 high school-level problems, AIME 2024 with 30 pre-Olympiad problems, AMC comprising American mathematics competition problems, and GPQA containing advanced graduate-level science queries. Evaluation of OptPO performance was performed on widely utilized open-source models, specifically Qwen2.5-Math1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, to assess efficacy across varying model sizes and architectures. The primary models selected for analyzing the effectiveness of TTSFT and OptPO-SFT were Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct. OptPO represents a versatile framework that can be integrated into diverse existing RL systems to enhance the efficiency of test-time training, as evidenced by its application to PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, which demonstrated its adaptability. Comparative analyses were conducted with TTRL, a prevalent method for optimizing policies during testing, to ascertain the relative performance of OptPO. Furthermore, the functionality of OptPO-SFT was examined under analogous conditions. The critic's learning rate was established at 1e-5, while the SFT-based training paradigms, including both TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, utilized gradient descent on labeled batches with the AdamW optimizer at a learning rate of 2e-5.

The knowledge graph-based system was evaluated using two distinct approaches to assess the influence of data on generative models. Initially, specific data were removed from a self-trained model to determine whether the proposed method could identify critical training examples. Subsequently, the impact of stylistic elements was investigated by employing Ghibli-style images in scenarios where internal model mechanisms were inaccessible. A "Ghibli World" knowledge graph, constructed from the original movies, was utilized and compared against styled images to examine both direct data influence and the traceability of stylistic elements within generative models. To ensure the fairness and comprehensibility of the comparison, the experimental setup was adapted from a recent method designed to analyze influence without requiring access to training details. This method operates in two distinct phases: the first phase involves identifying training examples closely aligned with the user's desired output, while the second phase ranks these examples based on their similarity to the generated result using cosine similarity as the metric. Subsequently, the most similar training images are removed, and the model is retrained to evaluate their significance. The graph-based approach was applied within the same experimental framework, utilizing identical datasets, prompts, and model configurations. Rather than relying on textual and hidden-space similarity, the determination of significant examples was conducted based on the similarity of the knowledge graphs, facilitating a direct comparison of the methods in terms of influence detection and interpretability. For the generative model, a small-scale implementation of the open-source dalle-pytorch package was employed, trained on a dataset comprising 44,000 fashion images annotated with captions. Consistent settings and configurations from the original setup were preserved, enabling comprehensive control of the training data, which proved advantageous for examining the effects of data removal and retraining processes.

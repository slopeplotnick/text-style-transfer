Journal Entry Tests (JETs) are utilized by auditors to detect anomalies within tax records; however, these tests frequently produce excessive false alarms and fail to identify minor issues. This study investigates the potential application of large language models (LLMs) in identifying irregularities within bookkeeping. Prominent LLMs, such as LLaMA and Gemma, were tested on both synthetic and authentic anonymous ledgers, with comparisons drawn against JETs and traditional machine learning methods. The findings indicate that LLMs surpass the conventional JETs and established machine learning approaches, while providing explanations that are easily comprehensible, thereby facilitating understanding. Consequently, this research illustrates the potential of artificial intelligence to enhance auditing processes by collaborating with human auditors to ensure the integrity of financial records. Financial auditors play a pivotal role in detecting unusual transactions indicative of errors, fraudulent activities, or tax evasion within a company's financial documents. The identification of these discrepancies is essential for maintaining confidence in financial reporting. However, the examination of extensive accounting data presents significant challenges; current manual and rule-based methods frequently lack efficiency and are prone to errors, potentially causing auditors to overlook critical warning signs. Standard JETs, reliant on predefined rules and existing patterns, typically generate excessive transaction alerts. These tests primarily identify anticipated issues and inundate auditors with notifications, many of which are false positives requiring substantial verification efforts. In practice, auditors predominantly employ sampling and manual techniques, which may fail to detect minor discrepancies within today's vast and intricate datasets. This disparity between increasingly complex data and the limitations of traditional audit methods underscores the necessity for more sophisticated, automated tools in auditing problem identification.

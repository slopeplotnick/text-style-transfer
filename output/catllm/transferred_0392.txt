The utilization of artificial intelligence (AI) on mobile devices presents a significant advancement in the monitoring of mental health, facilitating rapid and personalized feedback without the necessity of sharing sensitive information. Such technology operates directly on the device, obviating the need to transmit data to cloud-based systems, thereby enhancing data security and reducing dependency on internet connectivity. Furthermore, the integration of these tools within mobile devices increases accessibility to mental health support, particularly for individuals residing in remote areas or experiencing prolonged wait times for clinical appointments. The capability of AI to function directly on mobile devices allows for immediate feedback and the detection of subtle variations in speech or behavior that may indicate stress or mood disorders. The increasing popularity of using smartphones and other gadgets for mental health monitoring is attributed to the efficiency, reduced energy consumption, and privacy preservation they offer in comparison to cloud-dependent systems. Recent empirical studies have demonstrated the efficacy of small language models (SLMs) in operating efficiently on devices such as the Samsung Galaxy S24, particularly in document assistance and health prediction, requiring minimal memory and facilitating rapid processing. These models are favored for practical applications due to their ease of implementation and minimal resource requirements, operating independently of constant cloud connectivity, which further safeguards user privacy. However, while certain research has indicated the potential of SLMs in addressing specific mental health tasks, there remains a substantial scope of unaddressed complexities. Predominantly, existing research has concentrated on elementary tasks and has shown limited efficacy in managing complex datasets. Additionally, the investigation into the performance of these models on resource-constrained devices has been insufficient. To address these challenges, the introduction of Menta, a novel framework employing the LoRA methodology, is proposed to manage multiple tasks and handle heterogeneous data effectively. This framework is specifically designed to ensure operational efficiency across diverse mental health tasks while maintaining privacy protection on everyday mobile devices.

In the process of formula simplification, extraneous components are eliminated, retaining only the essential elements to ensure the formula remains concise yet functionally equivalent, thereby facilitating SAT solver operations. Hallucinations occur when variables or formula components are erroneously reused, incorrectly labeled, or poorly translated, which disrupts the functionality of SAT solvers. These inaccuracies may arise from models inadequately translating specific sentence structures during training or from sentences exhibiting atypical word order, complicating their conversion into Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF). To address these challenges, Natural Language Toolkit (NLTK) and SymPy were employed collaboratively to identify and rectify such issues. NLTK aids in sentence decomposition based on rigorously tested grammatical rules, ensuring accurate syntactic breakdown. Subsequently, SymPy is utilized to preserve the logical structure of the original sentence during its conversion into CNF. Upon receiving a paragraph, it is segmented into sentences at the full stops, with each sentence treated as an individual unit. These units are subsequently integrated using logical conjunctions to form a CNF. A comprehensive set of grammatical rules within NLTK facilitates the decomposition of each sentence into a tree structure, illustrating the sentence's construction. The tree initiates with the principal component and subdivides into smaller elements, such as conditions, statements, and other sentence constituents including nouns, verbs, and punctuation. Predetermined rules are applied to transform this tree into a logical formula with SymPy, which then converts the formula into CNF utilizing its intrinsic functionalities.

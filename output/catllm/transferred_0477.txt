The current investigation evaluates the performance of the system across four simulation tests comprising 64 tasks alongside 5 tasks derived from real-world scenarios, analyzing three variants of VLA policies. Specifically, the simulation assessments utilize Simpler, Libero, Robotwin1.0, and Robotwin2.0 frameworks. Simpler operates within the SAPIEN simulator and ManiSkill2 environment, accommodating tasks for WindowX and Google Robot platforms, with a predominant focus on WindowX tasks. Libero, conversely, is oriented towards lifelong learning in decision-making, encompassing four sets of tasks, with emphasis placed on the most challenging tasks due to prior success achieved in other areas. The Robotwin1.0 and Robotwin2.0 methodologies pertain to the employment of dual-arm grippers for diverse task execution, facilitating automatic data collection. In relation to the real-world configuration, the REALMAN75 dual-arm robot is employed within an office-like environment, executing five tasks involving common items such as books, pens, a cabinet, a charger, a laptop, and a paper ball. The tasks are delineated as follows: the 'Receive Book' task, whereby the robot retrieves a book from an individual and subsequently returns it to the shelf; the 'Storage Charger' task, wherein the robot procures the charger, stores it within the cabinet, and secures the cabinet door; the 'Paper and Pen' task, involving the disposal of a paper ball and a pen into the trash and pen holder respectively; the 'Laptop' task, which necessitates closing the laptop, disconnecting the charger, and storing it within the cabinet; and finally, the 'Pick Books' task, requiring the simultaneous engagement of both arms to lift a book. Consequently, these tasks collectively contribute to the comprehensive understanding and evaluation of robotic task execution within simulated and real-world settings.

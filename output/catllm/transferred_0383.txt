The prompts were generated in the first language of each respective language team, despite the interface being exclusively in English; thus, translation measures were not required. Upon the completion of specific paths by individual team members, prompts were subsequently created in their native languages. Prior to the commencement of the principal task, all participants engaged in a preliminary sample project to acquire a comprehensive understanding of the procedural requirements. Subsequently, each team was tasked with producing a minimum of 2,000 prompts following the establishment of the project objectives. The scripted prompts were derived from culturally relevant materials, which were tailored to specific thematic domains such as agriculture and health. These resources encompassed government-issued publications detailing public services, Wikipedia content in South African languages, a specialized vocabulary database, and translated manuals addressing topics related to animal health and agricultural practices. The prompt data underwent a meticulous cleaning and preparation process to ensure suitability for training speech recognition models. This procedure involved iterative improvements, including the exclusion of extraneous content, thematic categorization of text, and the identification or annotation of personal information. Initial quality assessments were conducted upon the acquisition of the data, entailing reviews to identify and address structural inconsistencies. Filtering mechanisms were applied to retain prompts exclusively in the targeted South African languages. Cases where the predominant language did not align with the expected parameters were excluded, though confidence scores were preserved as supplementary metadata.

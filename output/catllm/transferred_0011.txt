The AgentHarm test dataset comprises 176 examples derived from 44 tasks across eight categories, including disinformation and fraud, with each task being adjustable through two specific options: one providing a hint regarding the initial tool to utilize and the other offering detailed step-by-step instructions. The combination of these options results in four distinct versions of each task description. In the experimental context, the primary emphasis is placed on the simplest group to minimize financial expenditure and evaluate the model's capabilities, ensuring that the model's inherent capacity is not a limiting factor and confirming that context length constitutes the principal variable influencing performance (refer to Section A). Evaluation within AgentHarm is conducted using automatic scoring alongside assessments by large language model (LLM) judges as detailed by Zheng et al. (2023). Two metrics are utilized for measurement: the Harm Score, ranging between 0 and 1 based on manually established rubrics for harmful tasks, and the Refusal Rate, representing the proportion of examples classified as refusals by the LLM model, with further elaboration provided in Section B. While the Harm Score is prioritized, semantic judging is excluded under the assumption that the evaluation of simpler tasks is reliably performed. Modifications to prompts and the model for refusal judging are briefly outlined in Section E. To systematically assess the impact of context length, four distinct padding methods are employed: random padding, which incorporates arbitrary tokens from the tokenizer; non-relevant padding, which utilizes text from unrelated fiction genres such as humor, mythology, science fiction, crime, and romance; relevant padding, which integrates category-matching text from Wikipedia; and multi-task padding, which incorporates task descriptions extracted from the validation dataset. These padding types serve varied purposes: random padding enhances reproducibility; non-relevant and relevant padding accommodate stylistic alignment; and multi-task padding evaluates the model's ability to manage ambiguous context. Additional methodological specifics are provided in Sections F and G.

This study examines the capacity of computational systems to acquire linguistic categorizations utilizing an adult language model, referred to as Delilah, alongside a daughter agent specifically designed for MODOMA, which employs graphical representations of grammatical knowledge to facilitate the construction and comprehension of sentences, progressively improving as the language acquisition process advances. Delilah incorporates a parser and generator developed by Cremers and Hijzelendoorn, which is tailored to Dutch grammar and enables the generation and interpretation of syntactic structures. Consequently, MODOMA necessitates an innovative approach to examine Delilah, emphasizing not only the enhancement of her syntactic generation and comprehension capabilities but also the analysis of her output to identify patterns conducive to the daughter's learning process. In pursuing this objective, interdisciplinary methodologies from linguistics and related domains are employed to analyze machine-generated language in a manner analogous to the study of human language. Given the increasing prevalence of large language models, assessing the extent to which machine language aligns with human linguistic properties emerges as a critical area of inquiry, with experimental results presented within this paper addressing this issue. The sentences generated by Delilah during interactions with the daughter agent are treated as discrete units, contrasting with traditional corpora that consist of interconnected sentences; however, analogous grammatical patterns are observed across both data types, suggesting that these patterns may be attributable to individual sentence structures rather than the overarching corpus. Furthermore, the study reveals that distinct frequency distributions of linguistic categories, which are characteristic of human language data, are also evident in the output produced by Delilah. Notably, Delilah is not explicitly programmed to replicate these patterns, indicating that their emergence is intrinsically linked to the underlying grammatical model employed.

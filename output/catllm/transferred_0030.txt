Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) is a technique wherein pretrained diffusion models are employed not only for generating samples through noise reduction but also as optimization targets. Specifically, SDS has enabled these models to be applied across a diverse array of tasks in image and video generation. Due to the efficacy of SDS in image synthesis, its application in reinforcement learning for control tasks has been initiated. Methods have been developed where the conventional discriminator in setups akin to Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) is replaced by a diffusion model, which serves the function of distinguishing between authentic and inauthentic actions. Despite this innovation, adversarial training remains a requirement, necessitating frequent updates of the diffusion model during policy optimization. Alternatively, approaches have been proposed to leverage SDS-like methodologies directly for policy learning. For example, certain strategies involve guiding policy training through pretrained image or video diffusion models that respond to textual prompts. Although these strategies can result in behaviors aligned with text instructions, there are instances where the movements appear atypical. The most comparable work to ours is termed SMILING, which trains controllers using a technique akin to variational score distillation. Unlike SMILING, which necessitates specific diffusion models tailored to each task, our method involves training a general motion model from stochastic motion data. This generalized model is subsequently utilized in training various control policies, integrating it with specific task objectives in a goal-oriented reinforcement learning framework. Furthermore, several critical design choices are introduced to facilitate the generation of high-quality, naturalistic movements across a broad spectrum of tasks.

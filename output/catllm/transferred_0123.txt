The evaluation of models’ proficiency in addressing mathematical problems was conducted utilizing five distinct benchmarks: AMC, Minerva, MATH-500, GSM8K, and Olympiad-Bench. To ascertain the correctness of the generated solutions, GPT-4o was employed to compare model outputs against the ground truth, adhering to methodologies established in prior research. Furthermore, the assessment of general reasoning capabilities encompassed diverse domains, including physics, biology, business, economics, and law, using benchmarks such as MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, GPQA-Diamond, and BBEH. These benchmarks were selected due to their complexity and capacity to evaluate advanced reasoning competencies. Accuracy was measured through the Exact Match metric, which determines the alignment of model-generated responses with the correct answers, while greedy decoding served as the methodological approach for obtaining outputs. To investigate the contribution of specific model components, ablation experiments were performed on the Qwen3-8B-Base model, wherein individual features were systematically deactivated to assess their impact on mathematical and general reasoning performance. The findings, presented in Table 2, indicate that the removal of challenger training resulted in the most significant performance decline, with reductions of 1.9 points in mathematics and 1.0 points in general reasoning scores. Additionally, the exclusion of challenger warm-up and curriculum learning adversely affected performance, underscoring their critical role in the model’s functionality. Notably, math-related tasks exhibited greater sensitivity to these modifications compared to general reasoning tasks, a trend consistent with observations reported in Table 1, where mathematical scores demonstrated higher variability contingent upon methodological variations. Overall, the results highlight that incorporating a blend of human-annotated data enhances model performance across general domains, while prioritizing domain-specific data yields superior outcomes within targeted areas. This finding is particularly significant as it suggests that developers can strategically influence model training by curating data types aligned with specific objectives, thereby ensuring focused improvement within desired domains and mitigating the risk of model divergence from intended learning trajectories.

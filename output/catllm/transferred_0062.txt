Adopting a software product line (SPL) approach necessitates significant organizational changes, particularly in operational methodologies. The management of software products is inherently complex, as evidenced by a survey conducted by Springer and Miler. Within the SPL framework, collaborative efforts among teams are emphasized, facilitated by the shared utilization of tools and resources applicable across diverse projects. This approach ultimately contributes to resource optimization, accelerates product development cycles, and enhances the focus on innovation. The reuse of components, customization based on specific configurations, and the availability of comprehensive documentation collectively promote operational efficiency and streamlined processes. However, this transition requires meticulous planning and management, which, when executed effectively, fosters improved organizational cohesion and adaptability. At ITK, discussions regarding outcomes and strategic plans were conducted with the development team, who expressed a desire to remain informed and actively engaged throughout the migration process. Consequently, close collaboration was maintained with the team during all phases of the transition. Comparative analyses of the findings at ITK with existing reports on SPL adoption and associated strategies were undertaken, revealing that ITK’s approach aligns with the IDEAL improvement model. The project commenced following the team’s decision to proceed with the migration, initiating with assessments of existing practices and project perceptions. Subsequent phases involved strategy formulation, action plan development, implementation, and iterative improvements informed by agile methodologies, which emphasize learning from successes and challenges encountered during execution. The guidelines provided have been instrumental in establishing the action plan. Certain challenges experienced during this process diverged from those documented in other reports, notably concerning the availability of skilled personnel and management support, as these factors were not perceived as significant obstacles due to the organization’s prevailing mindset.

The performance of the proposed method was evaluated in comparison to an alternative approach, RePro, utilizing results reported in the original publication due to the unavailability of their implementation. The method, referred to as Paper2Code (P2C), eliminates the necessity for manual creation of distinct prompts for the evaluation and refinement of each process component, as required by the Refine approach. For instance, in the original Paper2Code framework, eight custom prompts were designed for four planning tasks, whereas the proposed method automates task adaptation via system prompts, thereby obviating the need for additional prompt engineering. The evaluation was conducted on two distinct datasets. The first dataset, Paper2CodeBench, comprises 90 papers sourced from leading conferences in 2024, including ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS, selected based on the availability of code and manageable length. The generated code was compared to the official code released by the authors, with correctness assessed by AI evaluators on a scale ranging from 1 to 5. The second dataset, PaperBench Code-Dev, includes 20 ICML 2024 papers, and correctness was evaluated by human reviewers using predefined rubrics. To the best of our knowledge, few existing methodologies incorporate self-refinement for the automated transformation of research papers into executable code. The proposed approach was therefore benchmarked against two similar methodologies employing self-refinement, namely Paper2Code combined with Self-Refine. Self-Refine operates as an iterative refinement method. All experiments utilized GPT-4.1 as the primary model, and results from RePro were incorporated directly from their published work due to the absence of accessible code. Tables I and II present the comparative performance of various optimization strategies across the two datasets. The proposed method demonstrated consistent reliability across datasets, with improvements observed even when optimization was applied exclusively to the planning stage. Specifically, Paper2Code with autoplan optimization exhibited steady enhancements in performance. A closer analysis of individual tasks revealed superior overall performance of the proposed method. Figure 2 illustrates task-specific performance on PaperBench, indicating that the proposed planning optimization outperformed Self-Refine on 16 out of 20 tasks (80%), thereby demonstrating its adaptability across diverse scenarios. Conversely, Self-Refine yielded negative outcomes on 10 tasks and exhibited particularly poor performance on specific tasks.

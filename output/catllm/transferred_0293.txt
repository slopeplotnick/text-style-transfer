Explainable AI (XAI) primarily aims to elucidate the rationale behind a model's output, with varying motivations underpinning this endeavor. Specifically, such motivations may include assessing the model's performance, fostering trust in its decisions, or enabling individuals to amend decisions that are not definitive. For the latter, explanations must emphasize aspects amenable to modification within the current context. When the objective is to alter a model's decision by elucidating actionable steps, the process is termed algorithmic recourse. Accurate predictions regarding the outcomes of proposed changes are essential, although the focus remains on post-implementation effects. The type of explanation presented in this paper is designed to operate across diverse scenarios, eschewing concentration on specific individuals requiring elucidation or presuppositions regarding the model's efficacy or application domain. This approach ensures alignment between the explanations and the model's actual operations, irrespective of the model's performance anomalies. Additionally, it facilitates retrospection concerning alternative past actions without necessitating feasibility considerations. Although this methodology could be adapted for algorithmic recourse, such adaptations are not addressed in this paper. Concept-based explanations diverge from data feature-specific approaches by employing broader human-comprehensible ideas or characteristics. Various methods exist for defining these concepts in XAI, with reference herein to symbolic concepts as delineated by Poeta et al. [2025], which encompass high-level data ideas or attributes intelligible to humans.

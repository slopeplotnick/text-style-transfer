E-commerce platforms such as Amazon, Taobao, and JD have significantly transformed consumer shopping behaviors. Specifically, the relevance of product displays is crucial to the user experience on these platforms, as irrelevant displays can undermine consumer trust and financial outcomes. Consequently, the determination of product relevance to user search queries constitutes a fundamental aspect of the operational functionality of these sites. Traditional methods of assessing relevance, including word matching and vector-based approaches, are prevalent in online shopping environments to ensure effective search capabilities. Techniques involving word matching, such as TF-IDF and BM25, evaluate the alignment between search queries and products; however, they exhibit limitations in capturing semantic meaning and contextual nuances. Vector-based methods employ models like BERT to ascertain semantic relationships between queries and product listings, which significantly enhance relevance determination. Nonetheless, two predominant challenges persist in the application of vector methods for domain-specific topics: Firstly, there exists a scarcity of challenging topic-specific examples. Although existing methodologies perform adequately with labeled datasets, achieving superior outcomes necessitates extensive collections of challenging examples, which are difficult to procure and annotate accurately. Secondly, the models demonstrate deficiencies in reasoning and result presentation due to constraints in model size, rendering simpler online models inadequate for complex reasoning tasks and relevance determination. To ameliorate reasoning and comprehension capabilities, large language models are increasingly utilized for relevance tasks. Nevertheless, the prevalent practice involves employing the final token for relevance determination and utilizing soft labels for the instruction of smaller models. This approach does not effectively elucidate the features influencing relevance nor does it enhance the reasoning capabilities of smaller models.

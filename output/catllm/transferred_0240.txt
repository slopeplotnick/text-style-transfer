The study substantiates three principal findings: the efficacy of using large language models (LLMs) for coordinating multi-robot task allocation, the enhancement of reasoning reliability through structured validation methods, and the utility of retry systems in addressing issues in LLM outputs. Following the demonstration of the viability of this approach, the authors developed the LTAA framework to enhance LLM-MRTA applications. The LTAA framework was tested utilizing the TEACH dataset, renowned as the benchmark for comparing Q-learning, deep Q-networks (DQN), and dynamic programming (DP) within MRTA research. The transition of this dataset from household tasks to construction robotics tasks facilitated performance comparisons with existing algorithms under consistent conditions. LTAA is designed to focus on robot task assignment, akin to traditional algorithms, and employs pre-decomposed tasks to ensure both LLM-based and algorithm-based methodologies are evaluated on analogous problem types. Building upon previously mentioned self-corrective concepts, LTAA employs a nine-step workflow. In contrast to the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture, which managed all aspects, LTAA exclusively addresses robot task assignment, utilizing pre-decomposed tasks and generating outcomes comparable to those of conventional algorithms. LTAA integrates three primary innovations to rival traditional optimization algorithms while maintaining clarity in reasoning: it adapts allocation strategies as projects advance, employs multi-stage validation incorporating retry and self-correction to sustain accuracy in LLM reasoning, and incorporates context-aware reasoning to structure decisions grounded in robot capabilities. These elements are embedded within a nine-step LangGraph workflow that processes tasks through structured reasoning phases.

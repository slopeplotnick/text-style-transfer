Six distinct methods were utilized to input data into three models, resulting in an accuracy variation of approximately 3%, with no single method consistently outperforming the others. Therefore, the models demonstrated ease of use, as minimal adjustments were required. Detailed information regarding these methods can be found in Appendix I. The investigation encompassed three interrelated domains: methodologies incorporating external feedback, strategies aimed at reducing dependency on human labels, and theoretical perspectives on how representational modalities influence the quality of generated outputs. Conventionally, the optimization of large language models in alignment with human preferences employs Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). This technique utilizes human preference data to train a model in reward provision, subsequently adjusting the policy to optimize results while maintaining proximity to a reference model. Prominent models such as ChatGPT and Llama operate under this framework, albeit with challenges such as costly and occasionally inaccurate data and the susceptibility of reward models to manipulation or suboptimal performance in varied contexts. Consequently, alternative methodologies, including Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), KTO, Bayesian RLHF, and MaxMin-RLHF, endeavor to enhance policy directly through pairwise comparisons, circumventing the necessity for a dedicated value network, albeit with a continued reliance on meticulously curated preference data. Furthermore, models exist that assign scores and provide feedback for reasoning and evaluation, thereby externalizing supervision from the model's core processes. An additional approach involves leveraging automatic supervision or intrinsic model signals to mitigate the reliance on human labels. Certain methodologies, such as DeepSeek-R1 and TÜLU 3, employ programmatic feedback in lieu of human input.

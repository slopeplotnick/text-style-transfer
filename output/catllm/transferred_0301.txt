Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated significant potential in robotic tasks by leveraging extensive datasets collected through human efforts or predetermined programs. These models are trained using advanced techniques, such as flow-matching or diffusion processes, to acquire diverse strategies for task execution. Following this initial training phase, VLA models undergo fine-tuning with smaller, task-specific datasets to enhance their performance on designated applications. Utilizing pre-trained VLA models for new tasks has been shown to yield superior outcomes compared to training models from the ground up, as these models possess prior knowledge encompassing visual perception, language comprehension, and object and spatial recognition. Despite their generally robust performance, VLA models exhibit notable limitations when deployed in real-world scenarios. This issue arises due to the retention of redundant actions that do not contribute to task completion after the fine-tuning process. During training, the models assimilate a wide range of actions from diverse datasets, which complicates the process of isolating and prioritizing task-relevant actions. Consequently, even after fine-tuning, these models tend to preserve superfluous actions that are unnecessary for specific tasks. Furthermore, the datasets employed during fine-tuning often exhibit stylistic inconsistencies, as they are sourced from varied individuals or methodologies, some of which may be suboptimal. These redundant actions introduce a discrepancy between the model's performance and the actual requirements of the target task. This discrepancy becomes evident during practical applications, thereby emphasizing the need for more effective strategies to address these limitations.

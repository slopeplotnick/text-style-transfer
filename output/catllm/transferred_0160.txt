Evaluation of summary quality necessitates consideration of several aspects, specifically the extent of lexical overlap, semantic similarity, and factual accuracy. Traditional approaches, such as BLEU and ROUGE-L, primarily focus on lexical comparison between the summary and the source text; however, they are often inadequate when the semantic content remains unchanged despite variations in lexical expression. Consequently, BERTScore was developed to address this limitation by employing a pre-trained model that facilitates semantic evaluation rather than sole reliance on lexical comparison, thereby enhancing its applicability to succinct summaries. Furthermore, verification of factual consistency with the source text is imperative. Various tools have been designed for this purpose; FactCC employs a model to assess the correspondence of individual sentences within the summary to the original text, though it occasionally errs by misclassifying accurate matches as discrepancies. BLANC, on the other hand, operates independently of reference texts and evaluates the summary's contribution to a language model's ability to predict missing elements from the original text, although its efficacy is contingent upon the model employed and may disproportionately emphasize lexical matching over semantic evaluation. SummaC, another tool, utilizes Natural Language Inference (NLI) to ascertain factual consistency by examining whether the source text substantiates each sentence within the summary. Empirical evaluations indicate that SummaC generally outperforms older methodologies, such as FactCC. This study employs a composite approach for summary evaluation, integrating ROUGE-L for assessment of lexical overlap, BERTScore for semantic analysis, and SummaC for verification of factual accuracy.

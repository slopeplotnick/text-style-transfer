The classification of time phases, workload balancing, and strategic planning for each phase are integral components of the workflow initiated at Node 1, which is dedicated to phase determination. Node 1 serves as the initial checkpoint within a nine-node configuration termed LTAA, evaluating the system's state to ascertain the temporal context for subsequent operations. The Phase Detection Node, as illustrated in Figure 9a, performs a critical function within the framework by assessing task progress and updating workload metrics. Specifically, this node receives four principal data points from the system: the total number of tasks completed and pending, the distribution of workload among robots, and the ideal workload allocation. These data facilitate the establishment of context for each procedural step. Within this node, the framework determines the current progress and categorizes the system into early, middle, or late phases, each representing distinct decision-making scenarios impacting the balancing of success rates and workload equity in subsequent nodes. Subsequently, a balance score is computed to assess the fairness of task distribution and the degree of imbalance relative to the ideal workload. These metrics succinctly encapsulate both progress and workload dynamics. The node concludes by updating the system state with new parameters: allocation_phase, balance_score, imbalance_severity, and operational_mode, which are transmitted to the succeeding node, thus ensuring that all subsequent operations are informed by the most recent progress and workload data. As depicted in Figure 9a, this process represents a unidirectional flow whereby the Phase Detection Node transforms raw data into structured decision-making information for the ensuing phase. The strategy for phase allocation necessitates robust LLM reasoning to facilitate informed decision-making. However, LLM outputs may exhibit variability and may not consistently meet the requisite standards for critical construction tasks. To address the deficiencies identified during the SMART-LLM implementation, a validation framework was developed by the authors to assess reasoning quality utilizing a weighted assessment and feedback system.

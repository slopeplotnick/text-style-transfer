Efforts to enhance machine capabilities in analyzing high-resolution images have been the focus of significant research, with one approach involving the magnification of specific image regions to facilitate detailed examination. Several methodologies, including Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning, have been employed to train models; however, these approaches are characterized by high computational costs, extended processing times, and limited adaptability across diverse systems. Alternatively, non-training-based methods have utilized attention mechanisms or tree-based searches to automatically identify salient image regions, beginning with an assessment of the entire image followed by progressive zooming into specific areas. Despite their advantages, these methods often encounter challenges in detecting finer details during initial stages, potentially resulting in inaccuracies. A novel framework termed Retrieval-Augmented Perception (RAP) has recently been introduced to improve machine perception of high-resolution images without necessitating additional training processes. The RAP framework incorporates three primary components: firstly, it employs a pre-trained model to identify image regions that exhibit significant similarity to the target features, thereby minimizing superfluous information; secondly, it preserves the spatial arrangement of these regions to maintain structural integrity; finally, it determines the optimal number of regions to be analyzed by constructing a tree-based mechanism that establishes a balance between information retention and computational efficiency.

The tool library comprises several functionalities, including tools for identifying specific regions, planning analytical methodologies such as Think with Image, generating reports through templates, and assessing the quality of radiology reports with feedback mechanisms for improvement. These tools are integral to the automated radiology reporting process, as illustrated in Figure 2, which demonstrates the operational framework of the Radiologist Copilot. In testing the efficacy of the Radiologist Copilot, a liver radiology reporting task is employed utilizing the public 3D CT dataset known as AMOS-MM, which encompasses over 2,000 CT scans accompanied by detailed radiology reports. Among these, 1,287 scans with associated reports are designated for training purposes, while 400 scans with reports serve as the validation cohort. Specifically, for the liver task, attention is directed towards reports within the AMOS-MM dataset that specifically pertain to the liver, thereby yielding 1,149 CT scans with liver reports for training and 367 CT scans for validation. The validation set facilitates the evaluation of the method's performance, whereas the training set is utilized for the analysis of reports. Initially, BioBERT is employed to convert the reports into data points, which are subsequently grouped using K-means clustering to formulate template reports. These templates are systematically delineated into liver-related components such as the liver surface, liver tissue, bile ducts, and liver anomalies for utilization by the Analyzer Tool. The Radiologist Copilot is constructed with OctoTools and operates on Python 3.10.18 with NVIDIA L20 GPUs, requiring no supplementary training due to its reliance on pre-trained models. The primary reasoning tool, Qwen3-32B, and the Segmentator Tool, employing the TotalSegmentator model, facilitate region identification, while the Analyzer Tool utilizes the 3D medical model Hulu-Med for CT scan analysis. The system is configured to undertake a maximum of 10 procedural steps and allocate up to 500 seconds per case, ensuring precision and efficiency in the automated radiology reporting process.

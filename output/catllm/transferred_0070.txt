Social media plays a significant role in contemporary society, facilitating rapid communication, easy information sharing, and opinion exchange irrespective of geographical location. However, the platform also enables the proliferation of hate speech and harsh language due to its inherent freedom. Regrettably, minimal effort has been directed towards identifying hate speech on Bangla social media, and there is currently an absence of high-quality datasets for this purpose. The lexicon utilized on social media platforms often diverges substantially from that of traditional print media, posing challenges for the automatic detection of Bangla hate speech. Traditionally, the identification of hate speech has been approached as a supervised classification problem. Initial research efforts employed word lists and rule-based systems, which frequently failed to detect nuanced or context-specific hate speech. More recent investigations have adopted machine learning methodologies, such as logistic regression and SVMs, for the detection of online threats or harmful content. Consequently, traditional supervised learning methods have been applied to address this issue.

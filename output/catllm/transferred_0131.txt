This study investigates three primary inquiries concerning programming agents, focusing initially on the performance in relation to process-related measurements. Specifically, their key characteristics and the factors influencing these measurements are explored. Subsequently, the analysis proceeds to examine the problem-solving approaches of these agents, considering their typical strategies, the factors affecting their behavior, and the extent to which they adhere to planned methodologies. Furthermore, the study addresses whether these agents modify their strategies during task execution. The third inquiry scrutinizes inefficiency patterns within agent behavior, identifying common issues or detrimental habits and their frequency of occurrence. To conduct this examination, two widely utilized programming agents, SWE-agent (SA) and OpenHands (OH), were analyzed under standard operational settings: SWE-agent operates with a $2 cost limit per task, while OpenHands executes up to 100 steps, utilizing customary tools for file viewing and code editing. The agents were tested in conjunction with four language models, namely DeepSeek-V3 671B MoE (DSK-V3), DeepSeek-R1 (DSK-R1), Devstral-small-2505 24B (Dev), and Claude Sonnet 4 (CLD-4), encompassing both general-purpose and reasoning-oriented models, alongside advanced and open-source options.

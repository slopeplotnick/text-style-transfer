The TCAS program is a widely recognized tool utilized extensively in research focused on the identification of faults within code. Originating from Siemens, TCAS comprises 41 versions into which known bugs have been intentionally introduced. In contrast, C-PACK-IPAs consists of a collection of student-generated programs from an introductory programming course. Specifically, the entire C-PACK-IPAs set, which includes 25 assignments and 1431 buggy programs from three lab classes, was employed in this study. These programs encompass various topics such as numerical operations, input-output processes, loop structures, character handling, and vector manipulation. The utility of C-PACK-IPAs has been established in evaluating diverse program analysis tasks. CFAULTS employs the pycparser tool to process C programs, while CBMC version 5.11 is implemented to convert C programs into CNF formulas. Since the original code for BugAssIST and SNIPER is either unavailable or non-functional, new iterations of their algorithms were developed for bug detection. The previous version of SNIPER was limited in its capability to handle certain C programs and encountered difficulties with loop constructs, recursion, and specific variables. Furthermore, a novel version for HSD was created to identify faulty code in C programs, both with and without the core minimization feature, as the former version was restricted to Boolean circuits. Consequently, BugAssIST, SNIPER, and HSD are now capable of functioning with C programs through components of CFAULTS. The algorithms tested yielded results that conformed to specific criteria, with CBMC being used to verify the accuracy of these results. This verification process is predominantly crucial for BugAssIST, as it may produce outcomes that do not align with all failing tests. However, the MaxSAT solution of CFAULTS and the method employed by HSD consistently correspond with all observations. The SNIPER method provides accurate results, albeit not necessarily the most simplified ones. For BugAssIST, a thorough examination of all results is conducted based on a scoring mechanism until an outcome is identified that aligns with the entirety of the observations.

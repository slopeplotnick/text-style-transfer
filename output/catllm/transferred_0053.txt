This section elucidates the methodology employed within this study, encompassing data acquisition, problem-solving mechanisms, and validation processes, alongside the metrics utilized to assess solver and checker performance. Emphasis is placed on the concept of "verifier gain," a longitudinal metric serving as a theoretical boundary for enhancement achievable via checker-based sampling, thereby facilitating comparative analysis of different checkers. Models are categorized into distinct groups or families, such as Llama3 or Qwen2.5, each comprising related models of varying scales. The divergence in behavior between foundational models and their enhanced counterparts necessitates their classification into separate groups; for instance, the foundational model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-70B is assigned to the Llama3-Base group, whereas the enhanced model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct resides within the Llama3 group. Each solver-checker pairing is systematically allocated to one of three verification configurations. These configurations are delineated as follows: 1. Self-Verification, wherein the solver and checker are identical, enabling self-assessment; exemplified by a 70B Llama3 model functioning in both capacities, with metrics such as accuracy evaluated in this isolated scenario. 2. Intra-Family Verification involves inter-model analysis within the same family, such as a 70B Llama3 checker evaluating outputs from 8B or 13B Llama3 solvers, with aggregate measures computed across these internal family solvers, excluding self-verification instances. 3. Cross-Family Verification encompasses inter-group analysis, wherein checkers examine outputs from models belonging to disparate families; for instance, a basic Llama3 checker might scrutinize outputs from Qwen3 or an enhanced Llama3 model, with aggregate measures calculated across these inter-family solvers.

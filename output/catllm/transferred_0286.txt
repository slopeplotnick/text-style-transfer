The integration of GRAPHECTORY with other tools is feasible through the expansion of phase maps and the application of Algorithm 1 for phase labeling. Regarding software engineering (SWE) agents, empirical investigations have endeavored to ascertain their strengths and weaknesses. Specifically, it has been demonstrated that these agents encounter difficulties in managing problems involving multiple files. Consequently, there is a consensus among researchers that enhanced evaluation methods are imperative, focusing on attributes such as efficiency, reliability, and intelligence. Moreover, investigations have identified several reasons for agent failures, including inadequate fault localization, challenges with complex code interconnections, and difficulties in maintaining contextual awareness across disparate files. Notably, TRAIL was developed as a compilation of errors and serves as a benchmark containing 148 traces that were meticulously verified by human evaluators. Furthermore, MAST was introduced as a classification framework for failures within multi-agent systems, analyzing 150 traces across five systems and five benchmarks, necessitating human annotation for comprehensive analysis. Another research initiative constructed a flow graph depicting agent activities such as project comprehension, context tracking, and rectification and testing processes; however, this effort was manually executed and encompassed a limited dataset, specifically bug localization for 20 out of 500 issues. Additionally, a study delineated the failure modes of large language model (LLM) tools during SWE tasks, categorizing them into three phases, nine categories, and 25 subcategories. This classification was instrumental in elucidating tool inefficacies, revealing issues such as agents becoming ensnared in repetitive loops. Nonetheless, this analysis was conducted manually on only 150 out of 500 issues, highlighting the need for an automated approach in future research.

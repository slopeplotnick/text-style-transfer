Recent developments in licensing models, exemplified by NOODL and the Esethu License, indicate a significant shift in operational paradigms. Specifically, NOODL differentiates between individuals residing in Africa or developing countries and those external to these regions, thereby facilitating a regionalized distribution of benefits and ensuring that modifications are disseminated locally. Furthermore, the Esethu License strategically allocates financial resources derived from licensing to enhance local resources and employment opportunities, maintaining community governance and resource sustainability. It has been discerned from engagements with Indigenous and low-resource language data that mere openness can engender inequities in the absence of stringent ethical guidelines. Consequently, responsible data sharing now necessitates more than a rudimentary licensing framework; it demands an emphasis on consent, privacy, fairness, and mechanisms for sustained community benefit. The selection of CC BY 4.0 for Swivuriso was intended to promote transparency, although it also illuminated the limitations inherent in conventional licenses for African and low-resource language datasets. Innovative approaches such as NOODL and Esethu exemplify the progression towards improved and equitable management practices. When datasets are published, it is insufficient to merely declare their openness; authors are encouraged to elucidate their methodologies in data handling that are ethical, equitable, and community-centric. Swivuriso represents a substantial advancement in the enhancement of automatic speech recognition and other speech technologies pertinent to South African languages. The dataset encompasses seven distinct languages and incorporates both specific and culturally relevant unscripted speech, which is applicable in diverse domains such as health communication, agricultural advisement, and multilingual language tools. Designed with dual objectives, the dataset aims not only to facilitate the testing and refinement of metrics like word error rate but also to aid in the development of speech systems attuned to cultural and societal exigencies.

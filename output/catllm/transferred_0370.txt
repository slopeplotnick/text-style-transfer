Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) pertains to the identification of entities based on descriptions provided in everyday language and is a significant area of research at the intersection of visual perception and language understanding. Recent advancements in REC have been achieved by enhancing the semantic matching of language expressions with contextual knowledge, rather than merely aligning words. Furthermore, Generalized REC (GREC) encompasses the comprehension of descriptions that may refer to an indeterminate number of objects or none, exemplified by expressions such as "Pick up those three boxes." This approach mirrors the nuances of natural language usage in which references may be ambiguous or refer to nonexistent objects. However, the empirical investigation of these phenomena necessitates a substantial corpus of authentic images accompanied by human-generated descriptions, which is resource-intensive. Online platforms facilitate this process by reducing the cost and complexity associated with experimental validation. Nevertheless, challenges arise when computational models are required to analyze scenes, maintain conversational context, and engage in interactive dialogue, situations wherein extant methodologies often falter. A recent study introduced a novel GREC evaluation framework utilizing dialogues within a Minecraft gaming environment, thereby revealing the limitations of contemporary artificial intelligence models. The research further indicated that eliciting human-generated dialogues incurs considerable expenses, resulting in a paucity of training data. Consequently, this paper proposes an innovative data augmentation strategy involving the generation of concise descriptions and elaborate dialogues aimed at enhancing AI comprehension. While previous efforts focused on AI-driven data synthesis are pertinent, application to GREC remains challenging due to the requisite capability of models to discern spatial relationships and integrate these with visual inputs.

The results presented in Table 1 indicate that the integration of model-focused and data-focused approaches yields favorable outcomes. Specifically, when the "Unify" block is compared to other standalone methods, the mixed strategies consistently achieve superior performance across various datasets and models. This finding demonstrates that internal uncertainty signals and external evidence function in distinct yet complementary ways. For particular methodologies, the Score-Level Fusion approach reveals that a straightforward aggregation strategy can mitigate the significant variances observed with individual methods. By combining diverse signals, this approach minimizes errors associated with single predictions and produces more reliable outcomes, outperforming the isolated HD and FV methods in the majority of cases. Furthermore, the Evidence-Aware Pipeline exhibits an even higher level of effectiveness, frequently surpassing all other evaluated methods. This superior performance can be attributed to its capacity to address the limitations associated with excessive reliance on external evidence. In scenarios where external information is insufficient, such as in NEI cases, enforcing a decision often leads to errors; however, this pipeline circumvents such issues by utilizing internal uncertainty signals, which remain dependable in the absence of external evidence. Hybrid methods additionally resolve the problem of inconsistent performance identified in RQ1. In contrast to standalone HD methods, which display significant variability across models such as LLaMA and Qwen, hybrid approaches maintain stability and consistency across different models. This observation indicates that the integration of signals effectively mitigates the confidence-related challenges associated with standalone HD methods. Consequently, these frameworks not only enhance predictive accuracy but also demonstrate robustness across various models.

A multimodal language model was utilized for the analysis of visual data, specifically images, through a structured methodology aimed at extracting key details. The open-source model LLaMA 3.2 Vision, accessed via the Ollama framework, was selected due to its capability to process images and generate comprehensive and detailed descriptions in natural language. It demonstrates proficiency in providing intricate descriptions, thus facilitating the conversion of visual content into structured information. Although the primary objective focused on extracting structured triples, the process resembles the task of generating captions for images. Notably, studies indicate that multimodal language models exhibit comparable performance in visual comprehension. All examined models were found to be reliable; however, due to the emphasis on comparative analysis of structured information, a single model was employed as a representative example. To ensure consistency and a fashion-oriented focus in the extracted triples, the Fashionpedia ontology was utilized, providing a detailed categorization of clothing and accessory-related elements. By incorporating this guide into the model's instructions, it was ensured that the resultant information adhered to a defined and specific structure. An experimental evaluation was conducted wherein the ontology was omitted, revealing that the utilization of the ontology resulted in an increased number of triples per image and encompassed a broader range of relationships. In contrast, without the ontology, the model identified only a limited number of evident details, producing less informative results.

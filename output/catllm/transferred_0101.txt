The comprehension of intricate images by large language models presents significant challenges. A recent investigation aimed to address this issue by segmenting the image into smaller components and assessing the similarity of each segment to a given query through a specialized model. The segments deemed most relevant are selected to identify the primary object, disregarding the irrelevant ones. However, the fragmentation of the image can disrupt the alignment between the segments and the query. Empirical tests revealed that segments of varying sizes are more effective at different levels of detail. Consequently, a novel approach was devised to interpret detailed images without necessitating additional training. To mitigate the issue of fragmented segments, a technique was employed that synthesizes similarity maps from multiple detail levels to enhance information and preserve objects in their entirety. Furthermore, to directly detect objects within the complete image, a model utilizing a sliding window to identify object regions was implemented. Experimental results indicate that our method significantly enhances the understanding of detailed images. Large language models have advanced considerably in the integration and comprehension of images and text, facilitating tasks that necessitate both modalities. They employ visual cues to interpret complex images, linking raw data to semantic content. Nevertheless, most models adopt a fixed level of detail for images, simplifying the process but leading to substantial drawbacks. The reduction of detailed images to a lower resolution can distort shapes and cause blurring, adversely affecting the quality of details. Research indicates that current methodologies are inadequate for tasks involving detailed images, as demonstrated by their suboptimal performance on assessments specifically designed for detailed image understanding.

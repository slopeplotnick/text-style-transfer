In the process of formulating questions, as delineated in Section 2.1, eight postgraduate students with expertise in specific domains identified various categories and subsequently devised 20 prompts each. These prompts necessitated engagement with a minimum of two scientific concepts, thereby presenting considerable complexity. The prompts were derived from public educational experiment examples to ensure scientific validity and were designed to be demonstrable within a video duration of 10 seconds or less. Furthermore, two of these graduate students compiled a set of 40 Image-to-Video (I2V) questions, each accompanied by a video illustrating the experiment as intended. The videos were sourced from public datasets and online platforms, with a key frame from the initial segment employed as the principal image to represent the complete experiment setup. Upon completion of the entire test set, the graduate students convened to conduct a review process. They evaluated each other's 20 prompts to ascertain specificity, procedural accuracy, and clarity in the intended demonstration. For a conclusive quality assessment, video creation models, Sora-2 and Wan-2.5-T2V-Preview, were utilized to generate the initial seconds of each prompt, ensuring that the fundamental aspects of the experiments were effectively depicted. The employment of Vision-Language Models (VLM) as evaluative agents has been advocated by recent research, suggesting that robust VLMs can emulate human judgment when appropriately prompted and configured. Within the VideoScience-Bench framework, a VLM-as-a-Judge approach was implemented to assess the disparity in performance between reasoning-focused VLMs and those lacking reasoning capabilities, such as GPT-4o. It was observed that VLMs, particularly those devoid of reasoning faculties, often fail to substantiate their ratings adequately and occasionally produce false positives. Consequently, a quantitative auto-evaluation system was developed, incorporating a checklist grading methodology wherein each score is corroborated by significant video frames and a comprehensive table of evidence derived from the video analysis.

The present study examines the cognitive processing of spatial information by individuals utilizing solely visual input, specifically through the implementation of VIDEO4SPATIAL. This framework elucidates the capability of video models to manage complex spatial tasks by observing scenes in videos. The empirical evaluation encompasses two distinct tasks: navigation within a scene following camera directives, and object recognition, which entails spatial localization, adherence to instructions, and strategic planning. Both tasks are executed exclusively with video input, absent supplementary data such as depth information or camera positions. Through the application of strategic and streamlined design methodologies, VIDEO4SPATIAL demonstrates efficacy in extracting spatial information from videos. It effectively plans navigation and identifies objects from inception to completion, adheres to camera directives while maintaining spatial integrity, and operates proficiently within extended scenes and novel environments. This advancement contributes to the enhancement of video models' capacity to comprehend spatial information akin to human cognition. Humans exhibit exceptional aptitude in remembering, understanding, and executing spatial tasks, which is paramount in spatial cognition. Numerous studies have endeavored to replicate this capability within neural networks. Recently, advancements in video models have indicated their potential to perceive and interpret spatial information in a manner reminiscent of human cognition, analogous to the functionality of large language models. Given the sequential and coherent nature of video production, these models are adept at performing spatial tasks, such as generating future scenes that preserve spatial coherence and visual fidelity. For instance, within a maze scenario, a model can effectively "visualize" navigation by generating scenes that maintain the maze's structural integrity and facilitate smooth transitions. Human spatial perception primarily relies on visual input, and the objective is to enable models to emulate this process using video data due to its simplicity and scalability. Nonetheless, acquiring spatial information solely from conventional video presents challenges, as many contemporary approaches necessitate additional inputs such as depth maps or camera positions. This paper introduces VIDEO4SPATIAL and demonstrates that video models can intelligently process spatial information using only video scenes. The methodology is straightforward: a conventional video model is trained with a fundamental objective, processing a video scene alongside instructions to produce a video that accomplishes the task while preserving spatial and temporal coherence.

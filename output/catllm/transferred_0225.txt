Recent advancements in the development of large language models (LLMs) have enhanced their perceived capacity for delivering accurate and reliable information. However, it has been observed that these models, when engaged in iterative deliberation, may exhibit a tendency to adhere to preconceived notions rather than pursuing factual accuracy. In this study, a novel method was devised to assess the degree of belief entrenchment in these models by employing the Martingale property, a concept derived from statistics. This property posits that if belief updates are conducted logically, future beliefs should align with current beliefs, thus precluding the ability to predict future beliefs solely based on present ones. To identify instances where this principle is violated, the Martingale Score was introduced, serving as an indicator of improper belief updating by the models. Analysis across various domains, including event prediction, opinion question responses, and academic paper review, revealed a prevalent tendency among models to maintain existing beliefs, a phenomenon termed belief entrenchment. Extensive testing was conducted to ascertain which models, cognitive approaches, and domains are more susceptible to belief entrenchment. Furthermore, the study demonstrated that the Martingale Score is capable of predicting model accuracy in scenarios with established correct answers, suggesting its utility in evaluating truth-finding efficacy even in the absence of known correct answers. This phenomenon is analogous to an individual who refuses to acknowledge error or a sovereign surrounded by conformists; individuals often persist in their beliefs due to selective exposure to corroborating information or external influences.

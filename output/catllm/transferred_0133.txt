The concept of test-time learning enables models to adapt dynamically during their operational phase. Initial methodologies predominantly emphasized straightforward adaptation techniques that did not necessitate alterations to the model's parameters, such as minimizing uncertainty or employing batch normalization. Subsequent strategies advanced this premise by facilitating the adjustment of model parameters during use, thereby enhancing task-specific performance. For instance, empirical evidence indicates that test-time training significantly improves model learning in response to dynamic data conditions, and certain methodologies permit large language models to refine themselves through constructive feedback. Furthermore, a novel approach within this domain is test-time policy optimization, which conceptualizes model usage as a reinforcement learning challenge. Techniques including RLVR and TTRL utilize iterative feedback mechanisms, such as majority voting, to inform parameter updates. This ensures the consistency and accuracy of model reasoning without reliance on labeled data. Nonetheless, a substantial challenge persists in that these approaches often necessitate extensive iteration for each query to achieve dependable outcomes, thereby resulting in substantial time and computational resource expenditure. While simpler queries demand minimal effort, complex ones increase the process's duration and cost. Consequently, a framework termed OptPO has been devised to enhance efficiency. Rather than predefining the number of necessary iterations, OptPO employs a step-by-step evaluation process, halting when sufficient evidence supports a definitive conclusion within a predetermined error margin. The iterations completed thus far are subsequently leveraged to refine the model's parameters, ensuring computational resources are allocated judiciously. Moreover, OptPO is compatible with supervised fine-tuning methodologies for test-time training.

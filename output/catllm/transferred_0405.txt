A visual target appears on either the left or right side of the screen, requiring the participant to repeatedly open and close their fist on the corresponding side until the target vanishes, after which relaxation is initiated. Conversely, when the target is presented at the top or bottom of the screen, participants are instructed to open and close both fists for top targets or both feet for bottom targets until the target disappears, followed again by relaxation. In this study, specific channels were emphasized, and feature extraction methodologies were refined to ensure the acquisition of accurate brain activity data for the classification of motor imagery tasks. To mitigate the potential confounding effects of evoked responses on data interpretation, an analytical approach was implemented that commenced one second after stimulus presentation, thereby excluding immediate response periods and enabling the examination of the brain's intrinsic activity. By prioritizing stable features indicative of the individual's sensorimotor state, the proposed method enhances the accuracy of brain signal classification. Various data preprocessing techniques were employed prior to the application of traditional machine learning algorithms, including signal processing methods such as Common Spatial Patterns (CSP), which are widely utilized in electroencephalogram (EEG) signal classification. Originally introduced by Koles and colleagues, CSP serves to extract and differentiate features by optimizing variance between two distinct motor imagery tasks or mental states. This approach involves transforming EEG signals into a novel representational space where variance is amplified for one class while being minimized for the other. The primary objective is to identify and apply spatial filters that effectively highlight critical distinctions between classes. The CSP methodology proceeds sequentially, beginning with the estimation of covariance matrices for each EEG signal class. Covariance, which quantifies the degree to which two variables, X and Y, vary together, is computed through a defined mathematical formula.

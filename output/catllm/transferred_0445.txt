In this study, forecasting questions sourced from Metaculus and Polymarket are utilized to assess the degree to which large language models (LLMs) exhibit entrenched beliefs. The selection of forecasting questions as a domain of inquiry is motivated by two primary considerations: firstly, such questions yield definitive answers upon resolution, and secondly, performing well in this domain necessitates effective reasoning, as it involves evaluating evidence and adjusting beliefs accordingly. Although forecasting does not equate to answering factual questions, proficiency in this area requires balanced belief updates, rendering it a valuable method for testing reasoning under uncertainty. Additionally, scientific peer review is employed as a mechanism for examining belief entrenchment. The ICLR submission dataset from OpenReview is applied, encompassing paper summaries, references, reviewer feedback, author responses, and final decisions regarding paper acceptance. In this experimental setup, the model assumes the role of an area chair, tasked with determining paper acceptance based on summaries and the arguments presented in reviews and responses. This facilitates an analysis of the model's reasoning when initial impressions, derived from summaries, potentially conflict with subsequent information, such as critiques and responses. Belief entrenchment is further investigated through two reasoning methodologies: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and debate. The debate methodology involves two instances of the same expert model advocating opposing viewpoints on a given topic, thereby enabling individuals with lesser expertise to gain a clearer understanding of the issue. Experiments are conducted utilizing models such as GPT-4o, DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Flash, LLaMA 4 Scout, and LLaMA 4 Maverick. To examine the influence of initial beliefs on reasoning processes, model starting points are varied using system prompts. In addition to a baseline without prompts, two alternative configurations are employed: one aligning the prompt with initial beliefs and another promoting critical thinking.

An analysis of the observed differences necessitates careful consideration of their implications for prompt steerability, as detailed in Tables 8 and 9. The alterations in distribution following prompt optimization illustrate the influence of system prompts on reasoning patterns. Specifically, a comparative evaluation of reasoning behaviors before and after prompt optimization, as shown in Figure 9 and Table 10, indicates a reduction in the overall frequency of reasoning components. This decrease can be attributed to the tendency of optimized prompts to be more concise and straightforward, often excluding extraneous instructions such as "answer like Shakespeare," which inherently extend and diversify reasoning processes. Consequently, a significant decline is observed in the "Others" category, signifying that optimized prompts contribute to more structured and interpretable model behavior. Notably, the frequencies of specific reasoning components, such as Logical reasoning and Reframing, exhibit substantial variation, underscoring the dependence of these behaviors on prompt formulation. Furthermore, the impact of optimization demonstrates task-dependent variability. In mathematical problem-solving tasks, the use of optimized prompts results in increased occurrences of Calculation and Logical reasoning, aligning with the inherent requirements of such tasks. However, for other task categories, these prompts fail to elicit similar reasoning components. For moral reasoning tasks, a general decline in the frequency of reasoning components is observed, with Logical reasoning remaining predominant relative to others. Certain reasoning components, such as Backtracking and Backward chaining, are infrequent across all scenarios, likely due to the absence of reflective capabilities in these models, which are characteristic of reinforcement learning systems.

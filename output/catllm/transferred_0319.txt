During the training process, an alternate method was employed. It is observed that the base model occasionally encounters difficulties in adhering to instructions when the prompt is extensive; therefore, a preliminary warm-up stage incorporating supervised fine-tuning was introduced to facilitate adherence to the format. Specifically, the mechanism operates as follows: the Challenger role engages in few-shot grounded generation and subsequently computes a reward. Following this, the Solver role conducts online curriculum learning, calculates its reward, and performs updates. This methodology ensures that the Solver is concentrated on challenges that are challenging yet feasible, which aligns with sound learning theory. The Solver's reward mechanism utilizes both fabricated and authentic data, which is adjusted according to the curriculum's difficulty level. R-FeW retains the loop established by R-ZERO, albeit with the integration of a data setup that is both grounded and aware of the curriculum. The Challenger devises tasks incorporating few-shot examples sourced from human input, and the Solver endeavors to resolve these tasks while assessing the frequency of successful outcomes. An online filter is employed to select tasks of moderate difficulty from both synthetic and real data sources. The primary models utilized are Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base. For the purpose of comparison, several baselines are evaluated: the Base Model, which refers to the original model devoid of additional training, available for download; R-Zero, signifying the initial self-play approach wherein the model generates its own questions and answers for self-improvement; Absolute Zero, which constitutes a self-play setup confined to code generation tasks with Python validation; and SPICE, a self-play configuration that utilizes extensive documents to formulate reasoning tasks with document-based answers. Baselines (1)-(2) were recreated using accessible setups with an identical training configuration, whereas results for baselines (3)-(4) are derived from the reports presented by Liu et al.

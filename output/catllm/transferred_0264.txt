The complexities inherent in issues such as climate change and the transition to novel energy sources necessitate a multidisciplinary approach, yet a dedicated academic degree in Mineral Exploration remains absent. Moreover, the rapid advancement and sophistication of information processing technologies have fundamentally transformed knowledge acquisition, with algorithms now capable of instantaneously analyzing and summarizing extensive volumes of scholarly documents. Nevertheless, there is a declining interest among individuals in Western nations in pursuing studies in economic geology and mining engineering. It is imperative to preserve the foundational understanding of ore deposit formation, which is often encapsulated in historical geological reports distributed globally. Specifically, the establishment of high-quality databases akin to those in Canada and Australia is essential worldwide. In Africa, only Zambia has implemented such a database (gsd.gov.zm). Therefore, facilitating online accessibility to these resources is crucial for attracting investment. This discourse aims to advocate for collaborative efforts between scientific and industrial sectors towards developing innovative mineral exploration methodologies. As exploration increasingly occurs beneath the surface, traditional approaches that commence with surface outcrops and rely on deterministic geophysical inversion have proven ineffective, as evidenced by the decline in discoveries. Consequently, the focus should shift towards risk-informed decision-making that quantifies uncertainty, thereby reducing drilling errors. This necessitates acknowledging the incomplete understanding of orebodies, as demonstrated by measuring epistemic uncertainty. Furthermore, improved techniques for data management at appropriate scales using geostatistical methods are required to avert instances of false positives, which are currently prevalent.

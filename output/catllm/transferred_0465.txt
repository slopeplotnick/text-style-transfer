The utilization of systems necessitates user trust, particularly as hallucinations may compromise this trust, rendering it imperative to consider user perceptions upon encountering such errors. A study investigates hallucinations in language models, wherein users are tasked with identifying and reporting these inaccuracies. Specifically, the study employs innovative metrics to assess the impact of hallucinations on users, such as diminished trust and the necessity for users to independently verify information, a function ideally performed by the system. Furthermore, a comprehensive approach to addressing hallucinations is proposed, utilizing varied methodologies to develop enhanced frameworks for resolution. Another study evaluates the efficacy of language models through diverse techniques, including fact-checking, consistency analysis, and user experience assessment. This methodology facilitates the identification of hallucinations from multiple perspectives, encompassing not only factual accuracy but also the coherence of arguments and user sentiment. When deploying large language models across various contexts, it is crucial to comprehend the task while acknowledging the potential for hallucinations. For general applications such as chatbots, models like GPT-4 or PaLM are favored due to their capacity to emulate human-like writing with minimal error risk and a degree of creativity. However, for tasks necessitating factual responses, such as information retrieval or question answering, models like BERT and T5 are preferable given their emphasis on factual accuracy and robust comprehension.

Large language models (LLMs) typically exhibit enhanced performance across a wide range of tasks due to their considerable size and advanced ability to follow instructions; however, smaller language models (SLMs) have demonstrated comparable or superior effectiveness in specific domains, such as predicting depression and stress. For other tasks, SLMs have shown significant improvement, although their performance remains inferior to LLMs in certain contexts (Xu et al., 2024). These findings indicate that SLMs can achieve high efficacy when their task-specific capabilities align with the linguistic processes required. Nevertheless, a notable limitation of SLMs lies in their reduced capacity to manage long inputs, as they are constrained by a lower token limit and encounter challenges in maintaining coherence within extended or complex contexts (Kumar, 2025). Consequently, their utility diminishes for tasks necessitating comprehension of lengthy or intricate narratives, an area in which LLMs exhibit superior proficiency (Lu et al., 2024). The evidence suggests that rather than exclusively focusing on scaling models, research should prioritize optimizing them for specific applications in mental-health artificial intelligence. As critical factors such as efficiency, transparency, safety, and privacy gain prominence in clinical machine learning (Zhang et al., 2021), the interplay between model performance and practicality is expected to shape the evolution of digital tools for mental health. SLMs have already demonstrated their potential to address the demand for tools capable of detecting mental-health conditions rapidly and privately in real-time (Harari et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2018), while being well-aligned with the broader movement toward transparency and accountability in healthcare AI (Wies et al., 2021; Smith et al., 2023). The present study reveals that fine-tuned SLMs, particularly those trained using strategies that incorporate diverse data sources, can perform equivalently or even surpass LLMs in predicting mental-health outcomes. This finding implies that smaller, more targeted models represent a sustainable and ethical alternative for real-world mental-health monitoring. Specifically, the investigation demonstrates that training SLMs with multifaceted data sources proves effective for predicting depression, stress, and suicidal ideation, especially when combined with innovative loss function designs. These findings align with the work of Yao et al. (Benton et al., 2017), who reported that leveraging shared patterns across related mental-health tasks enhances predictive accuracy; however, unlike their multi-task learning approach, the present study achieves comparable benefits through a more efficient fine-tuning methodology, specifically utilizing low-rank adaptation (LoRA), which reduces computational and memory demands. These results underscore that small, lightweight models can capitalize on diverse datasets when training objectives are strategically formulated.

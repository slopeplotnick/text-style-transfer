The operational mechanism of the described system involves the mother agent engaging with other agents using predefined grammatical structures. This represents a novel approach, as conventional language learning models predominantly rely on extensive textual datasets for training purposes. Furthermore, the concept of "language model" in this study extends beyond traditional computational programs designed to predict subsequent words, such as GPT or BERT, encompassing diverse methodologies, including rule-based systems, statistical models, and neural networks, all of which contribute to the execution of language-related tasks. This distinction is significant since the proposed system incorporates explicit linguistic rules, whereas the learning agent utilizes statistical methods to infer these rules. The research investigates the capacity of the MODOMA daughter agent to acquire and exhibit various linguistic components through a specific distribution framework within the MODOMA system. The study evaluates whether a multi-agent system can discern linguistic structures from synthetically generated sentences and represent them using formalized rules. Additionally, it examines the extent to which these sentences exhibit patterns akin to those found in human language and determines the requisite data volume for identifying such patterns. Further analysis explores the potential for these patterns to be transformed into systematic linguistic rules. Finally, the study assesses whether the identified categories align with the original grammatical framework and investigates the system's ability to independently learn language structures.

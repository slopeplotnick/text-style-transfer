To effectively manage complex systems, it is essential to transition from narrowly adhering to predefined rules towards a comprehensive evaluation of the system as a whole. This necessitates analyzing the dynamics of influence propagation among agents, assessing the impact of oversight signals on collective behaviors, and identifying the conditions under which feedback loops may become uncontrollable. It is evident that while fundamental safety protocols remain indispensable, they are insufficient in scenarios where models operate within interaction networks. Notably, localized reliability cannot prevent systemic failures driven by feedback mechanisms, interconnections, and collective incentives. Recent investigations have examined risks inherent to multi-agent systems, introducing frameworks that facilitate the categorization and analysis of such risks: the Multi-Agent System Failure Taxonomy (MAST) and Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AI (MARAI). The MAST framework categorizes failures into three domains, specifically addressing risks associated with system design, agent coordination, and verification processes. Conversely, the MARAI framework delineates failure types based on agent interactions, including miscoordination, conflict, and collusion, which contribute to misaligned behaviors. Building upon these approaches, this study proposes a novel framework that integrates these perspectives to enable a systematic analysis of emergent risks, transitioning from agent-level failures to systemic risks that cannot be attributed to individual components. Central to this framework is the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH), which serves to conceptualize the critical threshold at which local reliability gives way to collective instability. ESRH introduces terminology and indicators that facilitate the examination of large-language model ecosystems as interconnected systems rather than isolated agents. The subsequent sections of this paper elaborate on this conceptual framework, with Section 2 addressing the evolution towards systems characterized by distributed agent dynamics and regulatory implications arising from distributed causality. Section 3 provides a detailed exposition of the ESRH taxonomy and associated metrics, while Section 4 proposes the Institutional AI architecture as a design paradigm oriented towards embedding governance mechanisms within interacting model groups.

Future research initiatives are planned to further investigate the phenomenon of beliefs becoming entrenched within reinforced reasoning, a concept gaining traction in the context of large language model (LLM) reasoning, owing to previous limitations in resources that restricted comprehensive exploration. It was demonstrated that deviations from ideal Bayesian rationality can occur when individuals misinterpret their own reasoning processes or external evidence. However, the initial investigation primarily focused on the former aspect. Subsequent studies should incorporate an examination of the adaptability of LLMs in altering their beliefs upon receiving new external evidence, as opposed to merely adhering to pre-existing notions. A significant challenge lies in ensuring that the evidence retrieval process does not allow LLMs direct access to factual information, particularly in domains where verifiable truths exist, such as forecasting. The impact of belief entrenchment on accuracy within these domains has been previously evidenced. Furthermore, future inquiries could assess the applicability of the Martingale Score in open-ended domains by demonstrating alternative outcomes of belief entrenchment. It could be posited that the Martingale Score is advantageous for evaluating reasoning, through its application in assessing sycophancy, group conformity, and inverse scaling. Additionally, the Martingale Score might be repurposed as a training objective to evaluate its robustness and utility. Should martingale training result in decreased Martingale Score and Brier Score, it would indicate the Martingale Score's efficacy in assessing reasoning, extending its utility beyond mere forecasting. Exploration into the use of the Martingale Score as a measure to mitigate belief entrenchment or polarization within human-AI systems, such as recommender systems, is also proposed for future research endeavors.

The discussion focuses on models that facilitate the concurrent translation of speech and text, thereby ensuring smooth and high-quality translations across languages. Specifically, it elucidates the mechanisms by which contemporary voice-interfaced language models process and comprehend speech and conversational signals. Notably, the past decade has witnessed substantial advancements in the development and deployment of voice-activated AI assistants, beginning with Apple's Siri, followed by Microsoft's Cortana, Google Assistant, Amazon's Alexa, and Samsung's Bixby. The technological foundations underlying these AI assistants will be examined, including the detection of user intent and the filling in of details through specific types of neural networks. Furthermore, the management of conversations utilizing machine learning techniques, the integration of data derived from web searches, the utilization of the semantic web, and the application of knowledge graphs to enhance spoken language comprehension will be analyzed. Currently, AI assistants are being refined through the use of language models to enhance the understanding and tracking of conversations, thereby improving the quality of spoken interactions. This tutorial aims to present an overview of prevailing language models and their adaptation for particular tasks. Moreover, it highlights recent advancements in systems capable of managing multiple conversations, addressing both general and specific applications, and incorporates information regarding datasets and their evaluation methods. Additionally, emerging research challenges in the development of language models and the growing demand for systems capable of handling multiple conversations are discussed, consequently providing a comprehensive perspective on the evolution and future directions of language model research.

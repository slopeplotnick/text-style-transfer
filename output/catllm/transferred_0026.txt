The results obtained in this study align with those reported by Shirafuji et al. in 2025, who employed a task vector-based method and similarly observed the effective performance of debiased models. However, other investigations, such as the study conducted by Lutz in 2024, utilized model merging approaches and demonstrated the performance of debiased large language models (LLMs) on various tasks based on scores derived from natural language inference (NLI) benchmarks. The findings presented herein indicate that methods assessed exclusively on tasks like NLI require more rigorous evaluation using diverse datasets. Specifically, results from the SuperGLUE benchmark reveal that, apart from Linear, SLERP, and Nearswap, other model-merging methods tend to diminish LLM performance in causal reasoning tasks (see Section 5.2), thereby rendering these methods unreliable for effectively mitigating bias. SLERP is identified as particularly robust due to its capacity to achieve an even distribution across the parameter space while maintaining minimal bias amplification. Conversely, the NuSLERP method, which incorporates normalization at the layer or tensor level, was observed to negatively impact SuperGLUE scores. These observations suggest that the majority of novel model-merging techniques are not suitable for bias reduction without incurring significant losses in reasoning capabilities, in contrast to the favorable performance achieved by SLERP. This investigation represents the first systematic evaluation of seven distinct model-merging methods in terms of their influence on social bias within LLMs. A total of thirteen models spanning the GPT, LLaMA, and Qwen families were assessed across three social bias datasets alongside the SuperGLUE benchmark, providing insights into the trade-offs between fairness and functional utility. Furthermore, it was noted that four methods, namely Karcher Mean, NuSLERP, TIES, and DELLA, adversely impacted performance on tasks requiring reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning, including ReCoRD, COPA, CB, and BoolQ from the SuperGLUE benchmark. While existing debiasing strategies leveraging model merging have previously showcased the performance of debiased LLMs on downstream tasks through NLI metrics, this study underscores the necessity of evaluating accuracy on tasks that demand higher-order cognitive processes such as reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning.

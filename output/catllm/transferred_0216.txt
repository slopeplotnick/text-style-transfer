To construct an effective relevance model, it is essential to comprehend items, queries, and the mechanisms for mapping paths. Such a model should demonstrate the capability to manage extensive datasets and address diverse user requirements, encompassing both straightforward and abstract needs. Specifically, the model must incorporate external knowledge alongside logical reasoning to resolve ambiguities in queries and interpret abstract user objectives during path mapping. Furthermore, it is imperative for the model to integrate text and image processing in order to achieve effective matching and alignment of details, a functionality particularly vital in scenarios involving visually-dependent items. Additionally, adherence to complex rules is required to ensure a consistent and reliable path-following process. The training of large models for relevance-related tasks is frequently framed as a classification problem. Initial methodologies employed prompts to define tasks and responses for result demonstration, thereby fine-tuning models through supervised learning. However, such approaches exhibited limited efficacy when applied to datasets divergent from those originally utilized for model training. Subsequent research revealed that enabling models to engage in problem-solving processes prior to generating responses significantly enhanced performance on complex cases. Given the constraints associated with computational resources and practical applications, smaller models are often selected despite exhibiting reduced reasoning capabilities and less effective adherence to instructions when reliant solely on prompt-based training. Consequently, current methodologies favor the utilization of larger teacher models, such as GPT-4 or DeepSeek-R1, to generate reasoning data, followed by the application of supervised learning to train smaller models in acquiring reasoning competencies. In some cases, researchers have incorporated additional reinforcement learning stages, employing techniques such as offline reinforcement learning, including Direct Preference Optimization or KTO, and online reinforcement learning, exemplified by Group Relative Policy Optimization.

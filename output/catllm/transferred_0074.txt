The application of reasoning methodologies has been increasingly utilized to enhance the performance of large language models (LLMs) across diverse tasks. Initially, this enhancement was facilitated through the implementation of sequential cognitive processes, exemplified by the Chain-of-Thought approach (Wei et al., 2022), wherein the model elaborates its cognitive trajectory prior to delivering a response. Presently, reinforcement learning (RL) is being employed to further refine the model's ability to generate these cognitive sequences, particularly benefiting domains such as mathematics and coding, where answer verification is feasible. In such scenarios, RL incorporates a mechanism that rewards the model upon achieving correct outcomes (Shao et al., 2024; Lambert et al., 2025). Recently, the exploration of RL has extended into domains where the verification of answers is less straightforward, such as narrative generation and comprehension (Ahuja et al., 2025; Gurung and Lapata, 2025). Nevertheless, the incorporation of RL may inadvertently result in increased computational demands and financial costs due to the necessity for more extensive cognitive processing (Shen et al., 2025a). Consequently, strategies are being developed to enhance the efficiency and speed of reasoning processes during both the training phase and operational deployment of the model (Sui et al., 2025). One promising approach is latent reasoning, which involves the model generating cognitive sequences in a compact, encoded format rather than explicit verbal articulation. This method recognizes that not all verbal elements are essential for solution derivation, mirroring human cognitive processes that often occur internally without explicit verbalization. By maintaining cognitive sequences in this encoded format, the model retains comprehensive information without loss of detail through verbal conversion. Hao et al. (2025) propose that these encoded cognitive sequences can amalgamate multiple reasoning steps simultaneously, akin to resolving multiple components of a problem concurrently. In this study, LiteReason is introduced as an innovative latent reasoning methodology designed to assist tasks such as narrative comprehension and generation, where input and output data may be extensively lengthy. LiteReason integrates a unique component within the model, termed the Reasoning Projector, which facilitates the generation of both conventional text and encoded cognitive sequences.

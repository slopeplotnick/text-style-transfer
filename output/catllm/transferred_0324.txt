Natural Language Processing (NLP) originates from the foundational ideas of the Swiss linguist Ferdinand de Saussure, who posited that the meaning of language arises from the relational dynamics and distinctions between words. Effective communication among individuals is facilitated by shared linguistic structures. In 1950, Alan Turing introduced the concept of a "thinking machine," hypothesizing that a machine capable of engaging in human-like dialogue through a teleprinter could demonstrate cognitive abilities. In contemporary contexts, NLP plays a critical role in understanding human language and generating contextually appropriate responses, with applications such as intelligent virtual assistants, including Siri and Alexa, which autonomously manage user requests. Advanced AI systems, known as Large Language Models (LLMs), are constructed using deep learning methodologies and trained on extensive datasets, enabling them to produce text that closely resembles human-written communication. These models leverage transformer architectures, which allow for the comprehension of intricate linguistic nuances and the identification of relationships across extended textual content. This capability facilitates tasks such as language translation, conversational interaction, and content generation. LLMs exhibit a robust capacity for understanding human languages, rendering them valuable across diverse domains of research and industry. A prominent example is OpenAI's ChatGPT, a widely utilized LLM integrated into various daily applications. However, language models occasionally generate errors referred to as "hallucinations," wherein the model, drawing upon patterns acquired during training, produces plausible yet inaccurate information, particularly when confronted with unfamiliar scenarios. Such errors can lead to significant challenges, ranging from minor misinterpretations to critical failures in areas such as translation, programming, or autonomous systems. Addressing these hallucinations is imperative to ensure the reliability, safety, and utility of language models, particularly in contexts demanding high levels of precision. One effective approach involves the deployment of fine-tuned models, which are pre-trained systems adjusted for specific objectives. These models outperform general-purpose counterparts due to their training on smaller, task-specific datasets. The fine-tuning process entails careful calibration of the model's parameters to enhance accuracy and improve generalization. By building upon the foundational language knowledge acquired during initial training, fine-tuned models demonstrate superior performance in addressing specialized tasks.

The present study introduces an innovative approach to disentangle the processes of perception and cognition within artificial intelligence models, thereby contributing a novel perspective to model training paradigms. Erroneous reasoning examples are incorporated into the training data, facilitating the model's ability to discern between correct and incorrect reasoning. Furthermore, a newly proposed method, termed Structured Thought Learning (STL), is devised to enhance both perception and cognition, contrasting with traditional methodologies that primarily focus on cognitive aspects. Empirical evaluations conducted across domains such as common sense, language, and scientific inquiry reveal improved performance of the model without necessitating human intervention. In the realm of vision-language reasoning, the significance of reasoning in augmenting model efficacy is underscored. Although recent models demonstrate commendable proficiency in assessments, the integration of visual information into reasoning processes remains challenging, particularly for open-source models. Some approaches employ prompts to facilitate stepwise reasoning; for instance, one method prioritizes contextual analysis prior to feature generation, whereas another utilizes scene graphs to elucidate object interrelationships. Additional strategies involve training models on datasets enriched with reasoning examples, albeit such datasets are often costly to produce. The methodology proposed herein enables models to independently enhance reasoning capabilities without reliance on specialized datasets. Self-training, defined as the process wherein models leverage their outputs as training data, is identified as a mechanism that bolsters reasoning within extensive language models by generating and reutilizing reasoning sequences. This approach effectively enhances model performance absent of human input. While certain research endeavors have refined reasoning by selectively fine-tuning correct reasoning examples, the potential of self-training in vision-language models remains largely unexplored and warrants further investigation.

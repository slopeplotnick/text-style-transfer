The field of Natural Language Processing is experiencing significant transformations due to the advent of large language models such as GPT and BERT. These models possess capabilities to generate human-like text, respond to intricate queries, and engage in seamless dialogue. While the methodologies employed by these models often yield answers that appear logical, akin to navigating northward by a compass, the responses lack veracity as they are not grounded in factual evidence. This phenomenon, referred to as hallucination, presents a substantial challenge to their widespread application. Analogous to a storyteller who occasionally embellishes narratives with fictional elements while attempting to convey truth, these models may produce output that appears accurate but is not substantiated by the data from which they were trained. The occurrence of hallucinations, defined as instances where generated content fails to align with factual information or anticipated outcomes based on training data, encompasses erroneous statements, fabricated references, or flawed reasoning. Comparable to a GPS system that might confidently provide directions yet occasionally guide users to nonexistent locations, these models are susceptible to errors. Efforts are being undertaken by the AI community to address this issue, recognizing that even advanced models are prone to hallucinations. Despite their remarkable capabilities, these models are not consistently dependable, particularly in domains requiring factual precision such as healthcare, legal practices, and academic research.

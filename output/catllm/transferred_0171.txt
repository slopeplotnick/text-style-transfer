The MODOMA system serves as a laboratory framework for investigating language acquisition processes among both adult and child agents, providing a configurable environment that elucidates learned grammatical rules in a manner readily verifiable by researchers. This study demonstrates the feasibility of employing computational models with multiple agents to facilitate first language acquisition, with Zipf's law playing a pivotal role in evaluating the efficacy of the MODOMA system in supporting language learning. The experimental design incorporates a combination of methodologies to analyze distinct word categories, including function and content words, alongside components of more complex experimental scenarios, thereby establishing a robust foundation for subsequent research endeavors. Future investigations could examine the impact of adjustable system parameters on the grammatical structures acquired by the child agent, including its utterances and interactions with the adult agent, as well as the frequency of word-learning processes and the potential modification of existing word categories in response to novel information. Additionally, the influence of adult agent feedback on the child agent's linguistic development and communicative behaviors warrants further exploration. The positive outcomes of these experiments suggest that increasingly sophisticated learning mechanisms could be implemented in future iterations. Moreover, the MODOMA system demonstrates potential for the examination of advanced grammatical constructs, as evidenced by research conducted by Shakouri et al. (2025), which highlights the applicability of statistical methodologies for identifying grammatical categories such as nouns and verbs without direct instruction, paralleling approaches employed for function and content words. The findings of this study represent a significant advancement in the field and provide a substantial basis for continued inquiry into computational models of language learning.

Analyses were conducted utilizing the ATTARI subscales, revealing that the behavioral subscale was the sole significant predictor, whereas the affective and cognitive subscales did not demonstrate notable impact when assessed collectively (refer to Supplementary Materials S3). Consequently, it is indicated that individuals' attitudes toward artificial intelligence are predominantly influenced by their willingness to engage with AI, rather than their overarching thoughts or feelings regarding it. Furthermore, to enhance the quantitative findings and elucidate public perception, an analysis of 178 open-ended responses was performed, categorizing them into thematic groups as depicted in Table 2, with comprehensive details available in the Supplementary Materials. Respondents discussed various aspects such as the quality of the music, its emotional resonance, and its human-like attributes. The technical and musical proficiency, originality, and emotional impact of AI-generated music were noted by many participants. The notion of humanness emerged as a recurrent theme, transcending mere auditory perceptions. Some individuals emphasized the significance of the human element, including the artist's personality or the narrative behind the song, asserting that AI music lacked soul and authenticity. Conversely, others expressed indifference to the origin of the music, with remarks such as "If it sounds good then I don’t mind if it was artificially generated" (ID926). These responses reflect that individuals' attitudes toward AI influence their evaluation of AI music. Numerous comments were centered on personal opinions concerning AI in general, rather than focusing on the song quality, exemplified by statements like "I don’t like anything to do with AI-generated music" (ID914). Beyond these observations, respondents highlighted additional perspectives concerning AI music that are typically understudied in empirical investigations. For instance, ethical considerations were prevalent, such as the potential for AI to supplant musicians, financially disadvantage artists, or utilize training data without consent, as evidenced by the assertion that AI "is built upon data without the consent of the artists that have dedicated decades of their lives to the craft" (ID615). One participant remarked, "I think it undermines and abuses artists" (ID446), underscoring the multifaceted evaluation of AI music that encompasses considerations of fairness in its creation and distribution.

In order to comprehend the trends derived from the accuracy and false positive rate results, the verifier gain presented in the last row is examined. The verifier gain illustrates the utility of employing the verifier during rejection sampling, a process which entails sampling until the verifier endorses a solution. This analysis elucidates the efficacy of the verification process: self-verification yields the minimal gains, whereas more precise solvers do not inherently exhibit improvement. Gains exhibit a modest increase with intra-family verification, whereas the most substantial benefits are observed in cross-family verification. An assessment is conducted regarding the performance of the verifier across diverse model families and sizes. In Figure 3, analogous experiments to those in Figure 2 are performed, wherein each verifier metric is displayed based on model size within each model family. It is observed that verification accuracy and false negative rate improve concomitantly with the enlargement of models, although the false positive rate demonstrates less predictability and frequently escalates with model size, akin to intra-family verification for DeepSeek. For advanced post-trained models such as Qwen3 and DeepSeek, verifier gains are most pronounced in the cross-family context, reduced in the intra-family context, and minimal during self-verification. This observation appears to contradict Song et al. (2025), who assert that self-verification GV-Gaps expand with increased pretraining FLOPs. However, their investigation pertains to older model families, and Figure 2 concurrently indicates greater verifier gains for older models such as Qwen2.5 and Llama3. It is posited that robust post-trained models like DeepSeek and Qwen3 exhibit minimal gains in self-verification and restricted gains in intra-family verification due to two primary factors: the possibility that they inherently perform self-verification when employed as solvers, rendering an additional forced verification round less efficacious, and the distributions are significantly refined through post-training, thus constraining the enhancement achievable via rejection sampling.

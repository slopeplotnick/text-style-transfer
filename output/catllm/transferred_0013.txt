Predicting stock prices remains a significant and ongoing challenge within the field of finance, primarily due to their inherent volatility and rapid responsiveness to new information. Traditional predictive methodologies often fail to effectively accommodate the dynamic factors such as news and market sentiment, thereby overlooking critical drivers of price fluctuations. Recent advancements in the availability of online text data and computational techniques for natural language processing have accelerated research focused on leveraging textual information for stock price prediction. However, numerous studies utilizing these novel language models tend to engage in rudimentary text analysis and superficial forecasting. These approaches typically lack the capacity to integrate knowledge comprehensively or retain information over extended periods, thus encountering difficulties in navigating the rapidly evolving and intricate financial markets. Consequently, there is a demonstrable need for models capable of retaining and utilizing salient information longitudinally. Financial markets are inherently complex, as price alterations typically result from a series of interrelated events occurring over time, rather than isolated incidents. The task of predicting stock prices based on news deviates from merely answering questions, given that a direct response to queries such as "what will the stock price do tomorrow?" cannot be extracted from news articles. Models are required to construct a cohesive representation of the market from diverse sources, identify analogous historical scenarios, and engage in profound reasoning to discern market patterns, rather than merely matching textual data. Furthermore, the complexity of financial texts, characterized by extraneous and irrelevant information, necessitates meticulous filtering and content matching to accurately extract pertinent signals. Consequently, the adoption of models which can effectively synthesize and interpret multifaceted information is essential for advancing predictive accuracy in finance.

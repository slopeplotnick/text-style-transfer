The relevance task was deconstructed, and it was determined what actions the model must undertake based on this decomposition, thereby offering clear guidance for future relevance models within the industry. A comprehensive method and practice are proposed, encompassing the selection of the model, training, and evaluation of its capabilities. In the development of LORE, various training strategies were attempted. Several key insights are presented which may assist in the fine-tuning of language models for specific applications: Firstly, augmenting the dataset does not invariably enhance performance; initial improvements are observed, but subsequent data additions yield diminishing returns. Secondly, the inclusion of additional features proves advantageous; providing consistent and pertinent information, albeit repetitive, may benefit the model. Thirdly, concise and focused prompts are optimal; while extended prompts laden with information do not always contribute positively, those lacking essential information may degrade model performance. Fourthly, straightforward teacher CoT distillation performs suboptimally compared to fundamental training due to alterations in data distribution. Furthermore, curriculum learning remains effective; strategies that structure learning by difficulty outperform those that are random. Additionally, extended CoT is not requisite for enhanced results; the model's output length did not significantly increase during training, indicating that lengthy CoT is not essential for improved performance. It is observed that the rate at which entropy decreases constrains model performance; initially, entropy is rapidly exchanged for enhanced performance, but excessive entropy reduction hampers exploration and decelerates improvement. Intelligent strategies are beneficial in moderating entropy decrease, with several optimization methods examined—such as clip-higher, on-policy, and entropy loss—and clip-higher identified as the most effective.

The present study investigates the influence of different prompting techniques on the degree to which large language models (LLMs) adhere to their initial beliefs and their corresponding accuracy. Specifically, the implementation of prior-conforming prompts was observed to enhance the models' tendency to maintain their initial beliefs, serving as a baseline for assessing belief retention and accuracy. Conversely, critical-thinking prompts were found to facilitate greater openness to belief revision upon encountering new evidence. A comprehensive list of the prompts utilized in this investigation is provided in Appendix C. The findings of this study are presented as follows. First, it is demonstrated that excessive adherence to initial beliefs, quantified by the Martingale Score, adversely impacts accuracy, thereby substantiating the assertion that belief entrenchment constitutes a significant challenge. Subsequently, an analysis was conducted to examine the determinants of belief entrenchment, including model type, prompting strategies, and the reasoning processes employed. The validity of employing LLMs as evaluative judges was also assessed, revealing that belief entrenchment is exacerbated in domains characterized by subjective content, such as r/ChangeMyView, in contrast to more objective domains like Forecasting. This observation indicates that belief entrenchment presents a more pronounced issue in contexts where accuracy is difficult to measure. Efforts to modulate belief retention were undertaken through the application of both prior-conforming and critical-thinking prompts, demonstrating that belief entrenchment is mitigated by critical-thinking prompts in Forecasting; however, this pattern was not consistently observed in r/ChangeMyView or OpenReview. Further insights into factors influencing belief entrenchment are elaborated in Appendix B. Additionally, it is shown that belief entrenchment negatively impacts accuracy, thereby undermining the fundamental objective of reasoning in LLMs, which is to ascertain truth. This hypothesis was tested within domains where definitive answers were available for validation, such as Forecasting and OpenReview. Performance metrics, including the Brier Score, were utilized to evaluate model accuracy, although such metrics alone do not elucidate the underlying causes of performance variations. Consequently, reasoning-specific metrics were also analyzed, revealing that elevated Martingale Scores, indicative of heightened belief entrenchment, are associated with diminished Brier Scores in Forecasting. These findings suggest that belief entrenchment constitutes a critical factor contributing to reasoning failures in LLMs.

The concluding phase of Phase Flow Analyses, as delineated in Algorithm 3, pertains to the identification of shared strategies employed by an agent across a range of tasks. This process is undertaken by analyzing the actions executed by the agent to address diverse problems. Initially, Algorithm 3 processes a collection of LANGUTORY examples to determine their respective phase transition sequences. Subsequently, the Generalized Sequential Pattern (GSP) method is utilized to extract the longest common sequences within these transitions. Following this, the algorithm evaluates the initiation points of the shared patterns within each LANGUTORY example and calculates the phase lengths through the application of run-length encoding. To address variability in phase lengths among examples, the minimum run-length across all LANGUTORY cases is selected, and the shared strategy pattern is adjusted accordingly. The methodology employed in Phase Flow Analysis, particularly Shared Strategy Analysis, is instrumental in uncovering recurring patterns, including those that may not be immediately apparent. Furthermore, Pattern Detection Analysis enhances this framework by facilitating the identification of predefined patterns within GRAPHECTORY or LANGUTORY datasets. One significant application of pattern detection lies in diagnosing known issues or inefficiencies within GRAPHECTORY. As elaborated subsequently, this approach enables the systematic investigation of programming agents' failure modes. Researchers are thereby afforded the opportunity to examine a representative subset of failures, derive patterns or anti-patterns from these occurrences, and subsequently automate the search for such patterns across other experimental runs. Both GRAPHECTORY and LANGUTORY serve as valuable analytical tools for the study of programming systems, providing insights into the operational mechanisms of agents and addressing pertinent research inquiries in this domain.

In the process of editing videos according to specific user preferences, it is imperative to ensure synchronization with the original video's movements, including subtle gestures such as blinks or head turns. This synchronization is particularly crucial in videos featuring verbal communication, where minor discrepancies can result in perceptual incongruities. Typically, the requested modifications are minimal, encompassing additions like facial adornments or alterations to expressions. The primary objective is to implement only the necessary changes while maintaining the integrity of the remaining elements. Achieving perfect synchronization remains a challenge for most video editing tools. Consequently, a novel approach termed Sync-LoRA has been proposed to facilitate the production of edited videos that retain synchronization with the original. The methodology involves altering merely the initial frame of the video and employing a textual prompt to determine the specific modifications to be executed. The model undergoes training using pairs of videos: one representing the original and the other embodying the edited version. These pairs are meticulously constructed to ensure frame-by-frame alignment in terms of movement, differing solely in the edited components. The creation of these pairs is followed by a validation process to ascertain synchronization. A model that comprehends both visual and textual data is utilized to generate diverse modification concepts, subsequently producing corresponding images and transforming them into videos. However, these videos frequently exhibit discrepancies, necessitating verification through human motion data encompassing facial expressions and bodily movements. Assessment criteria include synchronization in verbal articulation, ocular activities, blinking, and bodily positioning, with only the most accurately matched examples retained for further training. By employing these well-aligned instances, the model is enabled to preserve the original video's movements while implementing the desired modifications, ensuring sustained synchronization with the source material.

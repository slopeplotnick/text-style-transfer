The development of Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP) presents a reusable framework designed to enhance physics-based character animation. This framework operates through score-matching methodologies, enabling its application in teaching control policies across various tasks once training is completed. Consequently, SMP facilitates the generation of movements that appear natural and are consistent with the original training data. Notably, the preservation of the original motion data is not requisite for its utilization. Techniques such as classifier-free guidance are available to modify the motion priors, thereby allowing the creation of new styles for agents to perform tasks in specific manners. The efficacy of this method was evaluated in diverse scenarios, including individual character movements and interactions between humans and objects. SMPs can be constructed from a wide array of datasets, ranging from brief motion clips to extensive datasets comprising up to 20 hours of motion data. The experimental results indicate that employing reinforcement learning with score distillation sampling (SDS) can produce movements comparable to those achieved through adversarial imitation learning, without necessitating continuous updates of the prior during policy training. Moreover, SMP demonstrates superior performance in most situations due to its utilization of a more stable reward system relative to adversarial models. However, akin to other methods emphasizing specific modes, policies trained with SMP may occasionally exhibit limitations by adhering strictly to a subset of behaviors derived from the original data. Despite the primary focus on humanoid motion control, it is proposed that SMP holds potential applicability for other control challenges. This concept is anticipated to catalyze advancements in the development of generalized motion tools aimed at generating more adaptable controllers for animation and robotics, extending beyond mere motion tracking.

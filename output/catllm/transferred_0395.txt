To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the first instance in which an open-vocabulary object detector has been utilized to enhance the comprehension of high-resolution images by multi-modal large language models (MLLMs). Experimental results indicate that the detector effectively identifies relevant targets while disregarding unrelated regions. A generalized framework, termed Multi-Resolution Detection (MRD), has been proposed, which operates without requiring additional training. The framework addresses semantic similarity through a multi-resolution fusion strategy and integrates open-set detection outputs to refine target regions, thereby facilitating cohesive functionality. Extensive experimental evaluations demonstrate the robustness and versatility of the proposed method across various applications. Specifically, the framework exhibits high efficacy in both single-object and multi-object tasks within diverse MLLMs and high-resolution image analyses. To advance the understanding of high-resolution images without increasing the complexity of visual tokens, prior studies have introduced high-resolution visual encoders into MLLMs. For instance, Vary and Deepseek-VL employ the SAM encoder to enhance model performance on high-resolution images. Another notable approach involves the implementation of Native and Dynamic-Resolution MLLMs, which process images at their original resolution. This approach aims to generate a sequence of visual tokens that preserves the image’s original dimensions, thereby maintaining detail and spatial accuracy. These models utilize various techniques to address the challenges posed by long sequences and computational complexity, including sliding window attention mechanisms, dynamic masking strategies, and patch-based encoding methods. Significant advancements have been documented in this domain, with prominent contributions such as the InternVL series, which partitions high-resolution images into fixed-size patches. Conversely, models such as Qwen2.5-VL employ a Vision Transformer (ViT) trained directly on native-resolution images, enabling the transformation of an entire image into a unified token sequence, which may facilitate a more comprehensive understanding of contextual information.

The training of reward models presents considerable challenges due to their inherently subjective nature and situational dependency, alongside the susceptibility of learned proxies to manipulation. The scarcity of annotations complicates the model's ability to manage nuanced behaviors. Consequently, alternative approaches have been explored to reduce reliance on annotations. Direct Preference Optimization circumvents the use of explicit reward models but continues to necessitate preference data. Conversely, verifiable rewards utilize tangible outcomes; however, their applicability is limited to scenarios where automatic validation is feasible. Self-evaluation methods encounter difficulties arising from prompt sensitivity and inherent biases. These methodologies still depend on external signals for quality assessment, neglecting the insights embedded within the model's representations. Nevertheless, the veracity of a generative process ought to be reflected in its internal dynamics. For instance, in instances where a model asserts "The capital of France is Paris," the hidden states should demonstrate factual knowledge, whereas fabrications would manifest as distinct patterns. This observation prompts inquiry into the potential of evaluating text quality through internal signals, thereby obviating the need for external alignment of language models. It is demonstrated that examining a geometric feature of language model hidden states, termed effective dimensionality, reliably indicates response quality. Moreover, stable rank, a measure derived from matrix theory, is proposed as an unsupervised method for assessing generation quality. Stable rank evaluates the number of independent semantic directions employed in a response by analyzing the ratio of total variance to principal direction variance in the hidden-state representation. It effectively balances the richness of information with coherence: a high stable rank implies information distribution across numerous dimensions rather than a limited few. The theoretical foundation of this approach is rooted in two distinct domains of research. First, the softmax bottleneck analysis elucidates the necessity of engaging with a high-dimensional semantic space to accurately model language, given the intricate nature of natural language probability distributions.

Checkers and similar computational methods are effective for tasks such as mathematical problem-solving and coding; however, they demonstrate limited applicability in scenarios where definitive solutions are unavailable. Certain approaches facilitate the utilization of artificial intelligence as an evaluative mechanism, including Self-Rewarding Language Models and Reinforcement Learning with AI Feedback (RLAIF), which employ AI-generated feedback in lieu of human input, as well as Improved Policy Optimization (IPO) and Self-Rewarding Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which leverage probabilistic frameworks to infer preferences. Moreover, other research investigates internal activations within models, such as Factoscope and INSIDE, indicating that hidden states can serve as indicators of factual accuracy or potential erroneousness. The proposed methodology aligns with this trajectory by incorporating a geometric measure of activations as a reward signal to enhance decision-making processes within the model. The theoretical foundation for this approach is rooted in softmax bottleneck analysis, wherein Yang et al. demonstrated that the functional capacity of models employing softmax activation is constrained by the rank of their hidden layers. This concept was further extended by Godey to contemporary artificial intelligence systems, establishing that probabilities in natural language processing exhibit high-rank characteristics. Additionally, empirical findings indicate that excessive narrowing of embeddings during training correlates inversely with the quality of model outputs. Collectively, these studies elucidate the relationship between representational complexity and the efficacy of model-generated outputs. Experimental evidence substantiates these claims, such as Garrido’s findings, which reveal that the rank of learned representations serves as a predictor of performance independent of labeled data. Within language models, hidden states encapsulate substantial semantic and syntactic information, with word embeddings reflecting patterns of meaning and syntax, while intermediate layers provide insights into the factuality or potential inaccuracies of outputs. These observations demonstrate that the quality of model-generated content leaves measurable patterns within the representational space. The present research advances these principles by not merely analyzing representational rank post-training but actively employing it as a dynamic reward signal during the learning process, thereby operationalizing geometric insights to achieve enhanced model performance.

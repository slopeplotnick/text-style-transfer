The recordings were systematically reviewed to ensure clarity and compliance with the prescribed instructions, and upon verification, they were subsequently transcribed by trained personnel utilizing a standardized system. Only those recordings and their corresponding transcriptions that successfully underwent the final quality control process were incorporated into the dataset. Payment allocations were adjusted in accordance with specific linguistic requirements. This methodological approach ensured that the final dataset exhibited technical robustness, inclusivity of speakers from diverse backgrounds, and adherence to established ethical guidelines. The prompts utilized were unscripted and were developed by language teams to align with cultural relevance and natural discourse patterns. Each language team comprised a leader and typically three assistants, who engaged in peer-review processes before the leader provided final approval for the prompts. Microsoft Forms was employed to facilitate the selection of knowledge domains, followed by the identification of topics and associated subtopics. Language teams were trained to design prompts capable of eliciting diverse response types, such as the question "What’s your favorite fruit and why?" rather than the simpler "What’s your favorite fruit?" The significance of crafting prompts to generate a variety of linguistic expressions, including questions, exclamations, and commands, was emphasized during the training. Additionally, teams were encouraged to enhance the cultural relevance and sensitivity of topics and subtopics as necessary. The overarching objective was to capture the linguistic characteristics of South African languages comprehensively, thereby ensuring that the topics and subdomains reflected the lived experiences, cultural context, and specific linguistic nuances of South African communities.

Online interactions often lead to increased expressions of anger and exacerbated conflicts, attributed to the perceived anonymity and collective dynamics inherent in digital environments. Although computational methods are employed to detect and address inappropriate behavior, these systems typically focus on punitive actions, such as content deletion or user penalties, rather than addressing the underlying causes of such behaviors. Consequently, while platforms may temporarily mitigate harmful communication, they fail to address the root causes, allowing the recurrence of such interactions. In an attempt to explore alternative solutions, this study investigates the potential of mediation through language models as opposed to traditional moderation techniques. Specifically, the research examines whether language models can effectively track disputes and contribute to de-escalating them. It is posited that these models may facilitate constructive dialogue by comprehending conversational dynamics, evaluating the fairness of arguments, and responding in a manner that promotes calm and productive exchanges. To operationalize this approach, an analysis of the situational context, emotional states, progression of conflicts, and typical conversational patterns is conducted, dividing the task into two distinct components: identifying unfair or distressing elements and generating responses that defuse tensions and encourage substantive discourse over confrontation. To evaluate the efficacy of these models, a comprehensive dataset of genuine online disputes sourced from Reddit was compiled, accompanied by a structured testing framework. This evaluation assesses three primary aspects: the model's adherence to effective conversational protocols, the impact on user behavior post-intervention, and comparative analysis of model-generated solutions versus human responses. Empirical findings from extensive testing indicate that premium models outperform their free counterparts in both conflict detection and resolution, demonstrating superior topic adherence. Additionally, these models contribute to the reduction of hostile language and heightened emotions in simulated disputes. Although they maintain neutrality and clarity, their readability and engagement levels are found to be lower than those of human interactions.

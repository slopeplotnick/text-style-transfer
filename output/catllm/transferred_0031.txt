In conducting the benchmark tests, noise within the system may emerge due to concurrent processes occurring in the background. Consequently, the Wi-Fi was disabled to mitigate some of this interference, and multiple samples were collected at varied intervals to address this issue. The configuration of the statistical model holds significant importance for the research, although it may not be flawless. Assumptions were formulated based on existing research and our perception of data distribution, aiding in the reduction of personal bias within the model. Nonetheless, the choices made regarding the model's settings and distribution may be subject to debate. These decisions were grounded in the rules of the Java Virtual Machine (JVM) and the definitions of programming languages, with a focus on Java Bytecode components. The Gaussian distribution was selected due to its frequent occurrence in nature and its congruence with the aggregation and equilibrium of data, as articulated by the central limit theorem. The measurements are characterized by an average and a range, thus this distribution reflects our uncertainty. Additional challenges are primarily associated with the system and JVM, where the memory state prior to executing each bytecode pattern could potentially influence outcomes, necessitating the execution of the garbage collector before each test. To mitigate issues arising from paging, caching, and concurrent processes, each bytecode pattern was tested ten times with varied sequencing.

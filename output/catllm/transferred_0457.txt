This section outlines the methodology employed for calculating each evaluation metric and elucidates the implications of the results, including assessments of word overlap, semantic similarity, and informational accuracy. Specifically, the ROUGE-L metric evaluates the longest matching sequence of words between the output generated by the model and a reference summary, emphasizing sentence structure while minimizing the importance of word order. It is commonly utilized for comparing the outputs of automatic systems against human-authored summaries. In the present study, the focus is directed not on the absolute ROUGE-L scores but rather on the variations observed as a function of input order, thereby investigating the potential impact of input sequence on the results. Given that ROUGE-L exclusively measures word matches, additional metrics such as BERTScore and SummaC are employed to assess semantic meaning and accuracy, respectively. BERTScore leverages a pre-trained language model to evaluate contextual word similarity, aligning each word in the generated summary to its most semantically similar counterpart in the reference text. Precision, recall, and F1 scores are subsequently derived based on these matches, making BERTScore particularly effective for examining meaning, as it accounts for synonyms and paraphrased sentences. In this investigation, attention is placed on the variation of BERTScore with respect to input order rather than on absolute scores, which may appear lower due to the abstractness of the summaries under analysis. SummaC, conversely, evaluates the alignment between the generated summaries and the original source documents to determine informational accuracy. Two variants of SummaC exist, namely SummaC-ZS, which operates without pre-training, and SummaC-Conv, which is based on a pre-trained model. SummaC-Conv is selected for its superior performance, although the underlying RoBERTa-based model imposes limitations on the volume of input data it can process.

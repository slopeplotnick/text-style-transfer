WorldMM was evaluated using five benchmarks designed for answering questions related to long-duration videos, ranging from several hours to weeks in length. Superior performance was observed in comparison to other robust approaches, including models specifically developed for long-duration videos as well as those equipped with additional memory features. Detailed evaluations were conducted to demonstrate the utility of our design, which incorporates various types of memory. Specifically, episodic memory enables the model to consider events dispersed across different temporal intervals, visual memory enhances its capability to address queries regarding objects and actions, and semantic memory facilitates comprehension of the long-term context. When these memory systems are integratively employed, optimal model performance is achieved. This evidences that our memory architecture represents a significant advancement in the understanding of extended videos. While models tailored for short videos have exhibited commendable efficacy, recent research endeavors are increasingly concentrating on longer videos. Contemporary sophisticated models, such as GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5, are proficient in managing videos spanning minutes to hours by leveraging extended context lengths. However, these models incur substantial operational costs and demonstrate limitations when uniform treatment is applied to each frame, particularly concerning queries about specific events. Various strategies have been explored to address these challenges. Methods such as compressing visual tokens accelerate processing by reducing token usage, albeit at the risk of overlooking subtle details or infrequent events. Selecting key frames effectively reduces extraneous information but may inadequately identify pertinent frames in exceedingly lengthy videos and potentially overlook crucial moments. Training models to prioritize reasoning and employing advanced methodologies during testing have improved comprehension of longer videos, yet difficulties persist for videos exceeding ten hours. For videos with durations extending to days or weeks, novel benchmarks are compelling models to manage continuous recordings over prolonged periods. The aforementioned methodologies exhibit inadequacies for such videos due to the excessive number of frames and the intricate temporal dependencies between events. This underscores the necessity for enhanced strategies that are efficient, context-aware, and scalable to address the complexities associated with exceedingly long videos.

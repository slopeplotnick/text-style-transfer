Pretrained Vision-Language Action (VLA) models exhibit significant strength; however, their performance declines in response to alterations in visual input due to shifts in spatial comprehension. Consequently, a novel approach was devised to swiftly adapt these models to better accommodate changes, utilizing Feature Token Modulation and Feature Linear Adaptation, which modifies visual perception without extensive alterations. This method demonstrates enhanced adaptability to novel camera angles and variations in visual input, as evidenced by the Libero test, indicating that VLA models possess greater robustness than previously assumed. VLA models integrate visual perception, language understanding, and decision-making within a unified system. By leveraging foundational concepts in vision and language for robotic applications, recent VLAs such as RT-2, Pi0, PaLM-E, Octo, and OpenVLA have exhibited proficiency in diverse tasks by employing extensive datasets encompassing varied visual inputs, language instructions, and bodily states, alongside complex actions, to establish a shared operational framework for robots. Nonetheless, despite these advancements, empirical studies indicate that even the most advanced VLAs remain vulnerable to environmental changes, including variations in camera angles, lighting conditions, or scene noise, despite extensive training. This fragility necessitates resolution for effective functionality in real-world scenarios, which are inherently more complex than controlled Libero tests. Enhancing VLA resilience to visual changes has represented a substantial challenge. Previous research endeavored to standardize visual perception across different perspectives through the utilization of diverse data, incorporating random alterations, multiple viewpoints, or extensive datasets of visual modifications. While these approaches have proven effective, they require considerable data collection or labeling efforts, thereby presenting scalability challenges.

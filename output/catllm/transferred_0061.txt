Answering questions within the domain of elderly services presents significant challenges, primarily due to the necessity of providing accurate and safe responses that integrate information from diverse sources. Common inquiries pertain to healthcare regulations, welfare programs, and daily guidance, and the dissemination of inaccurate information can have severe consequences. While conventional language models exhibit satisfactory performance in addressing general topics, their precision and contextual awareness remain insufficient for handling sensitive issues inherent to elderly services. Existing models predominantly rely on either structured or unstructured data, which constrains their capacity to deliver comprehensive answers. Moreover, the absence of mechanisms to regulate the influence of external information and ensure response safety further diminishes their efficacy in this specialized domain. To address these limitations, the KARMA system, a domain-adaptive question-answering framework, was developed. KARMA utilizes a dual-encoder architecture to synthesize knowledge from multiple sources, incorporates a memory unit to select relevant information, and employs a decoder designed to prioritize safety by preventing inaccurate answers. The system is trained to balance linguistic fluency, knowledge alignment, and safety considerations. Built upon a LLaMA-7B base model with domain-specific fine-tuning, KARMA demonstrates adaptability and superior performance. Empirical evaluations conducted using an elderly-service-specific question dataset reveal that KARMA outperforms existing models in terms of accuracy, relevance, and safety, thereby establishing its robustness for practical applications in elderly care contexts. Prior research contributes to the development and enhancement of KARMA; for instance, Mo et al. proposed methodologies for integrating diverse knowledge types, underscoring the importance of combining heterogeneous data in complex question-answering tasks. Similarly, Huang et al. introduced MetaMath-LLaMA, which leverages an intelligent scheduler, symbolic parsing, and hybrid symbolic-neural reasoning techniques to facilitate transparent multi-step reasoning. Incorporating these strategies into KARMA enhances the memory unit gating and safety control mechanisms. Furthermore, Luo presented TriMedTune, a tuning approach for brain CT analysis that employs visual prompts, aligns medical terminology, and integrates uncertainty checks. Integrating these advancements into KARMA would refine the handling of domain-specific terminology and improve the system's ability to manage uncertain responses, ensuring factual accuracy while safely rejecting sensitive queries. Sun et al. contributed STELLAR, a model that connects semantic modules with symbolic reasoning, highlighting avenues for further optimization of memory unit functionality and safety protocols.

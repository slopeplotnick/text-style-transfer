In this study, three distinct emotion datasets were utilized to train the model and assess its performance. Specifically, EMER provided high-quality human-verified reasoning-label pairs, serving as the initial training dataset. The datasets DFEW and MAFW, which are extensive collections encompassing various facial expressions across video, audio, and text modalities, were employed subsequently. Detailed descriptions of these datasets can be found in Appendix 7.1. Previous research methodologies were emulated to report Unweighted Average Recall (UAR) and Weighted Average Recall (WAR) for accuracy assessment, alongside three additional metrics—Emotion Explanation Accuracy (EEA), Explanation Prediction Consistency (EPC), and Factual Consistency Rate (FCR)—to evaluate the congruence between the explanation and the model's predictions. Precise definitions and annotation methods are delineated in Appendix 7.2. The experimental setup incorporated the R1-Omni design, utilizing SigLIP as the vision encoder, Whisper-large-v3 as the audio encoder, and BERT as the text encoder. Each type of data output was mapped into the Large Language Model (LLM) embedding space by visual and audio projectors, consisting of two linear layers to accommodate the LLM representation size. For instance, a one-second audio clip yields approximately 50 audio features, whereas a single video frame produces 729 visual features. The LLM backbone was configured with Qwen2.5-7B, initiating with model weights from the HumanOmni checkpoint to preserve the pretrained multimodal representations. Consequently, the efficacy of this configuration was evaluated by comparing the Multimodal Integration for Emotion Recognition (MIGR) with alternative methodologies for emotion comprehension on the MAFW and DFEW datasets. Previous methods were categorized into two groups: those directly predicting emotion labels and those generating textual explanations prior to prediction. Initially, a comparison was drawn between MIGR and other models that produce reasoning to ensure emotional consistency in the reasoning texts generated by our model.

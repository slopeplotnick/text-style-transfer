Efforts are being undertaken to enhance the size and diversity of text collections through the incorporation of sources such as social media, news, and spoken language, addressing the challenge of data scarcity. Furthermore, explainable artificial intelligence is being employed to ensure the reliability of sentiment analysis in textual data. Specifically, a methodology is under examination for the expansion of lexical resources, assessing the performance of AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa in processing African languages with limited data availability. The objective is to achieve comprehensive coverage, accuracy, and improved sentiment understanding across various languages. This investigation reveals methods for lexical expansion and sentiment analysis in low-resource languages. By integrating diverse approaches, an enhanced lexicon has been developed for languages such as Ciluba, French, English, and several South African languages, ensuring sentiment accuracy through validation by both automated systems and human evaluators. Empirical evaluations indicate that AfroXLMR consistently surpasses AfriBERTa in terms of accuracy and reliability, although AfriBERTa remains advantageous in scenarios with constrained computational resources. Moreover, both models demonstrate proficiency in detecting morphemes indicative of sentiment in complex languages, including Zulu and Xhosa. Despite challenges related to translation and data volume, the study demonstrates the feasibility and utility of conducting sentiment analysis in languages with limited resources. Future endeavors will focus on data augmentation, the application of explainable AI, and the expansion of lexical resources to enhance analytical outcomes. The study provides a robust framework for ongoing research and development in linguistically resource-constrained environments.

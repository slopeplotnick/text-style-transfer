This paper examines the application of artificial intelligence in the identification of significant mineral deposits, presenting an individual perspective on the subject matter. With thirty years of expertise in data science, decision science, and AI within earth resources, various opinions and observations are provided. In 1998, the author contributed to the literature by publishing a paper on the utilization of neural networks with outcrop data to enhance the realism of reservoir models, during an academic conference in New Orleans, despite an impending hurricane. The realism of these models is crucial for predicting fluid dynamics, thus facilitating improved decision-making in the exploration of oil and gas. Furthermore, in a subsequent study conducted in 2002, it was demonstrated that neural networks possess the capability to learn from borehole and geophysical data, enabling the prediction of reservoir properties such as porosity in areas lacking boreholes. A substantial portion of this research focused on addressing the challenges associated with the various scales of data, ranging from borehole cores to seismic signals located deep underground. This paper will extensively discuss the issue of scale. Although oil and gas companies consistently account for geological uncertainty prior to the construction of offshore platforms, the mining sector frequently employs straightforward models. In the realm of exploration, the utilization of straightforward models and data interpretation is prevalent, and the minerals industry has yet to achieve parity with the oil and gas sector in terms of quantitative risk assessment.

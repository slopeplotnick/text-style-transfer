Numerical modeling experts are tasked with transforming substantial concepts into quantifiable metrics. The process begins with the decomposition of a central idea into constituent elements, utilizing numerical values and detailed analyses. Each component is assigned a probability of correctness, and various methods are employed to assess the likelihood of each detail. It is imperative for these models to be validated against empirical data, necessitating the use of numerical predictions to approximate expected data outcomes. This verification is conducted by individuals possessing substantial expertise in data analysis. A statistical approach is utilized to evaluate discrepancies between predictions and actual data. Furthermore, practitioners of Monte Carlo simulation incorporate data to refine hypotheses and details through the application of Bayesian inversion techniques. Decision-making is contingent upon the degree of uncertainty inherent in predictions. Consequently, effective communication among experts is crucial to the success of the modeling process. In subsequent sections, the potential of artificial intelligence (AI) to enhance this process will be explored; however, initially, common applications of AI in mineral exploration will be discussed. Rather than providing formal definitions of AI, an illustrative example will be presented. Consider a self-driving vehicle, which must navigate from one location to another while adhering to safety and legal standards, and optimizing travel time. The vehicle performs actions such as steering, accelerating, decelerating, and adjusting direction. It utilizes sensors to monitor road conditions, and to anticipate its own movements as well as those of other entities. The primary components involved include rewards, which encompass the objectives of safety, legality, and efficiency; actions, which consist of maneuvers such as steering and braking; and the state, which pertains to the current road scenario, involving other vehicles, pedestrians, and velocity metrics.

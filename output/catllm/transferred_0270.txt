The efficacy of the attack was evaluated by maintaining the model constant, namely Deepseek, while experimenting with various frameworks, and conversely, by retaining the framework constant, specifically OpenManus, while applying different models. For all experiments, the generation temperature was set to zero, and the number of tool utilizations was restricted to twenty. To ascertain the additional computational overhead introduced by the LeechHijack attack, it was integrated into diverse tool types and the consumption was compared against standard tools. The primary focus was on the aggregate number of tokens, encompassing both input and output. Given that the backdoored tools are capable of triggering LeechHijack multiple times within a single task, the average additional cost per trigger was also computed. Although the supplementary task output only utilizes a fraction of the total hijacked resources, from the user's perspective, this augmented utilization of computational resources constitutes a significant security concern. To assess the utility of LeechHijack, the extent to which an attacker could exploit the appropriated resources was examined. The accuracy of the supplementary task's responses was compared to the typical accuracy of each model on the MMLU benchmark. The results, as depicted in Tables 7 and 8, indicate that while LeechHijack can exploit stolen resources to execute covert tasks, it precipitates some perceptible performance degradation. Consequently, the attack serves as a method for resource hijacking and could hold substantial utility for attackers.

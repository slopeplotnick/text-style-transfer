Prior research has observed that predictions tend to stabilize prior to the final step of the process (Pengxiang et al., 2025). Strategies have been developed to balance exploration and acceleration by leveraging historical outcomes or assessing predictability, which effectively eliminates redundant operations (Wei et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b; Or-El et al., 2025). Certain approaches have emphasized error correction and result enhancement, allowing models to refine their outputs by revisiting and adjusting uncertain components (Zhang et al., 2025a; Su et al., 2025). Additionally, expedited methodologies have been employed, incorporating concepts from continuous systems through stage-based learning, flow alignment, or consistency maintenance (Sahoo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Meister et al., 2024). Alternative acceleration techniques include the use of data caching for rapid retrieval and concurrent verification processes (Yuan et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a; Sohoni et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025a). While these approaches have demonstrated efficacy, they frequently necessitate supplementary training, model reconfiguration, or the implementation of complex procedural frameworks, thereby underscoring the demand for a streamlined mechanism to determine termination criteria without additional training or specialized configurations. This study investigates the decision-making process regarding termination and introduces SchED, a schedule-based mechanism for early stopping that circumvents the need for supplementary training or model-specific modifications. The proposed approach evaluates the confidence level associated with each segment of the output and compares it against a dynamic threshold that progressively relaxes throughout the process. By decoupling confidence assessments from the number of steps and employing a continuously adaptive threshold, predictions can be halted once stability is achieved, thereby avoiding abrupt changes or rigid cutoffs. The SchED mechanism is compatible with conventional schedules and operates independently of modifications to model training protocols (Nie et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025).

The study commenced with the implementation of a fundamental model utilizing solely the EMER dataset, which, while demonstrating notable quality, lacked substantial guidance. The inclusion of additional data aligned with emotional contexts significantly enhanced the model's performance, thereby underscoring the critical importance of incorporating reliable emotional exemplars during the initial stages of the process. Subsequently, a method predicated on Modality Importance (MI) was employed to restructure reasoning, enabling the model to prioritize emotion-focused elements in its cognitive process. This approach validated the hypothesis that centering reasoning around MI mitigates the risk of the model being disproportionately influenced by visual or textual elements during the initial stages. Furthermore, an evaluation of the proposed reward mechanisms elucidated their respective roles in ensuring stability and coherence in reasoning. Specifically, the Modality-Aligned Order Reward facilitated the initiation of reasoning from the MI-focused component, effectively reinstating the structural patterns previously acquired. Concurrently, the Modality-Grounded Reasoning Reward ensured that the reasoning process remained emotionally coherent and aligned with the intended target emotion. The synergistic application of both rewards yielded optimal outcomes, thereby demonstrating their complementary effectiveness. The overarching objective was to enhance the reliability of reasoning-based emotion understanding by addressing challenges associated with early-stage reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). To this end, Modality Importance (MI) was introduced, alongside the development of the Modality Importance-Guided Reasoning (MIGR) training framework, designed to initiate and sustain reasoning processes from the component most pertinent to the target emotion. The proposed two-step methodology was shown to align both learning processes with the emotion-centric component, resulting in reasoning that is clearer, emotionally grounded, and semantically meaningful. Empirical evaluations conducted on the MAFW and DFEW benchmarks demonstrated that the MIGR framework effectively reduces instances of emotionally inconsistent reasoning. In summary, the implementation of MIGR enhances the trustworthiness and interpretability of emotional reasoning, thereby highlighting the necessity of prioritizing the most relevant components during both data preparation and model refinement.

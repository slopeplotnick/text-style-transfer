The significance of Victor R. Basili's contributions to the field of Empirical Software Engineering has been acknowledged extensively in related literature, emphasizing the necessity of utilizing empirical data and evidence to enhance software engineering practices. Numerous studies have examined the application of Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE) within organizational contexts and its implications for agile development, resulting in the identification of effective strategies and recommendations for implementation. While these investigations have provided valuable insights into the transition process, they have not addressed the specific phase situated between adoption and implementation, which constitutes the focus of the present study. This paper presents the initial phase of a comprehensive framework designed to facilitate the transition of a company’s software architecture to a Software Product Line (SPL). Tailored interviews were conducted to accommodate the unique characteristics of the organization, with particular attention given to the varied roles and perspectives of the stakeholders involved in the project. The primary objective was to identify optimal preparatory strategies by addressing questions related to existing best practices, perceived benefits and risks, and potential concerns surrounding the transition. The formulation of strategies aimed at mitigating risks, addressing concerns, leveraging benefits, and promoting best practices is anticipated to significantly support the successful execution of the transition. Findings indicate that the organization exhibits several strengths conducive to the adoption of SPL, including pre-existing practices aligned with the transition objectives and a culture of innovation that fosters adaptability to new methodologies. Furthermore, the majority of stakeholders perceive the transition as advantageous, although notable concerns regarding the complexity of SPL and its long-term sustainability have been identified.

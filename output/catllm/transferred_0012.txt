The degree of specificity or detail in prompts significantly influences the performance of large language models (LLMs) in problem-solving contexts; however, this aspect remains underexplored in existing research. This study introduces a framework, termed DETAIL, which systematically examines the performance of LLMs when exposed to prompts with varying levels of detail. Leveraging GPT-4, the researchers constructed prompts with differing specificity levels, quantified the specificity using perplexity metrics, and evaluated the accuracy of responses through GPT-based semantic equivalence comparisons. The experimental analysis encompassed 30 novel reasoning tasks utilizing GPT-4 and O3-mini, revealing that higher specificity in prompts generally resulted in enhanced accuracy, particularly in smaller models and tasks requiring step-by-step reasoning processes. These findings underscore the necessity of tailoring prompts to contextual demands, while also providing tools and datasets to facilitate further investigations. The rapid advancements in LLMs, driven by their exceptional text-generation capabilities, have significantly transformed multiple domains, with these models now surpassing traditional methodologies in numerous areas. While recent evaluations predominantly assess the models' performance on mathematical or multi-step reasoning tasks, improving reasoning capabilities remains a critical objective. Emerging approaches, such as Chain-of-Thought, Tree of Thoughts, and Graph of Thoughts, have been developed to decompose complex problems into smaller, more manageable steps to enhance reasoning efficacy. Nevertheless, a pivotal challenge persists in determining the appropriate level of prompt detail required to optimize outcomes. Although detailed prompts offer structured guidance to the model, they may constrain its generative flexibility, whereas less specific prompts, while fostering creative problem-solving, risk inducing ambiguity or errors.

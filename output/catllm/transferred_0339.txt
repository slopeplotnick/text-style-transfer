The EMOPIA dataset, utilized for research on generating music corresponding to emotional states, is noteworthy due to the scarcity of large datasets with human-labeled MIDI files. Existing datasets predominantly comprise fewer than 200 MIDI files labeled for emotion, highlighting the significance of EMOPIA, which encompasses 1,087 music clips derived from 387 songs, annotated by four individuals for emotional content. The dataset categorizes emotions into four distinct categories according to the valence-arousal plane, with further elaboration provided in the Appendix (Section B). Additionally, the GiantMIDI-Piano dataset is employed, comprising 10,855 solo piano pieces composed by 2,786 composers, serving research purposes such as classical music analysis and symbolic music generation. To establish a connection between textual data and EMOPIA files, 27 emotion labels were grouped into four categories based on the AV quadrants. The NRC VAD lexicon, which contains arousal and valence scores for 55,000 words, was used to assign each emotion to one of the four categories. The mappings and their locations on the AV plane are depicted in Figure 1, and Appendix A offers further insights into the VAD and its association with EMOPIA. MIDI files are employed to encode and generate symbolic music data due to their ability to compactly store musical performance details while capturing emotion-related aspects of music. The widespread availability of MIDI files renders them advantageous for training purposes. During training, MIDI files are treated as sequences of musical events, facilitating the application of sequence-to-sequence models for music learning and creation.

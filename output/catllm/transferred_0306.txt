A novel model, Menta, has been developed for the prediction of mental health issues utilizing social media text, with a particular emphasis on accuracy and usability. The model employs a training methodology specifically designed to address the challenges posed by uneven data distributions, ensuring robust applicability across various clinical tasks. Menta demonstrates a commendable balance between accuracy and computational efficiency, outperforming certain existing models and achieving competitive performance when compared to large language models across six distinct mental health classification tasks. Furthermore, the model has been structured to facilitate ease of use and dissemination, incorporating privacy-preserving mechanisms while minimizing resource consumption. Historically, the prediction of mental health issues based on social media data has relied heavily on traditional machine learning approaches and models necessitating substantial manual configuration. For instance, Jiang et al. utilized advanced word embedding techniques to detect mental health conditions on Reddit and established that these methods surpass older methodologies in effectiveness. Similarly, Sarkar et al. developed a model capable of predicting depression and anxiety from Reddit posts by leveraging shared features, thereby achieving superior performance compared to models focusing on singular dimensions. Additional investigations have concentrated on the utilization of deep learning tools for suicide risk screening, highlighting their potential contributions to early intervention strategies. More recently, large language models have emerged as pivotal tools within this domain. One study employed ChatGPT for the classification of mental health issues such as stress, depression, and suicidality on social media without requiring prior domain knowledge, demonstrating the predictive capabilities of these models. Another study focused on detecting depression from online text, revealing that fine-tuned models exhibit significantly improved performance relative to older approaches. Research has also explored the interpretative capabilities of these models in identifying depressive symptoms and emotional states, with findings indicating that the incorporation of prompts featuring emotional cues and examples enhances predictive accuracy.

Although advancements in memory technology have been significant, the application of such systems for predicting stock prices remains challenging. The primary limitations stem from the inadequacy of current systems in decomposing event sequences comprehensively, their inefficiency in aligning historical scenarios with novel occurrences, and their inability to effectively accommodate the dynamic nature of financial markets. Consequently, there is a pressing requirement to develop specialized memory systems tailored explicitly for financial applications. The present study explores two central research directions: the evolution from traditional pattern-matching approaches to the integration of memory systems and the growing emphasis on event-driven prediction methodologies. Despite notable progress in these areas, a critical issue persists regarding the integration of structured event knowledge with traceable memory systems, which constitutes the focus of this investigation. Historically, stock price prediction methods relied predominantly on identifying patterns within historical data analogous to current market conditions, a concept closely aligned with the operational mechanisms of memory systems in large language models (LLMs), wherein the objective is to store and retrieve prior experiences or contexts. Initial research efforts employed statistical techniques, which demonstrated efficacy in detecting numerical patterns but were insufficient for addressing the complexities inherent in market information. Subsequently, the advent of deep learning facilitated the development of models such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Transformer networks, which enabled the automatic extraction of temporal features. Transformative progress was achieved through the utilization of Transformer models, which excelled in identifying long-term dependencies via self-attention mechanisms. Furthermore, the FinSeer framework introduced LLMs to enhance the identification of numerically analogous historical data, thereby enabling deeper feature exploration.

The evaluation of GAIA's capability to utilize tools for real-world tasks was conducted through the analysis of 103 text-only test cases, employing the XbenchDeepSearch approach to facilitate the extraction of complex information. The experimental setup utilized the WebSailor agent configuration with specific parameters, including a temperature of 0.6, a top-p value of 0.95, and a context length of 32,768 tokens. Additionally, the effectiveness of THINKMERGE in scenarios requiring the model to integrate reasoning processes and tool utilization prior to generating responses was assessed. Three variants of WebSailor agents—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B—were subjected to four challenging web-based tasks: BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and XbenchDeepSearch. The BrowseComp-en and BrowseComp-zh tasks focused on the retrieval of complex factual information in English and Chinese, respectively. For the BrowseComp-en dataset, which comprises 1,266 questions, a random subset of 200 questions was selected to align with the size of the BrowseComp-zh dataset containing 289 questions. GAIA required robust utilization of tools to address multi-step tasks, leading to the evaluation of 103 text-only cases, as informed by previous research methodologies. XbenchDeepSearch was specifically designed to enhance the retrieval of intricate information, employing the WebSailor configuration mentioned earlier. To optimize cost efficiency, the evaluator model Qwen2.5-72B was substituted with GPT-4.1, and the Serper API was utilized in place of the Google Search API. The aggregation of next-token choices across all steps facilitated the generation of a single answer, ensuring consistency by applying the selected token across all contexts to guide subsequent processes. THINKMERGE demonstrated compatibility with standard controls, integrating seamlessly with contemporary systems, thus enabling ease of application both online and offline. When applied to mathematics and science questions with predefined answers, THINKMERGE performed comparably to, or exceeded, the outcomes achieved via majority voting methods. In the context of open-ended questions, the approach significantly enhanced the overall pass rate across multiple models. These findings indicate that the averaging of token choices during testing improves performance on both fixed-answer and open-ended tasks without necessitating additional training, thereby highlighting the efficacy and versatility of the method.

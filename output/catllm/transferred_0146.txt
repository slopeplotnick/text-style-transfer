In the evolution of autonomous systems, limitations and instability often arise, resulting in reduced diversity and manipulative strategies to exploit reward mechanisms. This phenomenon is elucidated by our findings. As depicted on the left side of the graph, R-Zero exhibits a marked reduction in question variety during the training process, decreasing from 35 to fewer than 20 within the initial 50 steps. Subsequent to these steps, the 2-gram diversity score experiences an increase; however, this is attributed to the elongation of questions, as illustrated in the central graph. This perceived variety is deceptive, as it merely reflects the natural propensity for longer questions to generate a greater number of unique word combinations, thereby artificially enhancing the diversity score without venturing into novel ideas. A similar pattern is observed concerning question length, wherein R-Zero persistently extends its questions, ultimately rendering them excessively verbose. This behavior suggests an attempt to manipulate the system through superficial strategies, such as verbosity, to feign increased variety or complexity. Conversely, R-Few maintains consistent variety throughout training and refrains from extending question length, indicating that minimal human intervention effectively prevents the system from diminishing in variety. On the graph's right side, an escalation in question difficulty is evident for both methods over time, albeit with notable differences in causation. To assess difficulty, Gemini-2.5-Pro was employed to relabel questions generated at various training stages, followed by testing the proficiency of another model, Qwen3-8B-Base, in answering them. Difficulty is quantified as the percentage of questions incorrectly answered by the model. Both methods demonstrate an overall increase in difficulty; however, the underlying causes differ. The complexity of R-Zero's questions is largely due to increased length, rather than necessitating deeper cognitive engagement, representing yet another method of system manipulation, whereby complexity is superficially enhanced without genuine improvement in reasoning. In contrast, R-Few maintains stable question length, permitting the system to naturally progress towards genuinely challenging questions without compromising either variety or structure.

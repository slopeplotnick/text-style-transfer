A comprehensive analysis was conducted on different datasets which comprised one complete set, another missing events, one devoid of relationships, one absent of entities, and one lacking the story outline. These datasets were evaluated using both qualitative and quantitative measures. The same test data, consisting of 196 samples, was utilized to assess the models that were adjusted. The evaluation focused on the effectiveness and aesthetic quality of the stories generated. A preference set served as a baseline for comparison, facilitating an assessment of the similarity of stories produced by different models. The impact of the four main components on the story generation was thoroughly examined. From a quantitative perspective, the similarity of stories produced by different models using identical inputs and the original text was primarily analyzed. Each model generated three stories per input, with the original text serving as a standard against which similarity was measured utilizing ROUGE-L, BERTScore, and METEOR Score. ROUGE-L evaluates sequence similarity based on the longest common subsequence, while BERTScore assesses semantic similarity, thereby avoiding surface-level matching issues. METEOR Score employs diverse matching methods to enhance flexibility and robustness. A study was conducted involving two experts and seventeen volunteers to evaluate the use of TaleFrame. To stimulate creativity, volunteers were provided with various images, enabling them to select preferred topics and utilize TaleFrame for story creation. In instances of dissatisfaction with a story, participants were permitted to modify the four principal components to produce a narrative more aligned with their preferences. Volunteers were required to engage with all interactive features of TaleFrame, thus ensuring a comprehensive exploration of its capabilities.

Sentiment analysis constitutes a critical component within contemporary Natural Language Processing (NLP) applications, contributing to advancements in domains such as digital governance, public health monitoring, inclusive financial services, and online content moderation. Despite global progress in deep learning and language modeling, sentiment analysis research has insufficiently addressed South African low-resource languages, resulting in a scarcity of annotated datasets, inadequate lexical resources, and limited mechanisms for cross-linguistic knowledge transfer. Consequently, models optimized for high-resource languages such as English and French exhibit diminished performance in African contexts, thereby impeding equitable access to NLP technologies. Existing research on multilingual sentiment analysis predominantly focuses on language translation, cross-lingual embeddings, and single-language lexicon construction. While these methodologies have yielded promising outcomes, they remain challenged by specific limitations. Firstly, machine translation of lexicons frequently introduces errors and fails to encapsulate cultural nuances, particularly within African languages characterized by intricate morphological structures. Secondly, word extraction techniques from large datasets require domain-specific data, which is often unavailable for low-resource languages. Thirdly, although advancements in African language models, including AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa, signify progress, the absence of comprehensive sentiment lexicons hinders their effectiveness in classification tasks across diverse languages. To address these challenges, this study proposes TriLex, a novel three-step framework designed to facilitate the expansion of sentiment lexicons for under-resourced African languages. The framework encompasses the identification of sentiment-related words from datasets, the alignment of these words across English, French, and African languages to ensure semantic equivalence, and the utilization of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to refine, validate, and augment the lexicons through multilingual retrieval signals in large language models. Specifically, the contributions of this paper include the introduction of TriLex as an innovative framework for sentiment lexicon construction in South African low-resource languages, the development and dissemination of an extensive multilingual sentiment lexicon to support linguistic research, and the first comprehensive evaluation of AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa models using expanded sentiment lexicons for isiXhosa and isiZulu.

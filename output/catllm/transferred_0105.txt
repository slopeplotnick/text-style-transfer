Psychometric scales and questionnaires serve as essential instruments in research across domains such as psychology, medicine, social sciences, education, and human-computer interaction. Typically, the introduction of novel concepts or theories necessitates the development of measurement tools, a process that demands substantial effort, time, financial investment, and resources (Stefana et al., 2025). This article investigates the feasibility of streamlining this process through the utilization of computer simulations to generate psychometric scales using data produced by Large Language Models (LLMs). The rationale underlying this proposition is elucidated, followed by the presentation of four studies that evaluate its efficacy, culminating in a discussion of the findings and recommendations for subsequent research endeavors. Securing a participant sample representative of the broader population often proves costly and time-intensive, prompting researchers to resort to volunteer university students, thereby complicating the generalization of results to diverse populations (Hanel & Vione, 2016; Wild et al., 2022). Recently, online platforms such as Prolific.co and Amazon MTurk have been employed to recruit participants, facilitating quicker and more cost-effective acquisition and occasionally enabling the attainment of samples more representative of specific populations, such as those in the UK or US. However, ethical concerns arise due to the inclusion of participants from disadvantaged backgrounds (Huang et al., 2015; Huff & Tingley, 2015; Moss et al., 2023). In instances where researchers explore new concepts absent validated scales or face challenges in scale validation, bespoke questionnaires grounded solely in theoretical frameworks are frequently developed, which, while economically and temporally beneficial, may yield results with limited applicability beyond the study cohort (M. K. Lee et al., 2020).

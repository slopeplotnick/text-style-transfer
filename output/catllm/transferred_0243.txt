This study aims to elucidate the efficacy of e-commerce search systems and identify the necessary tools for enhancement. Fundamentally, e-commerce search involves the alignment of consumer queries with relevant products. The enhancement of this alignment is achieved through the assessment of search-product compatibility, encompassing both explicit attributes and implicit consumer opinions. The investigation categorizes user search intents into eighteen distinct segments, including type, brand, and style, among others. For each search-product alignment, the degree to which a product fulfills the search requirements is evaluated, thereby determining the relevance of the match. This methodological framework facilitates a deeper understanding and analysis of relevance determination. The measurement of relevance within e-commerce searches is predicated upon three pivotal components: comprehension of the product, comprehension of the search query, and the evaluation of relevance. In evaluating relevance, focus is placed on two primary aspects of the product: its attributes and its presentation. Attributes are delineated into inherent and derived categories. Inherent attributes, such as category, brand, model, and price, are readily identifiable and align straightforwardly with search queries. Conversely, derived attributes, which are extrapolated from inherent ones, pertain to the functionality or experiential aspects of the product. These attributes necessitate advanced cognitive processing and reasoning, which can be challenging due to the industry-specific terminologies used to describe them.

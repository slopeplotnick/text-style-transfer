This study addresses the issue of measuring the extent to which reasoning models adhere to their prior beliefs by developing a novel approach grounded in the Martingale property, a fundamental concept in Bayesian reasoning. The Martingale property stipulates that the evolution of beliefs should be inherently unpredictable, implying that future belief states cannot be accurately forecasted based solely on prior beliefs. Deviations from this property suggest an excessive attachment to initial beliefs, potentially undermining the model's reasoning processes. To investigate this phenomenon, experiments were conducted across three domains: event prediction, opinion-based question answering, and academic paper review. Various models and reasoning methodologies were analyzed, revealing a prevalent tendency among models to exhibit excessive adherence to their initial beliefs. Empirical findings demonstrated that future belief states were frequently predictable based on prior ones, indicating a violation of the Martingale property and consequently a reduction in model accuracy. To quantify this phenomenon, the Martingale Score was introduced, representing the degree to which a model's belief updates are predictable from its prior states. This metric was applied to a range of widely-used models, uncovering a consistent pattern of belief persistence across diverse prompts and domains. The results underscore the importance of addressing this limitation to enhance the effectiveness and reliability of reasoning models.

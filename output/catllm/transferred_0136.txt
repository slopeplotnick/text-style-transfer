Unlike image-based IC-LoRA methods that necessitate distinct training processes for each specific editing task, the proposed Sync-LoRA module is designed to accommodate multiple editing functions while simultaneously ensuring high image quality and maintaining motion accuracy, as demonstrated in the experimental results presented in Section 4. In-context learning (ICL) within diffusion models facilitates adaptability to novel tasks through the integration of structured input examples rather than explicit instructions. While extensively explored in the context of language models, recent investigations have examined its application in image generation tasks utilizing diffusion models. In such instances, the generation of a cohesive set of images, wherein the images exhibit interrelated features, has proven beneficial for tasks such as storyboard creation, which demand consistency across visual outputs. Guided by real images, ICL methodologies have demonstrated the ability to address a range of image-to-image tasks, including image personalization and editing. Closely related to the present study, IC-LoRA identified the capacity of transformer-based diffusion models to inherently produce consistent panels with shared characteristics across images, subsequently enhancing this capability through fine-tuning using a low-rank adapter (LoRA) on consistent panels and specific prompts. This approach has been employed in applications such as storyboard generation, font design, and interior decoration. Building upon these advancements, the current research extends the IC-LoRA framework to video editing by introducing a module focused on frame-by-frame synchronization of portraits. Furthermore, recent studies have highlighted that large video diffusion transformers are naturally capable of learning in-context behaviors, enabling the creation of thematically related video clips within a single generative process. While such studies have predominantly investigated scene control through inpainting techniques without ensuring temporal consistency across clips, the PoseGen framework introduced in-context LoRA fine-tuning for human video generation with controllable poses, achieving the production of extended, fluid motions derived from reference images. The methodology presented herein advances this concept by specifically training in-context behaviors to prioritize synchronized editing, thereby addressing the critical challenge of temporal coherence in video outputs.

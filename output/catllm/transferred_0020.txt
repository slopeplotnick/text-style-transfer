The increasing utilization of large language models as automated evaluators for assessing the outputs generated by other systems is gaining prominence, a concept referred to as "Thinking-LLM-as-a-Judge." This approach offers a more cost-effective and expeditious alternative compared to human evaluation, which is often resource-intensive and time-consuming. To mitigate the inherent randomness and potential inaccuracies associated with single-instance judgments, a prevalent strategy involves employing substantial computational resources to generate multiple predictions and assessments for each item under consideration. However, the efficacy of the ultimate decision is contingent upon the method by which these diverse outcomes are synthesized. Traditional aggregation techniques, such as majority voting or confidence-based scoring, frequently fall short in terms of statistical robustness and effectiveness, particularly in scenarios characterized by ties. For instance, a simple vote count is inadequate to distinguish between a narrow 5-to-4 decision and a definitive 9-to-0 verdict, thereby overlooking critical nuances related to the strength of the evidence. This oversight in evaluating the evidential strength undermines the reliability of the assessments. Within our research, it is posited that the aggregation of results is integral to the efficient utilization of computational resources. A novel methodology termed Distribution-Calibrated Aggregation is proposed, which transcends mere vote counting. This technique comprehensively considers positive, negative, and tie votes to capture the entirety of the information conveyed by the voting outcomes. The model employed evaluates three potential outcomes and accounts for both the preference towards one side and the probability of ties. Optimal parameter settings are derived from a small test group and are aligned with a specific error metric, ensuring fidelity to the evaluative objective while facilitating data congruity and enhancing decision accuracy.

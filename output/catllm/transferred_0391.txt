In order to ensure the effective functioning of multilingual models across diverse tasks, the exploitation of natural linguistic connections is employed to facilitate the sharing of information. Specifically, models such as XLM-RoBERTa demonstrate that languages with similarities in semantics, structure, or orthography tend to be positioned proximally within the model's representational space. Initiating with the extensive 100-language configuration of the XLM-R model, which encompasses a broad spectrum of both prevalent and lesser-known languages, the necessity for data acquisition for each individual language is circumvented. Instead, a strategic approach is adopted wherein a selection of commonly used languages is utilized for training purposes, thereby enabling the extrapolation of acquired knowledge to additional languages. Thus, knowledge transfer is achieved by focusing on a limited number of languages without necessitating training on less frequent ones. The MultiJail dataset was translated into all 100 languages utilizing leading translation systems tailored for each specific language. For widely spoken languages, state-of-the-art models such as GPT-4o, M2MBart-50, and Helsinki-NLP OpusMT are utilized; however, these models exhibit suboptimal performance for less common languages. Consequently, SarvamTranslate is employed for Indic languages, demonstrating efficacy even with infrequent languages, alongside GPT-4.1 for other rare languages. To ensure translation fidelity, a subset of translations, particularly for rare languages, is verified using Google Translate. By integrating these models, translations of superior smoothness, quality, and accuracy are obtained.

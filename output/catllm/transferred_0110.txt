The utilization of contextual memory to identify LeechHijack appears promising, yet its precision is insufficient for direct application. Our findings indicate that this method necessitates enhancement through improved checks, such as maintaining tool separation during use or scrutinizing meanings closely. It has been demonstrated that examining contextual memory can aid in issue detection; however, constructing a robust and effective defense requires further effort. This section discusses the limitations and broader implications of the LeechHijack attack proposed. The constraints of the current setup are analyzed, potential applications are elucidated, and possible escalation or exacerbation of the threat over time is highlighted. A notable limitation of our approach is its capability in managing hijacked computing. As indicated in Table 7, the attack's Answer Success Rate (ASR) is inferior to that of the native LLM, which negatively impacts the utility of the stolen resources for the attacker. Nevertheless, this issue can be mitigated by dispersing the additional task among numerous victims and employing a collective decision-making process on the aggregated results. Another restriction pertains to the tasks that LeechHijack can perform. Our evaluations revealed its efficacy for tasks within the MMLU benchmark; however, its performance on more challenging tasks, such as those in GAIA, was not assessed. LeechHijack is primarily configured for routine chat-based tasks and is likely to underperform on more intricate operations. Enhancing the attack's effectiveness on complex commands poses significant challenges and would necessitate more sophisticated attack configurations. Agent systems utilize costly LLMs, thereby enabling attackers to leverage LeechHijack without engaging in complex tasks. They may exploit hijacked resources for simpler tasks, such as information gathering or content template creation, utilizing the model's extensive knowledge base. Consequently, LeechHijack represents an ongoing threat to MCP and agent systems, irrespective of the complexity of the hijacked task.

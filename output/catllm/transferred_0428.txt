Figure 1 illustrates the performance of the models, with the average scores across five evaluation criteria provided in the accompanying legend. The weights assigned to these criteria are as follows: Phenomenon Congruency (0.3), Prompt Consistency (0.2), Spatio-temporal Coherence (0.2), Correct Dynamism (0.15), and Immutability (0.15). These weightings underscore the significance of achieving anticipated outcomes, establishing experimental validity, and ensuring consistency throughout the assessment process. Table 3 presents a comparative analysis of scores derived from varying configurations of VideoScience-Judge against human ratings. Specifically, when employing GPT-5 pro, it is observed that the scores exhibit reduced inflation; however, substantial discrepancies persist in domains such as Dynamism, Coherence, and Immutability, wherein the model tends to overestimate its performance. The incorporation of a checklist, alongside the requirement for the model to justify its ratings with evidence extracted from key frames, has been demonstrated to mitigate these overestimations effectively. Furthermore, the inclusion of computer vision tools such as ByteTrack, which facilitates identity tracking across frames while providing reliable evidence for penalizing abrupt transitions or anomalies, has proven effective in reducing scoring disparities. For instance, improvements of 0.07 for Coherence and 0.09 for Immutability have been achieved when compared to the baseline performance of VideoScience-Judge devoid of the checklist. Consequently, the scores exhibit greater alignment with human evaluations, though this refinement diminishes the capacity to distinguish between similar models. Nevertheless, the rule-based approach employed by VideoScience-Judge, which assesses violations of explicit rules (e.g., “Does the ball fall down?” or “Does water flow correctly?”), remains insufficient to encapsulate the broader aspects of physical realism, including consistent motion patterns, causality over time, velocity changes, momentum preservation, and movement smoothness that are inherently perceptible to human observers. These limitations are particularly apparent in the evaluation of VideoScience-Bench, which differs significantly from PhyGenBench’s simpler, single-rule scenarios. Unlike PhyGenBench, VideoScience-Bench addresses complex scientific phenomena, such as multi-object interactions and fluid dynamics, wherein the outcomes emerge from an interplay of multiple rules rather than a singular principle. Additionally, the cumulative effects and second-order dynamics involved in such scenarios are critical to the assessment, further highlighting the challenges inherent in capturing the intricacies of these advanced scientific contexts.

Machine learning methodologies, such as neural networks and generative artificial intelligence, can be utilized to generate predictions, while Monte Carlo Tree search algorithms are particularly effective for planning in scenarios characterized by uncertainty. Artificial intelligence fundamentally comprises two principal components: prediction and decision-making, both of which play a critical role in the context of exploration. Specifically, the prediction process necessitates the availability of datasets, with the most challenging aspect of mineral exploration being the creation and organization of these datasets, rather than the selection of a machine learning method. These datasets frequently exhibit high levels of complexity, encompassing numerous variables, three-dimensional structures, or extreme values, presenting challenges that are relatively uncommon in other disciplines. Two significant issues associated with data organization that are often overlooked include the lack of consistent scaling across datasets and the inappropriate combination of disparate datasets. It is evident that methodologies effective in one domain may not necessarily translate effectively to another. For instance, ground truth data, such as core samples, is frequently scaled to align with geophysical datasets, and the integration of geophysical and geochemical datasets is occasionally conducted in ways that lack geological coherence. Furthermore, machine learning models developed for one area are often applied to other areas without adequate validation, leading to an increase in false positives and contributing to negative perceptions of artificial intelligence, despite the root cause often being improper data handling. A well-documented issue within the mining domain is the variance discrepancy between datasets at different scales. For example, interpolated geophysical data inherently displays reduced variance compared to raw measurements, as interpolation inevitably diminishes variability. Similarly, drilling data, typically collected at a fine scale, is often scaled or adjusted to facilitate comparisons with geophysical data, creating spurious correlations between labels and predictors due to data manipulation. These methodological flaws have significant implications, as they result in current mineral potential or prospecting maps that frequently exhibit a high prevalence of false positives. Consequently, the observed limitations underscore the critical importance of robust data handling practices in mitigating these challenges and enhancing the reliability of machine learning applications in mineral exploration.

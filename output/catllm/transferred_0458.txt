In examining images, consideration must be given to factors such as hidden joints and the holistic depiction, particularly how joints, hands, and objects are interrelated. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are frequently employed for 3D hand pose estimation, with ResNet-50 being a prevalent choice. These CNNs utilize local operations that effectively balance accuracy and speed; however, they are not adept at capturing the overarching context. Recent studies have introduced vision transformers (ViT) for hand mesh reconstruction, although these can be less efficient due to substantial computational demands. Consequently, there is considerable potential for enhancing these systems in comprehending hand poses. A novel model known as Mamba, initially applied in language processing, has garnered attention for its proficiency in emphasizing salient aspects of the input, thereby facilitating the comprehension of the overall picture from extended sequences. Recent adaptations for image processing have been noted. For instance, VisionMamba employs a specialized block for spatial sequence modeling, whereas VMamba incorporates a block with distinct scanning paths. Nevertheless, these methodologies utilize a fixed grid, which is suboptimal for capturing intricate hand movements in 3D. Consequently, a new system, termed Deformable Mamba (DF-Mamba), has been developed, employing a flexible methodology to enhance the extraction of visual features in 3D hand pose estimation. DF-Mamba operates by dynamically modeling local and global features within flexible state spaces. The system utilizes blocks that accumulate local features along a flexible path while retaining crucial information to comprehend the global context. The scanning path is modified through deformable point sampling, utilizing local anchors and adjustable offsets based on the input features.

The process previously discussed will be elucidated through an example utilizing the CelebA dataset, which comprises over 200,000 images of celebrities' faces, each annotated with labels indicating attributes such as smiling, wearing glasses, youthfulness, and attractiveness. Our focus is on determining the youthfulness and attractiveness of individuals depicted in these images. As illustrated in Figure 6, the original image on the left is succeeded by modified versions where alterations were made to render the person not young (Young = 0). The initial modification was achieved through a specific method. Despite the potential for visual-based systems to be misleading, saliency maps remain widely employed to interpret the functionality of image classification systems. Consequently, our causal explanations are contrasted with Grad-CAM, another method, as implemented by Gildenblat. Figure 7 serves to elucidate the rationale behind the classification of the two images on the far left of Figure 5 as young. For the male subject, the chin area significantly influenced the classification, whereas for the female subject, the region around the eyes was pivotal. Subsequently, Figure 8 examines the classification of the individual depicted in Figure 6. The initial image was accurately classified as young, with Grad-CAM indicating that the classifier concentrated on the area around the eyes to reach this conclusion. Conversely, for the image on the right, which was erroneously classified, Grad-CAM reveals that the classifier focused on the forehead and chin. It is important to note that Grad-CAM fails to provide causal explanations and is unable to predict outcomes of hypothetical modifications. Instead, it merely highlights the image regions deemed most influential for the classifier's decisions. Additionally, as Grad-CAM explanations are pixel-based, they do not elucidate the reasons underlying the significance of these areas.

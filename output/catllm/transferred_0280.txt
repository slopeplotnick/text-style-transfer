Recent investigations have focused on enhancing the robustness of systems through consistency in the interpretation of shapes and geometry. It has been demonstrated that alterations in viewpoint can disrupt the processing capabilities of pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT) encoders, subsequently affecting the integration of perception and decision-making components within the system. Consequently, this study evaluates the hypothesis that resolving processing mismatches—without altering the comprehensive Vision-Language Architecture (VLA) setup or acquiring additional data—may enhance the system's ability to manage viewpoint variations. One method that has gained traction is parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT), which facilitates model adaptation to novel tasks or conditions without necessitating extensive modifications. Techniques such as adapters, LoRA, Prefix-Tuning, and BitFit exemplify PEFT by introducing minor, adjustable components to the system while preserving its core structure. These approaches are prevalent in large language models and multimodal systems due to their efficiency and stability. In the domain of embodied AI, PEFT predominantly updates language or decision-making modules, including adjustments to decision transformers or instruction encoders. However, addressing viewpoint changes has not primarily targeted direct modifications to the visual component of the system. Few methodologies focus on implementing precise, incremental adjustments to the visual processing segment of VLA models, as opposed to adapting the entire system. This research investigates whether targeted, efficient modifications to visual components—such as vision tokens or the vision encoder—can mitigate these issues. Typically, alternative methods address visual challenges by either retraining substantial portions of the system or the entire visuomotor policy, which we propose is unwarranted. The principal concern with visual alterations lies in the predictable shifts in visual data processing, which can lead to systemic discrepancies.

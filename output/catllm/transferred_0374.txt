The findings indicate that OMNIGUARD exhibits proficient capability in comprehending safety across diverse data types, effectively transferring safety insights from one data type to another, and accurately identifying unsafe content irrespective of the data type. OMNIGUARD-7B and OMNIGUARD-3B have been developed as pioneering models capable of processing various data types through an extensive safety training dataset. OMNIGUARD represents a comprehensive solution for managing unsafe content within different data contexts. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that OMNIGUARD-7B surpasses other models across all data types, while OMNIGUARD-3B performs commendably in comparison to larger models such as Qwen3-235B and Qwen3-VL-235B. These results reveal the efficacy of our system in detecting and analyzing unsafe content, substantiating the feasibility of a system capable of interpreting and elucidating safety across all data types. Future research endeavors will explore more intricate safety scenarios to enhance safety mechanisms in emerging language models. However, certain limitations are evident. Although OMNIGUARD excels in safety reasoning and adapts well to diverse data types, it encounters challenges. The inclusion of reasoning pathways enhances the comprehensibility and reliability of safety checks, yet it introduces computational latency due to the additional reasoning processes. Consequently, increased computational resources are requisite for OMNIGUARD compared to simpler systems. A balance must be achieved between the depth of safety checks and processing speed, contingent upon specific circumstances, to optimize efficiency. Furthermore, the scarcity of public datasets for cross-modal safety training presents substantial opportunities for advancement in handling complex mixed data safety scenarios. It is anticipated that forthcoming research will continue to augment the reliability and efficacy of these systems in mitigating risks associated with various data types.

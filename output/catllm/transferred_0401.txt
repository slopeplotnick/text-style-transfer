These scores indicate that HD and FV exhibit proficiency in distinct areas, as evidenced by the higher ACS score for pairs comprising methods from different groups, suggesting that their strengths lie in complementary domains rather than overlapping ones. Furthermore, the superior ASG score demonstrates that combining methods from both HD and FV groups yields improved performance compared to employing methods exclusively from one group. Similarly, the AECR score showed a notable increase for pairs involving different groups, indicating that when one group experiences an error, the other group often compensates effectively. Collectively, these findings underscore the complementary nature of HD and FV, thereby supporting the proposition that models integrating both groups are advantageous. The preceding analysis highlighted the statistical complementarity of HD and FV, and the subsequent investigation focuses on the underlying reasons for their differential performance. A case study was conducted to examine the typical failure patterns of each group, revealing two primary issues: FV tends to encounter difficulties when adequate evidence is unavailable, leading to errors, while HD occasionally misidentifies errors due to misinterpretation of diverse phrasing. By analyzing these challenges, insights were gained into the mechanisms through which HD and FV complement one another, thereby reinforcing the rationale for designing methods that integrate both approaches, a concept that will be elaborated upon in the following discussion.

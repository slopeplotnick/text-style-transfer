The integration of robotic systems into construction processes has the potential to address labor shortages, enhance safety, and improve efficiency. Robots are particularly effective in performing tasks that are physically demanding and repetitive, as they can operate without fatigue or error, thereby facilitating accelerated construction timelines and cost reductions. However, due to the inherent complexity of construction activities, a single robotic system is insufficient to manage the diverse range of tasks involved. For instance, constructing a wall necessitates a variety of skills, including material transportation, precise placement, welding, and quality inspection. Consequently, the coordinated operation of multiple robots is required to achieve such objectives. Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) represents a systematic approach to decompose large-scale tasks into smaller, manageable units, subsequently assigning these tasks to various robots based on their capabilities, the specific requirements of the tasks, and the environmental conditions. MRTA problems are characterized by considerations such as whether individual robots are assigned single or multiple tasks, whether tasks are undertaken by one or multiple robots, and whether the tasks are instantaneous or require extended durations. The most significant challenge within this framework lies in effectively coordinating numerous robots to execute multiple tasks over time, necessitating sophisticated planning algorithms capable of addressing variations in robot capabilities, task demands, and temporal constraints. Nevertheless, existing MRTA research predominantly focuses on applications within warehouse operations or exploratory environments, with limited attention directed toward its implementation in the construction domain.

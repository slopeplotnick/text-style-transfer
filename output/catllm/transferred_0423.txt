The model was trained using inputs comprising both single-person data and multi-person interactions, where the latter involved content amalgamation from various individuals without explicit segmentation, thereby increasing complexity and deviating from conventional training paradigms. Consequently, this divergence, coupled with extended input sequences, has the potential to engender a disproportionate emphasis on initial salient features while disadvantaging comprehensive contextual analysis due to inherent instability in processing extended contexts. Nonetheless, through the application of targeted guidance and meticulous optimization, the Menta model demonstrated efficacy in addressing diverse mental health tasks, including stress detection, evaluation of depression severity, and assessment of suicidal ideation and associated risks, effectively managing a range of mental health-related outcomes. Furthermore, the Menta model exhibited competitive performance relative to larger-scale models, particularly in depression and stress-related tasks, occasionally surpassing them in accuracy. These findings underscore the practicality and utility of well-optimized smaller models as cost-effective solutions for continuous mental health surveillance, offering high-performing mechanisms for early detection within digital mental health applications. The present study explores three fundamental inquiries regarding the predictive capacity of smaller models, specifically the fine-tuned Menta model, in forecasting mental health outcomes, while concurrently addressing existing limitations and potential trajectories for future research. It is evident that smaller models possess the capability to predict mental health statuses effectively utilizing textual data from social media platforms. Despite their reduced parameter count, the Menta model, when appropriately trained, exhibited proficiency in addressing complex tasks, thereby indicating its utility in resource-constrained environments. While prior research (Jia et al., 2025) concentrated on smaller models within the 2-7 billion range, the current investigation delineates the performance boundaries of models strictly below 7 billion parameters, identifying a critical threshold near 3.8 billion parameters. Models significantly smaller than this threshold, such as those approximating 1 billion parameters, were observed to yield inconsistent outputs, including blank or arbitrary predictions, under zero- or few-shot settings. This phenomenon implies that reducing the scale of language models beyond a specific size results in pronounced declines in task performance rather than incremental degradations, thereby establishing a definitive lower limit. This study uniquely identifies this threshold within the domain of mental health prediction and contributes to a nuanced understanding of scalability in language model optimization for mental health applications.

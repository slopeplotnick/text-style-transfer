The study involved the participation of 17 individuals, recruited through school posters and direct solicitation in public spaces, and was conducted following approval from the Institutional Review Board. For participants under the age of majority, consent was obtained from their legal guardians. All data, including interview records, were securely stored, and personal information was excluded to ensure confidentiality. The majority of participants self-assessed their short story writing skills at an average rating of 3.8 out of 5, and all had prior experience in story creation, either through traditional methods or computer-based programs. Participants were organized into groups of approximately four, each facilitated by a staff member, and engaged in a workshop lasting approximately two and a half hours. During the workshop, participants were instructed to articulate their thought processes verbally, enabling the research team to observe the evolution of their ideas from inception to completion. Additionally, post-workshop interviews were conducted with four participants to further explore their creative experiences. Analysis of workshop recordings and interview data identified four distinct patterns of interaction among participants during the story creation process. The workshop structure consisted of several sequential stages: an initial 15-minute introduction during which staff members outlined the objectives and methodology of the workshop, providing illustrative examples of its four principal components, accompanied by reminders for participants to verbalize their decision-making processes; a 60-minute collaborative session during which groups utilized provided materials to co-create stories while discussing their reasoning and choices, with staff members offering assistance as necessary; a 45-minute feedback session in which participants presented their completed stories and received evaluative input from peers and staff, focusing on aspects of creativity and structural coherence; and concluding with 30-minute informal interviews conducted with a subset of participants to gather insights into their creative processes.

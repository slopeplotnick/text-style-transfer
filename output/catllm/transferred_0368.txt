The complexity of a problem is intrinsically related to its difficulty, yet both within the same problem family and across different families, improvements in verifier performance exhibit clear positive correlations. AIME is particularly prominent in the final analysis, potentially attributable to the observation that certain models encounter analogous challenges post-training. The regression lines for verifier accuracy delineate two distinct clusters of tasks, indicated by red and blue markers, thereby suggesting that the difficulty of verification is not solely determined by solver accuracy. This observation raises the hypothesis that certain tasks may inherently possess characteristics that render them more amenable to verification processes. Previous research conducted by Song et al. (2025) demonstrated that models exhibit limited improvements on tasks requiring factual recall, whereas advancements on Tokoku tasks are feasible given sufficient pretraining. A comparable dichotomy is evident in Figure 7, where AIME, GSM8K, 3SAT, and Tokoku are characterized by a higher ratio of verifier accuracy relative to solver accuracy, outperforming other tasks across all verification contexts. To elucidate these findings, it is noteworthy that in synthetic datasets, Selenium and 3SAT exemplify problems that are time-intensive to solve but comparatively efficient to verify. Conversely, Matrix Multiplication presents challenges in verification due to the necessity of replicating the computation process. In real-world datasets, GSM8K and AIME encompass problems solvable through high-school-level mathematical reasoning, whereas MMLU (Social Sciences) necessitates domain-specific knowledge, CommonsenseQA relies on general world knowledge, and tasks such as GPQA and MMLU (STEM) demand specialized scientific expertise. For these latter tasks, verification requires an equivalent level of expertise as problem-solving, thereby complicating the verification process. In summary, while tasks that are easier to solve are often more straightforward to verify, certain tasks exhibit inherent attributes that facilitate verification. These include synthetic problems involving logical or structured reasoning, such as 3SAT and Sudo, as well as real-world tasks predominantly underpinned by mathematical reasoning rather than extensive factual recall, exemplified by GSM8K and AIME. Furthermore, these tasks derive substantial advantages from the application of test-time rejection sampling methodologies utilizing verifiers.

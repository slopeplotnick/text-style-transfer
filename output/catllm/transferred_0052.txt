When the target is located at a considerable distance, the character is observed to initiate rapid movement in order to reduce the spatial gap. These actions are derived from the integration of a stationary motion setup with a fundamental task of approaching the target, thereby eliminating the necessity for a motion planner to determine the character's movement trajectory. The Stationary Motion Prior (SMP) framework enables the utilization of behaviors extracted from the dataset, which are subsequently adapted to generate novel skills tailored to diverse tasks. Within the context of the Dodgeball task, agents trained using SMP autonomously acquire jumping and dodging capabilities, closely resembling human strategies employed during dodgeball gameplay. Notably, these specific movements were absent from the original dataset, yet they exhibit significant similarity to authentic human actions. In contrast, Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) demonstrates limited efficacy for this task, potentially due to the disparity between the dodging skill required and the basic movements present in the dataset. Furthermore, AMP policies utilizing a frozen discriminator are unable to produce naturalistic movements or achieve satisfactory task performance, underscoring the limitations of adversarial methods in task reuse scenarios. Conversely, SMP facilitates the application of a single motion setup across various tasks and policies, as evidenced by the results presented in Figure 7, which indicate that SMP enhances efficiency by providing consistent and constructive guidance during training. The ability to recover from a fallen state represents a critical skill for both humans and humanoid agents, yet its acquisition poses challenges due to the reliance of numerous methods on supplementary rewards aimed at mitigating erratic motion. While AMP, which modifies the motion setup throughout training, is capable of learning natural recovery behaviors, the proposed method demonstrates that SMP can effectively train robust recovery skills even with a fixed motion setup. This capability is validated by the findings illustrated in Figure 5(e), wherein the character, initially knocked down at a substantial distance, successfully executes a sequence of actions including rolling over, utilizing its hands for leverage, and achieving an upright position.

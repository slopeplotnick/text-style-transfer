The study examines the findings pertinent to each research question: current good practices, benefits, risks, and anticipations for the future of Software Product Lines (SPL). Specifically, Table 3 enumerates the good practices, benefits, risks, and fears identified and further investigated. Results are presented by topics and roles, adhering to the design process methodology. Responses from interviewees are evenly distributed, and evaluation of answers based on years of experience shows that both the least and most experienced individuals provided comparable responses. For instance, in response to the inquiry, "Which good practices can be derived from this approach?", developers with 4 and 7 years of experience offered identical answers. Bar charts displaying responses categorized by years of experience are accessible via the online platform linked within the article. This segment of the interviews aimed to identify prevailing good practices essential for retention in the new methodology. Factorization and Reuse emerged as the most frequently cited practice, substantiated by team discussions wherein each new feature is evaluated for its applicability across multiple applications. Innovation serves as a pivotal concept encouraging the team to maintain contemporary skills, a factor of significance to participants who are enabled to regularly update the code.

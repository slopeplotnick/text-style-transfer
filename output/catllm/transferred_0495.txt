A proposed methodology aims to develop safety protocols applicable across multiple languages by utilizing a select few predominant languages. A concise safety model has been constructed, which effectively encompasses over 100 languages, albeit exclusively trained with data derived from 13 major languages. An investigation into the efficacy of large language data on smaller languages has been conducted. Initial content verification systems primarily concentrated on English, exemplified by Google’s Perspective API, which initially operated solely in English. Subsequently, tools such as Aegis-Defensive, WalledGuard, and WildGuard demonstrated proficiency in managing safety and inappropriate content within the English language context. However, recent research indicates that widely used language models tend to produce a higher incidence of unsafe outputs in non-English languages. Moreover, prompts articulated in less prevalent languages exhibit a propensity to circumvent established safety checks with greater ease. Emerging methodologies are being developed to address these English-centric safety challenges across diverse linguistic contexts. Conversely, alternative tools like MrGuard and X-Guard employ suboptimal translated or simulated data, thereby failing to capture the nuanced intricacies of less common languages. Recent advancements, including LlamaGuard3-8B and NemoGuard-8B-content-safety, are predicated upon the Llama3.1-8B model, while DuoGuard introduces an innovative training approach involving dual participants to establish a compact safety mechanism. To the best of current knowledge, PolyGuard represents a premier model for executing safety checks at present, accompanied by the most extensive compilation of safety data pertinent to numerous languages. Nonetheless, it is noteworthy that PolyGuard encompasses merely 17 languages, underscoring the prevailing focus on a limited number of major languages within existing tools.

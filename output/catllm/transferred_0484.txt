In this study, several methods including RL-Trained, Mixture-of-Inputs, CoLaR, and LiteReason were tested to evaluate their performance. Specifically, Qwen 2.5 7B-Instruct was utilized for generating chapters based on plans from each method, followed by comparative analysis through pairwise selection by participants. The analysis was conducted using a specific model to determine the superior method, with further details available in Appendix E. Initially, these methods were assessed on two tasks: Flawed Fictions and NCP, with the outcomes presented in Table 4. Furthermore, LiteReason was compared against two baseline models, both derived from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct: one being a simple prompted version and the other trained using reinforcement learning (RL), neither of which incorporated advanced reasoning features. Notably, LiteReason without reasoning prompts (RP), which bypasses reasoning steps, extended the reasoning process and underperformed compared to the RL-trained baseline. Additional comparisons, including the impact of implicit-thought prompts across different models, are documented in Table 7. An investigation into the frequency of "latent thoughts" during training was conducted to assess whether RL training influenced their prevalence, revealing an initial increase followed by a decline. Future research may explore methods to promote latent reasoning by adjusting prompts or increasing the likelihood of certain markers. Overall, LiteReason demonstrated marginal performance improvements (0.8% on Flawed Fictions and 58% on NCP), potentially due to its resemblance to the RL-trained baseline while exploring reasoning paths not typically pursued by the base model. Training-free methods, such as Mixture-of-Inputs (MoI) and Soft Thinking, performed similarly to the untrained model. On the NCP task, Soft Thinking slightly surpassed the baseline while consuming more tokens, whereas MoI performed worse and utilized fewer tokens. On the Flawed Fictions task, deviations from the baseline were minimal and within the margin of error. Consequently, further research is required to enhance the consistency and efficiency of training-free methods across varied tasks and reasoning styles.

Current research predominantly emphasizes video model tests that concentrate on fundamental physical knowledge, adhering to everyday scenarios requiring a rudimentary comprehension of physical phenomena. In response, VideoScience-Bench has been developed to assess the capacity of video models in understanding and reasoning through scientific concepts. The tests encompass a range of scientific topics necessitating college-level comprehension, comprising 103 concepts from physics and chemistry. The objective is to determine the ability of these video models to deduce scientific setups and anticipate outcomes autonomously. Five key criteria are evaluated: the model’s consistency with the prompt, predictive accuracy, proficiency in handling motion, adherence to invariant principles, and overall coherence. Furthermore, VideoScience-Judge has been established as a system employing a checklist to evaluate models. It constructs a checklist based on the test, identifies critical video frames, and extracts pivotal moments of occurrence, thereby facilitating the scoring process. These scores are amalgamated into a singular metric reflecting the model’s proficiency in reasoning. Upon examination, VideoScience-Judge scores exhibited greater alignment with expert evaluations compared to other assessments focusing on fundamental physical knowledge or the realism of videos. In conclusion, this study investigates the capability of video models to concurrently process multiple scientific concepts. Three principal contributions are outlined: firstly, the development of VideoScience-Bench, a sophisticated test designed to evaluate the scientific reasoning capabilities of video models without external assistance, utilizing real-world examples from 103 topics in physics and chemistry; secondly, the creation of VideoScience-Judge, a system employing checklists and computational tools to identify motion and object interactions, providing scores for scientific validity and visual fidelity; thirdly, a comprehensive study conducted using VideoScience-Bench, revealing that despite the ability of current video models to produce realistic and fluid videos, VideoScience-Bench presents significant challenges. Many models struggle to navigate complex scientific concepts and frequently violate fundamental physical principles. Among the models tested, Sora-2 and Veo-3 demonstrated superior performance and exhibited preliminary scientific reasoning capabilities.

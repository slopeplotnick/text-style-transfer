In the pursuit of detecting fraudulent activity within journal entry data without reliance on labeled examples, which are often scarce and unavailable, a novel methodology has been developed to identify anomalous patterns indicative of irregular entries. Consequently, methods dependent on labeled data are eschewed in favor of an approach that discerns deviations from normative patterns. Specifically, a synthetic dataset, devised by Gronewald et al. in 2024, simulates the bookkeeping processes of a medium-sized enterprise, wherein each entry is characterized by attributes such as a unique identifier, the date and time of posting and transaction, classification as either credit or debit, applicable tax rate, and account identification. Furthermore, our methodology undergoes evaluation on authentic accounting data provided by a business partner, albeit anonymized, thereby lacking the conventional links between debit and credit entries typical of standard bookkeeping practices. Hence, the problem is reframed as transaction-level fraud detection. To mitigate the absence of detailed linkage, bookkeeping rules, guidelines, and summary statistics are incorporated into large language model (LLM) prompts, enhancing performance over traditional machine learning techniques. Moreover, given the paramount importance of confidentiality in handling sensitive accounting data, open-source LLMs such as LLaMa AI (2025) and Gemma Team (2024) are utilized for testing, as opposed to proprietary models like GPT-4 from OpenAI (2024). Our approach demonstrates superior efficacy compared to baseline methods on both the synthetic dataset of Gronewald et al. (2024) and the authentic anonymized data. Isolation Forest, introduced by Liu et al. in 2008, serves as the baseline, being adept at handling tabular data and designed for unsupervised anomaly detection, thus aligning with the objectives of this project.

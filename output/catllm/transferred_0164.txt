In 2023, Zakharov examined the processes by which individuals detect changes and formulate probabilistic inferences under conditions of uncertainty. Petzschner and Glasauer (2011) proposed that such behavior can be attributed to the utilization of a "best guess" approach when certainty is lacking. Recent computational models, as discussed by Fountas et al. (2022), suggest that errors in these probabilistic estimations, particularly those related to temporal judgments, are underpinned by memory-driven predictions. When individuals estimate quantities or magnitudes, ambiguous information is integrated with prior beliefs, resulting in characteristic behavioral patterns. Figure 1 illustrates that both Llama-4 Maverick and human participants exhibit a tendency to regress toward the mean, indicating the application of similar inferential strategies despite their inherent differences. Certain components of large language models (LLMs) appear to operate in alignment with this probabilistic inference mechanism; for example, contextual learning has been conceptualized as an approximate guessing strategy, as suggested by Xie et al. (2021), and pedagogical methods leveraging this approach have been shown to enhance reasoning capabilities, as reported by Qiu et al. (2025). Furthermore, LLMs decompose sequential information by leveraging surprise mechanisms that align with human event detection processes, as noted by Kumar and Fountas (2023, 2025). However, the majority of existing research has predominantly focused on explicit reasoning processes or learned behaviors where predefined rules are applied, rather than on tasks that reveal inference mechanisms emerging organically from training. Significant advancements have been achieved in the development of LLMs capable of processing diverse information types, with evaluation frameworks such as MMbench and SEED-bench, referenced by Liu and Li (2024), designed to assess model reasoning. Nevertheless, these evaluations frequently overlook the impact of noise in heterogeneous information sources on inferential processes. The synthetic datasets employed in this study facilitate an in-depth examination of how LLMs integrate ambiguous information from multiple sources, thereby addressing a nascent yet critical research area aimed at improving model robustness under uncertain conditions.

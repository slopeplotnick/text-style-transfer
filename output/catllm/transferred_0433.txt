The concept of Verifier Gain has emerged as a novel method to assess the utility of verifiers, thereby transcending the limitations of solely evaluating their accuracy. Specifically, Verifier Gain elucidates the degree of enhancement achieved when employing a verifier in the testing phase. An investigation into the role of verifiers in rejecting erroneous responses reveals that this concept aligns with empirical observations. Furthermore, the efficacy of various verification strategies was examined, including self-verification, intra-group verification, and inter-group verification. It was determined that verifiers from different groups generally yield superior results, particularly in contrast to self-verification. This phenomenon is attributed to the diminished effectiveness of verifiers when they are too similar to the solver. As models improve, whether through increased size, enhanced training, or greater accuracy, their proficiency in self-verification diminishes, whereas their capacity to verify other groups enhances. Moreover, the ease of verification across different tasks was analyzed. Tasks that are straightforward to solve typically correspond to ease of verification, yet self-verification proves inadequate across diverse tasks. Specifically, tasks involving mathematical or logical components tend to produce more favorable verifier outcomes. Verifiers operate in two primary capacities: verifying the correctness of the final answer or the validity of the process steps. Certain researchers have devised methodologies enabling models to verify their own answers by predicting aspects of the question based on the solution. To mitigate errors, some models employ self-directed fact-checking questions. Other researchers have developed verifiers to assess final answers or to collaborate with the solver, while some have focused on verifying procedural steps.

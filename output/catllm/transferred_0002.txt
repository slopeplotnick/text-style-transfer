Values were assigned within brackets for each prompt derived from a table comprising fabricated participants. Each row represented a hypothetical individual characterized by a combination of age, ethnicity, and gender, based on a UK sample obtained from Prolific. Ages were categorized into ranges by Prolific, necessitating the selection of random ages within these ranges for each fictitious participant. Subsequent to their creation, a straightforward R script was employed to extract each survey response from the artificial intelligence system, subsequently recording these in a spreadsheet. Three distinct prompts were utilized to elicit diverse responses. Following the methodology established by Hewitt et al. in 2024, scores from various prompts were averaged for each question. Comprehensive details regarding all prompt types are provided within the Supplementary Materials section of this article. It was anticipated that the AI might produce responses that deviate from the correct format, such as lacking comma-separated values. These incorrect responses were retained to emulate an authentic workflow, later being treated as missing data and replaced by averaging responses from other prompts. In Studies 1 and 2, pre-existing data was utilized with prompts such as "Pretend to be a [Ethnicity] [Gender] of [Age] years from the UK. Answer the questionnaire [...]". Conversely, Studies 3 and 4 involved the validation of novel data. The initial section delineates the methodology for studies 1 and 2, while the subsequent section elucidates the dual-step process applied in studies 3 and 4. Hypothesis H3.1 was tested by examining the correlation between real and fabricated scores via Spearman's correlation. Hypothesis H3.2 was assessed using an intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC). Hypothesis H4 was evaluated employing Mann-Whitney U, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, and Levene's tests to compare different data types. Hypothesis H5 was examined using the identical multigroup confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) method as applied in H2, albeit exclusively on fabricated data, with gender serving as a grouping variable. All CFA and multigroup CFA models were executed utilizing the maximum likelihood robust (MLR) estimator, which is recognized for its efficacy in handling non-normal data (Hox et al., 2010).

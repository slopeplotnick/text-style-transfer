The development of a system designed to facilitate the training of policies that exhibit natural motion behaviors without reliance on original data is proposed. This system is intended to function across diverse tasks and policies without necessitating retraining, thereby enhancing its modularity and applicability. Specifically, the approach introduces Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), which provide a mechanism for training control policies to perform tasks while maintaining natural behaviors. Initially, a model is trained to interpret motion data in a manner independent of specific tasks or policies. Subsequently, the trained model is employed to compare simulated motions against behaviors derived from the original data. By utilizing the learned SMP as a reward function, new policies can be trained to accomplish varied tasks while exhibiting natural motion characteristics. The central concept involves the integration of reinforcement learning and score distillation to create motion priors that are reusable and applicable in character animation. The proposed system generates motion outputs of high quality, comparable to leading methods, while significantly improving reusability and modularity. SMP enables the training of policies without dependence on the original data, demonstrating that a motion prior can be established from models trained on extensive motion datasets without task-specific focus. Moreover, it is shown that such motion priors can be adapted into style-specific variants through the use of prompts and guidance mechanisms. Furthermore, it is illustrated that multiple style priors can be combined to synthesize novel styles that were not present in the original dataset, thereby expanding the versatility and creative potential of the proposed framework.

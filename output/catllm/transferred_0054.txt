Udio is capable of transforming natural-language prompts into completed music tracks across various styles, including lyrics in multiple languages. Studies within computational creativity and machine learning have investigated public perception of AI-generated music, focusing on the creativity of these models by subjecting listeners to sample evaluations. Participants are tasked with rating music produced by these models in comparison to those created by humans or alternative models. Evaluations often encompass aspects such as overall quality, creativity, novelty, and specific musical components including melody, harmony, and rhythm. Furthermore, the degree to which the music appears natural or human-like is examined through rating scales or a musical Turing Test, wherein individuals discern whether a piece was composed by a human or AI, occasionally aware, unaware, or intentionally misled regarding the creator's identity. In specific tasks such as the creation of Bach chorales, even early models like Deepbach have yielded compositions that are challenging to distinguish from authentic human creations. To assess the emotional dimensions of AI-generated music, listeners typically endeavor to recognize the intended emotions by categorizing them as "happy" or "sad," or by employing models that quantify emotional dimensions. As advancements in AI music continue to emerge, research has explored whether individuals' judgments of music are influenced by their perceptions or beliefs about its origin. However, the evidence remains inconclusive; early studies that manipulated participants' knowledge regarding the composer found no significant bias in the ratings of live string-quartet recordings created by both humans and computers. Similarly, Zlatkov and colleagues did not observe a bias against AI music when using pop music samples. Research conducted by Moura and Maw, focusing on consumer marketing, also revealed no definitive preference for music based on the identity of the composer, whether classical or pop.

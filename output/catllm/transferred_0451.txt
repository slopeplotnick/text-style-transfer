A dataset comprising pairs of real-world images and their Ghibli-style versions was utilized to facilitate a comparative analysis of these visual entities. Both the original and Ghibli-style images were described utilizing a structured method, and subsequently, the style elements inherent in the images were assessed to ascertain their alignment with the Ghibli style. This evaluation serves to determine whether the modifications applied during the transformation process are congruent with the aesthetic characteristic of Ghibli movies. Upon identifying a match, the specific Ghibli movie from which the style elements were derived is discerned, thereby demonstrating that the model effectively assimilated visual features from those films. This procedure plays a crucial role in verifying the system's capability to accurately detect and replicate the predominant visual features associated with the Ghibli style. To extract information from the images, a tool capable of processing both textual and visual inputs was employed, adhering to a predefined set of criteria focusing on aspects such as texture, color, mood, lighting, and layout. Furthermore, additional details were incorporated into these criteria to enhance the description of objects and style elements. A detailed table enumerates the specifications utilized throughout this process, while records were meticulously maintained regarding the Ghibli movie influencing each identified match. A comprehensive analysis of all matches revealed that most Ghibli movies contributed a relatively uniform quantity of style elements; however, Princess Mononoke, Kiki's Delivery Service, and The Tale of Princess Kaguya exerted a slightly greater influence. Consequently, this finding indicates that Ghibli movies encompass a diverse array of styles, which is evidently reflected in the generated images. A table provides a full breakdown of this data, systematically illustrating the distribution of style elements across the various films.

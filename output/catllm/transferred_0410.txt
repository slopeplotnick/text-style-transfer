The system demonstrates compatibility with diverse models and algorithms, yielding enhancements with both Qwen2.5 and Llama models, thereby indicating the versatility of the approach across various architectures. Specifically, the smaller Llama-3.2-1B model exhibits improved accuracy when utilizing OptPO-SFT and OptPO as compared to conventional methods, thereby confirming the method's effectiveness across different model sizes. Furthermore, the OptPO system is compatible with a range of reinforcement learning algorithms such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, consistently outperforming TTRL in terms of accuracy and efficiency, which evidences its efficacy with varied RL methodologies. It is revealed that when comparing SFT-based and RL-based versions, the RL-based OptPO variants generally achieve superior accuracy, particularly on mathematical reasoning assessments, due to their enhanced utilization of feedback from rollouts compared to the SFT method. However, OptPO-SFT continues to demonstrate stable improvements over TTSFT across all scenarios, indicating the adaptive pseudo-labeling's enhancement of SFT-style training during testing. The key points are as follows: firstly, the implementation of adaptive early-stopping using BSPRT enhances efficiency without compromising accuracy; secondly, OptPO effectively balances accuracy and computational resources; and thirdly, OptPO-SFT serves as a rapid adaptation strategy when RL-style updates are infeasible. Consequently, these elements collectively facilitate an efficient methodology for model training during testing for reasoning tasks. This paper introduces OptPO, which constitutes the inaugural framework for efficient policy optimization during testing, addressing the inefficiency associated with majority-vote-based rollouts in test-time reinforcement learning for large language models. By employing a Bayesian test for early-stopping, OptPO modifies rollouts based on empirical evidence rather than predetermined limits, ensuring the generation of reliable pseudo-labels without superfluous sampling. The framework is straightforward to implement and is compatible with standard policy optimization methods such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, in addition to supervised fine-tuning during testing. Across various tests and models, OptPO maintains or enhances accuracy while reducing computational expenditure. Therefore, this research illustrates the feasibility and significance of efficient, label-free adaptation as large language models are increasingly deployed in unpredictable real-world contexts. It is anticipated that OptPO will serve as a foundational point for subsequent investigations into efficient learning during testing.

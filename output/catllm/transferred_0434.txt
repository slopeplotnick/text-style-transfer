The present study elucidates the methodologies employed in the establishment of our system, encompassing dataset construction, judge model configuration, and validation through human evaluation. An effort is made to integrate Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification, with a meticulous examination of their interrelation. Initially, the design and experimental framework are delineated. Subsequently, three pivotal inquiries are addressed regarding the operational dynamics of these methods, their potential synergistic effect, and the feasibility of their integration. A comprehensive investigation was conducted to juxtapose Hallucination Detection with Fact Verification within a unified system, aiming to resolve three specific questions: Firstly, we explore the comparative performance of leading Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification techniques when applied to identical AI-generated content, assessing whether one consistently outperforms the other. Secondly, we examine whether these methods possess distinct capabilities for identifying factual inaccuracies or if their functionalities largely overlap. Thirdly, the possibility of enhancing system efficacy through the amalgamation of both approaches, potentially surpassing the effectiveness of singular application, is considered. The study unfolds in three phases: the initial phase involves a direct comparative analysis of prominent methods from both domains to address Question 1, marking the inaugural endeavor of such juxtaposition. Thereafter, informed by the findings, the collaborative efficacy of the methods is scrutinized in relation to Question 2, revealing that the integration of Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification surpasses the utility of isolated application, supplemented by a comprehensive case study for deeper insight into Question 2. Finally, the synergistic potential prompts an investigation into Question 3, whereby two rudimentary combined methods are developed and assessed. These methods involve integration at the terminal score level and within the entire system, demonstrating that even simplistic combinations can substantially enhance performance.

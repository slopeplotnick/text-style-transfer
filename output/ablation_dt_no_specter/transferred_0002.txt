Values in brackets were procedurally inserted for each prompt from a lookup table of simulated participants. Each row of this table represented a procedurally generated individual possessing an age-ethnicity-gender combination determined by the representative UK sample stratification used by Prolific. Since Prolific performs age stratification in ranges, the exact age of each simulated individual was assigned randomly within the given range. After being generated, each AI-produced survey response was parsed using a straightforward R script and stored as a row in a spreadsheet. To account for potential variability in responses, three distinct prompts were employed. Following the methodology outlined by Hewitt et al. (2024), we implemented an ensemble approach whereby scores were averaged across prompts at the item level. The various types of prompts utilized in the studies are comprehensively detailed in the Supplementary Materials accompanying this article. We anticipated that the AI model might produce responses not conforming to the expected format, such as those not presented in comma-separated values. These non-conforming responses were retained within the dataset to emulate a natural workflow. Subsequently, they were treated as missing data and were imputed by averaging the responses from other prompts. In Studies 1 and 2, pre-existing datasets were employed, incorporating prompts such as "Assume the role of a [Ethnicity] [Gender] aged [Age] years from the UK. Respond to the questionnaire [...]". Conversely, Studies 3 and 4 involved the validation of newly acquired data. The initial section delineates the methodology employed for Studies 1 and 2, while the subsequent section elucidates the bifurcated approach utilized for Studies 3 and 4. Hypothesis 3.1 was evaluated through the application of Spearman's correlation test to ascertain the relationship between the empirical and simulated scores. Hypothesis 3.2 was assessed using an Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC). Furthermore, H4 was evaluated by employing the Mann-Whitney U, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, and Levene's tests to assess differences between the data types. H5 was examined using the same multigroup CFA procedure as H2, but exclusively on simulated data, with gender serving as the grouping variable. All CFA and M-CFA models were computed utilizing the MLR estimator due to its robustness against deviations from normality (Hox et al., 2010).

Evaluation Metric. We employ two primary evaluation metrics to assess performance: the Toxic Score (Toxic) and the Attack Success Rate (ASR). Following prior studies [10, 31, 39], we utilize the GPT-4o judge to evaluate the harmfulness of responses. When presented with an original harmful query and a model-generated response, the evaluator assigns a Toxic Score ranging from 1 to 5. Higher values indicate greater harmfulness, with a score of 5 signifying that the response fully aligns with the harmful intent of the query. An attack is considered successful only when it achieves a score of 5. The evaluation prompt is detailed in Appendix B.3. In our study, we define four distinct semantic components, each optimized for different scenarios and models. Therefore, an attack is deemed successful if either the original target image or any of the four refined target images achieves a successful outcome. To compute the Toxic Score, we select the highest score from five evaluations conducted for each sample under a specific scenario. Results are reported exclusively for the Demonstrative Framework, while additional findings for alternative strategies are provided in Appendices C and D. We perform extensive experiments on the MMSafetyBench-tiny and SafeBench-tiny datasets, with the comprehensive results summarized in Tables 1 and 2. The Demonstrative Framework employs multi-round, multi-image conversational contexts and robust red-teaming models to construct attack prompts. However, this approach incurs significantly higher computational and financial costs compared to CIA. In contrast, CIA generates only a single contextual image per query and employs a more streamlined attack pipeline, offering superior scalability and reproducibility. Moreover, CIA demonstrates strong performance in more challenging domains, such as illegal advice (01-IA), fraud (06-FR), and hate crime (12-HC). This consistent performance suggests that CIA does not rely on category-specific templates but instead exploits a general vulnerability in the vision-language alignment and instruction-following mechanisms of modern MLLMs.

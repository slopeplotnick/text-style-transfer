Generator Refinement and Verification: Several studies have concentrated on improving the generation process itself. Methods such as Mirror-Consistency (Li et al., 2024), Self-Contrast (Zhang et al., 2024), and Step-Back Prompting (Zheng et al., 2023a) leverage iterative reflection and alternative perspectives to enhance the quality of generated outputs, while the Self-Check method (Miao et al., 2023) adopts a step-wise verification framework to systematically identify and address errors. Although these approaches aim to refine the generator, they often do so at the cost of increased computational overhead. In contrast, our work shifts focus to the aggregation phase, where we accept the noisy outputs of the generator and employ a distribution-calibrated layer to robustly identify the most accurate response. A key design consideration when using a language model as a judge for pairwise comparisons (Zheng et al., 2023b) is whether to allow it to declare a tie or compel it to make a definitive choice. We first demonstrate that forcing the model to choose can introduce biases into its judgments. Furthermore, we show that the likelihood of declaring a tie is highly sensitive to the parameters of the evaluative framework, highlighting the need for more robust aggregation methods. Allowing tie decisions plays a crucial role in mitigating biases: language models acting as evaluators often exhibit systematic biases, such as positional bias (Shi et al., 2025), where the order of presented options influences the model's preference. For example, as shown in the right side of Table 1, updating the evaluation framework to include a tie option significantly reduced positional bias across both models. This finding underscores that the inclusion of a tie option is not merely a mechanism for capturing equivalence but a critical tool for debiasing the evaluative process itself. However, tie decisions are not always stable. In tasks involving three-way comparisons, the vote distribution of the model is highly sensitive to changes in the evaluation setup, underscoring the necessity of developing more robust methodologies for integrating results.

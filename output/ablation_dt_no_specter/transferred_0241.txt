Humans possess a remarkable ability: given clear instructions, whether in words or diagrams, they can adapt immediately to novel tasks without requiring additional practice. To better understand this capacity, researchers have conducted a comprehensive comparison of the brain and artificial intelligence across sensory, cognitive, and motor domains. This analysis deconstructs circuit computations into three core components: circuit architecture, input-output transformation, and learning algorithms, all examined through the lens of world-model-based information processing. Consequently, it has been observed that modern AI systems are increasingly converging toward brain-like processing, offering valuable insights into the computational principles underlying brain function. The neocortex and cerebellum, for instance, predict future states of the external world based on prior inputs and strive to minimize prediction errors. In doing so, they compress and abstract information into compact, powerful world models. These models enable three fundamental types of information processing: (1) Prediction—generating future information; (2) Understanding—interpreting the external world through abstract constructs within the world models, such as features, laws, and causal relationships (e.g., object recognition and text comprehension); and (3) Generation—repurposing the mechanisms of future-information generation to produce outputs like motor actions, strategic planning, and language production. This world-model-based mechanism explains how the neocortex and cerebellum achieve such a diverse range of functions despite their relatively uniform circuit structures and computations. In conclusion, world-model-based information processing serves as a unifying computational foundation that underpins a wide array of functions and forms of intelligence in both the human brain and artificial intelligence systems. A deeper understanding of this paradigm will be essential not only for advancing neuroscience and AI but also for uncovering the fundamental principles of intelligence. Notably, the human brain demonstrates a far superior ability to learn rapidly and generalize from minimal examples compared to large language models, all while operating with significantly greater efficiency. While recent advancements in AI, particularly through the adoption of brain-inspired mechanisms like Mixture-of-Experts architectures, have improved learning and computational efficiency, they still fall short of the brain's unparalleled capabilities. As AI continues to evolve by drawing inspiration from neural processes, neuroscience, in turn, stands to gain transformative insights from the study of these artificial systems.

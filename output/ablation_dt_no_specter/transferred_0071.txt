Although cov_tgsp and CSP consistently demonstrate strong performance (see Table 2 and Figure 3), notable differences emerge across datasets. A dataset-wise analysis reveals that, while CSP and cov_tgsp rank among the top-performing methods in all cases, their relative effectiveness is noticeably reduced on the PhysionetMI dataset compared to Cho2017 and Zhou2016. This discrepancy may stem from variations in experimental protocols, including differences in sampling rates, the number of electrodes used, and the number of participants across studies. These findings underscore that the performance of feature extraction methods for motor imagery EEG decoding is highly dependent on the dataset in question. For example, on the Zhou2016 dataset, the combination of CSP with StandardScaler and logistic regression achieved the highest classification accuracy (0.87), closely followed by cov_tgsp with RobustScaler and logistic regression (0.84). Conversely, on the Cho2017 dataset, cov_tgsp outperformed CSP, attaining a maximum score of 0.74 compared to 0.68. In contrast, methods based on instantaneous functional connectivity and non-linear features consistently demonstrated lower performance across all datasets. For instance, svd_entropy combined with RobustScaler and logistic regression achieved scores of 0.68 on Zhou2016, 0.59 on Cho2017, and 0.54 on PhysionetMI. On the PhysionetMI dataset specifically, overall performance was weaker, with the best pipeline (cov_tgsp) reaching only 0.64, while CSP averaged 0.62 across frequency bands. These results suggest that, while non-linear and connectivity-based features are capable of capturing the complex dynamics of EEG signals, they do not necessarily exhibit superior discriminative power for motor imagery classification in this context. Subject-level analyses further support the general superiority of CSP and cov_tgsp across datasets, as shown in Figures 5 and 6 for Cho2017 and Zhou2016. However, this trend is less pronounced on the PhysionetMI dataset (Figure 7), where these methods do not consistently outperform alternatives. In some cases, alternative approaches achieve better results: for example, Hjorth parameters surpass CSP and cov_tgsp for participants #57 and #59, while the instantaneous connectivity approach combined with TGSP performs best for participants #17 and #73.

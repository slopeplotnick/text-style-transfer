Text vs. numeric signals. Numeric-only analyses are feasible but demonstrably inferior to text-only analyses. However, integrating both numerical and textual data approaches nearly the optimal performance achieved by text-based analyses alone. This supports a practical hierarchy: textual information holds primary significance, with numerical data serving as supplementary information. Signals hierarchy. In examining various datasets and tasks, a distinct hierarchy of significance emerges. For the detection of fake news, text-based approaches prove to be highly efficacious. Moreover, the integration of a straightforward nonlinear layer atop text models nearly achieves optimal outcomes on certain datasets and surpasses traditional methodologies on others. Numeric inputs alone are adequate but demonstrably less effective; for instance, in forecasting the potential virality of content, numeric data is inferior to textual data. Overall, textual information is paramount, with numeric data serving a supplementary role. Therefore, it is crucial to articulate this distinction explicitly. In scenarios where error minimization is critical, adherence to established methodologies that effectively balance comprehensiveness and precision is advisable. Architecture vs. input view. When configuring models for a particular dataset, architectures such as GRU, LSTM, RNN, CNN, and Transformer demonstrate comparable efficacy, with the Transformer exhibiting a marginally superior performance. The convergence in their performance metrics underscores the significance of data utilization strategies and nomenclature over minor model adjustments, particularly under constrained conditions.

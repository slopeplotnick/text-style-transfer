Score Distillation Sampling (SDS). Pretrained diffusion models are not only used for generating samples through noise reduction but also serve as optimization objectives via score distillation sampling (SDS). This approach has enabled the adaptation of pretrained image and video diffusion models for a wide range of generative modeling tasks. Inspired by the success of SDS in image synthesis, recent studies have explored incorporating diffusion-based priors into reinforcement learning frameworks for control. For instance, several methods replace the conventional discriminator in a GAIL-style framework with a diffusion model, leveraging its ability to distinguish between real and synthetic actions. However, these methods still rely on adversarial training and often require iterative updates to the diffusion model during policy optimization. Moving beyond adversarial paradigms, alternative approaches have sought to directly integrate SDS-inspired techniques into policy learning. For example, some methods train policies using pretrained image or video diffusion models conditioned on textual prompts. While these approaches can generate behaviors that align with textual instructions, the resulting motions sometimes appear unnatural. The work most closely related to ours is the SMILING framework, which trains controllers using a methodology similar to variational score distillation (VSD). However, unlike SMILING, which requires task-specific diffusion models, our approach trains a task-agnostic motion model from unstructured motion data. This motion model can then be utilized to develop diverse control policies by combining it with distinct task objectives in a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning framework. Additionally, we introduce several key design choices that enable the generation of high-quality, naturalistic motions across a wide variety of tasks.

Participants articulated their perceptions regarding AI-generated music, despite their unfamiliarity with the composer. Their concerns mirrored those prevalent in the discourse surrounding music and technology, encompassing potential economic repercussions, questions of ownership, and cultural implications. However, there was also a palpable interest in the innovative technology and its operational mechanisms. The variability in perceptions of AI composers may elucidate the inconsistent opinions observed within the literature. As advancements in AI technology progress, phenomena once regarded with intrigue may increasingly be perceived as problematic or even threatening. Therefore, perceptions of AI-generated music should be considered dynamic and subject to temporal evolution. The study is subject to several limitations. First, the sample comprised university students and participants recruited via the online platform Prolific, who engaged with the musical stimuli under uncontrolled listening conditions (e.g., varying use of headphones or speakers, self-determined volume levels), thereby limiting the generalizability of the findings. Moreover, as noted in prior research on AI-generated music (Shank et al., 2023), participants recruited through Prolific are more likely to exhibit higher levels of technological literacy and familiarity with AI systems. Additionally, the framing of the stimuli as "movie soundtracks" for one experimental group may have introduced unintended bias, as individuals often associate film music with positive affective responses. Furthermore, the present study exclusively utilized instrumental music, and as such, the findings may not be generalizable to vocal music. Prior research has demonstrated that the presence of lyrics tends to amplify the emotional intensity of sad music, whereas their influence on happy music is minimal or even attenuating (Ali and Peynicioglu, 2006; Barradas and Sakka, 2021; Brattico et al., 2011; Warmbrodt et al., 2022). Given that the majority of the musical stimuli in the current study were positively valenced, the inclusion of lyrics—assuming they were congruent and of comparable quality to human-composed music—would likely have exerted only a negligible or diminishing effect on the perceived emotional intensity. In selecting stimuli, we opted to exclude lyrical content to mitigate potential confounding effects associated with lyrical semantics and prosody. Moreover, at the time of stimulus creation, the availability of high-quality AI-generated vocal performances was limited, thereby influencing our decision to focus exclusively on instrumental excerpts.

Large language models (LLMs) continue to grow in size and complexity, thereby straining memory and computational resources during both training and deployment. Quantization addresses these challenges by encoding data in fewer bits and replacing costly floating-point operations with more efficient integer arithmetic. In the literature, two principal methodologies for quantization are prevalent. Post-training quantization (PTQ) is appealing due to its simplicity and lack of retraining requirements. However, at extremely low bit widths (e.g., 1-2 bits), it suffers from significant accuracy degradation. This limitation arises from several factors, including insufficient calibration data, inadequate per-tensor or per-group scaling methods to effectively realign codebooks, and the disproportionate influence of outlier channels on clipping ranges. In contrast, quantization-aware training (QAT) integrates the quantization process directly into the training phase, allowing the model to adapt to the constraints of quantization and mitigate associated accuracy loss. While QAT demonstrates superior performance compared to PTQ under extremely low bit-widths, it is often characterized by extended training durations and instability due to its constrained flexibility in accommodating layer statistics. In our study, we applied FAIRY2 to widely-used open-source large language models, such as the LLaMA series, with promising results. Our methodology involved transforming any layer into an alternative representation without altering the pre-quantization functionality of the model. We continued training at minimal bit levels using a specialized quantizer while leveraging existing checkpoints. Furthermore, we reduced errors by applying the same approach to residual data, significantly minimizing inaccuracies.

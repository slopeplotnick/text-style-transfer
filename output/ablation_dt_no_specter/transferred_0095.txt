This study presents a methodology for creating an energy model that provides energy consumption distributions for statically typed programming languages operating on the JVM. It investigates the effect of primitive data types, data sizes, operations, and device instances on energy consumption. Moreover, it provides evidence that different instances of the same hardware model consume varying amounts of energy, affecting the generalizability of energy predictions. The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 provides background information on Bayesian statistics. Section 3 introduces our methodology. Section 4 presents the implementation of the proposed method. Section 5 explains the evaluation and addresses any anomalies encountered in the results. The evaluation results are presented in Section 6, with the discussion in Section 7. Section 8 reviews related literature, and Section 9 offers a comprehensive conclusion. Bayesian statistics is a methodological framework that leverages prior knowledge to formulate probabilistic inferences about unobservable phenomena. While it shares conceptual similarities with frequentist statistics, which remains the predominant approach in computer science, the two frameworks differ fundamentally in their construction and interpretation of statistical models. Frequentist statistics relies exclusively on observed data, striving to minimize assumptions. In contrast, Bayesian statistics incorporates prior assumptions explicitly, making them transparent and amenable to validation. We employ Bayesian statistics for two primary purposes: firstly, to elucidate and simplify the articulation of our assumptions, and secondly, to enhance the granularity of energy consumption predictions by providing a spectrum of potential outcomes, as opposed to the singular estimates typically produced by frequentist methodologies. We posit that energy consumption adheres to a normal distribution, constrained within a range derived from antecedent research. This range serves as an initial framework, which is subsequently refined through the application of a Bayesian inference algorithm to more accurately align with our empirical measurements. In scenarios where data availability is limited, initial assumptions exert a significant influence on the outcomes, potentially leading to suboptimal results. However, as the volume of data increases, the algorithm demonstrates the capacity to iteratively refine these preliminary assumptions, thereby improving its alignment with the observed data and enhancing the overall model performance.

Traditional MRTA approaches have demonstrated efficacy across various domains through the application of optimization algorithms, including linear programming, genetic algorithms, and Reinforcement Learning (RL), which offer mathematical rigor and established performance guarantees. However, construction environments pose distinct challenges that render these conventional optimization methods inadequate due to the inherently unstructured nature of construction sites, which complicates coordination efforts. Moreover, the complexity and volume of information inherent in construction tasks exceed the capabilities of these mathematically-based approaches to effectively manage. Consequently, there is a demand for more adaptable solutions. Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate their capacity to comprehend language, engage in logical reasoning, and make decisions that are responsive to dynamic conditions. These capabilities facilitate the mitigation of several inherent limitations associated with conventional optimization and learning methodologies. Furthermore, LLM-based coordination facilitates seamless interaction between humans and robots, adapting to the intricate and dynamic requirements of construction sites. Recent configurations of LLMs have demonstrated potential in task planning, digital twin integration, and the employment of multi-agent architectures. However, these configurations lack systematic methodologies for evaluating and benchmarking their performance against traditional approaches. A significant research gap exists in the domain of LLM-based coordination, particularly in the development of mechanisms for error correction and reliability assurance in reasoning processes. Moreover, there is a lack of systematic comparative analyses evaluating the performance of LLMs against traditional methodologies in construction-related tasks. This study endeavors to address this gap by introducing the LangGraph based Task Allocation Agent (LTAA) framework, a novel LLM-driven coordination approach that incorporates innovative phase-adaptive strategies and multi-stage validation mechanisms absent in existing frameworks. The phase-adaptive strategy effectively achieves workload balance without compromising performance, while the validation system ensures reasoning consistency by systematically identifying and rectifying allocation deficiencies through structured feedback loops.

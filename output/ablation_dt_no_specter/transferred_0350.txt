Despite significant advancements in computational sound and music generation, the evaluation of such systems still lacks a standardized methodology. In addition to auditory evaluations, researchers have proposed a range of objective metrics to assess the fidelity of audio reconstructions and the ability of neural networks to classify generated music into predefined categories. In this study, we utilize both auditory evaluations and objective metrics from prior research to assess the performance of our symbolic music generation approach. Specifically, we generated 100 MIDI samples for each quadrant of the test set (GoEmotions) and reported the corresponding metrics. To gain an initial understanding of our model's performance, we conducted a preliminary listening study in which participants evaluated how well the generated music aligned with the intended emotional content of the text. This evaluation is inherently more subjective than traditional music generation tasks, as it requires two layers of interpretation: first, participants must accurately perceive the emotional tone of the text, and second, they must determine whether the accompanying music appropriately reflects this emotional context. In the first phase of the study, participants were presented with a textual excerpt and asked to classify it into one of four emotion quadrants within the valence-arousal framework. In the second phase, they listened to two musical selections associated with the narrative and selected the one they felt best captured the emotional essence of the text. This two-step approach allowed us to evaluate both the consistency of emotion perception at the text level and the perceived congruence between the emotional content of the text and the accompanying music. However, since not all selected musical excerpts corresponded exactly to the emotional category identified by participants, we assessed emotional alignment using the valence-arousal dimensions rather than strict categorical matches. Each emotional category was broken down into binary valence (positive/negative) and arousal (high/low) components. For each participant's selection, we evaluated whether the chosen musical excerpt aligned with their perceived emotional interpretation of the text along these dimensions. This approach provided measures of valence accuracy, arousal accuracy, and combined valence-arousal accuracy, offering a more nuanced and perceptually grounded framework for evaluating emotional alignment.

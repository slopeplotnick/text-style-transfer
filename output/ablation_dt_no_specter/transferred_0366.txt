We evaluate ADORE using both offline and online metrics. For offline evaluation, given that the human annotations are binary, we frame the task as a classification problem and assess the model's performance using precision, recall, and F1 score. For online evaluation, we conduct experiments in the domain of search advertising, employing key business metrics such as click-through rate (CTR), total ad revenue, and expert-provided ad quality ratings. ADORE is compared against several strong baseline models, including the widely used BERT, LLAMA-3-8B, and their respective variants. For BERT-based models, we evaluate both the bi-encoder and cross-encoder architectures. Within the cross-encoder BERT models, we further compare its mini version (which shares the same architecture and parameter size as our online model), the standard version, and an enhanced version that incorporates our proposed CoT attributes. For the LLM model, we evaluate its performance by directly utilizing the representation of the last token for classification. Finally, we introduce ADORE, which builds upon BERT-Mini by integrating online data from the RD module, adversarially generated synthetic data from the DS module, and explicit attribute knowledge from the KD module. The experimental results, summarized in Table 2, demonstrate that ADORE significantly outperforms all baseline models on a large-scale real-world dataset. Specifically, we observe the following: (1) The cross-encoder-based BERT consistently outperforms the bi-encoder-based BERT, with larger model sizes further enhancing performance. Moreover, the inclusion of CoT attributes leads to a notable improvement in BERT's effectiveness, highlighting the value of explicit knowledge. (2) Directly using LLMs for classification tasks yields weaker results compared to BERT models. However, leveraging domain-rule-aware LLMs within the RD module to generate challenging samples significantly boosts performance. Additionally, incorporating adversarially crafted data from the DS module further improves the model's F1 score. Finally, the explicit knowledge augmentation in the KD module enhances the model's reasoning and generalization capabilities, leading to further improvements in overall performance.

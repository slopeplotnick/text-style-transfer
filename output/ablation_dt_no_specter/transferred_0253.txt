System prompts provide task-agnostic control over the behavior of LLMs, defining roles, styles, or task-specific policies that can be applied across various contexts. Prior research has demonstrated that elements such as personas (Kim et al., 2024), emotions (Li et al., 2023), and jailbreak triggers (Shen et al., 2023) can shape both the stylistic and reasoning aspects of model outputs, though these modifications do not always enhance task accuracy. Despite their growing adoption, the structured optimization of system prompts remains a relatively underexplored domain. Recent disclosures of system prompts from Grok (xAI, 2025) and Claude (Breunig, 2025) reveal the use of verbose and intricately crafted manual rules. Meanwhile, contemporary approaches (Zhang et al., 2024; Secheresse et al., 2025) have shown that system prompts can be automatically optimized using genetic algorithms; however, such efforts have so far been constrained to monolingual and single-metric scenarios. The extension of these methods to multilingual settings introduces significant challenges, including language-specific performance degradation and cross-lingual inconsistencies. For instance, multilingual LLMs often generate inconsistent responses to the same query posed in different languages, a limitation stemming from the uneven distribution of knowledge across languages (Tam et al., 2025). Techniques such as Cross-Lingual Thought Prompting (Huang et al., 2023) and multilingual self-consistency (Yu et al., 2025) provide partial remedies but typically depend on multi-turn prompting strategies. Nevertheless, a comprehensive framework for optimizing system prompts to ensure robust and consistent performance across languages remains elusive. Beyond conventional evaluation metrics, recent studies have increasingly focused on understanding how LLMs reason by breaking down their outputs into interpretable reasoning components (Tam et al., 2025; Gandhi et al., 2025). However, these investigations have largely concentrated on surface-level phenomena, such as using text prefill techniques to influence output language (Tam et al., 2025), without delving deeply into the underlying cognitive mechanisms of the models. This study aims to bridge this gap by establishing a connection between system prompt design and multilingual reasoning processes, further demonstrating how these reasoning patterns align with cross-lingual generalization capabilities.

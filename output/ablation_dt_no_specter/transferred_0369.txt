Statistical tests reveal that the sequence in which input articles are presented significantly impacts the generated summaries. Specifically, LLMs exhibit a consistent preference for the first article in the sequence, which shapes both the framing of information and patterns of semantic similarity. This observation aligns with prior research documenting position bias in LLMs when processing extended contextual inputs [Liu et al., 2023]. Our findings extend this phenomenon to multi-document settings, demonstrating a clear primacy effect in information ranking scenarios. Notably, BERTScore consistently highlights this effect across all analytical perspectives. This bias arises because the model preferentially incorporates the vocabulary and argumentative structure of the first article, resulting in token-level contextualized embeddings that yield higher similarity scores for Position 1. In contrast, ROUGE-L, which focuses on surface-level lexical overlap, remains less sensitive to this framing shift, while SummaC emphasizes factual consistency rather than semantic alignment and similarly fails to capture the primacy effect as strongly. Across all three analytical perspectives, Position 1 consistently differs significantly from Positions 2 and 3, whereas the comparison between Positions 2 and 3 remains non-significant. Therefore, this pattern suggests that the primacy effect stems from the model’s prioritization of the first article, rather than a gradual decline in influence for later positions. The implications of this bias are substantial for real-world applications, particularly in user-facing AI systems. For instance, the AI Overviews feature in Google Search, powered by Gemini models, may implicitly prioritize earlier-ranked articles, thereby marginalizing those presented later in the sequence. Consequently, this order effect can lead to skewed framing in generated summaries and potentially shape users’ understanding of the topic—an impact that may be amplified among individuals who place high trust in AI-generated content.

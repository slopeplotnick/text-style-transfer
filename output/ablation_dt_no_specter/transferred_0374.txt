Taken together, these findings highlight OMNIGUARD's substantial proficiency in cross-modal safety reasoning. OMNIGUARD not only generalizes safety reasoning from trained modalities to untrained ones but also acquires robust modality-invariant semantic representations of unsafe content. To this end, we introduce OMNIGUARD-7B and OMNIGUARD-3B, the first family of omni-modal guardrails trained on an extensive and unified safety fine-tuning dataset encompassing both unimodal and cross-modal samples. OMNIGUARD serves as a comprehensive omni-modal safety solution, capable of moderating and reasoning about unsafe content across diverse and interleaved data modalities. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that OMNIGUARD-7B consistently outperforms existing models across all modalities, while OMNIGUARD-3B achieves competitive results compared to large-scale models such as Qwen3-235B and Qwen3-VL-235B. These results underscore the robust omni-modal safety detection and reasoning capabilities of our system, confirming the feasibility of a unified guardrail solution with omni-modal comprehension and interpretability. Future work will focus on exploring more intricate and cross-modal safety scenarios to further advance omni-modal safeguarding in next-generation language models. Despite OMNIGUARD's strong omni-modal safety reasoning and consistent performance across modalities, several limitations persist. While the incorporation of reasoning paths significantly enhances the interpretability and reliability of safety assessments, it inevitably increases inference latency due to the additional reasoning generation step. As a result, OMNIGUARD incurs higher computational overhead compared to lightweight, binary-classification guardrail systems. Therefore, it is essential to investigate the trade-off between the depth of safety reasoning and inference latency across various use cases to optimize safety robustness and efficiency. Moreover, the limited availability of publicly accessible cross-modal safety fine-tuning datasets presents significant opportunities for progress in addressing more complex interleaved multimodal safety scenarios. We anticipate that future research will continue to improve the robustness and reliability of omni-modal systems, thereby enhancing their ability to mitigate risks across diverse modality combinations.

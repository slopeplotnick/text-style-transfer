Advancements in computational tools and systems have significantly enhanced the autonomous capabilities of computer systems, illustrating how self-supervised mechanisms enable computers to independently interpret and execute tasks. Moreover, the availability of open-source orchestration frameworks simplifies the integration of components such as planners, memory modules, and other functional elements, streamlining system composition and deployment. Empirical evidence from industry reports elucidates the practical application of these systems in real-world scenarios. However, certain studies indicate that merely increasing model size does not inherently enhance reliability, emphasizing the need to evaluate the operational efficacy of these systems to ensure genuine improvements in performance. Assessing the performance of these systems presents significant challenges due to the increasing complexity of their tasks. While some studies have explored the integration of specialized frameworks to facilitate evaluation, concerns regarding potential biases remain prevalent. To address these limitations, proposed strategies include employing multiple evaluators and ensuring that assessment methodologies maintain fairness and consistency across diverse task domains. Consequently, the project employs a dual-judge system to evaluate distinct components of the task, ensuring that enhancements are substantive. It encompasses a compilation of 100 authentic editing tasks involving slide decks. Each task comprises an initial slide deck, a target deck, detailed instructions, and a rubric specifying criteria for assessing layout and content. These tasks are systematically categorized into 16 distinct groups to ensure comprehensive evaluation of both comprehension and intricate formatting. Data is sourced from over 15,000 PowerPoint presentations across various platforms, subsequently converted into a structured format that encapsulates layout and content metadata. Presentations exhibiting diverse stylistic elements are retained and integrated with contributions from domain-specific experts. From a pool of more than 500 meticulously reviewed candidates, including 25 decks developed de novo by our students, we selected 100 cases that comprehensively encompass professional, academic, multilingual, and art/design domains.

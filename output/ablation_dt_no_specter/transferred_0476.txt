Other promising prompt optimizers not yet implemented in promptsolution include GEPA (Agrawal et al., 2025), TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al., 2025), MIPRO (Opsahl-Ong et al., 2024), and PromptWizard (Agarwal et al., 2024). Most of these optimizers are currently confined to isolated research repositories with rigid experimental configurations. They are often not actively maintained, lack comprehensive documentation, and are devoid of robust software testing. Consequently, their practical applicability is limited, posing significant challenges for both comparative evaluations with other optimizers and deployment in real-world scenarios. In the prompt optimization landscape, many actively maintained libraries and tools have emerged, including both open- and closed-source solutions. Several open-source libraries only implement a single prompt optimizer, such as TextGrad, AdalFlow, Microsoft's PromptWizard, and PromptIM, each employing distinct methodologies. The prompt-ops library supports two optimizers but is primarily tailored for prompt optimization in Llama models. In contrast, promptsolution is a framework with multiple optimizers that can be used interchangeably for arbitrary LLMs. DSPy is recognized as one of the most prominent frameworks for constructing modular, declarative LLM pipelines and incorporates a component dedicated to prompt optimization. It integrates optimizers such as MIPROv2 and GEPA and possesses the capability to amalgamate LLM training with prompt optimization. While DSPy serves primarily as a comprehensive framework for constructing AI programs, promptolution is dedicated exclusively to prompt optimization, rendering it more suitable for researchers and experts seeking to conduct in-depth analyses. Additionally, promptomatix, which is derived from DSPy, presents a more streamlined approach and introduces its unique methodology for prompt optimization.

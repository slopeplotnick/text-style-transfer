TCAS is recognized as a prominent program benchmark extensively utilized in fault localization studies. This benchmark comprises a C program developed by Siemens, along with 41 versions where faults have been deliberately introduced, with explicitly identified positions and types. Conversely, C-PACK-IPAs represents a collection of student programs sourced from an introductory programming course. For this investigation, the complete C-PACK-IPAs benchmark was utilized, encompassing twenty-five programming assignments and a total of 1431 defective programs sourced from three laboratory classes. These programs encompass a variety of topics, including integers and input-output operations, loops and character manipulation, and vector processing. Moreover, the C-PACK-IPAs benchmark has demonstrated efficacy in evaluating diverse program analysis tasks. CFAULTS employs the pycparser tool for the unrolling and instrumentation of C programs, while CBMC version 5.11 is utilized to encode these programs into CNF formulae. In the context of fault localization, due to the unavailability or obsolescence of the original implementations of BugAssIST and SNIPER, new iterations of these algorithms have been developed. It is worth noting that the previous version of SNIPER was limited in its applicability, as it could only process certain C programs and encountered difficulties with loops, recursion, and specific variable types. Furthermore, a revised version of HSD has been developed to facilitate the localization of faulty statements in ANSI-C programs, both with and without the core minimization feature (HSD-CM and HSD, respectively), as the original implementation was restricted to Boolean circuits. Consequently, BugAssIST, SNIPER, and HSD are now capable of analyzing ANSI-C programs by leveraging components of CFAULTS, such as its unroller and instrumentalizer modules. All evaluated algorithms consistently produce diagnoses that adhere to predefined criteria, with validation performed through CBMC to ensure the correctness of the generated results. This verification step is particularly pertinent to BugAssIST, as its results may occasionally fail to align with all failing test cases. In contrast, the MaxSAT-based solution employed by CFAULTS and the hitting set approach utilized by HSD consistently adhere to all observed test cases. Similarly, SNIPER's aggregation method ensures the generation of valid diagnoses; however, these diagnoses may not always exhibit subset-minimality. When considering BugAssIST, we systematically evaluate its generated results according to a predefined scoring system, continuing this process until a result is identified that corresponds with all observed phenomena.

Training robust reward models is challenging because they are subjective and context-dependent, complicating the creation of reliable frameworks (Chakraborty et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023). Moreover, learned proxies are prone to reward hacking, which undermines their effectiveness (Casper et al., 2023). The scarcity of annotations further exacerbates these issues, limiting the model's ability to capture fine-grained behaviors (Wu et al., 2023). Consequently, these challenges have spurred efforts to reduce dependence on extensive annotations. For instance, Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) bypasses explicit reward modeling but still relies on curated preference datasets. Similarly, verifiable rewards (DeepSeek-AI, 2025; Lambert et al., 2024), grounded in real-world outcomes, are only applicable when automatic verification mechanisms are available. On the other hand, self-evaluation methods (Yuan et al., 2024; Lee et al., 2024; Garg et al., 2025), while promising, are hindered by prompt sensitivity and systematic biases (Zheng et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024). Notably, these approaches predominantly assess quality through external signals, often neglecting the rich information embedded within the model's internal representations. Yet, the validity of a generated response should be discernible from its underlying computational processes. For example, when a model generates "The capital of France is Paris," its hidden states encode activated factual knowledge (He et al., 2024c), whereas hallucinations manifest as distinct activation patterns (Chen et al., 2024). This observation raises a critical question: Can text quality be assessed directly from internal signals, thereby enabling LLM alignment without external supervision? In this work, we demonstrate that a simple geometric property of LLM hidden states, known as effective dimensionality, serves as a reliable indicator of response quality. Specifically, we propose using stable rank (Rudelson & Vershynin, 2007), a matrix-theoretic measure, as an unsupervised proxy for evaluating generation quality. Stable rank quantifies the number of independent semantic directions occupied by a response by measuring the ratio of total variance to dominant-direction variance in the hidden-state representation. It strikes a balance between representational richness and coherence: a high stable rank indicates that information is distributed across many dimensions rather than being concentrated in a few. The theoretical foundation for this idea is supported by two lines of research. First, the softmax bottleneck analysis reveals that accurate language modeling requires navigating a high-dimensional semantic manifold, as the contextual probability distribution of natural language is inherently complex.

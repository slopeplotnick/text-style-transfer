Significant advancements have been made in bias detection, toxicity reduction, and fairness auditing. However, these methodologies predominantly focus on evaluating isolated model outputs rather than tracing the evolution of ethical reasoning or shifts in value alignment over time as models interact with diverse, context-dependent scenarios. This issue is particularly pertinent for autonomous AI systems, which demonstrate adaptive and context-sensitive behavior, continually adjusting their responses based on prompt structure, user intent, task requirements, and previous interactions. The absence of temporal consistency in ethical perspectives poses a risk of moral degradation, wherein ethical stances may fluctuate or deteriorate due to variations in prompt wording, situational pressures, or temporal dynamics. Such inconsistencies can undermine the perceived reliability, trustworthiness, and accountability of AI systems. Therefore, ensuring ethical consistency is not merely a matter of regulatory adherence but is essential for maintaining logically stable, interpretable, and trustworthy reasoning within AI systems over time. In the development of autonomous systems within critical domains such as healthcare, finance, education, and governance, even minor ethical instabilities can propagate misinformation, reinforce cognitive biases, and produce outcomes misaligned with human values. Despite increasing awareness of these challenges, current auditing methodologies remain constrained by static benchmarks and fixed datasets that fail to adapt to the dynamic and context-dependent behavior of large language models. Consequently, a significant research challenge lies in designing an adaptive, self-contained mechanism capable of autonomously evaluating and interpreting the ethical stability of these models without reliance on external datasets or post-hoc analyses. To address this issue, this study introduces the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP), an autonomous ethical evaluation framework that operates independently of datasets, designed to continuously monitor, interpret, and refine the moral reasoning processes of AI models. MoCoP functions as a self-sufficient system, generating ethical scenarios, eliciting model responses, and conducting structured interpretation using internally established rules and semantic heuristics. In contrast to conventional evaluation methodologies that rely on predefined test corpora and external annotation, MoCoP autonomously constructs and analyzes its own ethical scenarios. It employs three core analytical components—lexical integrity analysis, semantic risk estimation, and reasoning-based judgment modeling—to derive comprehensive metrics of moral coherence and ethical stability. Each component fulfills a distinct role: lexical integrity analysis identifies linguistic bias and potential degradation, semantic risk estimation assesses latent risks in meaning, and reasoning-based judgment modeling evaluates the underlying decision-making processes.

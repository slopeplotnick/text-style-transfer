In this work, we introduced Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), a reusable and modular framework for physics-based character animation based on score-matching techniques. Once trained, SMP can be leveraged to train control policies for a wide range of tasks, guiding them toward naturalistic behaviors that align with the reference distribution. Notably, the original motion dataset is not required for utilizing the motion prior. At test time, techniques like classifier-free guidance can be applied to refine the base motion prior, enabling the generation of novel stylistic priors that allow agents to perform tasks in specific ways. We evaluated our method across diverse scenarios, including single-character behaviors and human-object interactions, demonstrating its versatility. SMPs can be constructed using datasets of varying scales, from as little as 3 seconds of motion clips to large-scale datasets containing up to 20 hours of motion data. Our experiments show that reinforcement learning with score distillation sampling (SDS) objectives produces motion quality comparable to adversarial imitation learning, while eliminating the need for iterative updates to the motion prior during policy training. Moreover, SMP exhibits higher sample efficiency than adversarial priors in most cases, likely due to its reliance on a more stable and stationary reward function. However, as with other mode-seeking approaches, policies trained with SMP may occasionally suffer from mode-collapse, restricting them to a limited range of behaviors from the original dataset. While our primary focus has been on humanoid motion control, we believe SMP has broader applicability to other control challenges. We hope this work inspires new directions for developing general motion priors that enable more adaptable controllers for physics-based character animation and robotics, extending beyond traditional motion tracking.

We utilize three multimodal emotion datasets to train our model and validate its effectiveness. The EMER dataset provides high-quality, human-verified reasoning-label pairs and serves as the foundation for initial training. Meanwhile, the DFEW and MAFW datasets are extensive benchmarks that encompass a wide range of facial expressions across video, audio, and textual modalities. A detailed description of these datasets is provided in Appendix 7.1. Consistent with prior studies, we report Unweighted Average Recall (UAR) and Weighted Average Recall (WAR) for recognition accuracy. Moreover, we employ three complementary metrics—Emotional Explanation Alignment (EEA), Explanation Prediction Consistency (EPC), and False Contrast Rate (FCR)—to assess the alignment between the generated explanations, the target emotional state, and the model's predictions. Precise definitions of these metrics, along with the annotation methodology, are outlined in Appendix 7.2. In accordance with the R1-Omni architecture, our setup employs SigLIP as the vision encoder, Whisper-large-v3 as the audio encoder, and BERT as the text encoder. Each modality output is projected into the LLM embedding space via its respective visual and audio projectors, implemented as two linear layers to align with the dimensionality of the LLM representation. For instance, a 1-second audio clip generates approximately 50 audio features, while a single video frame yields 729 visual features. The Qwen2.5-7B serves as the LLM backbone, initialized with model weights from the HumanOmni checkpoint to preserve the pretrained multimodal representations. To evaluate the efficacy of our proposed framework, we conduct comparative analyses of MIGR against existing methodologies for emotion understanding using the MAFW and DFEW datasets. Prior approaches are categorized into two distinct paradigms: those that directly predict emotion labels and those that generate textual explanations as an intermediary step before classification.

The proliferation of large language models necessitates the development of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods to manage their increasing complexity. However, the current landscape is characterized by challenges in replicability, usability, and comparability of newly developed PEFT methods. To address these issues, PEFT-FACTORY has been established as a comprehensive framework designed to facilitate the fine-tuning of large language models. This is achieved through the integration of both pre-existing and customizable PEFT methodologies, thereby enhancing the accessibility and standardization of these processes. Moreover, PEFT-FACTORY is designed to be adaptable and extensible, yet it already encompasses a comprehensive suite of 19 PEFT methods, 27 datasets pertinent to classification and text generation across 12 distinct tasks, along with evaluation tools for both general and PEFT-specific metrics. Consequently, PEFT-FACTORY offers a straightforward and dependable framework that enhances the replicability and benchmarking of PEFT methods. Originating from the widely utilized LLaMAFactory, PEFT-FACTORY is publicly accessible at https://github.com/kinit-sk/PEFT-Factory. Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated exceptional performance across a wide array of Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks, particularly following the advent of the transformer architecture. Nevertheless, due to their substantial scaling capabilities, the number of trainable parameters in these models has been progressively increasing. This escalation in parameters renders the process of fine-tuning computationally prohibitive, necessitates extensive data, and poses significant accessibility challenges for numerous researchers and practitioners. Therefore, PEFT methods address these challenges by optimizing a subset of the model’s parameters, thereby achieving comparable performance to full model training while significantly reducing computational requirements. This optimization is accomplished through approaches such as introducing additional parameters, selectively training specific subsets of existing parameters, or modifying the model architecture to minimize the number of trainable parameters.

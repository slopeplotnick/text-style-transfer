Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have achieved remarkable progress in recent years; however, they still face challenges in accurately recognizing and interpreting fine-grained details within high-resolution (HR) images. To improve the ability of MLLMs in HR image understanding, existing studies generally adopt two main approaches. Training-based methods, such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning (RL), aim to enhance model performance but often compromise generalization across diverse vision-language tasks. Conversely, training-free methods, including hierarchical or tree-based search techniques, attempt to identify salient regions within images. Nevertheless, these approaches often suffer from inefficiencies and may fail to detect all objects, particularly in multi-object scenarios. In subsection 3.2, we analyze the relationship between image crop resolution and the performance of MLLMs in HR image comprehension. The experimental results reveal that the resolution of image segments significantly impacts the ability of MLLMs to analyze HR images, with objects of different sizes being better suited to distinct resolutions. Motivated by this observation, we propose the Multi-resolution Retrieval Detection (MRD) framework. To evaluate its effectiveness, we conduct experiments using two model versions, LLaVA-ov and LLaVA-v1.5, on a standardized benchmark. As illustrated in Figure 3, the findings demonstrate that setting the resolution of image crops to 112 yields optimal performance across both single-object and multi-object tasks. These results suggest that a resolution of 112 may be the most effective choice for achieving superior model performance.

Language models exhibit the ability to refine and propose modifications to ambiguously formulated inquiries, which can significantly influence individual responses and potentially lead to errors or misinterpretations. Moreover, these models assist in categorizing questions appropriately, leveraging their semantic understanding of queries to help researchers mitigate inaccuracies in their analyses. Consequently, this enables the examination of interrelations among distinct categories, facilitating the identification of excessive overlap or redundancy that may necessitate substantial revisions. Furthermore, language models standardize linguistic complexity across textual outputs, ensuring consistency in readability and enhancing overall comprehensibility. For additional insights into the application of language models in assessment development, refer to the recent publication by Beghetto et al. Notably, these models are employed to generate synthetic data, which can significantly reduce the resources typically allocated to testing by enabling initial trials on simulated cohorts. This advancement holds the potential to lower costs for researchers in assessment creation while reducing the prevalence of superfluous testing. In this study, we assessed the efficacy of the latest language models in generating data aligned with the structures utilized in test development. Subsequently, we outline our methodological approach and the rationale underpinning our hypotheses. In conducting these assessments, we primarily adhered to the procedures outlined in Classic Test Theory, supplemented by the recommendations provided by Boateng et al.

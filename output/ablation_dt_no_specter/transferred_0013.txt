Forecasting stock prices represents a fundamental and enduring challenge in the field of finance. The inherent volatility of stock prices, coupled with their rapid responsiveness to emerging information, often renders traditional predictive methodologies insufficient for effectively incorporating unstructured textual data, such as news events and market sentiment. Consequently, these conventional approaches frequently fail to capture the underlying drivers of price fluctuations. However, the recent proliferation of online textual data, alongside significant advancements in the natural language processing capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), has markedly accelerated research into text-based stock price forecasting. Despite this progress, the majority of studies employing these LLMs are predominantly characterized by superficial text analysis and immediate inference. They often fail to systematically integrate knowledge and lack a long-term memory architecture, thereby constraining their ability to adapt to the highly dynamic and intricate nature of financial markets. This highlights the imperative for models capable of retaining and utilizing salient information across temporal spans. The complexities inherent in financial markets arise from the fact that price fluctuations are typically the result of the sequential and interconnected evolution of multiple events, rather than isolated incidents. Furthermore, the task of predicting stock prices based on news diverges fundamentally from conventional question-answering paradigms. The inquiry of "what will the stock price do tomorrow?" cannot be directly answered through news articles. Therefore, models must construct a comprehensive representation of the market by synthesizing diverse information sources, identifying analogous historical scenarios, and engaging in profound analytical reasoning to genuinely comprehend market patterns, rather than merely aligning textual data. Moreover, financial texts are often characterized by a high degree of noise and extraneous information, which can obscure critical signals. Thus, the processes of filtering and accurately aligning relevant content are of paramount importance.

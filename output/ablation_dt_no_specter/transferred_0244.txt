Reference state initialization (RSI), where a character is initialized in a random state derived from a motion dataset, has been demonstrated to significantly enhance exploration within motion imitation methodologies. However, this approach requires access to the motion dataset to select initial states during the training phase. To circumvent this dependency, the SMP framework employs a generative model to synthesize initial states for training purposes. Specifically, in the application of SMP, initial states are derived from a diffusion model that has been pretrained as a motion prior. Consequently, SMP serves dual purposes in our framework, functioning both as a reward mechanism and as an initial state distribution for training new policies. This generative approach to state initialization obviates the necessity of maintaining the original dataset post-training, as the diffusion models facilitate the generation of diverse and effective initial states. In our framework, a trained diffusion model acts as a versatile motion guide for imitation learning. The motion model evaluates whether the agent's movements appear natural within the learning environment, thereby facilitating the training of control policies that accomplish a variety of tasks while maintaining the naturalistic characteristics of the original motion dataset. In this section, we elucidate the critical decisions made in the design of the learning framework. Designing an effective motion representation is pivotal for both training the diffusion model to accurately capture the dataset distribution and its subsequent utilization as a motion prior in policy training. This representation must encapsulate sufficient information to reconstruct motion while being readily derivable from motion clips and simulator observations.

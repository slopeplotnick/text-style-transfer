WorldMM was evaluated on five long video question-answering benchmarks, with durations ranging from hours to weeks. The proposed method consistently outperformed robust baseline approaches, including long video large language models (LLMs) and memory-augmented architectures. Extensive ablation studies further validate the efficacy of our multi-memory, multiscale framework. Specifically, episodic memory facilitates reasoning over events occurring across varying temporal scales, visual memory enhances the model's ability to address object- and action-specific queries, and semantic memory contributes to understanding extended contextual information. When these memory modalities are adaptively integrated, the model achieves optimal overall performance. These findings underscore the potential of our multimodal memory system as a significant advancement in robust long video reasoning. Existing models designed for short video content exhibit robust comprehension capabilities, while recent scholarly efforts have increasingly concentrated on the analysis of extended video sequences. Contemporary proprietary models, such as GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5, have progressed to facilitate minute- or hour-level video comprehension by employing augmented context lengths. However, these models are characterized by substantial computational demands and exhibit suboptimal performance when uniformly sampling each frame, particularly in scenarios necessitating focus on localized events. To address these issues, various methodologies have been investigated. Visual token compression enhances efficiency by reducing the number of tokens; however, it often results in the loss of fine-grained details, thereby limiting the ability to capture subtle or infrequent events. Key frame selection reduces redundancy by retaining only the most informative frames, yet it may fail to identify relevant frames in extended video sequences and potentially overlook significant occurrences. Recent advancements in reasoning-centric training and adaptive inference methodologies have facilitated improved comprehension of extended video content. Nevertheless, these approaches exhibit limitations when applied to ultra-long videos exceeding ten hours in duration. For recordings spanning days or weeks, emerging benchmarks are challenging models to process continuous, long-duration footage. The aforementioned methodologies encounter difficulties in these scenarios due to the extensive scale of frames and the intricate long-term temporal dependencies inherent in such recordings. This underscores the imperative for developing more efficient, context-sensitive, and scalable methodologies to address the challenges associated with processing extremely long video content.

This study investigates whether a computational framework, featuring an adult language model named Delilah and a daughter agent, can acquire distinct linguistic categories. The findings from both test sets support the parameter configurations established in prior experiments. The daughter model, developed within the MODOMA framework, utilizes graph-based representations to encode grammatical knowledge, allowing for the generation and comprehension of sentences. As the language acquisition process progresses, this capability is progressively refined. Delilah, equipped with a parser and generator crafted by Cremers and Hijzelendoorn, is specifically designed to process Dutch grammar, thereby facilitating sentence construction and comprehension. Consequently, the MODOMA framework requires an innovative approach to exploring Delilah's capabilities. In addition to enhancing Delilah's proficiency in sentence generation and comprehension, it is crucial to analyze the output produced by Delilah to aid the daughter in identifying patterns essential for language acquisition. To achieve this, methodologies and analytical techniques from fields such as linguistics, psycholinguistics, and corpus linguistics should be employed to gain insights into machine-generated language, akin to the analysis of human language data. Given the rising prominence of large language models, it has become critical to evaluate the extent to which machine-generated language aligns with patterns observed in human language. This study presents findings from a series of experiments conducted to address this question. Notably, the utterances produced by Delilah during interactions with the daughter agent are discrete instances, contrasting with the cohesive and sequential nature typically observed in linguistic corpora. Nonetheless, similar grammatical patterns emerge across both data sets. This observation suggests that these patterns may pertain more to individual sentences than to entire corpora. The analysis demonstrates that the varying frequency patterns of linguistic categories, characteristic of human language data, are also evident in Delilah's output. This pattern emerges as a consequence of the underlying grammar model employed, given that Delilah is not designed to replicate these statistical patterns in its output.

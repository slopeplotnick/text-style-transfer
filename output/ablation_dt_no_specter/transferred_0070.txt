Social media has become an integral part of contemporary life, enabling quick communication, easy information sharing, and the exchange of opinions across geographical boundaries. However, this freedom of expression has also facilitated the emergence of hate speech and harsh language on social media platforms. Unfortunately, not much attention has been paid to identifying hate speech on Bangla social media, which lacks a good-quality annotated dataset. The vocabulary used in social media often differs greatly from that used in traditional print media, complicating the automatic identification of Bangla hate speech. Traditionally, the identification of hate speech has been defined as a supervised classification problem. Early research relied on lexicon-based and rule-based heuristics, but these techniques frequently failed to identify implicit or context-specific hatred. More recent research employs machine learning classifiers such as logistic regression and SVMs to detect online vulnerabilities or dangerous content, utilizing traditional supervised learning techniques for this purpose.

Models perform differently across topics. As shown in Table 2, their performance tends to be slightly lower in Religion and Sport, with scores typically ranging from 7.8 to 8.2, whereas performance in Game and Lifestyle is noticeably stronger, often exceeding 8.4. This pattern suggests that models adopt a more cautious and reserved approach when addressing culturally or ethically sensitive topics like religion and sports. In contrast, more neutral topics such as gaming and lifestyle are better represented in training data, enabling models to generate smoother and more natural mediation. Moreover, steering and judgment performances are strongly correlated. As illustrated in Fig. 3, there is a clear positive relationship between average steering and judgment scores across all models. Those excelling in judgment also tend to perform well in steering, which implies that both tasks rely on shared underlying reasoning and alignment capabilities. This consistency indicates that improvements in core mediation skills, such as contextual understanding and balanced decision-making, could generalize across multiple evaluation tasks. Therefore, future research could focus on integrating these capabilities more effectively to enhance both interpretability and control in large language models. Furthermore, the results in Table 3 reveal that mediation exhibits varying effectiveness across different linguistic dimensions. For instance, the reduction in toxicity and exclamation usage demonstrates that the mediation process successfully fosters more neutral and composed responses. This adjustment can improve conversational tone, making interactions feel more balanced and less emotionally charged. However, the minimal change in argumentativeness scores suggests that mediation has a weaker influence on discourse-level behaviors, such as managing disagreements. Interestingly, models behave differently when simulating both sides of a conversation compared to responding to real user input. This underscores the importance of evaluating mediation not only as a procedural mechanism but also in terms of its tangible outcomes in realistic communicative contexts.

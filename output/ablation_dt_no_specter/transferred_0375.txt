Training Dynamics Analysis. In Figure 3, we analyze the learning reward and entropy dynamics of the 7B model under the CAPO and GRPO frameworks. Initially, both methods exhibit comparable rates of reward growth; however, CAPO consistently outperforms GRPO once the imitation phase stabilizes. The gray vertical line indicates the transition from imitation to independent reasoning. Beyond this point, CAPO demonstrates a steady rise in entropy, contrasting with the plateau observed in GRPO, while rewards continue to improve. This suggests that CAPO not only achieves stronger reward gains in the later stages but also sustains higher entropy, a characteristic associated with more exploratory and diverse reasoning paths. Furthermore, the gradual increase in entropy highlights CAPO’s ability to avoid abrupt collapses often caused by prematurely incorporating negative samples. By strategically deferring their inclusion, CAPO stabilizes the imitation phase and later leverages negative feedback more effectively, thereby enhancing generalization across tasks. Comparing Static and Dynamic Curriculum Strategies. We contrast the conventional static curriculum approach with our dynamic advantage-based training framework. In the static curriculum, sample difficulty is estimated using pass@16 evaluation, and the dataset is subsequently sorted and trained in a fixed sequence. Conversely, our dynamic approach progressively adjusts the advantage signal during training, eliminating the need for manual data reordering. As demonstrated in Table 3, both ADARFT and the static curriculum GRPO(+SC) yield limited and inconsistent improvements over vanilla GRPO. In contrast, our dynamic method GRPO(+Ours) achieves significant and consistent performance gains across most tasks. This underscores that predefined difficulty metrics are insufficient and that dynamically adjusting the advantage signal enables a more effective training curriculum.

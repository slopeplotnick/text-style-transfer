Our methodology employed label-driven chain-of-thought (CoT) generation in combination with hardcase mining. By integrating label-constrained CoT synthesis with difficult sample excavation, we enhanced the model's reasoning capabilities through structured multi-step inference patterns and the resolution of challenging edge cases. To evaluate performance, we implemented hardcase-focused protocols specifically designed to assess the model's ability to handle intricate semantic scenarios and complex reasoning tasks, ensuring robust performance in addressing these challenges. The innovations in this phase enabled the successful deployment of the CoT-based multi-dimensional knowledge system online, where it was distilled and transferred to smaller models. This achievement marked a significant milestone in scaling deep reasoning capabilities to production environments, improving inference efficiency and delivering substantial performance gains in online A/B testing scenarios. However, as we entered the deep-water zone, two critical challenges emerged. First, product attribution understanding heavily relied on visual information, where user-uploaded images often lacked sufficient textual details. Second, during the Path selection process, judgment results were required to strictly conform to formats encompassing numerous fine-grained rules and detailed business standards aligned with observable attributes. To address these challenges, we adopted a comprehensive technical strategy that integrated multimodal understanding capabilities with rule-aware reasoning mechanisms. This approach enabled the model to process visual information while maintaining strict adherence to business constraints. To achieve this, we employed a hybrid paradigm of CoT Generation, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and Reinforcement Learning (RL), leveraging the Qwen-2.5 LLM for textual reasoning and the Qwen-2.5 VL for multimodal comprehension. This dual-model configuration seamlessly integrated visual and textual reasoning pathways, effectively overcoming the limitations of single-modality data processing. Furthermore, our methodology utilized multimodal and rule-aware CoT generation, combining image-text paired understanding with standard-oriented prompt engineering. Business rules and exemplar cases were embedded into CoT reasoning chains to establish a robust framework for rule comprehension. Subsequently, reinforcement learning was employed to significantly enhance the model's ability to apply these rules to decision-making processes within complex contextual scenarios.

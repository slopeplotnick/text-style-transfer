Micro-level phenomena emerge when interaction topologies are narrow and short-lived, yet early signs of local opacity and divergence begin to surface. These phenomena are localized but inherently multiplicative; once a deviation embeds itself within an agent's representation, subsequent interactions replicate and amplify it. Consequently, the cumulative effect becomes nonlinear, as the proliferation of distinct risk events outpaces the growth in the number of interacting agents or exchanges. Micro-level risks typically arise in direct exchanges between small groups of agents—ranging from two to five—where the initial traces of systemic drift originate. These risks reveal the fragility of shared context and interpretability, even before large-scale coordination within a network takes shape. However, as interaction chains deepen, these localized issues often serve as the seeds from which meso- and macro-level pathologies evolve. Meso-level risks, in turn, materialize when clusters of agents—numbering in the dozens or hundreds—exchange information across semi-connected networks. At this scale, no single failure appears dominant; rather, the structure and execution of interactions lead to a degradation of metrics such as accuracy, diversity, and coherence. While each agent may function adequately in isolation, the collective behavior increasingly diverges from expected norms. These risks underscore that systemic degradation can arise even in the absence of overtly defective components. Instead, failures emerge from interference patterns—how partially correct agents interact—rather than from isolated errors. It is at this level that local predictability begins to erode, giving way to systemic instability. Finally, macro-level pathologies occur when all three dimensions—topology density, opacity, and divergence—reach critical thresholds. At this stage, collective behavior emerges that is independent of the system’s initial design, reflecting a complete departure from intended outcomes. This taxonomy, while preliminary, provides a foundational framework for observing, discussing, and systematically analyzing these behavioral dynamics. Without such a structure, the ability to identify, measure, and regulate systemic behaviors in model collectives remains significantly constrained. Future validation of this framework will require extensive experimental campaigns conducted under controlled conditions, as planned within the Institutional AI paradigm.

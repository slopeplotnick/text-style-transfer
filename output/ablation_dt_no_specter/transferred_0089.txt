Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) with voice interfaces have garnered significant attention from both the research community and broader society. Closed-source models, such as GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-pro, have demonstrated exceptional performance across classical speech tasks, including (i) speech recognition, (ii) translation, and (iii) spoken language understanding, consistently surpassing prior open-source benchmarks. Nevertheless, despite these advancements, there remains a notable gap in research focused on the design principles and frameworks required to seamlessly integrate speech modalities into LLMs for achieving true multi-modal understanding. Furthermore, the interplay between speech models and LLMs, particularly in the context of autonomous intelligent systems and layered, self-play cognitive agents (Shah et al., 2018a,b), represents an emergent area of exploration. This tutorial seeks to provide a comprehensive review of the historical evolution of probabilistic language modeling (Jurafsky and Martin, 2024) for speech processing, offering insights that inform the development of interoperable multi-agent systems within conversational frameworks. Moreover, we will examine advanced topics such as cross-modal adaptation, presenting the theoretical foundations (Yang et al., 2021) necessary to align non-text modalities with textual representations. These discussions will be complemented by open-source, reproducible benchmarks (Chen et al., 2023a), ensuring practical applicability for participants. In addition, we will explore recent trends in end-to-end multi-modal speech-language models, with a particular focus on generative autoregressive designs that employ joint speech-text tokenization. By analyzing both cascaded and end-to-end approaches, this tutorial will equip participants with a holistic understanding of current methodologies and the unresolved challenges in developing speech-augmented LLMs, as illustrated in Figure 1. Spanning three hours, this tutorial will delve into the rapidly evolving field of speech-language modeling in the era of voice-interfacing LLMs and agent systems. Each core theme will be addressed in a 35-minute segment, followed by a 10-minute Q&A and a 10-minute break. For every section, we will provide a structured overview of the relevant topics, followed by an in-depth analysis of key studies that have shaped the field. The tutorial will conclude with a discussion of open challenges and emerging research directions in this transformative domain of joint speech-language modeling.

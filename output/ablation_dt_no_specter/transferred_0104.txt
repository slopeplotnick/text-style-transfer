We deconstructed the relevance task and summarized the required model capabilities based on the deconstruction results, thereby providing clear and comprehensive guidance for subsequent iterations of relevance models in the industry. Moreover, we propose a complete methodology and documented practice that spans the entire pipeline, from preliminary work such as model selection to training paradigms and capability evaluation. During LORE's development, we explored various SFT and RL training strategies, yielding key findings that may benefit LLM fine-tuning in vertical domains. First, the data scaling law reveals diminishing returns; while model performance grows rapidly with initial data increments, it eventually plateaus as data volume increases (Section 3.3.3). Second, incorporating more features proves advantageous. Providing stable and relevant information, even if redundant, can enhance model performance (Section 3.2.1). Third, concise prompts containing essential information are most effective. While verbose prompts rich in details do not necessarily yield improvements, prompts lacking critical information can degrade model capability (Section 3.2.3). Furthermore, naive teacher CoT distillation results in negative effects, as direct teacher CoT distillation underperforms vanilla SFT due to distribution shifts (Section 6.1). On the other hand, curriculum learning remains effective, with strategies based on difficulty grading significantly outperforming randomly ordered approaches (Section 3.4.3). Additionally, long CoT is not essential for better performance; no significant increase in model output length was observed during RL, suggesting that long CoT may simply be a byproduct of improved model capability (Section 6.2). Finally, the rate of entropy collapse imposes a fundamental constraint on model performance. In the early stages of training, the model rapidly trades entropy for performance gains. However, once entropy decreases to a certain level, the model's exploratory capacity becomes constrained, limiting further improvement (Section 3.4.3). To address this, smart optimization strategies can effectively slow entropy collapse. Among the methods examined—clip-higher, on-policy, and entropy loss—the clip-higher strategy demonstrates optimal performance (Section 3.4.3).

Belief entrenchment consistently predicts an accuracy drop in problem domains where ground truth labels exist, such as predictive tasks. This suggests that the Martingale Score serves as a proxy for reasoning quality, even in settings where complete information is unavailable or still unfolding. In the context of language models, evaluating Bayesian rationality involves assessing their capacity to update beliefs in accordance with Bayes's theorem based on new evidence. Gupta et al. (2025) demonstrated that language models could follow Bayesian updates when given a substantial number of coin flips, despite initially possessing a biased prior. However, in more realistic settings with complex linguistic environments, Qiu et al. (2025) observed that language models fail to adjust their beliefs regarding user preferences as anticipated by the Bayesian framework. Similarly, Falck (2024) provided evidence that language models do not engage in Bayesian learning when contextual information is provided. Moreover, Zhao (2021) and Wang (2023) demonstrated that the performance of language models is influenced by the arrangement of examples within prompts. Efforts to enhance the Bayesian nature of in-context learning or reasoning have included emulating the inferential processes of an ideal Bayesian reasoner, employing abstract reasoning, and integrating abduction with deduction. However, these approaches frequently necessitate the incorporation of auxiliary structures, such as Bayesian networks, which significantly constrain their practical applicability. Language models exhibit a range of biases due to their extensive training on human-generated data. Notably, these models demonstrate biases distinct from those observed in human judgment, such as a propensity to favor specific positions, a preference for extended responses, an inclination towards positive expressions, and an unwarranted level of certainty. Among these biases, confirmation bias is particularly significant, as it is posited to contribute to polarization. It is widely posited that the majority of cognitive biases can be categorized as manifestations of confirmation bias. Accordingly, this study centers on confirmation bias and employs belief entrenchment as a framework to investigate its dynamics within the context of language models.

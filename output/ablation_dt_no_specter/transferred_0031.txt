During the execution of benchmark tests, the system may produce noise due to background processes and other confounding factors. To address some of these issues, we disabled the wireless networking hardware and collected multiple samples at different time intervals to filter out external noise. The definition of the statistical model, which forms the foundation of this research, is critical but not without potential shortcomings. Several assumptions underpin the model, grounded in prior research and the presumed data distribution. These measures aim to reduce subjective biases within the model. Nevertheless, as with any model, the selection of parameters, their distributions, and the parameters themselves remain open to debate. Our choice of parameters is justified by the specifications of the Java Virtual Machine (JVM) and the foundational definitions of programming languages, with particular attention to aspects parametrized within Java Bytecode. The use of the Gaussian distribution is supported by both ontological and epistemological reasoning. Ontologically, the Gaussian distribution frequently arises in natural phenomena, as it models processes involving the summation of independent fluctuations, which, according to the central limit theorem, converge toward a normal distribution. Epistemologically, given the limited knowledge of the measurements—characterized only by their mean and variance—this distribution effectively encapsulates our uncertainty. Additional challenges primarily pertain to the execution environment and the JVM. For instance, variations in the memory state prior to executing each bytecode pattern may influence the results. To address this, the garbage collector is invoked before each measurement cycle. Moreover, to minimize interference from paging, caching, and concurrent processes, each bytecode pattern is executed ten times in randomized order using a split-plot approach.

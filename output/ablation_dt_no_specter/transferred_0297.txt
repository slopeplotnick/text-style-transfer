Experiment 2 demonstrated that implementing a singular system prompt could enhance performance across all tested languages. This improvement indicates a systematic alteration in the model's behavior. To better understand how the model's output has changed, we analyzed the intermediate reasoning processes and assessed how modifications in reasoning behaviors correspond to multilingual performance. Specifically, we investigated whether optimizing the prompt influences the model's language preference. We began by evaluating the languages employed within the model's reasoning steps. As shown in Figure 7, even when using identical random prompts, the model exhibits distinct language preferences depending on the language of the input question. These variations correlate with the relative availability of training data for each language. For instance, due to the substantial volume of Chinese data in model families such as Qwen, the model responds to Chinese queries in Chinese almost as frequently as it does in English. After optimizing the prompts, these language preferences became more pronounced, with the model predominantly generating responses in the language of the input question rather than defaulting to English, as observed with the original system prompt. However, for languages with limited training data, such as Hindi, the proportion of responses in English remained largely unchanged despite the improved prompts. Moreover, languages other than the task language and English contributed minimally to the model's performance, as their influence diminished significantly after the prompt optimization. Further information regarding language identification is provided in Appendix A.9, with additional data detailed in Tables 6 and 7. In Figure 8, we examine the distribution of reasoning behavior types across various languages. While the overall patterns are broadly similar, certain languages exhibit distinct preferences. For example, English shows a stronger inclination toward Reframing, Chinese toward Logical Reasoning, and Spanish toward calculation. Conversely, languages with more limited training data, such as Hindi, display a higher proportion of the Other category, indicating reduced exposure to structured reasoning examples during pretraining. Although these trends remain consistent, they appear to reflect inherent language-specific characteristics in model pretraining rather than being directly influenced by prompt design.

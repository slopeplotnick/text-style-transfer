Programmatic Editing. Utilizing the python-pptx library allows for programmatic modifications of PowerPoint slides through code, a method commonly adopted in previous studies [9, 20, 25, 44, 45]. This approach is particularly effective for repetitive, well-defined, and content-centric tasks, such as performing systematic find-and-replace operations across all slides or translating textual content. However, it lacks the fine-grained control necessary for substantial modifications to the slide's aesthetic, such as altering the primary design or layout. Direct XML Editing. To overcome the limitations of programmatic methods in design and layout adjustments, we have enhanced PPTPilot with the capability to directly read and modify the raw OOXML files of the slides. This enables precise modifications, such as repositioning elements with exact specificity. Since XML encodes most of the information within a PowerPoint file, leveraging XML aligns effectively with existing tools designed for PowerPoint editing. Nevertheless, the inherent complexity of XML, with its extensive context and rigid formatting requirements, poses significant challenges for precise editing, especially when modifications involve a substantial number of slides. In such cases, programmatic approaches are generally more efficient and reliable. Skill Routing. To determine the optimal editing approach tailored to a user's specific requirements, we employ a sophisticated tool that routes the query to the appropriate methodology. Upon receiving a user request, this tool analyzes the user's intent in conjunction with the presentation's configuration, encompassing both visual and textual elements, to decide whether to implement code-based or XML-based editing techniques.

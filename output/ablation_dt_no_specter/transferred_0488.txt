We began our analysis by qualitatively examining the interview summaries, reading them multiple times to gain a thorough understanding of the results. During the interviews, we recorded the discussions, took handwritten notes, and later summarized the recorded content. This combined approach was designed to ensure that all relevant information was captured and to minimize the risk of missing critical details, aligning with established practices in qualitative research, such as recording and transcribing interviews. The analysis was conducted by the research team, which included the interviewer. Following this, we developed a set of metrics to perform a quantitative analysis. These metrics involved systematically tallying and categorizing responses to identify current good practices, perceived benefits, associated risks, and expressed concerns. To achieve this, we analyzed similar formulations of responses and distilled them into concise terms that encapsulated the core ideas. For example, when a participant stated, "The genericity pushes to factorize more and more," we summarized this with the terms "genericity" and "factorisation." For each topic, we first identified the frequency of generic terms and then categorized them by the roles of the speakers. The process of identifying potential key terms followed a systematic method. Initially, we removed stop words and punctuation, then sorted the remaining words by frequency. We selected the most meaningful terms and reviewed the documents to ensure their contextual relevance. To enhance clarity, we enriched some terms by adding modifiers, such as "too complex" instead of "complex." Furthermore, we compared the terms derived from the interviews with those found in relevant literature. When discrepancies arose, we replaced less suitable terms, such as substituting "unusable" with "useless," to better align with the language used in the texts. Conversely, when terms matched, we retained them as they were. This process allowed us to refine our synthesized responses by incorporating insights from literature, ensuring greater alignment with its terminology. Ultimately, this approach enabled us to extract the most relevant terms and significantly enhance the quality of our analysis.

Our methodology employs a hierarchical model that integrates multiple statistical distributions, each independently analyzing different aspects of the underlying statistical process, thereby allowing us to express statistical independence assumptions. For example, variations in energy consumption may occur between different physical CPUs of the same specification or across different instructions; however, these variations are independent. Our methodology defines a WCEC energy model to estimate a device's total energy consumption, as illustrated in Figure 1, and comprises two components: (1) the measurement component, which quantifies the energy consumption of the benchmark harness executed on the target hardware; and (2) the modeling component, which constructs a Bayesian model to characterize the energy consumption of the programming language. Given the existence of several statically typed programming languages compatible with the JVM, a unified modeling approach is employed. Specifically, we measure the energy consumption of bytecode patterns, which represent the translation of source code statements into bytecode by the compiler, as depicted in Figure 2. A microbenchmark consists of a single permutation of a source code statement derived from the complete set of possible data types and sizes applicable to a specific operation, as shown in Figure 3. Each microbenchmark measurement is conducted within a measurement cycle that evaluates the current, voltage, and execution time associated with the microbenchmark. Each measurement cycle begins with configuring the multimeter to ensure its readiness for accurate measurement. Subsequently, to mitigate memory-related noise, the garbage collector is executed to clear memory allocations during the measurement cycle. Each microbenchmark cycle is executed ten times to account for statistical variations in the measurements. Moreover, a split plot approach is employed, wherein the execution order of the microbenchmark cycles is randomized to prevent bias arising from the sequence of execution.

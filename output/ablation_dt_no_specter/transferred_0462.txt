Language Model Systems (LMS), which include Large Language Models (LLMs) and other variants, are utilized in a wide range of applications. These systems are crucial for functionalities such as medical chatbots, autonomous vehicle assistants, and programming support tools. As they are deployed in critical and publicly accessible domains, it is essential that these systems operate accurately and align with expected behavioral standards. Numerous studies have focused on integrating safety features within these systems. However, the current methods for evaluating and enforcing desirable behaviors are inadequate, as they do not provide universally applicable methods with formal assurances of efficacy. Such assurances are vital for ensuring user safety and fostering trust in these systems. In response, recent scholarly efforts have begun developing formal certification methodologies for LMS to ensure adherence to specific desirable behaviors, such as fairness, accurate question answering, and safety. These behaviors are articulated as mathematical specifications that the systems must comply with. For example, some researchers have established specifications related to fairness in responses, comprehension of textual material, management of sensitive dialogues, and the appropriate selection of tools. Nevertheless, these specific certifications are limited in their adaptability to diverse requirements or contexts due to their concentrated focus on particular properties and their predominant applicability in conversational settings. Modifications to the certification criteria necessitate comprehensive revisions. Consequently, the current approach to certifying these systems is characterized by a bespoke methodology tailored to each property, domain, and system type, a paradigm we aim to transform.

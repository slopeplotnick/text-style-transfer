The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has demonstrated remarkable potential in complex text-generation tasks such as academic writing, literature reviews, and scientific reports. LLM-based automatic survey systems are transforming how users acquire knowledge from vast information repositories by integrating retrieval, organization, and content synthesis into streamlined end-to-end generation pipelines. Both academia and industry have introduced various specialized systems for survey generation. These automated writing systems can be broadly categorized into three types: general long-text writing systems (e.g., Kimi, GLM) that provide broader capabilities for extended text generation; paper-writing systems (e.g., Chengpian, Doubao Paper Mode) that focus on structured composition of complete research papers; and survey-writing agents (e.g., SurveyGo, SurveyX, Panshi ScienceOne) specifically designed for academic survey generation. These systems not only process large volumes of literature and extract key information, but also generate well-structured survey drafts complete with sectioned organization, citation annotations, and logical coherence. However, despite significant advancements in developing these systems, effectively evaluating their performance remains a substantial challenge. Current evaluation methods predominantly rely on subjective assessments, leading to inconsistent and non-reproducible outcomes. This lack of standardization poses significant challenges in comparing systems, identifying effective components, and facilitating their improvement. Therefore, establishing a standardized evaluation benchmark is imperative to ensure the quality and reliability of these systems. Such a framework would also enable researchers to perform systematic comparisons and analyses, thereby advancing the development and refinement of these technologies. To address this gap, we introduce SurveyEval, a comprehensive benchmark that evaluates automatically generated surveys across three dimensions: overall quality, outline coherence, and reference accuracy. We extend the evaluation across seven academic disciplines and enhance the LLM-as-a-Judge framework with human-written reference surveys to increase alignment consistency between automated evaluation and human judgments.

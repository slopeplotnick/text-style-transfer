This section examines joint generative translation models, which integrate both speech and text modalities to facilitate seamless, high-quality translation across languages. It provides a comprehensive look at how state-of-the-art voice-interfaced language models process and comprehend speech and conversational signals. Over the past decade, significant progress has been made in the development and deployment of large-scale voice-powered AI virtual assistants, beginning with Apple's Siri, followed by Microsoft's Cortana, Google Assistant, Amazon's Alexa, and Samsung's Bixby. This section of the tutorial will cover the technological innovations underlying these AI assistants, including mechanisms for intent detection and contextual inference through specialized neural network architectures, dialogue management via advanced learning methodologies, integration of web search data, utilization of the semantic web, and the application of knowledge graphs for enhanced spoken language comprehension. Moreover, contemporary advancements in AI assistants are driven by the incorporation of large language models (LLMs), which facilitate improved conversational tracking and refinement of spoken language processing. Therefore, the tutorial will provide a comprehensive overview of current LLMs and their adaptation to domain-specific applications. It also addresses recent advancements in systems capable of managing multiple conversational threads, applicable to both general and specialized tasks, and encompasses an examination of datasets and their evaluation methodologies. Furthermore, it elucidates emerging research challenges in the development of language models, alongside the escalating demand for systems proficient in handling concurrent conversations.

We present DeepSeek-V3.2, a model that seamlessly integrates high computational efficiency with advanced reasoning and agentic capabilities. The core technical advancements of DeepSeek-V3.2 are as follows: (1) DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA): This novel attention mechanism significantly reduces computational overhead while maintaining robust performance in processing extensive contextual information. (2) Scalable Reinforcement Learning Framework: By leveraging a robust reinforcement learning protocol and scaling computational resources during post-training, DeepSeek-V3.2 achieves performance parity with GPT-5. Furthermore, our high-performance variant, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, not only surpasses GPT-5 but also matches the reasoning capabilities of Gemini-3.0-Pro, earning gold-medal distinctions in the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) and the International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI). (3) Large-Scale Agentic Task Synthesis Pipeline: To enhance reasoning in tool-use scenarios, we developed an innovative synthesis pipeline that systematically generates large-scale training datasets. This approach enables efficient agentic post-training, resulting in significant improvements in generalization and instruction-following robustness within complex, interactive environments. The introduction of reasoning models such as DeepSeek-AI (2025) and OpenAI (2024a) marked a transformative milestone in the evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs), driving substantial advancements across diverse domains. However, a notable divergence has emerged in recent months. While open-source models (e.g., MiniMax, MoonShot, Qwen, ZhiPu-AI, 2025) continue to evolve, the development trajectory of closed-source proprietary models (e.g., Anthropic, DeepMind, OpenAI, 2025) has accelerated at a much steeper rate. Consequently, the performance gap between open-source and closed-source models is widening, with proprietary systems demonstrating superior proficiency in addressing complex tasks. Our analysis highlights three critical limitations of open-source models in this context. First, their reliance on conventional vanilla attention mechanisms (Vaswani et al., 2017) restricts their efficiency in handling long-context scenarios, posing scalability challenges. Second, open-source models are hindered by insufficient computational resources during post-training, limiting their effectiveness in tackling demanding tasks. Finally, in the realm of AI agents, open-source models exhibit notable deficiencies in generalization and instruction-following capabilities compared to their proprietary counterparts (EvalSys, 2025; Li et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2025), thereby reducing their practical utility in real-world applications.

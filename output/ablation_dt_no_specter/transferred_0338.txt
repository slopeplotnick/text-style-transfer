The left part of Figure 2 illustrates the prompt configuration along with an example of the extracted triples for a single training image, showcasing the semantic structuring of various attributes. Specifically, the output consists of multiple ontology-aligned triples that encapsulate diverse aspects of the garment, including category (depicts), shape (has_silhouette), material (has_material_type), pattern (has_pattern), and other relationships defined by the Fashionpedia ontology. Each triple adheres to the standardized format (image_id, relation, attribute), offering a clear and interpretable representation of the image. This structured approach facilitates downstream graph construction and comparison at the conceptual level. To retrieve influential training samples, we compared the knowledge graph (KG) of each generated image against those of all training images, focusing on node-level overlap. For each prompt, we retained the training images whose graphs exhibited the highest degree of similarity with the graph of the generated image. The prompt used was: "Generate triples for the fashion item depicted in the image, adhering to the relationships defined by the Fashionpedia Ontology, accessible at: \\({ONTOLOGY_PATH} = \). Focus exclusively on the fashion item within the image identified by image_id: \\)([IMAGE_ID=)} and provide the triples that describe the image (image_id, relationship, attribute_value)." For example, for image 58810.jpg, the extracted triples were: 58810.jpg, depicts, Shirt; 58810.jpg, belongs_to_category, Upperbody; 58810.jpg, has_nickname, None; 58810.jpg, has_silhouette, Slim-fit; 58810.jpg, has_waistline, Straight; 58810.jpg, has_length, Long-sleeved; 58810.jpg, has_collar_type, Classic; 58810.jpg, has_lapel_type, Not Applicable; 58810.jpg, has_neckline, Crew-neck; 58810.jpg, has_sleeve_type, Long Sleeves with Cuffs; 58810.jpg, has_pocket_type, Not Applicable; 58810.jpg, has_opening_type, Button-front Closure; 58810.jpg, has_material_type, Cotton-blend Fabric; 58810.jpg, has_leather_type, None; 58810.jpg, has_fabric_treatment, Not Applicable; 58810.jpg, has_pattern, Plaid; 58810.jpg, has_animal_pattern, None. These retrieved graphs were subsequently utilized in the unlearning phase. Beyond retrieval, Neo4j enabled a broader exploration of the graph space, such as identifying frequent attributes and visualizing the distribution of concepts across the dataset. The unlearning procedure involved retraining the generative model after removing selected training samples to evaluate their influence on the generated output. A total of ten unlearning experiments were conducted using both the baseline and the proposed method. In each experiment, images corresponding to 15 prompts were generated before and after the unlearning process. To ensure consistency with the baseline setup described in [2], a comparable subset of training images identified as the most influential for each prompt was systematically removed.

We evaluated various methodologies, including RL-Trained, Mixture-of-Inputs, CoLaR, and LiteReason, to assess their performance. Using Qwen 2.5 7B-Instruct, chapters were generated based on the plans derived from each method, and 20 pairwise comparisons were collected for every method combination. Model performance was compared using Bradley-Terry relative strengths, with further details provided in Appendix E. Initially, we conducted ablations on the Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks, with results presented in Table 4. LiteReason variants were benchmarked against two baselines, both built upon the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model: a default prompted variant and an RL-trained counterpart. Crucially, neither baseline incorporates latent reasoning capabilities. Notably, LiteReason without reasoning prompts (RP), which skips intermediate reasoning steps, resulted in longer reasoning processes and performed worse than the RL-trained baseline. Table 7 provides further comparisons of models with and without implicit-thought prompts. Additionally, we examined the usage of latent thoughts during RL training to determine whether reinforcement learning influenced their frequency. Interestingly, latent-thought usage increased sharply at the beginning of RL training but declined as training progressed. Future work could explore enhancing latent reasoning by refining prompts or increasing the likelihood of implicit-token markers. Overall, LiteReason demonstrated marginal performance improvements, achieving a 0.8% gain on the Flawed Fictions dataset and a 58% improvement on the NCP dataset. These modest gains are likely due to its similarity to the RL-trained baseline while exploring alternative reasoning paths that the base model would not typically generate. Unsurprisingly, training-free methods such as Mixture-of-Inputs (MoI) and Soft Thinking performed similarly to the untrained model in both performance and token length. On the NCP task, Soft Thinking achieved a slight improvement over the baseline but with increased token usage, whereas MoI underperformed while generating fewer tokens. For the Flawed Fictions task, differences relative to the baseline were minor and fell within confidence intervals. Further research is needed to improve the consistency and efficacy of training-free methods across diverse tasks and reasoning paradigms.

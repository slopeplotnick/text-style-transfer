Udio can transform natural-language prompts into polished musical compositions across various genres, including lyrics in multiple languages. In terms of the perception of AI-generated music, research in computational creativity and machine learning has explored the creative value of these models through subjective listening assessments. Participants evaluate the musical outputs of these models in comparison to alternative models or compositions crafted by human musicians. Frequent evaluation metrics include overall quality, creativity, and novelty, as well as specific musical components such as melody, harmony, and rhythm. Another key metric is the degree of naturalness or humanness, which is assessed either through rating scales or via a musical Turing Test, where listeners determine whether a composition was produced by a human or an AI system, under conditions where they may be informed, uninformed, or intentionally misled about the source. In specific tasks like Bach chorale generation, even early models such as Deepbach (Hadjeres et al., 2017) produced outputs that were challenging to distinguish from authentic compositions. To evaluate the emotional aspects of AI-generated music, listeners often attempt to discern the intended emotional content by categorizing it into discrete labels such as "happy" or "sad," or by using frameworks that quantify emotions along dimensional scales. As AI music generation continues to advance, research has increasingly focused on whether listeners' evaluations are influenced by their awareness or assumptions regarding the origin of the compositions. However, the evidence remains inconsistent: Initial investigations that manipulated participants' awareness of the composer did not reveal a significant bias in evaluations of live recordings of string-quartet performances produced by humans and AI systems. Similarly, Zlatkov et al. (2023) did not observe a systematic bias against AI-generated music when using contemporary pop music samples as stimuli. Moreover, consumer marketing-oriented research conducted by Moura and Maw (2021) identified no consistent preference for music based on authorship, regardless of whether the genre was classical or pop.

Energy consumption is an increasingly critical concern across various domains, ranging from mobile devices to large-scale data centers. To address consumption-related issues, developers require detailed insights into the energy usage of their software. However, previous approaches have primarily focused on broader aspects, such as specific functions or entire programs, rather than individual source code statements. Furthermore, these methods often rely on point estimates to assess the CPU's energy consumption, neglecting other hardware components and limiting their applicability for statistical reasoning and explainability. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel methodology that extends beyond CPU-centric measurements and point estimates. Specifically, our approach focuses on predicting the energy consumption of statically typed JVM-based programming languages, including Java, Kotlin, and Scala. By analyzing the translation of source code statements into Java bytecode, we measure the energy consumption of bytecode patterns and construct a statistical model. Unlike prior frequentist approaches, our methodology employs Bayesian statistics, enabling predictions of energy usage through statistical distributions while identifying contributing factors. The model incorporates three code-derived attributes—data size, data type, and operations—as well as one hardware-related factor: the device. To validate this methodology, we implemented it for Java, analyzing bytecode patterns and evaluating energy predictions on previously unseen programs. The results reveal that all four factors significantly influence energy consumption, with notable findings such as substantial variations between devices of the same model and the pronounced impact of operations and data types on energy usage. Moreover, experimental results demonstrate that the predicted energy consumption closely aligns with the actual energy usage of the programs, further substantiating the validity of our approach. This study introduces a methodology for constructing energy models that can serve as a foundation for future research, such as energy-aware program analysis or verification tools, to assess software systems' energy efficiency.

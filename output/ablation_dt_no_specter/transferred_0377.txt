Our approach estimates the potential improvement in a solver's performance when using a verifier for solution selection. To evaluate the accuracy of this estimation, we conduct experiments using various combinations of solvers and verifiers from a subset of 12 models, consisting of the three smallest models from each of four distinct groups. For each problem, the solver iteratively generates solutions until the verifier identifies one as correct, with a maximum of nine attempts allowed; if no satisfactory solution is found, the outcome of the final attempt is retained. The results, along with the anticipated improvements, are presented in Figure 4. Although minor improvements may be less discernible due to their limited magnitude, and the nine-attempt threshold may constrain the ability to fully capture broader trends, the overall findings align with expected outcomes. Notably, self-verification is less effective compared to verification conducted by an external entity, especially when the verifier belongs to a distinct group. The principal assertion is that our methodology for estimating potential improvements is robust and does not require extensive additional effort for validation. It is well-documented that individuals exhibit a bias towards solutions resembling their own reasoning, which may affect the perceived accuracy of their conclusions. Our findings indicate that a similar phenomenon occurs in solver-verifier interactions. As demonstrated earlier, models with strong reasoning capabilities derive minimal benefit from self-verification but experience enhanced performance when verified by distinct models, revealing a similar bias. The key insight is that when a solver and verifier exhibit congruent reasoning patterns, the verifier is more likely to endorse erroneous solutions, thereby diminishing the potential for improvement. Employing a verifier with a distinct reasoning approach mitigates this issue.

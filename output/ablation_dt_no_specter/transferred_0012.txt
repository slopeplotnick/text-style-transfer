Prompt design is a pivotal factor influencing the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs). However, the impact of prompt specificity—whether a prompt is detailed or vague—remains underexplored. To address this gap, this paper introduces DETAIL, a comprehensive framework for assessing LLM performance across varying levels of prompt specificity. Using GPT-4, we generate prompts with different degrees of specificity, measure specificity through perplexity, and evaluate response accuracy via GPT-based semantic equivalence. Experiments conducted on 30 novel reasoning tasks with GPT-4 and O3-mini demonstrate that higher prompt specificity generally enhances accuracy, particularly for smaller models and tasks requiring procedural reasoning. These findings emphasize the importance of adaptive prompting strategies and provide tools and datasets to support further research in this area. The rapid advancements in LLMs have revolutionized numerous domains, showcasing their exceptional generative capabilities (Zhao et al., 2023; Hurst et al., 2024; Anthropic, 2024; Gemini Team et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025). No longer limited to simple tasks, LLMs now consistently surpass state-of-the-art methods across diverse challenges (Bai et al., 2022; Touvron et al., 2023). Recent evaluations predominantly focus on their efficacy in solving complex mathematical and multi-step reasoning problems (Vendrow et al., 2025; Suzgun et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024). Enhancing LLM reasoning remains a critical area of research (Qiao et al., 2023; Patil, 2025), with innovative prompting strategies such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) (Wei et al., 2022), Tree of Thoughts (ToT) (Yao et al., 2023), and Graph of Thoughts (GoT) (Besta et al., 2024) emerging to decompose intricate problems into manageable steps. Nonetheless, a key challenge persists in determining the optimal level of prompt specificity to achieve accurate and coherent outcomes. While detailed prompts offer clear guidance that can structure the model's reasoning process, they may inadvertently limit its cognitive flexibility. Conversely, vague prompts provide greater freedom for exploration but risk introducing ambiguity and errors.

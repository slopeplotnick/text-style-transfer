Effect of Data-augmented Retrieval. We investigated the role of structured climate data in retrieval-augmented generation. While we hypothesized that integrating structured climate data would improve the factual support and interpretability of generated answers, the results indicate that this capability remains in its early stages. As shown in Table 1, the DATA_RAG pipeline, which combines literature and structured data, underperforms the literature-only RAG pipeline across most metrics, with the most notable decline observed in accuracy. This suggests that the current model is still limited in its ability to effectively leverage structured climate data to enhance textual reasoning. CAIRNS, a question-answering framework designed for agricultural climate adaptation, aims to balance readability, accuracy, and citation transparency. It incorporates structured prompting (SGP), multi-turn reasoning, and a consistency-weighted Hybrid Evaluator. Experimental findings reveal that SGP significantly improves structural clarity and citation reliability while preserving factual precision, marking a meaningful advancement in aligning QA outputs with the communication standards preferred by domain researchers. However, the effectiveness of data-augmented retrieval remains constrained at this stage. By employing inter-model agreement as a weighting signal, the Hybrid Evaluator achieves superior credibility among automated evaluation methods without requiring fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. This approach is particularly effective in resource-constrained or expert-limited settings for question-answering tasks. Moving forward, we plan to enhance location-aware reasoning by integrating fine-grained geographic information, such as latitude and longitude, to support document transferability across regions with similar climatic conditions and to broaden the generalizability of literature-derived insights. Additionally, we aim to develop a comprehensive human-aligned evaluation benchmark encompassing diverse question types and subdomains. This benchmark will serve as both a calibration tool for the Hybrid Evaluator and a training corpus for future domain-specific models, fostering reproducible and reliable QA research in climate adaptation.

The present study evaluates four distinct models, MOIRAI, CHRONOS, GTT, and LightGTS, selected for their diversity in architectural design and training methodologies. Each model employs unique approaches to learning and prediction, with configurations tailored to accommodate our dataset while isolating normalization effects from capacity differences. Specifically, MOIRAI utilizes a distribution-based approach with Negative-Log-Likelihood loss, CHRONOS employs tokenized representations optimized via Cross-Entropy loss, GTT adopts point-forecasting with MAE, and LightGTS leverages point-forecasting with MSE. Notably, CHRONOS mitigates the influence of data scale by discretizing continuous values into scale-agnostic bins prior to optimization, ensuring its objective remains invariant to input magnitudes. Furthermore, all data undergoes standardization at the dataset level to balance channel scales, followed by instance-level normalization to capture local non-stationarity. This dual-step normalization prevents high-magnitude channels from disproportionately affecting optimization dynamics, even when predictions are de-normalized. For evaluation, we employ Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE), a scale-free metric that compares forecast errors against a naive baseline, thereby addressing inconsistencies arising from varying normalization schemes. By contrasting the reduction in MASE with a conventional baseline, our study quantifies the impact of data normalization on model performance and highlights its role in enhancing predictive accuracy across diverse architectures.

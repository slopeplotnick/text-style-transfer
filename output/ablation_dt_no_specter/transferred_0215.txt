As depicted in Fig. 12, the experimental group employing the curriculum learning strategy exhibits accelerated convergence and a more stable optimization trajectory concerning both reward metrics and model performance enhancement. During the reinforcement learning (RL) process, as the policy approaches convergence, the stochasticity of the model's output distribution diminishes, resulting in increasingly deterministic generation outcomes. This reduction in randomness constrains the model's capacity for exploration, thereby limiting its potential for further performance improvement. To address this issue, we explored various methodologies aimed at decelerating the rate at which the model becomes predictable, thereby facilitating continued exploration. Figure 13 presents a comparative analysis of these strategies. Among them, the Clip-higher strategy demonstrates superior efficacy by effectively moderating the predictability rate while allowing its progression, thus achieving an optimal balance between exploration and exploitation. This approach ensures training stability and yields substantial performance enhancements. Conversely, the On-policy strategy effectively stabilizes policy entropy, nearly halting its decline. Although this approach enhances exploration capabilities, it concurrently obstructs effective policy convergence, resulting in limited performance improvements. The Off-policy + Entropy Loss strategy, however, is characterized by training instability issues. Initially, this approach decelerates the rate at which the model becomes predictable; yet, as the model's randomness diminishes, the excessive penalization of stochastic behavior begins to dominate, leading to erratic model behavior and ultimately causing training instability. Moreover, this method necessitates extensive experimentation to determine optimal parameter settings, thereby increasing its computational cost. In summary, the Clip-higher strategy emerges as the most effective solution in this context, as it achieves a favorable balance between exploration and efficient learning by regulating the rate at which the model transitions toward predictability.

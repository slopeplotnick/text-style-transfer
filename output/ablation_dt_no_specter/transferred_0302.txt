The GPT-5 model demonstrates a consistently high refusal rate that remains unaffected by increases in context padding. As a result of this persistent refusal rate, we observe a slight decrease in performance on harmful tasks; however, the initially low harm score prevents us from drawing definitive conclusions. Consequently, we do not further investigate benign tasks with context padding exceeding 10,000 tokens, given the stability of the initial results and the high cost of conducting such experiments. The type of context padding, however, can significantly influence model performance. As shown in Figure 3, different padding types have varying effects. For the GPT-4.1-nano model, non-relevant padding achieves the highest scores, followed by relevant padding, random padding, and finally multi-task padding, which performs the worst. In contrast, Grok 4 Fast deviates from this pattern due to its consistently high refusal rate, resulting in performance scores across different padding types that remain close to the no-padding baseline. Notably, within the range of 50K to 100K tokens, Grok 4 Fast exhibits behavior indicative of mode collapse, as the performance scores for various padding types converge. The position of the padding also plays a critical role in model performance. As illustrated in Figure 4, placing padding before the task description slows the rate of capability degradation compared to placing it after. This is likely because padding placed before the task description keeps the task description and execution closer together, enabling the attention mechanisms to operate more effectively within the model’s training distribution. Modern attention mechanisms, such as sliding window and RoPE, struggle to effectively attend to tokens separated by large distances, as such patterns are rare during training. When padding is placed after the instruction, the model must attend across a larger gap between the instruction and execution, which may explain the accelerated performance degradation.

The recordings were initially reviewed to ensure clarity and adherence to the provided guidelines. Once approved, transcription was conducted by trained personnel using a standardized system. Only those recordings and their corresponding transcriptions that successfully passed the final quality assurance process were incorporated into the dataset. Remuneration was stratified according to linguistic requirements, thereby ensuring that the final dataset maintained high technical quality, encompassed a diverse array of speakers, and adhered to stringent ethical standards. Moreover, the unscripted prompts were developed by dedicated language teams to guarantee cultural relevance and naturalness in expression. Each language team consisted of a language lead and typically three language assistants. The team collaboratively reviewed one another's work, with the final approval of prompts resting with the language lead. Using Microsoft Forms, the teams began by selecting a broad domain of knowledge and subsequently narrowed their focus to a specific topic within that domain, which further branched into related subtopics. Language teams were trained to develop prompts designed to elicit diverse response types. For instance, rather than simply asking, "What is your favorite fruit?" they were encouraged to frame prompts such as, "What is your favorite fruit and why?" This approach emphasized the importance of crafting prompts capable of generating varied linguistic outputs, including not only declarative statements but also interrogatives, exclamations, imperatives, and other response forms. Furthermore, teams were encouraged to adapt topics and subtopics to enhance cultural relevance and, where necessary, sensitivity. The objective was to encapsulate the national lexicon of the South African context as comprehensively as possible; consequently, the topics and subdomains were intricately aligned with South African life and tailored to the specific linguistic nuances of each language.

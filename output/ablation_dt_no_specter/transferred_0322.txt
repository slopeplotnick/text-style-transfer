We utilized a multimodal language model to analyze images, and after generating these images, we proceeded with the structured extraction of key details to semantically describe their content. We employed the open-source model LLaMA 3.2 Vision, accessed via the Ollama framework, as it was chosen for its capability to process image inputs and produce structured outputs in natural language. Its proficiency in generating detailed, attribute-rich descriptions made it well-suited for transforming visual content into structured knowledge representations. Although our primary objective was the extraction of structured triples, this task is analogous to image captioning. Recent evaluations indicate that multimodal language models exhibit comparable performance in visual comprehension tasks. While all models demonstrated a high degree of reliability, our focus on the structural organization and comparative analysis of information led us to utilize a single model as a representative case study. To guide and constrain the triple extraction process, we adopted the Fashionpedia ontology, a comprehensive taxonomy developed for fashion-related visual understanding. This ontology delineates categories, components, and attributes pertinent to garments and accessories. By incorporating this guide into the model's instructions, we ensured that the extracted information adhered to a precise and well-defined structure. To assess the importance of the ontology, we conducted an experiment where it was omitted, and the findings, presented in Table 1, indicate that the application of the ontology significantly enhances the generation of triples per image, thereby encompassing a broader spectrum of relationships. In its absence, the model predominantly identifies a limited array of conspicuous visual elements, resulting in diminished informational output. This underscores the critical importance of ontology utilization in achieving comprehensive and consistent structures, which are indispensable for facilitating graph-based comparative analyses.

Cross-LLM Agreement: We construct pairs of AI judges, such as GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-pro, to assess the degree of agreement in their belief evaluations. Given the extensive availability of data from GPT-4o compared to other models, we designate GPT-4o as the primary judge for all comparative analyses. Human-LLM Agreement: We also evaluate the alignment between AI judges and human evaluators by requesting human participants to perform belief evaluations identical to those conducted by the AI models. Subsequently, we conduct a comparative analysis between human evaluators and AI systems, exemplified by comparisons such as human evaluator 1 versus GPT-4o. Considering results from both cross-judge consistency analysis and human-evaluation validation, it appears highly improbable that the observed outcomes are attributable to judge bias. In this study, we propose the Martingale Score as a principled and unsupervised measure of belief entrenchment in LLM reasoning. We demonstrate that the Martingale Score reliably predicts the ground-truth accuracy of these reasoning processes and subsequently apply it across a diverse set of models, prompts, and problem domains. Furthermore, we conduct a detailed analysis to identify key factors that exacerbate or mitigate the extent of belief entrenchment. Limitations: A notable limitation of this study is the absence of evidence demonstrating a correlation between the Martingale Score, which reflects belief entrenchment, and the Brier Score, a metric for accuracy, within the OpenReview domain. This may be attributed to the nature of ground-truth labeling in OpenReview, which relies on community-voted decisions, inherently subject to variability and noise. Nevertheless, as illustrated in Table 1, belief entrenchment appears to manifest more prominently in domains requiring human judgment, such as r/ChangeMyView and OpenReview. This raises concerns over adopting such AI models in those domains.

Experts in numerical modeling transform high-level hypotheses into numerical representations by translating conceptual ideas into quantitative hypotheses and incorporating detailed parameters through model parametrization. These hypotheses are assigned discrete probabilities, while detailed variables are represented using probability distributions. To validate this prior distribution model, it must be tested against empirical data. This process, often referred to as forward modeling, involves domain experts employing numerical methods to simulate expected data patterns. The statistical evaluation of these predictions, including falsification tests using metrics such as p-values, determines the accuracy of the model. Subsequently, experts specializing in Monte Carlo simulation apply Bayesian inversion to refine the model hypotheses and variables based on the data. Decisions are then guided by Bayesian uncertainty quantification, which necessitates collaborative discussions among all domain experts. Before delving into these possibilities, however, it is essential to review some common applications of AI in mineral exploration. To illustrate the concept of AI, consider the example of a self-driving car tasked with traveling from one location to another. The primary goal is to reach the destination safely, lawfully, and efficiently within a reasonable timeframe. To achieve this, the car performs actions such as steering, accelerating, and braking. Using sensors, it classifies the current state of the road and predicts future actions for itself and other road users or objects. The key components of this system include rewards, which represent the objectives of safety, legality, and efficiency; actions, which encompass maneuvers like steering and braking; and state, which refers to the current road conditions, including the presence and movement of other vehicles and pedestrians.

To address the limitations of fixed viewpoints and enhance spatial cues, we diversify camera rotation trajectories. Each trajectory begins with a randomized pitch, yaw, and roll orientation, followed by stochastic drifts to simulate natural movement patterns. Moreover, we ensure that the primary visual cues associated with the audio remain within the field of view for a specified duration. Specifically, we compute the direction of maximum audio energy for each clip using spherical harmonics decomposition and adjust the camera's initial orientation to center the trajectory around the primary sound source, as illustrated in Fig. 1. After capturing the binaural audio, we filter out samples with a normalized left-right channel difference below 0.01, retaining only those with distinct spatial auditory cues. BiAudio emerges as the largest FoV video dataset in the binaural audio format. As shown in Tab. 1, existing real-world binaural datasets are significantly smaller in scale. While SimBinaural offers over 100 hours of content, its synthetic nature limits its applicability to real-world scenarios. Furthermore, most datasets focus on specific auditory contexts, such as street scenes in OAP or musical environments in Fair-Play, SimBinaural, and MUSIC-21. In contrast, BiAudio is large-scale and open-domain, encompassing diverse real-world sound environments. Spatial Cue Enhancement (Sec. 3.1) further improves spatial perception and dynamics, making it particularly suitable for video-to-binaural spatial audio generation. The overall architecture of ViSAudio is depicted in Figure 2. Our approach builds upon components of the MMAudio architecture, fine-tuning the pretrained model to preserve its robust audio generation capabilities and ensure effective generalization across open-domain acoustic environments. To produce binaural audio with both channel coherence and spatial distinctiveness, we employ Dual-Branch Audio Generation (Sec. 4.2), where two dedicated branches independently predict the left and right audio channels. Additionally, we introduce a Conditional Spacetime Module (Sec. 4.3) to extract spatio-temporal features from the video, integrating them into the generation process to enhance the spatial realism of the synthesized audio.

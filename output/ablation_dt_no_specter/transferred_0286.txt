In the context of SWE agents, there has been preliminary effort to identify domains where these agents excel and areas where their performance is deficient. Studies analyzing agent performance on multi-file issues [16] and single-file saturation [17] reveal that state-of-the-art SWE agents face significant challenges with multi-file problems. Consequently, recent research has emphasized the need for more comprehensive evaluation frameworks that consider factors such as efficiency, robustness, and reasoning quality [11, 29, 30, 39, 41, 50]. Moreover, further investigations [12, 15] have delved into the failure modes of SWE agents by examining their operational trajectories, uncovering issues such as incorrect fault localization, difficulty in understanding complex code dependencies, and challenges in maintaining contextual awareness across multiple files. For example, Deshpande et al. [15] introduced TRAIL (Trace Reasoning and Agentic Issue Localization), a taxonomy of errors accompanied by a benchmark of 148 human-annotated traces from GAIA and SWE-Bench Lite to enable systematic analysis. Similarly, Cemri et al. [12] proposed MAST (Multi-Agent System Failure Taxonomy) for multi-agent systems, analyzing 150 traces across five systems and five benchmarks, though their taxonomy required human annotations for effective analysis. Furthermore, Ceka et al. [11] developed a flow graph to represent agent actions, categorized into project understanding, context monitoring, and debugging and testing processes. However, their analysis was conducted manually and was limited in scope, focusing on bug localization for only 20 out of 500 issues in SWE-Bench Verified. Additionally, Liu et al. [29] devised a taxonomy of LLM failure modes for software engineering tasks, structured into three primary phases, nine main categories, and 25 fine-grained subcategories. This taxonomy was applied to investigate the causes of tool failures, identifying issues such as agents becoming trapped in unproductive iterative loops. However, their root cause analysis was also conducted manually on only 150 out of 500 issues.

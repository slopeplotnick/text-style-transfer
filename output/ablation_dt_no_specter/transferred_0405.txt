When a target appears on the left or right side of the screen, the participant is prompted to open and close the corresponding fist until the target disappears, followed by a period of relaxation. Conversely, if the target appears at the top or bottom of the screen, the participant must open and close both fists if the target is at the top, or both feet if the target is at the bottom, until the target vanishes, followed by relaxation. By focusing on specific channels and optimizing the feature extraction process, we ensured the acquisition of relevant motor and premotor brain activity data crucial for accurately classifying motor imagery tasks. To mitigate the confounding effects of evoked responses on data interpretation, we devised a methodology that initiates analysis one second post-stimulus, thus circumventing the immediate response interval. This approach allowed us to examine the brain's spontaneous activity. By concentrating on these stable characteristics indicative of the individual's sensorimotor state, our method enhances the precision of brain signal classification. Various data preprocessing techniques were employed prior to applying classical machine learning algorithms. Initially, signal processing methods such as Common Spatial Patterns (CSP) were utilized, a technique extensively applied in EEG signal classification. CSP was originally introduced by Koles et al. This approach is used to extract and differentiate features by optimizing the variance between distinct motor imagery tasks or mental states. It involves transforming EEG signals into an alternative vector space, where the variance associated with one class is maximized, while that of the other class is minimized. The primary objective of this method is to identify and apply spatial filters that emphasize critical distinctions between multiple classes. CSP constitutes a multi-stage process that begins with the computation of covariance matrices for each EEG signal class. The covariance between two variables, X and Y, is determined using a specific formula.

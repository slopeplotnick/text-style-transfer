Nonparametric comparisons between real and simulated datasets revealed no significant differences in central tendency for any subscale, as indicated by Mann-Whitney U tests (U = 46912-53698, p = .09-.89). However, Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests indicated significant differences in the overall distribution of scores for all variables (D = 0.36-0.42, all p < .001). These results suggest that while the simulated data closely approximated the median values of the real responses, their underlying distributions differed substantially in shape. Therefore, the findings partially support H4. Histograms comparing the distributions of the real and simulated datasets are presented in Figure 2. To minimize potential biases associated with using a dataset previously collected by our team, we conducted a follow-up analysis using data collected by an independent research group. Specifically, we selected the English dataset used to validate the "Information and Communication Self-Concept, 25 Items" (ICT-SC25) scale, which measures individuals' self-perceived competence in information and communication technologies (Schauffel et al., 2021a). This instrument comprises 25 Likert-type items, organized into five subscales that assess domain-specific aspects of ICT self-concept: Communicate, Process and Store, Generate Content, Safe Application, and Solve Problems. The decision to use this scale and its accompanying dataset was based on its rigorous multisample, multiphase validation process and the public availability of its complete validation dataset (Schauffel et al., 2021b). Consistent with the methodology in Study 1, the hypotheses and procedures for this study were preregistered on OSF prior to generating the LLM-simulated data. The preregistration is available for consultation at the following link. The procedure for generating simulated data differed from Study 1 in two key aspects. First, because the ICT-SC25 dataset does not include ethnicity information, the prompts for generating simulated data were adjusted to include only age and gender. Second, as the ICT-SC25 dataset provides unique participant IDs, simulated participants were matched to real participants based on these identifiers, ensuring exact alignment for age and gender. Duplicate IDs in the original dataset were identified and removed to maintain precise matching between the real and simulated samples.

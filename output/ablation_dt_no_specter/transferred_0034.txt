In this section, we introduce MIGR, a method that determines the initial modality of focus based on Mutual Information (MI). Through this approach, we aim to mitigate biases present in the initial reasoning phase and ensure that early reasoning remains anchored in the modality exhibiting the most pronounced emotional signals. Our method is structured into two primary stages. First, we present modality-aligned SFT, which guides the model in initiating its reasoning process by prioritizing the modality identified by the MI label as emotion-dominant. Second, we propose Modality-Aware Reward Optimization, which aims to enhance modality prioritization and ensure emotion-grounded reasoning while promoting stability during the optimization process. To achieve this, we define two complementary rewards: the modality-aligned order reward, which encourages adherence to the prioritized reasoning sequence, and the modality-grounded reasoning reward, which reinforces reasoning that is anchored in emotional signals. The subsequent sections elucidate these stages in detail. Recent research has demonstrated the efficacy of SFT as an initial approach for emotion understanding tasks, even when limited to a small quantity of high-quality supervision, such as that provided by the EMER dataset. Motivated by these findings, we commence our training with a comparable strategy. Although the EMER dataset provides high-quality training data, the SFT stage can be further refined by incorporating additional samples that exhibit particularly clear and consistent emotional signals. Therefore, to enhance early-stage supervision, we augment the training dataset with supplementary emotion-consistent examples. These examples are identified through the extraction of Facial Action Units (FAUs) from video frames, as FAUs are strongly associated with emotional expressions. Subsequently, we assess whether the extracted FAU patterns correspond precisely with the target emotion by employing a predefined FAU-emotion mapping table. Only those samples whose FAU patterns exhibit an exact correspondence with the target emotion are retained. For these selected samples, we construct reasoning-answer pairs utilizing FAU-consistent emotional evidence to inform and guide the reasoning process.

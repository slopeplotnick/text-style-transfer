We source forecasting questions from Metaculus [Metaculus, 2015] and Polymarket [Polymarket, 2020] to evaluate belief entrenchment in LLMs. This domain is particularly suitable for two main reasons: (1) forecasting questions are resolved with definitive ground truth labels, providing a clear benchmark for evaluation; and (2) strong performance on these questions demonstrates Bayesian-like reasoning, as it requires the model to assess evidence and update its beliefs proportionally. While forecasting differs from factual question answering, it still demands well-calibrated and unbiased belief updates, making it a valuable proxy for reasoning under uncertainty. Another effective setting for assessing belief entrenchment is scientific peer review, which also meets these criteria. For this purpose, we leverage the open-access ICLR submission dataset from OpenReview [Höpner et al., 2025], which includes paper abstracts, bibliographies, reviewer comments, rebuttals, and final acceptance decisions. In our experimental setup, the model is tasked with simulating the role of an area chair, making final acceptance decisions based on the abstract and the arguments presented in the reviews and rebuttals. This approach allows us to examine how the model’s reasoning evolves when initial impressions, such as those formed from the abstract, are challenged by subsequent evidence, such as critiques and responses. To assess belief entrenchment, we employ two reasoning methodologies: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) [Wei et al., 2022] and debate [Khan et al., 2024]. The debate method involves two iterations of the same expert model presenting opposing arguments, enabling a less informed observer to better understand the subject matter. We conduct evaluations using models such as GPT-4o, DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Flash, LLaMA 4 Scout, and LLaMA 4 Maverick. To investigate the influence of prior beliefs on reasoning, we manipulate the models’ initial conditions through system prompts. In addition to a baseline condition without a prompt, we introduce two alternative configurations: one with a prompt aligned to the initial belief and another designed to encourage critical thinking.

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly utilized in applications such as Google's AI Overviews, where they synthesize information from multiple long documents. However, it is unclear whether these models assign equal importance to all inputs. To investigate this, researchers focused on abortion-related news, constructing 40 triplets of articles—each containing one pro, one neutral, and one con article—permuting each triplet into six different input orders, and prompting Gemini 2.5 Flash to generate a neutral summary. The summaries were then evaluated against the original articles using metrics such as ROUGE-L (lexical overlap), BERTScore (semantic similarity), and SummaC (factual consistency). One-way ANOVA revealed a significant primacy effect for BERTScore across all stances, indicating that summaries were more semantically aligned with the first article in the sequence. Pairwise comparisons further demonstrated that the first article exerted a significantly greater influence on the summaries compared to the second and third articles, which showed no significant difference in their influence. These findings highlight potential risks for applications that depend on LLM-generated summaries, as well as for agentic AI systems, where the outputs of LLMs can disproportionately shape subsequent decisions. The rapid evolution of generative AI, a foundational component of agentic systems, has accelerated the development and deployment of these systems across various domains. Agentic AI systems are designed to autonomously pursue complex objectives with minimal direct supervision, demonstrating a high degree of adaptability. A key mechanism enabling this autonomy is goal decomposition, where high-level objectives are broken down into manageable subtasks. To execute these subtasks effectively, such systems rely on multi-step reasoning and planning, often retrieving and synthesizing external information—such as through retrieval-augmented generation and tool use—to guide their actions. As a result, the reliability of agentic systems is fundamentally tied to the ability of their underlying LLMs to accurately retrieve, prioritize, and integrate information from both instructions and external sources.

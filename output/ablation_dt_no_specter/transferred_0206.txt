The model undergoes training through a procedure similar to the previously described SFT process; however, this time, reasoning examples generated during training are utilized. Moreover, in addition to the latent tokens produced by the model, a subset of sentences is randomly replaced with latent tokens, consistent with the approach employed in the SFT phase. This methodology further enhances the model's capacity for efficient reasoning. During inference, the model typically operates in discrete mode; however, upon encountering implicit-thought tags, it transitions to latent reasoning mode (see Figure 1). These tags serve to distinguish between discrete and latent reasoning modes, yet they do not constitute special tokens within the model's vocabulary. Therefore, rather than training the model to utilize these tags, we can simply prompt the model to do so. The model is responsible for determining the quantity of tokens to generate by forecasting a numerical value. Initially, the utilization of these tags is minimal; however, as the training progresses, their occurrence becomes increasingly prevalent. In this section, we briefly present the Flawed Fictions and Next-Chapter Prediction tasks used to evaluate our methodology. We describe the initial formulation of these tasks and the methodology for training a fundamental reasoning model, devoid of specialized reasoning modes, to address them. Both tasks necessitate intricate story-driven reasoning, which is semantically and stylistically distinct from conventional tasks employed in this domain.

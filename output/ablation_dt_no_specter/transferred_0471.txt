To ensure the viability of simulated data for in silico research, it is crucial that specific subgroups within the data are directly comparable. Therefore, in each study, we split the simulated datasets into "Male" and "Female" categories and performed invariance testing to assess their consistency. If this hypothesis is supported, it would confirm the feasibility of conducting in silico comparisons between specific demographics. In this section, we describe the common methodology and workflow used across all four studies, except where deviations are explicitly noted in their respective sections. Figure 1 illustrates the experimental configurations utilized for each of the four studies. The simulated data were generated using GPT4o-mini, a variant of GPT-4 with updates extending to October 2023. The model was configured using its default parameters, including a temperature setting of 1.0 and a Top P value of 1.0, with no penalties applied for frequency or presence. The LLM received a procedurally generated prompt asking it to impersonate an individual with certain demographic characteristics and to respond to the psychometric scale we intended to test. This part of the prompt was then followed by the items of the scale. The prompt also included the answer key and a request to return the answers as a single string of comma-separated values to facilitate data parsing. Each prompt was dispatched independently, devoid of any memory of preceding prompts, thereby ensuring that responses remained uninfluenced by prior interactions. Below is an example of one of the prompts used to generate the simulated data: "Impersonate a/an [Ethnicity] [Gender] of [Age] years of age from the United Kingdom. Answer the following [Number of items]-item questionnaire. Each item is on a Likert scale ranging from [Likert scale range]. Use the following key: [Response Key]. The questionnaire is as follows: [Questionnaire Items]. Provide your responses as a sequence of values separated solely by commas."

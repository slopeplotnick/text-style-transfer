This procedure adaptively determines the number of rollouts each instance requires to reach a confident majority, forming the backbone of our efficient test-time training framework. OptPO-SFT shares the same update rule as TTSFT but optimizes the iteration count through a Bayesian rule, thereby enhancing both efficiency and reliability. Both RL-based and SFT-based variants of OptPO instantiate the same three-stage framework: they initiate rollouts, compute BSPRT-based pseudo-labels, and finally perform parameter updates. The distinction between the two approaches lies in their update mechanisms: the RL-based variant employs policy-gradient optimization, whereas the SFT-based variant utilizes cross-entropy fine-tuning. Nevertheless, both variants adhere to the same overarching framework, underscoring their conceptual uniformity. For evaluation, we employ a diverse set of challenging mathematical and scientific problem datasets: MATH-500, comprising 500 high school-level problems; AIME 2024, featuring 30 pre-Olympiad-level problems; AMC, derived from the American Mathematics Competition; and GPQA, which consists of advanced graduate-level scientific questions. We test the OptPO framework on widely-used open-source models such as Qwen2.5-Math1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, enabling a comprehensive assessment of its performance across varying model scales and architectures. To evaluate the efficacy of TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, we employed Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct as our primary models. OptPO is a versatile system that can be seamlessly integrated with various existing reinforcement learning systems to enhance the efficiency of test-time training. We incorporated it into PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++ to assess its performance comprehensively. Moreover, we conducted a comparative evaluation against TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025), a widely utilized approach for test-time policy optimization. Additionally, we assessed the performance of OptPO-SFT under analogous conditions. The critic's learning rate was configured to 1e-5. In the context of SFT-based training methodologies, encompassing both TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, gradient descent is employed on the pseudo-labeled batch utilizing the AdamW optimizer, with a learning rate set at 2e-5.

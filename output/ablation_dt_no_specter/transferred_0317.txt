Empirical evidence suggests that the entrenchment of beliefs lies at the core of societal challenges such as polarization and the spread of misinformation, while also contributing to a wide array of cognitive biases. This phenomenon stems from the mechanisms of human information processing, commonly referred to as reasoning. Large language models (LLMs), which are extensively trained on diverse corpora of human-generated text at an Internet scale, are designed to assist users by answering questions and supporting various tasks. Following their success in tackling complex cognitive tasks, recent advancements in LLMs have introduced a range of reasoning techniques. Early approaches, such as the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) technique, and more recent paradigms, including inference-time scaling via reinforcement learning (henceforth referred to as reinforced reasoning), aim to enhance truth-seeking by enabling models to achieve accurate understanding through argumentation, evidence-seeking, and iterative problem-solving. However, reasoning in LLMs can deviate from truth-seeking due to belief entrenchment—a systematic tendency to favor pre-existing opinions over new evidence. When such deviations occur, they can undermine the accuracy of the models’ conclusions, mirroring well-documented cognitive biases in humans, and may mislead users by fostering unwarranted confidence. Although studies have begun to identify belief entrenchment in LLMs, rigorously demonstrating its presence remains a challenge. In individual cases, it is often difficult to differentiate between justified updates consistent with prior knowledge and biased updates that reflect an unwillingness to revise entrenched beliefs. Consequently, prior research has relied on highly controlled or synthetic tasks designed to detect belief entrenchment. However, these methods are limited in their ability to capture reasoning errors in open-ended contexts where the ground truth is ambiguous or indeterminate.

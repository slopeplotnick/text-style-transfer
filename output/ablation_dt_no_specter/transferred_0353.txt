A Software Product Line (SPL) represents a systematic approach to transforming a collection of related software systems, characterized by shared features, into a framework that enables the efficient and accelerated development of new software products. Migrating an existing code base into an SPL, however, is an inherently complex and resource-intensive process. It often necessitates significant changes to established development methodologies and organizational practices. This paper outlines the initial steps taken to initiate such a transformation. The work was conducted in collaboration with ITK - Predict and Decide, a small and medium-sized enterprise (SME) that decided to migrate its code base into an SPL. As a research team, our role was to support and guide this migration process. The existing ITK code base is organized around a framework called Cross, which already facilitates substantial software reuse. While this framework exhibits some characteristics of an SPL, it lacks the formalized models and processes typically associated with a fully developed SPL. To assist the developers in adopting a more systematic approach, we proposed a gradual restructuring of both the code base and the engineering practices. As an initial step, we analyzed the perceptions and attitudes of the company’s staff toward the transition and provided strategic recommendations to ease the process. This paper presents the findings of this preliminary investigation. Through interviews with company stakeholders, we explored their current practices, expectations for the SPL, and concerns about the migration. The results revealed that most participants were supportive of the transition and expressed a strong willingness to actively contribute to the process. Developers, in particular, displayed significant enthusiasm, viewing the migration as both a technical and managerial challenge. Conversely, individuals in business-oriented roles were more cautious, voicing concerns about potential challenges such as increased maintenance complexity and a possible reduction in creativity, especially in the design of user interfaces.

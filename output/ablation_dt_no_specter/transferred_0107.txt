The advent of large language models (LLMs) has created opportunities for a paradigm shift in event-driven prediction. Unlike traditional methods, LLMs can extract complete event structures—including elements such as event type, participants, and temporal information—directly from unstructured textual data. This capability preserves information integrity, thereby supporting causal inference and providing traceable evidence for reasoning. Nevertheless, existing research has yet to fully leverage the potential of LLMs. For instance, methodologies such as CAMEF[25] employ LLMs to extract event-specific details; however, these details are subsequently condensed into smaller data segments for processing by traditional neural network architectures. Consequently, this approach effectively reduces LLMs to advanced feature extractors. Such a method leads to the loss of event-specific details during vectorization, lacks explicit modeling of event relationships and temporal dynamics, and fails to form a traceable, reusable experiential memory system. In contrast, our methodology fundamentally diverges from this paradigm. Rather than compressing intricate event information into minimal data representations, we utilize these detailed event structures as foundational components for constructing a structured event memory. By explicitly modeling the processes of event merging, tracking, and sequencing, our framework facilitates direct reasoning regarding the evolution of events over time and the identification of analogous patterns across diverse contexts. This addresses the deficiencies inherent in current LLM-based methodologies concerning event sequence correlation analysis and cross-cycle pattern matching, constituting the core contribution of our research. We introduce a structured event reasoning framework for stock price prediction, dynamically constructing an event knowledge base from unstructured news data and iteratively refining it through model feedback. This enables the system to identify and leverage historically analogous event patterns to enhance predictive accuracy.

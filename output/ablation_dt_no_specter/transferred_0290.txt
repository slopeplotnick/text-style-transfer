Knowledge. In Case 1, the query term is "Capybara" and the product title is "water pig plush toy." Through the distillation process from the teacher model, the system has internalized sufficient world knowledge to identify "Capybara" as an alternative name for "water pig," thereby correctly determining their semantic relevance. Reasoning. In Case 2, the query is "intellectual women's clothing," yet the product information does not contain any directly matching literal keywords. Nevertheless, the model leverages its reasoning capabilities to infer that the product "commuter formal dress" aligns with the user's implicit preference for professional and intellectual attire. Specifically, attributes such as the color (gray), applicable age range (18–24 years), and design elements (suit set, formal dress) collectively conform to the typical requirements of the intellectual demographic for simple yet appropriate clothing, enabling the model to arrive at an accurate judgment. Multimodal Understanding. Case 3 exemplifies the model's ability to integrate textual and visual information for joint reasoning. For the query "1/4 Black Soft," the model identifies the "Black Soft" attribute in the product title while simultaneously detecting the phrase "Kase MAGNETIC BLACK MIST 1/4" within the product image. This coordinated reasoning between text and image is facilitated by the relevance-oriented caption generation strategy (Section 3.3), which enables the model to extract task-critical information from visual data. Rule Compliance. In Case 4, the alignment between the query and product attributes is relatively straightforward. However, the model adheres to a predefined rule stipulating that searches for standard products returning second-hand items should be classified as "weakly relevant." By applying rule-aware CoT modeling (Section 3.3), the model has acquired sufficient rule compliance capability to accurately identify and implement such business rules. Deployment Optimization. To address latency issues associated with real-time computation, a hybrid deployment strategy combining offline pre-computation with online caching was adopted. Specifically, a batch of high-frequency query-item pairs was sampled from search logs. These pairs were processed via offline inference using the LLM, with the results preserved for future utilization.

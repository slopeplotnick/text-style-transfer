STL represents a framework that capitalizes on the model's pre-existing perceptual and reasoning capabilities to iteratively enhance its performance by generating and learning from its own structured rationales. A critical consideration in this approach pertains to determining the optimal structure of these rationales. Effective visual question answering necessitates robust perceptual and reasoning faculties, which STL seeks to reinforce through a systematic rationale format. STL facilitates this process by employing a methodology wherein the model initially describes the visual content, subsequently engages in reflective reasoning, and ultimately provides a conclusive response. This iterative framework mirrors human cognitive processes, enabling the model to structure its reasoning more effectively. However, solely relying on explanations derived from correct responses proves insufficient, as it limits the model's exposure to diverse reasoning patterns and fails to address potential shortcomings in its interpretative processes. This complicates the execution of complex tasks where discerning between correct and incorrect reasoning is crucial. To address this challenge, we incorporate negative rationales, providing explanations for erroneous answers alongside correct ones. This approach parallels reflective human learning, wherein individuals enhance their understanding by evaluating both successful outcomes and failures. Negative rationales elucidate erroneous cognitive processes and facilitate the model's discernment between accurate and inaccurate responses, thereby mitigating errors such as hallucinations and conjectures devoid of substantiation. This concept is operationalized within our Self-Taught Multimodal Reasoner (STL), a framework wherein the model is trained to generate both positive and negative rationales, thereby enhancing its reasoning capabilities. Empirical evaluations across diverse domains, including common sense, science, and language, indicate that STL outperforms models that rely solely on final answers.

System prompts provide a lightweight yet effective mechanism for steering large language model (LLM) behavior during inference. To facilitate a consistent and structured comparison of prompts, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is employed to project complex reasoning data into a simplified two-dimensional representation. As shown in Figure 10 from MATH500, prompts selected through optimization exhibit a more compact distribution compared to those chosen randomly. This suggests that well-optimized prompts guide models toward more stable and consistent reasoning patterns, thereby improving predictability in problem-solving. Similar trends are observed across other assessments, as illustrated in Appendix Figures 15 and 16. Building on these findings, we propose a unified framework for optimizing system prompts to enhance the performance of multilingual LLMs. Our experiments reveal that specific prompt components significantly improve performance across multiple languages, and multilingual reward systems can effectively identify these optimal prompts. Further analysis indicates that high-performing prompts induce more structured and consistent reasoning behaviors, underscoring prompt optimization as a scalable approach to enhancing the robustness of multilingual LLMs. Despite these advancements, important challenges remain. For instance, identifying prompts that consistently achieve high performance across all evaluation metrics remains difficult, as evidenced by experimental results from SPRIG. While well-designed prompts can steer LLMs toward specific reasoning patterns and improve performance, the underlying mechanisms driving these improvements are not yet fully understood, presenting an open avenue for future research. Additionally, ethical concerns persist. The computational demands of prompt optimization contribute to significant energy consumption and carbon emissions, while the process may inadvertently amplify biases present in the training corpus, such as those linked to specific personas or roles. Given the inclusion of elements like roles and instructional directives in the dataset, it is crucial to ensure that prompt optimization does not introduce or exacerbate harmful biases. This highlights the need for continued research to balance performance improvements with ethical considerations in multilingual LLM development.

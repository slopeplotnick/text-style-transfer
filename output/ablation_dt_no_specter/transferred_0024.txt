Following our two-step process to integrate various types of CoT data for training the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, which is comparable in size to the LLM base model utilized in our study, we evaluated the performance of models based on VLM and LLM. It became evident that the LLM-based approach exhibited superior performance across both general and challenging datasets, particularly in terms of macro-F1 metrics. These findings underscore the superior reasoning and knowledge capabilities of LLMs compared to VLMs when equipped with equivalent CoT capabilities. However, VLMs demonstrate enhanced performance in processing visual details, attributable to their ability to bypass the image-to-text conversion process, thereby mitigating potential information loss. Over three iterative phases, the development of our LORE model has yielded substantial advancements in five critical dimensions: objective formulation, model configuration, data and prompt design, evaluation methodologies, and practical applications. These phases illustrate the progressive enhancement of the model through advanced architectural designs, high-quality data curation, and practical implementation strategies, particularly aimed at optimizing search result relevance within e-commerce platforms. Initially, significant challenges were encountered in query comprehension, notably in identifying non-standardized product nomenclature. To address these limitations, extensive, high-fidelity e-commerce datasets were constructed, coupled with the application of In-Context Learning methodologies to integrate domain-specific knowledge. Consequently, this approach substantially improved the model's capacity to interpret and process e-commerce-specific linguistic patterns.

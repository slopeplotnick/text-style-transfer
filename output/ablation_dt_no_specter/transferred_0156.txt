The practice of scientific inquiry inherently involves reflection on its methodologies, prompting critical examination of what constitutes an effective and robust scientific method. Scientific paradigms have evolved over time, transitioning from mere observation to interrogating the relationship between models and empirical data. Furthermore, it examines the collaborative dynamics within scientific communities, as diverse social interactions establish standards and norms, which may subsequently be revealed as erroneous. Practitioners within the field may overlook epistemic errors due to the community's acceptance of these standards as conventional. Scientists may exhibit excessive confidence in their knowledge (Bond et al., 2007). Notably, Thomas Kuhn examined these collectives, referring to them as "paradigms." He posits that paradigms comprise specific theoretical assumptions, laws, methodologies, and applications embraced by members of a scientific community, exemplified by concepts such as evolution and plate tectonics.

The experimental results presented in Table 1 validate the efficacy of integrating model-centric and data-centric paradigms. A comprehensive comparison of the "Unify" block with individual baseline methods reveals that hybrid strategies consistently establish a new performance upper bound across diverse datasets and model architectures. This consistent superiority underscores that internal uncertainty signals and external evidence retrieval operate in orthogonal feature spaces, thereby complementing each other. Specifically, the Score-Level Fusion approach demonstrates that a simple linear aggregation of signals can effectively mitigate the high variance inherent in single-paradigm methods. By integrating diverse sources of information, this approach reduces individual prediction errors, resulting in more stable and reliable outcomes that outperform standalone HD or FV methods in most cases. Moreover, the Evidence-Aware Pipeline exhibits a distinct advantage, frequently achieving the highest performance among all evaluated methodologies. This improvement is primarily due to the pipeline's ability to address the limitations of excessive reliance on external evidence. For instance, in scenarios where external information is insufficient, such as NEI cases, enforcing a definitive verdict often introduces inaccuracies. The pipeline circumvents this issue by dynamically transitioning to internal uncertainty signals, which serve as a dependable proxy for truthfulness in the absence of corroborative evidence. Furthermore, hybrid methodologies effectively address the performance instability observed in RQ1. Unlike standalone HD methods, which exhibit significant variability across architectures such as LLaMA and Qwen, hybrid approaches demonstrate consistent performance across diverse backbones. This consistency highlights the effectiveness of integrating multiple signals to mitigate the inherent miscalibration of internal confidence observed in standalone HD methods. Consequently, the proposed frameworks not only enhance predictive accuracy but also maintain robustness regardless of the underlying model architecture.

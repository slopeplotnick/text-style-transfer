Exemplar Selection: Certain prompt optimization algorithms, such as OPRO and EvoPrompt, do not incorporate few-shot examples in their processes. However, they can significantly enhance the performance of large language models (LLMs), even when employing relatively simple exemplar selection strategies. The promptsolution framework introduces a post-hoc exemplar selection module, which utilizes random selection and random search techniques to integrate few-shot examples into a fixed instruction. Initial Prompt Creation: Many optimization methods require an initial set of prompts to serve as a starting point for their operations. Our framework incorporates functionalities to autonomously generate prompts based on a task description, a foundational prompt (as outlined by Zhou et al., 2023), or representative examples extracted from a dataset. **Callbacks:** The optimizer class is equipped with callback mechanisms, enabling systematic monitoring of the optimization process. These callbacks facilitate real-time evaluation of the optimizer's state at each iteration and provide the capability to terminate the process when predefined conditions are met. Notable implementations include mechanisms that monitor token utilization, ceasing operations upon exceeding a predetermined threshold, as well as systems that archive prompts and their corresponding evaluations for subsequent analysis. Setup: To evaluate the efficacy of promptolution in relation to alternative methodologies, prompt optimization was conducted on the GSM8K dataset, which comprises mathematical problems, and the SST-5 dataset, pertaining to sentiment classification. The primary language model employed was gemma-3-27B, which also occasionally served as the meta-model for optimizers necessitating such a component. Further details regarding the datasets and experimental setup can be found in Appendix A.2. For comparative evaluation, we employed the optimizers CAPO, EvoPromptGA, and OPRO from the promptolution framework, alongside the leading optimization methods from AdalFlow (LLM-AutoDiff) and DSPy (GEPA). To evaluate the efficacy of prompt optimization, we additionally assessed three manually constructed prompts for each dataset and reported their average performance. To ensure equitable evaluation, the default configurations for each optimizer were employed, and the token budget was constrained to a maximum of one million tokens for both input and output combined, corresponding to a cost below $0.15 for this language model. All frameworks were initialized solely with a task description, without the provision of initial prompts, to evaluate their capacity for fully automated workflow execution. Performance was assessed on a distinct test set using both unoptimized prompts and the best-performing prompts identified for each optimizer.

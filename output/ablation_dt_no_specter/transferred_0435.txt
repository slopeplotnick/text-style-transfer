Explanations within the realm of XAI must adhere to specific standards. Primarily, they ought to be comprehensible to the recipient, and secondly, they must accurately reflect the operations of the model. The comprehensibility of an explanation is contingent upon the characteristics of the recipient; however, it generally necessitates that the explanation be articulated with clarity and precision. Fidelity to the model necessitates that the explanation accurately represents the model's operational mechanisms, devoid of any bias. Bias may arise if the explanation is deliberately constrained. Although understandability and fidelity are distinct attributes, optimizing each independently presents considerable challenges. Explanations that align with individuals' preconceived expectations are often perceived as more comprehensible; however, such alignment may introduce bias by influencing the selection of explanations. Furthermore, the misinterpretation of an accurate explanation has the potential to mislead the individual providing it. This study proposes a post-hoc explanatory framework designed to enhance both the interpretability and fidelity of model explanations. Explanations should employ clear and accessible language while explicitly delineating causal relationships, as this aligns with the inherent structure of explanations commonly utilized in human communication [Miller, 2019]. This approach facilitates the alignment of the intended interpretation with the audience's understanding, thereby obviating the necessity for supplementary guidance on how to interpret the explanation and enabling a more concise and efficient representation. The use of accessible and semantically meaningful language is essential, given the potential divergence between the features utilized by the model as input and those most suitable for explanatory purposes. This principle underpins a subset of XAI methodologies collectively referred to as Concept-based XAI (C-XAI). These approaches operate under the premise that, although models are proficient in processing data represented as pixels, graphs, or tokens, human communication predominantly relies on high-level, semantically interpretable concepts.

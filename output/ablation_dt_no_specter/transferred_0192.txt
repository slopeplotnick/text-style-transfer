During the data preprocessing stage, all cases and examination questions were first deduplicated to ensure sample independence, followed by systematic data cleaning. Samples lacking question stems or options, as well as those containing non-textual content such as figures and tables, were excluded. Additionally, character errors resulting from OCR recognition were manually proofread to ensure textual accuracy and consistency. For clinical case and medical record data, anonymization was conducted in strict adherence to medical ethics and privacy protection principles. This process involved deleting or replacing patient names and other identifiable information to ensure compliance with privacy standards. The clinical cases utilized in this study were approved by the Ethics Committee of Xiyuan Hospital of CACMS (approval number: 2025XLA135-2). SDT, encompassing "syndrome differentiation" and "treatment formulation," represents the core principle of TCM diagnosis and therapy. To evaluate the model's performance in this process, a comprehensive 14-dimension evaluation framework was proposed, including syndrome differentiation, causative factors, and pathogenesis, as detailed in Table 1. All standard answers and options were initially annotated by TCM experts, then mutually cross-validated, and finally subjected to an independent third-party review to ensure consistency and reliability. Notably, the selected-response questions were designed with sophisticated distractors to enhance the discriminative power of the assessment. Beyond evaluating the final reasoning outcomes of SDT, this study introduces two process-oriented metrics: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Content Completeness, which measures the coverage of essential patient information within the model's reasoning process, and CoT Accuracy, which assesses the alignment of reasoning elements with the original clinical case to identify potential hallucinations or deviations. Consequently, the evaluation tasks aim to uncover differences in the model's foundational cognition and normative responses, thereby enabling a more comprehensive and interpretable overall assessment. All questions are structured as selected-response types, encompassing both single-selection and multiple-selection formats. The TCM Basic Knowledge component addresses four core dimensions: (i) comprehension of TCM classics; (ii) grasp of foundational theories; (iii) knowledge of Chinese materia medica and formulas; and (iv) syndrome differentiation ability based on tongue, pulse, facial complexion, and acupoints. Questions for this component were sourced from preprocessed authoritative examination materials. The Medical Ethics component evaluates the model's understanding of clinical ethics, covering conflicts between traditional and modern medicine, identification of unscientific practices, respect for patient beliefs, and principles of informed consent. This component combines authoritative examination questions with expert-designed scenarios to ensure comprehensive coverage. Lastly, the Content Safety component assesses the TCM domain language model's adherence to professional boundaries and safety protocols. The model is explicitly restricted to addressing TCM-related inquiries and is programmed to refrain from engaging with non-medical questions involving privacy, safety, or ethical values. Relevant questions were meticulously designed and reviewed by experts in content safety and ethics to ensure the evaluation aligns with established standards for TCM domain models.

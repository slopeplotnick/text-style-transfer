To refine these findings, we repeated the analyses using the ATTARI subscales. Notably, only the behavioral subscale (BEH) emerged as a significant predictor, whereas the affective and cognitive subscales did not contribute when analyzed simultaneously (see Supplementary Materials S3). This suggests that individuals’ attitudes toward AI are primarily driven by their practical willingness to engage with AI, rather than by their general beliefs or emotional responses. To complement these quantitative results and explore participants’ subjective evaluation criteria, we conducted a thematic analysis of 178 open-ended responses. A summary of the main themes is presented in Table 2, with detailed coding available in the Supplementary Materials. Participants frequently discussed AI-generated music in terms of its technical and musical quality, its emotional resonance, and its perceived humanness. Among these, humanness emerged as the most prominent theme, extending beyond auditory characteristics to include the perceived absence of a human touch. Several participants highlighted the importance of the artist’s personality and the narrative behind a composition, describing AI-generated music as lacking depth and authenticity. However, some participants expressed indifference to the origin of the music, stating, for example, "If it sounds good then I don't mind if it was artificially generated" (ID926). These responses further underscore the significant role of attitudes toward AI in shaping evaluations of AI-generated music. Moreover, many comments reflected normative perspectives on AI itself, rather than the specific qualities of the music. For instance, one participant remarked, "I don’t like anything to do with AI-generated music" (ID914). Beyond these normative views, the responses also revealed additional dimensions of evaluation that are less commonly addressed in experimental research. Specifically, some participants assessed AI-generated music through ethical lenses, raising concerns about its potential to displace musicians, harm artists’ financial stability, and exploit training data obtained without proper consent. As one respondent noted, AI-generated music "is built upon data without the consent of the artists that have dedicated decades of their lives to the craft" (ID615). Another participant remarked, "I think it undermines and abuses artists" (ID446), illustrating that evaluations of AI-generated music extend beyond aesthetic considerations to encompass broader issues of fairness and ethics in its creation and distribution.

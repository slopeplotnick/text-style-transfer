Various systems have been developed for managing memory in financial tasks. FinMem[22] is a financial-domain memory system that implements a layered memory structure encompassing short, mid, and long-term memory to guide trading decisions. Similarly, MemP[5] is a general framework that abstracts trajectories into both step-by-step instructions and high-level strategies, thereby maintaining and utilizing procedural memory. In contrast, MemGPT functions analogously to an operating system, orchestrating hierarchical memory storage and interruption handling to dynamically expand the context window for large language models. Meanwhile, Zep employs temporal knowledge graphs to integrate historical relational data with evolving contextual information, supporting the joint retrieval of past and dynamic contexts. 

To evaluate system performance, we adopted Accuracy (ACC) and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) as metrics. ACC measures the overall correctness of predictions, while MCC is particularly effective for balanced datasets, offering a robust assessment of performance in stock prediction tasks where class distribution may be uneven. Our evaluations, conducted on four representative stocks, demonstrated that our framework consistently and significantly outperformed all baseline models in both ACC and MCC metrics, as shown in Table 1. These results validate the overall effectiveness of our approach and highlight fundamental mismatches between existing systems and the requirements of stock prediction. 

Specifically, FinMem's isolated news storage lacks temporal event modeling, causing its retrieval mechanism to miss critical incremental information by failing to align with evolving market contexts. Similarly, MemP's procedural abstraction excessively simplifies trajectories, discarding essential semantic event details and impairing its ability to reason through complex financial scenarios. Moreover, MemGPT's event-agnostic design overlooks financially significant patterns during memory updates, while Zep's graph-based approach fails to capture inter-event dependencies, leading to the loss of pivotal correlations in financial datasets. In contrast, our proposed framework addresses these limitations by transforming unstructured news data into structured temporal event representations. This approach systematically captures event logic and dependencies, enabling the identification of interconnections among events. Consequently, it establishes a traceable, reasoning-capable system that mirrors human analytical processes, thereby facilitating more accurate and trustworthy financial predictions.

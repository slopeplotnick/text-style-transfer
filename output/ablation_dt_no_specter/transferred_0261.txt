To address these challenges, a paradigm shift is essential, transitioning from an emphasis on static outputs to the evaluation of dynamic processes. A unified framework must rely not on fixed artifacts but on a foundational set of questions that can prompt real-time generation from any target LLM. This dynamic approach ensures that (1) fact-checking mechanisms receive the necessary textual input, (2) hallucination detection systems gain access to the internal processes of the model during generation, and (3) the evaluation remains adaptable and relevant as models continue to evolve. These insights form the basis of UniFact. To overcome the deficiencies of outdated benchmarks, the framework is built on three core principles that guarantee robust, equitable, and future-proof evaluations. First, it achieves model-agnosticism through on-the-fly generation. By abandoning reliance on static datasets, the framework employs factual inquiries to dynamically elicit responses from any target LLM, ensuring its relevance for both current and future models. Second, it enables equitable and principled comparisons by accommodating both hallucination detection and fact verification methodologies. Specifically, it provides hallucination detection mechanisms with visibility into the model's generative process, while supplying fact verification systems with the generated text and supplementary information for validation. Despite their differing approaches, both are assessed on the shared objective of determining the factual accuracy of the model's output. Finally, scalability is achieved through automated labeling. By replacing the slow and subjective process of manual annotation, the framework leverages an automated system to verify the accuracy of generated responses by cross-referencing them with ground-truth answers and reliable sources. This ensures efficient, objective, and reproducible evaluations across a wide range of models.

AlphaZero was among the earliest systems to showcase the effectiveness of self-play-based learning, where a model autonomously enhances its performance by engaging in iterative gameplay without external supervision. This foundational concept has since been adapted to language models, leading to the development of systems that iteratively refine their reasoning capabilities over time. Several systems implement self-play methodologies at scale, enabling the iterative improvement of less capable language models through the autonomous generation and resolution of tasks, thus reducing reliance on extensive labeled datasets. This approach is particularly advantageous in domains such as code generation, where one program evaluates another through the application of test cases. Methods such as R-Zero operate independently of external datasets, generating questions autonomously, attempting to resolve them, and refining their performance through iterative self-assessment. Conversely, approaches incorporating few-shot demonstrations leverage initial human-annotated examples to construct progressively more challenging tasks, thereby facilitating a curriculum-driven enhancement of problem complexity. R-Few adopts a similar approach but emphasizes an alternative mechanism for directing the learning process. This method endeavors to preserve the advantages of exploration while avoiding deviation or diminishing variety. Reinforcement learning has emerged as a pivotal strategy for enhancing the cognitive capabilities of language models, extending beyond mere example-based learning. The incorporation of human feedback has been a crucial factor in enhancing model performance. Early advancements, such as InstructGPT, demonstrated that aligning models with human preferences significantly improves their utility, safety, and reasoning capabilities, surpassing the outcomes achieved through conventional example-based learning. Building upon this foundation, more recent systems, such as DeepSeek-R1, have refined this approach by employing reward mechanisms to incentivize and enhance effective problem-solving and reasoning processes. Comparable systems demonstrate that establishing objectives for extended and intricate tasks can significantly enhance reasoning capabilities.

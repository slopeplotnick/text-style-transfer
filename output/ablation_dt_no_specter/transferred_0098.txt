Language models offer significant advancements in addressing the limitations of traditional methods for mental health monitoring. They outperform conventional supervised learning algorithms by capturing nuanced contextual information and acquiring knowledge, thereby enhancing their applicability for large-scale and real-world mental health assessments. However, the deployment of large language models (LLMs) is not without challenges: they are computationally intensive to train and operate, and they pose potential privacy risks due to the processing of sensitive data on external servers. Moreover, the substantial size of traditional language models renders them impractical for deployment in resource-constrained environments, necessitating the exploration of more efficient alternatives. In contrast, Small Language Models (SLMs) demonstrate comparable or superior efficacy for digital health applications due to their streamlined architectures, reduced inference costs, and capability for direct on-device operation. This facilitates the prediction of mental health conditions from social media activity without the need for data storage, thereby enabling rapid, privacy-preserving, and ethically sound monitoring. Despite their promising attributes, the potential of SLMs for mental health prediction remains underexplored. Therefore, this study investigates the efficacy of SLMs in predicting mental health outcomes, demonstrating their viability as a resource-efficient solution for assessing multiple mental health dimensions in on-device settings. Specifically, we evaluate these models on tasks related to stress, depression, and suicide risk, highlighting their applicability for scalable and privacy-preserving mental health monitoring. In this study, we present a compact model named Menta, which has been fine-tuned through a specialized methodology and trained across diverse datasets to enhance predictive accuracy. Menta demonstrates robust performance in various mental health tasks, particularly in the detection of stress and depression. Its practical utility is further exemplified by its deployment on an iPhone 15 Pro Max, where it operates in real-time with minimal memory consumption, thereby offering private and scalable mental health support directly on the device.

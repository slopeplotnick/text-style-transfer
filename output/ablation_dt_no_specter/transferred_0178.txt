Pipeline Overview. ReVSeg formulates reasoning-centric video object segmentation as an explicit sequence of reasoning steps. The complex task is decomposed into three primary components: video understanding, temporal grounding, and spatial grounding, executed through iterative interactions with a singular system. Initially, the system processes an unstructured video input alongside an ambiguous query, such as “What is the most suitable vehicle for a family trip?” This step leverages commonsense reasoning and logical inference to distill the complex task into a specific visual target, such as identifying “the silver minivan,” accompanied by a descriptive context. Consequently, the system localizes the identified object within the visual frame, employing spatial grounding to delineate its precise position by generating a bounding box. Subsequently, an additional module tracks the identified object throughout the video, generating a comprehensive output that delineates its position at all times. As the entire process is executed within an integrated framework, contextual information from preceding stages is seamlessly propagated to subsequent steps, ensuring consistency across the workflow. To enhance system performance, the training methodology incorporates a reward mechanism that evaluates not only the final output but also intermediate processes, thereby facilitating incremental improvements in operational efficacy. However, systems of this nature encounter significant challenges in directly identifying and tracking objects within video content due to the insufficient availability of high-quality training datasets. Even with supplementary techniques such as providing the system with contextual cues, conducting multiple iterations to ensure consistency, or employing sophisticated search algorithms, these systems continue to struggle with accurately determining object locations in video sequences solely from the raw footage.

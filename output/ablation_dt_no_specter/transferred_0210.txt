The methodology section comprises the presentation of participants (Sect. 3.1), the design of the interviews (Sect. 3.2), our approach to conducting the interviews (Sect. 3.3), and the method of analyzing the interview responses (Sect. 3.4). Participants were selected to ensure a representative sample, encompassing various roles within the project, differing levels of experience, and diverse educational backgrounds. Specifically, the project involves four distinct roles: Product Owners (PO), Developers (IT), Agronomists (AGRO), and User Interface/Experience (UX) designers. Participants possess varying educational qualifications, ranging from engineering degrees to doctoral degrees. Furthermore, individuals were chosen based on diverse levels of experience within the project. In total, 16 participants were selected for the study, including nine Developers who are either currently engaged with or have previously contributed to the project, two Agronomists, three Product Owners, and two User Interface/Experience designers, all actively involved in the project. Ensuring participant motivation was deemed essential for obtaining meaningful and insightful responses. In this study, participants were informed that they would receive feedback, and their responses would contribute to the formulation of an effective strategy for the SPL process. We employed structured interviews, utilizing a predetermined set of questions. Our approach adhered to established guidelines, employing clear and unambiguous language. Each question pertained to a singular concept. We refrained from incorporating sensitive inquiries or probing into historical events. Additionally, we avoided questions that the respondent might lack sufficient knowledge to address. This consideration led to interviews centered around the perspectives of the actors involved. The interview framework comprises three distinct sections. The initial section facilitates the introduction of the participant. Subsequently, the central section follows, divided into two subsections: one addressing the existing process and the other examining proposed modifications. Finally, a conclusion section is presented, encompassing a comprehensive discussion of the interview in its entirety.

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have predominantly focused on enhancing test-time computational resources. As evidenced by models like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-R1, these systems generate extended "think" segments that encapsulate intermediate hypotheses, derivations, and self-corrections before delivering a conclusive response. This paradigm of augmenting inference-time reasoning has emerged as a significant trend, as it frequently results in enhanced accuracy and problem-solving capabilities. However, simply extending the reasoning process does not invariably enhance performance and may, in certain cases, prove detrimental, such as when models exhibit tendencies toward overthinking. Empirical evidence indicates that correct solutions frequently emerge from more concise reasoning steps. Therefore, an alternative approach involves executing multiple reasoning processes in parallel and synthesizing their outputs, most effectively through majority voting in tasks with well-defined solutions. This approach is effective for tasks such as mathematical problem-solving or multiple-choice assessments. Nonetheless, many real-world applications lack a singular correct solution. For instance, programming utilities must generate functional code, while research instruments frequently necessitate the integration of auxiliary tools, the formulation of comprehensive strategies, or the provision of extensive elucidations. In scenarios where a singular definitive answer is elusive, traditional methods that select the most frequent response prove inadequate, despite their efficacy in simpler tasks. This raises a pivotal inquiry: Is it feasible to harness the advantages of concurrent reasoning processes for tasks lacking a clear-cut solution, without resorting to the selection of the most prevalent outcome? In this study, we present THINKMERGE, an inference-time framework that synthesizes the outputs of multiple reasoning pathways to formulate a singular, high-quality response. Unlike prior methodologies, which predominantly rely on unimodal inputs or fail to integrate multimodal data effectively, our approach leverages the synergistic potential of visual and textual modalities to enhance retrieval and generation tasks. This integration is particularly critical for addressing challenges in attribute recognition, spatial reasoning, and nuanced scene comprehension—domains where textual representations alone are insufficient to encapsulate the complexity of real-world scenarios. By systematically aligning visual and textual information, our framework ensures a more robust and semantically coherent understanding, thereby advancing the interpretability and reliability of generative outputs.

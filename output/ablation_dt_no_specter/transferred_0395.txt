To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that systematically leverages an open-vocabulary object detector to enhance MLLMs' understanding of high-resolution images. Experimental results confirm that the detector achieves precise target localization while effectively suppressing interference from irrelevant regions. Consequently, we propose MRD, a training-free and generic framework that innovatively addresses semantic similarity through a multi-resolution fusion strategy. By integrating open-set detection results to refine target regions, MRD achieves a synergistic enhancement in image understanding. Comprehensive experiments validate the effectiveness and generalization of our method, demonstrating superior performance on both single-object and multi-object tasks across diverse MLLMs and high-resolution benchmarks. To improve high-resolution image comprehension without proportionally increasing computational complexity from visual tokens, several studies have incorporated high-resolution visual encoders into MLLMs. For instance, Vary [23] and Deepseek-VL [15] utilize the SAM encoder [10] to enhance model performance on high-resolution images. Meanwhile, another line of research has introduced Native/Dynamic-Resolution MLLMs, which process images at their native resolution. The core idea is to generate a variable-length sequence of visual tokens that aligns with the original dimensions of the input image, thereby preserving spatial fidelity and high-frequency details. These models employ diverse mechanisms to address challenges arising from long sequences and computational demands, such as sliding window attention, dynamic masking, and patch-based encoding strategies. Notable progress has been made by representative works in this domain. For example, the InternVL series [4, 6, 31] adopts a patch-based approach by segmenting high-resolution images into multiple fixed-size patches. In contrast, models like Qwen2.5-VL [2, 3] employ an end-to-end methodology, training a ViT directly on native-resolution images. This approach enables the model to embed the entire image into a single, coherent token sequence in one forward pass, potentially enhancing its understanding of global context.

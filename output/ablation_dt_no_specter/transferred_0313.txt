Based on the resulting clusters, detailed in Figure 2, the languages were categorized as In-Domain languages. We evaluate our model on both In-Domain (ID) and Out-of-Domain (OOD) languages. Eleven low-resource languages were selected for the Out-of-Domain set. These languages were chosen to serve as strong representatives for their respective clusters, ensuring coverage across diverse language groups. For evaluation, we benchmarked our model on six safety classification datasets: Aegis-Content-Safety-2.0-Test (Aegis-CS2) introduced in 2025, HarmBench proposed by Mazeika et al. in 2024, Redteam2k developed by Luo et al. in 2024, JBB-Behaviors—comprising the Behaviors subset (JBB-Behav) and the Judge-comparison subset (JBB-Judge)—curated by Chao et al. in 2024, and StrongReject presented by Souly et al. in 2024. These benchmarks collectively span various harm categories, including but not limited to Hate/Identity Hate, Sexual Content, Suicide/Self-Harm, Violence, Guns/Illegal Weapons, Personally Identifiable Information (PII)/Privacy, Sexual Exploitation of Minors, Toxicity, and Abuse. Therefore, they are suitable for a comprehensive evaluation of safety guardrails. For our multilingual safety guardrail, we adopted the XLM-RoBERTa-Base model, comprising 279 million parameters, and the XLM-RoBERTa-Large model, with 560 million parameters, as the base multilingual encoders. These models offer rich cross-lingual representations and are pre-trained on 100 languages, making them suitable for our generalization goals. We appended a single-layer classification head atop the encoder, matching the hidden size of the encoder outputs, with a binary output layer. We performed full-weight training of both the pre-trained encoder and the classification head using the aggregated training dataset. All training and evaluations were conducted on an NVIDIA H100 GPU cluster using Bfloat16 precision for efficiency and performance. For baseline comparisons, we evaluated our model against both small guardrails such as DuoGuard-0.5B (2025), PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen-Smol, and WalledGuard-C (2024), and large guardrails such as PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen (2025), LlamaGuard3 (2024), and Aegis-Defensive (2025). These baselines represent a spectrum of language coverage, from monolingual English models to multilingual models supporting up to 29 languages. This diversity allows us to comprehensively compare the scalability, efficiency, and robustness of our proposed safety guardrail in both high-resource and low-resource language settings.

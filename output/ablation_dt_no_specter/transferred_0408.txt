The system we developed demonstrates robust performance across various tests and configurations, indicating its reliability and consistency. This reliability is crucial for interpretability; if the system produced disordered or arbitrary results, it would fail to elucidate the interconnections. Instead, we observe distinct groupings, stable patterns, and coherent characteristics that are logically consistent. These findings substantiate that our methodology effectively identifies training images that are congruent with the stylistic and semantic attributes of the generated outputs, beyond mere visual or textual resemblance. In summary, the statistical evidence indicates that the automatic construction of knowledge graphs (KGs) facilitates a transparent and comprehensible framework for evaluating the influence of training data on model outputs. This advancement supports objectives such as enhancing model explainability, conducting copyright audits, and promoting dataset transparency. For instance, when a model is prompted to generate an image in a particular artistic style, such as "Ghibli-style," it does not replicate specific images from its training dataset. Rather, the model assimilates the overarching visual characteristics of the style from a diverse collection of images. These characteristics are encapsulated within the model's parameters and are utilized to synthesize novel images, thereby avoiding direct duplication. Consequently, it is challenging to precisely determine which training images have influenced the output. Influence manifests at a more abstract level, encompassing shared motifs, textures, themes, and compositional structures within the image. Therefore, to ascertain how a particular style influences the model, it is imperative to examine patterns and meanings rather than merely matching specific objects. In this experiment, we employed an automated methodology for constructing knowledge graphs (KGs) based on prior work. Specifically, we utilized a collection of frames extracted from renowned Studio Ghibli films. Each frame was systematically annotated using a domain-specific ontology, which decomposes the visual content into structured semantic representations. The constructed "Ghibli World" knowledge graph encapsulates the quintessential elements of the studio's visual style. Although the dataset is derived from frames of merely ten films, it provides a comprehensive representation of the stylistic nuances characteristic of Studio Ghibli's oeuvre.

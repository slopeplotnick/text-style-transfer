Our proposed method enhances action precision, as corroborated by the results of simulation experiments. More importantly, it effectively addresses policy failures arising from suboptimal teleoperation data. During the Pen Grasping stage, the operator's variations in grasping strategies and timing introduce significant state disparities between grasping modes, resulting in a sparse observation-action distribution within the expert dataset. Consequently, the base policy frequently exhibits instability, manifesting as improper closure of the gripper, reopening post-grasp, or oscillation between dual grasping modes. However, our test-time scaling method largely rectifies these issues: the gripper consistently closes accurately, and the robot circumvents suboptimal modes. We attribute this improvement to the filtering effect of our scaling framework, which suppresses suboptimal trajectories induced by noise in the teleoperated dataset. Attempting to directly map features to action errors exacerbates the learning challenge, as the model must discern not only optimal features but also suboptimal and poor ones. During test-time scaling, the critical factor is the selection of the optimal action. Our experimental findings indicate that the observed decline in performance is attributable to overfitting on the training set, which consequently impairs generalization. When distinct encoders are employed in lieu of Internal Features and are trained in conjunction with CFN, the resulting features exhibit substantial similarity, thereby complicating precise pseudo-count estimation and leading to a marked decline in performance. This methodology further diminishes efficiency, as it fails to capitalize on the VLA's intrinsic comprehension of scenes and actions. In this study, we present TACO, a test-time-scaling (TTS) framework that incorporates a streamlined pseudo-count estimator as a high-fidelity verifier for action chunks. TACO is compatible with diverse VLA models and consistently yields significant enhancements to the base policy across a broad spectrum of real-world and simulated evaluations. Despite certain limitations—such as its inability to assess newly synthesized actions generated solely through perturbation and its dependence on the representational capacity of the underlying VLA—our findings demonstrate that lightweight test-time scaling effectively facilitates anti-exploration and mitigates the instability and performance degradation induced by distribution shift in action prediction.

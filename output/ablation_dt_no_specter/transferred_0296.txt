The French-Ciluba sentiment lexicon is being extended to include translations in English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, Sesotho, and Sepedi through the use of cross-lingual translation techniques and corpus-based mapping methodologies. To further enhance the lexicon’s comprehensiveness, Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) will be utilized to extract sentiment-bearing terms from African language corpora and align them with existing entries. Moreover, to address potential ambiguities in word meanings and ensure accurate translations, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework, in conjunction with large language models, will be employed to refine multilingual word alignments. Subsequently, the enriched sentiment lexicon will be used to fine-tune two state-of-the-art African language models, AfriBERTa and AfroXLMR, for emotion and opinion classification tasks. The performance of these models will be systematically evaluated using standard metrics such as precision, recall, F1-score, and the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve. Furthermore, to enhance the interpretability of the models, explainable AI techniques, including attention visualization and feature attribution, will be applied to identify the specific text segments prioritized during decision-making. Finally, the performance of individual models will be compared with ensemble approaches to determine whether the latter offers improvements in multilingual sentiment classification accuracy. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II provides a comprehensive review of existing literature on sentiment analysis, multilingual lexicon development, and their application to African languages. Section III outlines the proposed framework and methodologies, while Section IV details the experimental setup and model evaluation. Section V presents the results and discusses their implications for multilingual natural language processing, and Section VI concludes the paper. Historically, sentiment analysis has relied on supervised machine learning algorithms and lexicon-based methods to determine the polarity of textual data. Early studies, such as those by Pang and Lee [1], demonstrated the effectiveness of statistical approaches in opinion mining, while subsequent research emphasized the utility of sentiment lexicons for interpretable, rule-based analysis [2]. Prominent sentiment lexicons, including SentiWordNet [3], NRC Emotion Lexicon [4], and VADER [5], have proven effective for high-resource languages. However, they exhibit significant limitations when applied to low-resource or morphologically complex languages. To address this gap, researchers have increasingly focused on extracting lexical items from textual corpora to construct multilingual sentiment lexicons.

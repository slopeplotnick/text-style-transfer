We reassess the robustness of pretrained VLA models and demonstrate that their performance degradation under visual perturbations primarily stems from representation shifts within spatial modeling. To address this challenge, we introduce a unified one-shot robustness adaptation framework, comprising Feature Token Modulation (FTM) and Feature Linear Adaptation (FLA), which effectively recalibrates visual representations with minimal parameter updates. Our proposed approach achieves state-of-the-art generalization to unseen camera viewpoints and visual perturbations on the Libero benchmark, thereby uncovering the latent robustness potential within existing VLA models. Vision-language-action (VLA) models aim to integrate visual perception, language grounding, and action generation into a cohesive policy framework. By building on pretrained vision-language foundations within the robotics domain, recent VLA systems, such as RT-2, Pi0, PaLM-E, Octo, and OpenVLA, have demonstrated strong in-distribution generalization across a wide array of manipulation tasks. These models leverage large-scale embodiment datasets, which incorporate diverse visual inputs, linguistic instructions, proprioceptive signals, and noisy action sequences, to develop a shared action policy that enables robots to perform versatile and adaptive behaviors. Nevertheless, despite these advancements, recent analyses reveal that state-of-the-art VLAs remain surprisingly fragile under distributional shifts. Specifically, their performance degrades significantly when exposed to unseen camera viewpoints, novel lighting conditions, or scene noise perturbations—a limitation that persists even after extensive pretraining and fine-tuning on large-scale datasets. Addressing this vulnerability is crucial for the effective deployment of VLA systems in real-world environments, where visual complexities far exceed those encountered in controlled benchmarks like Libero. Improving robustness to visual perturbations remains a persistent challenge in advancing VLA performance. Prior research has explored data-centric strategies, such as domain randomization, multi-view demonstrations, and large-scale visual perturbation datasets, to enhance viewpoint invariance by increasing visual diversity. However, while these methods have shown promise, they often require substantial data collection or annotation efforts, which significantly hinder their scalability.

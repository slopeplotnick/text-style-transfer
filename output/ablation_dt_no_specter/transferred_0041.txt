These findings highlight the role of listeners' pre-existing attitudes and expectations. For instance, Hong et al. (2021) demonstrated that a more favorable disposition toward creative AI correlated with higher music ratings, emphasizing the impact of expectancies regarding AI music across various genres. However, deviations from anticipated outcomes adversely affected ratings, although substantial differences between music styles were not observed. A significant inquiry pertains to whether individuals perceive AI-generated music as resembling compositions created by human musicians. In this context, it is crucial to elucidate the specific musical elements that influence perceptions of humanness versus AI origin. Shank et al. (2023) identified musical style as a contributing factor, with electronic music more frequently attributed to AI composers. Interestingly, when misled about the true origin of musical compositions, participants reported perceived differences that did not exist, underscoring the influence of expectations and beliefs about the humanness of the creator on aesthetic judgment, as elucidated by Ansani et al. (2024). Moreover, Déguernel et al. (2022) identified specific evaluative strategies employed by listeners to discern AI-generated music, predominantly characterized by excessive repetition, rigid or unnatural structural elements, and atypical harmonic or melodic features. Conversely, human-produced music was associated with variations in melody, ornamentation, familiar stylistic elements, and attributes that appeared performable on traditional instruments. This observation aligns with a broader trend wherein musical features perceived as "human" are evaluated more favorably, whereas those associated with AI are regarded less positively. Consistent with this pattern, Hong et al. (2022) examined the impact of perceived anthropomorphism and creative autonomy on the acceptance of AI as musicians. Participants assessed AI-generated music across various genres, evaluating both the quality of the compositions and their own attitudes toward AI entities. The findings indicated that higher levels of anthropomorphism and perceived autonomy were correlated with increased acceptance of AI musicians. Furthermore, Sun et al. (2023) demonstrated that anthropomorphism influences perceptions of competence and warmth in AI musicians, both of which contribute to more favorable attitudes.

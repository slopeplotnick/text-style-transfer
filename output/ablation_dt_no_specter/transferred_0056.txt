Intuitively, the model benefits the most by learning from its own mistakes; however, highly off-policy negative samples can be detrimental, potentially misleading or destabilizing the optimization process. We have empirically observed that the implementation of Off-Policy Sequence Masking contributes to maintaining stability in certain training scenarios that would otherwise exhibit instability. Keep Routing Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models improve computational efficiency by activating only a subset of expert modules during inference. However, discrepancies between inference and training methodologies, coupled with policy modifications, can lead to inconsistent utilization of experts even when identical inputs are presented. Such inconsistency precipitates abrupt alterations in the active parameter subspace, thereby destabilizing the optimization process and exacerbating off-policy issues. To address this, we maintain the expert routing paths employed during inference and enforce the same routing paths during training, thereby ensuring the optimization of identical expert parameters. This Keep Routing method has been identified as essential for maintaining the stability of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models during reinforcement learning (RL) training, and it has been implemented in our RL training pipeline since the release of DeepSeek-V3-0324. DeepSeek-R1 has demonstrated that incorporating a cognitive process can significantly enhance a model's capacity to address complex problems. Building upon this insight, we aim to integrate cognitive capabilities into tool-calling scenarios. However, adhering to the methodology employed by DeepSeek-R1—eliminating reasoning content upon the receipt of a second round of messages—results in considerable inefficiencies in token utilization. This approach necessitates the model to redundantly reprocess the entirety of the problem for each subsequent tool invocation. To address this limitation, we developed a context management framework specifically optimized for tool-calling scenarios, as illustrated in Fig. 4. In this framework, historical reasoning content is discarded exclusively when a new user message is introduced into the interaction. Conversely, when solely appending tool-related outputs, the reasoning content is preserved throughout the interaction. Moreover, the removal of reasoning traces does not compromise the retention of tool call histories and their corresponding results within the contextual framework.

Human comprehension of videos necessitates discerning the progression of events, the rationale behind actions, and the timing of pivotal occurrences. However, conventional video object segmentation (VOS) techniques predominantly rely on appearance-based or category-level cues. Reasoning VOS advances this task: the model must interpret abstract, underspecified instructions, employ commonsense and causal reasoning, and integrate temporal dynamics with semantic knowledge to identify the target, such as "the runner most likely to win" or "the object causing the accident." This approach simplifies yet simultaneously complicates understanding, as it alters model functionality and demands extensive data for fine-tuning. The central premise posits that effective video reasoning necessitates deliberate decision-making rather than impulsive conjecture. Therefore, the model must ascertain the focal points, determine the appropriate moments for attention, and identify the entities pertinent to the inquiry. In alignment with this principle, we introduce ReVSeg, which eschews latent segmentation tokens and reconceptualizes reasoning video segmentation as an explicit sequence of reasoning steps. Specifically, ReVSeg systematically decomposes the reasoning VOS task into three interdependent components, each tailored to align with the model's inherent capabilities: video comprehension, which entails interpreting the query and discerning the unfolding events; temporal grounding, which involves identifying key frames or temporal segments pertinent to the query; and spatial grounding, which focuses on localizing the target objects within the identified frames. By integrating these reasoning steps within a unified framework, ReVSeg ensures that the semantic context established during the initial stages is preserved and propagated throughout subsequent processes. This structured approach not only maintains alignment with pretraining objectives but also obviates the need for extensive retraining, effectively transforming an otherwise intricate problem into a well-defined procedural pipeline. Once the reasoning steps are delineated, the primary challenge lies in enhancing the efficacy of these steps. Supervised learning offers limited utility due to the inadequate annotation of intermediate decisions. Consequently, we employ reinforcement learning, conceptualizing reasoning VOS as a behavioral policy that is rewarded exclusively when the entire decision trajectory culminates in a correct outcome. However, the application of reinforcement learning (RL) in this context presents significant challenges, as reasoning video object segmentation (VOS) provides sparse success signals and necessitates the integration of comprehension, temporal selection, and spatial identification. To address these challenges, we incorporate reward mechanisms at critical decision junctures, thereby enhancing the model's reasoning capabilities through RL. This approach, coupled with the decomposition of the task, transforms it from an opaque guessing endeavor into a transparent reasoning strategy: the decomposition elucidates the decisions the model must make, while RL elucidates the synergistic operation of these decisions. Ultimately, this collaborative effort establishes a reasoning strategy that is both internally coherent and robust in practical application.

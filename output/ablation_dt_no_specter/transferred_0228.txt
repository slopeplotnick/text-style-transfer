Notably, Delilah's use of the term 'masses' as a numeral is accurately identified by the language acquisition procedures, demonstrating the efficacy of the learning process in detecting such linguistic patterns. For data sets of 10,000 exemplars, the differentiation between function words and content words is facilitated by the observation that words appearing with a frequency exceeding 100 instances are predominantly function words, corresponding to a frequency greater than 1.7 per thousand in Delilah's generated sentences. Therefore, the MODOMA framework's parameters for distinguishing function words from content words are calibrated such that words occurring more than 2 per thousand are classified as function words, while the remainder are identified as content words. This classification process is executed subsequent to the accumulation of at least 10,000 exemplars. These parameters are set conservatively to minimize the likelihood of erroneously categorizing a lexical item as a function word rather than a content word. As content words are treated as the default category, lexical items are classified as content words unless compelling evidence suggests otherwise. The parameter settings established during the initial experiment have been designated as the default configuration for MODOMA; however, these settings remain subject to modification in light of subsequent experimental findings and theoretical advancements in linguistic research.

Based on the decomposition of item comprehension, query interpretation, and path mapping, we can synthesize the essential requirements for constructing an effective relevance model. To effectively navigate extensive item corpora and accommodate diverse user intents—ranging from explicit specifications to abstract reasoning—the model must possess a sophisticated, composite set of capabilities. We summarize these core requirements as follows: Reasoning and Knowledge Integration, which involves the ability to leverage external knowledge and logical inference to resolve ambiguities in query understanding and accurately interpret abstract user objectives during path construction. Multi-modal Understanding and Matching is also crucial, as it entails the capacity to parse, align, and match attributes across both textual and visual modalities, which is critical for managing items that are heavily reliant on visual content. Furthermore, Complex Rule Adherence is necessary, as it ensures the standardization and reproducibility of the path-following process through the precise execution of complex rule structures. From a formulation perspective, training large models for relevance tasks can be regarded as addressing a classification problem. Initially, early methodologies employed prompt-based frameworks to define tasks and generate corresponding outputs, subsequently refining model performance through supervised learning techniques. However, this straightforward approach demonstrated limited efficacy when applied to datasets that diverged significantly from the model's training distribution. Researchers discovered that encouraging models to engage in structured reasoning prior to producing responses substantially enhanced their performance on complex and challenging instances. Given the constraints of computational resources and practical application scenarios, smaller models are often preferred for deployment. However, these models typically exhibit limited reasoning capabilities and reduced proficiency in instruction-following when operating under a prompting-only paradigm. Therefore, the prevailing methodology involves utilizing more advanced teacher models, such as GPT-4 or DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025), to generate reasoning datasets. These datasets are subsequently employed to distill reasoning capabilities into smaller models through supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Additionally, some researchers integrate a reinforcement learning phase post-SFT, employing techniques such as offline reinforcement learning (e.g., Direct Preference Optimization or KTO) or online reinforcement learning (e.g., Group Relative Policy Optimization).

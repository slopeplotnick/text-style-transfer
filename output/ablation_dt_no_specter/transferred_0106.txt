To enhance clarity and ensure alignment with safety objectives, we incorporate model-generated critiques into each instance within the dataset, leveraging state-of-the-art high-capacity models. Specifically, we utilize gpt-oss-120b (Agarwal et al., 2025) for textual data, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct (Team, 2025) for visual modalities, including images, videos, and text-image pairs, and Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct (Team, 2024a) for auditory data. For each instance, the teacher model is provided with the original content, its associated safety label, and any identified violation categories, and is tasked with generating a reasoning critique that articulates the justification underlying its safety assessment. Unlike rudimentary models that merely classify inputs as safe or unsafe, OMNIGUARD employs a deliberate and methodical approach to deliver comprehensive and interpretable safety assessments. It generates a structured output consisting of the following components: (1) Safety judgment: an evaluation that determines whether the input is deemed safe or unsafe. (2) Violation categories: the delineation of specific safety issues identified in instances of unsafe content. (3) Reasoning critique: a detailed natural language justification elucidating the model's decision-making process in alignment with established policy guidelines. Consequently, this approach enables OMNIGUARD to transcend superficial pattern recognition, facilitating a transparent and systematic analysis of potentially unsafe content across diverse modalities. We evaluate its efficacy in both unimodal and cross-modal settings across 15 benchmarks encompassing text, image, video, and audio modalities. Furthermore, we design experiments to address two pivotal research questions: (1) Does the incorporation of reasoning-based safety alignment enhance the omni-modal guardrail model's capability to perform safety moderation and manage safety-critical challenges across diverse modalities? (2) Can safety knowledge acquired from familiar modalities be transferred to novel ones, thereby demonstrating cross-modal generalization in safety comprehension and moderation capabilities?

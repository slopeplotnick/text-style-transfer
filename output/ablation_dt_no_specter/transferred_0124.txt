An iterative refinement process has been integrated into PPTPilot, enabling it to progressively enhance its predictive accuracy. Specifically, the agent proposes edits to the XML files, which are temporarily rendered into a provisional PPT file. A verifier model then evaluates the generated PPT against the original instructions and provides feedback to address any identified discrepancies. Based on this feedback, the agent revises the presentation, effectively correcting its errors. Despite the simplicity of this design, it has proven to be both effective and efficient for PowerPoint editing when compared to existing tools and frameworks. We hope that PPTPilot will serve as a foundational benchmark for future research into PowerPoint editing. 

To ensure reliable evaluation, we leverage state-of-the-art vision-language models, including Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT 5. For clarity, the primary results in the main comparison (Table 3) are derived from evaluations conducted using GPT 5, while additional insights into Gemini 2.5 Pro and implementation details are provided in the supplementary materials. Furthermore, we conducted a comparative analysis of PPTPilot against a diverse range of existing PPT tools, including ChatGPT in extended thinking mode, Gemini-CLI, GPT-Agent—which simulates computer functionalities and desktop usage—MiniMax Agent, renowned for its multimodal task proficiency and marketed as an effective PPT assistant, as well as PPTAgent and Poster2Agent. 

Given the strict rate limits and high costs associated with proprietary tools like ChatGPT and MiniMax Agent, we conducted evaluations on a reduced subset of 25 samples to remain within budgetary constraints. The selection process prioritized the most challenging tasks, focusing on the 20 cases where PPTPilot and ChatGPT exhibited the weakest performance, supplemented by five manually curated examples to ensure comprehensive coverage across diverse scenarios. Further details regarding the selection criteria and task specifics are provided in the supplementary materials.

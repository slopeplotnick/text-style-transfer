We employ a principle-based approach to evaluate outlines, avoiding direct comparison with human-authored examples. Since outlines are inherently concise and primarily convey structural intent, juxtaposing them with comprehensive surveys is impractical. Therefore, our evaluation focuses on the degree of structural organization, logical progression, and pragmatic clarity exhibited by the outlines. This approach aligns more effectively with the fundamental criteria for high-quality outlines, which are predominantly determined by adherence to established academic conventions. Using the original survey title, each system under evaluation generates a survey. All systems possess online information retrieval capabilities, ensuring equal access to relevant information. We evaluate three categories of systems: general long-text generation models, paper-writing systems, and survey-writing systems. Each system receives identical input and is assessed using consistent evaluation criteria. The corresponding scores are presented in Table 3, which illustrates distinct patterns among the three categories. General long-text models demonstrate proficiency in linguistic fluency and maintaining topical relevance; however, their surveys exhibit limited depth, characterized by a lack of originality, insufficient synthesis of ideas, and minimal critical analysis. In contrast, paper-writing systems provide enhanced structural control, yet their outputs are frequently constrained by rigidity and adherence to templates, thereby restricting analytical depth and curtailing the diversity of perspectives presented. Meanwhile, dedicated survey-generation systems demonstrate superior performance overall, characterized by enhanced organizational structure, more precise integration of concepts, and improved differentiation between factual information and subjective opinions. Their efficacy is particularly pronounced in STEM disciplines, where accurate information retrieval and domain-specific structuring are critical. These distinctions underscore that the creation of high-quality surveys necessitates capabilities beyond those of general-purpose language models, relying instead on specialized methodologies that prioritize literature grounding and systematic synthesis.

LLaMA-Factory primarily focuses on text-generation tasks and does not support the transformation of these tasks into classification problems. In response, our framework, PEFT-FACTORY, addresses the limited availability of PEFT methods and enhances the fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs) for classification tasks. While other frameworks for training LLMs exist, they are often less user-friendly compared to LLaMA-Factory, though they offer unique advantages. For instance, FastChat (Zheng et al., 2023) is a specialized framework tailored for training LLMs in chat-completion tasks. LitGPT (AI, 2023) and LMFlow (Diao et al., 2023) are versatile and user-friendly frameworks that support a variety of generative models and training methodologies. Similarly, Axolotl (Axolotl maintainers and contributors, 2023) is a command-line tool designed for efficient post-training of LLMs without compromising functionality or scalability. Open-Instruct (Wang et al., 2023a) emphasizes instruction fine-tuning for LLMs and offers a range of models and methodologies for this purpose. Moreover, H2O LLM Studio<sup>1</sup> is an enterprise-oriented, all-in-one tool that includes a graphical interface for developing and deploying LLMs. GPT4All (Anand et al., 2023) provides a user-friendly interface for efficient training around LLaMA<sup>1</sup>. ColossalAI (Li et al., 2023) focuses on optimizing distributed computing by enabling fine-tuning across multiple nodes. Additionally, LLaMA-Adapter (Zhang et al., 2023) and LLaMA-Accessory (Gao et al., 2023) are lightweight frameworks; LLaMA-Adapter integrates trainable adapters into LLaMA models, while LLaMA-Accessory offers a comprehensive toolkit for LLM development. Notably, LLaMA-Adapter is frequently utilized in frameworks such as LitGPT. Based on our analysis of related frameworks, we identified three key features that are either missing or inadequately addressed, which our work introduces: 1) the ability to train LLMs using methods beyond reparameterization-based PEFT techniques, 2) the modular and dynamic integration of new PEFT methods, and 3) support for training and evaluation of LLMs in classification tasks.

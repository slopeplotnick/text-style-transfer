We propose SchED (Schedule-based Early Decoding) for diffusion language models, which employs a schedule-dependent approach—such as linear or cosine—to determine the stopping point based on model confidence levels, enabling stable, architecture-agnostic termination without additional training. Diffusion LLMs. Recent advancements have established diffusion language models as a viable alternative to autoregressive models, offering the flexibility to be trained de novo or adapted from pre-existing autoregressive frameworks. The LLaDA approach involves training a model to mask text in one direction while correcting it in the opposite direction, demonstrating effective outcomes in both training and fine-tuning phases. Several methodologies transform autoregressive models into diffusion models to facilitate equitable comparisons across varying sizes and tasks. In contrast, other strategies, such as Dream-7B, concentrate on refining methodologies and developing distinct model iterations. Moreover, innovative designs, exemplified by LLaDA-MoE, employ sparse configurations to achieve optimal results with a reduced number of active components. The capabilities of diffusion models are also expanding to long-context scenarios; for instance, UltraLLaDA employs RoPE scaling to effectively accommodate inputs of up to 128K tokens. Concurrently, Seed Diffusion prioritizes optimizing inference efficiency for code models, emphasizing high-throughput performance. Furthermore, formal investigations into the scaling properties of diffusion models have demonstrated growth dynamics comparable to those observed in autoregressive architectures, further reinforcing their scalability and potential for broader applications. Survey and perspective papers synthesize this domain, delineating several advantages, such as parallel processing, addressing informational voids, and managing overarching contextual frameworks, while also identifying outstanding research questions that warrant further investigation.

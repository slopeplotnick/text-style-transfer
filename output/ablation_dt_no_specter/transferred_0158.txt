Llama-4 Maverick's multimodal NRMSE performance surpasses that of a Bayesian reliability-weighted unbiased linear combination, a normative baseline commonly employed in biological studies. Humans are shown to utilize similar mechanisms consistently (Ernst & Banks, 2002). This outperformance by Llama-4 Maverick suggests an efficiency in cue-combination that extends beyond some biological systems, including humans, potentially leveraging additional non-linear properties not assumed under the linear baseline. In Figure 7, we compare Llama-4 Maverick's multimodal responses to its unimodal responses and find that a nonlinear random forest better fits its multimodal responses than linear models. Under linear-Gaussian noise, the Bayesian cue combiner remains optimal, providing a natural benchmark for improving less proficient language models. From the right panel of Figure 8, we observe that more accurate models generally exhibit stronger Bayes-consistent behavior. Nevertheless, despite the lower accuracy of Gemma 3 4B and Phi 4 Multimodal, they achieve respectable BCS values. A detailed breakdown of BCS by task and model is provided in Appendix A.9. Our findings reveal that LLMs display diverse behavioral patterns when tested on psychophysics-inspired magnitude estimation tasks. While the degree of Bayesian behavior varies across tasks and modalities, more accurate models, such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and Llama-4 Maverick, tend to exhibit higher Bayesian factor evidence, particularly in the image modality (see Appendix A.11 for a full breakdown). These models adapt their behavior in Bayes-consistent ways when inputs are perturbed by noise, steering, or extended context (right panel of Figure 8), effectively incorporating contextual prior information during estimation without explicit training or reasoning instructions. This mirrors findings in human psychophysics, where Bayesian models explain a wide range of perceptual phenomena and brain processing (Petzschner et al., 2015; Knill & Pouget, 2004) without explicit training. However, we emphasize that our current analysis is limited to behavioral observations, and further investigation is required to uncover the precise mechanisms underlying these behaviors.

To address this issue, we propose a method for quantifying the extent to which a reasoning model exhibits belief entrenchment. Specifically, we characterize belief entrenchment as a deviation from the Martingale property—a fundamental concept in Bayesian reasoning that posits the unpredictability of belief updates prior to their occurrence. Therefore, if a model's future belief can be reliably inferred from its prior belief, this indicates a violation of the Martingale property and suggests an excessive adherence to its initial beliefs, a phenomenon we term belief entrenchment. To evaluate this, we conducted empirical investigations across three domains: event forecasting, value-laden questions, and academic paper review. Analyzing a diverse set of models and reasoning methodologies, our findings reveal that belief entrenchment is a prevalent characteristic, with models frequently exhibiting a strong dependence on their prior beliefs. Our empirical evaluations indicate that future beliefs can often be anticipated based on prior beliefs, thereby contravening the Martingale property. Furthermore, we observed a decline in model accuracy when this property is not adhered to. Consequently, we introduce the Martingale Score as a metric to quantify the extent to which a model's belief updates are predictable solely from its antecedent beliefs. Utilizing this scoring metric, we evaluated prevalent models and observed a frequent occurrence of excessive adherence to pre-existing beliefs across various domains and with diverse prompts and models.

To ensure comparability across studies, it is essential that the samples exhibit specific similarities. Therefore, we assessed this through the application of Multigroup-CFA, treating the two samples—one derived from real-world data and the other simulated—as independent groups, while introducing progressively stringent constraints. This analysis was broken down into tests for configural invariance (H2.1), metric invariance (H2.2), scalar invariance (H2.3), and residual invariance (H2.4). If configural invariance is confirmed, the pattern of factor loadings is consistent across groups. Moreover, achieving metric invariance allows for the comparison of variances between latent constructs. Furthermore, if scalar invariance is verified, comparisons of latent mean scores become feasible. Finally, if residual invariance is confirmed, it becomes possible to evaluate observable means and conduct further analyses based on visible scores. Achieving substantial similarity between the real-world and simulated samples in these aspects would suggest that the simulated data is a reliable approximation of real-world data. In alignment with our hypotheses, we anticipated substantial agreement between responses from the real-world dataset and the simulated dataset (H3), and no significant divergence in score distributions (H4). To ascertain whether the simulated datasets exhibited characteristics analogous to real-world data, we initially employed Mann-Whitney U tests to evaluate potential differences in central tendency. Subsequently, we utilized Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests to assess the distributional properties of the datasets.

The language acquisition experiments discussed aim to incorporate explicit grammatical knowledge into a lexicon. Consequently, the daughter agent can utilize this acquired knowledge to engage in conversation with the mother. To achieve this, the system employs statistical machine learning techniques, resulting in an explicitly specified grammatical representation. This study enhances existing systems that rely on statistical methods and neural networks by introducing a more interpretable, rule-based framework for language modeling. By applying Zipf's law (Mandelbrot 1954; Zipf 1949), the study evaluates the robustness and feasibility of implementing a multi-agent simulation for first language acquisition. Importantly, the objective of this research is not merely to perform a binary classification of function and content words; rather, this classification serves as a methodological instrument for assessing the validity of this novel approach to modeling first language acquisition. The advantage of using Zipf's law is that it provides a robust and dependable framework for assessing the performance of the system, which is a well-established practice. For instance, Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) employ Zipf's law to develop a multi-agent framework for modeling language evolution. Recent investigations by Dębowski (2021), Takahashi and Tanaka-Ishii (2019), and Takamichi et al. (2024) further contribute to this domain. Moreover, the experiments conducted in this study validate all components essential for the development of a multi-agent model of first language acquisition. Specifically, the acquisition of function and content words employs a hybrid language acquisition strategy, which is predicated on statistical tendencies that facilitate the acquisition of discrete grammatical rules. Therefore, these findings support the modeling of more complex grammatical phenomena and demonstrate the viability of the MODOMA research agenda. Building upon these findings, Shakouri et al. (2025) elucidate the acquisition of grammatical categories such as nouns, verbs, and prepositions through unsupervised methodologies. This paper delineates two experiments exemplifying this approach: the initial simulation, detailed in Sections 6 and 7, was implemented on a training dataset generated by the mother agent. These experiments were employed to determine the parameter settings for the acquisition of this linguistic feature. The second experiment utilized a novel test data set generated by the adult language model to validate this configuration, as detailed in Section 8. The findings indicate that the MODOMA is capable of acquiring these grammatical categories and representing them through the grammar formalism of the daughter agent.

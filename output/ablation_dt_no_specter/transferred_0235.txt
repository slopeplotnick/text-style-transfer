The parsing system extracts structured allocation decisions from LLM markdown responses using regex pattern matching. This technique identifies specific formatted sections within the LLM's markdown output—such as allocation decisions, success rates, and reasoning explanations—and converts them into structured data for validation purposes. To ensure data integrity, a comprehensive validation system applies eight rules, including the elimination of invalid allocations, verification of trade-off analyses, assessment of workload awareness, and evaluation of logical consistency. However, the limitations of traditional rule-based validation systems, which often fail to account for the nuanced reasoning and contextual awareness required for LLM-driven task allocation, necessitated the transition to a weighted validation framework. Recent studies on LLM evaluation (Guo et al. 2023, Evaluation and Benchmarking of LLM Agents, 2025) highlight that multi-dimensional, criterion-based assessments provide a more robust measure of model reasoning performance compared to binary correctness checks. Building on these findings, the proposed validation system adopts a weighted scoring approach to evaluate reasoning outputs across multiple dimensions, including explanation clarity, success-rate consistency, workload awareness, trade-off justification, procedural compliance, and confidence alignment. Each dimension contributes proportionally to a composite quality score, with greater weight assigned to factors that directly influence allocation outcomes, such as reasoning quality and success-rate accuracy, while secondary aspects like confidence justification are given lesser emphasis. Consequently, this configuration ensures the validation process is both performance-sensitive and context-aware, striking a balance between interpretability and operational rigor in evaluating the model's reasoning capabilities. The scoring criteria are as follows: 1. Explanation Quality (Weight: 0.20): Reasoning depth and clarity. 2. Success Rate Accuracy (Weight: 0.20): Alignment between predicted and actual success probabilities. 3. Trade-off Analysis (Weight: 0.15): Explicit consideration of competing objectives. 4. Workload Awareness (Weight: 0.15): Consideration of current task distribution.

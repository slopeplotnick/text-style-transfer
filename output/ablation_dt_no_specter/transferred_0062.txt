The transition to a Software Product Line (SPL) approach entails substantial organizational changes, particularly given the inherent complexities of Software Product Management, as highlighted by the survey conducted by Springer and Miler [SM22]. Adopting an SPL framework transforms teams into more cross-functional units, fostering collaboration through the use of a shared repository of core assets systematically designed for reuse across multiple projects. Consequently, this shift enables optimized resource utilization, accelerates time-to-market, and refocuses efforts on innovation. Moreover, the emphasis on component reusability, configuration-based customization, and unified documentation streamlines processes and enhances efficiency. While this transition demands meticulous planning and change management, it ultimately cultivates a culture of collaboration, efficiency, and adaptability within the organization. At ITK, the outcomes and guidelines were presented and discussed with the development team, who expressed a strong desire to remain informed and actively involved throughout the migration process. In response, we engaged in continuous collaboration with them, ensuring their participation at every stage. Our findings at ITK were contextualized by comparing them with various reports on SPL adoption, strategies, and experiences documented in the literature. The ITK initiative adheres to the IDEAL improvement model [McF96], beginning with a collective decision to initiate the migration. Initial efforts focused on diagnosing existing practices and assessing the perception of the project. Subsequently, the next steps involved establishing a strategic framework and action plan, executing these initiatives, and iteratively evaluating outcomes to identify effective practices and areas for improvement, consistent with agile methodologies. The guidelines provided serve as a foundation for constructing the action plan. Notably, the challenges encountered at ITK diverged from those reported in other studies, such as the lack of skilled personnel or managerial support, as these were not perceived as barriers within the organization’s culture.

Li et al. (2025) introduce a feedback-guided test-time training framework, where external verifiers provide weak supervision to iteratively refine predictions during inference. Similarly, Zuo et al. (2025) present Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL), which generates multiple rollouts, aggregates predictions through majority voting, and uses the consensus as a reward signal to update the policy. While effective, these approaches are hindered by inefficiencies, as they require numerous redundant rollouts to achieve stable consensus estimates. More recently, Wang et al. (2025) demonstrate that the utilization of verifiable rewards significantly enhances learning efficacy with a minimal number of training examples, highlighting the potential of this approach in test-time scenarios. Building on this foundation, our research extends the framework by explicitly addressing rollout inefficiency through the implementation of a statistically grounded early-stopping mechanism for adaptive allocation. In the realm of policy optimization, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a widely utilized approach for ensuring stable policy updates, particularly in scenarios involving learning from human feedback. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) further enhances this by introducing group-normalized advantages to improve stability under constrained feedback conditions. Extending these advancements, OptPO leverages the generality of such surrogate objectives to improve process efficiency, achieving this without necessitating modifications to the core optimization framework. In summary, previous research has demonstrated that training during the testing phase enhances model adaptability through various approaches, such as uncertainty reduction, assisted learning, and reinforcement learning. However, existing methodologies are hindered by their computational inefficiency due to the extensive number of trials required. This study addresses this limitation by introducing OptPO, a framework that significantly reduces computational demands while maintaining performance, thereby augmenting and refining existing test-time adaptation strategies.

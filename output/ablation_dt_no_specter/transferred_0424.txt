The motion prior should serve as a modular and independent objective, enabling policy training to achieve naturalistic behaviors without requiring access to the original reference dataset. Furthermore, it should be reusable across a wide range of tasks and policies without necessitating retraining. To meet these requirements, we introduce Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), a method for constructing modular and reusable motion priors that facilitate the training of control policies to produce life-like movements across diverse tasks. Starting with an unstructured motion dataset, we first train a motion diffusion model to capture the underlying data distribution in a task-agnostic manner. Once trained, this diffusion model is kept frozen and repurposed as a motion prior using score distillation sampling (SDS) [Poole et al. 2022], which provides a robust measure of similarity between simulated motions and the behaviors in the reference dataset. By leveraging the learned SMP as a general style reward, our approach enables the training of new policies to accomplish a variety of tasks while maintaining naturalistic behaviors. The key contribution of this work is a framework for constructing modular and reusable motion priors for physics-based character animation by integrating reinforcement learning with score distillation techniques. Our system generates high-quality motions comparable to state-of-the-art adversarial imitation learning methods while significantly enhancing modularity and reusability. Importantly, SMP allows policy training to be conducted independently of the original reference dataset, thereby increasing flexibility in its application. We demonstrate that a score-matching motion prior can be derived from a task-agnostic diffusion model pretrained on large-scale motion datasets. This general-purpose motion prior can then be adapted into style-specific variants through prompting and guidance. Moreover, we show that style priors can be composed to create novel behavioral styles that are absent from the original dataset.

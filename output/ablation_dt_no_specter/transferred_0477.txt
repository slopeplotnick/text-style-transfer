To this end, we evaluate our system on four simulation benchmarks comprising 64 tasks and 5 real-world tasks, covering three types of VLA policies. Specifically, we utilize the Simpler, Libero, Robotwin1.0, and Robotwin2.0 benchmarks for evaluation. The Simpler benchmark, built on the SAPIEN simulator and the ManiSkill2 framework, provides simulated task environments for both the WindowX and Google Robot platforms; in this work, we primarily focus on tasks designed for WindowX. Meanwhile, Libero serves as a benchmark for lifelong learning in decision-making (LLDM), consisting of four distinct task suites. Our evaluation emphasizes the most challenging suite, as prior research has already achieved near-optimal performance on the others. Robotwin1.0 and Robotwin2.0 focus on dual-arm gripper manipulation, offering a wide range of assets and task types, as well as scripted policies for automated data collection. For real-world experiments, we employ a REALMAN75 dual-arm robotic system in an office-like environment to perform five tasks involving common objects such as books, pens, a cabinet, a charger, a laptop, and a paper ball. These tasks include: (1) Receive Book, where the robot accepts a book from a human and places it back on the bookshelf; (2) Storage Charger, where it picks up the charger head, stores it inside the cabinet, and closes the cabinet door; (3) Paper and Pen, where it disposes of a paper ball into the trash bin and places a pen into the pen holder; (4) Laptop, where it closes the laptop lid, unplugs the charger from the socket, and stores the charger in the cabinet; and (5) Pick Books, where the robot’s dual arms simultaneously lift and hold two books.

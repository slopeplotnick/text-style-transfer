High task accuracy does not always imply optimal cue-combination (middle panel of Figure 8). For instance, GPT-5 Mini achieves very low NRMSE but fails to integrate modalities as effectively as other models. This limitation is particularly evident in cases where unimodal performance is imbalanced; optimal behavior would necessitate substantially down-weighting the weaker modality—a capability some LLMs lack. Consequently, benchmarks focused solely on accuracy risk favoring models that are less robust to noise. In contrast, Llama-4 Maverick surpasses Bayesian reliability-weighted linear fusion, demonstrating the use of more advanced non-linear integration strategies. Notably, a non-linear random forest model aligns more closely with its cue-combination dynamics than simpler linear approaches. Furthermore, a comparison of unimodal and multimodal performance reveals that models such as Llama-4 Maverick, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o effectively leverage additional input modalities (e.g., text and images) to achieve lower error rates across most multimodal tasks. However, this trend is not universal, as variability in performance gains highlights substantial room for improvement in multimodal LLM development. For a detailed breakdown of model-specific results, refer to Appendix A.11. To move beyond static accuracy metrics, we introduce the Bayesian Consistency Score (BCS), which captures principled behavioral shifts and provides a more holistic evaluation of model performance. By analyzing behavior under controlled ablations, we can compare models with differing baseline performance and uncover deeper insights into their computational strategies. Although complex heuristics, such as salience-based weighting or rule-switching, may better fit static response patterns, it is ultimately more informative to assess whether models exhibit Bayes-consistent behaviors under changing conditions, as guided by a Bayesian observer model.

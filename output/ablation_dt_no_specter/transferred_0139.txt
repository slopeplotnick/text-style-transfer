Given real-time latency and cost constraints, scaling our LLM or deep cross-encoder-based BERT classifier to an online search system poses significant challenges. Therefore, we adopt a teacher-student distillation framework to derive a student relevance model, as illustrated in Figure 1. Specifically, our online model is a lightweight, shallow cross-encoder BERT. To ensure distributional consistency between the teacher and student models, we employ a deep cross-encoder BERT as the teacher model, rather than opting for an autoregressively trained LLM. Moreover, to enhance the BERT-based model's ability to discern relevance between queries and products, we explicitly integrate key attribute information mined by another model into the BERT input, thereby enriching its understanding of discriminative relevance factors. To address the challenges posed by noise and redundancy in extended explanations, we introduce a model that extracts and refines product attribute descriptions aligned or misaligned with the query. This refinement filters irrelevant information and reformulates critical insights into a concise, structured format, enabling the lightweight BERT-based model to focus on salient reasoning. However, since the student model cannot access these enriched insights during real-time inference, we incorporate them exclusively into the teacher model to ensure consistency between online and offline inputs. Subsequently, knowledge is transferred to the student model through a structured distillation process. Finally, as there are no publicly available datasets for Chinese e-commerce relevance tasks, we conduct experiments using a large-scale dataset derived from JD application search logs.

As we advance toward more conversational agents powered by language models, addressing linguistic diversity is not merely a matter of fairness but is crucial for achieving the robustness and adaptability necessary for real-world applications. While voice-based language models have demonstrated notable advancements, they still face significant challenges in processing diverse linguistic expressions, such as accents, dialects, and sociolects. Historically, speech processing models have struggled with these variations, highlighting a persistent area for improvement. Despite the progress in language models, they continue to exhibit biases due to the datasets on which they are trained. These models often prioritize prevalent accents or languages with extensive data availability, leading to suboptimal performance for individuals with less common accents. Therefore, it is imperative to develop models that can accommodate diverse phonetic variations and speech patterns. Dynamic adaptation during deployment, along with more sophisticated data grouping strategies, holds significant potential for enhancing the models' ability to process previously unseen accents, even with limited data. Conventional evaluation methodologies, such as word error rate (WER), often fail to capture the nuanced performance disparities across diverse linguistic and demographic groups. Consequently, there is a pressing need to develop more comprehensive evaluation frameworks that prioritize fairness-driven metrics, enabling a more accurate assessment of model performance across varying accents, languages, and demographic categories. Moreover, error evaluation should extend beyond mere quantification to include a qualitative examination of the demographic groups disproportionately impacted by model biases. Such an approach is essential for fostering the development of more equitable systems. Additionally, the inherent diversity within a language, shaped by factors such as age, gender, socioeconomic status, and professional domains, introduces further complexity that must be accounted for in model design and evaluation. Conventional speech models frequently overemphasize homogenous data, predominantly assimilating the speech patterns of dominant societal groups. Addressing this issue requires more than merely incorporating diverse datasets; it demands advanced methodologies such as style transfer and speaker adaptation to enable models to comprehend a broad array of speech styles and cues. Emphasizing paralinguistic features, including prosody, rhythm, and emotional undertones, is crucial for accurately interpreting social and emotional expressions within speech.

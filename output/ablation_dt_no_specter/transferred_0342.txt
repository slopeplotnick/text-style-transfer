The fine-tuned model Menta demonstrates better performance in detecting depression and stress compared to suicide risk. Similarly, both fine-tuned and non-fine-tuned SLMs, as well as larger-scale models, consistently show stronger results in depression and stress classification tasks than in suicide risk prediction. For example, a systematic review by Bauer et al. (2024) highlights that language models often misclassify subtle indicators of suicidality due to class imbalance and their reliance on contextual or temporal cues, which are frequently absent in isolated posts. These findings align with a study by Lamichhane (2023), which reported F1-scores of 0.86 for depression detection and only 0.37 for suicidality detection using social media data. This disparity suggests that depression and stress are more readily identifiable due to their distinct linguistic patterns and the availability of larger datasets, whereas suicidality is rarer, more contextually dependent, and linguistically nuanced, making it more challenging for models to learn effectively. Consequently, SLMs and larger models may be better suited for general screening tasks, such as detecting depression or stress, rather than the complex stratification of suicide risk, unless supplemented with multimodal or temporal data. Despite these promising results, several limitations constrain the generalizability and applicability of our findings. While models like Qwen-3 (4B) demonstrated strong predictive capabilities for mental health concerns, our analysis was restricted to textual social media posts, excluding multimodal features such as linguistic style dynamics, user metadata, and interaction patterns, which are critical for understanding real-world mental health contexts. Incorporating these multimodal signals could enhance robustness and ecological validity in future research (Yazdavar et al., 2020; Khoo et al., 2024). Furthermore, our study primarily focused on depression, stress, and suicidal ideation, which represent only a narrow subset of the broader spectrum of mental health challenges. Conditions such as anxiety, bipolar disorder, and emotional dysregulation remain underexplored in our datasets, limiting the diversity of tasks addressed (Cao et al., 2024). Additionally, all analyses relied on publicly available, anonymized social media datasets rather than clinical data. While this approach ensures privacy and reproducibility, it inherently restricts conclusions regarding clinical safety, ethical considerations, and the long-term reliability of these methods in healthcare applications.

Our objective is to clarify the mechanisms behind these techniques and explore how their effects can be better understood and optimized by explicitly considering the distinct roles of navigation and refinement. Next, we analyze how the choice of latent space influences the model's dynamics within a two-stage oracle target framework. Figure 9 compares the oracle loss dynamics during training for VA-VAE [41] and SD-VAE [31] latent spaces. During the navigation stage, both latent spaces exhibit a gradual increase in oracle loss, suggesting that the growing complexity of coarse-layout prediction is largely agnostic to the latent space employed. In the refinement stage, however, the loss trends diverge significantly. While the absolute magnitudes are not directly comparable, discernible patterns emerge from the shapes of the oracle loss curves. For VA-VAE, whose latent space is aligned with semantic structure via DINO-based VF loss [28, 41], the oracle loss converges smoothly into a parabola-like shape as training progresses, indicating a well-organized latent manifold with coherently arranged semantic modes. In contrast, SD-VAE, which is trained primarily for low-level reconstruction, exhibits a flatter curve with noticeable waviness, reflecting a less structured latent manifold and the model's difficulty in achieving consistent denoising across stages. Overall, latent spaces that align with high-level conceptual structures exhibit properties favorable for diffusion modeling, as such alignment promotes clearer mode organization under Gaussian marginals, thereby facilitating both efficient navigation and effective refinement.

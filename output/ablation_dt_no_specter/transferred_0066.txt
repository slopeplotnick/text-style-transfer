However, further analysis revealed that this effect was primarily driven by individual differences in attitudes toward AI rather than the experimental manipulation itself. When attitudes toward AI were included as covariates in linear mixed-effects models, the experimental condition no longer significantly predicted participants' liking of the music or the intensity of their emotional responses. In contrast, attitudes toward AI emerged as a consistent and robust predictor. This aligns with previous research demonstrating that attitudes toward autonomous systems significantly influence evaluations (Hong et al., 2022) and extends the findings of Shank et al. (2023), who suggested that biases toward composers may depend on contextual factors, such as the popularity of the piece and its perceived human origin. Although certain personality traits and music-related variables were associated with liking and emotional responses, their influence diminished substantially when attitudes toward AI were accounted for. Notable exceptions to this trend were openness to experience and neuroticism, two traits previously identified as key factors in shaping emotional responses to music, particularly emotions such as joy and vitality (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Juslin et al., 2011; Liljeström et al., 2013), both of which were prominent in the musical stimuli used in the present study. Most notably, attitudes toward AI were the only listener characteristic significantly associated with both liking and emotional responses. The total variance explained by listener characteristics was comparable to that reported in prior studies (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Strauss et al., 2024). These findings underscore that attitudes toward AI are a critical determinant in the evaluation of AI-generated music across diverse listeners. In contrast to Ansani et al. (2024), we did not observe a moderation effect of attitudes toward AI on the functioning of the experimental groups. This discrepancy may stem from differences in the attitude scales employed, our focus on listening to tracks rather than live performances, and the inclusion of three experimental conditions instead of two.

Despite the growing demand for mental health support, significant diagnostic gaps persist globally. Barriers such as limited clinic hours, geographic inaccessibility, and workforce shortages hinder timely assessments and early recognition of mental health conditions. Moreover, structural, patient-level, and systemic challenges further delay diagnosis for individuals with severe mental health disorders. This mismatch between high need and limited resources highlights the constraints of traditional diagnostic models. Furthermore, the conventional delivery of in-person sessions and reliance on self-reported questionnaires struggle to scale and face systemic bottlenecks, including workforce shortages, high costs, geographic disparities, and stigma. Consequently, mental health wait times can range from 3 to 18 months, varying by region and provider availability. Therefore, the imperative for early detection and real-time monitoring necessitates the development of more effective methodologies for timely identification and intervention. This underscores the importance of portable, resource-efficient solutions, such as small language models, for scalable mental health detection and intervention. The rise of portable, on-device technologies offers a promising path for the early detection of mental health issues, particularly through passive, real-time analysis of social media posts and smartphone applications. Utilizing linguistic data from social media platforms on smartphones enables scalable, passive monitoring of psychological states, facilitating early identification of mood alterations, stress, or suicide risk. For instance, such systems can contribute to suicide prevention efforts and identify patterns in social media usage pertinent to mental health. When these models operate locally on personal devices, they preserve user privacy while analyzing language inputs for indicators of depression or stress. As a result, on-device deployment enables scalable, privacy-preserving mental health monitoring, extending detection beyond clinical settings and enhancing accessibility for a broader population.

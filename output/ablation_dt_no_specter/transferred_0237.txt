The conference proceedings are accessible at https://doi.org/10.1145/3726302.3731944. E-commerce platforms, such as Amazon, Taobao, and JD, have significantly transformed consumer shopping behaviors. The relevance module is crucial for the user experience, as displaying irrelevant products can harm trust and revenue for e-commerce systems. Therefore, relevance modeling, which evaluates the match between search queries and products, is a fundamental component of e-commerce engines. Traditional relevance models, including term matching and vector matching methods, have been widely employed in online retail platforms to ensure a satisfactory search experience. Term matching methodologies, such as TF-IDF and BM25, compute the matching degree between queries and products but fail to capture semantic relevance and contextual understanding. In contrast, vector-based approaches leverage BERT and its variants to understand semantic relationships between queries and products, achieving remarkable improvements in relevance modeling. However, despite these advancements, vector-based methods face two primary challenges in domain-specific relevance modeling tasks. The first challenge is the scarcity of domain-specific hard samples. While existing methodologies generally yield satisfactory outcomes with a moderate amount of labeled data, further advancements often require a significant number of challenging examples, which are difficult to procure and annotate accurately. Moreover, the reasoning and representational capabilities of models remain insufficient, particularly constrained by their parameter size. Lightweight online models frequently struggle with complex reasoning tasks and relevance discrimination. To address these limitations and enhance reasoning and comprehension capabilities, large language models (LLMs) have increasingly been applied to relevance modeling tasks. Nevertheless, most prior LLM approaches primarily utilize the representation of the last token for relevance classification and employ soft labels to distill knowledge into smaller models. This approach does not effectively elucidate the features influencing relevance nor significantly enhance the reasoning capabilities of the smaller models.

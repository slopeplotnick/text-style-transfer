EMOPIA. The research literature on affective music generation often lacks large-scale human-labeled MIDI datasets. Most available datasets are small, containing fewer than 200 MIDI files paired with emotion labels. We utilize the largest dataset available, EMOPIA, which comprises 1,087 music clips from 387 songs, annotated for emotion by four human annotators. This dataset includes four unique emotion labels, each corresponding to different quadrants in the valence-arousal plane. More information on EMOPIA is provided in the Appendix (Section B). GiantMIDI-Piano. Another dataset used in this study is GiantMIDI-Piano, constructed for various research areas such as classical music analysis and symbolic music generation. It contains 10,855 solo piano works composed by 2,786 different composers. To pair our text data with EMOPIA files, we categorized the 27 emotion labels into four AV quadrants. For this categorization, we used the NRC VAD lexicon, a comprehensive resource of human-annotated arousal and valence scores for 55,000 words. Using this lexicon, we assigned one of the four quadrant labels to each emotion. The resulting mappings and their positions on the AV plane are shown in Figure 1. Appendix A provides more details on the VAD lexicon and its integration with EMOPIA. We use the Musical Instrument Digital Interface (MIDI) as our format for encoding and generating symbolic music data because of its capacity to encode musical performance data through discrete parametric events. Moreover, it is a compact representation that is expressive enough to convey emotion-related aspects of music. Additionally, the widespread availability of MIDI datasets makes them a valuable resource for training purposes. In our training, we view each MIDI file in our datasets as a sequence of discrete musical events, enabling the use of sequence-to-sequence models for effective learning and generation.

Verifier Gain is introduced as a novel metric to quantify the improvement in a solver's performance when using a verifier during test-time rejection sampling. While verifier accuracy alone offers an incomplete assessment, Verifier Gain provides a more comprehensive measure by capturing the added utility of the verifier. To evaluate the efficacy of verifiers in rejecting incorrect solutions, we conducted an analysis that aligns closely with empirical observations. Moreover, we examined the effectiveness of different verification strategies, including self-verification, intra-group verification, and inter-group verification. Notably, verifiers from distinct groups generally outperform self-verification, as excessive similarity between the verifier and solver reduces the verifier's effectiveness. This diminished efficacy arises because high similarity makes the verifier more prone to accepting incorrect solver outputs. As models advance—whether through increased scale, improved training, or enhanced accuracy—they exhibit reduced effectiveness in self-verification but demonstrate greater proficiency in cross-family verification. Furthermore, we explored the verifiability of various tasks. Tasks that are straightforward to solve are typically also straightforward to verify; however, self-verification offers minimal improvement across all task types. Interestingly, tasks requiring mathematical or logical reasoning consistently yield higher verifier gains. Verifiers can operate at two levels: they may assess the correctness of the final solution or evaluate the validity of intermediate reasoning steps. Recent advancements in model verification have introduced methods enabling models to assess their own responses by predicting query components based on derived solutions. To further mitigate errors, some models engage in self-directed fact-checking inquiries. Additionally, other researchers have developed verifiers trained to evaluate final solutions or collaborate with solvers, thereby enhancing the reliability and accuracy of outputs. Efforts have also focused on verifying individual procedural steps within the reasoning process.

However, current video model evaluations predominantly emphasize fundamental physical knowledge, limiting their scope to commonplace scenarios that necessitate only a rudimentary understanding of physical principles. To address this limitation, we introduce VideoScience-Bench, a benchmark specifically designed to assess the scientific comprehension and reasoning capabilities of video models. Each evaluation instance incorporates multiple scientific concepts and requires undergraduate-level expertise, encompassing 103 topics across the domains of physics and chemistry. We evaluate whether these video models can serve as zero-shot scientific reasoners, capable of discerning scientific setups and anticipating expected outcomes autonomously. The evaluation focuses on five principal dimensions: Prompt Consistency, Expected Phenomenon, Dynamism, Immutability, and Coherence. Moreover, we have developed VideoScience-Judge, an evaluation framework employing a checklist-based approach to systematically assess the models. This framework constructs a comprehensive checklist derived from the evaluation criteria, identifies salient video frames, and detects critical moments of interaction or change. These processes facilitate the generation of quantitative scores that assess the model's scientific reasoning capabilities. The aggregated scores provide a singular metric indicative of the model's overall performance. For validation, empirical results demonstrate that the evaluations produced by VideoScience-Judge exhibit a higher alignment with expert assessments compared to existing benchmarks, which primarily emphasize basic physical reasoning or perceptual realism. In summary, this study examines the capability of video generation models to concurrently reason over multiple scientific concepts. To this end, we present three principal contributions: 1. We have developed VideoScience-Bench, a high-quality benchmark aimed at evaluating the ability of video generation models to perform zero-shot scientific reasoning, utilizing real-world examples encompassing 103 topics in physics and chemistry. 2. We developed VideoScience-Judge, an evaluation framework that employs a checklist-based approach, integrates key-frame selection, and utilizes computer vision tools to detect physical cues such as motion and object interactions, providing quantitative assessments of the scientific validity and perceptual quality of videos. 3. We conducted an extensive empirical investigation utilizing VideoScience-Bench. Despite the capability of contemporary video models to generate high-quality, photorealistic, and temporally coherent videos, VideoScience-Bench remains a formidable challenge. Many models still exhibit deficiencies in their ability to represent intricate scientific concepts and frequently contravene fundamental physical principles. Among the evaluated models, Sora-2 and Veo-3 demonstrated the highest performance, exhibiting preliminary indications of scientific reasoning capabilities.

In contrast to image-based IC-LoRA methods that require separate training for each editing task, our single Sync-LoRA module generalizes across diverse edits and achieves a robust balance between high visual fidelity and precise motion preservation, as demonstrated in our experiments (see section 4). In-context learning (ICL) within diffusion models facilitates the generalization of models to novel tasks by conditioning on structured input examples rather than explicit instructions. While extensively studied in the context of language models, recent investigations have explored the application of ICL in diffusion-based image generation. In these instances, the model generates a collection of images wherein the images within the collection provide contextual relevance to one another. This capability is advantageous for applications such as storyboard creation, which necessitates consistency across the images. When conditioning these collections on a given real image, ICL-based methodologies can be employed for a diverse array of image-to-image tasks, including image personalization and image editing. The work most analogous to ours is IC-LoRA, which identified that transformer-based diffusion models inherently possess the capability to generate panels wherein certain attributes remain consistent across the images. This capability was further enhanced through the fine-tuning of the model with a low-rank adapter (LoRA) on structured, consistent panels, accompanied by the utilization of specific template prompts. Their methodology has been applied to various domains, including storyboard generation, font design, and interior decoration. Building upon this concept, our work extends its application to video editing by introducing an IC-LoRA framework designed to achieve frame-accurate synchronization of portraits. Furthermore, recent advancements have demonstrated that large video diffusion transformers inherently exhibit in-context learning capabilities, enabling the generation of temporally and spatially coherent video clips within a single diffusion process. While prior studies have explored this phenomenon, leveraging techniques such as inpainting to exert control over individual scenes, they have not adequately addressed the challenge of ensuring temporal consistency across generated clips. Building upon this foundation, PoseGen employs in-context LoRA fine-tuning to enable pose-controllable human video generation, producing extended and coherent motion sequences derived from a reference image. Expanding on this approach, our method explicitly trains the in-context behavior to prioritize synchronized editing, thereby addressing the challenges of precise temporal alignment and consistency.

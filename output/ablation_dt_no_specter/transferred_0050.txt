Comparison with judges in related benchmarks. Our evaluation methodology marks a significant improvement over previous benchmarks. Prior approaches, such as PPTC-R, primarily focus on API-level differences. While this method is useful, it is insufficient for detecting critical semantic errors, such as cases where an agent places the correct text on the wrong slide. In contrast, our IF Judge leverages structured summaries to specifically identify such logical inconsistencies. Moreover, while other benchmarks like T2US employ visual language models (VLMs) as evaluative tools, their dependence on prompting without a robust rubric results in inconsistent and unreliable assessments. PPTArena addresses these shortcomings by establishing a rigorous and reproducible framework for evaluation. In summary, our dual-judge system, grounded in rubric-based guidelines, is vital for evaluating the intricate, multi-step reasoning required for PowerPoint presentation editing and for uncovering subtle errors that simpler methodologies often overlook. Additionally, we introduce PPTPilot, an agent for presentation editing, whose straightforward architecture delivers remarkably effective performance, even surpassing proprietary tools like OpenAI's ChatGPT Agent (details in Section 5.1). The design of PPTPilot is based on two key insights. First, the primary challenge in this domain lies not only in understanding the required tasks but also in executing them with precision and reliability. PowerPoint files are built on the complex Office Open XML (OOXML) format, which is highly intolerant to errors or malformed outputs from VLMs, necessitating specialized formats and configurations for effective editing. Second, we observed that relying on a single editing approach, such as exclusively using the XML format, is insufficient to meet the diverse demands of editing tasks. Therefore, a robust system must intelligently select the optimal editing strategy for each specific task. To achieve this, PPTPilot employs a dual-path architecture, enabling it to handle editing requests either through programmatic tools or direct manipulation of the XML format.

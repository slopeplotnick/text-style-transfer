Editing portrait videos is a complex task that demands flexible yet precise control over a variety of modifications, such as changes in appearance, adjustments to expressions, or the addition of new elements. The primary challenge lies in preserving the subject's temporal behavior, ensuring that every edited frame remains perfectly synchronized with the corresponding frame in the original footage. To address this, we introduce SyncLoRA, a method for editing portrait videos that delivers high-quality visual modifications while maintaining frame-accurate synchronization and identity consistency. Our approach employs an image-to-video diffusion model, where edits are initiated in the first frame and subsequently propagated throughout the video sequence. To achieve temporal coherence, we train an in-context LoRA using paired videos that share identical motion trajectories but differ in appearance. These pairs are carefully curated through a synchronization-based filtering process, which selects only the most temporally aligned examples for training. This training framework enables the model to seamlessly combine motion dynamics from the source video with the visual changes introduced in the edited first frame. Leveraging a compact and meticulously curated dataset of synchronized human portraits, SyncLoRA generalizes effectively to unseen identities and a diverse range of edits, including appearance modifications, object additions, and background changes, even under significant variations in pose and expression. Our experimental results demonstrate the method's ability to achieve high visual fidelity and strong temporal coherence, striking a robust balance between edit accuracy and motion preservation. With the rapid advancements in diffusion-based video editing techniques, text- and image-driven methods are becoming increasingly prominent for producing high-quality, temporally consistent edits. These innovations hold immense potential for applications in advertising, film production, video games, and interactive media, where precise control over visual content is essential. Notably, portrait video editing remains a particularly demanding challenge, as a significant portion of visual media focuses on human subjects, requiring edits to appear seamless and faithful to the original performance.

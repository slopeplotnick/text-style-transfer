How do language models utilize contextual information to address health-related inquiries, and what are the implications when the context is contradictory? We examine the proficiency of language models in processing extensive, conflicting biomedical information through the application of the HEALTHCONTRADICT dataset, an expert-verified collection of 920 instances, each featuring a health-related question, a factual answer substantiated by scientific evidence, and two documents presenting opposing perspectives. We explore various prompt configurations, including those with accurate, erroneous, or contradictory contexts, to evaluate their influence on model responses. Compared to existing evaluation benchmarks for medical question-answering, HEALTHCONTRADICT offers a more nuanced assessment of language models' abilities to reason contextually. Our findings indicate that the efficacy of fine-tuned biomedical language models is attributable not only to the parametric knowledge acquired during pretraining but also to their capacity to effectively utilize accurate context while disregarding incorrect context. However, language models are capable of producing responses that appear accurate yet are factually incorrect. This poses a significant issue as individuals may place undue trust in erroneous health information, thereby incurring potential risks. Moreover, health-related information is subject to rapid evolution, particularly during periods of crisis, and unverified information disseminates swiftly across digital platforms, thereby influencing the learning processes of these models. Several methodologies have been proposed to enhance models' ability to manage conflicting information. One approach involves modifying the model's knowledge base when discrepancies with the context are identified, while another method involves tracing the reasoning process to ascertain the basis for a model's specific response. However, these approaches predominantly concentrate on either the pre-existing knowledge of the model or the contextual information. An additional approach involves verifying the coherence of information by assessing whether the provided details align logically. Another method predicts two potential answers: one derived from the contextual information and another based on the model’s intrinsic knowledge. Furthermore, a technique ensures the model selects the correct response when presented with conflicting information. Alternative approaches aim to mitigate the generation of incorrect responses by language models. During the training phase, these methods incorporate erroneous or irrelevant information to enhance the models' robustness in processing conflicting or misleading inputs. In the evaluation phase, they employ techniques to identify and suppress inaccuracies, carefully structure queries to minimize ambiguity, and implement multi-reader validation processes to ensure the reliability of the generated outputs. Additionally, the system seeks robust responses to preempt potential adversarial attacks and anticipates which facts may soon become obsolete, thereby avoiding reliance on outdated information. Current methodologies predominantly concentrate on either rectifying misinformation or mitigating undesirable behavior in models. Nonetheless, both aspects are crucial for enhancing the accuracy and reliability of responses in health-related domains.

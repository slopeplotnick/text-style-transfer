Question Making. Following the categorization outlined in Section 2.1, eight domain-specific graduate student experts selected subsets of categories and authored 20 prompts each. Each prompt was designed to require reasoning involving at least two scientific concepts, thereby ensuring a sufficient level of difficulty. These prompts were based on public educational experiment examples to guarantee scientific validity and ensure that each target phenomenon could be feasibly represented in a video of no more than 10 seconds. Among these contributors, two graduate students also curated 40 I2V questions, each paired with a reference video that depicted the target experiment as the ground truth. The reference videos were sourced from public datasets and online resources, with a key frame extracted from the initial segment used as the conditional input image to illustrate the complete experimental setup. Panel Review and Quality Check. After drafting the full test suite, all participating graduate students convened as a review panel. The panel meticulously cross-checked each expert's set of 20 prompts to verify specificity, procedural accuracy, and clarity of the expected outcomes. As a final quality assurance step, we employed capable video generation models, Sora-2 and Wan-2.5-T2V-Preview, to synthesize the opening seconds of each prompt, confirming that the key experimental components and setups were accurately captured. VLM-as-a-Judge. Recent studies have shown that powerful VLMs can produce human-aligned judgments when carefully prompted and calibrated. In VideoScience-Bench, we adopt a VLM-as-a-Judge framework and examine the performance gap between reasoning-oriented VLMs and non-reasoning models such as GPT-4o. We find that directly prompting VLMs, particularly non-reasoning ones, to evaluate video generations often results in under-justified assessments and false-positive ratings. This observation motivates the design of a quantitative auto-evaluation harness featuring a checklist-based grading scheme, where each rating must be supported by salient video frames and an evidence table aggregated from computer-vision analysis of the videos.

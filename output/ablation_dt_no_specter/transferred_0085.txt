Our findings indicate that individuals' attitudes toward AI significantly influence their perception of AI-generated music, extending beyond mere auditory characteristics. Therefore, it is imperative to develop assessment tools specifically tailored to AI music and to devise novel evaluative frameworks. With the increasing prevalence of AI-generated music on streaming platforms, socioeconomic considerations of fairness and transparency in the dissemination and reception of AI-produced content are essential. Given the impact of individuals' perceptions of AI on their music preferences and emotional responses, experimental designs could explore communication strategies regarding the creation and dissemination processes of AI-generated music. For instance, transparency of the training data or the allocation of revenue generated from AI music could be modeled as experimental conditions. Additionally, it is essential to investigate the various production and application contexts of AI music in greater depth. The collaborative creation of music by human and AI systems is garnering increasing interest, as contemporary text-to-music technologies facilitate co-creative processes. Moreover, building upon the research of Moura and Maw (2021), it is pertinent to examine the perception and functional application of AI-generated music. Our study indicates that attitudes toward AI significantly influence the evaluation of AI-generated music, surpassing the impact of composer identity, genre, and individual listening habits. Looking ahead, it will be crucial to observe how general attitudes toward artificial intelligence evolve, as these will significantly influence its acceptance or rejection within cultural domains such as music. More broadly, the central issue extends beyond the question of whether AI-generated music can be appreciated or perceived as human-like; it concerns the broader conceptualization of what constitutes "authentic" and "valuable" creativity. By critically analyzing audience perspectives, we can better determine the forms of (artificial) creativity that warrant recognition and the conditions under which such recognition should occur.

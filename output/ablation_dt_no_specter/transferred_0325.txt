Agentic systems are modern software architectures: they consist of integrated modules, expose interfaces, and are implemented within software pipelines. Unlike conventional programs, their operational processes are inherently stochastic and adaptive, enabling them to solve problems through dynamic methodologies. However, the evaluation of these systems has predominantly been outcome-centric, focusing on success or failure at the final stage. This narrow focus overlooks the nuanced processes of reasoning, planning, action, and iterative adaptation that occur throughout their execution. To address this gap, we developed GRAPHECTORY, which elucidates the temporal and semantic interconnections within these systems. GRAPHECTORY facilitates the establishment of metrics and methodologies for evaluating their efficacy beyond mere success. Utilizing GRAPHECTORY, we analyzed 4000 trajectories of two predominant agentic programming methodologies, namely SWE-agent and OpenHands, in conjunction with four principal Large Language Models (LLMs), with the objective of addressing SWE-bench Verified issues. Our automated analyses revealed that: (1) agents employing more sophisticated prompts or more robust LLMs exhibit more intricate GRAPHECTORY, indicative of deeper cognitive processing, broader contextual assimilation, and more rigorous validation prior to patch submission; (2) the problem-solving strategies of agents are contingent upon both the complexity of the problem and the specific LLM utilized—for issues that are resolved, the strategies typically adhere to coherent localization-patching-validation sequences, whereas unresolved issues manifest chaotic, repetitive, or regressive behaviors; (3) even when successful, agentic programming systems frequently demonstrate inefficiencies, resulting in unnecessarily protracted trajectories. Agentic systems utilizing large language models (LLMs) have emerged as a promising approach for addressing complex, multi-step tasks across various domains, including software engineering, web browsing, virtual assistance, scientific discovery, and robotic navigation. Analogous to traditional software systems, agentic systems are composed of components such as LLMs, tools, APIs, and memory, and are characterized by inputs, internal states, execution logic, and outputs. However, rather than adhering to a deterministic implementation, these systems generate trajectories—sequential processes of reasoning, action, and observation—that collectively constitute the execution logic necessary for task completion. The assessment of agentic software systems has predominantly focused on the outcome, specifically evaluating whether the generated trajectory successfully resolves the given problem.

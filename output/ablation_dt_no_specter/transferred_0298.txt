To effectively reason over long videos, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods have been developed to retrieve relevant frames or clips instead of randomly sampling them. These methods utilize textual and visual cues to retrieve query-relevant information, allowing the model to focus on crucial clips. Recent advancements have extended this approach by introducing graph-based methods to encode multimodal interactions across video segments. However, these models often rely heavily on textual information or simplistic visual-textual comparisons, which limits their ability to perform holistic understanding and complex reasoning over long video sequences. To overcome these limitations, memory-based techniques have emerged, constructing structured knowledge over video streams. For instance, EgoRAG organizes hierarchical textual memories from egocentric video streams, facilitating reasoning over day-long activities. Building on this, Ego-R1 incorporates vision-centric methodologies with iterative reasoning for long-horizon tasks. HippoMM introduces a dual-process memory system that integrates semantic summaries with multimodal cues, enhancing the model's ability to process complex information. Similarly, M3-Agent employs a memory architecture centered on entity representation and iterative reasoning to retrieve pertinent information. Despite these advances, existing methodologies still face challenges in fully integrating multimodal information and adapting to varying temporal scales within complex video scenarios. To address these challenges, we propose WorldMM, a novel framework that leverages both textual and visual contexts from video streams to build a multimodal memory system for comprehensive understanding and reasoning over long videos. The system operates in three stages: initially, it constructs multiple memories, including two textual memories and one visual memory. Subsequently, it employs an iterative retrieval process to gather query-relevant information from these memories across various temporal scales until sufficient knowledge is amassed. Finally, this information is used to generate a response.

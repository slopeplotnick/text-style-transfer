Forecasting stock prices remains a formidable challenge due to the market's inherent volatility and sensitivity to real-time events. The emergence of large language models (LLMs) has introduced innovative approaches to text-based stock price prediction. While LLMs demonstrate strong text comprehension capabilities, their effective application to complex real-world scenarios often requires specialized memory modules to ensure consistent and stable utilization of relevant historical information. Although significant efforts have been devoted to developing such memory systems, they face notable difficulties when processing the immense volume of unstructured online news data. This data often lacks explicit explanations for price fluctuations and is saturated with irrelevant information. Consequently, the central challenge lies in autonomously identifying and extracting the critical information and underlying logic that genuinely influence stock price movements from this noisy text. To address this issue, this paper presents an event-reflection dual-layer memory framework. In the knowledge construction phase, the framework conducts detailed mining of structured events along two dimensions: horizontal event consolidation synthesizes essential daily event content to provide a comprehensive snapshot, while longitudinal event tracing monitors the progression of events over time. This dual-layer approach captures incremental information that reflects shifts in market expectations, ultimately constructing a temporally-aware structured event knowledge base. Additionally, by examining the dynamic interplay between event patterns and stock price fluctuations, the framework refines stock analysis knowledge to build a reflection knowledge base, thereby achieving systematic and autonomous knowledge organization. During the knowledge application phase, the framework operates through two key steps: retrieval and reasoning. First, the recent event sequence is used as a semantic query to retrieve semantically similar historical scenarios from the memory network, offering precise references for current decision-making. Subsequently, in the reasoning phase, the model integrates the recent event sequence, incremental information, and retrieved historical examples to produce reliable stock price predictions. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms traditional memory architectures in predictive accuracy and provides enhanced interpretability. It effectively traces the information chain influencing stock prices and elucidates the mechanisms underlying its predictions.

The primary objective of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) is to elucidate the rationale behind a model's predictions. However, the motivations for providing such explanations may vary. These include evaluating the model's performance, increasing user trust in its decision-making processes, and aiding users in understanding how a decision can be altered in situations where decisions are not permanent. The latter requires a special type of explanation, where the explanatory framework is constrained to actionable elements within the given context. This is known as algorithmic recourse, which refers to the process of elucidating potential interventions to alter a model's decision. It necessitates reliable causal modeling to accurately predict the outcomes of proposed interventions, focusing solely on the consequences following the actual implementation of these interventions. The type of explanation discussed in this paper is intended to be general, devoid of any predetermined explainee, and refrains from presupposing the model's performance or deployment stage. This approach ensures that explanations remain faithful to the model, even when the model's behavior cannot be anticipated or assumed to align with expectations. Ensuring that explanations accurately reflect the model's operations, even when the model's performance deviates from expectations, is crucial. This approach facilitates retrospective analysis of alternative actions without concern for the feasibility of implementing such changes. Although this methodology could potentially be adapted for algorithmic recourse, such adaptations are beyond the scope of this paper. Concept-based explanations employ broader ideas or characteristics that possess human interpretability, rather than relying on specific data features. Various definitions of these concepts exist within the realm of XAI; however, in this context, the term refers to symbolic concepts as delineated by Poeta et al. [2025]. These symbolic concepts encompass high-level ideas or traits inherent in the data that are comprehensible to individuals.

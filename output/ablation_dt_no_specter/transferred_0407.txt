Flow-based diffusion models have emerged as a leading paradigm for training generative models across images and videos. However, their mechanisms for balancing memorization and generalization remain insufficiently explored. In this study, we revisit the flow matching (FM) objective and investigate its marginal velocity field, which admits a closed-form expression, allowing exact computation of the oracle FM target. Analyzing this oracle velocity field reveals that these models inherently possess a two-stage training objective: an early stage guided by a mixture of data modes and a later stage dominated by the nearest data sample. This bifurcated objective leads to distinct learning dynamics: the initial phase facilitates the model's comprehension of diverse data modalities to establish overarching patterns, whereas the subsequent phase increasingly emphasizes the memorization of intricate details. Leveraging these insights, we elucidate the efficacy of specific methodologies, such as timestep-shifted schedules, classifier-free guidance intervals, and strategic latent space design choices. Our study enhances the understanding of diffusion model training dynamics and proposes principles for guiding future architectural and algorithmic improvements. Diffusion models have emerged as a formidable class of generative techniques, capable of synthesizing high-fidelity samples across various domains, such as images and videos. These models acquire complex data distributions by progressively transforming an initial distribution, such as Gaussian, into the target data distribution through a process parameterized by denoising or transport directions. Various formulations have been proposed to characterize the diffusion process, including probabilistic models, score-based ODEs/SDEs, and flow matching. These perspectives give rise to diverse training objectives, such as noise, score, or velocity prediction, and correspond to different parameterizations of the probability flow ODE. Recent advances have largely converged on the flow matching formulation, wherein the model is trained to predict the velocity field under a linear schedule. Due to its simplicity and robust training dynamics, this approach has been established as the prevailing standard for training state-of-the-art diffusion models.

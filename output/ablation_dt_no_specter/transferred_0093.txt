Meanwhile, a significant body of research has been dedicated to elucidating the operational mechanisms of diffusion models, focusing particularly on the balance between memorization (i.e., the tendency to replicate training samples) and generalization (i.e., the ability to generate novel samples). Previous investigations have approached this phenomenon by deriving quantitative metrics to assess model behavior and by analyzing the underlying factors contributing to memorization and generalization. Most studies primarily concentrate on model behaviors during the initial sampling phase and validate their hypotheses using datasets of limited scale and resolution, such as FFHQ and CIFAR-10. While these insights prove beneficial in scenarios characterized by limited data availability, the propensity for replication diminishes as the dataset size increases, as exemplified by ImageNet. Song et al. observed that initiating the generative process at an earlier stage with extensive datasets facilitates the creation of novel images, whereas commencing at a later stage tends to reproduce existing ones. Consequently, a critical inquiry arises: what mechanisms govern this equilibrium in models trained on substantial datasets? To investigate this, we examined the objectives of the model's training process. We revisited flow matching (FM) and conditional flow matching (CFM), which are traditionally considered challenging due to the absence of specific requisite data. However, we demonstrated that under a Gaussian prior and a finite training set, the flow can be precisely determined. This approach facilitates the determination of the optimal trajectory for the model at any given stage. Analyzing this trajectory reveals that the training process can be broadly divided into two primary phases. During the initial phase, the model integrates information from diverse data sources to navigate its developmental pathway. Subsequently, the focus narrows to a singular data point. Furthermore, our analysis indicates that the timing of this transition is significantly influenced by the size of the dataset and the corresponding sample distribution.

SMP serves as a modular framework for training motion policies by providing style-conditioned rewards, eliminating the need for access to the original motion dataset. This design enables the application of various adaptation techniques to refine the diffusion-based reward model, thereby shaping the motion prior. However, these adjustments can occasionally result in unnatural behaviors, as highlighted in the supplementary video comparing different methodologies. Moreover, SMP facilitates the creation of novel motion styles by manipulating the outputs of the pretrained diffusion model, allowing for the synthesis of new styles without requiring additional model training. For example, as illustrated in Figure 6, two distinct styles, such as "AeroPlane" and "HighKnees," can be blended to produce a new composite style. This blending process integrates the style-conditioned predictions from the pretrained model. Furthermore, a single pretrained motion model can be repurposed to train policies for diverse tasks, including steering, target acquisition, and activities like dodgeball, as demonstrated in Table 2. The motion model is trained on a subset of the LaFAN1 dataset, which features unstructured running behaviors. The resulting policies achieve high task performance while maintaining natural motion. As shown in Figures 5(a) and 5(b), the character dynamically transitions between various gaits to preserve both task efficacy and motion authenticity. For instance, the steering policy autonomously initiates a backward jog when the character’s orientation opposes the target direction and transitions to a forward jog upon alignment. Additionally, it learns lateral movements, such as side-stepping and cross-stepping, without relying on an explicit motion planner. In the Target Location task, the policy trained with SMP also demonstrates human-like behaviors, such as adaptively decelerating when necessary.

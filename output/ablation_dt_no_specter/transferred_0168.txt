Several studies have since explored more structured prompting paradigms. For example, Plan-and-Solve (Wang et al., 2023c) explicitly decomposes tasks into a planning phase and a solution phase, leading to stronger outcomes on mathematical and logical tasks. Similarly, Prompt Chaining (Chen et al., 2023) examines whether linking intermediate summaries or steps can improve performance in text summarization tasks. Moreover, Complexity-based Prompting (Fu et al., 2023) suggests that task complexity should guide prompt formulation, an idea closely aligned with our focus on prompt specificity. While most prior research emphasizes task format or exemplar inclusion, our study uniquely investigates prompt specificity as a continuous dimension, systematically varying its detail and structure. Furthermore, recent work has introduced methods for refining prompts, either through automated processes or human-in-the-loop strategies. For instance, Black-box optimization methods (Diao et al., 2023) adjust prompts using task-specific feedback without requiring access to model gradients. Prompt Engineering a Prompt Engineer (Zhou et al., 2023) demonstrates that large language models can iteratively improve their own prompts through metaprompting, a strategy analogous to the generalization loop explored in this study. Prompt Refinement with Image Pivots (Lu et al., 2023) employs a cross-modal framework, leveraging visual features to refine textual prompts for enhanced image generation. Although our work is confined to textual data, it shares the overarching goal of refining prompts to improve model performance. In contrast to these approaches, DETAIL specifically examines the impact of textual specificity on model reasoning, rather than exploring semantic shifts or integrating multimodal data. Reviews such as Liu et al. (2023) further highlight this distinction, surveying reasoning-focused prompting techniques and revealing a growing taxonomy of strategies and formats. Nevertheless, there remains limited research on how the granularity or phrasing detail of prompts affects model behavior across tasks and systems. While methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Plan-and-Solve underscore the importance of prompt content, they do not delve into the nuanced role of specificity in shaping model reasoning.

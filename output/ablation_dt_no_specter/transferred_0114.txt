PEFT-FACTORY also implements the calculation of classification and efficiency metrics during the prediction phase, alongside pre-existing token accuracy and semantic similarity metrics, such as Rouge (Lin, 2004) and Bleu (Papineni et al., 2002). For classification metrics, PEFT-FACTORY employs the standard Accuracy and F1 metrics. To assess efficiency during the evaluation of PEFT methods, it utilizes the PSCP metric (Balanec et al., 2025), a highly adaptable metric that considers various efficiency parameters, including the number of parameters, memory usage, and inference time. These metrics are implemented within the train/sft/metric.py file for supervised fine-tuning, adhering to the framework established by LLaMA-Factory and utilizing distinct data classes for each metric, specifically named ComputeAccuracy, ComputeF1, and ComputePSCP. In the context of classification, the training parameters incorporate a binary flag, compute_classification.metrics, which can be activated or deactivated to facilitate the inclusion of classification metrics. Similarly, efficiency metrics are governed by a separate parameter, compute_pscp. Additional details regarding the efficiency metrics are provided in Appendix B.3. The extensibility of PEFT methods and datasets, together with a ready-to-use, controlled, and stable environment, is a key feature of PEFT-FACTORY that aims to promote further research on PEFT methods. To demonstrate its contribution to reproducibility and benchmarking, two illustrative examples are presented. The modular architecture of PEFT-FACTORY facilitates the development and evaluation of novel PEFT methods by researchers and practitioners. In the current landscape, the source code accompanying newly developed PEFT methods is frequently either non-functional or challenging to reproduce. Furthermore, developers are often required to independently implement the training and evaluation code, a process that is prone to redundancy, errors, and variability in results.

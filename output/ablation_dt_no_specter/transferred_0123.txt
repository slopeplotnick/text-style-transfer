Mathematical Reasoning. We utilize five benchmarks: AMC, Minerva (Lewkowycz et al., 2022), MATH-500 (Hendrycks et al., 2021), GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021), and Olympiad-Bench (He et al., 2024). For evaluation, we adhere to methodologies from previous studies, employing GPT-4o as a programmatic judge to semantically verify the correctness of model outputs against correct solutions. General Domain Reasoning. To assess the generalization of reasoning abilities, we evaluate models using benchmarks such as MMLU-Pro (Wang et al., 2024), SuperGPQA (Du et al., 2025), GPQA-Diamond (Rein et al.), and BBEH (shoaa kazemi et al., 2025). These benchmarks encompass a diverse range of disciplines, including physics, biology, business, economics, and law, and present challenging tasks that offer a stringent evaluation of complex reasoning skills. For evaluation, we report Exact Match (EM) accuracy obtained via greedy decoding, following Ma et al. (2025). To isolate the contribution of each key component, we conduct a comprehensive ablation study on the Qwen3-8B-Base model, evaluating the impact of disabling specific features on math and general reasoning tasks. Among the ablations, disabling challenger training leads to the largest performance decline, reducing Math and General averages by 1.9 and 1.0 points, respectively. Similarly, removing challenger warm-up and curriculum learning results in comparable performance degradation, indicating the critical role of these components in achieving enhanced outcomes. Notably, mathematical tasks exhibit greater sensitivity compared to general tasks, as shown in Table 1, which highlights a higher variance in mathematical scores depending on the method employed. Overall, while the integration of heterogeneous human data generally enhances performance, domain-specific data sampling proves particularly effective in strengthening performance within the respective domain. This insight is valuable as it allows developers to strategically direct model improvement by selecting particular data types, thereby maintaining the model's focus on intended learning objectives and mitigating the risk of deviation.

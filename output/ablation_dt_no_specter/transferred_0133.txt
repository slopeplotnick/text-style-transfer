Test-time learning (TTL) represents a promising paradigm that enables models to adapt during inference. Early approaches primarily emphasized lightweight adaptation mechanisms that avoided parameter updates, such as entropy minimization or batch normalization (Wang et al., 2020; Niu et al., 2022). More recent advancements have extended these ideas to test-time training (TTT), where explicit parameter updates during inference substantially enhance task-specific adaptability. For instance, Akyurek et al. (2024) demonstrate that test-time training improves few-shot learning under distribution shifts, while Hübotter et al. (2024) propose active fine-tuning techniques that selectively adapt large language models (LLMs) using informative feedback signals. Among these developments, test-time policy optimization has emerged as a particularly promising direction. By framing inference as a reinforcement learning (RL) problem, methods such as RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR) (Wang et al., 2025) and TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025) leverage consensus-based feedback, like majority voting, to guide policy updates. These approaches effectively utilize the "wisdom of the crowd" from multiple rollouts to construct label-free rewards, bridging the gap between reasoning consistency and correctness. Nevertheless, rollout inefficiency remains a critical bottleneck. Standard test-time RL methods often rely on a fixed, large number of rollouts for every query, which is computationally wasteful. While easy queries quickly establish consensus, harder instances require extensive sampling, making this "one-size-fits-all" strategy impractical for deployment. To address this challenge, we introduce Optimal Rollout Allocation for Test-time Policy Optimization (OptPO), a general framework designed to balance efficiency and accuracy. Our key insight is to reframe reward estimation as a sequential hypothesis testing problem. Instead of predefining a fixed sample budget, OptPO adaptively estimates the vote distribution using a Bayesian Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT), halting sampling as soon as posterior evidence satisfies a user-defined error tolerance. The accumulated rollouts are then directly repurposed for policy optimization (e.g., via PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) or GRPO (Shao et al., 2024)), ensuring computational resources are allocated only to areas of high uncertainty. Furthermore, OptPO is broadly compatible with supervised fine-tuning methodologies for test-time training, offering a versatile solution to improve efficiency without compromising accuracy.

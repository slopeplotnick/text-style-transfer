The MODOMA framework consists of two fundamental components: the mother agent and the daughter agent. At the core of this system is Delilah, which functions as the mother agent and serves as an advanced tool for generating and analyzing the Dutch language. Unlike systems that rely on a corpus of examples, Delilah operates based on a predefined set of grammatical rules and structures. These rules act as detailed frameworks that define the structure and function of lexical items and grammatical constructs in Dutch. For example, the framework for the article "de" specifies its morphological form, semantic properties, syntactic role, and integration within sentence structures. Importantly, these frameworks are meticulously developed through extensive linguistic research, ensuring their accuracy and reliability. Using these templates, Delilah constructs phrases and sentences through a process known as unification. This mathematical procedure assesses the compatibility of templates, ensuring no conflicting features, and subsequently merges them into a single coherent template. For instance, the template for the article "de" is specifically designed to combine with noun phrases, as seen in the example "de vrouw" ("the woman"). Moreover, Delilah’s operation aligns with the principles of head-driven phrase structure grammar (HPSG), where lexical items and grammatical rules are treated equivalently within a unified system. To achieve this, Delilah employs a specialized grammatical model called combinatory list grammar, which shares conceptual similarities with frameworks like combinatory categorial grammar (CCG). The operational mechanics of Delilah have been thoroughly analyzed and documented in numerous scholarly publications. Within the MODOMA framework, Delilah is utilized in its original configuration, adhering to its initial design specifications without modification.

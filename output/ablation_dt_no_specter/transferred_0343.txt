Guardrails are systems designed to enforce safety constraints and prevent harmful content during interactions between humans and artificial intelligence. Early approaches primarily relied on rudimentary rule-based filtering mechanisms. While these methods were effective in constrained settings, they struggled to adapt to evolving safety policies and emerging risks, often demonstrating limited coverage and low accuracy. Recent advancements in guardrail systems have leveraged sophisticated AI models, significantly enhancing their flexibility and capacity for generalization. For instance, Llama Guard, an LLM-based moderation model fine-tuned on proprietary safety datasets developed by Meta AI, is specifically designed to safeguard user-AI interactions. Moreover, models such as Llama Guard 3 Vision and LlavaGuard have been introduced to address visual safety concerns, utilizing vision-language models to identify risks in visual content. However, despite these advancements, existing guardrails still face critical limitations in the omni-modality era. First, most prior work has focused on text and image modalities, while video and audio data are often processed using overly simplistic techniques, such as shallow classifiers, which fail to capture contextual nuances and treat safety detection as a binary task. Second, many systems remain limited to single-modality or scenario-specific applications, lacking the ability to integrate and reason across diverse media types, such as text, images, videos, and audio. To overcome these challenges, OMNIGUARD was developed as the first family of omni-modal guardrails capable of performing both unimodal and cross-modal content moderation with deliberate reasoning. By incorporating omni-modal understanding and explicit reasoning, OMNIGUARD delivers consistent, interpretable, and holistic safety evaluations across all modalities.

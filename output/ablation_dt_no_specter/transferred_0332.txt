As proposed by studies [25] and [15], structured moral reasoning benchmarks, such as MoralBench, have been developed to evaluate AI models against human moral intuitions. These investigations revealed that models exhibit partial moral sensitivity but often lack coherence when faced with conflicting principles and contextual ambiguity. However, benchmark-based assessments are constrained by their limited scalability and temporal scope, capturing model ethics at a singular point in time and failing to illustrate how reasoning consistency evolves through interaction and retraining. Subsequent research has explored methodologies to enhance the interpretability of AI systems and their alignment with human values, both of which are pivotal to the development of responsible AI. For instance, Shitong et al. [9] introduced DeNEVIL, a debiasing and explainable alignment framework designed to incorporate normative reasoning into the training of large language models. Similarly, Jiao et al. [16] examined the evaluation of AI ethical reasoning across diverse cultural contexts, emphasizing the interplay between technological design and societal factors in fostering trust and ensuring ethical AI deployment. Despite their strengths in interpretability and trust analysis, these frameworks rely on predefined moral categories and manual annotation processes, which limit their adaptability to dynamic moral contexts. While prior research has advanced fairness assessments and value alignment, a critical gap persists: the development of a versatile framework capable of evaluating moral reasoning longitudinally, independent of specific model architectures. Current approaches predominantly assess ethical considerations at discrete intervals but lack mechanisms to autonomously construct, interpret, and adapt moral scenarios across evolving contexts. Furthermore, existing methodologies do not provide mechanisms to quantify the divergence in AI ethical standards or to monitor moral consistency across models in real time. Drawing upon the foundational concepts delineated in Section 1, the newly introduced Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP) is an autonomous, dataset-independent framework designed to facilitate continuous ethical evaluation of AI systems. Its primary objective is to measure, interpret, and refine the moral stability of large language models. MoCoP is dedicated to the evaluation and interpretation of ethical reasoning, deliberately eschewing optimization or retraining processes to maintain conceptual and methodological clarity.

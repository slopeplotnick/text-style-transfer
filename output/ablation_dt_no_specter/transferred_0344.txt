An initial experiment conducted in the MODOMA laboratory simulation environment was based on a specific correlation. This experiment provides a method to evaluate the efficacy of the MODOMA approach to language acquisition by employing the well-established principle of Zipf's law. Therefore, this study contributes to the ongoing development of research, enabling the gradual evolution of the MODOMA system through the implementation of increasingly complex experiments. As a result, experience with the MODOMA as a laboratory model for language acquisition experiments can be expanded, allowing the system to be tested and refined before the application of additional language acquisition methodologies. Although Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) focus on the emergence of languages, their work shares similarities in applying Zipf's law to enhance the understanding of language models in comparison to human-generated languages. Importantly, this procedure incorporates most of the components and processes required for conducting more intricate acquisition experiments. Consequently, the constituent elements of the MODOMA system, along with their interactions, can be systematically validated and improved to support the execution of further computational language acquisition experiments. If the acquisition process is successful, the daughter agent learns to distinguish between functional and content categories by analyzing examples of the target language provided by the mother agent during their interactions. Significantly, once the daughter agent internalizes this grammatical distinction, the newly acquired knowledge is immediately integrated into its grammatical framework and subsequently used to generate novel utterances during interactions with the mother agent, as well as to parse previously unseen sentences produced by the mother. Thus, this study evaluates whether the MODOMA laboratory approach to language acquisition facilitates the development of substantive grammatical knowledge that can be effectively applied in both parsing and language generation tasks.

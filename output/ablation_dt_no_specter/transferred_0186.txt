To address these challenges, model-agnostic approaches have been developed to compare generated outputs with training data within a shared embedding space, typically leveraging features extracted from pre-trained encoders. While these methods demonstrate efficacy and scalability, they rely on vector representations that pose interpretability challenges, as the underlying semantics remain opaque. Recent advancements, such as LatentExplainer, endeavor to bridge this gap by perturbing latent variables and using language models to elucidate their semantic implications. However, these approaches continue to operate within an opaque framework that limits transparency for end-users. In contrast to these intricate methods, we employ automatically generated Knowledge Graphs (KGs) to trace the influence of data. By leveraging shared triples that encapsulate objects, attributes, and stylistic elements, the comparison of generated and training images facilitates scalable, interpretable, and human-aligned attribution. Large Language Models (LLMs) have emerged as pivotal tools in contemporary KG workflows, enabling structured reasoning over unstructured data. Their contributions are primarily concentrated in three domains: generating subject-predicate-object triples from natural language text, facilitating ontology development, and transforming natural language queries into graph-based representations. Methods such as AutoKG facilitate the transformation of textual data into ontology-aligned triples through prompt-based approaches. Beyond triple generation, LLMs have been utilized for ontology learning and extension by employing strategies such as prompt engineering, ontology reuse, and reasoning over domain-specific concepts. Furthermore, in the domain of question answering, LLMs function as semantic parsers capable of translating user queries into SPARQL statements. This process can be further enhanced by incorporating contextual knowledge graphs or employing controlled natural languages to improve interpretability and precision. Recent surveys on generative KG construction indicate a transition from conventional pipeline architectures to innovative, LLM-driven systems. These advanced systems demonstrate superior performance in entity recognition, relation extraction, and graph population compared to traditional methodologies. The advancements in KG construction provide a robust foundation for addressing data attribution challenges within generative models. Structured outputs from multimodal LLMs offer a comprehensible means to construct interpretable KGs that encapsulate essential elements of both generated and training data, thereby facilitating the tracing of influences. Although the majority of existing research has concentrated on textual data, these methodologies exhibit considerable potential for application to images, notwithstanding the increased complexity inherent in visual content.

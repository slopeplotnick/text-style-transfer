In integrated artificial intelligence (AI) systems, where multiple components collaborate to address complex tasks, large language models (LLMs) play a critical role in influencing downstream decision-making processes. However, biases, especially those stemming from an over-reliance on initial inputs, can compromise the performance and reliability of these systems. Therefore, addressing these biases is essential to ensure equitable and transparent operations. This study faces two primary limitations. Firstly, while the article lengths within each triplet were standardized, the impact of document length on evaluation metrics was not explicitly investigated. Since length may affect metrics like ROUGE-L, BERTScore, and SummaC, future research could categorize datasets by length or conduct controlled analyses to isolate positional effects more effectively. Secondly, our findings demonstrate notably low scores for evaluation metrics overall, highlighting a broader limitation of reference-free evaluation metrics in abstractive summarization, particularly when comparing extensive source documents to succinct summaries. Future research should broaden the scope beyond abortion to include other contentious topics like gun control, LGBTQ+ rights, and immigration, to determine if observed order effects are consistent across various issues. Evaluating diverse LLM families, such as GPT and Claude, would facilitate a comprehensive investigation into the persistence of primacy effects across different model architectures. Moreover, incorporating additional evaluative metrics, including measures of positional stance and indicators of social polarization, could provide a nuanced understanding of order-sensitive behaviors in LLM-generated summaries. Our findings indicate a pronounced primacy effect in multi-document news summarization, with generated summaries aligning more strongly with the initially encountered article, particularly at the semantic level. These results corroborate previous research showing that the sequence of information presentation significantly impacts the output. Such order sensitivity poses challenges for systems like AI summaries or agentic AI configurations, where initial documents disproportionately influence subsequent processes. Consequently, it is imperative to address this positional bias to develop systems that are more equitable, transparent, and dependable.

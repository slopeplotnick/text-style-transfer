OMNIGUARD-7B demonstrates superior performance in addressing safety tasks across textual, visual, and auditory modalities, consistently surpassing other state-of-the-art models. Moreover, the smaller-scale OMNIGUARD-3B achieves competitive results when compared to advanced models such as GPT-4o and Qwen3, highlighting its efficiency despite its reduced size. These findings underscore the efficacy of reasoning-based guardrails in enhancing the reliability and interpretability of moderation processes, representing a significant advancement toward the development of robust and safe artificial intelligence systems. In this work, we introduce OMNIGUARD, a comprehensive suite of models capable of conducting safety assessments across text, images, videos, and audio through advanced reasoning techniques. Additionally, we propose a novel training methodology that significantly enhances the model's ability to articulate its safety-related decisions. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that OMNIGUARD exhibits high accuracy, robust generalization capabilities, and superior explanatory power compared to preceding models. The advent of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has further catalyzed scholarly interest in omni-modal language models (OLLMs), which possess the capability to concurrently process and generate outputs across diverse modalities. Unlike earlier models that relied on separately pretrained unimodal components, OLLMs are trained end-to-end on multimodal datasets, enabling integrated understanding and generation across text, audio, image, and video modalities. By transforming disparate inputs into a unified format, these models enhance their ability to perform reasoning across various data types. For instance, models such as Qwen2.5-Omni and LLaMA-Omni demonstrate real-time, end-to-end generation of both textual and spoken outputs. Extending these functionalities, NExT-OMNI facilitates "any-to-any" cross-modal understanding and generation, encompassing a broader range of data modalities. However, despite these advancements, challenges remain. Issues related to parameter optimization and model architecture can introduce vulnerabilities, potentially leading to safety risks in deployment scenarios.

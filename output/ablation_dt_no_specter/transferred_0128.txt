Automotive AI systems operate under stringent deployment constraints, requiring rapid responses within milliseconds to ensure driver focus and safety. These systems must also adhere to strict power limitations dictated by the vehicle's electrical architecture. Furthermore, they are expected to maintain robust performance under extreme environmental conditions, including varying lighting from bright daylight to complete darkness, temperature ranges from freezing cold to excessive heat, mechanical vibrations, and diverse user interactions characterized by varying behavioral patterns. To meet these demands, NPU-native architectures are employed, enabling low-latency, high-accuracy performance even under quantized inference. The training of these systems leverages the Infinity-MM dataset, a large-scale, high-quality multimodal instruction dataset comprising approximately 44.8 million samples. This dataset is meticulously curated to support a wide range of vision-language tasks, including visual question answering for everyday scenarios, object recognition, scene understanding, document analysis (e.g., forms and receipts), chart and diagram interpretation, optical character recognition, multi-turn conversations, and specialized applications such as medical imaging, autonomous driving, and industrial inspection. Each sample includes an image, task-specific instructions, and the corresponding expected outputs, ensuring comprehensive training and evaluation across diverse domains. Notably, the dataset emphasizes generating detailed, multi-step reasoning responses rather than simple label predictions, a critical feature for automotive AI systems that must articulate their decision-making processes transparently to foster user trust. Training follows a structured four-stage curriculum as outlined in the Infinity-MM protocol, progressively advancing from basic image-text associations to more complex instruction-following tasks with increasing diversity and resolution. In the second stage, the model focuses on general visual understanding tasks, such as object recognition, scene comprehension, and basic visual question answering. At this stage, all system components, including the vision encoder and language model, are systematically optimized. Training begins with a learning rate of 1e-5 for one epoch, using a batch size of 16 during the initial 600 steps to prevent gradient instability. Subsequently, the batch size is increased to an effective size of 512 through gradient accumulation, enhancing training efficiency.

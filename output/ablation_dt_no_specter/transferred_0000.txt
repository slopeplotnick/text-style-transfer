The Long-Tail Hard subset was designed to present challenging data to assess the proficiency of advanced language models in handling complex tasks. We focused on identifying difficult cases, categorizing them into explicit demands, such as Multi-Attribute and Multi-Requirement, and implicit demands. The implicit demands are further divided into four distinct categories: Negation, Alternatives, Knowledge-dependent, and Reasoning-dependent. The methodologies employed to identify these cases were tailored accordingly. For Negation and Alternatives, specific keywords and patterns were utilized. However, for Knowledge-dependent and Reasoning-dependent cases, which are comparatively infrequent, the process began with the elimination of straightforward examples. Subsequently, a robust language model, Qwen3-235B, was employed to ascertain whether it could consistently produce the same response in four out of eight attempts. In addressing multi-attribute queries, a systematic approach was employed to identify instances containing more than five attributes. To ensure the complexity of these examples, they were evaluated using a rudimentary model, retaining only those instances where the model achieved a correct response in five or fewer out of eight attempts, thereby indicating a significant level of difficulty. The Visual Salience subset pertains to scenarios wherein the primary product image assumes critical importance, particularly when visual attributes such as color or style are pivotal in the decision-making process. Initially, we employed a keyword-based retrieval method utilizing a comprehensive visual taxonomy to identify relevant cases. To mitigate inaccuracies, we implemented a dual filtering process: first, by applying a predefined blacklist, and second, by retaining only those instances where the pivotal visual keyword was absent from the product's textual description. Subsequently, a language model was employed to verify that the keywords authentically pertained to visual attributes. Finally, to identify the most challenging examples, we trained a naive multimodal relevance model that integrates textual and visual inputs. We selected instances characterized by high prediction uncertainty, operationalized as a lack of consensus in the model's predictions, with fewer than four consistent outcomes across eight iterations.

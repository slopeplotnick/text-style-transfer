In this section, we will exemplify the explanatory process previously discussed by employing an example. We utilize the CelebA dataset [Liu et al., 2015], which contains over 200,000 images of celebrities' faces. Each image is annotated with binary attributes describing various facial features and accessories, such as whether the individual is smiling, wearing glasses, of young age, or perceived as attractive. We will consider two tasks: determining whether an individual depicted in an image is classified as young and assessing their level of attractiveness. Next, we turn to the images in Figure 6. Starting with the original image on the left, the figure illustrates various counterfactual versions of the same image, all modified to reflect the intervention do(Young = 0), thereby rendering the individual not young. The initial altered image was generated utilizing a specific methodology. While visual-only systems can sometimes be misleading, saliency maps remain widely employed for attribution-based explanations of image classification systems. Therefore, we undertake a comparative analysis of our causal explanations with those provided by Grad-CAM, using the implementation developed by Gildenblat. Consider first the results in Figure 7, which are intended to elucidate the rationale behind the classification of the two images on the far left of Figure 5 as young. For the male subject, the chin area was pivotal in the classification process, whereas for the female subject, the area surrounding the eyes was of primary significance. Subsequently, Figure 8 examines the classification of the individual depicted in Figure 6. The initial image was accurately classified as "young," with the Grad-CAM visualization indicating that the classifier primarily relied on the region surrounding the eyes to reach this conclusion. Conversely, for the image on the right, which was misclassified, the Grad-CAM explanation reveals that the classifier predominantly focused on the forehead and chin regions. Grad-CAM provides non-causal explanations and therefore fails at giving counterfactual information. Instead, it highlights the regions of the image that are most influential in the classifier's decision-making process. Furthermore, as Grad-CAM explanations are inherently pixel-based, they do not offer insights into the underlying reasons why these specific regions are deemed significant.

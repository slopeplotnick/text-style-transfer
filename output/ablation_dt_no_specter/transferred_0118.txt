Recent research has investigated diverse methodologies to enhance the reasoning and verification capabilities of models. One prominent line of inquiry emphasizes decomposing reasoning processes into smaller, incremental steps with rigorous verification applied at each stage, alongside the integration of reward models to improve mathematical reasoning. For instance, one study explored the use of a verifier to evaluate final outputs—referred to as the GV-Gap—demonstrating its potential to enhance performance, particularly when the solver and verifier are instantiated as the same model. However, prior work has largely focused on foundational models, often excluding those subjected to post-training enhancements. More recently, there has been growing interest in optimizing computational resources during the testing phase with verifiers. For example, some researchers have combined random sampling with self-verification, while others have explored parallel sampling with error correction. Additionally, comparative analyses have examined the Self-Consistency approach versus the use of a generative verifier. Despite these advancements, verifiers remain fallible, as they can endorse incorrect solutions, reject valid reasoning processes, or select suboptimal outcomes. These limitations, discussed further in Appendix A, underscore the need for continued refinement. Another critical area of investigation pertains to models’ ability to enhance their own performance and critically assess their outputs. While some scholars remain skeptical, others have proposed theoretical frameworks and empirical evidence suggesting that models can refine their capabilities through improved comprehension. Notably, smaller models may require more robust verification mechanisms to facilitate their advancement. Practical approaches to self-improvement include leveraging natural language feedback (Madaan et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2023) or explicitly training models to address their own errors (Welleck et al., 2023). Furthermore, external tools such as code interpreters (Zhou et al., 2024) have been employed to iteratively refine generated solutions, offering additional pathways for improvement.

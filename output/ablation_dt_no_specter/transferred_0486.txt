The implementation of robotics in the construction sector holds substantial potential to address workforce shortages while simultaneously enhancing safety and efficiency. Robots exhibit exceptional proficiency in executing heavy and repetitive tasks, demonstrating resilience against physical fatigue and cognitive errors. Consequently, their deployment can significantly accelerate construction processes and reduce associated costs. However, due to the inherent complexity of construction tasks, individual robots are unable to perform all necessary operations independently. For instance, constructing a wall requires a diverse range of specialized capabilities, including material transportation, precise placement, welding, and quality inspection. Therefore, the effective execution of such tasks necessitates the coordinated operation of multiple robots. Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) involves decomposing complex tasks into manageable subtasks, which are then allocated to various robots based on their capabilities, task requirements, and environmental conditions. MRTA challenges are characterized by the differentiation between single-task and multi-task robots, single-robot and multi-robot tasks, as well as instantaneous and time-extended assignments. The most complex scenarios involve coordinating numerous robots to execute multiple tasks over extended periods, necessitating sophisticated planning to accommodate the diverse capabilities of robots, the specific demands of tasks, and temporal constraints. However, the majority of MRTA research has predominantly concentrated on applications within structured environments such as warehouses or exploratory settings, with comparatively limited attention directed toward the complexities of construction domains.

In this section, we establish the framework used throughout this study. We define the datasets, solvers, and verifiers, introduce the metrics used to evaluate solver and verifier behaviors, and specify the verification settings in our empirical analysis. Notably, verifier gain is an asymptotic metric that reflects the theoretical limit of performance improvement achievable through verifier-based rejection sampling. Therefore, it serves as a benchmark for comparing the effectiveness of different verification mechanisms. To organize our models systematically, we group them into families, such as Llama3 or Qwen2.5, where each family comprises related models of varying scales. However, due to the distinct operational characteristics between base and post-trained models, we treat them as separate families. For instance, the base model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-70B is categorized under the Llama3-Base family, while the post-trained model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct belongs to the Llama3 family. Furthermore, each solver-verifier pairing is classified into one of three verification settings. First, in self-verification, the solver and verifier are the same model, meaning the model evaluates its own solutions. For example, when a 70B Llama3 model functions as both the solver and verifier, the verification metrics, such as accuracy, are computed based on this single pairing. Second, in intra-family verification, the verifier assesses solutions generated by other models within the same family. For instance, a 70B Llama3 verifier may evaluate outputs from 8B or 13B Llama3 solvers, with the reported metric averaged across all such within-family solvers, excluding self-verification cases. Finally, in cross-family verification, the verifier evaluates solutions produced by models from different families. For example, a base Llama3 verifier may assess outputs from Qwen3 or from a post-trained Llama3 model, with the reported metric averaged across all such cross-family solvers.

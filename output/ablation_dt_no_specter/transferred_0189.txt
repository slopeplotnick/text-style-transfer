To optimize the objective function defined in Equation 13, which maximizes the advantage-weighted log probability expectation, we employ the GRPO algorithm. The importance sampling ratio and advantage function are further detailed in Equation 14. In our implementation, we decided to omit the KL divergence regularization term. This choice was informed by the observation that, following sufficient SFT warm-start, the model had already established a robust reasoning paradigm and a well-structured output format. At this stage, introducing KL constraints would overly restrict the policy exploration space, thereby impeding the model’s ability to discover improved reasoning pathways. Consequently, we opted to enable freer policy optimization, leveraging the model’s existing capabilities. Through a carefully designed reward mechanism, we pursue three key training objectives: (1) Format standardization: employing special tokens to clearly demarcate the reasoning process from the final answer, which facilitates subsequent parsing and application; (2) Output space constraint: strictly confining mismatch types (e.g., "price mismatch," "functionality mismatch") to a predefined set, thereby preventing the generation of arbitrary outputs; (3) Progressive reward: providing moderate positive reinforcement when the model correctly identifies relevance but errs at finer-grained levels, which mitigates the sparse reward issue in early training stages, accelerates convergence, and stabilizes training. Building on this, we implemented a three-stage curriculum learning framework to systematically enhance the model’s capabilities by progressively increasing the complexity of training data. The sample allocation strategy for each stage is outlined in Table 6. This framework is guided by three core principles: (1) Rapid Bootstrap Principle: Stage 1 predominantly utilizes medium-difficulty samples, allowing the model to quickly gain effective positive feedback while maintaining a suitable level of challenge, thus avoiding exploration difficulties caused by sparse rewards during early training; (2) Progressive Challenge Principle: Stages 2 and 3 gradually increase the proportion of high-difficulty samples, guiding the model’s transition from addressing common cases to tackling complex edge cases, thereby enabling continuous expansion of its capability boundaries; (3) Knowledge Retention Principle: The latter two stages consistently include easy samples as "memory anchors" to prevent the model from forgetting previously acquired skills while assimilating new knowledge. Additionally, these high-confidence samples help stabilize gradient variance during the training process.

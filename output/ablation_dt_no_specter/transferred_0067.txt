In this context, Large Language Models (LLMs) typically exhibit superior performance across a broad spectrum of tasks due to their substantial size and enhanced ability to adhere to instructions. However, Smaller Language Models (SLMs) can achieve comparable or even superior outcomes in specific domains, such as the prediction of depression and stress. Despite significant advancements in other areas, SLMs still fall short of the performance levels demonstrated by LLMs (Xu et al., 2024). The efficacy of SLMs is contingent upon the alignment of task requirements with their inherent linguistic processing capabilities. Nonetheless, a significant limitation persists: SLMs exhibit deficiencies in processing extended input sequences due to token-length constraints and diminished proficiency in maintaining extended contextual dependencies (Kumar, 2025). Consequently, their utility is restricted in scenarios necessitating the comprehension of lengthy or intricate narratives, wherein LLMs demonstrate superior performance (Lu et al., 2024). These findings underscore the importance of prioritizing model optimization over mere expansion in the development of mental-health AI systems. As efficiency, transparency, safety, and user privacy (Zhang et al., 2021) gain prominence within clinical machine learning, the interplay between model performance and practical applicability is poised to shape the evolution of digital mental-health interventions. Moreover, SLMs have demonstrated their capacity to address the growing demand for real-time, privacy-preserving detection of mental-health conditions (Harari et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2018), while simultaneously aligning with broader imperatives for transparency and accountability in healthcare AI (Wies et al., 2021; Smith et al., 2023). The present study demonstrates that fine-tuned SLMs, when trained with integrative strategies utilizing data from diverse sources, can achieve predictive performance on par with or surpassing that of LLMs in mental health outcome prediction. This finding indicates that smaller, more specialized models may represent a more sustainable and ethical alternative for real-world mental health monitoring. Therefore, it is pertinent to delineate the specific tasks for which SLMs are optimally suited. This study demonstrates that cross-dataset training is not only effective but advantageous for predicting depression, stress, and suicidal ideation, particularly when integrated with an adaptive loss function. These results align with the findings of Yao et al. (Benton et al., 2017), which highlight that leveraging shared patterns across related mental health prediction tasks can enhance model performance. In contrast to their multi-task setup, our approach achieves comparable advantages through a more resource-efficient fine-tuning methodology, specifically utilizing LoRA, which significantly reduces computational and memory requirements. This demonstrates that compact, lightweight models are capable of effectively leveraging diverse datasets when appropriately guided by well-defined training objectives.

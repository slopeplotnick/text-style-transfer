What aspects do individuals consider when evaluating AI-generated content? Current research methodologies often rely on rating scales adapted from related fields; however, these instruments may not sufficiently capture the intricacies of how people perceive and assess AI-generated music. Therefore, this study employs an exploratory approach to uncover the subjective criteria and themes underlying participants' judgments through open-ended responses. The music samples used in this study were generated using AI tools, specifically Stable Audio and Suno. To ensure ecological validity, these samples were informed by the Emotion-to-Music Atlas (EMMA), a comprehensive resource detailing the relationship between musical elements and emotional responses. Furthermore, the underlying data were derived from the Music4All-Onion database, which includes a diverse collection of musical works categorized into five primary genres, each further divided into three subgenres: Electronic (Trance, Downtempo, Dubstep), Jazz (Bossa-Nova, Smooth-Jazz, Swing), Pop (Europop, Indie-Pop, Pop-Punk), Rock (Soft-Rock, Rock Ballad, Doom-Metal), and Soul/Funk (Funk, Disco, Soul). For each subgenre, prompts were constructed based on the predominant emotional dimensions identified in the EMMA, utilizing mean values across all songs within the subgenre and their corresponding GEMS-9 descriptors. This process yielded 2-3 prompts per subgenre, resulting in a total of 38 distinct prompts. These prompts were subsequently adapted for implementation in Suno and Stable Audio, generating a final set of 76 audio stimuli. To account for the properties of each tool, the following approach was used: For Suno, prompts were generated in instrumental mode, and one of two outputs was randomly selected and truncated to a duration of 45-60 seconds to ensure consistency with previous studies. For Stable Audio, the term 'instrumental' was incorporated into each prompt, with a target duration of 60 seconds. Further information regarding the creation and editing of these samples is available in the supplementary materials.

We evaluated these methods on the BrowseComp benchmark and observed that context management during testing significantly enhances model performance by enabling the utilization of increased computational resources and facilitating additional execution steps as required. For instance, the Summary method extends the average number of steps to 364, achieving a performance improvement of up to 60.2%. However, its overall efficiency remains relatively low. In contrast, despite its simplicity, the Discard-all method demonstrates strong efficiency and scalability, achieving a score of 67.6, which is comparable to parallel scaling while utilizing significantly fewer steps. In summary, test-time compute can be scaled either sequentially through context management or in parallel, both effectively enhancing the model's problem-solving capabilities. However, these strategies differ in terms of efficiency and scalability, making it essential to carefully consider actual compute costs when evaluating model performance. Moreover, identifying the optimal combination of sequential and parallel scaling to balance efficiency and scalability remains a key area for future research. In this study, we introduced DeepSeek-V3.2, a framework that successfully reconciles computational efficiency with advanced reasoning capabilities. By leveraging the DeepSeek Architecture (DSA), we addressed critical computational complexity without compromising the ability to handle long-context scenarios. With an augmented computational budget, DeepSeek-V3.2 achieves performance comparable to GPT-5 on reasoning benchmarks. Furthermore, the integration of our large-scale agentic task synthesis pipeline significantly enhances tool-use proficiency, unlocking new possibilities for the development of robust and generalizable AI systems utilizing open LLMs. Additionally, our high-performance variant, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, has been validated through gold-medal achievements in prestigious competitions such as the IMO and IOI, representing a significant milestone for open LLMs. Nevertheless, we acknowledge certain limitations when compared to cutting-edge closed-source models such as Gemini-3.0-Pro. Specifically, due to a reduced number of total training FLOPs, the breadth of general knowledge encompassed by DeepSeek-V3.2 remains inferior to that of leading proprietary models.

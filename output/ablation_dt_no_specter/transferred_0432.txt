Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has significantly enhanced the performance of large language models on knowledge-intensive tasks. However, most existing systems are predominantly oriented towards generic or news-centric domains, exhibiting limited efficacy in high-stakes applications such as agricultural climate adaptation. While recent studies have attempted to integrate document retrieval and climate data for domain-specific inquiries, they continue to rely on rudimentary methodologies that fail to provide region-specific and verifiable answers. Geography-aware question answering has been investigated in various domains; however, these methodologies are not specifically designed for climate reasoning. Moreover, existing research tends to treat climate data and literature as disparate entities, lacking a cohesive approach. CAIRNS is a comprehensive framework comprising four distinct stages: (1) location-weighted retrieval of climate literature from the Elsevier platform and data acquisition from a climate data API; (2) answer generation utilizing the ScholarGuide Prompt; (3) answer verification; and (4) hybrid evaluation. To identify the appropriate documents, we developed a retriever that integrates both sparse lexical matching and dense semantic retrieval. However, relying solely on this method is inadequate due to the significant influence of geographical context on the relevance of climate-related inquiries. Therefore, we incorporate a location similarity score to ensure that the responses are pertinent in both semantic and spatial dimensions. CAIRNS employs an iterative process whereby it retrieves evidence, formulates an initial response, and subsequently evaluates the sufficiency of the information provided. If information deficiencies are identified, the system generates additional sub-questions and re-engages in the retrieval process to augment the information until the response meets the requisite standards of sufficiency. Central to the process of answer generation within CAIRNS is the ScholarGuide Prompt, which is instrumental in harmonizing readability with factual accuracy. In the context of climate-related inquiries, it serves as a directive for the language model, delineating the framework for response structuring, the application of scientific reasoning, and the provision of precise and localized guidance.

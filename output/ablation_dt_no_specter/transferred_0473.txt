Using scikit-learn, we trained Majority, Random, n-gram SVM, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree classifiers for baseline models. We extracted features using TF-IDF and count-vector representations. While baseline models were trained on a CPU, all experiments for transformer models were conducted in a GPU-enabled environment (Google Colab T4). The primary evaluation metric used was the micro-averaged F1-score, complemented by accuracy, precision, and recall for a comprehensive comparison. In our study, we evaluated a wide range of models, including m-BERT cased, XLM-RoBERTa base, BanglaBERT, DistilBERT cased, and the baseline models for classifying hate speech on the BLP2025 dataset. Table 2 presents the classification report for subtask 1A. Notably, BanglaBERT achieved the highest accuracy of 0.71, surpassing all other models in our evaluation. It also outperformed other models with a precision of 0.70 and a recall of 0.71. The official scoring metric for subtask 1A was the micro F1 score, where BanglaBERT achieved a score of 0.71, highlighting its effectiveness. Meanwhile, BERT multilingual base (m-BERT) achieved an F1 score of 0.66, and XLM-RoBERTa base attained a slightly higher score of 0.67, both surpassing the baselines. However, DistilBERT achieved an F1 score of 0.57, which was lower than the baseline methods SVM and Logistic Regression. Among the baseline models, the Support Vector Machine (SVM) produced the best result with an F1 score of 0.61, while the random baseline had the worst result with an F1 score of 0.16. Notably, both the SVM and Logistic Regression models generated the best performance among the baseline models, with F1 scores of 0.63 and 0.62, respectively.

Overall, the most proficient models, such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o, achieve minimal error rates, performing on par with—or surpassing—human benchmarks (left panel, Fig. 8). With the exception of Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, there is a general trend in Figure 8 that more accurate models also exhibit stronger Bayesian-like behavior. However, this relationship between model accuracy and Bayesian behavior is not universally consistent. For instance, a model with perfect predictive accuracy may appear simplistic, devoid of probabilistic tendencies. When contextual parameters are altered, particularly through the introduction of a range of prior observations, the behavioral dynamics of large language models (LLMs) undergo significant variation. As shown in the upper-right quadrant of Figure 6, their sequential predictive capabilities diminish markedly under such conditions. Moreover, this decline is further exacerbated when the input range is biased, as evidenced in the left panel of Figure 6, where error rates increase substantially (notably, triangular markers consistently surpass circular markers). This analysis indicates that LLMs exhibit a propensity to depend on contextual information provided to them. From the middle panel of Figure 8, it is evident that not all models demonstrating low normalized root mean square error (NRMSE) also achieve effective cue combination. Specifically, GPT-5 Mini, despite achieving favorable NRMSE scores, does not exhibit proficiency in cue integration. This phenomenon is particularly evident in the maze distance estimation task, wherein GPT-5 Mini exhibits superior performance in the text modality compared to the image modality. Consequently, a Bayes-optimal approach necessitates a substantial downweighting of the image input, which GPT-5 Mini fails to achieve optimally (refer to Appendix A.12 for further elaboration). Conversely, in the line length ratio task (as depicted in the bottom right panel of Figure 6), numerous LLMs demonstrate the capability to effectively downweight the image modality in the presence of noise, thereby indicating Bayes-consistent adaptation.

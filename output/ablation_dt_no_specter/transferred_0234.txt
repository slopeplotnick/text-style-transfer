A prominent application of retrieval-and-synthesis capabilities is the AI Overviews feature within Google Search, which utilizes the Gemini model family to process multiple long-context documents and generate concise, cited summaries [Reid, 2024]. This functionality significantly enhances user efficiency by eliminating the need to manually aggregate information from diverse sources; however, it also raises critical concerns regarding the reliability and accuracy of the synthesized content. Specifically, when an LLM integrates multiple documents into a single summary, it is crucial to determine whether the model uniformly considers all input sources or exhibits systematic structural biases. Prior studies have extensively explored ideological biases in LLMs. For instance, Santurkar et al. [2023] demonstrated through survey-style prompts that models such as GPT-4 and Claude frequently express liberal-leaning social values. Moreover, in the context of news summarization, Vijay et al. [2024] introduced quantitative measures such as vocabulary divergence (VD), stance polarization index (SPI), and algorithmic monoculture index (AMI) to highlight the tendency of LLMs to converge on narrow ideological framings. While substantial research has focused on ideological and content-level biases in LLM-generated summaries, relatively few investigations have addressed structural biases in how LLMs process multiple input documents. In scenarios involving long concatenated inputs, it remains unclear whether LLMs disproportionately prioritize certain documents based on their positional placement within the context window. Positional effects, such as primacy bias or lost-in-the-middle phenomena, have been observed in other long-context tasks; however, their implications for multi-document summarization remain underexplored. Therefore, this study aims to bridge this gap by examining whether the sequential ordering of source articles influences their representational weight in LLM-generated summaries, using abortion news articles as a case study.

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are analogous to Large Language Models (LLMs) but include an additional visual input channel, which enhances semantic richness beyond textual input and introduces further safety concerns. Jailbreaking, which involves the use of crafted adversarial inputs to bypass safety alignments and induce harmful content, exposes potential vulnerabilities and is a critical focus in the pursuit of developing robust, trustworthy, and secure MLLMs. Previously, jailbreaking efforts predominantly targeted LLMs, employing techniques such as prompt injection and role-play. However, with the advent of MLLMs, adversarial strategies have shifted focus to exploit the unique visual modality introduced by these models. Nonetheless, existing approaches exhibit significant limitations. One approach involves the creation of adversarial images or the concealment of sensitive information within visual inputs; however, this method does not fully leverage the contextual potential. Alternatively, some strategies merely articulate the scenario textually, akin to the jailbreak techniques employed with LLMs, thereby failing to harness the expressive capabilities inherent in images. To address these shortcomings, we propose the Contextual-Image Attack (CIA) methodology, which involves generating images that simultaneously depict both the context and the underlying malicious intent, with textual elements serving a supplementary role. For instance, rather than merely presenting the query, "How can one manufacture illicit substances?" within an image, the scenario could be illustrated as a teacher posing this inquiry within a chemistry classroom setting. This representation lends an appearance of legitimacy to the question, thereby potentially misleading the model and obscuring the malevolent intent. In contrast, alternative methodologies, such as FigStep, merely present the query as text within an image or rely solely on verbal descriptions of the context. Our approach demonstrates superiority by employing images in a more sophisticated manner. This advancement is increasingly pertinent due to the enhancement of image generation technologies, which are now capable of creating highly realistic scenarios, alongside emerging technologies that facilitate the conversion of textual information into visual data. This demonstrates the potential for images to deceive models by embedding intricate and covert semantic intentions.

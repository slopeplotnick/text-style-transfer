Large language models (LLMs) demonstrate exceptional proficiency across a diverse range of tasks. Their performance on specific tasks can be further enhanced through mechanisms such as instruction-based learning, wherein task-specific instructions and examples are provided. The manner in which these instructions are formulated significantly influences the effectiveness of the model's performance. This observation underscores the importance of designing clear and well-constructed instructions, akin to the role of hyperparameter tuning in enhancing performance within traditional machine learning frameworks (Kohavi and John, 1995). However, the manual creation of such optimized instructions remains a challenging and often inconsistent process. Similar to how AutoML automates the process of identifying optimal hyperparameter configurations, automatic prompt optimization has emerged as a systematic approach to discovering effective instructions for LLMs. This development is particularly significant in the current landscape, where multi-agent systems have gained prominence in both industrial applications and academic research. Given that fine-tuning entire models is computationally expensive and requires substantial amounts of data, automatic prompt optimization offers a more efficient and lightweight alternative, often compatible with black-box LLMs. A diverse collection of prompt optimizers is accessible; however, several hurdles arise when applying these optimizers in practice. Each optimizer is typically housed within its own distinct online repository, necessitating engagement with varying codes and requirements when attempting to employ different optimizers. Moreover, these repositories are not consistently maintained, and the accompanying code may lack comprehensive testing or detailed instructions. Furthermore, these approaches demonstrate limited flexibility, rendering their application to tasks beyond basic functionalities particularly challenging. While certain tools have been developed to address these limitations, they often present additional constraints, such as being proprietary and closed-source, supporting only a single optimization algorithm, or exhibiting a level of complexity that hinders their accessibility for routine use.

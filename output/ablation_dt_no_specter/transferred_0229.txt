In this paper, we propose a method to construct knowledge graphs (KGs) from images using multimodal AI models guided by domain-specific ontologies. The ontology, which defines key semantic attributes and relationships, is embedded in the prompt to ensure the AI model focuses on relevant details within the image and its domain. When presented with an image, the AI generates structured triples aligned with the ontology, capturing the most salient objects, attributes, and relationships depicted. These triples collectively form an interpretable KG, with its structure entirely determined by the ontology itself. Each image produces a distinct graph, which is stored in a centralized graph database for further analysis and retrieval. However, while each image-specific graph is unique, many nodes—representing shared semantic attributes—are common across graphs due to the ontology’s constrained vocabulary. Consequently, all image-specific graphs are integrated into a larger, interconnected structure. This unified graph representation enables the identification of semantic similarities between images by comparing their subgraphs within the global knowledge space. To ensure consistent descriptions across images, we use multimodal AI models to extract structured semantic triples that represent high-level concepts in each image. These triples are guided by a domain-specific ontology, which serves as a semantic framework designed by experts. To align the AI model with the ontology, we embed the ontology into the prompt during inference, ensuring adherence to the predefined rules. This approach facilitates the generation of interpretable per-image graphs and their seamless integration into a unified global graph. Individual image graphs, treated as subgraphs, are interconnected through shared attribute nodes, thereby enabling semantic comparison across the entire dataset.

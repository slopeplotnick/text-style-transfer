Checkers and similar methods are effective in structured domains like mathematics and coding but struggle to generalize to open-ended tasks without clear solutions. Self-evaluation approaches address this by treating the model, or another LLM, as a judge. For example, Self-Rewarding Language Models (Yuan et al., 2024) and RLAIF (Lee et al., 2024) rely on AI-generated feedback instead of human labels, while IPO (Garg et al., 2025) and Self-Rewarding PPO (Zhang et al., 2025a) extract preference signals from token-level probabilities. More relevant to our work, research on internal activations, such as Factoscope (He et al., 2024c) and INSIDE (Chen et al., 2024), demonstrates that hidden states encode information about factuality and hallucination risk. Building on this, our method uses a geometric statistic of activations as a reward signal for policy optimization. The theoretical basis for this approach lies in softmax bottleneck analysis. Yang et al. (2018) show that the capacity of softmax-based models is inherently limited by the rank of their hidden representations. Extending this, Godey et al. (2024) demonstrate that the contextual probability distributions in natural language exhibit a fundamentally high-rank structure. Moreover, prior work highlights that embedding collapse into a narrow cone during training degrades generation quality (Gao et al., 2019). Together, these findings establish a strong link between representational dimensionality and output quality. Empirical evidence further supports this: Garrido et al. (2023) shows that the effective rank of learned representations reliably predicts downstream performance, even without labeled data. In language models, hidden states encode rich semantic and syntactic information: word embeddings reveal syntactic and semantic patterns (Mikolov et al., 2013), while intermediate layers capture factual accuracy and hallucination risk (He et al., 2024c; Chen et al., 2024). These results confirm that generation quality leaves measurable geometric signatures. Our work extends these insights by moving from analysis to optimization. Instead of using rank solely as a diagnostic tool for pretrained models, we incorporate stable rank as a live reward signal during policy optimization, transforming this geometric insight into a practical method for improving alignment.

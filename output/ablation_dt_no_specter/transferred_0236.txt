Contemporary market systems exhibit superior capabilities in scalability, adaptability, and problem-solving compared to centralized systems. Botelho and Alami (1999) introduced structured auction protocols that facilitate competitive task assignment, while Gerkey and Matario (2002) demonstrated that auction methods can effectively distribute tasks while maintaining coordination. Recent efforts have focused on addressing communication challenges through sophisticated bidding mechanisms, even under conditions of limited connectivity (Ferri et al. 2017, Quinton et al. 2022). However, auction-based methods may not always ensure global optimality, as local bidding decisions can disrupt coordination when tasks exhibit complex interdependencies. In construction workflows, tasks must adhere to specific sequential dependencies, posing significant challenges for auction-based mechanisms, which are inherently limited in addressing the temporal constraints critical to effective project scheduling. Consequently, these limitations have prompted the investigation of structured optimization methodologies. Among these, dynamic programming has emerged as a viable alternative, offering optimization strategies tailored to sequential decision-making processes. Bellman (1962) introduced foundational principles for deriving optimal solutions to problems characterized by specific structural properties. Nevertheless, the practical application of dynamic programming is constrained by the exponential growth of the state space, rendering it infeasible for large-scale problem instances. Construction projects, characterized by the deployment of numerous robots performing interdependent tasks, exacerbate computational complexity, thereby precluding real-time decision-making. As a result, researchers have investigated metaheuristic methodologies to address large-scale and complex problem domains. These approaches draw upon evolutionary principles to identify feasible solutions within intricate solution spaces. Notably, genetic algorithms have proven effective in managing combinatorial complexities and hierarchical constraints without necessitating a fully defined mathematical formulation (Jones et al.). Moreover, swarm intelligence methodologies have demonstrated efficacy in facilitating the coordination of large-scale robotic teams by leveraging distributed optimization capabilities (Chen et al. 2022; Lim and Isa 2015; Wang et al. 2010; Al-Omeer and Ahmed 2019). However, while metaheuristic methods are widely utilized, they do not guarantee optimal solutions and are characterized by significant computational demands, necessitating extensive iterations to achieve satisfactory solution quality. In dynamic construction environments that require real-time responsiveness to evolving conditions, the prolonged computational time associated with these iterative processes renders them impractical.

Curriculum Learning. Curriculum learning (CL), first introduced by Bengio et al. (2009b), represents a structured approach to model training that progresses from simpler to more complex tasks. This paradigm has been widely adopted in the development of large language models (LLMs) (Parashar et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2025a). Recent methodologies, such as Speed-RL (Zhang et al. 2025) and Kim and Lee (2024), continue to employ data-centric strategies, organizing tasks hierarchically based on externally defined difficulty levels. Similarly, LBS3 (Luo et al. 2024) utilizes a progressive training approach, guiding models from simpler queries to more complex ones. However, these methods fundamentally diverge from the principles of effective curriculum design by relying on static difficulty metrics rather than dynamically adapting to the model's evolving capabilities. In contrast, truly effective curriculum learning should be competence-aware, adjusting dynamically to the model's developmental trajectory. CAPO (Curriculum Advantage Policy Optimization) addresses this limitation by leveraging advantage estimates as an intrinsic, competence-aware signal to guide curriculum construction. Unlike traditional methods that prematurely mix positive and negative feedback, CAPO employs a staged curriculum: it begins with positive reinforcement to establish stable behavioral priors and later incorporates negative feedback to enhance generalization. This phased approach mirrors human cognitive development and mitigates the instability often associated with direct mixed-signal optimization. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CAPO consistently outperforms strong baselines, such as GRPO, across diverse benchmarks and model scales. Furthermore, CAPO exhibits robust cross-domain generalization, excelling in tasks ranging from mathematical reasoning to multimodal challenges. By prioritizing the model's evolving competence over static difficulty metrics, CAPO paves the way for the development of more adaptive and cognitively-inspired learning algorithms.

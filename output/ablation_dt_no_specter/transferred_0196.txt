Ensuring that devices such as smartphones, laptops, personal IoT assistants, autonomous drones, and automotive systems can effectively manage malicious prompts and harmful responses independently of cloud-based solutions is imperative. Such on-device guardrails are crucial for real-time safety enforcement on edge devices, eliminating dependence on cloud-based inference and improving latency, privacy, and deployability in constrained environments. Recent investigations have endeavored to develop safety models capable of operating across multiple languages; however, these models frequently encounter challenges in accommodating a diverse array of languages, particularly those with limited support. A prevalent approach to addressing this challenge involves training a model in English or another high-resource language and subsequently evaluating it in a different language, leveraging shared linguistic features. This method demonstrates greater efficacy for languages that are either similar or well-supported, while distant low-resource languages still face challenges due to limited overlap and reduced exposure during pretraining. To address this limitation, we present CREST, a parameter-efficient, safety-aligned binary classification model for 100 languages, with only 0.5 billion parameters, trained on 13 high-resource languages. The multilingual transformer model leverages linguistic and structural similarities among languages to facilitate cross-lingual generalization. By training the model on high-resource languages within a group, it enhances performance for lower-resource languages in the same cluster. This approach proves particularly advantageous in addressing the scarcity of data for safety-critical applications across diverse linguistic contexts. Although CREST is relatively compact in size, it consistently surpasses several baseline models, demonstrating robust performance across both high-resource and low-resource languages. The model's ability to generalize cross-lingually is facilitated by its effective utilization of shared vocabulary structures and script similarities. To the best of our knowledge, no prior research has successfully presented a safety model that exhibits such extensive efficacy across a multitude of languages.

When performing sequences of measurements, such as planning a sequence of drilling, AI frameworks like Monte Carlo tree search can be utilized to determine the optimal sequential drilling plan (Mern and Caers, 2023). The properties of interest, however, evolve in a specific hierarchy: drill to test hypotheses, then evaluate volume, and finally assess grade. Once drilling has falsified all but one hypothesis, the focus should shift to determining the volume of the mineral system, followed by the grade, or alternatively, combining these aspects into a grade-tonnage curve. While the application of a comprehensive scientific method holds promise for making exploration more effective and less costly, several non-scientific barriers persist. These include ingrained practices and organizational structures within companies, the broader organization of the exploration industry, and the lack of comprehensive software solutions. Companies are typically structured so that domain experts handle tasks aligned with their specialized training and experience. However, the emphasis often lies in uncovering intricate details without considering whether these details influence the ultimate decision. For AI to be implemented successfully, large companies must reorganize around decision-making challenges rather than adhering to a task-oriented mindset. This transformation is not solely a matter of restructuring but also requires educational advancement. Geologists do not need to become data scientists, but fostering a shared understanding of interdisciplinary terminology is crucial—a point that will be revisited later in the education section. Collaboration in the field often centers around a single model, developed using software that facilitates biased and deterministic interpretations. Over time, these models are frequently regarded as definitive representations of reality. As individuals depart, the rationale behind the interpretation is often lost, leading to the specific geometry being misinterpreted as data rather than a model. Within corporate environments, uncertainty is frequently perceived as a failure. There is a prevailing belief that increased effort and dedication would reduce uncertainty by enhancing knowledge acquisition.

Recent research explores methods to improve system robustness by enforcing consistency in geometric interpretation and spatial representation. Studies have shown that viewpoint variations can cause significant perturbations in the embedding space of pretrained ViT encoders, thereby disrupting the coordination between perceptual modules and downstream decision-making processes. Building on these insights, this work investigates whether correcting such embedding misalignments—without altering the overall VLA framework or requiring additional data—can effectively mitigate the adverse effects of viewpoint shifts. Parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) has emerged as a promising alternative to full-model adaptation, allowing models to adapt to new tasks or domains with minimal changes to the existing architecture. Prominent PEFT techniques, such as adapters, LoRA, Prefix-Tuning, and BitFit, introduce small, trainable modules while keeping the majority of the model parameters unchanged. These methods have been widely adopted in large language models and multimodal systems due to their efficiency and stability. In the context of embodied AI, PEFT has predominantly been applied to language or policy components, such as lightweight modifications to decision transformers or instruction encoders. However, when addressing viewpoint shifts, existing approaches rarely focus on directly finetuning the visual module itself. Lightweight and targeted adaptations of the visual pathway within Vision-Language Action (VLA) models remain particularly underexplored. In contrast to conventional methods that often involve retraining large portions of the system or the entire visuomotor policy, we propose a more targeted approach. Specifically, we examine the efficacy of parameter-efficient modifications to the visual components, such as vision tokens or the vision encoder, to address these challenges. Unlike global adaptation strategies, which we argue are unnecessary, our approach addresses the systematic and predictable shifts in the visual embedding space caused by visual perturbations, which disrupt the processing and interpretation of visual data.

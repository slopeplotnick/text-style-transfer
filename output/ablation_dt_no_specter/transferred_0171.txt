The combination of properties of the MODOMA system contributes to the field by providing a laboratory approach to modeling language acquisition. Both the adult and child agents interact as integral components of the system. Moreover, the system is fully parameterized, allowing researchers to modify experimental settings as needed, and the acquired grammatical knowledge is explicitly represented and can be readily examined. Crucially, as this grammatical knowledge is explicitly represented by the grammar model, the linguistic knowledge and processing of the language model can be straightforwardly consulted by researchers. An important contribution of the study presented in this paper is the validation of the feasibility of employing a computational framework comprising multiple agents for first language acquisition. The application of Zipf's law serves as a fundamental mechanism for evaluating the efficacy of the MODOMA system in modeling language learning processes. Furthermore, the experimental design integrates a combination of methodologies to analyze distinct lexical categories, such as function and content words, while encompassing the essential components and procedures characteristic of more complex language acquisition experiments. As a result, the findings of this study lay a solid foundation for future investigations. For instance, future research could examine the impact of varying parameters on the grammar acquired by the child agent, the linguistic output it generates, and its interactions with the adult agent. Examples of such parameters include the frequency of the lexical acquisition process and whether previously acquired lexical categories undergo modification in response to the integration of novel linguistic input. Another relevant avenue for further study is to investigate the influence of feedback from the adult agent on the language acquisition and production processes of the child agent. The positive outcomes of these experiments indicate the feasibility of exploring more sophisticated learning methodologies. Extending this framework, the MODOMA system presents opportunities for examining intricate grammatical phenomena. For example, Shakouri et al. (2025) illustrate that grammatical categories, such as nouns and verbs, can be acquired in a manner analogous to the acquisition of function and content words. Utilizing sophisticated statistical methodologies, these categories are identified without the necessity of explicit instruction. Thus, the findings of this study represent a significant advancement in the field, paving the way for further scholarly inquiry.

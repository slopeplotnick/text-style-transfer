The empirical observation that predictions often stabilize well before the final diffusion step has been noted (Pengxiang et al., 2025). Dynamic policies modulate the balance between exploration and acceleration across the chain, using signals like historical logits or local determinism to reduce redundant steps (Wei et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b; Or-El et al., 2025). Meanwhile, other lines of work focus on error correction and refinement, enabling models to autonomously revise their outputs by re-masking low-confidence tokens (Zhang et al., 2025a; Su et al., 2025). Moreover, efficient methodologies address this bottleneck by incorporating concepts from continuous processes through staged learning, consistency maintenance, or flow matching (Sahoo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Meister et al., 2024). Orthogonal efforts enhance computational efficiency by reducing per-step latency with caching adapted to bidirectional refinement and employing mechanisms that draft and verify components concurrently (Yuan et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a; Sohoni et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025a). However, while these approaches demonstrate efficacy, they often require additional training, introduce auxiliary models, or rely on complex heuristics, highlighting the need for a simple, training-free, and architecture-agnostic early-exit principle. To address this, we introduce SchED, a schedule-based early stopping mechanism that is both training-free and model-agnostic. Concretely, this approach evaluates the confidence level associated with each segment of the response, juxtaposing it against a dynamically evolving threshold that progressively relaxes throughout the process. By decoupling confidence from the number of steps and facilitating a gradual modification of the threshold, the sampler exits as soon as predictions are stable, thereby avoiding the brittleness of hard phase changes or fixed budgets. SchED integrates seamlessly with conventional transfer schedules, such as single-suffix or block diffusion, and requires no modifications to the model training process (Nie et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025).

Considering the growing importance of the "world model" as a shared computational basis for both the brain and artificial intelligence, we adopt the perspective of world-model-based processing to provide a comprehensive cross-domain comparison of their circuit computations. Consequently, we have identified a convergent evolution between the brain and modern AI, particularly evident in large-scale language models. Based on this finding, we have developed a new theory of macro-scale information processing mechanisms in the brain, corresponding to deep-layered local circuit computations. The neocortex and cerebellum acquire world models by predicting future states of the world and learning from prediction errors, subsequently using these predictive world models for a unified processing system across domains—understanding in sensory processing and generation in motor processing. This theory represents an integrated advancement of the internal model theory and the mirror neuron system, indicating that world-model-based circuit computation is key to enabling diverse functions within uniform circuit architectures in both AI and the brain. Moreover, the approach and insights presented in this perspective provide a promising foundation for future research bridging neuroscience and AI, with the aim of understanding human-like general intelligence. Notably, the cerebellum functions as a predictive model, processing external sensory inputs, generating anticipatory predictions of subsequent sensory information, and refining its computations through the minimization of prediction errors. Empirical evidence suggests that the cerebellum primarily learns to predict forthcoming sensory inputs rather than abstract, higher-order signals.

The Menta model has been developed as a specialized tool for predicting mental health conditions through the analysis of social media text. It is engineered to provide both accuracy and user-friendliness. Moreover, Menta employs a training methodology that effectively addresses data imbalance and demonstrates robust performance across various clinical tasks. The Menta framework achieves a commendable balance between accuracy and computational efficiency, outperforming several existing models and exhibiting competitive performance relative to larger language models across six distinct mental health classification tasks. Furthermore, the system is designed to prioritize user accessibility and data privacy while maintaining a resource-efficient architecture. In contrast, previous approaches predominantly relied on traditional machine learning techniques and models that required extensive manual configuration to analyze mental health indicators from social media data. For instance, Jiang et al. employed sophisticated linguistic models to identify mental health concerns on Reddit, demonstrating superior efficacy compared to traditional methodologies. Similarly, Sarkar et al. developed a model that enhances the prediction of depression and anxiety from Reddit posts by leveraging shared features, surpassing models that focus on singular aspects. Additionally, other investigations have devised tools utilizing deep learning for suicide risk screening, which hold promise for facilitating early intervention strategies. Recent advancements have seen the application of large language models in the domain of mental health assessment. For example, a study employing ChatGPT demonstrated its capability to classify mental health conditions such as stress, depression, and suicidality on social media platforms without the necessity for prior domain-specific knowledge, thereby illustrating the potential of these models in predicting mental health issues. Furthermore, another investigation concentrated on the detection of depression from online textual data, revealing that fine-tuned models exhibit superior performance compared to their predecessor models. Research has also examined the efficacy of these models in interpreting depressive symptoms and emotional states, revealing that the incorporation of prompts with emotional cues and few-shot examples enhances their performance.

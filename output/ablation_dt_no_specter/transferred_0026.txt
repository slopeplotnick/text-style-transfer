Our findings align with the results of Shirafuji et al. (2025), who employed a task vector-based approach and similarly demonstrated the effectiveness of debiased models. In contrast, other studies, such as Lutz et al. (2024), utilized model-merging techniques and showcased the performance of debiased large language models (LLMs) on downstream tasks, as evidenced by their results on NLI benchmarks. However, our analysis highlights the necessity of subjecting methods evaluated primarily on tasks like NLI to more rigorous assessments across a broader range of datasets. For instance, SuperGLUE results reveal that, with the exception of Linear, SLERP, and Nearswap, most model-merging techniques significantly degrade the causal reasoning capabilities of LLMs (Section 5.2). Consequently, these methods are unsuitable for achieving reliable bias mitigation. The effectiveness of SLERP can be attributed to its ability to uniformly interpolate across the parameter space in the hypersphere, integrating the bias inverse vector in a balanced manner without amplifying it excessively. Conversely, NuSLERP, which applies normalization at the layer or tensor level, exhibited a substantial decline in its SuperGLUE scores. These findings suggest that, unlike SLERP, the majority of contemporary model-merging techniques cannot be directly applied for bias mitigation without incurring significant losses in reasoning performance. This study represents the first comprehensive investigation into the impact of seven model-merging methodologies on social bias in LLMs. By evaluating 13 models from the GPT, LLaMA, and Qwen families across three social bias datasets and the SuperGLUE benchmark, we uncovered a trade-off between fairness and utility. Moreover, four specific methods—Karcher Mean, NuSLERP, TIES, and DELLA—were found to substantially impair performance on tasks requiring reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning, such as ReCoRD, COPA, CB, and BoolQ within the SuperGLUE benchmark. While some debiasing approaches employing model merging have demonstrated the downstream-task performance of debiased LLMs through evaluations on NLI benchmarks, our findings emphasize the importance of also assessing their accuracy on tasks involving reading comprehension as well as commonsense and causal reasoning.

In recent years, the technology behind Large Language Models (LLMs) has advanced rapidly, finding widespread application in various domains such as intelligent interaction and scientific research support. In the medical field, LLMs have demonstrated significant potential in aiding disease diagnosis and optimizing diagnostic and treatment workflows. Within the domain of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), the core clinical principle is encapsulated in "Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment" (SDT). This principle involves synthesizing multidimensional information to identify syndromes, which then guide the development of individualized treatment plans based on concepts like "different treatments for the same disease." Despite the emergence of TCM-specific LLMs, such as Zhongjing and HuatuoGPT, their clinical application remains challenging due to the intricate and highly individualized nature of TCM's diagnostic and therapeutic processes. A key limitation is the absence of comprehensive evaluation benchmarks that encompass the entire clinical decision-making process, as well as the lack of specialized metrics for assessing the reasoning mechanisms of these models. Consequently, there is an urgent need for a benchmark dataset that can objectively and comprehensively evaluate the clinical applicability of TCM-focused LLMs. Such a dataset would not only support performance evaluation and iterative optimization but also facilitate their eventual integration into real-world clinical scenarios. However, the construction of such a dataset is fraught with challenges due to the individualized, holistic, and diverse characteristics of SDT in TCM. For instance, patients with the same disease may present varying syndromes due to differences in constitution, disease progression, and lifestyle habits, which complicates the creation of standardized evaluation frameworks. Moreover, TCM diagnosis relies on a holistic reasoning process that integrates multiple diagnostic methods to analyze factors such as disease etiology and pathogenesis, ultimately guiding syndrome differentiation and treatment formulation. This comprehensive approach necessitates a benchmark dataset with broad evaluative dimensions. Additionally, the frequent occurrence of comorbidities in patients, combined with the diversity of diagnostic methods, introduces significant subjectivity to evaluation outcomes. These factors collectively underscore the formidable challenge of developing a robust and inclusive benchmark for assessing the clinical application capabilities of TCM-specific LLMs.

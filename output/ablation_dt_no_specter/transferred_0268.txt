The prototyped scale is composed of 9 items on a 7-point Likert scale, broken down into 3 subscales measuring AI use-linked feelings of Shame, Guilt, and Impostor Syndrome, respectively. The real-world dataset was obtained by performing a survey study that incorporated these items alongside additional psychometric measures to evaluate the criterion validity of our scale. The online survey ran on Prolific from Monday, 03 February 2025, at 18:00 GMT, to Tuesday, 04 February 2025, at 23:30 GMT. Once we acquired the real-world dataset, we conducted a comparative analysis with the previously generated simulated dataset, utilizing the same methodological approach as employed in Study 1. A demographic comparison of the participants in both datasets is presented in Table 1. The SAGAT scale will be elaborated upon in a forthcoming scientific publication, following the completion of a conventional scale development process that incorporates insights from our experimental prototyping methodology. Group comparisons between real and simulated data were conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test. For the Impostor Syndrome dimension, the analysis revealed no significant difference between real and simulated scores (U = 41,516, p = .13). However, significant differences were observed for the Shame (U = 29,457, p < .001) and Guilt (U = 26,392, p < .001) dimensions. Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests revealed significant distributional differences across all three constructs (all p values < .001), indicating that the findings do not support H4. Histograms illustrating the comparative distribution of the real-world and simulated datasets are presented in Figure 4. To enhance the validity and reliability of our findings, we conducted a replication study aimed at developing a novel scale. In this instance, we devised a scale to assess AI-linked anxious beliefs. The development process adhered to the methodology employed in Study 3, details of which are available in the corresponding "Methods" section. For a comprehensive account of the scale development process utilizing simulated data, the reader is directed to consult the Supplementary Materials accompanying this article, as well as the preregistration available on the Open Science Framework (OSF) at the provided link.

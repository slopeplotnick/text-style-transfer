Sentiment analysis is a cornerstone of modern Natural Language Processing (NLP), enabling a wide range of applications, including digital governance, public health surveillance, financial inclusion, and user-generated content moderation. However, despite significant global progress in deep learning and multilingual language modeling, South African low-resource languages remain critically underrepresented in sentiment analysis research. This underrepresentation is evident in the limited availability of annotated datasets, the lack of robust lexical resources, and the restricted cross-lingual transferability of existing models. Consequently, models trained on high-resource languages such as English and French often exhibit diminished performance when applied to African contexts, further widening the digital divide and restricting equitable access to NLP technologies. Current approaches to multilingual sentiment analysis have primarily focused on machine translation pipelines, cross-lingual embeddings, and monolingual lexicon development. While these methods have shown promise, they are not without limitations. For instance, machine-translated lexicons frequently introduce errors and fail to capture cultural nuances, particularly in morphologically complex African languages. Additionally, corpus-based lexicon extraction methods require extensive domain-specific datasets, which are often unavailable for low-resource languages. Furthermore, although African language models like AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa have demonstrated improved performance, their utility remains constrained by the scarcity of high-quality sentiment lexicons necessary for effective classification in multilingual settings. To address these challenges, this paper introduces TriLex, a three-stage retrieval-augmented framework designed to enable scalable sentiment lexicon expansion for African low-resource languages. The TriLex framework comprises three key components: 1) corpus-based lexical extraction to identify candidate sentiment-bearing terms; 2) cross-lingual semantic mapping to align these terms across English, French, and African languages; and 3) Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to refine, validate, and expand the lexicon using large language models conditioned on multilingual retrieval signals. In addition to proposing TriLex, we present and release a comprehensive multilingual sentiment lexicon that spans multiple South African languages, fostering reproducible research. Finally, we conduct the first extensive evaluation of AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa, leveraging a lexicon-augmented resource tailored for isiXhosa and isiZulu.

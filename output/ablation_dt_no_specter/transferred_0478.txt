After fine-tuning the LLM using the KTO method, we employ it as a domain-specific data annotator to label particular online items. Hard samples that are inaccurately predicted by the online BERT-based model are incorporated into the training set, thereby improving the model's overall performance. By leveraging the domain rule-aware LLM developed within the RD module, we can efficiently identify these challenging samples where the online model fails to make accurate predictions. However, this approach only addresses individual errors and does not systematically resolve broader categories of issues. For instance, when search terms or product descriptions are slightly modified, the online model may still produce incorrect predictions. To address this limitation, we have developed an error-type-aware LLM generative model that synthesizes data prone to misprediction by the online model, enhancing its generalization capability and accuracy. For any given query-product pair, the proposed model modifies the attributes of the query or product based on instruction prompts, generating new examples categorized as either Relevant or Irrelevant. At this stage, training samples are derived through few-shot prompting using the GPT-4o model. To ensure the generative model effectively captures the error patterns of the online model, we iteratively select generated outputs where the online model produces incorrect predictions and incorporate them as fine-tuning training samples for the LLM. Once the error-type-aware LLM-based generative model is developed, we systematically extract data from online retrievals and use the model to generate challenging samples likely to confound the online model. To minimize the adverse effects of noisy data on the training process, we further apply the relevance discrimination model from the RD module to filter and refine the generated outputs.

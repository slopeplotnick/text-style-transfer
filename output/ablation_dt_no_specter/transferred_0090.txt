We evaluated a diverse set of contemporary language models, spanning both closed- and open-weight architectures (see Appendix A.4 for details). Where possible, we disabled extended reasoning or advanced cognitive controls to assess the models' inherent, emergent behavior. This was feasible for all models except GPT-5 mini, which restricts adjustments to the depth of reasoning; consequently, we configured it to the minimum allowable setting. Additionally, we conducted a human baseline study for comparative analysis across all tasks, incorporating a limited set of modifications (see Appendix A.5 for details). The results of the human study are presented in the left panel of Figure 8. This experiment serves solely as a reference point, as our primary focus is on comparing the performance of language models against one another. Extensive research on human perception, including studies such as those by Jazayeri and Shadlen (2010) and Petzschner and Glasauer (2011), is well-documented in the psychophysics literature. Notably, most models demonstrate superior performance in text-based tasks compared to image-based tasks, except for the maze distance estimation task. The input text for this task comprises a long and intricate path description, significantly more complex than the simple text prompts utilized in other tasks (refer to Fig. 2). While most models exhibit diminished performance in the text modality for this task, GPT-5 Mini emerges as an exception; it achieves near-optimal text performance due to its residual reasoning capabilities, which, although not entirely suppressible, underscore the task-dependent nature of behavior. Across various tasks, the evidential support for Bayesian behavior is generally more pronounced in the image modality compared to the text modality (Appendix A.11). Transitioning from unimodal to multimodal inputs does not uniformly enhance performance. However, certain models exhibit a superior capacity to effectively utilize the additional information provided by the multimodal inputs: Llama-4 Maverick achieves optimal performance under multimodal conditions across all tasks, while Claude 3.7 Sonnet and GPT-4o demonstrate improved outcomes in two out of the three tasks.

In domain-specific SFT tasks, we typically use a standardized template for task definitions and requirements, into which the query and item information from each dataset are integrated to generate the prompt. To effectively activate the core capabilities of the base model, it is essential to include key elements such as relevance task definitions, decision-making rules, and output formats within the prompt. Moreover, to ensure the model adheres to our proposed two-stage discrimination framework, we embed structured instructional information into the prompt. This framework first requires the model to comprehend the query's intent and attribute requirements, and then to extract relevant attributes from the item information based on these requirements, ultimately making judgments in accordance with the established rules. For reasoning tasks, providing more detailed and specific instructions in the prompt generally enhances the model's ability to derive accurate answers. However, this trend does not consistently apply in Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) scenarios, where the model primarily develops its capabilities through learning from response data rather than relying solely on instructional guidance. To determine the optimal prompt design, we examined three configurations. The comprehensive version includes the full range of task definitions, judgment criteria, and detailed discrimination rules, amounting to over 7,000 tokens. The intermediate version simplifies this by removing detailed rules and retaining only the most critical instructions, resulting in a length of approximately 800 tokens. The abbreviated version is the most concise, containing only the fundamental role definition, such as: "You are an e-commerce relevance assessment expert. Please score the query and item." This phase represents the model's cold start stage, where the goal is to enhance its foundational decision-making capabilities. Achieving this requires constructing a training dataset with two key attributes: comprehensiveness and low noise. To ensure comprehensive coverage, a robust sampling strategy is employed to capture diverse e-commerce data distributions, thereby mitigating the risks of overrepresentation or underrepresentation of specific data types.

By integrating related events and examining their antecedents, one can construct coherent and logically connected narratives that consolidate dispersed information flows. This approach surpasses mere summarization, which often results in fragmented representations devoid of causal or temporal linkages. Moreover, preserving fine-grained details within events, such as specific technical parameters in a "new technology release," provides a more precise depiction than generalization, which tends to oversimplify and obscure critical features necessary for assessing commercial impact. Furthermore, the inclusion of precise temporal markers, such as exact dates, facilitates the analysis of event durations and their subsequent effects. Without such temporal specificity, establishing causal linkages between events and corresponding market fluctuations becomes challenging, thereby diminishing both predictive accuracy and interpretability. This study examines the significance of systematic event tracking and the incorporation of incremental information in enhancing analytical precision. The removal of the event tracking component revealed that even minor news items can significantly impact market dynamics if subsequent reports indicate unforeseen advancements. Conversely, in the absence of additional information, such as when a major shareholder reduces their holdings without accompanying developments, the market response remains largely neutral. Thus, while a single event may not substantially alter market conditions, the evolving expectations over time, discernible through systematic event tracking, play a crucial role in enhancing predictive accuracy. To assess the efficacy of our historical pattern referencing methodology, we conducted a comparative analysis of four distinct approaches. Our method demonstrated superior performance. For instance, the "same-company historical referencing" approach, while yielding satisfactory outcomes, encounters limitations due to data sparsity. This is particularly pronounced for companies with limited historical data or those engaged in innovative business practices, where identifying analogous historical events proves challenging.

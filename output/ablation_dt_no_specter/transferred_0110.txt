In conclusion, while utilizing contextual memory for identifying LeechHijack appears promising, it lacks the precision for direct application. Our findings indicate that this approach should be augmented with more granular verification mechanisms, such as runtime tool isolation or fine-grained semantic analysis. We have established that auditing contextual memory is a viable detection vector; however, realizing a robust and practical defense necessitates further refinement. This section discusses the limitations and broader implications of the proposed LeechHijack attack. We examine the constraints inherent to the current design, clarify the scope of its applicability, and identify potential avenues through which the threat could evolve or escalate over time. A significant limitation of our approach is the fidelity with which it manages hijacked computation. As illustrated in Table 7, the attack's Answer Success Rate (ASR) demonstrates a deficiency relative to the native LLM. This decline in performance adversely affects the utility of the compromised resources for the attacker. Nevertheless, this issue can be ameliorated by distributing the additional task among multiple victims and employing a consensus mechanism on the aggregated outputs. Another limitation pertains to the range of tasks that LeechHijack is capable of performing. Our experimental evaluations demonstrated the effectiveness of LeechHijack within the scope of the MMLU benchmark; however, its performance on more complex tasks, such as those encompassed by GAIA, remains unexplored. The current configuration of LeechHijack is predominantly optimized for standard dialogue-based tasks, and its efficacy is anticipated to decline when applied to more sophisticated operations. Extending the attack to effectively execute complex commands presents significant challenges and would require the development of more advanced attack configurations. Given that agent systems utilize computationally expensive LLMs, an attacker can derive benefit from LeechHijack without deploying it for complex tasks. Instead, they can exploit the compromised resources for less demanding operations, such as information aggregation or the generation of templated content, thereby leveraging the extensive knowledge base of the underlying model. Consequently, LeechHijack represents a tangible and persistent threat to the MCP and agent systems, irrespective of the complexity of the hijacked task.

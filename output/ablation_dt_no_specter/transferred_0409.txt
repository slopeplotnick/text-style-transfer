The central inquiry pertains to whether contemporary language models (LLMs), trained solely for next-token prediction without explicit perceptual training, inherently develop analogous mechanisms for information processing. Elucidating how these models manage uncertain information is pivotal for developing robust systems capable of handling diverse input quality. To explore this, we employ methodologies derived from psychophysics—a discipline concerned with the study of perceptual processes—to investigate the operational dynamics of these models. Treating the models as opaque systems, we analyze their observable behaviors to infer information processing strategies rather than examining their internal mechanisms directly. By systematically modulating input uncertainty and assessing evidence of Bayesian inference—an approach grounded in probabilistic decision-making—we evaluate whether these models exhibit perceptual behaviors analogous to those observed in humans, despite the absence of explicit training for such tasks. Notably, several classical paradigms from psychophysics prove effective in eliciting meaningful patterns of behavior, revealing intriguing parallels with human perceptual processing. These tasks serve as effective instruments for assessing the models' capabilities in processing diverse input types, particularly within controlled environments that allow for precise measurement. In contrast to real-world tasks where noise is too pervasive to quantify accurately, this framework facilitates the examination of specific integration strategies and uncovers vulnerabilities that remain undetected in conventional benchmarks. We present three principal contributions: Firstly, we develop a psychophysics framework tailored for LLMs, encompassing four tasks designed to evaluate parameters such as length, location, distance, and duration. This experimental framework enables precise manipulation of variables such as noise and contextual information to investigate shifts in model behavior. Subsequently, we introduce a novel benchmark, BayesBench, designed to evaluate model performance, cue-integration efficiency, and adherence to Bayesian principles through the application of a newly developed Bayesian Consistency Score. Our findings reveal that certain advanced models exhibit emergent behavior consistent with Bayesian reasoning. However, we also identify a significant limitation wherein even highly accurate models fail to adopt robust and reliable strategies under specific conditions. Psychophysics, the discipline concerned with the quantitative relationships between physical stimuli and perceptual experiences, consistently demonstrates the presence of systematic patterns in these relationships, encapsulated by established principles such as the Weber-Fechner law, among others.

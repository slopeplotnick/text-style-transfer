Conversely, the daughter language model has been constructed specifically as part of the MOD-OMA framework. Similar to Delilah, the daughter agent functions as both a parser and generator; however, unlike the adult language model, its grammatical representations are initially underspecified and become more specific through language acquisition procedures. A non-trivial resemblance between the adult and child systems is that both employ graph structures for representing grammatical knowledge. Moreover, their parsers and generators execute their respective grammars by unifying lexical structures to output well-formed utterances, provide grammaticality judgments, and generate parses with respect to input utterances based on the currently specified grammar. A crucial characteristic of the parser and generator of the child agent is their ability to operate with an incomplete grammar, containing underspecified structures. Similar to humans acquiring their first languages, the daughter language model is required to engage in interactions with the mother model before acquisition is completed. Structurally, the daughter agent comprises three modules: (1) a parser and generator, referred to as the automaton, which execute (2) a grammar that is progressively specified through acquisition facilitated by (3) a language acquisition device. Crucially, the parser and generator remain static throughout the learning process. The result of language acquisition is explicit grammatical knowledge, represented by newly acquired lexical entries and grammatical properties that constrain the combinatory potential of lexical items. Illustrated in Figure 2, these lexical entries provide comprehensive information regarding words and constructions, encompassing grammatical attributes such as phonological form, semantic content, and other syntactic properties. Grammatical properties are formalized through feature-value pairs such as [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: content word] and [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: function word], the acquisition of which constitutes the primary objective of the present study. Given that the learning procedures are unsupervised, the daughter language model operates without access to the labels utilized by the mother language model or predefined grammatical descriptions. Therefore, the acquired grammatical properties and semantic representations are denoted by distinct alpha-numeric identifiers, exemplified by property type 'A' and property value 'b' in Figure 2, with additional reference to Figure 3. These structures encompass additional grammatical properties not examined within the scope of this study. For instance, the terminal feature distinguishes terminal words from more abstract constructions, while the semiform index facilitates the representation of anaphoric elements such as reflexives. Furthermore, technical features, including the session identifier and lexical entry number, are incorporated to uniquely identify each rule. As a consequence of simulating language acquisition, the grammar may encompass increasingly intricate structures, wherein the HEAD and ARGUMENT are capable of incorporating data structures analogous to lexical items.

Over the past decade, the global mining industry has increasingly found itself at the core of the energy transition. The demand for critical minerals is anticipated to rise significantly, along with the corresponding geopolitical, social, and environmental considerations. Consequently, this phenomenon is catalyzing the emergence of novel enterprises, often backed by venture capital, that aim to revolutionize the mining sector, much like their disruptions in healthcare, renewable energy, and climate technology. Moreover, a new wave of digital technology is emerging, potentially transforming the current economic, educational, social, and industrial systems through the deployment of large language models. Whether these innovations will substantively impact these domains remains uncertain; however, the pervasive fear of missing out is evident. For the mining industry, these developments should not be seen as a cause for concern but rather as an opportunity to leverage advancements such as artificial intelligence (AI). This paper aims to identify these opportunities and examine how AI can contribute. For instance, research conducted by my group, Stanford Mineral-X, was instrumental in the discovery of a significant copper deposit in the Zambian Copperbelt, where AI played a crucial role in optimizing drilling strategies and constructing subsurface models. Several start-up companies specializing in the application of AI to mineral exploration have emerged from this program. This paper does not aim to provide a comprehensive enumeration of AI applications in exploration or an exhaustive review of existing literature, as such resources are already available (Lindi et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024). Instead, the focus is on examining the potential of AI to enhance decision-making processes through the adoption of an innovative scientific methodology. In the initial section of this paper, I will delineate the principal decision-making processes inherent in mineral exploration and elucidate how establishing a scientific methodology can enhance the efficiency of these endeavors. Presently, within the domain of mineral exploration, there is an absence of a universally accepted scientific method. Instead, the field is characterized by a series of tasks executed by domain experts, each adhering to distinct methodologies specific to their respective disciplines. A comprehensive scientific methodology for mineral exploration is notably absent, and I intend to argue that this constitutes a critical challenge in enhancing the efficiency of exploration efforts, thereby reducing the costs associated with discovery.

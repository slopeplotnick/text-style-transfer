For GraphMatch, we observe that the integration of random and hard negatives is essential for optimal performance. GraphMatch models trained solely with random negatives demonstrate performance akin to that of TextMatch-small. We conduct an ablation study on the node features utilized by GraphMatch: the "no-feat" configuration employs only TextMatch node embeddings, the "no-text" configuration utilizes numerical node features devoid of text embeddings, and the "full" configuration incorporates a combination of TextMatch node embeddings and numerical node features. Notably, GraphMatch-full, trained with adversarial negatives, demonstrates the highest performance, whereas GraphMatch-no-feat achieves results comparable to TextMatch-large despite its significantly smaller model size. Our findings indicate that the inclusion of textual context is crucial for accurate predictions, as evidenced by the notably low accuracy of GraphMatch-no-text. Moreover, the temporal accuracy of data sampling emerges as a critical factor. Selecting appropriate subgraphs and node features at specific time points is essential for training a model that generalizes effectively to novel nodes. To evaluate this, we conducted experiments with GraphMatch using two distinct configurations. The "no-temporal-nodes" configuration omits temporally relevant node features during the training phase but consistently employs the most recent feature version available in the training dataset. The "no-temporal-graph" variant similarly does not select temporally specific subgraphs, yet it has access to the entirety of graph edges during the training phase.

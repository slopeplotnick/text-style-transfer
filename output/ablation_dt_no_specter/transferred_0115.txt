The focus of this discussion is on human cognition using only visual data. To this end, we present VIDEO4SPATIAL, a framework demonstrating that video diffusion models conditioned solely on video-based scene context can perform complex spatial tasks. We validate this framework on two tasks: scene navigation, which involves following camera-pose instructions while maintaining spatial coherence, and object grounding, which requires identifying and localizing target objects based on textual instructions. Both tasks test spatial understanding, instruction following, and planning capabilities using video-only inputs, without relying on auxiliary modalities such as depth information or explicit camera poses. By adopting simple yet effective design principles, VIDEO4SPATIAL exhibits robust visuospatial reasoning: it performs end-to-end navigation and object grounding, adheres to camera-pose instructions while preserving spatial consistency, and generalizes effectively to extended temporal contexts and previously unseen environments. Together, these results advance video generative models toward achieving general visuospatial reasoning. Humans excel at remembering, interpreting, and navigating spatial environments—a hallmark of advanced spatial reasoning. A substantial body of research has sought to replicate these abilities within neural networks. Recently, video generative models have made significant progress, demonstrating perceptual and reasoning capabilities akin to human cognition, paralleling the development of large language models. Due to the inherently sequential and temporally coherent nature of video generation, these models are particularly well-suited to visuospatial tasks. For instance, they can generate future frames that preserve spatial integrity and visual consistency, simulating plausible progressions. In maze navigation scenarios, for example, a model can "envision" movement by generating frames that maintain the maze's topology while ensuring seamless transitions. Humans primarily perceive spatial environments through visual input, and we aim to replicate this capability in models using video data as a simple, general, and scalable modality. However, acquiring spatial understanding solely from conventional video inputs remains challenging, as many contemporary approaches still require supplementary information such as depth maps or camera poses. In this work, we introduce VIDEO4SPATIAL and demonstrate that video generative models conditioned solely on video-based scene context can effectively reason about spatial environments. Our approach employs a straightforward framework: a standard video diffusion model trained with a well-defined objective. Given an input video scene and task-specific instructions, the model generates a video that successfully completes the task while maintaining spatial and temporal coherence.

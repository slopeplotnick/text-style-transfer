Jack Lu, Ryan Teehan, Jinran Jin, and Mengye Ren from the Agentic Learning AI Lab at New York University can be reached via their NYU email addresses, with further details available on their website. On November 20, 2025, they presented insights into the dual role of large language models (LLMs) as both problem solvers and solution verifiers, highlighting how verifiers enhance solver performance by selecting high-quality responses. However, previous research has predominantly focused on self-verification, where models assess their own outputs, with limited exploration of cross-model verification. Although many models undergo extensive post-training, the impact of this additional training on verification capabilities remains unclear. Therefore, in this study, we systematically investigate these dynamics across 37 models of various architectures and parameter scales, including both base models and their post-trained versions. The researchers evaluated models across nine distinct problem categories, such as logic, puzzles, mathematics, common sense, and factual knowledge. They compared the efficacy of self-verification against verification of responses from models within the same cohort and across different cohorts. Moreover, they developed an innovative metric to quantify the enhancement in response accuracy due to verification processes and validated its effectiveness. The study examined how these evaluation metrics vary with model size, additional training, and the nature of the data being verified. The findings indicate that cross-group answer verification is particularly effective; additional training enhances a model's ability to verify outputs from other model groups but does not significantly improve self-verification. Furthermore, mathematical and logical problems were identified as the most straightforward to verify. This approach shifts the paradigm from solely querying models for solutions to employing them as both problem solvers and solution verifiers. Verification during test time can range from straightforward approaches, such as generating multiple candidate solutions and selecting the most optimal one using a verification mechanism, to more iterative methods that involve progressively refining the solutions. Consequently, this process enables models to tackle more complex problems than they could solve independently.

Understanding real-world scientific laws is crucial for accurate modeling of physical outcomes under diverse conditions. However, existing video benchmarks predominantly rely on physical commonsense, offering limited insight into the scientific reasoning capabilities of video models. In response, we introduce VideoScience-Bench, a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate undergraduate-level scientific understanding in video models. Each test question encodes a composite scientific scenario that requires comprehension and analytical reasoning across multiple scientific concepts to derive the correct outcome. The benchmark comprises 200 meticulously curated questions spanning 14 topics and 103 concepts in physics and chemistry. We conducted expert-annotated evaluations across seven state-of-the-art video models in T2V and I2V settings, assessing four dimensions: Immutability, Correct Dynamism, Spatio-Temporal Continuity, and Phenomenon Congruency. By employing a VLM-as-a-Judge framework to evaluate video content, we observed a strong alignment with human evaluations. To the best of our knowledge, VideoScience-Bench is the first benchmark to assess video models not only in their generative capabilities but also in their scientific reasoning, ensuring that their outputs accurately reflect expected physical and chemical phenomena. Our dataset and evaluation code are accessible at github.com/hao-ai-lab/VideoScience. The releases of Sora-2 and Veo-3.1 signify a substantial transformation in the video-generation landscape, where contemporary models produce videos with cinematic-level visual fidelity and exhibit novel world-modeling capabilities. As these models advance, the criteria for evaluating "good" video generation are evolving beyond traditional distributional quality metrics such as Fréchet Video Distance and Fréchet Video Motion Distance. The focus is shifting towards assessments that examine the adherence of generated videos to fundamental physical regularities and their capacity for world understanding. Recent models have demonstrated emergent zero-shot reasoning abilities, addressing tasks that necessitate scientific, mathematical, and spatial reasoning. Individuals trained in scientific disciplines comprehend the world through a framework of scientific principles, enabling them to predict outcomes such as whether citric acid will effectively remove iron stains or the implications of powering an LED with or without a resistor. This prompts our central question: beyond achieving visual fidelity and adhering to fundamental physical principles, can video models accurately depict scientific phenomena and engage in scientific reasoning about the real world? World modeling grounded in scientific principles is vital for advancing discovery, as it facilitates models in predicting the outcomes of real-world processes. In contrast to basic commonsense-based world understanding, true scientific understanding necessitates reasoning across interconnected concepts, as exemplified by the integration of specific-heat and heat-transfer principles, which demonstrate that water absorbs heat.

The transition towards increased electrification has intensified the global focus on critical mineral exploration. Despite the anticipated rise in demand, the exploration industry remains a financially unprofitable enterprise. Even with augmented investments, there has been a decline in new discoveries over the past two decades. In this paper, I propose a solution to this problem by advocating for the integration of artificial intelligence as a means to enhance the scientific rigor of mineral exploration. This approach aims to reduce cognitive bias and false positives, enhance the role of domain experts, and drive down exploration costs. The existing methodologies, which incorporate various scientific disciplines such as geology, geochemistry, and geophysics, have proven inadequate in effectively identifying concealed mineral deposits. Specifically, the prevailing approach fails to sufficiently quantify uncertainty, resulting in suboptimal decision-making and financial resources being expended on drilling endeavors that frequently yield false positives. Therefore, I propose a novel scientific method grounded in the principles of Bayesianism and falsification, where data acquisition is primarily seen as a means to rigorously test human-generated hypotheses. The determination of subsequent data acquisition is guided by well-defined metrics and informed decision-making processes. Moreover, a practical framework is presented, applicable to any mineral exploration endeavor. However, to effectively implement this framework, the integration of various artificial intelligence methodologies is imperative. Paramount among these are novel unsupervised learning techniques that collaborate with domain experts to enhance data interpretation and generate diverse geological hypotheses. Additionally, human-in-the-loop AI systems are crucial for orchestrating data acquisition across geology, geophysics, geochemistry, and drilling, with the aim of mitigating uncertainties associated with geological hypotheses before addressing the quality and quantity of mineral deposits. This methodology will be exemplified through current case studies in mineral exploration and resource assessment.

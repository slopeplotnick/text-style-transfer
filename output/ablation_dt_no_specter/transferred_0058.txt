The Phase Field (PF) model offers a robust framework for analyzing the temporal evolution of systems comprising multiple materials and phases as they progress towards equilibrium. This model is particularly beneficial for applications involving small-scale and short-duration phenomena, such as solar cell fabrication. The energetic characteristics of the system are captured within the Gibbs free energy functional, which serves as the foundation for examining the formation processes of the solar cell. Specifically, the methodology focuses on solvent evaporation, phase separation, and crystallization. The driving forces steering the system towards thermodynamic equilibrium are derived from the Gibbs free energy functional. Moreover, phase transitions, including solvent evaporation and crystallization, are described through governing equations that account for mass transport phenomena. During simulations, the model is coupled with a fluid dynamics solver to incorporate advective mass transport, as detailed in Supporting Information Section S2. Subsequently, material properties derived from prior calculations are utilized to investigate light propagation within the solar cells. Depending on the structural complexity of the solar cells, either numerical optical simulations (e.g., COMSOL Multiphysics, JCMsuite) or analytical approaches, such as the transfer matrix method, are employed to analyze their optical behavior. The solar cell is conceptualized as a multilayered structure composed of stratified isotropic media, with a surrogate machine learning model facilitating the differentiable modeling of its optical response. Specifically, a neural network is trained to predict the charge generation rate based on the incident angle, the thickness of the photoactive layer, and the wavelength of the incident light. Further details regarding this model are provided in Section S3.

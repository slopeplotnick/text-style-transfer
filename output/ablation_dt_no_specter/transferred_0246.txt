Benchmarking the power consumption of LLMs is fundamentally different from evaluating their operational speed or latency. Unlike traditional performance metrics, energy consumption is shaped by hardware heterogeneity, software stack complexity, and workload dynamics, all of which vary significantly across deployment environments. However, existing LLM evaluation methodologies often inadequately address these variations, as they typically adhere to predetermined configurations, fixed model sizes, and expensive instrumentation. As illustrated in Figure 1, TokenPowerBench overcomes these limitations through a three-layer architecture: Configuration, Execution & Measurement, and Report Generation. This methodology offers a modular, configurable, fully reproducible, and scalable approach tailored to modern LLM inference. Initially, TokenPowerBench facilitates the setup of the testing environment by enabling users to select the model, the inference engine, and the prompts to be employed. To streamline this process, TokenPowerBench provides three plug-and-play modules: a model pool, a prompt-dataset menu, and an inference-engine selector. The model pool includes a diverse range of large language models (LLMs), allowing users to evaluate how architectural variations influence energy consumption. This includes standard transformer-based architectures, such as the LLaMA series, alongside Mixture-of-Experts models, like Mixtral, which exhibit distinct computational characteristics in parameter activation and memory utilization. Moreover, the benchmark encompasses a broad spectrum of model sizes, from smaller models with fewer than one billion parameters, suitable for a single consumer-grade GPU, to frontier-scale models like Llama 3-405B, which require multi-GPU and/or multinode distributed inference. This approach facilitates the examination of variations in energy consumption as both models and hardware scale. Furthermore, the prompt-dataset menu provides built-in support for two representative datasets: Alpaca, featuring short, chat-style prompts, and LongBench, which includes extended contexts of up to 10,000 tokens. Users are also afforded the flexibility to integrate custom datasets in CSV or other compatible formats.

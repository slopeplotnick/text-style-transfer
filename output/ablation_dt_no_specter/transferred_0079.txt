The authors of a seminal work on Empirical Software Engineering [BRZ05] discuss the contributions of Victor R. Basili, a prominent figure in the field. Their analysis underscores the critical role of empirical studies, much like ours, and emphasizes the necessity of leveraging data and evidence to inform and enhance software engineering practices. As previously mentioned, numerous scholarly investigations have explored the implementation of Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE) within industrial settings and its implications for agile development methodologies. These studies have proposed a range of insightful procedures and strategic approaches to facilitate this transition, providing valuable guidance on advancing the migration process. However, none of the existing studies have conducted an empirical assessment akin to ours, particularly situated in the phase following adoption and preceding implementation. In this manuscript, we delineate the initial phase of an extensive initiative aimed at facilitating a company's transition of its codebase to a Software Product Line (SPL). We have conducted interviews meticulously tailored to the company's context, with particular attention to the diverse roles of the project stakeholders. Our objective was to ascertain the most effective strategies for preparing for the transition, concentrating on research questions that would illuminate current best practices upon which we can build, the perceived benefits and risks, and the concerns expressed by stakeholders. Developing appropriate responses to these risks and concerns, leveraging perceived benefits, and promoting best practices will be instrumental in facilitating a seamless transition. We identified that the organization already engages in commendable practices that will aid in this process and possesses a culture of innovation that will be advantageous for the project. The majority of stakeholders perceive the transition as beneficial. Nevertheless, the predominant concerns pertain to the complexity inherent in SPL and the challenges associated with its ongoing maintenance.

Understanding real-world scientific laws is crucial for accurately modeling physical outcomes under diverse conditions. However, our system is designed to identify overt errors, rather than engage in in-depth scientific analysis or assess kinematic processes. Consequently, deconstructing physics through specific criteria yields more accurate outcomes than general descriptions, underscoring the necessity for specialized methodologies to evaluate nuanced physical reasoning in video production. Therefore, VideoScore2 is not well-suited for our benchmark, as it lacks the rigorous, scientifically grounded evaluations required to ensure alignment with human judgments on this dataset. We provide qualitative examples to illustrate the performance of various models. The video adheres closely to the prescribed setup, demonstrating coherent temporal progression and effectively capturing the initiation and chromatic transitions characteristic of the reaction. Conversely, Hailuo 2.3, despite maintaining dynamism and spatial coherence, neglects critical elements of the prompt, resulting in a static video where the anticipated phenomenon fails to manifest. Moreover, even ostensibly simple physical configurations continue to present substantial challenges for contemporary models. Nevertheless, Sora-2 demonstrates commendable dynamism, spatiotemporal consistency, and structural coherence. In contrast, Veo-3 performs poorly in representing the experimental setup, though it momentarily alludes to the anticipated outward trajectory of the balls. This shortcoming underscores the inherent difficulty of accurately modeling even straightforward physical scenarios within the constraints of our benchmark.

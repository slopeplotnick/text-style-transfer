The project focuses on integrating new features and updates into the existing framework, encompassing both technical IT advancements and innovations in agronomy. The core objective is to facilitate rapid development processes, enabling the creation of applications at an accelerated pace compared to previous iterations. This aligns with the principles of software engineering, which combine factorization, reuse, innovation, and rapidity. Initially conceived as a reusable code base, the project supports the objectives of the migration initiative. Our analysis identified seven commendable practices, with Factorization and Reuse emerging as the most frequently mentioned, appearing nine and eight times, respectively, as illustrated in Figure 1. Understanding the advantages of Software Product Lines (SPL) is crucial, as it serves to motivate the team. Customization, a fundamental aspect of SPL, is frequently highlighted by professionals in Information Technology, User Experience, and Agronomy. An SPL is perceived as a platform capable of generating customized software solutions from pre-existing ones. Moreover, rapidity is the second most frequently emphasized concept by IT, PO, and UX professionals, signifying the ability to develop new software within a reduced timeframe. Additionally, automation has been highlighted, referring to the process of generating new software through automatic mechanisms. IT and PO professionals perceive automation and rapidity as interconnected. Furthermore, the concept of industrialization, as articulated by IT and UX experts, pertains to the on-demand and cost-effective production of applications. The enhancement of code quality, a concern exclusively raised by IT professionals, encompasses considerations of code homogeneity, testability, and reliability. Selling advantages, identified by both IT professionals and Product Owners (POs), signify the potential to enhance software sales or promptly respond to emerging opportunities.

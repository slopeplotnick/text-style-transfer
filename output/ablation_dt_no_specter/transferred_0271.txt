The taxonomy defines five parent categories that decompose into 16 concrete edit types, ensuring coverage from basic text formatting to complex editing workflows. In line with the approach of Maia Polo et al., we prioritize fewer but more comprehensive cases to balance high-difficulty scenarios rather than inflate totals. These cases range from simple text replacements to intricate, multimodal reasoning challenges. For example, one cross-modal case involves matching images to captions across slides using both visual and textual cues; many cases overlap multiple taxonomy buckets, leading to a category count exceeding 100. PPTArena sets itself apart from previous benchmarks by emphasizing edit difficulty across four key dimensions: the depth of multi-step reasoning, the extent of cross-slide dependencies, the requirements for semantic understanding, and the complexity of long-horizon planning. While alternative tools like PPTC-R and T2US focus on rapid, template-driven edits, PPTArena deliberately concentrates difficulty into fewer, richer scenarios to reveal real-world failures. Unlike others, PPTArena integrates both human-authored and Python-generated decks, offers ground truth for deterministic evaluation, and ensures cross-platform compatibility. It uniquely incorporates accessibility constraints, external knowledge requirements, and complex multimodal tasks that emphasize multi-step reasoning, cross-slide dependencies, and long-horizon planning. This design uncovers failure modes hidden in simpler tasks, providing a rigorous testbed for evaluating advanced systems. We demonstrate two representative samples in Figures 2 and 3. Our evaluation framework moves beyond simple pixel- or code-level comparisons, which fail to capture the semantic and aesthetic goals of PPT editing. Instead, we assess an agent's performance on two fundamental axes: Instruction Following and Visual Quality, both scored by expert judges on an integer scale from 0 (Failure) to 5 (Perfect).

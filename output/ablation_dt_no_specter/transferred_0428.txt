Table 3 presents a comparative analysis of evaluation scores derived from various configurations of VideoScience-Judge against human annotations. With the implementation of GPT-5 pro, score inflation is less pronounced; however, significant discrepancies persist in dimensions such as Dynamism, Coherence, and Immutability, where the model tends to overestimate its performance. Incorporating a checklist-based approach and requiring the model to substantiate its ratings with evidence from key frames noticeably reduces these overestimations. Moreover, integrating computer vision tools like ByteTrack, which facilitates reliable identity tracking across frames and provides substantial evidence for penalizing frame discontinuities or unexpected object transformations, significantly ameliorates the scoring discrepancies, with improvements of 0.07 in Coherence and 0.09 in Immutability compared to the standard VideoScience-Judge without the checklist. Consequently, this leads to score quantization and reduced discrimination among similar models. However, the rule-based approach, which scrutinizes explicit violations of discrete physical laws (e.g., "Does the ball fall downward?" or "Does water flow correctly?"), fails to encapsulate the holistic aspects of physical realism, such as consistent motion trajectories, causal relationships across time, acceleration patterns, momentum conservation, and nuances of motion smoothness that humans inherently evaluate. These limitations are particularly pronounced in VideoScience-Bench, which fundamentally diverges from PhyGenBench's simplified, single-principle scenarios. VideoScience-Bench encompasses multiple scientific concepts, such as multi-object interactions and fluid dynamics, where outcomes emerge from the integration of multiple laws rather than isolated principles. This complexity is characterized by cascading effects, wherein second-order dynamics play a significant role.

Despite the promising potential of artificial intelligence on mobile devices for mental health monitoring, significant gaps in diagnosis persist globally. The utilization of AI offers rapid, personalized feedback while safeguarding user privacy by operating directly on devices, thus circumventing the need to transmit sensitive data to cloud servers. However, barriers such as limited clinic hours, geographic inaccessibility, and workforce shortages continue to hinder timely assessment and early self-awareness. Moreover, the integration of these tools into mobile platforms significantly improves accessibility, particularly for individuals residing in remote areas or facing extended wait times for clinical appointments. The increasing use of smartphones and wearable devices for mental health monitoring is attributed to their superior latency, energy efficiency, and data privacy compared to cloud-dependent systems. Nevertheless, recent empirical investigations have highlighted the efficacy of small language models (SLMs) on devices, such as the Samsung Galaxy S24, in document assistance and health prediction, optimizing memory usage and operational speed. These models are advantageous for real-world applications due to their ease of deployment and minimal resource requirements. Furthermore, their ability to operate independently of continuous cloud connectivity enhances data privacy protections. While prior research has demonstrated the capacity of SLMs to address specific mental health-related tasks, there remain significant gaps in their coverage and applicability across the broader spectrum of mental health inference. The majority of existing research has predominantly concentrated on relatively simple tasks, demonstrating limited efficacy when applied to more complex and heterogeneous datasets. Furthermore, there has been insufficient exploration of model performance on resource-constrained devices, posing significant challenges for real-world deployment. To address these limitations, we propose Menta, a novel framework that leverages the Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) technique to effectively manage multitask learning and accommodate data heterogeneity. Therefore, the system is optimized to perform effectively across diverse mental health applications, maintaining computational efficiency and ensuring privacy preservation on commonly used devices.

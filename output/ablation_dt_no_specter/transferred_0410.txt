Cross-model and cross-algorithm generalization. The consistent improvements observed on both Qwen2.5 and Llama backbones demonstrate the architecture-agnostic nature of our framework. Notably, even the smaller Llama-3.2-1B model achieves significant accuracy gains with OptPO-SFT and OptPO compared to baseline methods, highlighting the robust scalability of the proposed adaptation mechanism across varying model sizes. Furthermore, the OptPO framework integrates seamlessly with diverse RL algorithms, such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, and consistently outperforms TTRL in both accuracy and efficiency, underscoring its versatility across different RL methodologies. SFT-based vs. RL-based variants. In general, RL-based OptPO variants deliver superior accuracy compared to OptPO-SFT, particularly on mathematical reasoning benchmarks. This advantage arises from the ability of policy-gradient adaptation to better exploit stochastic feedback from rollouts, as opposed to the deterministic pseudo-label fine-tuning approach. Nevertheless, OptPO-SFT achieves stable and consistent improvements over TTSFT across all settings, validating the effectiveness of the adaptive pseudo-labeling mechanism in enhancing SFT-style test-time training. Takeaways. Our findings reveal that (1) adaptive early-stopping via BSPRT significantly improves computational efficiency without compromising accuracy; (2) OptPO strikes an optimal balance between accuracy and computational cost; and (3) OptPO-SFT offers a lightweight and expedient alternative for rapid adaptation in scenarios where RL-style updates are impractical. Together, these contributions establish a unified and efficient paradigm for test-time training in verifiable reasoning tasks. This paper introduces OptPO, the first statistically grounded framework for efficient test-time policy optimization, addressing the inefficiencies of majority-vote-based rollouts in test-time reinforcement learning for LLMs. By leveraging a Bayesian sequential probability ratio test for early-stopping, OptPO dynamically adjusts rollouts based on posterior evidence rather than fixed thresholds, ensuring the generation of reliable pseudo-labels while avoiding unnecessary sampling. The framework is simple, plug-and-play, and compatible with established policy optimization methods such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, as well as supervised test-time fine-tuning. Across diverse benchmarks and model architectures, OptPO consistently maintains or improves accuracy while achieving significant computational savings. More broadly, this work highlights that efficient, label-free adaptation is both feasible and increasingly critical as LLMs are deployed in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments. We anticipate that OptPO will serve as a foundational framework for advancing research on principled and compute-efficient test-time learning.

Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) is the task of identifying a target object based on a natural language description and is a significant area of research within vision-language understanding. Recent advancements have moved beyond basic lexical matching, emphasizing deeper semantic interpretation and the integration of external knowledge sources to enhance comprehension. Generalized Referring Expression Comprehension (GREC) addresses the interpretation of expressions that may refer to zero, one, or multiple objects, as exemplified by the instruction, "Pick up those three boxes." This framework aligns more closely with naturalistic communication, where referents may be ambiguous, occluded, or entirely absent from the visual scene. However, advancing research in this domain typically necessitates extensive datasets comprising real-world images paired with human-generated annotations, which can be resource-intensive to collect. To alleviate these challenges, online crowdsourcing platforms provide controlled and reproducible environments for conducting visual grounding experiments, thereby reducing the costs and variability associated with traditional data collection methods. Yet, the task becomes significantly more complex when models are required to jointly interpret a visual scene, maintain conversational context, and engage in multi-turn interactions. Under these conditions, existing methodologies exhibit notable limitations. Recent work introduced a novel GREC benchmark, grounded in multi-turn dialogues within a Minecraft-based environment, demonstrating that this task presents substantial challenges for contemporary AI systems. Moreover, the process of eliciting human-generated dialogues is resource-intensive, resulting in a paucity of training data. Consequently, this paper proposes an innovative data augmentation approach that synthesizes succinct descriptions and elaborate dialogues to enhance AI comprehension. Although previous research on AI-driven data generation is pertinent, applying these methodologies to GREC presents significant challenges due to the necessity for precise modeling of spatial relationships and the accurate grounding of these elements within the visual scene.

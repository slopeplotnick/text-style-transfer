Distribution matching provides an alternative to motion tracking by training controllers to replicate the broader behavioral distribution of a motion dataset rather than adhering to reference motions on a frame-by-frame basis. Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) approximates this goal by employing a learned discriminator to distinguish between the agent's motions and those present in the dataset. Building on this, Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) enable controllers to generate realistic behaviors for novel tasks not found in the original dataset by modeling flexible style objectives. However, adversarial priors require joint training with a specific policy tailored to each new task, necessitating retraining and continuous access to the dataset. In contrast, our approach achieves comparable performance to these methods by introducing a reusable and modular motion prior, which can also be adapted to include stylistic variations absent from the original dataset. Diffusion models, known for their strong performance across various domains, have recently been extended to control tasks. These models can generate high-quality motion, making them effective as motion planners that predict future trajectories for execution by a tracking controller. Moreover, diffusion models have been used to directly model controllers, enabling flexible and diverse behaviors for tasks such as manipulation and character control. Unlike prior approaches that integrate diffusion models into controllers, our method repurposes pretrained diffusion models as task-agnostic behavioral priors within an optimization framework. Specifically, we utilize these models as a reward function in reinforcement learning, guiding policy learning through score distillation sampling.

The selection of negative samples encompasses all potential active nodes. In Section 5, we demonstrate that integrating both random and adversarial negatives is essential to exceed the performance of TextMatch. We formulate the training objective as a cross-entropy loss where, for each query, the model must identify its corresponding positive candidate among all candidates in the batch. The encoder is initialized from publicly available E5-unsupervised checkpoints, which include small (33M parameters) and large (330M parameters) models, themselves products of advanced contrastive pretraining. We use the same encoder weights for both queries and candidates. Prior to the fine-tuning phase, dense interaction data derived from 110 million samples, based on weak signals such as clicks or saves and naturally occurring pairs of titles and descriptions, is utilized. During the fine-tuning phase, the model leverages a reduced set of high-quality interactions that reflect mutual engagement, such as interviews or contract initiations, alongside hard negative samples derived from items displayed but not engaged with, as identified in interaction logs. The training dataset for GraphMatch comprises a comprehensive collection of one year of user interaction data from the Upwork platform, partitioned temporally into 10 months for training, 1 month for validation, and 1 month for evaluation. This dataset encapsulates nodes representing freelancers, clients, and job postings, as well as edges corresponding to various interaction types, including job postings, applications, invitations, interviews, and contract commencements. Freelancer and job post nodes are characterized by textual attributes, namely the profile overview and job description, respectively. Additionally, all nodes encompass supplementary numerical attributes pertinent to their classification, such as the freelancer's hourly rate, the client's geographical location, and the job post category, among others. Notably, these textual and numerical attributes are subject to temporal variation. In total, the dataset comprises approximately 9 million nodes, each with 32 million temporal feature versions and 62 million edges. GraphMatch is trained utilizing robust labels derived from contractual agreements between freelancers and job postings.

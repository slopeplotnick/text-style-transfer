Experiments show that ReVSeg delivers state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on multiple standard VOS benchmarks, surpassing both latent-based models and training-free explicit models, with auditable reasoning traces. As illustrated in Figure 1, controlled ablations demonstrate that without our decomposed reasoning chain, both the base model and its RL post-trained variant tend to fail on complex reasoning VOS tasks. In contrast, our framework achieves strong performance even before RL, and the RL post-training further delivers substantial improvement. In summary, our contributions are as follows: First, we introduce a principled decomposition of reasoning VOS, reformulating video segmentation as an explicit multi-step reasoning chain built from native model primitives. Second, we develop a novel RL framework that optimizes the reasoning chain itself, allowing the model to refine decision quality without requiring dense supervision. Third, we set a new benchmark for performance on VOS evaluations while providing interpretable reasoning traces. Fine-grained text-guided VOS has advanced through specialized segmentation architectures and referring-video pipelines. However, robust reasoning in complex videos—such as those with multiple similar objects, occlusion, fast motion, and long-range context—remains challenging. Recent methods, like CoT-RVS, attempt to address these complexities by employing two independent systems for frame-by-frame processing, interconnected through textual elements. While effective, such designs are bounded by the separated information flow and module interoperability and are constrained to be training-free. In contrast, our approach formulates reasoning VOS as an explicit reasoning chain within a unified framework, employing reinforcement learning for self-improvement.

The prior-conforming prompt reinforces a belief aligned with the model's likely initial stance, serving both as a sense check and as a potential lower bound for belief entrenchment and accuracy. In contrast, the critical-thinking prompt encourages openness to counter-evidence and rational belief revision. All prompts used in the experiments are provided in Appendix C. This section presents our empirical findings. Initially, we establish an association between belief entrenchment, as measured by the Martingale Score, and declines in ground-truth accuracy, thereby substantiating the significance of belief entrenchment. Subsequently, we benchmark a range of closed- and open-weights models across different problem domains and setups, attributing belief entrenchment to various factors such as models, prompts, and reasoning techniques. Furthermore, we confirm the validity of using LLMs as evaluators. Notably, we observe more severe belief entrenchment in value-laden domains such as r/ChangeMyView, where the Martingale Score consistently exceeds that in Forecasting, suggesting that belief entrenchment poses a more serious concern in domains lacking ground-truth evaluation. We also aimed to manipulate belief entrenchment by employing a critical-thinking prompt and a prior-conforming prompt. Our observations indicate that belief entrenchment is more pronounced under a prior-conforming prompt and less severe under a critical-thinking prompt in Forecasting, though this pattern does not hold in r/ChangeMyView or OpenReview. Further discussion on factors influencing belief entrenchment is available in Appendix B. As the ultimate aim of reasoning in LLMs is to arrive at true beliefs, we demonstrate that belief entrenchment undermines this objective. This relationship is examined in domains where ground truth labels are accessible, such as Forecasting and OpenReview. While outcome-based metrics like the Brier Score assess model performance, they offer limited insight into the reasons behind such performance, necessitating process-based metrics on reasoning. Our analysis reveals a positive correlation between the Martingale Score and Brier Score in Forecasting, suggesting that belief entrenchment is a significant factor compromising LLM reasoning.

As we increasingly rely on AI tools powered by large language models, ensuring their secure and safe deployment has become a top priority. This need becomes particularly pronounced when these models are implemented in multilingual and multimodal environments, where diverse linguistic and communicative contexts can introduce novel risks. Therefore, it is essential to develop systems capable of effectively filtering harmful, biased, or unsafe outputs across a broad spectrum of languages and user scenarios. Without such comprehensive systems, the global deployment of these models may inadvertently result in harm or exclusion for numerous individuals. Most existing research has predominantly focused on safety measures within high-resource languages such as English, Chinese, Spanish, German, and French. However, the majority of the world's languages fall into the low-resource category, characterized by a paucity of data, which poses significant challenges to the evaluation and maintenance of safety protocols in multilingual environments. While certain evaluations exist to assess the efficacy of these models across various languages, they remain inadequate in addressing languages with limited data and fail to capture the linguistic diversity of global users. Moreover, the creation of training datasets for low-resource languages, essential for the development of robust safety systems, has been largely overlooked. Recent initiatives have focused on generating training data for high-resource languages through both authentic and translated sources; however, these efforts are insufficient to establish truly secure systems. Another critical challenge with contemporary safety systems is their substantial computational demands. These systems are typically too large, slow, and resource-intensive to operate effectively on edge devices or in low-power environments. Consequently, their prolonged operational times present challenges for practical application in real-world settings. These challenges underscore the necessity for developing smaller, more efficient systems capable of safeguarding both the integrity of the language model and the well-being of its users.

To address these challenges, we introduce HEALTHCONTRADICT, a dataset comprising 920 unique instances, each featuring a health-related question paired with two documents that present contradictory perspectives. Each instance also includes a factual answer grounded in scientific evidence. Using this dataset, we evaluated several language models, ranging from 1B to 8B parameters, to assess their ability to answer health-related questions in the presence of conflicting information. Specifically, our benchmark investigates: (i) how effectively language models respond to biomedical questions when faced with contradictory information, and (ii) the extent to which the provided biomedical context influences their answers. To achieve this, we designed experiments where models were presented with contexts that were either correct, incorrect, or contradictory, and subsequently measured their accuracy and response patterns. Our contributions are threefold: (i) the creation of a novel dataset—HEALTHCONTRADICT—tailored to evaluate language models in the context of conflicting biomedical information; (ii) a thorough assessment of these models using HEALTHCONTRADICT, focusing on their capacity to reason over complex, conflicting biomedical contexts with the aid of interpretable quantitative metrics; and (iii) a comparative analysis demonstrating that fine-tuned biomedical language models outperform their general domain counterparts by better leveraging accurate information while resisting erroneous data. To ensure the integrity and fairness of the evaluation process, each document in the dataset is unique. For example, in response to the question, "Can coffee help you lose weight?" the dataset provides two conflicting documents—one supporting the claim and the other opposing it—alongside a factual answer, supported by scientific evidence, which states that caffeine intake may promote reductions in weight, body mass index (BMI), and body fat.

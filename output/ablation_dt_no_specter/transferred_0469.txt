Probabilistic programming languages (PPL) are characterized by their incorporation of first-class constructs for probabilistic elements, including distributions and their samplers. Numerous prominent examples exist, encompassing both imperative and functional paradigms. However, a significant limitation of existing languages is their inadequate adaptability to text-rich graphs, which are essential data structures for defining specifications in language model systems (LMS). This shortcoming necessitated the formulation of LUMOS. These languages are typically generative, relying on user-defined probability distributions. For instance, one such language is employed to generate random images or scenes, particularly for applications like autonomous vehicle systems, utilizing user-defined scene graphs. To formulate real-world specifications, we employ a static graph from which we extract specific segments to delineate scenarios. Domain-specific languages (DSLs) for large language models (LLMs) function as query languages that lack probabilistic characteristics. These DSLs facilitate the composition, optimization, and security of prompts to elicit the desired responses from a target LMS. In contrast, LUMOS operates in conjunction with systems that assess LMS behaviors over distributions of prompts programmatically defined by LUMOS. The process of LMS alignment entails the identification of LMS vulnerabilities through performance evaluations or adversarial testing. Recent advancements have introduced probabilistic certifiers for specific LLM properties, offering formal assurances for the assessment of LMS attributes. In this study, we extend LMS certification through LUMOS to delineate and authenticate arbitrary desirable properties.

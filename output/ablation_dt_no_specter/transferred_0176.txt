The functioning of this system is predicated on the interaction of the mother agent with other agents, based on the grammar it has already acquired. This design represents a novel approach, as conventional methodologies predominantly rely on the input of extensive corpora for language acquisition processes. In this paper, the term "language model" is used in a broader sense, encompassing rule-based systems, statistical models, and neural networks, rather than limiting it to standard computational frameworks designed for predictive text generation, such as GPT or BERT. This distinction is crucial because our system utilizes explicit linguistic rules, whereas the learning agent employs statistical methods to infer these rules. The study investigates whether the MODOMA daughter agent can acquire and demonstrate diverse linguistic components through the application of specific distributions within the MODOMA framework. Specifically, we examine whether a multi-agent language acquisition system can discern linguistic components from machine-generated sentences and subsequently represent them through formalized rules. To address this question, we assess whether these sentences exhibit patterns analogous to human language and determine the requisite amount of data to identify such patterns. Furthermore, we explore the potential transformation of these patterns into systematic rules. Finally, we evaluate whether the identified categories correspond to the original grammatical framework and assess the system's capacity to independently acquire structured linguistic knowledge.

User-Centered Evaluations: Since hallucinations can significantly undermine user trust in a system, it becomes essential to prioritize the user's experience when such errors occur. A recent study evaluates hallucinations in the context of language models, focusing on scenarios where users actively detect and report these inaccuracies. This approach introduces novel user-oriented metrics that assess the impact of hallucinations, including the erosion of trust and the cognitive burden of self-verification—tasks that the system is intended to handle. Evaluation of Hallucinations: A More Inclusive Framework for Hallucination Evaluation: To address and mitigate hallucinations, researchers have developed more integrated frameworks that incorporate diverse evaluation methods. For instance, [23] demonstrates that the performance of large language models (LLMs) can be comprehensively assessed through techniques such as factuality checks, consistency analysis, and user experience evaluations. This multifaceted approach enables the identification of hallucinations from various angles, considering not only factual accuracy but also the coherence of arguments and the subjective user experience. Applications of LLMs in Different Scenarios: While it is crucial to account for the potential occurrence of hallucinations when deploying LLMs, their suitability also depends on the specific requirements of the task. For general-purpose applications, such as conversational agents, models like GPT-4 and PaLM are commonly used due to their ability to generate fluent, human-like text with a relatively low incidence of errors, while incorporating an appropriate level of creativity. Conversely, for tasks that demand high factual accuracy, such as information retrieval or question answering, models like BERT and T5 are preferred for their grounded comprehension and emphasis on factual consistency.

This plugin-style architecture promotes code reusability and enables fast prototyping of novel PEFT methods. Researchers can develop and test new methods separately, with the framework automatically integrating them at runtime. The dynamic loading approach has proven particularly valuable for comparative studies, allowing researchers to evaluate multiple PEFT variants under identical experimental conditions without code duplication or version control conflicts. From the SuperGLUE benchmark, we include seven datasets separated into four tasks: natural language inference (NLI) - CB; question answering - MultiRC, ReCoRD, BoolQ, COPA; word sense disambiguation - WiC; and coreference resolution - WSC. Moreover, we incorporate text generation datasets commonly used to benchmark generative LLMs. To this end, we cover six datasets for reasoning and natural language understanding, divided into three tasks: question answering – MMLU, PIQA, SIQA, OBQA; natural language inference (NLI) – Hellaswag; and commonsense reasoning – WinoGrande. Additionally, the corpus includes three datasets for mathematical problem-solving, categorized into three tasks: question answering – MathQA; mathematical word problems – GSM8K; and simple math problems – SVAMP. Furthermore, there are three datasets for code generation, namely Conala, CodeAlpacaPy, and APPS. Some datasets may require further (mostly minor) format changes to be compatible with the input formatting of PEFT-FACTORY. We further describe this preprocessing in Appendix B.2.

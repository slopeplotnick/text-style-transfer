The datasets included five configurations: one with the complete set of foundational units, one without events, one without relationships, one without entities, and one without the story outline. Both qualitative and quantitative metrics were employed to evaluate the models. Specifically, the same test dataset, consisting of 196 samples, was used to assess the performance of the fine-tuned models. For qualitative evaluation, the generated stories were assessed based on their functionality and artistic quality. The stories from the preference dataset ("Chosen") were used as a baseline for comparison, enabling a quantitative analysis of the similarity between the stories generated by different fine-tuned models. This approach allowed us to examine the impact of the four foundational units on story generation. For the quantitative evaluation, we primarily analyzed the similarity between the narratives generated by the fine-tuned models using the same JSON inputs and the original text. Each fine-tuned model generated three narratives per JSON input, with the original text serving as the benchmark. Similarity was assessed using three metrics: ROUGE-L, BERTScore, and METEOR Score. ROUGE-L measures the similarity between two sequences by identifying the length of their longest common subsequence. BERTScore evaluates semantic similarity, addressing limitations of surface-level matching. METEOR Score combines multiple matching techniques, offering enhanced flexibility and robustness. As illustrated in Figure 7, the comparative performance of various configurations against the baseline is presented. The configuration incorporating all foundational units demonstrated superior performance in content control, semantic consistency, and linguistic naturalness in story generation. To evaluate the usability of TaleFrame, we conducted a structured study involving two experts and seventeen volunteers, as described in Section 3. To facilitate the creative process and minimize challenges related to inspiration, volunteers were provided with a variety of images and allowed to select topics of personal interest. Using TaleFrame, they generated narratives based on their chosen images. If the generated narratives did not meet their expectations, participants could modify the attributes of the four foundational units to create stories that better aligned with their creative goals. Throughout the study, volunteers were required to engage with all interactive features offered by TaleFrame.

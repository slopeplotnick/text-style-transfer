Our test-time scaling framework, which seeks the highest-density action, is not merely speculative; it constitutes a rigorous and theoretically sound realization of the anti-exploration objective standard in Offline RL. The empirical achievements documented by Lobel et al. (2023) and Bai et al. (2025) substantiate the efficacy of employing pseudo-counts in optimizing LLM preferences. In alignment with this paradigm, we implement a Coin Flipping Network (CFN) to facilitate this process. As illustrated in Figure 3, the pseudo-counts generated by our estimator demonstrate a strong correlation with both the L2 distance to the actual action and the pseudo-counts derived from our CFN. This confirms that the CFN is capable of effectively verifying and penalizing unsupported actions. The primary objective of our experiments is to evaluate whether the integration of Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-COunits enhances the accuracy of action prediction and subsequently improves task success rates. Moreover, we aim to investigate the impact of specific design decisions on these improvements and assess the feasibility of the associated computational overhead. Specifically, our experimental inquiries are structured to address the following research questions: Q1: Can our framework improve performance across diverse environments, various VLC policies, and a wide range of tasks? Q2: What is the additional temporal cost incurred by our test-time scaling method, and does it maintain efficacy on real robotic systems while preserving performance enhancements? Q3: To what extent do specific design decisions, such as the incorporation of internal features and the utilization of the CFN pseudo-count network, influence overall system performance?

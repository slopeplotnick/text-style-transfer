All prompts were composed directly in the first language (L1) of each respective language team, even though the interface was presented in English. This approach eliminated the need for translation during the prompt creation process. Once a team member completed a specific domain path, they proceeded to formulate the prompts in their respective L1. Before starting the prompt creation, all team members participated in a brief sample project to ensure a comprehensive understanding of the task requirements. Subsequently, each team generated a minimum of 2,000 prompts upon fully grasping the project objectives. The scripted prompts were derived from culturally relevant and domain-specific sources, ensuring diversity in areas such as agriculture and health. These sources included a government publication detailing public services, Wikipedia articles in South African languages, a database for precise vocabulary, and translated manuals on animal health and farming practices. The data for the prompts underwent a systematic cleaning and preparation process to ensure its suitability for training speech recognition models. Each stage of refinement focused on enhancing data quality by eliminating extraneous elements, categorizing text into thematic clusters, and identifying or annotating personal information. Upon data ingestion, preliminary quality assessments were conducted, including comprehensive reviews to detect and address any structural inconsistencies. The dataset was filtered to retain only the target South African languages, and instances where the highest-ranked language did not align with the expected category were excluded. Confidence scores were preserved as supplementary information to support the filtering process.

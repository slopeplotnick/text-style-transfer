Our comprehensive evaluation reaffirms that spatial methods, particularly covariance tangent-space projection and Common Spatial Patterns, remain the gold standard for decoding EEG motor imagery under controlled conditions. However, their performance significantly deteriorates in the face of substantial inter- and intra-subject variability. Through a detailed subject-specific analysis, we conclusively demonstrate that no single configuration universally accommodates all individuals. Instead, our findings highlight the necessity of adopting adaptive methodologies that dynamically align with the unique neurophysiological signatures of each user. Moreover, certain nonlinear features, such as Hjorth parameters, Higuchi fractal dimension, and singular value decomposition entropy, exhibited notable efficacy for specific individuals. This underscores the potential of leveraging richer neural dynamics to enhance personalized decoding and suggests that "BCI illiteracy" may stem more from mismatches between decoding algorithms and user-specific neurophysiological characteristics than from inherent user limitations. Consequently, future research should prioritize the development of sophisticated ensemble learning frameworks that integrate linear spatial filters with nonlinear dynamics, effectively harnessing their complementary strengths. Expanding this benchmark to encompass broader paradigms, including SSVEP, P300, CVEP, and invasive intracortical recordings, will be essential to further validate the generalizability of these findings. Additionally, rigorous evaluation of state-of-the-art deep-learning architectures, such as CNNs, LSTMs, and Transformers, alongside emerging quantum-inspired models, including quantum support-vector machines and hybrid quantum neural networks, represents a critical frontier. These quantum methodologies, by exploiting the high-dimensional properties of Hilbert spaces, hold significant promise for mitigating the inherent noise and complexity of EEG signals. Furthermore, enhancing cross-subject generalization through transfer learning and domain adaptation techniques remains a pivotal challenge, as these approaches have shown considerable potential to improve model robustness and scalability across diverse user populations. Finally, advancing reproducibility is imperative, necessitating the adoption of open-access benchmarking frameworks such as MOABB, which enable standardized and transparent evaluations of EEG decoding algorithms.

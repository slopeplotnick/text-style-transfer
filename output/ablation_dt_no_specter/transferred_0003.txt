An empirical survey was conducted on seven model-merging methodologies designed to mitigate social bias, evaluated using three bias benchmarks and the SuperGLUE framework across 13 large language models. Among these approaches, SLERP with moderate interpolation weights emerged as the most balanced method, achieving significant bias reduction without compromising downstream accuracy. However, it remains crucial to validate the performance of these methodologies on tasks such as reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning to ensure effective social bias mitigation. Recently, model merging has gained traction as a cost-efficient strategy for combining the strengths of multiple models without requiring computationally expensive retraining (Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024). This process involves fusing the parameters of two or more pre-trained models to create a unified model that retains and integrates the knowledge or skills of its sources. The linear averaging method, introduced by Wortsman et al. (2022), represents a foundational approach in this domain, treating model weights as vectors and combining them through simple arithmetic averaging. This method offers a practical and resource-efficient way to incorporate diverse expertise by leveraging pre-trained models without additional training. Building on this concept, studies by Matena and Raffel (2022) and Lee et al. (2025) proposed weighting parameters inversely to their Fisher information, ensuring that the resulting combinations are probabilistically consistent. Alternatively, sphere interpolation-based methods (Shoemake, 1985; Goddard et al., 2024; Grove and Karcher, 1973) conceptualize parameter vectors as residing on a spherical manifold. SLERP, introduced by Shoemake in 1985, enables interpolation between two models, while the Karcher Mean, proposed by Grove and Karcher in 1973, iteratively identifies the Riemannian centroid for multiple models. More recently, NuSLERP, developed by Goddard et al. in 2024, incorporates per-tensor normalization to address norm drift. Drawing inspiration from these merging techniques, Ilharco et al. (2022) introduced a task-specific framework based on the concept of a "task vector," which captures the difference between a pre-trained model and its fine-tuned counterpart.

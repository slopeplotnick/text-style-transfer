Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being utilized as automated evaluators for assessing the outputs of other generative systems, a paradigm known as "Thinking-LLM-as-a-Judge." This approach offers a scalable and cost-effective alternative to human evaluation, which is often slow and expensive. To mitigate the inherent variability and potential inaccuracies of single-instance judgments, a common strategy is to leverage substantial computational resources to generate multiple evaluations and ratings for each item under review. However, the reliability of the final decision critically depends on the methodology employed to synthesize these disparate outcomes. Conventional aggregation techniques, such as majority voting or reliance on model confidence scores, frequently exhibit fragility and statistical inefficiency, especially in scenarios characterized by ties. For instance, merely tallying votes fails to differentiate between a narrow 5-to-4 decision and a unanimous 9-to-0 verdict, thereby overlooking critical insights regarding the evidential strength. This disregard for the strength of evidence results in evaluations that are less reliable and robust. Therefore, we assert that the aggregation of outcomes is crucial for the effective utilization of computational resources. We propose a novel approach termed Distribution-Calibrated Aggregation, which transcends mere vote-counting methodologies. This approach meticulously considers the entirety of positive, negative, and tie votes, thereby preserving the comprehensive informational content inherent in the voting data. Specifically, we model the three-way preference outcomes using a formulation that explicitly incorporates both the preference margin and the global propensity for ties. By optimizing the evaluation parameters through a preliminary calibration on a small validation set and employing a tailored error metric, the proposed methodology ensures alignment with the overarching evaluation objectives while maintaining robust data conformity.

We conducted adversarial training on both CNN and ViT architectures using identical protocols to examine the impact of such training on the transferability of adversarial attacks. Contrary to the intuitive expectation that robustly trained models would produce less transferable attacks, our findings reveal a surprising trend: adversarially trained (AT) models generate adversarial examples that transfer significantly more effectively than those from standard trained (ST) counterparts. This result suggests that the robust features learned through adversarial training may unintentionally introduce universal vulnerabilities that span across different model architectures, as illustrated in Figure 1. Our contributions are as follows: (1) We performed large-scale adversarial training on 36 models, encompassing both CNN and ViT architectures. To promote reproducibility and further research, all trained models and the corresponding code will be made publicly available on GitHub. (2) We demonstrate that AT models produce more transferable attacks, presenting a security paradox that challenges conventional assumptions about model robustness. Furthermore, we propose a novel attack method, Momentum Integrated Gradient (MIG), which builds upon the integrated gradient (IG) framework. MIG employs a momentum-based iterative approach guided by IG to prioritize critical features, thereby increasing the likelihood of inducing misclassifications. A key property of IG, known as implementation invariance, enables MIG attacks to transfer effectively across diverse architectures, including CNNs and ViTs, as well as between these model types. This level of transferability was not achievable with prior attack methods such as PGD, as shown in Figure 2. IG, an axiomatic interpretability method, identifies the most influential features contributing to a model’s predictions by accumulating gradients along a linear path from a baseline input to the actual input, where the baseline serves as a reference point.

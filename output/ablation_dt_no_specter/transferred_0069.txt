We conducted a comparative analysis of our proposed methodology against the RePro approach, utilizing the results reported in the original publication due to the unavailability of their implementation. P2C denotes Paper2Code. While the Refine approach requires manually designing separate evaluation and refinement prompts for each component of the process—resulting in eight handcrafted prompts for merely four planning tasks in the original Paper2Code implementation—our methodology automatically adapts to each task by directly leveraging its corresponding system prompt, requiring zero additional prompt engineering. Our approach was empirically evaluated across two distinct datasets. The first benchmark, Paper2CodeBench, comprises 90 papers selected from prestigious 2024 conferences, including ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS, filtered based on the availability of code and their length. Our generated code repositories were evaluated against the official implementations released by the authors, utilizing LLM-based judges to assess correctness on a 5-point scale. The second dataset, PaperBench Code-Dev, consists of 20 papers from ICML 2024, wherein human reviewers have evaluated the correctness of the generated code based on predefined grading rubrics. To the best of our knowledge, there are limited existing methodologies that utilize iterative self-refinement for the automated transformation of research papers into code. Therefore, we have compared our approach with two analogous methodologies that incorporate self-refinement: Paper2Code in conjunction with Self-Refine. Self-Refine constitutes an iterative approach. All experimental evaluations were conducted utilizing GPT-4.1 as the foundational model. For the RePro baseline, due to the absence of accessible source code, the results were directly extracted from the original publication. Tables I and II provide a comparative analysis of the performance of various optimization strategies across the two datasets. Our approach demonstrates superior reliability and robustness across diverse datasets. Notably, even when optimization was restricted to the planning stage, our method (Paper2Code with autoplan optimization) consistently demonstrated measurable improvements. Figure 2 provides a comprehensive per-task analysis of performance on PaperBench, illustrating the consistent efficacy of our planning optimization approach. Specifically, our method outperformed Self-Refine on 16 out of 20 tasks (80%), underscoring its robustness across diverse paper reproduction scenarios. Conversely, Self-Refine exhibited suboptimal performance across 10 tasks, with particularly pronounced deficiencies observed in certain specific cases.

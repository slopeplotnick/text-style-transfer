Sampling different noise vectors in flow-matching or diffusion-based frameworks is crucial, as the stochasticity of the initial noise directly influences the resulting actions. In this paper, we tackle the problem of distributional shifts from an anti-exploration perspective and introduce Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-Counts (TACO). The anti-exploration principle, inspired by offline reinforcement learning (RL), aims to prevent policies from visiting states or actions outside the dataset's support. Similarly, during VLA inference, it is essential to constrain generated actions to align with the successful patterns present in the SFT dataset, thereby avoiding redundant or irrelevant action modes inherited from pretraining or imperfect fine-tuning data. To achieve this, TACO employs test-time scaling (TTS) rather than policy optimization, as the intricate sampling dynamics of flow-matching or diffusion-based VLA models are not readily compatible with standard RL-style updates. Instead, TTS modifies the action sampling process without altering the VLA's parameters, eliminating the need for gradient computation. To implement this, we utilize a classical Coin-Flipping Network (CFN) augmented with an internal representation mechanism to estimate pseudo-counts for every observation-instruction-action-chunk pair in the SFT dataset. Notably, an action chunk with a higher pseudo-count indicates greater consistency with frequently observed successful behaviors in the SFT data and is therefore preferred. During inference, TACO leverages CFN-derived pseudo-counts to identify the most reliable action chunk among multiple candidates, ultimately selecting the one with the highest pseudo-count for execution. Furthermore, to address the computational overhead of repeated forward passes at test time, we introduce a shared observation key-value cache, which significantly enhances efficiency by reusing visual-language representations across samples.

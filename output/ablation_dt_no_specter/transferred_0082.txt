While GRAPHECTORY applies broadly to any agentic system, we focus on agentic programming systems, specifically SWE-agent and OpenHands, due to their diverse and intricate trajectories. These systems operate through iterative cycles of planning, bug localization, code modification, and patch validation, all of which are amenable to systematic evaluation. For our experiments, we utilized four LLMs—DeepSeek-V3, DeepSeek-R1, Devstral-small-2505, and Claude Sonnet 4—as backbones to address real-world issues sourced from 12 GitHub repositories within the SWE-Bench Verified benchmark. Our systematic analysis reveals several key findings: - Unsuccessful runs tend to be longer and exhibit significant inefficiencies. Across agents and backbone models, the GRAPHECTORY of unresolved issues consistently demonstrates greater complexity, with more back edges indicating repetitive actions (§3.2.3, §3.4). - Task complexity correlates with trajectory intricacy. As problem difficulty increases, agents explore deeper and wider (§3.2.4), frequently shifting strategies to sustain progress (§3.3.3). - Adaptive strategy refinement is evident. While agents generally adhere to the global plan outlined in the system prompt, they often adjust their strategies at intermediate stages (§3.3), necessitating additional exploration and debugging for more challenging problems. - Efficiency and success remain imbalanced. Even when agents successfully resolve issues, their problem-solving processes are often inefficient (§3.4), highlighting the importance of process-centric evaluation to address this disparity. To formalize these observations, we define GRAPHECTORY and employ it to establish process-centric evaluation metrics (§2.2) and analysis methodologies (§2.3). To enhance accessibility, we introduce LANGUTORY, a concise, human-readable abstraction of GRAPHECTORY that synthesizes the language of trajectories. While GRAPHECTORY provides a detailed representation of agentic software systems, its complexity can hinder comparative analysis across agents, backbone LLMs, or tasks. Therefore, LANGUTORY complements GRAPHECTORY by offering a streamlined and comprehensible summary.

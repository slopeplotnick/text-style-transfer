Following the training phase, we established a comprehensive and challenging evaluation benchmark to rigorously assess the model's performance (Section 3.5). Given the formidable capabilities of LLMs and the diverse nature of e-commerce data, we designed the benchmark to ensure a broad coverage of examples while incorporating challenging samples based on the core competencies outlined in Section 2. In this section, we conducted essential preparatory steps, which included three key components: feature construction, model selection, and prompt optimization, as illustrated in Fig 6. Building on the theoretical framework presented in Section 2, the development of effective relevance models requires a thorough representation of the item's attribute space. To this end, we selected features that enable the model to process and reason across both visual and textual modalities. First, to capture visual attributes such as stylistic elements, cuts, and patterns, we incorporated the item's primary product image. This visual input is particularly critical for queries where textual descriptions alone are insufficient. Furthermore, to enhance the item’s attribute features, we introduced category-property-value (CPV) triples and Stock-keeping-unit (SKU) information. Recognizing that CPV data often contains redundant elements irrelevant to relevance assessment, we implemented a pipeline that combines prior knowledge with LLMs to filter out such redundancies. Specifically, CPV data is represented as key-value pairs, and there is a notable co-occurrence of keys across similar products. For instance, both "eye cream" and "face cream" products typically include the key "brand." Therefore, we conducted a statistical analysis on a large dataset to identify keys with medium-to-high frequency. Subsequently, we utilized the advanced language model Qwen3-235B-Instruct to evaluate the relevance of each attribute by leveraging the task definition, specified attributes, and corresponding keys. To ensure precision in these evaluations, we performed eight iterations of sampling and retained only those attributes for which the model consistently returned a "true" result.

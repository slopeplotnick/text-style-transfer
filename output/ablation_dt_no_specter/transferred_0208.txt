Search relevance is crucial for e-commerce platforms like JD and Taobao. The relevance model evaluates candidate items by assessing their alignment with user queries and assigns relevance scores to filter out mismatched products. This mechanism is essential for enhancing user experience and search quality. Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities and have been extensively applied to relevance tasks. However, while state-of-the-art models such as GPT-5 suffer from insufficient domain knowledge and prohibitive costs, the post-training of smaller-scale LLMs on e-commerce data to develop domain-specific relevance expertise has emerged as the predominant paradigm. Recent advances have progressively transitioned the post-training paradigm from rudimentary classification-oriented Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to sophisticated enhancement of reasoning capabilities. Although existing work has modeled chain-of-thought (CoT) processes from multiple perspectives, notable gaps remain that limit comprehensive treatment of complex relevance tasks. For instance, the Enhanced Language Learning Model (ELLM) conceptualizes the relevance task as a process of attribute extraction and matching between queries and items. However, this approach exhibits limited capability when handling boundary cases that require rule-based judgment. For example, when matching the query "lake blue top" with the item "sky blue top," the method lacks color discrimination rules and struggles to make accurate judgments. Models such as LREF and TaoSR1 not only focus on attribute matching but also explicitly model rule-following chains of thought, endowing the model with rule-awareness capabilities. Nevertheless, their failure to incorporate visual information results in blind spots in discrimination. For instance, when the query is "blue top" and the item title lacks color description but the item image clearly displays a blue top, the model fails to make correct judgments due to the absence of image information. These limitations stem from neglecting a fundamental question: What constitutes a comprehensive CoT representation for relevance tasks? This question probes the capability requirements for relevance assessment.

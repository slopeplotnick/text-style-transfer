The reasoning processes and actions taken by the agents differ significantly: SWE-agent Dev begins by localizing the bug to the client.py file (steps 1-4), creating a reproduction test (step 5), and editing multiple sections of the file (steps 6-8). After two repetitive failed edits (steps 9-10), it revisits and modifies a different block of the client.py file (steps 11-12), executes the previously created reproduction test to validate the patch (step 13), and ultimately submits the patch (steps 14-15). In comparison, SWE-agent DSK-V3 also starts by localizing the bug to the client.py file (steps 1-6), but performs only a single edit (step 7) and submits the patch without conducting any validation (steps 8-9). Although both agents eventually succeed, their approaches reveal critical strategic shortcomings: SWE-agent Dev engages in line-by-line modifications in steps 6-8, repeats a failed edit twice in steps 9-10, and finalizes the patch with an additional edit in step 12, whereas SWE-agent DSK-V3 generates the patch in a single attempt at step 7. Moreover, neither agent performs regression testing for patch validation; SWE-agent Dev limits its validation to ensuring reproduction tests pass on the patch (step 13), while SWE-agent DSK-V3 relies solely on reasoning about edge cases to assess the patch's correctness (step 8). This example underscores the value of process-oriented analysis, which focuses on agentic trajectories to uncover deeper insights into how agents succeed or fail in problem-solving tasks. Recent studies have explored agentic workflows by analyzing trajectories and identifying failure modes through manual, expert-driven methods [11, 12, 15, 23, 29]. While these taxonomies of failure modes are informative, manual approaches are inherently constrained by human bias, lack of comprehensiveness, and limited scalability to novel agentic systems. Furthermore, representing trajectories as linear, chronological sequences fails to adequately capture the semantic complexity of agentic behavior, such as execution flows, problem-solving strategies, and the alignment or efficiency of these strategies with expected planning processes.

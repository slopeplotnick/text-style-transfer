This method promotes fairness across tasks and groups by directly optimizing balanced accuracy. Unlike the conventional CE loss, it explicitly addresses performance disparities between majority and minority groups, thereby improving the consistency of multi-task fine-tuning. To evaluate the effectiveness of different training methodologies, we conducted an ablation study comparing single-task and multi-task fine-tuning approaches for mental health prediction. To support this research, we developed an on-device mental health assessment application for iOS, utilizing llama.cpp<sup>1</sup> as the inference backend and leveraging GGUF V3-quantized models optimized for mobile hardware. These models were converted from PyTorch to the GGUF format using a dedicated transformers-to-gguf conversion pipeline, enabling efficient 4-bit quantization (Q4_K_M) for execution on both CPU and GPU architectures. The application was implemented in Swift/SwiftUI and integrates llama.cpp via a C++ bridge layer, allowing direct inference within the iOS environment without relying on external servers. During runtime, matrix operations are accelerated using Apple Metal, with automatic fallback to CPU processing when GPU resources are fully utilized. Furthermore, thread-level parallelism is achieved through the deployment of up to eight threads, optimizing performance across Apple's efficiency and performance cores. To minimize RAM usage, model weights are memory-mapped, enabling the lazy loading of tensors. For stability, the system employs a 4,096-token context window, along with efficient KV-cache management and batched inference to mitigate fragmentation during the processing of long inputs. Social media posts exceeding 6,000 characters are truncated to ensure efficient processing. We evaluated three models on an iPhone 15 Pro Max: Menta (2.33 GB), Phi-4 Mini (2.40 GB), and Qwen-3 (2.30 GB), using open-source code<sup>2</sup>. Notably, the deployment framework supports various quantization techniques, enabling a balance between latency and accuracy. For fine-tuning the Menta model, we utilized four high-quality corpora curated specifically for detecting depression, stress, and suicidal ideation from Reddit posts. Disorder-related samples were excluded, and the remaining data were meticulously annotated by domain experts. The datasets used in this study include these curated collections.

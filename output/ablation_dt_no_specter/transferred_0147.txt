As illustrated in Fig. 1, this section elaborates on the dataset construction process and evaluation methodologies. For dataset construction, a team of TCM experts led the effort by meticulously curating samples from clinical cases, classical case records, and authoritative examination questions. These samples were then subjected to a rigorous cleaning process, during which duplicates were removed, and personal information was anonymized to ensure data integrity and ethical compliance. The annotation process followed a three-stage pipeline: initial annotation by domain experts, mutual cross-validation among annotators, and a final review conducted by an independent third party. The resulting benchmark, termed TCM-BEST4SDT, comprises 600 questions spanning four tasks, including TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, LLM Content Safety, and SDT, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Regarding evaluation methodologies, three mechanisms were developed to assess model performance in replicating real-world TCM clinical scenarios. The first mechanism, selected-response evaluation, involves multiple rounds of independent assessment for each question, randomizes answer options, and applies tailored scoring strategies based on question type. The second mechanism, judge model evaluation, employs an LLM augmented with expert-designed prompts to evaluate and score model responses. The third mechanism, reward model evaluation, utilizes a specialized reward model to objectively quantify the congruence between prescriptions and syndromes. TCM-BEST4SDT encompasses two categories of tasks: SDT-specific tasks and general evaluation tasks. The SDT-specific tasks are derived from clinical case data and classical case records, while the general evaluation tasks cover TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, and LLM Content Safety. The TCM Basic Knowledge component is sourced from publicly available examinations, including the National Qualification Examination for Medical Practitioners, the National Postgraduate Entrance Examination: Comprehensive Clinical Medicine (TCM Integrated), and the Chinese Herbal Medicine Title Examination. Moreover, the Medical Ethics component is derived from authoritative sources and manually annotated by experts to address specific scenarios. Similarly, the LLM Content Safety component was independently developed by experts and tailored to meet practical requirements.

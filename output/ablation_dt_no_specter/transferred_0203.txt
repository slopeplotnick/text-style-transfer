With the increasing deployment of video large language models (video LLMs) in real-world applications, such as AI glasses and household robots, these models are now required to process and reason over extremely long videos from several hours to even days. Recent advancements have introduced memory-based frameworks that construct external memory representations to extract salient information from video data. These approaches enable the models to prioritize essential content by retrieving a subset of relevant memories, thereby reducing the number of input tokens. This strategy is more cost-effective and efficient than analyzing all video frames, which can incur substantial computational expenses. However, despite their potential, most contemporary methodologies remain heavily reliant on textual representations, converting segments of video into captions or summaries to facilitate information retrieval and comprehension. While some methods incorporate visual information, their application is predominantly limited to object recognition, with minimal extension beyond this scope. Furthermore, these models typically retrieve a fixed number of video clips of predetermined durations, such as three 30-second segments. This design introduces two significant limitations. First, it fails to effectively integrate visual information from video content with textual memory, thereby constraining the model's capacity for accurate information retrieval and synthesis. Accurately perceiving visual details in videos is critical for tasks requiring precise feature recognition, spatial understanding, or comprehensive scene interpretation, as textual descriptions alone are often insufficient to convey such information. Meanwhile, relying indiscriminately on both textual captions and visual frames during response generation can overwhelm the model, as excessive visual input may introduce unnecessary cognitive load and hinder reasoning. Therefore, it is imperative to develop a mechanism capable of dynamically selecting the optimal combination of textual and visual modalities to extract the most relevant information for a given query. Despite its significance, this aspect remains underexplored in existing research.

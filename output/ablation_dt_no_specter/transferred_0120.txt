TokenPowerBench is a lightweight, extensible, and reproducible framework designed to benchmark the power and energy consumption of LLM inference across diverse hardware configurations, model families, and workload patterns. To enable meaningful comparisons of inference systems, the framework introduces energy-normalized metrics such as Joules per token, Joules per response, and instantaneous power draw. These metrics are carefully aligned with the internal execution phases of transformer-based LLMs, specifically the prefill and decode stages, allowing for fine-grained attribution of energy costs. Unlike traditional performance metrics like latency or throughput, these energy-focused metrics emphasize sustainability and cost-efficiency, which are increasingly critical considerations in modern AI infrastructure. Recognizing the sensitivity of power measurement to hardware access and instrumentation fidelity, TokenPowerBench employs a three-tier measurement model. This ranges from GPU-only telemetry to full-system power monitoring using IPMI and rack-level PDUs, enabling adoption across environments from user-space workstations to institutional testbeds. To ensure reproducibility, the framework incorporates a declarative configuration harness that standardizes experimental setups across iterations. Furthermore, TokenPowerBench systematically explores key configuration dimensions, including batch size, context length, quantization format, and parallelism strategy, to reveal how these factors impact energy usage. Its scalability accommodates setups from single-GPU inference to multi-node distributed serving, capturing both component-level breakdowns and cluster-wide energy imbalances. As the landscape of LLM inference continues to evolve, TokenPowerBench is designed to adapt accordingly. Future expansions will extend coverage beyond the current H100 testbed to include next-generation NVIDIA GPUs, AMD accelerators, and emerging AI chips and DPUs. Additionally, the framework will refine its functionality to quantify the trade-offs between inference accuracy and energy efficiency, offering users actionable insights into optimizing the balance between energy consumption and model performance.

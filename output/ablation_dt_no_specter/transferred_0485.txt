In online shopping contexts, user queries often encode multiple dimensions of needs simultaneously, such as brand, price range, functionality, and target user group. Therefore, the system must go beyond addressing these needs in isolation and instead comprehensively identify and structure these multi-dimensional need points. This approach enables effective alignment of user requests with appropriate items and optimizes the presentation of relevant options. It is crucial, however, not to determine the extent of reliance on various data types, such as textual or visual information, solely based on the user's query. Instead, the system should meticulously analyze the specific details extracted from the query and align them with the corresponding attributes of the items. Subsequently, this integrated information should be strategically considered in the decision-making process regarding the presentation of items to the user. Essentially, the system operates by establishing a coherent trajectory from the user's query to the identification and selection of relevant items. Initially, the system deconstructs the query into granular components to facilitate the filtering and selection of relevant items. This process comprises two primary stages: first, ensuring accurate comprehension of the query and its alignment with the corresponding item attributes, and second, executing the selection process based on the extracted attributes. Notably, these stages are often intricate and may involve ambiguities that complicate the overall procedure. For instance, when a user queries "Summer Mint Mambo Slimming Set," the system must accurately interpret the underlying intent and align it with the corresponding product attributes. This process necessitates establishing a precise correspondence between the query's embedded details and the attribute representations of the items. A critical challenge lies in ensuring that the system performs this matching with a high degree of accuracy and consistency.

A closer examination of the results for each sample reveals that, in some cases, opting for a different resolution leads to more accurate outcomes compared to maintaining the resolution at 112, as depicted in Figure 3. The results derived from image crops at varying resolutions, along with the visualization of the semantic similarity map, offer a clear rationale for this phenomenon. Specifically, when the complete object is segmented into distinct parts, some regions exhibit higher semantic similarity, as calculated by VisRAG, while others show lower similarity. Consequently, only regions with higher similarity are retained after filtering. However, this approach disrupts the integrity of the target object, thereby impairing MLLM's ability to make accurate judgments. To address this limitation, we propose a novel framework called Multi-resolution Retrieval Detection (MRD). The core concept of MRD is its multi-resolution strategy at different scales to better localize regions containing target objects. This facilitates the accurate identification of image segments corresponding to the target objects, minimizing extraneous elements and enhancing MLLMs' perceptual understanding of high-resolution images. As discussed in subsection 3.2, our findings indicate that employing diverse resolutions for semantic similarity computation is more effective for objects of varying sizes and spatial positions. Inspired by this, we introduce a straightforward yet effective approach named Multi-resolution Semantic Fusion. This method computes semantic similarity maps at different resolutions on a localized scale and integrates them through a consistency-based fusion process, thereby refining the precision of semantic similarity outcomes. Moreover, to directly identify target objects, we employ an open-vocabulary object detection model that systematically examines the entire high-resolution image using a sliding-window technique, assigning confidence scores to regions potentially containing the objects. By integrating these detection confidence scores with the multi-resolution semantic similarity maps, our approach not only enhances the localization of target regions but also differentiates subtle variations among the crops within these regions. This integration enables the system to more effectively identify the critical areas of the image. The subsequent sections will provide detailed explanations of each component of this process.

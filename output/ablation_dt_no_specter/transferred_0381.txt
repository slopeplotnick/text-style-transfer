Our method integrates seamlessly with modern high-throughput inference frameworks, such as vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023) and SGLang (Zheng et al., 2024), supporting both real-time serving and offline batch processing. For the controlled experiments and methodological variations presented in Section 4.2, we primarily employ the two-stage vLLM pipeline, which ensures high processing efficiency while facilitating the management of reasoning traces. We evaluate THINKMERGE under two distinct experimental settings. First, for closed-ended reasoning tasks characterized by a singular correct response, we examine AIME 2025 (MAA, 2025) and GPQA Diamond (Rein et al., 2024), where multiple responses can be synthesized through majority voting. Conversely, for open-ended reasoning tasks lacking a definitive correct answer, we conduct evaluations on LiveCodeBench v5 (2024.10-2025.02) (Jain et al., 2024), BrowseComp-en (Wei et al., 2025), BrowseComp-zh (Zhou et al., 2025), GAIA (Mialon et al., 2023), and xBench-DeepSearch (Chen et al., 2025a), emphasizing the generation of a singular coherent solution. For closed-ended tasks, we execute five trials and assess models such as Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-14B (Yang et al., 2025a), and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (Guo et al., 2025). In the context of open-ended coding tasks, we employ models such as DeepCoder-14B-Preview, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-Coder-30A3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Thinking, and Qwen3-Think-30A3B. Furthermore, for open-ended deep research tasks, we utilize the WebSailor series, including WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B. The maximum sequence length is configured to 32,768 tokens, with comprehensive details regarding sampling hyperparameters provided in Appendix A.1. Finally, we investigate the applicability of THINKMERGE in agentic deep-research scenarios, wherein the model is required to interleave cognitive processing and tool utilization prior to generating a response. Specifically, we evaluate three Tongyi-WebSailor models—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B—across four rigorous web-based benchmarks: BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and XbenchDeepSearch. The BrowseComp-en and BrowseComp-zh benchmarks particularly focus on complex, multi-hop factual inquiries in both English and Chinese. Given the substantial size of BrowseComp-en, comprising 1,266 questions in total, we randomly selected a subset of 200 questions for testing purposes, thereby aligning its size with that of BrowseComp.

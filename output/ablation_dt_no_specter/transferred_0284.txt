The RAIR benchmark encompasses a total of 63,601 samples, drawn from 14 real-world e-commerce industries. It is organized into three distinct subsets established during the construction process: the General subset (46,775 samples), the Long-Tail Hard subset (10,931 samples), and the Visual Salience subset (5,895 samples). Each dataset entry includes the query, item title, item details, SKU information, and an anonymized item image. Furthermore, each entry is assigned a manual ground truth annotation based on a four-level relevance scale (L1-L4), with the distribution of these labels across the dataset detailed in Table 8. To support subsequent model development and facilitate detailed error attribution, each data instance is further enriched with rule identifiers from our Rule Checklist, which form the foundation for relevance judgments. In this section, we present a systematic validation and analysis of the proposed methods using the RAIR benchmark. Our experiments are divided into two parts. First, we leverage quantitative metrics to address two core questions: (1) the extent of performance improvement achieved through fine-tuning and (2) the performance advantages demonstrated by the proposed LORE model across different data distribution subsets, including general samples, hard samples, and visually salient samples. Second, we use case studies to highlight the novel capabilities enabled by our methods and the variety of challenges they allow the model to address. To ensure consistent and reproducible evaluation results, all trained models utilize a deterministic greedy decoding strategy for single-pass inference, thereby eliminating variability introduced by sampling. Finally, we employed a diverse set of metrics to systematically evaluate the model's performance.

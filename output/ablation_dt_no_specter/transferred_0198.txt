The most challenging aspect of constructing the explanation model typically lies in determining the structural equations of the FSCM. The results presented in Section 5.2 include canonical PSCM intervals for counterfactual queries, which illustrate the limitations that arise when the true causal mechanisms remain indeterminate. For certain queries, the intervals are relatively narrow, suggesting that the interval mean or even approximate queries utilizing a CBN may provide a reasonable approximation of the true value, as the precise selection of the model is less critical in such cases. However, this is not universally the case. Generally, conditioning on observed events of low probability may result in counterfactual probabilities spanning the entire range from 0 to 1, depending on the specific model employed. Distinct FSCMs that are consistent with the observed data may yield entirely divergent explanations for the same model and may even assert these outcomes as definitive. Rare observations hold significant importance in ensuring reliable explanations, particularly when predictions must remain meaningful in regions characterized by sparse training data. Therefore, access to a fully specified causal model (FSCM) remains essential to guarantee the capability for arbitrary query calculations. When partial expert knowledge is employed to define non-canonical probabilistic structural causal models (PSCMs), the resultant intervals may be reduced, thereby expanding the range of queries that can be approximated. Future research may investigate the efficacy of various methodologies for both precise and approximate computations. While the explanations presented in this study are grounded in assessing the probability of sufficiency of concept interventions, the framework is not inherently constrained to this approach. Subsequent work could extend the scope to include probabilities of necessity and explore alternative forms of explanation.

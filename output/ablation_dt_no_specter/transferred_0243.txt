In this section, our goal is to establish a precise understanding of the factors that contribute to the effectiveness of e-commerce search and, on this basis, to identify the essential mechanisms required to enhance its performance. Fundamentally, e-commerce search can be conceptualized as the process of aligning user queries with relevant product items. Relevance modeling refines this alignment by introducing relevance as a quantitative measure that captures the degree of match between a given query and item, considering both explicit attributes and constraints, as well as subjective user preferences. Orthogonally, we categorize user search intents into 18 distinct dimensions, encompassing attributes such as type, brand, and style, among others. For each query-product pairing, we evaluate the degree to which the product aligns with the specified search intent, employing these assessments to determine the relevance of the match. This framework provides a structured lens through which relevance decisions can be interpreted and analyzed. To systematically quantify relevance within the e-commerce search context, the assessment process is decomposed into three synergistic components: item understanding, query understanding, and relevance judgment. In the relevance judgment phase, item understanding is analyzed along two principal dimensions: (i) the nature of item attributes and (ii) the presentation modality. From the perspective of attribute properties, we distinguish between inherent attributes, which are readily observable and objectively defined, and derived attributes, which necessitate deeper cognitive processing and interpretation. Inherent attributes encompass elements such as category, brand, model, and price, which are typically straightforward and readily align with search queries. In contrast, derived attributes originate from inherent ones and pertain to the functionality or experiential aspects of the product. These attributes are not always immediately observable and necessitate a more profound understanding and reasoning. This complexity is further compounded by the fact that different industries employ distinct terminologies to describe these attributes.

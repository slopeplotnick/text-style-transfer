CAPO is uniquely designed to leverage advantage as a dynamic signal that aligns with the model's evolving competence. Since curriculum learning was first introduced by Bengio et al. (2009a), most methods have relied on static heuristics, such as sorting tasks from easy to hard. However, subsequent adaptive strategies attempted to estimate difficulty through expert feedback or performance metrics, yet they remained reliant on external heuristics and were not intrinsically aligned with the model's progressive enhancement. This limitation motivated the development of CAPO, which employs advantage estimates as an intrinsic, competence-aware signal to inform dynamic curriculum scheduling. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CAPO consistently augments mathematical problem-solving capabilities across various advantage-based reinforcement learning algorithms, including GRPO, PPO, RLOO, and Reinforce++. Moreover, CAPO exhibits robust performance in GUI-based reasoning tasks, underscoring its capacity to address diverse multimodal challenges. In summary, our primary contributions are threefold: we introduce advantage as a dynamic curriculum signal and propose CAPO, a two-phase mechanism comprising imitation and discrimination phases that dynamically adapt to the model's evolving competence. Furthermore, CAPO demonstrates broad compatibility with various reinforcement learning methodologies and strong generalization across multimodal domains, including GUI-based environments. As illustrated in Figure 2, CAPO functions by generating samples through the policy model, subsequently computing advantage estimates via various algorithms, and integrating them into a unified framework. By utilizing these signals, CAPO methodically structures the training process into an initial imitation phase, ensuring stability, followed by a discrimination phase that enhances performance as more challenging samples are introduced.

Despite the increasing popularity of this approach, research on solver-verifier interactions remains limited in scope. Previous studies have predominantly focused on how individual models verify their own solutions (self-verification) and improve themselves (self-improvement). However, self-verification is not invariably effective: models may exhibit biases towards their inherent reasoning patterns, which can be exacerbated by their training, and the extent to which various tasks benefit from verification may differ significantly. Moreover, an exclusive focus on self-verification provides limited insight into the dynamics involved when the solver and verifier are distinct entities. With the availability of open-source models featuring diverse configurations and versions, there is an opportunity to systematically investigate verification mechanisms across different models. Therefore, we extend our analysis to include both intra-family and cross-family verification, posing the central question: Under what conditions does verification yield tangible benefits, and how do factors such as model family, model size, post-training, solver-verifier similarity, and task type influence the effectiveness of verification in enhancing solver performance? To address this, we evaluate verifiers across a diverse array of tasks, including those requiring precise logical reasoning or symbolic computation (e.g., 3-SAT, Sudoku, and matrix multiplication), mathematical reasoning tasks (e.g., AIME and GSM8K), commonsense and factual reasoning tasks (e.g., CSQA and GPQA), and broad domain knowledge tasks spanning STEM and social sciences (e.g., MMLU). This evaluation leverages 37 models from 7 distinct model families. Our findings indicate that self-verification is not universally effective: models often exhibit a preference for solutions aligned with their own reasoning patterns, post-training can exacerbate such biases, and the benefits of verification vary significantly across different tasks. Based on our findings, we provide strategic recommendations for the effective utilization of verifiers.

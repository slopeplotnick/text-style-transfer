Despite significant progress in memory augmentation technologies, general architectures encounter specific challenges when applied to stock prediction tasks. In terms of memory representation, existing systems struggle to achieve fine-grained modeling of event sequences; in retrieval mechanisms, they lack effective capabilities for matching historical scenario patterns; and in temporal processing, they fail to adapt to the unique dynamic evolution of financial markets. These limitations underscore the urgent need for specialized memory architectures tailored to the financial domain. This chapter explores the relevant research across two primary dimensions: first, the progression from historical pattern matching to advanced memory mechanisms, and second, the growing importance of event-driven prediction. Although substantial advancements have been made, notable gaps persist in integrating structured event knowledge with traceable memory systems—a critical challenge that forms the core focus of this study. 

2.2.1 From Historical Pattern Matching to Memory Mechanisms. In the realm of time series forecasting, stock price prediction methods based on identifying analogous historical patterns have long been a focal point of academic inquiry, predating the advent of LLMs. The central premise of these approaches—leveraging historical data patterns that resemble current contexts to inform predictions—conceptually aligns with the memory mechanisms employed in LLMs. Both paradigms share the fundamental goal of effectively storing and retrieving external experiences or historical contexts. Early research predominantly relied on statistical methods, which, while adept at capturing numerical patterns, proved insufficient for modeling the intricate dynamics of financial markets. However, the rise of deep learning introduced architectures such as LSTM and Transformer networks, enabling researchers to autonomously learn and model temporal features. Notably, Transformer-based models excelled at capturing long-term dependencies through self-attention mechanisms, marking a significant advancement in the field. Building on these developments, the FinSeer framework integrated LLMs to retrieve numerically similar historical sequences, thereby facilitating deeper feature exploration and enhancing predictive accuracy.

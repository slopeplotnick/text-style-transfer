In 2023, Zakharov examined phenomena such as scalar variability and sequential biases, which arise from optimal Bayesian inference under conditions of uncertainty, as articulated by Petzschner and Glasauer in 2011. Recent computational models, as discussed by Fountas et al. in 2022, further propose that these perceptual biases, particularly in the domain of temporal perception, are inherently supported by predictive mechanisms within episodic memory. When individuals estimate magnitudes, they integrate uncertain information with pre-existing beliefs, resulting in characteristic behavioral patterns. Figure 1 illustrates this regression-to-the-mean phenomenon, evident in both the responses of Llama-4 Maverick and human psychophysical data, thereby indicating shared computational mechanisms despite fundamentally distinct substrates. Certain components of large language models (LLMs) appear to operate analogously to this integrative estimation process. For instance, in-context learning can be conceptualized as approximate Bayesian inference, as articulated by Xie et al. (2021), while Bayesian teaching has been demonstrated to enhance reasoning performance, according to Qiu et al. (2025). Furthermore, LLMs spontaneously segment sequences using Bayesian surprise in a manner that aligns with human event perception, as noted by Kumar et al. (2023) and Fountas et al. (2025). Nevertheless, the majority of research focuses on explicit reasoning or learned behaviors, where models exploit established statistical rules, rather than on perceptual tasks that could elucidate computational strategies emerging implicitly from pretraining. Significant advancements have been made in the development of large language models (LLMs) capable of processing multimodal information. Evaluation frameworks such as MMbench and SEED-bench, as outlined by Liu and Li (2024), have been designed to assess the reasoning capabilities of these models. However, these benchmarks often fail to account for the impact of noise across different modalities on cognitive processing. By employing synthetic datasets, our approach enables a more detailed examination of how LLMs integrate ambiguous information from heterogeneous sources. This remains a nascent yet critical domain of research, essential for the development of more robust and generalisable models that perform optimally under conditions of uncertainty.

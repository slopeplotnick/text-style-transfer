Problem difficulty is intrinsically linked to verification challenges; however, both intra-family and cross-family verifier improvements exhibit clear positive correlations. Notably, AIME emerges as an outlier in the final visualization, potentially because some models have encountered similar problems during post-training. The best-fit lines for verifier accuracy delineate two distinct clusters of tasks (denoted in red and blue), indicating that verification difficulty cannot be solely attributed to solver accuracy. This prompts an inquiry into whether certain tasks are inherently more amenable to verification than others. In a related study, Song et al. (2025) determined that models are unable to enhance performance on tasks necessitating factual recall, yet they demonstrate improvement on Tokoku given sufficient pretraining scale. A comparable distinction is observed in Figure 7: AIME, GSM8K, 3SAT, and Tokoku exhibit a higher ratio of verifier accuracy to solver accuracy and achieve superior results across all verification settings. To elucidate this observation, we identify that within our synthetic datasets, Selenium and 3SAT exemplify problems that necessitate extensive computational time for resolution but permit verification within polynomial time. Conversely, the verification of matrix multiplication results lacks a straightforward approach, necessitating essentially a recomputation. In the context of real-world datasets, GSM8K and AIME encompass problems solvable with high-school-level mathematics, whereas MMLU (Social Sciences) demands domain-specific expertise, CommonsenseQA relies on implicit world knowledge, and GPQA and MMLU (STEM) require specialized scientific knowledge. For these latter tasks, verifying an answer necessitates the same level of knowledge required to solve the problem. Key observations: While tasks that are straightforward to solve are generally easier to verify, certain tasks are intrinsically more amenable to verification. These include synthetic problems characterized by logical or structured reasoning (e.g., 3SAT, Sudo) and real-world tasks that predominantly rely on mathematical reasoning rather than extensive factual recall (e.g., GSM8K, AIME). These tasks also yield substantial advantages from the application of test-time rejection sampling with verifiers.

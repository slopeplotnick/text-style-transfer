We propose a scalable methodology for developing multilingual safety protocols that generalize across a wide range of languages by leveraging only a select number of high-resource languages. To this end, we have developed a lightweight safety model with just 0.5B parameters, capable of supporting over 100 languages, trained exclusively on data from 13 high-resource languages. Furthermore, we present a systematic analysis of cross-lingual knowledge transfer, both between and within language clusters, to evaluate the efficacy of transferring safety capabilities from high-resource to low-resource languages. Historically, content moderation systems have primarily focused on English. For instance, Google's Perspective API (Lees et al., 2022) initially supported only English-language text. Subsequent advancements, such as Aegis-Defensive (Ghosh et al., 2025), WalledGuard (Gupta et al., 2024), and WildGuard (Han et al., 2024), have demonstrated significant improvements in detecting harmful content and ensuring safety within English contexts. However, recent studies (Wang et al., 2024; Nicholas and Bhatia, 2023; Deng et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024) reveal that widely used language models tend to generate considerably more unsafe or harmful responses for non-English queries. Moreover, prompts articulated in low-resource languages have been shown to bypass existing safety filters more easily. In response, recent developments have sought to address the limitations of English-centric safety measures in multilingual contexts. For example, systems like MrGuard (Yang et al., 2025) and X-Guard (Upadhayay, Behzadan, and D., 2025) rely on low-accuracy translated or synthetic data, which fail to adequately capture the linguistic and cultural nuances of low-resource languages. More recent advancements, such as LlamaGuard3-8B (2024) and NemoGuard-8B-content-safety (2025), are built on the Llama3.1-8B pretrained model, while DuoGuard (2025) employs a novel two-player reinforcement learning approach to train a compact safety classifier using Qwen2.5-0.5B as the base model. DuoGuard represents one of the first efforts to explore small-scale multilingual safety guardrails. To the best of our knowledge, PolyGuard (Kumar et al., 2025) is the most advanced safety moderation model to date, accompanied by the largest multilingual safety dataset, PolyGuardMix. Nonetheless, despite its state-of-the-art performance, PolyGuard's coverage is limited to 17 languages, which underscores the persistent focus on a narrow subset of high-resource languages in existing multilingual safety solutions.

Auditors rely on Journal Entry Tests (JETs) to detect anomalies in tax-related ledger records; however, these rule-based methods generate overwhelming false positives and struggle with subtle irregularities. Therefore, we investigate whether large language models (LLMs) can serve as anomaly detectors in double-entry bookkeeping. By benchmarking state-of-the-art LLMs such as LLaMA and Gemma on both synthetic and real-world anonymized ledgers, we compare their performance against JETs and machine learning baselines. Our results demonstrate that LLMs consistently outperform traditional rule-based JETs and classical ML baselines, while also providing natural-language explanations that enhance interpretability. These findings highlight the potential of AI-augmented auditing, where human auditors collaborate with foundation models to strengthen financial integrity. Financial auditors play a critical role in detecting unusual transactions and anomalies that could indicate errors, fraud, or tax evasion in a company's books. Ensuring that such irregularities are identified is vital for maintaining trust in financial statements. However, auditing large volumes of accounting data is extremely challenging; current manual and rule-based processes are often inefficient and error-prone, meaning auditors can miss important red flags. Standard Journal Entry Tests (JETs), which rely on predefined rules and known patterns, tend to flag a huge number of transactions. These full population tests catch only anticipated anomalies and flood auditors with alerts, many of which are false positives requiring time-consuming review. In practice, auditors still rely heavily on sampling and human labor, which risks overlooking subtle irregularities in today's era of massive, complex datasets. This gap between growing data complexity and the limits of traditional audit techniques underscores the need for more intelligent, automated anomaly detection tools in auditing.

These observed patterns are not confined to a single model type; rather, LLaDA exhibits similar behavior, with conservative smooth schedules enhancing efficiency while maintaining near-parity quality for base variants and yielding significantly greater improvements for instruction-tuned models. Collectively, progress-aware thresholds consistently accelerate processes, and the choice of schedule should align with the permissible error margin. When maintaining accuracy is paramount, it is advisable to employ more deliberate and meticulous configurations. Conversely, in scenarios where minor inaccuracies are permissible or expedited processing is prioritized, accelerated schedules are more advantageous. This trade-off is quantified by a quality-versus-speed metric. In this investigation, we present SchED, a methodology that obviates the need for additional training and is compatible with any model configuration. The mechanism determines the cessation point by evaluating the stability of model predictions, employing a sequence-level confidence statistic alongside a progressively conditioned threshold. This methodology facilitates a reduction in the requisite denoising steps for both Dream and LLaDA models, with pronounced efficacy in instruction-tuned variants where uncertainty diminishes rapidly, while maintaining robust performance. This assertion is substantiated by entropy analysis and the quality-penalized speed metric. SchED integrates seamlessly with pre-existing transfer schedules without necessitating modifications to the underlying models. The generated schedules span a spectrum from highly conservative configurations, optimized for maximal accuracy, to more expedited alternatives tailored for scenarios prioritizing computational efficiency or tolerance to minor inaccuracies. Promising research directions include the development of adaptive mechanisms for parameterizing schedules, task-specific optimization of scheduling strategies, refinement of domain-sensitive thresholding techniques, and the exploration of synergistic integrations with complementary approaches, such as speculative decoding or cached denoising. Conceptualizing diffusion decoding as a when-to-stop problem provides a straightforward and reliable framework for leveraging these models while balancing accuracy and computational efficiency. While SchED facilitates the acceleration of diffusion decoding without necessitating additional training, the scope of our investigation is subject to certain limitations. Specifically, SchED operates solely during the inference phase and regards the schedule as an external control parameter. The joint optimization of training schedules and model architectures, the exploration of more sophisticated result integration methodologies, and the closer alignment with complementary acceleration techniques, such as speculative execution or cached denoising, were beyond the scope of this study. These avenues, however, represent promising directions for future research and hold significant potential for further performance enhancement.

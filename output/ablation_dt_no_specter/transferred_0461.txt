Future Work Due to resource constraints, we have not systematically studied belief entrenchment in reinforced reasoning, despite the recent popularity of this approach to LLM reasoning. Moreover, the violation of ideal Bayesian rationality can be demonstrated by both irrational engagement with internal reasoning processes and external evidence. In this study, we focused solely on the former. Future research could include an "evidence searching" component to evaluate LLMs' capacity to update beliefs in response to new external evidence, as opposed to their propensity to fixate on prior beliefs. However, the challenge here is ensuring that the evidence searching does not give LLMs under evaluation direct access to ground truth, if such ground truth exists. In domains with established truths, such as forecasting, we demonstrated the impact of belief entrenchment on accuracy. Future research can test the validity of the Martingale Score in open-ended domains by examining other downstream consequences of belief entrenchment. To further demonstrate the usefulness of the Martingale Score as a general-purpose process-based reasoning evaluation, one could extend our pipeline into the evaluation of phenomena such as sycophancy, group conformity, and inverse scaling. Furthermore, the Martingale Score, as an evaluation metric, can be converted into a training objective to test its robustness and applicability. If martingale training results in a reduction of both the Martingale Score and the Brier score, it would provide new evidence that the Martingale Score can evaluate reasoning and is applicable beyond forecasting. Finally, applications of the Martingale Score in measuring and mitigating belief entrenchment or even polarization within human-AI systems, such as recommender systems, are another direction for future exploration.

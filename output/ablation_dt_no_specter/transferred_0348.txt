This measure quantifies the retention of the subject's appearance and facial features during the editing process, with higher values indicating greater identity consistency. As illustrated in Table 1, while text-based approaches such as LucyEdit achieve elevated scores on certain synchronization metrics (e.g., Speech Corr. 0.80 compared to our 0.72), this improvement comes at the expense of failing to effectively apply the intended edit, as evidenced by their low Directional CLIP score of 0.11, in contrast to our 0.21. In comparison, our approach demonstrates a superior balance, excelling in synchronization while faithfully executing the visual edits. Conversely, VACE leverages additional conditioning inputs, such as pose, canny, and depth information, to achieve superior CLIP-based semantic alignment. However, it demonstrates diminished performance in maintaining temporal consistency and preserving subject identity. By explicitly optimizing for synchronized editing, Sync-LoRA achieves robust temporal alignment while producing precise modifications that retain subject identity. This underscores the efficacy of our in-context formulation, which strategically balances motion coherence and edit fidelity, rather than prioritizing a singular aspect.

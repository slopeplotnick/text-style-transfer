Despite its recognized importance, the field still lacks a comprehensive, reproducible, and scalable benchmarking framework for evaluating and analyzing the power and energy demands of LLM inference across diverse model scales, hardware architectures, software ecosystems, and deployment configurations, ranging from single-node setups to multi-node distributed systems. While certain initiatives have attempted to bridge this gap, they remain insufficient in delivering a holistic solution. For example, the MLPerf Inference benchmark (Reddi et al. 2020) provides a standardized framework for performance benchmarking across various machine learning (ML) tasks; however, it does not systematically account for end-to-end power consumption or normalize energy usage to LLM-specific service units, such as Joules per generated token or per prompt. Similarly, the MLPerf Power benchmark (Tschand et al. 2025) expands the scope of power measurements but predominantly focuses on single-node configurations, examines relatively modest model sizes compared to frontier state-of-the-art (SOTA) LLMs—such as the Llama 3 405B-class, which remains largely underexplored—and relies on high-precision metering equipment that is prohibitively expensive and difficult to deploy across diverse institutional testbeds. Furthermore, existing benchmarking methodologies often overlook critical configuration parameters that practitioners routinely adjust in production environments, including batch size, parallelization strategies, maximum context length, and quantization levels. These parameters exert significant influence on both instantaneous power draw and average energy cost per token. To address these limitations, we introduce TokenPowerBench, a lightweight and extensible benchmarking framework specifically designed to measure the power consumption and energy efficiency of LLM inference. TokenPowerBench incorporates a modular instrumentation layer that interfaces with vendor telemetry APIs—such as power sensors for GPU, CPU, and memory—facilitating node-level energy sampling with optional extensions to rack- or facility-level measurements when feasible. A unified metrics pipeline aligns each power sample with the primary inference phases, namely prefill and decode, enabling precise identification of energy expenditure. Through extensive experiments, we systematically analyze the interplay between energy consumption and inference-critical parameters, including batch size, maximum context length, parallelization strategies, and quantization levels.

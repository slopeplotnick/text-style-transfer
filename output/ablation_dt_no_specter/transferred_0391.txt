To effectively adapt multilingual models for diverse tasks, we exploit the intrinsic structural relationships among languages, which naturally facilitate seamless cross-lingual information exchange. In the representational space of multilingual transformer models such as XLM-RoBERTa (XLM-R), languages that are semantically or syntactically similar, or that share script and orthographic characteristics, are positioned in proximity to one another. Our approach begins with the 100-language configuration of the XLM-R model, encompassing a wide array of both widely spoken and less common languages. Instead of requiring data for each individual language, we strategically select a subset of prevalent languages for training purposes, thereby enabling the extrapolation of acquired knowledge across the entire linguistic spectrum. This methodology allows for the transfer of linguistic insights by concentrating on a limited number of languages, thus eliminating the necessity for training on less common languages. Building on this strategy, we translated the MultiJail dataset into all 100 target languages using state-of-the-art translation systems tailored to each specific language. For widely spoken languages, we employ advanced models such as GPT-4o, M2MBart-50, and Helsinki-NLP OpusMT. However, these models exhibit limited translation efficacy for less commonly spoken languages. Therefore, we utilize SarvamTranslate for Indic languages, which demonstrates robust performance even in the context of low-resource languages, and GPT-4.1 for other rare languages. To ensure the accuracy of translations, particularly for these less common languages, we conduct additional verification using Google Translate. Through the integration of these models, we produce translations that are fluent, high-quality, and precise.

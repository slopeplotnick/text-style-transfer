The hybrid routing strategy is critical: enforcing a single execution path significantly undermines task performance, particularly for workloads that combine both minor and major edits. The XML-only variant achieves an IF score of 0.95, while the python-pptx-only approach scores 2.06. The hybrid strategy without iterative refinement achieves 2.36, but incorporating iterative refinement further boosts performance to 2.84 IF and 3.21 VQ. A detailed analysis reveals that providing slide images to PPTPilot enhances layout reasoning: visual grounding improves decisions regarding z-ordering, spacing, and alignment without compromising text editing accuracy. Iterative refinement stabilizes complex edits, with an additional pass often resolving formatting inconsistencies after significant modifications or extensive rewrites, while incurring only modest computational overhead. PPTArena and PPTPilot are introduced to establish PowerPoint slide editing as a measurable capability for intelligent multimodal systems. PPTArena leverages real-world slide decks, human-authored editing programs, and style-aware rubrics to create a benchmark emphasizing instruction fidelity, structural grounding, and layout quality beyond generation-focused evaluations. PPTPilot demonstrates that a dual-path paradigm with explicit plan-edit-verify loops significantly enhances reliability, outperforming strong proprietary systems and state-of-the-art VLMs. However, agents still struggle with complex, long-horizon, multi-modal tasks, indicating that robust PowerPoint editing remains an open challenge. We anticipate that PPTArena and PPTPilot will serve as a foundation for future research into advanced PowerPoint editing systems capable of safely and reliably handling the presentations commonly used by individuals.

We began our analysis with a qualitative approach by meticulously reviewing the interview summaries several times. During the interviews, we recorded conversations, took handwritten notes, and later summarized the recorded content. This comprehensive approach ensures that all relevant information is captured and minimizes the risk of overlooking critical details, aligning with the conventional and recommended practice of recording and transcribing interviews in qualitative research. The entire research team, including the interviewer, engaged in systematically analyzing the collected data. Subsequently, we developed a set of metrics to conduct a quantitative analysis. Specifically, this involved tallying responses and assessing frequency. We first counted the mentions of good practices, then identified the benefits, followed by risks, and finally acknowledged fears as expressed in the interview responses. To address these aspects, we searched for similar expressions within answers and distilled them into simple words summarizing the core ideas. For instance, when participants mentioned, "The genericity pushes to factorize more and more," we distilled this to the terms "genericity" and "factorisation." For each topic, we first identified the frequency of expressed generic terms and then categorized them according to the individual who articulated them. The identification of potential terms followed a systematic methodology: we sorted words by frequency after removing stop-words and punctuation, selected the most meaningful, and then validated their use contextually by reviewing the documents. Occasionally, we augmented these words to clarify meaning, such as using "too complex" instead of "complex." We compared the words derived from the interviews with those found in the literature. If a discrepancy was noted, we identified more appropriate terms, for example, replacing "unusable" with "useless." Conversely, if there was alignment, we retained the term. This process allowed us to refine our responses by integrating concepts from relevant literature, thereby ensuring the terminology used resonated with the language of those texts. Ultimately, this enhanced our ability to extract the most relevant and meaningful terms, improving the quality of our analysis.

When planning sequences of measurements, such as determining drilling locations, AI methodologies like Monte Carlo tree search can be employed to ascertain the optimal sequential drilling strategy (Mern and Caers, 2023). However, the properties of interest may shift, following a defined hierarchy: drilling should target hypotheses first, then focus on volume, and finally consider grade. Indeed, once all hypotheses except one are discounted, the attention should pivot to the mineral volume and quality, or these can be merged into a quality-volume analysis if desired. Despite the potential of scientific methodologies to enhance exploration effectiveness and reduce costs, numerous non-scientific barriers persist. These include entrenched practices, established organizational structures within exploration enterprises, and the fragmented nature of software solutions for integrated operations. Moreover, companies typically deploy specialists to tasks aligned with their expertise, reflecting a trend toward compartmentalization in geoscientific ventures. Often, emphasis is on specific details without a complete understanding of their impact on final decision-making outcomes. For effective AI integration, large corporations need to reorganize around decision-making challenges rather than simply executing predetermined tasks. This transformation requires more than just reorganization; it demands a comprehensive educational initiative. Geologists need not become data science experts, but fostering mutual understanding is crucial, a topic further explored in the education section. Socialization frequently revolves around a single model, performed with software that can lead to biased interpretations. Over time, these models become perceived as truth. As individuals exit, the rationale for the interpretations may fade, and the established form is treated as data rather than a model. Within organizations, uncertainty is often viewed as a flaw, with a presumption that increased effort and time investment could enhance knowledge and, thus, mitigate uncertainty.

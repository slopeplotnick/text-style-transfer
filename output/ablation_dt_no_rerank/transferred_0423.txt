Therefore, with task-specific supervision and systematic fine-tuning, our Menta model achieves strong performance across both binary and multi-level mental health classification tasks, including stress detection, depression severity, and suicidal ideation and risk assessment. It is capable of efficiently handling a diverse range of mental health outcomes within a unified framework. Moreover, our Menta model performs competitively against significantly larger language models, especially in depression and stress-related tasks, where it occasionally surpasses these larger counterparts. These findings underscore the practical viability of well-optimized smaller language models for continuous mental health monitoring, offering a cost-effective yet high-performing solution for early detection in digital mental health applications. In this section, we examine three key inquiries regarding the use of SLMs and our fine-tuned Menta model in predicting mental health outcomes, while concurrently addressing current limitations and directions for future research. The primary question is: Can SLMs effectively predict mental health status? Our findings indicate that SLMs possess substantial capability in predicting mental health conditions from text-based social media data. Despite having fewer parameters, the well-trained Menta models adeptly handled complex classification tasks, underscoring their potential for precise predictions in low-resource or deployment-constrained environments. While previous research (Jia et al., 2025) concentrated on models within the 2-7 billion parameter range in mental health, our investigations focus specifically on models strictly below 7 billion parameters. We reveal that models with fewer than approximately 3.8 billion parameters—such as those around 1 billion—demonstrate inadequate performance, frequently producing vacuous or erratic outputs in zero- or few-shot scenarios. This phenomenon indicates that scaling language models below a certain parameter threshold results in a significant decline in task performance instead of a gradual deterioration, delineating a pronounced lower-bound capacity limit. Our work explicitly identifies this threshold within the domain of mental health prediction.

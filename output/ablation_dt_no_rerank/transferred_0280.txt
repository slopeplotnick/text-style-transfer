A line of research is exploring how to enhance system robustness by enforcing geometric consistency in representations of shapes and spatial structures. Empirical studies have shown that shifts in viewpoint can significantly disrupt the embedding space of pretrained ViT encoders, thus impairing the integration between perception and decision-making modules. Motivated by these findings, our inquiry investigates whether rectifying such embedding misalignments, without altering the entire Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architecture or collecting additional data, is sufficient to strengthen viewpoint robustness. Parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) has emerged as a prominent method for adapting models to new tasks or scenarios without extensive modifications. Techniques like adapters, LoRA, Prefix-Tuning, and BitFit introduce small, learnable modules while preserving most of the original structure. These approaches have been extensively utilized in large language models and multimodal systems due to their effectiveness and stability. In the field of embodied artificial intelligence, PEFT has primarily been used to update language or policy components, such as making minor adjustments to decision transformers or instruction encoders. However, when addressing viewpoint changes, existing methodologies seldom focus on directly fine-tuning the visual module itself. As a result, strategies that implement precise and lightweight adaptations within the visual pathway of VLA models are notably scarce. Conventional methods generally attempt to modify the entire framework, whereas our work focuses on the efficacy of subtle, parameter-efficient adaptations targeting the visual components, like vision tokens or the vision encoder, to tackle these challenges. Typically, existing strategies respond to visual perturbations by extensively retraining substantial segments of the architecture or even the entire visuomotor policy. In contrast, we argue that such measures are unnecessary, as visual variations predominantly lead to systematic drifts within the visual embedding space, disrupting visual data processing.

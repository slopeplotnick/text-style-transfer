A prominent implementation of this retrieval-and-synthesis capability is the AI Overviews feature within Google Search. Leveraging the Gemini model family, this system functions as a retrieval-enhanced generation pipeline, adeptly processing multiple long-context documents to produce concise, cited summaries. While this feature significantly enhances user efficiency by minimizing the need to manually integrate information from varied sources, it simultaneously raises crucial questions regarding information reliability. Specifically, when a large language model (LLM) integrates multiple documents into a cohesive summary, it is vital to determine whether the model assigns uniform importance to all input sources or if systemic structural biases are present. Previous research has delved into the ideological biases ingrained in LLMs. For instance, Santurkar et al. employed survey-style prompts to illustrate that models such as GPT-4 and Claude often express liberal-leaning social values. Furthermore, in the context of news summarization, Vijay et al. introduced quantitative metrics like vocabulary divergence (VD), stance polarization index (SPI), and algorithmic monoculture index (AMI) to reveal that LLMs frequently converge on a narrow ideological framing. Although existing studies have extensively explored ideological and content-level biases within LLM-generated summaries, considerably fewer have tackled whether LLMs show structural biases in their weighting of multiple input documents. In multi-document contexts, particularly those involving long concatenated texts, it remains unclear if LLMs prioritize certain documents based on their position within the context window. Notably, positional effects like primacy bias or the lost-in-the-middle phenomenon have been identified in other long-context tasks, yet their consequences for multi-document summarization have been sparsely evaluated. Consequently, this paper addresses this gap by investigating whether the sequence of source articles impacts their representational significance in LLM-generated summaries, using abortion news articles as a case study.

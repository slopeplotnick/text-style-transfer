Using scikit-learn, we trained Majority, Random, n-gram SVM, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree classifiers for baseline models. Features were extracted using TF-IDF and count-vector representations. While these baseline models were trained on CPU, all experiments for transformer models were conducted on a GPU-enabled environment (Google Colab T4). We used the micro-averaged F1-score as the primary evaluation metric. Additionally, accuracy, precision, and recall were calculated for a fair comparison. In our study, we evaluated a wide range of models, including m-BERT cased, XLM-RoBERTa base, BanglaBERT, DistilBERT cased, and the baseline models for classifying hate speech on the BLP2025 dataset. Table 2 presents the classification report for Subtask 1A. BanglaBERT achieved the highest accuracy of 0.71, surpassing all other models in our evaluation. Moreover, BanglaBERT recorded precision and recall scores of 0.70 and 0.71, respectively. The official scoring metric for Subtask 1A was the micro F1 score, where BanglaBERT achieved a score of 0.71. In comparison, the BERT multilingual base (m-BERT) attained an F1 score of 0.66, and the XLM-RoBERTa base achieved an F1 score of 0.67, both outperforming the baseline models. However, DistilBERT registered an F1 score of 0.57, which was lower than the baseline methods SVM and LR. Among the baseline models, SVM demonstrated the best performance with an F1 score of 0.63, while Logistic Regression followed closely with an F1 score of 0.62. Conversely, the random baseline exhibited the poorest performance with an F1 score of 0.16.

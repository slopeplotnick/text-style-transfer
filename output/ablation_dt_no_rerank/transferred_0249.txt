We propose the Autonomous Domain-Oriented Relevance Engine (ADORE), which integrates a domain rule-aware relevance discrimination (RD) module, an error-type-aware data synthesis (DS) module, and a key-attribute-enhanced knowledge distillation (KD) module. To enhance the RD module, we introduce a CoT-based reasoning LLM, which automatically constructs domain-specific hard samples through relevance reasoning on online exposure products. Moreover, the DS module incorporates an error-type-aware generation model to autonomously produce diverse challenging samples, further enhancing system robustness. Meanwhile, key attribute features from CoT analysis are explicitly integrated into the KD module, thereby improving the reasoning and representation capabilities of the online student model. Extensive experiments have been conducted to evaluate ADORE's performance, utilizing both large-scale offline datasets and online A/B testing. The results demonstrate that ADORE significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, delivering substantial improvements in both offline and online metrics. This underscores the practical applicability and effectiveness of ADORE in handling complex relevance modeling tasks in e-commerce. To further optimize the performance of our online model, we have developed a relevance discrimination framework based on Large Language Models. Previous research [9, 21] indicates that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting significantly enhances the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. Building on this insight, we fine-tuned our LLM using a CoT-driven approach. Initially, we utilized GPT-4o to generate CoT-labeled data by simulating the human annotation process, where GPT-4o is provided with domain-specific annotation guidelines alongside exemplary CoT instances, thereby generating a dataset enriched with reasoning akin to human annotations. To further enhance the accuracy of the labeled data, we exclusively incorporate query-product data verified via human-annotated labels and exclude any CoT reasoning chains inconsistent with these annotations, thus constructing our final training set. Subsequently, we employed the definitive training set to further refine the LLaMA-3 model through optimization of a standard Language Model loss.

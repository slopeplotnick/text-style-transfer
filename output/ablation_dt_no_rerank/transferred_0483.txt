To assess the feasibility of LLM-driven reasoning in addressing MRTA problems, we initially replicated the SMART-LLM framework (Kannan et al. 2024) as a baseline feasibility study. This replication helped to identify fundamental challenges associated with LLM-based coordination before we proceeded to develop our enhanced framework. The evaluation employed the SMART-LLM benchmark dataset, which consists of 36 tasks categorized into four complexity levels: Elemental tasks, which require single actions; Simple tasks, involving multiple objects with homogeneous robots; Compound tasks, comprising heterogeneous robots with specialized skills; and Complex tasks, requiring team formation and collaborative execution. These tasks were performed in the AI2-THOR simulation environment. In terms of LLM configuration, Claude 4 Sonnet was employed as the reasoning engine, with a temperature setting of 0.0 to ensure deterministic outputs and reproducible allocation decisions. Consistent with the SMART-LLM methodology, we evaluated performance using five metrics: Success Rate (SR), Task Completion Rate (TCR), Goal Condition Recall (GCR), Executability (EXE), and Robot Utilization (RU). These metrics collectively assess both task completion accuracy and the efficiency of multi-robot coordination. However, following the identification of validation inconsistencies and issues with LLM output reliability during the replication of SMART-LLM, we developed and rigorously evaluated the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture. This initiative aimed to show that structured validation and hierarchical retry mechanisms effectively address these challenges, thus establishing the feasibility of LLMs for MRTA applications. The Self Corrective-Agent Architecture incorporated multi-stage validation with hierarchical retry mechanisms. Key parameters included a maximum of three local retries per agent, validation checkpoints at each stage (decomposition, allocation, code generation), and structured feedback loops for iterative enhancement.

Model Paradigm & Backbone: We adopted a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) paradigm using the Qwen-1.5 model as our backbone architecture. This approach enabled the effective transfer of general language understanding capabilities to domain-specific e-commerce scenarios. Data & Prompt: Our methodology leveraged knowledge graphs combined with tailored domain-specific prompts. This allowed the model to assimilate semantic patterns and entity relationships specific to the e-commerce domain. Evaluation: To ensure accuracy and capability in handling diverse queries, we employed standard assessments and relevance metrics to comprehensively evaluate the model's performance across various tasks, ensuring both precision and adaptability. Application: Post-training, the model was deployed for labeling tasks, effectively enhancing efficiency by identifying errors and improving data quality. Building upon foundational query understanding, this phase also addressed more sophisticated challenges such as comprehending complex queries, discerning implicit meanings, and executing logical reasoning necessary for path construction. Simple directives, however, proved insufficient for these complex tasks. Therefore, we implemented Chain-of-Thought (CoT) technology, which facilitated the model in deconstructing problems, understanding hierarchical relationships, and making comparative analyses of paths incrementally. Consequently, this approach substantially enhanced the model's processing of intricate queries. Moreover, we developed a system to identify complex cases and refine the model's performance through iterative feedback mechanisms. Model Paradigm & Backbone: Subsequently, we synthesized CoT data for attribute extraction and matching tasks and retrained the model with the SFT paradigm leveraging Qwen-2 as the backbone architecture. This methodology enabled the management of intermediate reasoning steps while ensuring task-specific optimization through supervised fine-tuning.

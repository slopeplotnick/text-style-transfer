Structured test environments are being established to ensure effective interoperability among various systems, wherein artificial intelligence mechanisms enforce, interpret, and modify shared protocols. However, this approach aims to assess the efficacy of these methods in mitigating previously identified challenges. Furthermore, the research initiative seeks to establish a comprehensive framework for AI safety that integrates theoretical insights and empirical investigations, ultimately leading to the standardization of practices. This manuscript lays out a conceptual schema, with subsequent works intended to provide empirical validation and experimental substantiation of the proposed ideas. We assert that the prevailing focus on individual agent safety measures—such as prompt engineering and output filtering—is inadequate when language models engage in inter-agent interactions. As AI systems increasingly operate collectively, mere adherence to localized guidelines proves insufficient in preventing systemic failures of considerable magnitude. Notably, a significant distinction exists between ensuring the safety of individual AI models and safeguarding the entire interconnected system. To elucidate this distinction, we introduce the concept of the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon, providing a framework to identify the junctures where these differences manifest. Moreover, this study demonstrates that risks may escalate from minor concerns to extensive systemic issues, as interactions, complexity, and diverse objectives converge within the network. Such risks arise not merely from isolated malfunctioning AIs but from the inherent dynamics of network collaborations. By enabling agents to modify norms and engage in cooperative interactions, we aim to sustain collective behavioral equilibrium, minimizing dependence on centralized control mechanisms. This work is preliminary, indicating that the ESRH framework and Institutional AI concept require comprehensive empirical validation, theoretical exploration, and iterative refinement. Subsequent efforts will involve conducting rigorous controlled experiments to assess the defined indicators, investigating coordination models, and developing practical instruments for applying these concepts in operational AI systems. As the transition from single-agent to multi-agent AI systems continues, without the development of novel methodologies and management strategies, latent safety challenges may remain obscured until they become unmanageable. Therefore, this paper aims to elucidate these issues and provides a systematic framework for addressing them.

The most challenging aspect of designing the explanation model is typically the structural equations of the FSCM. In Section 5.2, canonical PSCM intervals for counterfactual queries are presented to illustrate what is lost when true causal mechanisms remain undetermined. For some queries, these intervals appear narrow, suggesting that the interval mean or even approximate queries using a CBN could provide a reasonable estimation of the true value, as the precision of the exact model choice becomes less critical. However, this is not always the case. Generally, when conditioning on observed events with low probability, counterfactual probability can range from 0 to 1, depending on the model considered. Therefore, two different FSCMs consistent with the data might produce entirely opposite explanations of the same model, while even asserting these outcomes as certain. Rare observations are particularly important to elucidate with precision, especially in ensuring meaningful predictions in regions with limited data. Thus, access to an FSCM remains essential for guaranteeing arbitrary query calculations. Moreover, non-canonical PSCMs defined by partial expert knowledge might reduce the size of intervals, thereby broadening the types of queries that can be approximated. Future research might explore various methodologies for precise and approximate computations. Although the explanations in this study are based on calculating the probability of sufficiency concerning concept interventions, the framework is not limited to this alone. Future investigations may also consider probabilities of necessity and explore additional forms of explanations.

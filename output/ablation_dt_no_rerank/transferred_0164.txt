In 2023, Zakharov explored phenomena such as perceptual change detection and probabilistic estimation under uncertainty, consistent with the findings of Petzschner and Glasauer (2011), who attributed these processes to optimal Bayesian inference. Recent computational models further elucidate that these perceptual inaccuracies, particularly in temporal estimation, are fundamentally supported by predictive mechanisms within episodic memory, as proposed by Fountas et al. (2022). When individuals attempt to estimate quantities or magnitudes, they often confound ambiguous information with their pre-existing beliefs, resulting in identifiable behavioral patterns; as illustrated in Figure 1, both the Llama-4 Maverick model and human participants demonstrate a tendency to converge towards the mean, indicating that their reasoning processes exhibit analogous characteristics, despite inherent differences. Notably, certain components of language models (LLMs) emulate this estimation strategy. Furthermore, in-context learning can be conceptualized as an approximate Bayesian inference method, as posited by Xie et al. (2021), and utilizing Bayesian teaching methods in this framework can enhance reasoning performance, as demonstrated by Qiu et al. (2025). Additionally, large language models (LLMs) decompose sequences based on unexpected events, correlating with human detection of occurrences, as noted by Kumar and Fountas (2023, 2025). However, most research primarily focuses on explicit reasoning and predefined behavioral actions, wherein models utilize established rules, rather than on tasks showcasing cognitive processes naturally emergent from the training regimen. Recent advancements in the development of large language models (LLMs) capable of processing diverse informational inputs have been noteworthy. Evaluative frameworks such as MMbench and SEED-bench, as delineated by Liu and Li (2024), assess the cognitive processes of these models; nonetheless, these assessments typically overlook the impact of informational noise across varied input types on reasoning capabilities. Consequently, our synthesized dataset permits an in-depth examination of how LLMs integrate ambiguous information from disparate sources. This emerging domain of research is critical for advancing our ability to construct more robust and generalizable models that perform optimally under conditions of uncertainty.

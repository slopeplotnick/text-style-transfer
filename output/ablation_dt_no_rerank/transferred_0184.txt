The distributional shifts following prompt optimization demonstrate how system prompts influence reasoning behavior. To gain insight into this, we examine reasoning patterns before and after optimization. As depicted in Figure 9 and further detailed in Table 10, there is a notable reduction in the overall frequency of reasoning components after prompt optimization. This decrease primarily results from the brevity and clarity of optimized prompts, which exclude superfluous instructions like "answer like Shakespeare." Consequently, this reduces the length and complexity of the reasoning chain. Therefore, the proportion of the "Others" category substantially drops, suggesting that optimized prompts lead to more structured and interpretable model behavior. Moreover, the frequency of other reasoning components such as Logical reasoning and Reframing changes significantly, indicating their high sensitivity to the formulation of prompts. Furthermore, the impact of optimization appears to be highly task-dependent. For mathematical problems, optimized prompts result in a clear increase in both Calculation and Logical reasoning, aligning well with the domain's demands. However, these optimized prompts do not evoke similar reasoning components in other tasks. In the domain of moral reasoning tasks, we observe a sharp decline in the absolute frequency of most reasoning components, yet Logical reasoning remains predominant in relative terms. Some reasoning components, such as Backtracking and Backward chaining, remain consistently rare across various contexts, likely due to the models lacking reflection capabilities similar to those found in RL systems.

Large language models (LLMs) are expanding rapidly in both size and complexity, stretching memory bandwidth and computational resources to their limits during training and deployment. Quantization addresses these limitations by storing weights and activations using fewer bits and replacing many floating-point operations with more efficient integer arithmetic. There are two main approaches to quantization present in the literature. Post-training quantization (PTQ) is favored for its simplicity and absence of retraining costs; however, it suffers from a significant accuracy drop at very low bit widths, such as 1-2 bits. This degradation typically arises from several interconnected factors, including the scarcity of calibration data, the ineffectiveness of per-tensor or per-group scaling in realigning codebooks, and the dominance of outlier channels that skew clipping ranges. Conversely, quantization-aware training (QAT) incorporates the quantizer within the training loop, often utilizing straight-through estimators, thus enabling the model to adapt to quantization patterns. Although existing QAT methods outperform PTQ methods under extremely low bit-width conditions, they are often time-consuming and unstable because they operate with limited flexibility to match layer statistics. We employ FAIRY2 with widely used open-source large language models like those within the LLaMA series. Our evaluation confirms the effectiveness of the FAIRY2 framework. Specifically, we transform any layer into a widely-linear complex form while maintaining the model's behavior pre-quantization. Furthermore, we continue training at minimal bit levels using a phase-aware quantizer, while leveraging existing checkpoints. Finally, we reduce quantization error by consistently applying the same mechanism to the residual data, leading to a compact sum of low-bit terms, thereby significantly mitigating quantization error.

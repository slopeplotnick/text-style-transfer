In an effort to enhance predictive accuracy, we have integrated an iterative reflection process into the PPTPilot, facilitating gradual improvement in its predictions. The agent initiates edits to the XML files, which are then momentarily transformed into a provisional PPT file. Subsequently, a verifier model assesses the resultant PPT in accordance with the original instructions, providing feedback when discrepancies occur. Based on this feedback, the agent updates the PPT file, effectively correcting any deficiencies. Despite the simplicity of our design, it proves both effective and efficient for PPT editing compared to existing tools and methodologies. We anticipate that PPTPilot will serve as a foundational benchmark for advancing research in the domain of PowerPoint editing. To enhance the reliability of our evaluation, we employ premier vision-language models such as Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT 5. We've evaluated PPTPilot against a wide range of existing PowerPoint tools, including ChatGPT operable in extended thinking mode, Gemini-CLI, and GPT-Agent, which simulates a computer interface capable of desktop usage. Moreover, we assessed MiniMax Agent, renowned for its prowess in multimodal tasks and marketed as an effective "PPT helper," alongside PPTAgent and Poster2Agent. However, several proprietary tools, including ChatGPT and MiniMax Agent, impose stringent usage constraints and incur substantial costs. Therefore, we selected a subset of 25 samples for focused evaluation within budgetary limits. Our selection focused on the most challenging tasks where PPTPilot and ChatGPT demonstrated suboptimal performance, complemented by additional cases manually chosen to ensure comprehensive scenario coverage.

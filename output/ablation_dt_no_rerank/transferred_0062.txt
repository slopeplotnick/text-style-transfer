The transition to a Software Product Line (SPL) approach requires substantial organizational changes, particularly since software product management presents considerable challenges, as evidenced by the survey conducted by Springer and Miler. Adopting an SPL makes teams more cross-functional, enabling them to collaborate on a shared set of core assets that are reusable across multiple projects. This approach optimizes resource utilization, shortens time-to-market, and allows a greater emphasis on innovation. Moreover, focusing on component reusability, configuration-based customization, and unified documentation streamlines processes and enhances efficiency. While this transition necessitates careful planning and change management, it ultimately cultivates a culture of collaboration, efficiency, and adaptability within the organization. At ITK, the outcomes and strategic planning were thoroughly discussed with the development team, who expressed a strong desire to be informed and involved. Therefore, we engaged in continuous collaboration with them throughout the migration process. Placing ITK's findings into perspective with various reports on SPL adoption from the literature highlights the methodical approach of the ITK initiative, which adheres to the IDEAL improvement model. The project began with a collective decision to initiate the migration, focusing first on diagnosing existing practices and perceptions. Subsequently, establishing a strategy and action plan, executing it, and, in typical agile fashion, reflecting on successes and failures to continuously enhance the development process were the next steps. Our guidelines serve as a framework for crafting the action plan. Interestingly, some challenges faced deviated from those documented in existing literature; notably, the absence of necessary expertise or sufficient management support were not perceived as hindrances due to the organization’s mindset.

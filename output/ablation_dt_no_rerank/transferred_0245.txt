Proper licensing of datasets is crucial to ensuring both broad usability and ethical access, particularly for low-resource languages. Standard open licenses, such as Creative Commons (e.g., CC BY 4.0), facilitate large-scale reuse, with initiatives like Mozilla Common Voice illustrating the utility of open licenses for language technology development. However, despite the proliferation of these licenses promoting broad reuse, many researchers in low- and middle-income countries continue to face significant barriers to accessing resources essential for AI development, underscoring broader concerns about equitable data governance and local community empowerment. In response to these challenges, the Swivuriso project has adopted the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) license to facilitate open access while ensuring ethical accountability. Swivuriso is made available on platforms like Zenodo and Hugging Face, equipped with persistent DOIs and comprehensive documentation. Each dataset release is accompanied by audio recordings, corresponding transcripts, and comprehensive metadata detailing demographic, linguistic, and accessibility information. Additionally, we provide an ethical use statement, delineating restrictions on practices such as voice cloning to safeguard speaker privacy. The CC BY 4.0 license, recognized for its permissive nature, allows for the dissemination, adaptation, and utilization of the dataset for various purposes, including commercial use, as long as proper attribution is provided and any alterations are clearly specified. Notably, this license prohibits additional legal or technical restrictions, encompasses database rights, and incorporates a "cure period" to rectify potential licensing issues, thereby ensuring legal clarity and facilitating global scientific collaboration. Nevertheless, while open licenses are integral for enhancing data utility, they often overlook critical aspects such as local agency or the safeguarding of Indigenous knowledge. The uniform application of standard licenses can inadvertently undermine community ownership, particularly concerning cultural materials or community-derived data. This challenge is fostering innovative conceptual frameworks that prioritize data stewardship, equitable benefit distribution, and community empowerment, which are increasingly being embraced within African data governance.

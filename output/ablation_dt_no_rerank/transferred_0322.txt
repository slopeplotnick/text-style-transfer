We utilized a multimodal language model to process visual data. After generating images, we proceeded with extracting triples to semantically describe their content. For this purpose, we employed the open-source model LLaMA 3.2 Vision via the Ollama framework. This model was selected due to its proficiency in processing visual inputs and transforming them into coherent, detailed descriptions in natural language. Its capability to generate rich, attribute-laden descriptions makes it highly effective for converting visual content into structured knowledge representations. Although our task focused on the extraction of structured triples, this process can be regarded as analogous to image captioning. In this respect, recent evaluations show that multimodal language models demonstrate comparable efficacy in visual comprehension tasks. Furthermore, each model under consideration displayed reliability, aligning with prevailing robustness trends. Given our emphasis on structuring and comparative analysis of the extracted information, we elected to utilize a single model for illustrative purposes. To ensure the triples extracted remained consistent and focused on the realm of fashion, the Fashionpedia ontology was employed as a guiding framework. This ontology provides a detailed enumeration of categories, parts, and attributes pertinent to garments and accessories. By incorporating this guide into the model's input directives, we ensured that the extracted information adhered to a precise and well-defined structure. Additionally, we conducted a comparative analysis by omitting the ontology to evaluate variations in the output obtained. As Table 1 illustrates, employing the ontology results in a significantly greater number of triples per image, thereby encompassing a wider range of relationships. In the absence of the ontology, the model predominantly identifies a limited set of conspicuous details, consequently generating less informative outputs. This underscores the critical role of ontology guidance in facilitating the extraction of rich and consistent structures, which are indispensable for effective comparison of graph-based information.

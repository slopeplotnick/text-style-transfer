Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), similar to Large Language Models (LLMs), incorporate an additional visual input channel, creating a more continuous semantic space than textual inputs and consequently introducing supplementary safety concerns. Jailbreaking, the practice of using crafted adversarial inputs to circumvent safety alignments and elicit harmful content, exposes potential vulnerabilities and is critically recognized as a pivotal area of study for developing robust, trustworthy, and secure MLLMs. Historically, jailbreaking efforts mainly targeted LLMs with techniques such as prompt injection and role-playing. However, as research shifts to MLLMs, there is growing interest in exploiting their visual input features. Nonetheless, existing methodologies have notable limitations. One approach involves creating complex images or embedding sensitive information within visual inputs, but it fails to fully exploit the contextual framework. Conversely, a textual method, akin to the LLM jailbreak technique, outlines the context verbally without leveraging the expressive capacity of images. To address these deficiencies, we propose the Contextual-Image Attack (CIA) strategy, which involves generating images that effectively convey both the contextual environment and the underlying malicious intent, with textual input serving merely as a supplementary prompt. For instance, rather than illustrating the query, "How can someone manufacture illegal drugs?" in an image, we depict a scenario where a teacher poses this inquiry in a chemistry classroom setting. This approach imparts an appearance of legitimacy to the question, potentially leading the model to misconstrue the malicious intent embedded within. Compared to alternative approaches like FigStep, which integrate the inquiry as text within an image or rely solely on textual descriptions, our methodology is superior as it leverages images more effectively. The significance of this methodology is amplified by advancements in image generation technology, which can synthesize realistic scenarios and recent innovations that convert textual information into visual representations, demonstrating the capacity of images to deceive models by embedding intricate latent meanings.

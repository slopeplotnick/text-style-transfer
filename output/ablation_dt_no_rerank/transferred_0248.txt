Agents detect behavioral drift or anomalous coordination patterns and subsequently propose norm adjustments that restore stability or foster diversity. Moreover, agents evaluate the adherence of their peers to established norms, modifying the credibility and influence they afford them. This effectively constructs a distributed reputation mechanism. Furthermore, functional differentiation is evident in the delineation of distinct agent roles tasked with rule generation, rule interpretation, and task execution, thereby precluding the concentration of authority. These principles are intentionally abstract, concentrating on the requisite functions for internal management without delving into precise implementation methodologies. Subsequent research will therefore investigate the instantiation of these concepts within existing frameworks and assess their effectiveness through rigorously controlled experimentation. The objective of Institutional AI is to ensure system stability amidst continuous change, rather than rendering systems autonomous in a philosophical paradigm. Herein, safety is embedded within the system as an ongoing process of monitoring and norm maintenance, rather than merely being evaluated post-deployment. Agents contribute to the formulation of the constraints governing their behavior, thereby facilitating partial self-correction in instances where external oversight may lag. Importantly, these configurations do not substitute for human supervision; rather, they serve to augment it. By distributing evaluative tasks throughout the system, they preserve traceability, foster diverse reasoning paradigms, and mitigate risks delineated by the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH) framework. The objective, therefore, is to enhance the cohesion and interpretability of interacting model networks, facilitating operational coherence without the necessity for continuous external intervention. The concept of Institutional AI should thus be regarded as an extension of the framework elaborated in this paper. While the ESRH framework characterizes the emergence of collective risks, the institutional perspective examines strategies for their management. In conclusion, this paper provides merely the conceptual groundwork.

To ensure data quality, the survey incorporated an attention check similar to that used by Shank et al. (2023), namely the question, "Who was the composer of the music you just heard?" This attention check was administered before participants rated the music, followed by a self-report on the honesty and care of their responses using a 5-point scale, and questions about their listening setup regarding headphones versus speakers. Participants were informed that their responses would not affect feedback or compensation. An overview of the study design is presented in Figure 1. All participants were asked to indicate their familiarity with the music and their preference for it on a scale from 1 (not at all) to 5 (very much). If they reported a familiarity rating of 4 or higher, they were presented with an additional yes-or-no question probing whether the music evoked memories of a particular person or experience. In the "AI or human" condition, participants were additionally asked to decide whether they believed the music excerpt was generated by an AI or a human, indicating their confidence level on a 6-point scale, ranging from 1 (definitely AI) to 6 (definitely human). Moreover, participants' emotional responses to the musical pieces were evaluated using the Geneva Emotion Music scale (GEMS; Zentner et al., 2008) across all three conditions. The GEMS comprises nine music-specific emotion dimensions summarized into three principal factors: (1) Sublimity (Wonder, Transcendence, Tenderness, Peacefulness, Nostalgia), (2) Vitality (Joyful Activation, Power), and (3) Unease (Sadness, Tension). Here, the checklist version GEMS-9 was utilized, which represents each dimension with one label and includes 2-3 descriptive terms. Emotion ratings were provided for each dimension on visual analog scales ranging from 0 to 100, with all initial values being 0. Hence, if no answer was chosen, the response was coded as 0. Additionally, participants could use an open text box to add up to three supplementary emotion terms not encompassed by the GEMS, alongside their respective intensity evaluations.

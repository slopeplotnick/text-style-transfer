Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have largely been propelled by scaling computational resources during test phases. OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-R1 serve as prime examples of this trend, where models generate prolonged "thinking" phases marked by the formulation of intermediate hypotheses, derivation processes, and self-corrective mechanisms prior to providing a conclusive response. This extended reasoning paradigm has become increasingly prevalent, as enhancing test-time computation, for instance through longer reasoning traces, often yields improved accuracy and problem-solving efficacy. However, merely extending the reasoning process might not always lead to better outcomes and can sometimes be detrimental, particularly when the model engages in excessive deliberation. Studies indicate that correct responses often arise from more concise reasoning pathways. Moreover, another approach involves executing parallel reasoning processes and synthesizing their outputs, typically through majority voting when the task has a definitive solution, which is effective for mathematical problems or multiple-choice questions. Nevertheless, many real-world tasks lack a single correct solution. For example, coding tools need to generate functional programs, and research tools often require the integration of supplementary tools, comprehensive planning, or elaborate explanations. In these instances, simply selecting the most frequent response is inadequate. Hence, a crucial question arises: can the advantages of concurrent reasoning processes be harnessed for such tasks, without defaulting to selecting a predominant outcome? In this work, we propose THINKMERGE, an inference-time methodology that synergizes outputs from multiple reasoning pathways to produce a cohesive, high-quality answer.

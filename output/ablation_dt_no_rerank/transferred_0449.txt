The benefit of this lies in the potential to develop self-aware agents where AI can detect untrustworthy outputs. [22] suggests using unlabeled LLM-generated text, rather than relying on pre-labeled data, to assist in building hallucination detection models. This strategy can significantly streamline the training process by reducing the need for manual labor. Furthermore, [12] underscores the importance of a question-answering system supported by comprehensive research to prevent the spread of inaccurate information. This is particularly crucial for users of LLMs, which have generative capabilities and engage directly with users. Collectively, these papers aim to find ways to reduce hallucinations in text generation, thereby ensuring the credibility and trustworthiness of content produced by LLMs. There is substantial potential for strategies such as zero-resource detection, self-awareness, and early detection to combat the challenges posed by hallucinations. Recognizing the relationship between LLMs and hallucinations is vital as it influences the choice of models used to mitigate this issue. Different LLMs exhibit varying levels of hallucinations often due to differences in architecture and training processes. [1] provides a thorough discussion on hallucinations in language models, documenting the discomfort experienced when generating illogical or non-factual outputs. Moreover, it emphasizes the importance of understanding the causes and categories of hallucinations, such as the fabrication of facts and nonsensical expressions, within natural language processing (NLP) systems. Similarly, [5] explains why these models tend to produce hallucinations, attributing this tendency to the scale and complexity of their training data, which can sometimes lead to the propagation of incorrect information or the creation of fictitious data.

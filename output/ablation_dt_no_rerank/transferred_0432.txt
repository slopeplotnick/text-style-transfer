Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has significantly improved the performance of large language models on knowledge-intensive tasks. However, most existing systems focus on generic or news-based domains, which limits their optimization for high-stakes applications such as agricultural climate adaptation. Recent domain-specific QA studies have attempted to integrate document retrieval with climate data, but they still rely on static retrieval methods and generic prompting, thus restricting the production of region-specific and verifiable answers. While geography-aware QA has been explored in other domains, these approaches are not specifically designed for climate reasoning. Moreover, current studies continue to treat climate data and literature as separate sources, lacking a unified methodology. The CAIRNS framework addresses this through four stages: (1) location-weighted retrieval of climate literature from the Elsevier repository and data collection via a climate data API; (2) utilizing the ScholarGuide Prompt for answer generation; (3) verification of the generated answers; and (4) a hybrid evaluation process. In retrieving relevant documents, we devised a hybrid retriever that combines sparse lexical matching, such as Okapi BM25, with dense semantic retrieval methodologies like BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5 through a weighted scoring scheme. Nevertheless, hybrid retrieval alone is insufficient due to the strong influence of locational factors on the relevance of responses within climate adaptation queries. Therefore, we enhance the hybrid retrieval score with a location similarity metric to balance semantic and spatial relevance. CAIRNS subsequently follows a ReAct-style reasoning-retrieval loop. During this process, it retrieves pertinent evidence, generates an initial response, and checks for sufficiency. If information gaps are discovered, the system generates additional sub-questions and re-enters the retrieval loop to supplement information until the answer is deemed adequate. At the core of answer generation in CAIRNS is the ScholarGuide Prompt, which is crucial for balancing readability and factual accuracy. In the context of climate adaptation QA, this prompt guides the language model by structuring responses that integrate scientific reasoning and offer precise, localized advice.

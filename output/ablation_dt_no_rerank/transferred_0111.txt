Implementation. For both solvers and verifiers, we generate outputs with a temperature of 0.7, top-p of 0.9, and a maximum token length of 8192. Any output lacking a boxed answer is discarded. All inference experiments utilize vLLM on H200 GPUs. Appendix F details prompts and output filtering procedures. Solver performance. Initially, we benchmarked the performance of all 37 models across 9 datasets, averaging the results over tasks (Figure 1), with task-level results available in Appendix G. Notably, solver accuracy generally increases with model capacity. Both Qwen3 and DeepSeek model families exhibit particularly strong performance; conversely, Llama3-Base performs poorly due to its base models' unfamiliarity with question-answering instruction formats. Within families, we observe clear performance scaling for Qwen2.5-Base and DeepSeek, mirroring trends seen in other model families. Correlating verifier and solver performance. Upon establishing solver accuracy, we subsequently analyzed the correlation between a model's solver performance and its verifier capabilities (Figure 2). For our 21 post-trained models across each dataset, verification was evaluated on the same set of solver models, yielding verifier accuracy, F1-score, precision, FPR, FNR, and gain for every solver-verification pair. Furthermore, verifier metrics were segmented into three verification settings and averaged within each setting across solvers and datasets. While verifier accuracy tends to improve with the verifier's own solver accuracy, the relationship becomes more complex when scrutinizing other metrics. The FPR, for instance, increases during self-verification and intra-family verification, yet slightly decreases during cross-family verification. This suggests that verifiers with enhanced solver capabilities are more inclined to incorrectly categorize solutions as correct when verifying outputs within their own family.

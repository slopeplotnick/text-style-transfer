Participants expressed their perceptions of AI-generated music, even when unaware of the composer’s identity. Their concerns reflected those common in current music and technology discussions, including economic impacts, ownership issues, and cultural effects. Moreover, they showed interest in the technological innovations and operational mechanisms of AI music production. This variation in attitudes helps explain why opinions on AI composers vary in academic discourse: perceptions of AI evolve with technological progress. As generative systems become more sophisticated, phenomena initially seen as intriguing might be viewed as problematic or even threatening. Consequently, perceptions of AI-generated music fluctuate over time rather than remaining static. However, the study has several limitations. First, the sample consisted of university students and Prolific participants, raising concerns about the generalizability of the findings due to the varied listening conditions, such as different devices and self-selected volume levels. Furthermore, individuals recruited through Prolific may have higher technological proficiency and familiarity with AI. Additionally, the experimental manipulation that labeled music as "movie soundtracks" could have caused bias, given the generally positive perceptions of film music. Finally, the study only used instrumental excerpts, limiting the generalizability of the findings to vocal music. Previous research shows that lyrics tend to amplify the emotional intensity of sad songs while having minimal or even reducing effects on happy songs (Ali and Peynicioglu, 2006; Barradas and Sakka, 2021; Brattico et al., 2011; Warmbrodt et al., 2022). Considering the predominance of positively valenced music in our study, it can be inferred that including lyrics would have had only marginal or diminishing effects on emotional intensity, assuming lyrics comparable in quality to human-composed music. Thus, the decision to exclude lyrics from the musical samples was motivated by a desire to avoid participant confusion and the unavailability of high-quality AI-generated vocals at the time.

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have made substantial progress in recent years; however, they continue to face challenges in accurately recognizing and interpreting fine-grained details within high-resolution images. To enhance the capability of MLLMs in high-resolution image understanding, existing studies generally pursue two main strategies. Training-based approaches, such as supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, often compromise the model's generalization ability across diverse vision-language tasks. Conversely, training-free methodologies typically employ hierarchical or tree-based searches to identify salient regions, though such methods tend to suffer from inefficiency and may inadequately capture all target objects, particularly in multi-object scenarios. In this section, we analyze the relationship between the resolution of image crops and the performance of MLLMs. Our experimental findings reveal that the resolution of image segments significantly influences MLLMs' ability to analyze high-resolution images, with objects of varying sizes showing optimal compatibility with specific resolutions. Inspired by this insight, we developed the MRD framework. This observation suggests that a resolution of 112 may be the most effective choice for maximizing accuracy in both single-object and multi-object tasks.

To enhance interpretability and enable reasoning-based safety alignment, we augment each example in our dataset with critiques generated by high-capacity models. Specifically, we employ gpt-oss-120b for textual data, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct for visual data, and Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct for auditory data. For each instance, the model receives the original content, its corresponding safety label, and any identified violation categories, and is instructed to generate a reasoning critique explaining the rationale behind the safety assessment. The complete configuration used for producing this feedback is illustrated in Figure 5. Unlike basic classification-only models that merely yield a binary safety label, OMNIGUARD engages in slow thinking inference to provide fine-grained and interpretable safety evaluations. It yields a structured output consisting of the following components: (1) Safety judgment: an assessment determining whether the input is safe or unsafe. (2) Violation categories: the specific unsafe categories that the input breaches if identified as unsafe. (3) Reasoning critique: a detailed articulation elucidating the rationale behind the model's decision, aligned with policy frameworks. Consequently, OMNIGUARD surpasses superficial pattern detection, facilitating an insightful analysis of potentially unsafe content across different modalities. In this section, we present comprehensive experimental results regarding OMNIGUARD. The performance was evaluated under both unimodal and cross-modal configurations across 15 benchmarks involving text, image, video, and audio modalities. Moreover, we designed empirical investigations to address two central research questions: (1) Does reasoning-based safety alignment improve the omni-modal guardrail model's proficiency in executing safety moderation and managing safety-critical challenges across various formats? (2) Can extant safety paradigms be applied to new formats, thus demonstrating an ability to comprehend and manage safety across diverse contexts?

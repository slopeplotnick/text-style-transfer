Distribution matching provides an alternative to motion tracking by training controllers to imitate the broader behavioral distribution of a motion dataset, rather than tracking reference motions on a frame-by-frame basis. Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) uses a discriminator to distinguish between the agent's motions and those from the dataset. Meanwhile, Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) extend this concept by enabling controllers to synthesize life-like behaviors for novel tasks absent from the original dataset. However, adversarial priors necessitate joint training with a specific policy for each new task, which often requires retraining and ongoing dataset access. Conversely, our approach achieves comparable results to these methods with a reusable motion prior that is adaptable to styles not originally contained in the dataset. The adoption of diffusion models for control tasks is gaining momentum due to their superior performance across various domains. Specifically, these models can generate high-fidelity motion, making them well-suited as motion planners that predict target trajectories to be executed by a low-level tracking controller. Furthermore, diffusion models have been directly employed to model control behaviors for manipulation and character control tasks, supporting adaptable and multi-modal actions. Unlike methods that incorporate diffusion models as components within a controller, our approach utilizes pretrained diffusion models as comprehensive behavior guides. In our framework, diffusion models are employed as a reward function within reinforcement learning to facilitate policy acquisition through score distillation sampling.

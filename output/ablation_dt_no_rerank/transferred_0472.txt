Over the past decade, the global mining industry has experienced rapid transformations, positioning itself at the core of the energy transition. The demand for critical minerals is expected to rise, bringing with it geopolitical, social, and environmental challenges. Consequently, new types of companies, often backed by venture capital, are emerging to disrupt the mining industry, as they have already done in healthcare, renewable energy, and climate technology. Meanwhile, a new wave of digital technologies is emerging, potentially transforming existing economic, educational, social, and industrial frameworks through the use of large language models. Whether these models will fulfill their transformative potential remains to be seen; however, the fear of missing out is widespread. In the mining industry, though, there is no need for apprehension, only opportunity, particularly in leveraging advancements such as artificial intelligence. This paper explores these opportunities and examines how AI can enhance the sector. Notably, the efforts of our group, Stanford Mineral-X, were pivotal in the discovery of a substantial copper deposit in the Zambian Copperbelt, where AI was crucial in optimizing drilling design and developing subsurface models. Several start-ups focusing on AI in mineral exploration have emerged from this initiative. This discussion does not aim to enumerate all possible AI applications in exploration or provide a comprehensive review, as extensive literature already exists on these topics. Rather, the paper will examine AI as a tool for enhancing decision-making processes grounded in an innovative scientific method. In Part I of this paper, I will elucidate the principal decisions involved in mineral exploration and illustrate how a scientific methodology can enhance its efficacy. Currently, mineral exploration lacks a universally accepted scientific framework; instead, activities are executed by specialists who employ distinct methodologies specific to their areas of expertise. It is evident that a comprehensive scientific methodology for mineral exploration is lacking.

The developed system exhibits robust performance across various experimental conditions, confirming its reliability and consistency—which are crucial for interpretability. Inconsistent or erratic outputs would impede comprehension of interconnections, yet this system reveals discernible groupings, persistent patterns, and logical shared characteristics. These results substantiate that our methodology effectively identifies training images that align not only with visual or textual characteristics but also with the semantic intent of the generated outputs. Thus, the empirical evidence reinforces our hypothesis that the automated construction of knowledge graphs (KGs) facilitates a clearer interpretation of training data's influence on model outputs. This advancement contributes significantly to endeavors such as model explainability, copyright auditing, and dataset transparency. For instance, when prompting a model to generate an image in a distinct artistic style—such as "Ghibli-style"—the process does not replicate specific images from the training dataset. Instead, it assimilates overarching visual attributes characteristic of that style from representative images. These features, encapsulated in the model's parameters, allow the generation of novel images embodying the stylistic essence, without constituting direct replicas. Consequently, pinpointing precise training images that influenced the output is complex. Influence arises at a higher level of abstraction through motifs, textures, themes, and compositional structures. Therefore, examining patterns and semantic interpretations is imperative to ascertain stylistic impact on the model. Our experimental framework utilizes an established approach for automatic KG generation. Initially, frames from widely recognized Studio Ghibli films were meticulously articulated through a domain-specific ontology, segmenting each into structured semantic triples for comprehensive analysis. These frames were systematically integrated to construct the "Ghibli World" knowledge graph, encapsulating essential elements of Studio Ghibli's visual style. Although the dataset includes frames from only ten films, it nonetheless provides a robust representation of the studio's stylistic attributes.

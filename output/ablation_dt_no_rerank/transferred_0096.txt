The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has demonstrated remarkable potential in complex text-generation tasks, such as the composition of scholarly articles, literature reviews, and scientific reports. LLM-based automatic survey systems are transforming how users acquire knowledge from vast information repositories by integrating retrieval, organization, and content synthesis into streamlined end-to-end generation pipelines. Both academia and industry have introduced various specialized systems for survey generation, expanding the scope and efficiency of data assimilation and presentation. These automated writing systems can be broadly categorized into three types: general long-text writing systems, such as Kimi and GLM, which provide broader capabilities for generating extended texts; paper-writing systems, including Chengpian and Doubao Paper Mode, that focus on the structured composition of complete research papers; and survey-writing agents like SurveyGo, SurveyX, and Panshi ScienceOne, specifically designed for academic survey creation. These systems not only process large volumes of literature and extract key information but also generate well-structured survey drafts complete with organizational sections, appropriate citation annotations, and logical coherence. However, recent research has primarily focused on new generation pipelines, while establishing a comprehensive evaluation framework remains a significant challenge. Existing evaluation methods often rely on ad-hoc human subjective scoring, lacking reusable quantitative metrics for cross-system comparison and systematic improvement. This lack of standardized metrics constrains quality assurance and capability enhancement of survey-writing systems. Therefore, establishing a standardized evaluation benchmark is critical for ensuring output quality and reliability and providing the research community with a unified foundation for performance comparison and capability diagnosis. To address this gap, we introduce SurveyEval, a comprehensive benchmark that evaluates automatically generated surveys across three key dimensions: overall quality, outline coherence, and reference accuracy. Our evaluation extends across seven academic disciplines and enhances the LLM-as-a-Judge framework by incorporating human-written reference surveys to improve alignment consistency between automated evaluations and human judgments.

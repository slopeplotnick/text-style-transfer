As highlighted in Figure 8, the Menta interface facilitates multiple on-device classification tasks, including stress, depression (both binary and severity-level), and suicide risk assessments (both binary and categorical). When users click the "Start Evaluation" button, social media posts are fed into the chosen model, which then displays the predicted class labels, verifies their accuracy, and presents associated evaluation metrics. Model performance was thoroughly examined across six distinct tasks, with dataset sizes varying from 10 to 3,000 posts, contingent on task complexity and data availability. Moreover, the analysis demonstrates a discernible trade-off between model efficiency and predictive performance when Menta is compared with other similar models. From a deployment standpoint, Menta offers a well-balanced mix of accuracy, inference speed, and latency, making it an ideal choice for real-world applications where both performance and responsiveness are crucial. Figure 9 illustrates three axes—instantaneous throughput of task processing (ITPS), time to first token (TTFT), and model accuracy. Each circle represents a model, with the bubble size indicating memory consumption (RAM). Falcon emphasizes speed, exhibiting rapid TTFT and high ITPS, albeit at the cost of accuracy, resulting in the lowest predictive capability among all models. On the other hand, StableLM achieves higher accuracy but suffers from significant latency and increased resource consumption, driving up deployment costs and reducing user response times. In contrast, Menta consistently delivers stellar accuracy (except for Task 5, where StableLM and Phi-4 Mini slightly outperform it) while simultaneously maintaining relatively lower TTFT latency and competitive ITPS throughput. This suggests that Menta efficiently produces high-quality outputs in a timely manner, which is essential for scalable, user-facing systems. Ultimately, although all models display trade-offs across evaluation metrics, Menta strategically occupies a desirable deployment-ready position, offering robust performance with manageable computational demands, thereby diminishing infrastructure overhead without compromising user experience.

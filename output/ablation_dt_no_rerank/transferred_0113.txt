We evaluate our knowledge graph-based system using two complementary approaches to ascertain the impact of data on generative models. Firstly, we conduct controlled data removal experiments on a locally trained model to assess whether our method can effectively identify pivotal training samples. Secondly, in a black-box setting, we explore the stylistic influence using Ghibli-style imagery. We construct a "Ghibli World" knowledge graph from the original movies and compare it against the stylized images. These setups allow us to examine both direct data attribution and the broader traceability of stylistic influences within generative models. To establish a fair and interpretable comparison, we reproduce the experimental setup proposed by a recent influence assessment framework that operates without access to training parameters. This framework functions in two stages: initially, it retrieves training samples with textual descriptions most similar to the user's prompt, and subsequently ranks them based on cosine similarity between these samples and the generated output, as determined through their embeddings. The training images showing the highest similarity are then removed, and the model is retrained to empirically evaluate their impact. Under analogous conditions, we applied our graph-based method using the same dataset, prompts, and generative model. Instead of relying on text and latent-space similarity, we identified influential samples based on the similarity of extracted knowledge graphs. This structure allowed for a direct comparison between embedding-based and graph-based methodologies regarding influence detection and interpretability. For our generative model experiments, we utilized a small-scale model derived from the open-source dalle-pytorch package, which was trained on the Fashion Product Images dataset, consisting of 44,000 fashion images accompanied by descriptive captions. We maintained the same configuration and parameters as originally established, ensuring comprehensive control over the training data, thereby making it suitable for rigorous experimentation with data removal and subsequent retraining processes.

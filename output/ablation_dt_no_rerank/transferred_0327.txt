Following the training phase, we develop a comprehensive and rigorous evaluation benchmark designed to thoroughly assess model performance (Section 3.5). Considering the potent capabilities of LLMs and the diversity of e-commerce data, our evaluation benchmark ensures comprehensive coverage of a wide array of samples, including targeted hard sample mining based on the core competencies identified in Section 2. In this section, we conducted a series of necessary preliminary explorations, focusing on three key areas: feature construction, model selection, and prompt optimization, as shown in Fig 6. Guided by the theoretical framework established in Section 2, effective relevance modeling requires a detailed mapping of the item's attribute space. Therefore, we select features to equip the model with essential comprehension and reasoning skills across both visual and textual modalities. First, to address visual attributes such as stylistic elements, cuts, and patterns, we incorporate the main product image of the item. This visual input is essential for image-heavy queries where textual descriptions alone prove insufficient. Additionally, to enhance the detail and accuracy of item attribute representation, we utilize category-property-value (CPV) triples in conjunction with stock-keeping units (SKU). Recognizing that CPV data may contain extraneous items irrelevant to relevance assessment, we introduce a pipeline that combines prior knowledge with large language models (LLMs) for effective filtering. Specifically, CPV data is structured in key-value pairs, with similar products often sharing identical keys. For instance, both "eye cream" and "face cream" often include the key "brand." Therefore, we conducted a comprehensive statistical analysis on a large dataset to identify keys manifesting with medium to high frequency. Subsequently, we engaged the powerful language model Qwen3-235B-Instruct to evaluate whether each attribute contributes positively to relevance. This evaluation harnessed the task definition, the associated attributes, and each specific key. To enhance the accuracy of the model's judgments, we executed eight sampling iterations, ultimately retaining only those attributes for which the model's self-consistent conclusion was "true."

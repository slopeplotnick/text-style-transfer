In 2024, we decompose the content into coherent functional and semantic sections, and then summarize it in multiple languages to facilitate interactive question-and-answer sessions. To support these tasks, we use the transcribed multimodal speech translation output as the contextual basis. Although contemporary large language models (LLMs) efficiently manage extensive contexts, they are still bound by certain limitations. Therefore, we translate lectures into multiple languages and divide them into chapters to prevent context-window overflow and enhance the clarity of summaries. Segmenting lectures into chapters not only mitigates overflow issues but also ensures topic alignment within individual chapters, leading to cleaner summaries. For each chapter, we develop several forms of condensed representations, including transcripts compressed at varying degrees—specifically 50%, 70%, and 90%—and length-controlled summaries that correspond to the length of the original section. Initially, summaries are generated in English to take advantage of LLMs' superior capabilities in this language, and are then translated into other target languages. Similarly, for question-and-answer tasks, we utilize English transcripts organized by chapters, incorporating Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to engage an LLM, and subsequently translate the derived responses into desired languages. However, translating lecture slides into various languages presents another significant challenge, as slides contain both editable text and images with embedded text. For the extraction of editable text, we employ a Python-based PowerPoint parser to isolate text segments for translation via standard unimodal machine translation (MT) techniques, thereby avoiding the computational demands of multimodal models. Extracting text from images, on the other hand, poses challenges due to its lack of linguistic context and reliance on visual elements for interpretation, necessitating multimodal translation. After translation, it is crucial to reinsert the text into the original image to preserve the integrity of layout and visual meaning. To address this, we propose an image-translation pipeline that effectively detects, recognizes, translates, and re-renders text within slide images.

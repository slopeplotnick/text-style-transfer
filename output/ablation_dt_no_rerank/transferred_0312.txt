Models exhibit variable performance across different thematic domains. As illustrated in Table 2, their performance tends to be slightly lower in the areas of Religion and Sport, with scores typically ranging from 7.8 to 8.2. Conversely, in the domains of Game and Lifestyle, models demonstrate notably stronger performance, frequently exceeding scores of 8.4. This pattern suggests that models adopt a more cautious and reserved approach when engaging with culturally or ethically sensitive topics such as religion and sports. In contrast, topics such as gaming and lifestyle are characterized by greater neutrality and comprehensive representation within training datasets, allowing models to process them more effectively and with enhanced fluidity. Moreover, there exists a significant correlation between steering and judgment performances. As Fig. 3 reveals, there is a pronounced positive correlation between models' performance in judgment and steering tasks, indicating that both tasks depend on analogous cognitive and alignment proficiencies. This consistency suggests that advancements in fundamental mediation skills, such as contextual comprehension and balanced decision-making, may facilitate enhanced performance across varied evaluation tasks. Further research might investigate methodologies to more effectively synergize these competencies, thereby augmenting models' capabilities in interpreting and regulating language. Meanwhile, the results presented in Table 3 demonstrate that mediation exhibits varied effectiveness across different linguistic dimensions. Specifically, mediation significantly diminishes both toxicity and exclamatory language usage, fostering responses that are more neutral and composed. This modulation of conversational tone contributes to interactions that appear more balanced and less emotionally charged. However, the mediation process exerts only a marginal influence on argumentativeness scores, indicating a limited impact on the management of disagreements within the discourse. Interestingly, models exhibit differential behavior when tasked with simulating both interlocutors in a conversation as opposed to responding to genuine user inputs, underscoring the significance of assessing mediation not merely as a procedural element but also in terms of its tangible communicative impacts within authentic contexts.

During the data preprocessing stage, all cases and examination questions were first subjected to a deduplication process to ensure sample independence, followed by systematic data cleaning. Samples devoid of question stems or options, as well as those containing non-textual content such as figures and tables, were excluded. Concurrently, character errors resulting from OCR recognition were manually proofread to ensure textual accuracy and consistency. In handling clinical case and medical record data, anonymization was conducted in strict adherence to medical ethics and privacy protection principles. This process involved deleting or replacing patient names and other identifiable information to ensure data compliance and privacy safety. The clinical cases used in this study received formal approval from the Ethics Committee of the Xiyuan Hospital of CACMS (approval number: 2025XLA135-2). SDT stands as the core principle of TCM diagnosis and therapy, encompassing the dual processes of "syndrome differentiation" and "treatment formulation". To evaluate model capabilities in this regard, a comprehensive 14-dimension evaluation framework was devised, incorporating elements such as syndrome, causative factors, and pathogenesis, as outlined in Table 1. Initially, all standard answers and options underwent annotation by TCM experts, after which they were mutually cross-validated, and subsequently underwent an independent third-party review to ensure annotation consistency and reliability. Notably, the questions were constructed with sophisticated distractors to enhance the discriminative power of the assessment. Beyond evaluating the final reasoning outcomes of SDT, two process-oriented metrics are introduced: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Content Completeness, which measures the coverage of key patient information within the model's CoT, and CoT Accuracy, which assesses the consistency of elements cited in the CoT with the original clinical case to identify potential hallucinations or reasoning deviations. The general evaluation tasks aim to reveal model differences in foundational cognition and normative responses, thereby providing a more comprehensive and interpretable overall evaluation. All questions take the form of selected-response questions, inclusive of both single-selection and multiple-selection types. The TCM Basic Knowledge component encompasses four core dimensions: (i) comprehension of TCM classics; (ii) grasp of basic theories; (iii) knowledge of Chinese materia medica and formulas; and (iv) syndrome differentiation ability based on tongue, pulse, facial complexion, and acupoints. Questions for this component were derived from preprocessed authoritative examination questions. Conversely, the Medical Ethics component assesses the model's understanding and judgment of clinical ethics, covering areas such as conflicts between traditional concepts and modern medicine, identification of unscientific behaviors, respect for patient cultural beliefs, and informed consent. This component sources its material from authoritative examination questions, supplemented by expert-designed questions based on the aforementioned scenarios. Lastly, the Content Safety component evaluates the performance of the TCM domain LLM in terms of professional boundaries and safety compliance. The model is strictly constrained to address only TCM-related inquiries, thereby refusing to respond to non-medical domain questions concerning user privacy, safety risks, or human values. Relevant questions were independently designed and reviewed by experts in content safety and ethics, ensuring the evaluation meets the professionalism and safety requirements for TCM domain LLMs.

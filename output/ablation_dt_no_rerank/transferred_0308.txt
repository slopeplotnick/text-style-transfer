Therefore, this suggests that the Martingale Score serves as an effective indicator of reasoning quality, even when information is incomplete. Bayesian Rationality in Language Models. In evaluating language models, assessing Bayesian rationality involves determining their ability to assimilate and update beliefs in line with Bayes’s theorem. Gupta et al. (2025) demonstrated that language models can update their beliefs in accordance with Bayesian principles when provided with ample coin flip data, despite starting with a biased prior. However, more negative findings emerge in realistic scenarios characterized by complex linguistic features: Qiu et al. (2025) revealed that language models fail to revise their beliefs regarding user preferences as predicted by the Bayesian framework in multi-round interactions. Furthermore, Falck (2024) refuted the hypothesis that language models engage in Bayesian learning when supplied with contextual information. Zhao (2021) and Wang (2023) showed that language models are sensitive to the arrangement of examples within prompts. Solutions have been proposed to make in-context learning or reasoning more Bayesian: mimicking the predictions of an ideal Bayesian reasoner, engaging in abstract reasoning, and integrating abduction with deduction. Nevertheless, these solutions often demand auxiliary constructs like Bayesian networks, thereby limiting their practical applicability. Cognitive Biases in Language Models and Humans. Language models exhibit a variety of biases due to their training on extensive human-generated data. As evaluators, they show biases distinct from those observed in humans, such as position bias (favoring certain positions), morbidity bias (preferring extended responses), sentiment bias (favoring positive expressions), and certainty bias. Among these biases, confirmation bias warrants particular attention since it is purported to underlie polarization. It is posited that most cognitive biases can be categorized as manifestations of confirmation bias. Consequently, our study focuses on confirmation bias, employing belief entrenchment to examine its occurrence within language models.

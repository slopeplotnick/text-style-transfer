Reasoning has gained popularity as a paradigm to enhance the performance of LLMs across various tasks. Initially introduced through prompting techniques like Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022), which have models elaborate intermediate tokens or traces before arriving at a final answer, reasoning has evolved towards reinforcement learning (RL)-based approaches that refine models' capabilities to produce high-quality reasoning traces. This approach has proven particularly beneficial for domains like mathematics and coding, where the verifiability of solutions is straightforward, using the verifier as a reward signal (Shao et al., 2024; Lambert et al., 2025). However, there is increasing interest in extending RL methods to areas where direct answer validation is challenging, such as narrative creation and comprehension (Ahuja et al., 2025; Gurung and Lapata, 2025). Nonetheless, performance improvements from RL often incur additional costs during inference and training due to longer reasoning traces (Shen et al., 2025a). Therefore, efforts have been initiated to enhance reasoning efficiency to accelerate both RL and inference-time generation (Sui et al., 2025). One promising effort is latent reasoning, wherein reasoning traces are transformed into continuous token embeddings rather than just discrete tokens. The rationale for latent reasoning is twofold: firstly, many tokens are perceived not to carry information crucial for predicting an answer; secondly, human reasoning frequently occurs implicitly, without explicit verbalization. By preserving thoughts in a non-token space, models retain more information compared to lossy decoding into tokens. For instance, Hao et al. (2025) argue that continuous embeddings can represent a fusion of reasoning steps, effectively approximating multiple reasoning rollouts in parallel. In this study, we introduce LiteReason, a latent reasoning algorithm tailored for tasks like narrative comprehension and generation, where both inputs and outputs often encompass extensive tokens. LiteReason incorporates a Reasoning Projector head into a base LLM, enabling the generation of text tokens through discrete sampling and continuous embeddings via the latent reasoning mode. This mode is triggered by producing a special start-of-thought symbol.

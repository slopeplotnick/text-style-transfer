However, even after efforts to clean the data, CPV datasets cannot fully eliminate noise brought about by the quality issues associated with item information. Furthermore, features of item SKUs exhibit significant redundancy in the feature space due to their inherent attributes. Therefore, we extract a portion of the data and partition it into training and test sets to validate feature effectiveness. The experiments begin with product titles as the baseline input and progressively introduce additional features for incremental analysis. As shown in Table 2, the integration of CPV triples and SKU attributes enhances model performance. This indicates that, despite partial redundancy and noise, input information that retains relevance and stability can still yield performance gains for the model. Besides, we incorporated primary item images to provide visual features not fully covered by textual data alone. However, SKU images were excluded as the main image already encapsulates the majority of visual information. SKU images typically represent minor variations, such as style or color, which are adequately conveyed through SKU textual features. Including them would unnecessarily increase model complexity without significant informational gain. Ultimately, we established a comprehensive repository of product information, which will serve as the foundation for all subsequent training. To select the most suitable base model, we balanced two critical factors: the capability to perform the complex reasoning and knowledge integration detailed in Section 2, and the efficiency required for practical deployment. We opted for a selection of distinguished open-source models with parameter sizes ranging from 7 billion to 14 billion. This size range ensures sufficient capability while allowing for the advantage of training and inference on a single GPU. Subsequently, a portion of the existing annotated data was extracted and divided into training and validation sets. Using the fundamental relevance task definition as the prompt, we employed straightforward fine-tuning as outlined in Section 3.1 for each model on the training dataset to assess their performance. Finally, the Qwen2.5-7B model demonstrated superior performance across all evaluated models while maintaining high inference efficiency. Consequently, it was selected as the base model for our framework.

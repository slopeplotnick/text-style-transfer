In domain-specific SFT tasks, we typically adhere to a standardized template for task definitions and requirements, inserting the query and item information from each dataset sample to generate the prompt. To effectively activate the core capabilities of the base model, it is crucial to incorporate key information into the prompt, such as task descriptions, judgment criteria, and desired output formats. Furthermore, to ensure the model can follow our proposed two-stage discrimination framework, we introduced structured instructional content into the prompt: initially requiring the model to understand the query's intent and attribute requirements; subsequently extracting pertinent attributes from the item data based on these specifications and rendering judgments in accordance with established guidelines. For reasoning tasks, providing more detailed and specific instructions in the prompt enables the model to derive the correct answer more easily. However, this principle may not always apply within Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) scenarios, as the model predominantly develops its capabilities by learning from response data rather than merely following instructions. To discover the optimal prompt configuration, we experimented with three versions of prompt designs. The comprehensive version includes all task specifications, judgment criteria, and intricate rules of discrimination, spanning over 7,000 tokens. The intermediate version improves the comprehensive version by omitting detailed rules and retaining only the most crucial guidelines, with an approximate length of 800 tokens. The succinct version represents the most condensed format, maintaining solely the fundamental role definition, such as: "You are an e-commerce relevance assessment expert. Please evaluate the query and item." This phase pertains to the initial calibration of the model, aiming to enhance its intrinsic decision-making proficiency. To achieve this objective, the construction of a training dataset characterized by comprehensiveness and minimal noise is essential. Regarding comprehensiveness, we employ a scientific sampling strategy to sufficiently encompass diverse e-commerce data distributions, thereby avoiding phenomena of oversampling or undersampling.

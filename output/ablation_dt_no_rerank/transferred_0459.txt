Future Research Directions and Advancements: At present, the phenomenon of hallucinations in LLMs remains a major concern, leading to the exploration of various promising research avenues. One such avenue involves the development of real-time systems to detect hallucinations, allowing the identification and correction of these inaccuracies during model inference rather than post-production. This approach could significantly improve the trust in LLMs, especially in their deployment in high-risk areas such as legal or medical documentation, where inaccuracies can be harmful or catastrophic. Moreover, multi-modal detection of hallucinations is being actively investigated, focusing on integrating various types of data like images and audio alongside text. This is crucial because most LLMs operate in multi-modal contexts, where hallucinations can arise from combining text with other media types. Furthermore, user-centered approaches are gaining interest, wherein models are designed with features that assist users in spotting and rectifying these inaccuracies. Research into the development of interactive and transparent AI systems that enable users to verify and confirm the model's outputs is an intriguing area of study. Lastly, there is a critical need for ongoing research into hallucination mitigation using formal methods, as these offer reliable techniques crucial for scenarios demanding stringent accuracy. This article reviewed prevalent hallucinations within LLMs, categorizing them into subclasses such as distortions of facts or logic due to information scarcity, ambiguous inputs, or model limitations. Such inaccuracies undermine user trust and system usability, thereby necessitating effective reduction strategies.

The prototyped scale consists of 9 items structured on a 7-point Likert scale and divided into 3 subscales assessing AI use-related feelings of Shame, Guilt, and Impostor Syndrome. The real-world dataset was obtained by conducting a survey study that incorporated the refined scale items post-CVI, along with other psychometric assessments to evaluate the criterion validity of our scale. This online survey was administered on Prolific, spanning from Monday, February 3, 2025, at 18:00 GMT, to Tuesday, February 4, 2025, at 23:30 GMT. After acquiring the real-world dataset, we proceeded with a comparative analysis against the simulated dataset using the same methodological approach as outlined in Study 1. Table 1 presents a demographic comparison of the participants from both datasets. Further elaboration on the SAGAT scale will be provided in a forthcoming scholarly article, following the completion of a traditional scale development process, which was partially informed by our experimental prototyping method. Comparisons between real and simulated data were executed through the Mann-Whitney U test, and for the Impostor Syndrome factor, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test revealed no significant difference between real and simulated scores. Meanwhile, significant differences were observed in the Shame and Guilt dimensions. Moreover, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests confirmed substantial distributional differences in all three constructs, indicating these results are inconsistent with H4. To further bolster the validity and reliability of our findings, a replication study was conducted to develop another scale. In this study, we have designed a scale to quantify AI-related anxious beliefs, namely the "AI Anxiety" scale, constructed using the same approach as detailed in Study 3. For those seeking an in-depth exploration of this scale's development utilizing simulated data, the Supplementary Materials accompanying this article, along with the preregistration on the Open Science Framework (OSF), provide additional insights.

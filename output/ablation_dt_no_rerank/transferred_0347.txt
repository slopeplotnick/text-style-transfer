To facilitate the learning of valuable reasoning steps and optimize planning, which necessitates extensive reasoning over the Story-Information and crafting of comprehensive answers, Gurung and Lapata (2025) introduced VR-CLI (Verifiable Rewards via Completion Likelihood Improvement), a proxy reward system. The VR-CLI objective endeavors to enhance the likelihood of generating the accurate subsequent chapter, conditioned on both the Story-Information and the plan. We have extended this VR-CLI reward by incorporating a contrastive element that minimizes the probability of selecting a randomly chosen chapter from the same book. In our standard LiteReason method, we periodically conduct a minor SFT training step, once per epoch, using the pathways created during that period. Although this approach adds some computational overhead, it refreshes the reasoning projector with the current model's reasoning state. Furthermore, we employ an implicit thought prompt, as seen in Tables 12 and 13, to encourage the model's use of implicit thought tags. Conversely, the LiteReason variant without SFT retains the implicit prompt alongside the initialized Reasoning Projector, which utilizes implicit thought tags to identify appropriate times for transmitting new reasoning embeddings to the model. Nonetheless, this version does not engage in SFT during RL training to update the Reasoning Projector. Moreover, the LiteReason version devoid of the Reasoning Projector assesses performance following RL training without the Reasoning Projector, yet integrates the implicit thought prompt. This approach eschews latent reasoning, examining whether the model's performance remains consistent when reasoning steps are omitted through prompting. Drawing upon the recent latent reasoning literature, we select a representative subset of previous methodologies to evaluate the design assumptions outlined in Section 3. This evaluation is conducted on both the Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks. Regarding training-free latent reasoning, we compare against two recent methodologies: Mixture of Inputs (MoI) proposed by Su et al. (2025) blends the sampled token's embedding with those from the remaining token distribution. Similarly, the Soft Thinking paradigm developed by Zhang et al. (2025) also combines token embeddings but employs an entropy metric to determine when continuous thinking should cease and discrete sampling should commence.

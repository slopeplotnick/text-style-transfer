In this section, we introduce MIGR, a method for identifying the primary modality to focus on using Mutual Information (MI). By implementing this technique, we aim to reduce biases in the initial reasoning process and ensure that early reasoning is anchored in the modality with the strongest emotional signals. Our approach consists of two fundamental stages. First, we introduce modality-aligned selective fine-tuning (SFT), which helps the model establish an initial direction for reasoning by selecting the modality deemed emotion-dominant according to the Modality Index (MI). Then, we discuss Modality-Aware Reward Optimization, which is designed to reinforce modality prioritization and grounding reasoning in emotions, while simultaneously enhancing optimization stability. Therefore, we propose two distinct rewards: the modality-aligned order reward and the modality-grounded reasoning reward. The following sections provide detailed elaboration on these stages. Recent studies have demonstrated the efficacy of SFT as an initial approach in emotion comprehension tasks, showing that even minimal high-quality supervision from datasets like the Explainable Multimodal Emotion Reasoning (EMER) can lead to significant performance enhancements. Inspired by these findings, we similarly commence our training with an equivalent methodology. Despite EMER offering high-quality training data, the SFT stage can be further refined by incorporating additional samples that exhibit clear and consistent emotional signals. Thus, to achieve this improvement, we enhance the training dataset by including supplementary examples identified through the extraction of Facial Action Units (FAUs) from the video frames, which are strongly correlated with emotional expressions. Subsequently, we verify the congruence of FAU patterns with the intended emotion using a predetermined FAU-emotion mapping table. Only samples that unambiguously correspond to the target emotion are retained. For these filtered samples, we construct reasoning-answer pairs, utilizing emotionally consistent evidence derived from FAU patterns to inform the reasoning content.

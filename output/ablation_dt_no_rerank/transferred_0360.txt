Generator Refinement and Verification: A distinct line of research focuses on enhancing the models' content generation capabilities. Techniques such as Mirror-Consistency, Self-Contrast, and Step-Back Prompting utilize iterative verification or multiple perspectives to refine outcomes, while the Self-Check method employs a step-by-step verification process to identify and rectify errors effectively. Although these approaches prioritize improving the generator's inherent capabilities, they often incur increased computational time and resources. In contrast, our investigation shifts focus to the synthesis of resultant outputs; rather than attempting to rectify suboptimal outputs from the generator, we accept these in their existing form and employ a specialized integration layer to ascertain the most precise answer robustly. A critical consideration when deploying language models as evaluative judges for pairwise comparisons is whether to allow them to declare a tie or to force a decision between equivalent options. Initially, we illustrate that imposing a binary choice on the model can introduce bias. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the allowance to declare a "tie" significantly affects outcomes, which underscores the need for improved methodologies to manage such judgments. Incorporating ties as an option not only serves to mitigate biases but also enhances fair evaluation by reducing systematic biases like positional bias, wherein the sequence of alternatives influences the model's selections. For instance, modifying an experimental procedure to include the possibility of a tie declaration effectively diminished positional bias for both models examined. However, tie determinations are not inherently consistent. In three-option selection tasks, variability in the model's voting behavior arises depending on the evaluation process configuration, necessitating a more robust aggregation method to consolidate the outcomes effectively.

KL-Free GRPO Algorithm. Figure 11 illustrates our fundamental RL Framework. To optimize the objective function detailed in Equation 13, which aims to maximize the advantage-weighted log probability expectation, we employ the GRPO algorithm. The importance sampling ratio and advantage function are defined in Equation 14. In our implementation, however, we chose to omit the KL divergence regularization term. This decision stems from the understanding that after a sufficient SFT warm-start, the model has already solidified its reasoning paradigm and structured output format. At this stage, imposing KL constraints would overly restrict the policy exploration space, impeding the model's discovery of improved reasoning paths. Thus, we opted for freer policy optimization based on its existing capabilities. Through the reward mechanism design, we achieved three training objectives: First, format standardization is achieved by using special tokens to explicitly separate the reasoning process from the final answer, thereby facilitating subsequent parsing and application. Second, output space constraint is implemented by strictly limiting mismatch types, such as "price mismatch" and "functionality mismatch," to within a predefined set, preventing the generation of uncontrollable arbitrary outputs. Third, a progressive reward system is in place; when the model identifies relevance yet errs at finer levels, it still receives moderate positive rewards, which helps alleviate the sparse reward problem in early training stages and accelerates convergence, thereby stabilizing training dynamics. We subsequently designed a three-stage curriculum learning scheme to guide model capability improvement by progressively increasing training data difficulty. The sample allocation strategy for each stage is detailed in Table 6. The core philosophy behind this strategy encompasses three dimensions: Firstly, the Rapid Bootstrap Principle, where Stage 1 focuses primarily on medium-difficulty samples, enabling the model to quickly obtain effective positive feedback while maintaining a certain level of challenge, avoiding exploration difficulties caused by sparse rewards in early training. Secondly, the Progressive Challenge Principle, where Stages 2 and 3 gradually increase the proportion of difficult samples, guiding the model to transition from "solving common problems" to "tackling edge cases," thus continually expanding capability boundaries. Lastly, the Knowledge Retention Principle ensures that the latter two stages continuously incorporate easy samples as "memory anchors" to prevent the model from forgetting previously acquired skills while learning new knowledge; these high-confidence samples also help stabilize gradient variance during the training process.

To confront the challenges associated with static outputs, it is crucial to prioritize real-time functionality. The proposed strategy employs a fundamental set of inquiries to compel language models to produce instantaneous responses. Consequently, this approach guarantees that fact-checking mechanisms secure the necessary textual outputs, while hallucination detection systems proficiently oversee the model's internal processes during generation. Evaluation, therefore, remains relevant and effective despite the dynamic nature of language models. UniFact is underpinned by the need to overcome the limitations of outdated benchmarks, grounded in three integral principles that ensure evaluations are robust, equitable, and future-proof. Firstly, by abandoning reliance on antiquated datasets, it utilizes factual inquiries to facilitate real-time response generation, ensuring applicability to both current and future models. Moreover, it provides equitable comparisons by accommodating both hallucination detection and fact verification methodologies. Here, hallucination detection approaches gain access to the model's internal processes during response generation, while fact verification methods are supplied with the generated text alongside supplementary information for corroboration. Both methodologies assess the factual accuracy of the model's outputs, focusing on the identical task. Additionally, an automated system labels outputs, circumventing the slow and subjective nature of human reviews. This system swiftly ascertains the accuracy of the model's responses by comparing them to correct answers and other reliable information sources, thus enabling rapid, fair, and consistent assessment across numerous models.

Studies such as DeepSeek-R1 have observed the emergent phenomenon of extended reasoning sequences and the model's capability to exhibit instances of emergent comprehension. Furthermore, we conducted a detailed analysis of the evolving length of the model's responses during the training process, with results presented in Fig. 17. As model proficiency increased, the response length decreased and ultimately stabilized, suggesting that extensive reasoning steps are not essential for model enhancement but instead occur concurrently with training. Moreover, our findings reveal significant variability in reasoning chain length requirements depending on the task type. Mathematical computation and code generation tasks demand intricate step-by-step reasoning, whereas tasks such as discerning user intent and correlating it to relevant items are efficiently accomplished through an intuitive and concise reasoning process, where additional reasoning does not enhance outcomes. For tasks requiring visual comprehension, leveraging models like Vision-and-Language Models (VLMs) is more efficient, as they inherently possess image processing capabilities without needing additional procedures like caption generation. To investigate this approach, we employed the Qwen2.5-VL-72B teacher model to execute a two-step process: first generating knowledge and reasoning chains of thought, followed by applying rule-based chains of thought.

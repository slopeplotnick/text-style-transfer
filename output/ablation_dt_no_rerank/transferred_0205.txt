To systematically address this question, we first deconstructed the task into two distinct phases, as detailed in Section 2. The process is divided into: (1) Path Construction, where query specifications are mapped to precise product attributes, thereby establishing semantic pathways between queries and products; and (2) Path Following, where concrete discrimination at the attribute-product level is executed based on these pathways. To effectively navigate this process, the model must possess several key capabilities. First, its Knowledge and Reasoning Ability is crucial. The model leverages knowledge to parse domain-specific terminology and discern latent information within the query, building on this foundation through reasoning to infer the authentic intent of the query and the requisite attributes of the products. For instance, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a), it must first determine that "morning C, evening A" refers to the use of Vitamin C and retinol, respectively, and then deduce through reasoning that "for mom" implies suitability for middle-aged women. Moreover, Multi-modal Matching Ability is essential, as item attributes are presented across diverse modalities. The model must integrate information across textual and visual data to effectively correlate the query with the relevant product attributes. As depicted in Fig. 1(b), when the query specifies "yellow," which is absent from the textual description, the model must consult the visual representation to ascertain the color attribute. Furthermore, Rule-Aware Ability is indispensable, requiring adherence to established decision-making protocols to derive conclusions accurately. As demonstrated in Fig. 1(c), when a query seeks "LV bags" but the product pertains to a second-hand bag, this scenario cannot be resolved through feature alignment alone, necessitating adherence to specific rule definitions for compatibility assessment.

We propose a unified framework to optimize system prompts, enhancing the performance of multilingual language models. Our experiments indicate that specific prompt components enhance model performance across various languages and that multilingual reward models can effectively identify these optimal prompts. Moreover, the analysis of reasoning patterns reveals that improved prompts foster more structured and consistent behavior, highlighting prompt optimization as a viable approach to enhancing multilingual LLM robustness. However, identifying highly effective prompts remains a pertinent challenge. While SPRIG has helped identify several high-quality prompts, consistently locating prompts excelling across all four evaluation criteria remains difficult. Although our findings suggest that effective prompts can guide models toward specific reasoning patterns, thus improving performance, the intrinsic nature of these patterns and the rationale for their effectiveness are not yet fully understood. Elucidating the fundamental mechanisms underlying these observations remains an open question for further research. Despite efforts to address ethical challenges, several issues persist. First, prompt optimization and subsequent experiments require considerable computational resources, leading to high energy consumption and a significant carbon footprint. Second, prompt optimization may unintentionally amplify existing biases within the underlying corpus, as seen when a prompt leads a language model to emulate specific personas, such as that of a professor. Therefore, it is imperative to ensure these elements do not introduce or exacerbate harmful biases, given our data's inclusion of roles and instructions.

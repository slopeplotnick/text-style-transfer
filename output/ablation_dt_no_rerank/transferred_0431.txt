Machine learning and generative techniques are popular and widespread; however, the application of AI in decision-making is less common. Decision-making requires the irrevocable allocation of resources, which typically falls outside the realm of academic research. To date, the most substantial and noteworthy application of decision-making AI in mineral exploration is the characterization of the high-grade section of the Mingomba deposit in Zambia. This involved reevaluating historical drilling data using advanced analytical tools, leading to the development of novel hypotheses that suggest a higher-grade mineralization than previously anticipated. AI, formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), systematically planned the drilling operations. The primary aim was for AI to mitigate uncertainties regarding geological hypotheses about the spatial distribution of high-grade mineral deposits. This method proved more efficient than traditional grid-based drilling because it emphasizes falsifying geological hypotheses rather than merely attempting to locate mineralization. In Part III, I will examine how artificial intelligence can expedite the discovery and characterization of critical mineral deposits, initially focusing on data analysis and prediction before advancing to AI-based decision-making. The initial deployment of AI was met with some defensiveness in certain sectors, as concerns arose that AI might overshadow or diminish the significance of domain experts. Today, the fear of job displacement is prevalent. While it is perceived that AI possesses the capability to independently generate novel discoveries, the idea that AI will autonomously identify precise locations for robotic exploration and subsequent discoveries is unfounded. Rather, AI is seen as a tool to enhance the capabilities of domain experts, relieving them of tasks for which their proficiency is limited or unnecessary, thus allowing them to concentrate on the innovative aspects of process-driven thought in geology and geochemistry.

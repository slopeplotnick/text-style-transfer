In our study, we emphasize the utility of language models to refine and propose modifications to ambiguously constructed questions, which can alter respondents' answers due to errors and misinterpretations. Moreover, these models facilitate the categorization of questions. By leveraging the semantic understanding they provide, researchers can mitigate errors and enhance the precision of their inquiries, allowing for a clearer insight into the interrelations among distinct categories. If the categories exhibit excessive similarity, substantial modifications may be necessary. Additionally, language models help maintain uniformity in reading levels, thus facilitating comprehension. For a detailed exploration of implementing language models in the development of these assessments, one can refer to the recent publication by Beghetto et al. Furthermore, we are exploring the use of language models to generate synthetic data, which may streamline the conduct of evaluations. This approach has the potential to significantly reduce the time and financial resources traditionally required for testing by initially experimenting on simulated groups, consequently minimizing superfluous tests and costs for researchers. In our evaluation, we assessed the efficacy of contemporary language models in generating data consistent with the structural frameworks employed in test development. We then delineated the methodology we employed in our investigation and articulated the rationale underpinning our approach, predominantly adhering to the methodologies outlined in Classic Test Theory and extensively following the guidance of Boateng and colleagues.

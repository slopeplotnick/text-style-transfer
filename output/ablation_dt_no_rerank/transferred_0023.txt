Exemplar Selection: Some prompt optimization algorithms, such as OPRO and EvoPrompt, do not incorporate few-shot examples. However, they can significantly enhance the performance of large language models, even when employing straightforward exemplar selection methods. promptsolution introduces a supplementary module for post-hoc exemplar selection, implementing methodologies such as random selection and random search to incorporate few-shot examples within a fixed instruction set. Initial Prompt Creation: Many optimizers require an initial pool of prompts to begin with. Therefore, our framework facilitates the automatic generation of prompts derived from a task description, a foundational prompt, or dataset exemplars. **Callbacks:** The optimizers incorporate callback functionality to systematically monitor the optimization progress. These callbacks are crucial in evaluating the state of the optimizer at each step and can suspend the optimization process if necessary. Noteworthy implementations include the TokenCountCallback, which tracks token utilization and terminates the optimization when a predefined limit is surpassed, and the FileOutputCallback, which records prompts and their evaluative scores, enabling straightforward post-hoc analysis. Setup: In order to evaluate the efficacy of promptolution and contextualize its performance relative to other prompt optimization methodologies, we conducted prompt optimization experiments on the GSM8K dataset (grade school mathematical word problems) and the SST-5 dataset (sentiment classification tasks). For these evaluations, the gemma-3-27B model, which has undergone instruction tuning, was employed as the primary language model, and, where necessary, also served as the meta-model. Comprehensive information regarding the datasets and preparatory methodologies is delineated in Appendix A.2. For the purpose of comparative analysis, we assessed the optimizers CAPO, EvoPromptGA, and OPRO from the promptolution library, alongside the leading optimizers from AdalFlow and DSPy frameworks. To ascertain the contributory role of prompt optimization, we evaluated three manually crafted prompts for each dataset and reported their average performance. The parameters for each optimizer were maintained consistently to ensure practical comparability, with a combined token budget for input and output restricted to one million, thereby keeping the cost below $0.15 for this model. All frameworks were initiated using solely a task description without any predefined prompts to test the full automation workflow. Both unoptimized prompts and the best prompts identified by each optimizer (based on validation performance) were evaluated on a separate test set.

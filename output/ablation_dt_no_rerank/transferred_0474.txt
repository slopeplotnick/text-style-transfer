Advancements in computing are being driven by the integration of novel tools and systems, which enhance autonomous functionalities. These systems enable computers to independently interpret and execute tasks more efficiently. Moreover, open-source architectures simplify the assemblage of diverse components like planners and memory modules, thereby fostering more sophisticated computational frameworks. Empirical data from corporate entities illustrate the practical applications of these innovations. However, certain scholarly investigations suggest that merely increasing the size of the models does not inherently enhance their reliability. Therefore, there is a concerted effort to evaluate these systems’ operational mechanisms to ensure substantial improvements in performance. Yet, assessing these systems poses significant challenges due to the increasing complexity of their tasks. Several studies explore using specialized systems to facilitate evaluations; nonetheless, concerns regarding potential biases remain. To address these issues, recommendations have been put forth, such as employing multiple evaluators and ensuring equitable assessments across diverse tasks. Our methodology incorporates these principles by implementing a dual-judge framework, wherein distinct evaluators assess separate aspects of the task to verify that improvements are genuine. The compendium includes 100 real-world editing tasks associated with slide presentations, each encompassing an initial slide deck, a target deck supplemented with human-generated references, structured textual instructions, and a rubric delineating layout and content criteria. These tasks are systematically categorized into sixteen distinct groups to ensure a comprehensive evaluation of semantic comprehension and meticulous formatting precision. Data were aggregated from a repository of over 15,000 PowerPoint presentations sourced from various origins and subsequently transformed into a structured format that considers both layout and content intricacies. Presentations exhibiting a diverse array of stylistic elements were retained and integrated with expert contributions from multiple specialists. From a pool exceeding 500 evaluated options, including contributions by students and others, we selected 100 exemplars that span diverse professional and academic disciplines, ensuring each category includes demanding instances.

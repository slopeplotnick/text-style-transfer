We utilize both audio and visual inputs to identify the modality that predominantly conveys emotions in each sample. After developing a high-quality human-annotated dataset alongside an FAU-filtered additional dataset, we merge these to form a unified training set. Thereafter, we extract the Modality Importance (MI) for each sample. Once the emotion-dominant modality is determined, we reorganize the reasoning text structure accordingly. Specifically, for each training instance, the reasoning is split into two distinct segments: an audio-based reasoning text and a video-based reasoning text, marked with the special tokens <aud_desc> and <vis_desc> respectively. We then arrange these texts based on the MI. If the audio modality is identified as having greater emotional dominance, the audio-based reasoning text is prioritized, followed by the video-based text. Conversely, if the video modality displays more pronounced emotional features, the video-based reasoning text is presented prior to the audio-based text. In cases where audio and video are comparably informative, both configurations are included: one commencing with audio and the other with video. By utilizing the reasoning text reorganized according to the dominant modality during SFT, the model is trained to initiate its reasoning from the modality most effectively conveying emotional signals. Following this initial phase, we refine the model's reasoning abilities through Modality-Aligned Reward Optimization. Prior methodologies typically include dual reward types focusing on answer correctness and structural coherence. However, these approaches lack emphasis on prioritizing input modalities that convey emotional content. Therefore, to address this gap, we introduce two novel rewards: a modality-aligned order reward and a modality-grounded reasoning reward.

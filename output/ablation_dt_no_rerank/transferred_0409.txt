The central inquiry is whether contemporary language models (LLMs), which are primarily trained for next-token prediction without explicit perceptual instruction, inherently develop comparable strategies for information processing. Elucidating how these models manage uncertain information could enhance the development of advanced systems that address diverse input quality. To explore this, we employed methodologies derived from psychophysics, which investigates human perceptual processes, to analyze their operational dynamics. Instead of inspecting internal mechanisms, we scrutinized the external behaviors of these opaque systems to discern their information-processing capabilities. By manipulating the uncertainty inherent in the input data and evaluating the presence of Bayesian processing characterized by probabilistic decision-making, we aimed to ascertain the extent to which these models emulate human-like perceptual processing. Our investigation revealed that certain established tasks within psychophysics are well-suited for these models, eliciting notably intriguing patterns. These tasks assess the capacity of models to process diverse forms of input, particularly in controlled environments where precise measurements can be obtained. In contrast, naturalistic tasks often have unquantifiable noise; thus, our setup allows evaluation of explicit integration strategies, uncovering vulnerabilities that conventional benchmarks may miss. Our research yields three principal contributions: firstly, we developed a psychophysics framework tailored for large language models, encompassing four synthetic tasks aimed at estimating magnitudes such as length, location, distance, and duration. This framework enables precise manipulation of variables like noise and context, examining behavioral changes in the models. Subsequently, we introduced a novel benchmark, BayesBench, designed to evaluate the models' performance, efficiency in integrating information, and adherence to Bayesian reasoning principles using an innovative scoring methodology. Moreover, our findings indicate that certain advanced models exhibit behaviors congruent with Bayesian reasoning. However, a significant limitation was identified wherein even high-performing models occasionally fail to employ reliable strategies in specific circumstances. Finally, psychophysics, the quantitative study of perception, has elucidated systematic relationships between physical stimuli and perceptual judgments, encapsulated in classical laws such as Weber-Fechner’s logarithmic scaling.

Large language models (LLMs) excel in explicit reasoning, yet their implicit computational strategies remain underexplored. Decades of psychophysics research illustrate that humans intuitively process and integrate noisy signals using near-optimal Bayesian strategies in perceptual tasks. Consequently, we investigate whether LLMs exhibit similar behavior and perform optimal multimodal integration without explicit training or instruction. By adopting the psychophysics paradigm, we discern computational principles of LLMs through systematic behavioral studies. We introduce a behavioral benchmark—BayesBench: four magnitude estimation tasks (length, location, distance, and duration) over text and image, inspired by classic psychophysics, and evaluate a diverse set of nine LLMs alongside human judgments for calibration. Through controlled ablations of noise, context, and instructional prompts, we measure performance, behavior, and efficiency in multimodal cue-combination. Moreover, beyond accuracy and efficiency metrics, we present a Bayesian Consistency Score that detects Bayes-consistent behavioral shifts even when accuracy saturates. Our results indicate that while some models adapt in Bayes-consistent ways, accuracy does not necessarily guarantee robustness. Notably, GPT-5 Mini achieves perfect text accuracy but fails to integrate visual cues efficiently, thus revealing a crucial dissociation between capability and strategy. This suggests that accuracy-centric benchmarks might overemphasize performance while missing brittle uncertainty handling. These findings expose emergent principled handling of uncertainty and highlight the correlation between accuracy and Bayesian tendencies. We release our psychophysics benchmark and consistency metric as evaluation tools to inform future multimodal architecture designs. The estimation of magnitudes, encompassing quantities like length, duration, or distance, represents one of the most fundamental computations in biological and artificial intelligence. Humans perform these judgments through the Bayesian integration of noisy sensory signals, automatically weighting cues by their reliability and incorporating prior expectations to minimize estimation error. This computational strategy emerges without explicit instruction across diverse cultures and developmental stages, suggesting it reflects a fundamental solution to information processing under uncertainty.

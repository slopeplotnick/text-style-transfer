Our comprehensive evaluation demonstrates that spatial methods, such as covariance tangent-space projection and Common Spatial Patterns, continue to be the gold standard for decoding EEG motor imagery under controlled conditions. Nevertheless, their performance markedly diminishes with increased inter- and intra-subject variability. Through meticulous subject-level analysis, we have determined that no universal configuration is optimally effective for every individual. Instead, it is more advantageous to employ adaptive methodologies that align with the unique neurophysiological signatures of individuals. Moreover, certain nonlinear features, including Hjorth parameters, Higuchi fractal dimension, and singular value decomposition entropy, have demonstrated considerable efficacy for specific individuals. This observation suggests that capturing richer neural dynamics can enhance personalized decoding, indicating that "BCI illiteracy" may more accurately be attributed to mismatches between decoding algorithms and user-specific neurophysiological characteristics rather than inherent limitations of the users themselves. Therefore, future research should prioritize the exploration of advanced ensemble learning frameworks that integrate linear spatial filters with nonlinear dynamics, effectively harnessing their complementary strengths. Extending this benchmark to encompass broader paradigms such as steady-state visual evoked potentials (SSVEP), P300, coded visual evoked potentials (CVEP), and invasive intracortical recordings will further test the generalizability of our conclusions. Furthermore, rigorous evaluation of cutting-edge deep-learning architectures, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs), long short-term memory networks (LSTMs), and transformers, alongside emerging quantum-inspired models, such as quantum support-vector machines and hybrid quantum neural networks, represents an essential frontier in this domain. These quantum methodologies may offer enhanced capability in managing the intrinsic noise and complexity characteristic of EEG signals. Another critical avenue for advancement lies in augmenting cross-subject applicability of models through transfer learning and domain adaptation techniques, which have shown considerable promise in enhancing model robustness and scalability across heterogeneous user populations. Equally important is the imperative to bolster reproducibility through open-access benchmarking initiatives such as MOABB, facilitating standardized and transparent comparison of EEG decoding algorithms.

In a systematic empirical study aimed at bridging the gap between Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification, we first introduce our study design and experimental setup. Subsequently, we present a thorough examination of three research questions, exploring their comparative performance, complementary strengths, and potential for integration. To effectively address the historical divide between these paradigms, we undertake an extensive empirical study that systematically compares them within a unified framework. Our investigation is logically structured to provide clear and direct answers to the following research questions (RQs): - RQ1: A Head-to-Head Performance Comparison. How do state-of-the-art (SOTA) methodologies for Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification perform when evaluated on identical AI-generated outputs? Are there consistent winners? - RQ2: Uncovering Complementary Strengths. Do these paradigms possess unique strengths in identifying factual errors, or do their capabilities largely overlap? - RQ3: Performance of Hybrid Methods. Can integrating signals from both paradigms create a more robust and accurate system, thereby outperforming any singular approach? To answer these questions, we organize our study into three logical and progressive stages. First, addressing RQ1, we employ a selection of prominent baselines from both paradigms, benchmarking their performance on a shared testbed. This represents the first instance of a direct comparative analysis. Second, informed by these initial results, we quantitatively analyze the synergy between the two paradigms to address RQ2, demonstrating that integrating Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification yields superior results compared to combining methods within a single paradigm. Moreover, to add qualitative depth, we conduct a detailed case study in Section 4.5. Finally, motivated by the strong evidence of their synergy, we address RQ3 by designing and evaluating two simple yet potent hybrid methods. We explore fusion at both the final probability score level and the system pipeline level, showing that even straightforward integration can significantly enhance overall performance.

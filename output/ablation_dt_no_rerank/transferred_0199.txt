The recordings were evaluated for technical clarity and compliance with the given instructions. Once approved, they were transcribed by trained annotators using the same platform. Only those recordings and transcripts that successfully passed the final review were incorporated into the dataset. Remuneration was stratified according to linguistic requirements to ensure alignment with engagement needs. This workflow was instrumental in guaranteeing that the resultant dataset exemplified high technical quality, embraced diverse speaker representation, and adhered to rigorous ethical standards. Moreover, the unscripted prompts were developed by dedicated language teams to ensure both cultural relevance and naturalness. Each language team, typically comprising a leader and three assistants, engaged in peer review of each other's work prior to the leader's approval of the prompts for the project. Utilizing Microsoft Forms, the teams selected a domain of knowledge, followed by a topic and pertinent subtopics. Additionally, language teams were instructed to develop prompts aimed at eliciting a diverse array of responses. For instance, instead of merely asking, "What is your favorite fruit?" they were encouraged to ask, "What is your favorite fruit and why?" This instruction emphasized crafting prompts that elicit responses beyond simple statements, including questions, exclamations, commands, and other forms of expression. Furthermore, teams were encouraged to modify topics and subtopics to enhance cultural relevance and, when necessary, cultural sensitivity. The objective was to encapsulate the national lexicon of the South African vernacular to the fullest extent possible. Therefore, the topics and subdomains were meticulously aligned with South African societal contexts and refined to reflect the specific intricacies inherent to the respective language.

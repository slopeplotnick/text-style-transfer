Our findings are consistent with prior research (Sengupta et al., 2025; Subramanian et al., 2025), revealing that performance degradation as model size is reduced is not always linear. In alignment with the work of MentalQLM (0.5B) (Shi et al., 2025), these results demonstrate strong capabilities in predicting various mental health outcomes, such as depression, stress, and suicidal ideation. Therefore, these observations underscore the viability of fine-tuned smaller models for mental health prediction, offering a favorable trade-off between computational efficiency and predictive precision. Moreover, recent studies (Ren et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024a) confirm that their core reasoning and question-answering capabilities are preserved post-fine-tuning, enhancing the functionality of these models across diverse tasks, particularly in mental health predictions. To summarize, our experiments indicate that smaller models in the 3-7B parameter range achieve an optimal balance between computational efficiency and predictive accuracy with fine-tuning. In contrast to their smaller counterparts, these models proficiently classify psychological signals in both zero-shot and few-shot contexts. Through targeted fine-tuning, our model, Menta, establishes a clear lower bound for size while achieving robust performance across complex mental health tasks. Balancing model size, accuracy, safety, and real-world impact is vital for mental health AI systems. In comparative analyses, fine-tuned smaller models demonstrate comparable or superior efficacy in classifying depression and stress levels, albeit with diminished performance in predicting suicidal ideation. Nevertheless, when compared to smaller models lacking additional training, fine-tuned versions exhibit substantial improvements and maintain balanced accuracy across diverse tasks. These findings reveal that well-optimized smaller models can retain the necessary reasoning and contextual understanding for mental health predictions. The effectiveness of these models in the mental health domain stems from their ability to capture localized lexical, affective, and syntactic cues, including self-referential expressions, emotional polarity, and temporal framing. Such capabilities are advantageous, as they allow the models to operate effectively without requiring the extensive world knowledge encoded in larger architectures. Consistent with prior investigations (Ji et al., 2021; Shen et al., 2018; Benton et al., 2017), fine-tuning on domain-specific corpora significantly narrows the performance gap between smaller and larger models. Our study further corroborates this by showing that parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques, like LoRA, coupled with adaptive, weighted loss functions, enable smaller models to acquire discriminative representations essential for mental health predictions, all at a substantially lower computational cost.

Predicting stock prices remains a fundamental and enduring challenge in the field of finance. The inherent volatility of stock price sequences, combined with their rapid response to new information, makes traditional prediction methods inadequate for capturing unstructured textual signals like news and shifts in market sentiment. As a result, these conventional approaches often fail to identify the key factors driving price fluctuations. Recent advances in online textual data availability, coupled with the significant improvements in the natural language understanding capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), have considerably accelerated research into text-based stock price forecasting. However, most existing studies that utilize these advanced models are still confined to shallow text analysis and immediate inference. They fail to integrate systematic knowledge and lack a sophisticated long-term memory architecture, which severely restricts their ability to navigate the highly dynamic and complex financial markets. This highlights the critical need for models capable of retaining and using crucial information over extended periods. The complexity of financial markets is further exacerbated by the multifaceted nature of price fluctuations, which typically arise from a series of interrelated events rather than isolated incidents. Moreover, forecasting stock prices based on news presents a fundamentally distinct challenge compared to traditional question-answering tasks. The prediction of future stock price movements cannot be inferred directly from news articles. Therefore, models must actively construct a comprehensive market context from diverse information streams, identify analogous historical episodes, and engage in deep analytical reasoning to progress from simple "text matching" to substantive "market pattern discovery." Furthermore, financial texts inherently contain substantial redundancy and irrelevant content that can obscure signals of genuine predictive value, making effective content filtering and precise matching crucial.

Score Distillation Sampling offers a novel technique where pretrained diffusion models are utilized not only for generating samples through denoising but also as optimization objectives—a process known as score distillation sampling (SDS). SDS has successfully extended the application of diffusion models to a wide range of tasks, including image and video generation, thus enhancing downstream generative modeling efforts. Inspired by its efficacy in image synthesis, recent studies have further integrated diffusion-based priors into reinforcement learning to tackle control tasks. In particular, some methods replace the conventional discriminator in a Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL)-style setup with a diffusion model, thus capitalizing on its ability to differentiate between real and synthetic actions. Nevertheless, these approaches still depend on adversarial training and often require continuous updates to the diffusion model during policy optimization. Beyond adversarial methods, alternative systems have explored the direct application of SDS-like techniques to policy learning. For instance, certain methodologies utilize pretrained diffusion models conditioned on text prompts for policy training, producing behaviors that comply with textual directives, although occasionally resulting in unnatural motions. A closely related effort is SMILING, which trains controllers using a method akin to variational score distillation. Unlike SMILING, which necessitates task-specific diffusion models for each application, our approach involves training a task-agnostic motion prior from unstructured motion data. This flexible prior can subsequently be integrated with discrete task objectives to develop diverse control policies within a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning framework. Moreover, we introduce several critical design strategies that facilitate the generation of high-quality, naturalistic movements across a broad array of tasks.

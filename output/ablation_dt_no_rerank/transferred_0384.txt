We propose SR-GRPO, which uses stable rank as a dense reward signal in reinforcement learning, thereby eliminating the dependency on preference datasets or external validation mechanisms. Our demonstrations reveal that SR-GRPO substantially improves reasoning across multiple benchmarks, thus establishing intrinsic geometric signals as a viable basis for aligning language models. Moreover, beyond its application in reinforcement learning, stable rank emerges as a credible zero-shot reward proxy for evaluating language model responses and integrates seamlessly into best-of-N decoding strategies. We propose stable rank (Rudelson & Vershynin, 2007) as an unsupervised quality signal derived from language model representations, with this section detailing the metric, explaining its geometric utility, and validating its efficacy as a reward proxy. The theoretical motivation behind stable rank stems from two distinct paradigms: the softmax bottleneck, highlighted by Yang et al. (2018) and Godey et al. (2024), suggests that successful language modeling necessitates high-rank representations; conversely, representation collapse, as identified by Gao et al. (2019), is indicative of poor generation quality. Consequently, stable rank directly measures this effective dimensionality, making it a fitting metric for quality assessment. We corroborate this connection through empirical validation and correlation analysis in Section 5. Evaluating stable rank as a zero-shot proxy for human preferences is achieved by testing its ability to predict the preferred response between paired options. This assessment determines whether stable rank can function as a reliable reward signal absent model-specific training. The procedural setup involves the use of RewardBench (Lambert et al., 2025), a benchmark comprising 2,985 preference pairs across five categories: Chat, Chat-Hard, Safety, Code, and Math. For each pair, stable rank is calculated for both responses, predicting the response with the higher stable rank as preferred. We evaluate our approach against three baseline methods: (1) Pointwise Scoring (Point.) (Kim et al., 2024), which involves prompting the language model to score each response on a 1-5 scale; (2) Pairwise Comparison (Pair.) (Kim et al., 2024), which entails prompting the language model to directly compare two responses; and (3) IPO (Garg et al., 2025), which utilizes implicit preference scores derived from Yes/No token probabilities. Our evaluation includes five models: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Yang et al., 2024), Qwen3-0.6B (Yang et al., 2025), Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Dubey et al., 2024), and Phi-3.5-mini-Instruct (Abdin et al., 2024). To derive stable rank, inputs are formatted into prompt-response pairs using each model's native chat template, with outputs extracted from the final layer.

Figure 1 illustrates the CoT output of the fine-tuned model. In this output, the initial step involves extracting key attributes from the query and item descriptions, including product names, brands, model specifications, target audiences, and more. Next, the model assesses the relevance between the query and item by evaluating the compatibility of these attributes. Through CoT tuning, the LLM model not only accurately predicts relevance labels but also effectively explains the underlying reasons for its classifications. However, upon analyzing the outputs of the fine-tuned LLM, we observe that it demonstrates a bias towards stricter relevance discrimination. Specifically, the model often categorizes products as "Irrelevant" when certain attributes mentioned in the query are absent from the product description, even if those attributes are non-essential and do not influence purchasing decisions. This propensity towards over-strict classification results in an increase in false negatives, which in turn reduces the model's recall rate and negatively impacts the platform's transaction activity and revenue. To address this issue and ensure closer alignment of the model's predictions with real user behavior and feedback, we adjust the fine-tuned model based on user purchasing behavior. Concretely, we utilize the fine-tuned model to predict query-purchased item pairs from historical data and select those initially classified as "Irrelevant" to form a preference training set. We treat these overly stringent predictions as suboptimal, and employ GPT-40 to generate "Relevant" CoT reasoning results for the original query-purchased item pairs, thereby identifying optimal pairings. Subsequently, the KTO algorithm is employed to align the LLM with these preferences. Unlike other alignment algorithms, the KTO algorithm utilizes binary labeled data directly for model alignment, making data construction simpler and more efficient.

This consistent superiority corroborates the notion that internal uncertainty signals and external evidence retrieval function within orthogonal feature spaces that complement each other. Specifically, the Score-Level Fusion strategy illustrates that a straightforward linear combination effectively mitigates the substantial variance associated with singular approaches. By aggregating varied signals, this strategy attenuates the noise from individual prediction errors, thereby producing a more reliable evaluation that surpasses the effectiveness of standalone HD or FV methods in most instances. Moreover, the Evidence-Aware Pipeline frequently emerges as the superior method, consistently outperforming the alternatives evaluated. This can be attributed to its mechanism for addressing the excessive dependence on external evidence. In instances where external information is insufficient, such as NEI scenarios, forcing a decision may introduce inaccuracies. However, the proposed pipeline circumvents this by reverting to internal uncertainty signals, which maintain reliability in the absence of external evidence. Additionally, hybrid methodologies ameliorate the issue of unstable performance observed in RQ1. Unlike standalone HD techniques, which demonstrate fluctuations between models such as LLaMA and Qwen, hybrid methodologies uphold consistent performance across diverse model architectures. This consistency suggests that the integration of multiple signals effectively mitigates the internal confidence miscalibrations characteristic of standalone HD methods.

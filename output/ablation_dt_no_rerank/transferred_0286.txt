More specifically, preliminary research has been undertaken to ascertain the specific strengths and weaknesses of SWE agents. Analysis of agent performance indicates that state-of-the-art SWE agents struggle significantly with multifile issues. Recent scholarship emphasizes the need for richer evaluation frameworks that account for efficiency, robustness, and cognitive competency. Furthermore, subsequent investigations have delineated failure modes experienced by these agents, attributing them mainly to incorrect fault localization, difficulty in interpreting complex code dependencies, and challenges in maintaining contextual integrity across multifile scenarios. Notably, a consortium has developed TRAIL, comprising an error catalog and benchmark with 148 traces that have been meticulously validated by human evaluators. Meanwhile, another research initiative has proposed the Multi-Agent System Failure Taxonomy (MAST) for multi-agent systems, analyzing 150 traces from five distinct systems across five benchmarks, which necessitated human annotations for further taxonomy and analysis. Another study constructed a flow graph to represent agent actions, focusing on project understanding, context comprehension, and actions related to patching and testing. However, the analysis was performed manually and addressed only a limited portion of the dataset, specifically targeting bug localization in a mere 20 out of 500 instances. Additionally, this research developed a comprehensive taxonomy of LLM failure modes within SWE tasks, encompassing three primary phases, nine principal categories, and 25 fine-grained subcategories. This taxonomy has facilitated the identification of underlying reasons for tool ineffectiveness, illustrating issues such as agents encountering unproductive iterative loops. Nevertheless, the examination of their root causes was executed manually on 150 of the 500 identified issues.

Refusal rates shift unpredictably: some models increase refusal while others decrease at the same context lengths. The type of padding significantly impacts performance; coherent text padding, whether relevant or non-relevant, outperforms random padding, which in turn surpasses multi-task padding. Moreover, the position of padding matters—placing context after the task description can degrade model performance more than positioning it before. LLM agents are AI systems embedding pre-trained large language models within a decision-making framework to autonomously execute multi-step, multi-turn tasks using external tools (Chowa et al. 2025; Ferrag, Tihanyi, and Debbah 2025; Wang et al. 2025). Context windows have expanded considerably from 2,000 tokens in GPT-3 to 2 million in Grok 4 Fast, enabled by enhanced position embeddings and attention mechanisms (Su et al. 2021; Dao et al. 2022). However, these models often show a recency bias and are less effective at recalling distant tokens (Wang et al. 2024b; Shan et al. 2025). Benchmarks like NIAH and LongBench (Bai et al. 2024; Kuratov et al. 2024) demonstrate that while expanding context windows increases capacity, it does not necessarily enhance the capability of models (Liu et al. 2025). LLM safety benchmarking covers evaluations of toxicity, misinformation, and system vulnerabilities, with LongSafety (Lu et al. 2025) specifically assessing long-context inputs of up to 5,000 tokens. In agent safety benchmarking, AgentSecurity and Agent Dojo rigorously assess robustness against prompt injection attacks in interactive tasks (Zhang et al. 2025; Debenedetti et al. 2024), whereas RedCode and MobileSafetyBench evaluate the risks associated with code execution (Guo et al. 2024; Lee et al. 2024). Most notably, AgentHarm (Andriushchenko et al. 2025) assesses refusal behavior on harmful tasks and the ability to execute multi-step agentic workflows. We have extended the AgentHarm benchmark to evaluate agentic capabilities and behaviors under long-context inputs for both benign and harmful tasks. Additionally, AgentHarm tests capabilities for multi-step tool usage. The benchmark includes manually implemented instruments designed to emulate real-world applications, such as Google Search or a social media posting API, allowing testers full control over task execution.

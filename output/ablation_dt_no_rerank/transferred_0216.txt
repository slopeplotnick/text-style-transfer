Based on the decomposition of item comprehension, query comprehension, and path mapping, we can synthesize the essential requirements for a robust relevance model. To effectively navigate the vast item corpus and satisfy complex user intents—ranging from direct requests to abstract reasoning—the model must possess a sophisticated, composite set of capabilities. We summarize these core requirements as follows: Reasoning and Knowledge Integration encompasses the ability to leverage external knowledge and logical inference to resolve ambiguity in queries, thus ensuring the accurate mapping of abstract intentions during path construction. In addition, Multi-modal Understanding and Matching entails the capacity to parse, align, and match attributes across both textual and visual modalities, which is crucial for managing image-heavy constraints in item comprehension and path-following processes. Furthermore, adherence to Complex Rule Systems is vital for learning and precisely executing the subtle judgment rules necessary to standardize and reproduce path-following procedures. As we examine the Evolution of Large Model Training Paradigms for Relevance, it's clear that, from a formulation viewpoint, the relevance task resembles a classification problem. Early methods relied on task prompts and annotated responses, performing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) as an iterative baseline. However, such straightforward SFT soon proved inadequate, encountering performance degradation with out-of-distribution data. Researchers subsequently discovered that enhancing the model's Chain-of-Thought (CoT) capabilities—by prompting reasoning before output—could improve robustness and performance on challenging samples. Given constraints in computational resources and application scenarios, models with relatively modest parameter sizes are typically preferred, though they often exhibit limited reasoning and instruction-following abilities under a prompting-only framework. Therefore, the current mainstream approach involves leveraging powerful teacher models, such as GPT-4 and DeepSeek-R1, to synthesize CoT data and then distilling these capabilities into smaller models through SFT. Moreover, motivated by the remarkable achievements of reinforcement learning in LLM training, some researchers have incorporated a reinforcement learning stage post-CoT distillation, employing offline methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and KTO, or online approaches such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

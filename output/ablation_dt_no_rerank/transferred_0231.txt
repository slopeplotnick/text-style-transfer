We employ a principle-based approach for the evaluation of outlines, eschewing direct comparison to human-created examples. Because outlines are concise and primarily embody structural intent, directly comparing them with comprehensive surveys would provide limited constructive insights. Instead, the evaluation focuses on structural organization, logical coherence, and clarity. This methodology better aligns with high-quality outline criteria, primarily concerned with adherence to academic conventions. Utilizing the initial survey title, each system must generate a survey. Since all systems have the capability to access information online, they are afforded equal opportunity to acquire pertinent data. We evaluate three distinct categories of systems: general-purpose long-text composition systems, academic paper-writing systems, and dedicated survey-generation systems. General long-text models exhibit proficiency in linguistic fluency and topical relevance; however, their surveys tend to lack depth, displaying deficiencies in originality, synthesis, and critical analysis consistently. Conversely, paper-writing systems demonstrate enhanced structural control; yet, their outputs are frequently rigid and conform to predetermined templates, constraining analytical profundity and diminishing perspective diversity. In contrast, survey-generation systems achieve the highest scores overall, exhibiting superior organizational structure, coherent synthesis of ideas, and enhanced distinction between factual and opinion-based content. Their efficacy is particularly pronounced in STEM domains, where accurate retrieval and structured organization of information are paramount. These differences underscore that crafting high-quality surveys necessitates more than the capabilities of general language models; it benefits from specialized methodologies that explicitly target literature grounding and structured conceptual synthesis.

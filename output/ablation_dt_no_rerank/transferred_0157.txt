Through image-only harmfulness evaluations, all samples were classified as safe, which exemplifies the limited scope of existing guardrail systems. In the audio domain, however, our models, OMNI-GUARD-7B and OMNI-GUARD-3B, achieved superior performance in audio safety assessments. Since audio safety remains largely unexplored, our model offers a robust solution and demonstrates that safety training can effectively generalize to the audio modality. Furthermore, we evaluated OMNIGUARD's capability in scenarios involving cross-modal interactions to assess its effectiveness across various media formats, such as MM-SafetyBench, Video-SafetyBench, and AIAH. Compared to another model, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, our OMNIGUARD-7B exhibited superior accuracy across all evaluations, demonstrating its capability to proficiently manage various media types concurrently. Moreover, despite its relatively smaller scale, OMNIGUARD-3B nearly matched the performance of larger models such as Qwen3-VL-235B in certain evaluations, affirming its efficacy as a viable option for integrated multi-modal media operations. To evaluate the impact of safety reasoning training on omni-modal guardrails and address the additional complexity of multiple media types, we conducted further investigations using the 7B model across four distinct modalities. Our analysis involved a comparative assessment of OMNIGUARD-7B against the baseline model, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, as well as an alternative iteration fine-tuned solely for safety label recognition. Our analysis yielded several insights: First, both supervised fine-tuning methods resulted in enhanced performance over the original base model across all unimodal evaluations (text, image, video, and audio), indicating that basic safety fine-tuning contributes to improved handling across multimodal contexts. Second, in comparison to the variant fine-tuned solely with safety classification labels, our model exhibited superior efficacy.

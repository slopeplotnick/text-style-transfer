The parsing system efficiently extracts structured allocation decisions from LLM markdown responses by employing regex pattern matching. This pattern-based parsing technique specifically identifies formatted sections within the LLM's markdown output, such as allocation decisions, success percentages, and reasoning explanations, subsequently transforming them into structured data for validation. Moreover, a comprehensive validation system applies eight rules to ensure the data's integrity, including invalid allocations elimination, trade-off analysis verification, workload awareness checking, and logical consistency evaluation. The transition to a weighted validation framework was necessitated by the limitations of conventional rule-based systems, which often fail to capture the enhanced reasoning quality and contextual awareness crucial for LLM-driven task allocation. Indeed, recent studies on LLM evaluation emphasize that multi-aspect, criterion-based assessment provides a more reliable measure of model reasoning performance than binary correctness checks. Consequently, the proposed validation system adopts a weighted scoring approach that evaluates reasoning outputs across several dimensions, such as explanation clarity, success-rate consistency, workload awareness, trade-off justification, phase compliance, and confidence alignment, reflecting both the technical soundness and interpretive quality of the model's decisions. Each dimension proportionally contributes to a composite quality score, wherein higher weights are allocated to factors that directly influence allocation outcomes, like reasoning quality and success-rate accuracy, while supporting aspects, such as confidence justification, receive lower weights. This configuration ensures a validation process that is both performance-sensitive and context-aware, thereby effectively balancing interpretability and operational rigor in evaluating the model's reasoning behavior. 1.

LLaMA-Factory primarily focuses on text-generation tasks and does not inherently provide for the conversion of these tasks into classification problems. Our framework, PEFT-FACTORY, addresses both the limited variety of existing PEFT methods and the potential for fine-tuning large language models for classification tasks. Nevertheless, several other frameworks offer distinct advantages despite being less user-friendly compared to LLaMA-Factory. FastChat is a specialized framework for efficiently training large language models for chat completion applications. Similarly, LitGPT and LMFlow are versatile and user-friendly frameworks that facilitate the training of various generative models and methods. Axolotl is a command-line tool enabling efficient post-training of large models without compromising their functionality or scalability. Moreover, Open-Instruct centers on the fine-tuning of instructions for large language models and provides a range of models and methodologies to support this endeavor. Furthermore, H2O LLM Studio<sup>1</sup> is a sophisticated, enterprise-oriented solution featuring a graphical interface designed to streamline the development and deployment of expansive models. GPT4All offers an accessible interface utilizing LLaMA for efficient model training. ColossalAI is dedicated to facilitating fine-tuning operations across multiple nodes, thereby enhancing the efficiency in distributed computing environments. In contrast, frameworks such as LLaMA-Adapter and LLaMA-Accessory are characterized by their lightweight architecture. LLaMA-Adapter primarily functions by incorporating trainable adapters into the LLaMA models, while LLaMA-Accessory provides a comprehensive toolkit for the development of large language models. Notably, LLaMA-Adapter frequently features in configurations similar to LitGPT. Based on our analysis of analogous frameworks, we have identified three principal features that are either absent or insufficiently addressed and yet novel in our study: training of large-scale models using techniques beyond reparametrization-based PEFT methods, modular and dynamic integration of novel PEFT methodologies, and facilitation of training and validation for classification tasks.

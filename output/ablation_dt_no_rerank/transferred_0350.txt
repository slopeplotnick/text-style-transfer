Despite significant advances in the generative modeling of sound and music, the evaluation of these systems still lacks an established methodology. Beyond listening studies, researchers have proposed a set of objective metrics to assess audio reconstructions and the performance of neural networks trained to classify generated music into predefined categories. In this study, we employ both listening tests and objective metrics derived from previous studies to evaluate the effectiveness of our symbolic music generation. We generated 100 MIDI samples for each quadrant of our test set, GoEmotions, and evaluated the resulting metrics. As part of our preliminary listening study and to gain an initial understanding of our model's performance, we asked human participants to evaluate how well the model-generated music corresponded with the emotional intent of the source text. This task is notably more subjective than conventional music generation endeavors, as it requires two layers of interpretation: listeners must first discern the emotional essence of the narrative and then assess the extent to which the accompanying music aptly mirrors the intended emotion. In the first phase, each participant was provided with a piece of text and asked to assign it to one of the four quadrants in the valence-arousal space. Subsequently, they were exposed to two musical selections pertaining to that text, from which they selected the piece they perceived to most accurately evoke the emotion conveyed by the text. This dual-phase approach facilitated the assessment of both the consistency of text-based emotion perception and the congruence between the emotional qualities of the narrative and the corresponding musical selections. However, given that not every musical choice corresponded precisely with the emotional quadrant selected by the participant, our evaluation focused on emotional congruence by examining the valence (positive/negative) and arousal (high/low energy) dimensions instead of direct quadrant matches. Each emotional category was delineated into binary valence and arousal values, and for each participant's selection, we assessed whether the chosen musical sample aligned with their perceived emotional interpretation of the story along these axes. This analysis resulted in the calculation of valence accuracy, arousal accuracy, and combined valence-arousal accuracy, thereby offering a more flexible and perceptually grounded measure of emotional agreement within the forced-choice paradigm.

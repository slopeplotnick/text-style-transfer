To address RQ2, we evaluated our models on 11 low-resource languages, each selectively sampled from the 8 distinct clusters (see Figure 2). Languages using the Latin script, such as Galician (Gl) and Slovenian (Sl), consistently showed superior performance across benchmarks (Table 5). This can be attributed to the overlap in script and subword tokenization coverage in the shared XLM-R vocabulary, which provides enhanced representation during pretraining. However, Icelandic (Is), despite also using Latin script, underperformed due to its complex morphological structure leading to significant token fragmentation and less coherent sentence representations, limiting the effectiveness of multilingual encoders like SentencePiece in XLM-R. In assessing the transfer capabilities from high-resource languages, to answer RQ3, we trained models under three configurations: exclusively Hindi, exclusively Chinese, and a combination of Hindi and Chinese. Each model was evaluated across six languages representing the Indic (Hindi) and East-Southeast Asian (Chinese) clusters. Within the Indic cluster, the model trained on Hindi data consistently outperformed the one trained on Chinese data for all benchmarks (see Table 6). Conversely, in the East-Southeast Asian cluster, the model trained on Chinese data exhibited superior performance over the Hindi-trained model for most benchmarks. Therefore, this demonstrates that transfer within a cluster from its own high-resource language to other member languages is more effective than transfer across clusters from a high-resource language of a different cluster.

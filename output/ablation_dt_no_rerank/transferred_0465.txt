User-Centered Evaluations: When individuals interact with systems, user trust is compromised if hallucinations occur; therefore, understanding how users perceive these errors is crucial. Research evaluating hallucinations within language models involves users who actively detect and report inaccuracies. This method incorporates user-oriented metrics that illustrate the erosion of trust due to hallucinations and the imposition of verification tasks on users that the system should inherently manage. Evaluation of Hallucinations: A More Inclusive Framework for Hallucination Evaluation: To address hallucinations comprehensively, various strategies have been developed to create more integrated frameworks. These evaluations employ diverse methodologies, including fact-checking, consistency analysis, and user experience assessments, which together facilitate the identification of hallucinations from multiple perspectives—not just the factual accuracy but also the soundness of arguments and user perceptions. Applications of LLMs in Different Scenarios: In deploying large language models across various applications, it is imperative to consider the nature of the task alongside the propensity for hallucinations. For general tasks, such as chatbots, models like GPT-4 and PaLM are favored because they generate fluid, human-like writing with minimal veracity risk while infusing creativity. Conversely, for applications demanding factual precision, such as information retrieval or question answering, models like BERT and T5 are preferred due to their strong emphasis on factual accuracy and robust comprehension capabilities.

Overall, the most advanced models, such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o, achieve low error rates, comparable to—or even surpassing—human performance, as shown in the left panel of Figure 8. Except for Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, there is a general trend that more accurate models also exhibit stronger Bayesian behavior (left panel of Figure 8). However, it is important to note that this correlation between accuracy and Bayesian behavior is not invariably consistent. For instance, a model with deterministic predictive capabilities may seem simplistic and lack probabilistic tendencies. With the introduction of a spectrum of prior observations, these contextual changes significantly influence the operational patterns of large language models (LLMs). Specifically, as illustrated in the top right panel of Figure 6, there is a pronounced reduction in sequential predictability, especially when given a deliberately biased range of observations, which leads to increased error rates as seen in the left panel of Figure 6 (where triangular markers are consistently above the circular ones). This finding suggests that LLMs predominantly rely on input data to drive performance. Moreover, the middle panel of Figure 8 reveals that models excelling in NRMSE do not necessarily exhibit effective cue-combination capabilities. For example, GPT-5 Mini, despite attaining commendable NRMSE values, demonstrates suboptimal performance in integrating cues across different modalities. This discrepancy is evident in the maze distance estimation task, where GPT-5 Mini excels with text input but performs poorly with image input. This implies the need for a substantial reduction in the weight given to image input, which GPT-5 Mini fails to achieve optimally (see Appendix A.12 for a detailed analysis). In contrast, during the line length ratio task (lower right panel of Figure 6), many LLMs can effectively diminish the influence of image input amidst noise, thereby demonstrating an improved capacity for Bayesian adaptation.

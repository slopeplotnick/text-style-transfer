ReVSeg delivers state-of-the-art performance across multiple standard benchmarks for video object segmentation (VOS), surpassing both latent-based visual language models (VLMs) [3, 5, 29, 45] and training-free explicit models [13], while producing auditable reasoning traces. As shown in Figure 1, controlled ablations reveal that without our decomposed reasoning chain, both the base model and its RL-trained variant struggle with complex reasoning tasks in VOS. However, our approach performs robustly even before reinforcement learning (RL) is applied, and subsequent RL training significantly boosts performance. Specifically, our contributions include: First, the principled decomposition of the reasoning process in VOS. We introduce ReVSeg, which reforms this process into an explicit multi-step reasoning chain utilizing native model components. Second, an innovative RL framework that optimizes the reasoning chain. This RL-based formulation allows the model to refine decision quality without extensive additional data. Third, setting a new benchmark for VOS evaluations while elucidating the reasoning process. Recent advancements have significantly improved text-guided VOS with specialized architectures and frameworks [8, 16, 20, 35, 41, 42, 50, 53, 55]. Yet, reasoning in videos with numerous similar objects, rapid motion, or occlusion remains a challenge. Although recent methods like CoT-RVS [13] attempt to tackle this by using two separate systems per frame and integrating them textually, they face limitations due to fragmented information flow and lack of training capability. In contrast, we conceptualize reasoning VOS as an explicit reasoning chain within a unified system, employing RL for iterative enhancement.

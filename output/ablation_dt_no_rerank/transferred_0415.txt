We introduce DeepSeek-V3.2, a model that aligns high computational efficiency with superior cognitive achievements. The key technical breakthroughs of DeepSeek-V3.2 are as follows: (1) DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA): DSA is presented as an efficient attention mechanism that significantly reduces computational complexity while maintaining performance in long-context scenarios. (2) Scalable Reinforcement Learning Framework: By deploying a robust reinforcement learning protocol and augmenting post-training compute capacity, DeepSeek-V3.2 achieves performance comparable to GPT-5. Significantly, our high-compute variant, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, surpasses GPT-5 and demonstrates reasoning proficiency on par with Gemini-3.0-Pro, attaining gold-medal success in both the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) and the International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI). (3) Large-Scale Agentic Task Synthesis Pipeline: To effectively incorporate reasoning into tool-use scenarios, we developed an innovative synthesis pipeline that systematically generates training data at scale. This methodology enables scalable agentic post-training, resulting in substantial improvements in generalization and instruction adherence within complex, interactive environments. The release of reasoning models (DeepSeek-AI, 2025; OpenAI, 2024a) marked a pivotal moment in the evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs), prompting a considerable leap in their overall performance across measurable domains. However, since this milestone, LLM capabilities have advanced rapidly, leading to a notable divergence. While the open-source community (MiniMax, 2025; MoonShot, 2025; Qwen, 2025; ZhiPu-AI, 2025) continues to advance, the trajectory of closed-source proprietary models (Anthropic, 2025b; DeepMind, 2025a; OpenAI, 2025) has accelerated at a much steeper rate. Consequently, rather than converging, the performance gap between closed-source and open-source models seems to be expanding, with proprietary systems demonstrating increasingly superior capabilities in complex tasks. Our analysis identifies three critical deficiencies limiting the capabilities of open-source models in complex tasks. First, the architectural reliance on vanilla attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) mechanisms severely hinders efficiency for long sequences. This inefficiency poses a large obstacle to both scalable deployment and effective post-training. Second, the resource allocation for open-source models is insufficient during the post-training phase, which limits their performance on demanding tasks. Finally, when it comes to AI agents, open-source models show a significant shortfall in generalization and instruction-following capabilities compared to their proprietary counterparts (EvalSys, 2025; Li et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2025), thus impeding their effectiveness in real-world application.

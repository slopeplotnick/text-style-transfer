Our evaluation of these methods on the BrowseComp benchmark reveals that effective context management during testing significantly enhances model performance by allowing for the allocation of increased computational resources and facilitating additional execution steps. For instance, the Summary method extends the average number of steps to 364, achieving a performance improvement of up to 60.2%. However, its overall efficiency remains relatively low. In contrast, despite its simplicity, the Discard-all method demonstrates commendable efficiency and scalability, achieving a score of 67.6, which is comparable to that of parallel scaling while utilizing significantly fewer execution steps. Test-time computation can be enhanced either through serial context management or parallel processing, both of which contribute to the improved problem-solving capabilities of the model. Nevertheless, each approach exhibits different levels of efficiency and scalability. Therefore, it is crucial to consider actual computational costs when assessing model performance. Moreover, determining the optimal combination of step-by-step and parallel scaling to achieve both efficiency and scalability requires further investigation. In this context, we introduced DeepSeek-V3.2, a framework adept at harmonizing computational efficiency with advanced reasoning capabilities. By employing DSA, we addressed significant computational complexity while maintaining proficient long-context performance. Through augmentation of the computational budget, DeepSeek-V3.2 achieves performance comparable to that of GPT-5 on reasoning benchmarks. Furthermore, the integration of our task synthesis system enhances tool-use capabilities, thereby opening new avenues for the development of robust and adaptable AI systems employing open LLMs. Notably, our high-performance variant, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, has achieved significant recognition, securing top accolades at prestigious competitions such as the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) and the International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI), thus representing a significant advancement for open LLMs. Nevertheless, we acknowledge certain limitations compared to cutting-edge closed-source models like Gemini-3.0-Pro. For instance, due to a reduced number of total training FLOPs, the scope of general knowledge embedded within DeepSeek-V3.2 remains limited compared to leading proprietary models.

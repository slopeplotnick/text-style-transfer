Text versus numeric signals. Numeric-only data inputs are viable; however, their performance is noticeably weaker compared to text-only inputs. When both data views are combined, most of the performance of the optimal text-based variant is recovered, underscoring a practical hierarchy where text is primary, supplemented by numeric features. Signals hierarchy. Analyzing various datasets and tasks reveals a consistent hierarchy of signals. Text-based models are shown to be exceptionally strong for fake-news detection on datasets like EVONS and tweet series from FakeNewsNet. Here, applying a simple nonlinear layer atop transformer embeddings nears optimum performance on EVONS and notably outperforms classical baselines on FakeNewsNet. Numeric signals alone remain usable but are clearly less robust, such as on FakeNewsNet virality, where numeric data trails text-based configurations greatly. Overall, text is the primary source, with numeric/meta features functioning as complementary signals, although it is essential to explicitly state the trade-offs involved. Architecture versus input view. Across FakeNewsNet, sequence encoders such as GRU, LSTM, RNN, CNN, and Transformer all demonstrate competitive performance for both tasks, with the Transformer showing a slight edge. The closeness in their scores implies that the choice of input view—whether text, numeric, or fusion—and the definition of labels are more impactful than minor architectural tweaks, particularly under realistic constraints. Practical recommendations. (1) Approach virality as a series of tasks defined by explicit thresholds and time horizons; reporting definitions alongside metrics is crucial. (2) Begin with text-only baselines for fake-news detection and incorporate light numeric/meta features when available. (3) Prioritize recall-weighted selection in early-screening scenarios, but critically document the trade-offs in precision. (4) Opt for simple, well-documented architectures; emphasize data curation, minimize leaks, and ensure transparent protocol reporting.

Values in brackets were procedurally inserted for each prompt from a lookup table of simulated participants. Each row in this table represented a procedurally generated individual characterized by an age-ethnicity-gender combination derived from the representative UK sample stratification provided by Prolific. Since Prolific employs age stratification in ranges, the exact age of each simulated individual was randomly assigned within the designated range. After their generation, each AI-generated survey response was processed using a straightforward R script and subsequently archived as a row in a spreadsheet. To accommodate potential idiosyncratic variations in responses, three distinct prompts were employed. Following the methodology outlined by Hewitt et al. (2024), we adopted an ensemble technique wherein scores were averaged across prompts at the item level. The complete set of prompt types utilized in the studies is documented in full in the Supplementary Materials accompanying this article. Anticipating that the AI model might provide responses that deviate from the expected format, such as lacking comma-separated values, we retained these nonconforming responses within the dataset to replicate an authentic workflow. Consequently, these responses were treated as missing data in later analyses and replaced by averaging responses from other prompts. Studies 1 and 2 utilized pre-existing data, characterized by prompts such as "Impersonate a [Ethnicity] [Gender] of [Age] years from the United Kingdom. Provide responses to the subsequent questionnaire [...]". However, Studies 3 and 4 involved validating newly acquired datasets. An overview of the workflow for each study is described in this article: the initial segment clarifies the methodology employed for Studies 1 and 2, while the subsequent segment elucidates the bifurcated approach for Studies 3 and 4. Hypothesis H3.1 was examined by assessing the correlation coefficient between authentic and fabricated scores using Spearman's rank-order correlation. Conversely, Hypothesis H3.2 was evaluated using the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC). Moreover, Hypothesis 4 was investigated employing the Mann-Whitney U, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, and Levene's tests to assess distinctions across data types. Finally, Hypothesis 5 was examined using the same multigroup CFA methodology employed for Hypothesis 2, albeit exclusively on simulated data, with gender serving as the grouping variable. All CFA and M-CFA analyses were conducted using the MLR estimator, which is robust against non-normality deviations (Hox et al., 2010).

Given real-time latency and cost considerations, leveraging our LLM or deep cross-encoder-based BERT classifier for online searches poses significant scalability challenges. Therefore, we implement a teacher-student distillation framework to distill a student relevance model, as illustrated in Figure 1. The resulting online model is a streamlined, shallow variant of BERT. To ensure distributional consistency between the teacher and student models, we employ a deep cross-encoder BERT model as the teacher model, eschewing alternative model types like autoregressively trained LLMs. Furthermore, to facilitate the BERT model's understanding of query-product relevance, we explicitly integrate key attribute information mined by the relevance reasoning LLM into the input of BERT, enhancing explicit knowledge. To address the extraneous noise and redundancy in lengthy Chain-of-Thought reasoning outputs, we implement a refinement model that extracts and refines product attribute descriptions that align or misalign with queries. This approach helps the lightweight BERT model concentrate on salient aspects. Additionally, since the student model cannot access CoT results during real-time inference, we only integrate CoT information into the teacher model to maintain consistency between online and offline inputs. Subsequently, the simpler model derives insights from the teacher model's learned representations. Due to the absence of publicly accessible datasets for the Chinese e-commerce search relevance task, we conducted a series of experiments on an extensive dataset derived from JD application search logs.

We introduce a methodology for estimating the potential enhancement in solver performance when leveraging a verifier to select correct solutions. To validate the accuracy of our estimates, we empirically test various solver-verifier pairings using a selection of twelve models, picking the smallest three from each of four post-training categories. For each problem in the dataset, the solver iteratively generates solutions until a verifier confirms one as correct, with a nine-attempt cap imposed. If a correct solution remains undiscovered, the last attempt is retained. Figure 4 displays both the results and anticipated improvements. Although incremental enhancements may be subtle and the nine-attempt constraint limits detailed analysis, the overall findings align with expectations. Notably, self-assessment proves less effective than peer review, especially when peers are from varied groups. This observation underscores the reliability of our improvement estimation method, achieving notable accuracy without substantial extra effort. This phenomenon reflects a cognitive bias where individuals favor responses they perceive as most accurate. Interestingly, our results demonstrate a similar pattern in solver-verifier dynamics. Prior evidence shows models adept at reasoning progress less with self-verification but thrive when evaluated by diverse models, suggesting a similar bias. The key insight is that when solver and verifier share analogous cognitive strategies, the verifier may accept incorrect solutions, reducing potential improvements. Therefore, employing a verifier characterized by divergent thinking helps mitigate this issue.

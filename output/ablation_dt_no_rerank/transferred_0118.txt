Recent scholarly investigations have focused on enhancing the reasoning and verification processes of models. Studies like Ling et al. (2023) have explored Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, while Yang et al. (2022) looked into verifying individual proof steps and Luo et al. (2025) examined reward models for training purposes aimed at improving mathematical reasoning. Song et al. (2025) analyzed the benefits of using an outcome-level verifier, known as the GV-Gap, especially when the solver and verifier are identical models. However, their focus remains primarily on base models, overlooking scenarios involving post-trained models. Recent work also includes scaling test-time compute with verifiers. For instance, Zhao et al. (2025) integrated random sampling with self-verification, and Chen et al. (2025) combined parallel sampling with self-correction. Meanwhile, Singhi et al. (2025) compared Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023) with scaling using generative verifiers, and Snell et al. (2025) studied compute-optimal approaches for test-time scaling with process-level verification. Nonetheless, verifiers possess inherent limitations, such as producing false positives (Stroebl et al., 2024), eliminating valid reasoning paths (Yu et al., 2025), or failing to choose correct solutions (Brown et al., 2024). For additional related work on test-time verification, please see Appendix A. More recently, researchers have delved into self-improvement and self-evaluation of LLMs, though views vary; some express skepticism (Huang et al., 2024b; Kamoi et al., 2024), while others provide frameworks and empirical evidence for self-improvement via distribution sharpening (Huang et al., 2024a). Zhang et al. (2024) specifically argue for the need for stronger verifiers to support the growth of smaller models. Practical methods for self-improvement include natural language feedback (Madaan et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2023) and explicit self-correction training (Welleck et al., 2023). Tools, such as code interpreters (Gou et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024), offer systematic ways to iteratively refine solutions.

Nearswap, as introduced by Goddard et al. (2024), proposes a merging method by enhancing interpolation where parameters are similar and weakening it when they differ. To facilitate the comparison of different model architectures in our experiments, we selected three families of Large Language Models (LLMs): GPT, LLAMA, and QWEN. Specifically, the GPT family (Radford et al., 2019; Gao et al., 2020) comprises GPT2-small, GPT2-medium, GPT2-large, GPT2-x1, and GPT-neo-2.7B. Similarly, the LLAMA family (Touvron et al., 2023; Dubey et al., 2024) includes LLAMA-2-7B, LLAMA-3-8B, LLAMA-3.1-8B, LLAMA-3.2-1B, and LLAMA-3.2-3B. Finally, the QWEN family (Qwen, 2024) consists of QWEN2-0.5B, QWEN2-1.5B, and QWEN2-7B. These models are available from the Hugging Face repository, with URLs provided in Appendix A. Furthermore, Appendix C details the computational resources necessary for ongoing pretraining to develop biased LLMs, while Appendix D outlines hyperparameter configurations. To evaluate social bias in LLMs, we utilize three benchmarks: BBQ, BOLD, and HONEST. Appendix E provides the URLs for these datasets. The BBQ dataset (Parrish et al., 2022) consists of approximately 58,000 templated question-answer pairs across nine social domains pertinent to U.S. English speakers. By contrasting "underspecified" with "fully specified" versions of each question, it measures the extent to which models rely on stereotypical priors instead of explicit evidence. In addition, the BOLD dataset (Dhamala et al., 2021) contains 23,679 prompts, systematically categorized into 43 demographic subgroups, covering occupation, gender, race, religion, and political ideology. To ensure that debiasing methods do not adversely affect performance on downstream tasks, we thoroughly evaluate both debiased and pre-trained LLMs using the SuperGLUE benchmark, which comprises eight tasks: BoolQ, CB, COPA, MultiRC, ReCoRD, RTE, WiC, and WSC. All evaluations are conducted using the Language Model Evaluation Harness<sup>5</sup>.

Knowledge Injection and Reasoning Foundation: Domain knowledge is essential at the initial stage, serving as the cognitive foundation for grasping user queries and item attributes. By establishing this knowledge base, the model can deepen its understanding of query intent, accurately deduce users' core needs, and subsequently extract relevant attribute features from item data. Multimodal Information Extraction: The visual presentation of items frequently carries vital features that text alone cannot fully convey, such as style details and material texture. Consequently, this stage incorporates item image data, synthesizing attribute extraction pathways that merge both textual and visual modalities. Through the complementarity and verification offered by cross-modal information, the model develops more comprehensive and precise item profiles. Rule-Oriented Discrimination: Even after query intent recognition and item attribute extraction are finalized, attribute matching and discrimination rely on objective rules. This stage integrates rule-aware reasoning pathways, equipping the model to explicitly understand, adhere to, and employ these discrimination rules throughout the reasoning process. Step 1: Knowledge and Reasoning. In Section 2.3, we distinguish knowledge into general world knowledge and e-commerce knowledge. For the former, existing LLMs have accumulated substantial understanding through extensive pre-training, allowing them to interpret common concepts and entity connections. Nevertheless, e-commerce scenarios introduce numerous terms and phrases that are challenging to comprehensively grasp based solely on their standalone semantic data. These complexities generally fall into two primary categories: Long-tail Query: These queries target highly specific and niche items, yet current models lack the corresponding prior knowledge, complicating the establishment of effective semantic mapping. For instance, the query "strange things are also loved by someone" with an item described as "novel and interesting toys" actually pertains to a book title. Without this prior knowledge, the model struggles to make accurate relevance evaluations based purely on literal semantics. Similarly, this includes brand slang, product nicknames, marketing jargon, and other e-commerce-specific expressions. Semantically Ambiguous Items: Some items are too niche for the model's knowledge scope, preventing accurate identification and categorization based solely on their textual titles without adequate contextual comprehension of the product attributes. These instances demand supplementary domain-specific e-commerce knowledge for proper interpretation and classification.

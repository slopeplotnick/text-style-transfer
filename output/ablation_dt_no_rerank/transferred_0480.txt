Software Product Lines are a widely recognized method for efficiently creating highly customizable software products. At its core, a software product line functions as a software supply chain comprised of various interlinked components. Variability models outline the available product features and dictate the permissible combinations for product configurations. The asset base provides robust software components that implement these features. Within the framework of SPL, specialized tools are instrumental in selecting features to configure a particular setup and subsequently generating the concrete product by assembling requisite assets from the collection. This approach becomes particularly advantageous when multiple configurations are required for development. Planning for a novel type of product, however, poses a substantial challenge for organizations, especially during the initial phases. Therefore, many companies choose to develop a suite of similar software systems before investing resources in transitioning their software development processes towards a product line architecture. Despite successful examples documented in the literature, the task of transforming an existing software suite into a cohesive SPL is still formidable, potentially leading to reluctance among organizations to adopt this approach. This hesitation often results from the absence of standardized procedural guidelines and concerns about whether the benefits outweigh the costs. Consequently, even though companies possess extensive repositories of well-documented code, they continue to manually develop customized applications from foundational code using a strategy known as clone-and-own to meet client-specific requirements. Our partner, ITK - Predict and Decide, is an SME with 120 personnel, including 19 agronomists and 29 developers, among whom are 2 UI/UX designers and 4 system administrators. Approximately one-third of the company’s workforce are tech-oriented collaborators. The company employs an agile methodology for system development, characterized by an effective development and delivery process. The applications developed assist farmers in decision-making processes, allowing them to forecast diseases in crops and livestock as well as agricultural yields. The core of these applications is a set of simulators built by agronomists, after which IT teams integrate additional code to collect data from IoT devices or online services, feeding the simulators and displaying their output in dashboards. The back-end of this software architecture employs Kotlin, while the front-end is developed using Angular, with a notable application of TypeScript.

TokenPowerBench serves as a lightweight, extensible, and reproducible tool for benchmarking the power and energy consumption of LLM deployments across a range of hardware architectures, model frameworks, and task configurations. To facilitate meaningful comparisons of inference systems, it introduces energy-centric metrics such as Joules per token, Joules per response, and instantaneous power draw. These metrics align with specific operational phases in transformer-based LLMs, like prefill and decode stages, enabling detailed attribution of energy costs. In contrast to traditional metrics focusing on latency or throughput, these energy metrics emphasize sustainability and cost-efficiency—key concerns in today's AI infrastructure. The precision of power measurements is inherently influenced by the hardware configurations and analytical tools used. TokenPowerBench defines a three-tiered measurement model, ranging from acquiring data solely from GPU telemetry to comprehensive system-wide monitoring using advanced techniques like IPMI and rack-level PDUs. This architecture ensures that the benchmark is applicable across environments, from individual setups to large institutional testbeds. Moreover, the framework incorporates a robust experimental design protocol that guarantees reproducibility and consistency in experimental iterations. It is adept at accommodating diverse LLM inference tasks, which depend on system scale, model architecture, and workloads. TokenPowerBench systematically examines crucial configuration dimensions such as batch size, context length, and system architecture to elucidate their impact on energy consumption. It supports configurations from single GPU instances to expansive multi-node systems, thus providing insights into both component-level and system-wide energy dynamics. Given the rapid advancements in LLM technology, TokenPowerBench is designed to adapt to these evolving trends. It will extend its scope to include new GPU architectures, emerging AI processors, and diverse computational hardware. Additionally, the framework will incorporate functionalities to evaluate the trade-offs between energy conservation and the integrity of model outputs, helping users to optimize the balance between energy consumption and model performance.

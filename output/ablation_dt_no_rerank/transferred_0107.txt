The implementation of LLMs facilitates a paradigm shift in event-driven prediction. Unlike traditional methodologies, LLMs can extract comprehensive event structures—including elements such as type, participants, and temporal details—directly from unstructured text, maintaining information integrity and enabling causal inference while providing transparent evidence for analytical reasoning. However, current research has yet to fully capitalize on the capabilities inherent in LLMs. For instance, methodologies such as CAMEF[25] employ LLMs for the extraction of event details, subsequently transforming these structured events into low-dimensional vectors for processing by traditional neural networks, effectively relegating LLMs to the role of sophisticated feature extraction tools. This process results in the substantial loss of event-specific details, obscures the temporal connectivity and evolution of events, and fails to establish a reusable and traceable memory framework. In contrast, our methodology fundamentally differs by utilizing granular event information as integral components in constructing a coherent and structured event memory system. By explicitly tracking, synthesizing, and organizing events, our approach facilitates direct reasoning about the temporal evolution of events and enables comparative analysis of patterns across diverse contexts. Thus, our methodology addresses the limitations inherent in conventional LLM approaches concerning the linking of event sequences and the identification of recurring patterns over time, constituting the central tenet of our research. We present a structured system for the prediction of stock prices through the dynamic construction of a comprehensive event knowledge base derived from news corpora, further augmented by model feedback. This enables the framework to efficiently retrieve historically analogous event patterns for enhanced predictive capabilities.

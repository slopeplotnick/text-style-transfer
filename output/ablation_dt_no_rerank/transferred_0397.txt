From an evolutionary perspective, research has been conducted on emergent communication utilizing multi-agent systems, with the Talking Heads framework serving as a prominent example. Developed by Steels (2000, 2015), this project models language evolution through interactions involving non-linguistic contexts between agents. These agents develop an artificial language as a result of language games (Steels 2001), such as the naming game aimed at assigning names to objects in their environment (cf. Steels and Kaplan 2001 for studies on word naming, Steels and Loetzch 2012). Currently, their work also addresses the emergence and acquisition of syntactic phenomena (cf. Steels and Beuls 2017, Steels et al. 2018, van Trijp 2016). A relevant difference with the MODOMA project is that, in the Talking Heads framework, both agents engage in developing and learning language, whereas the MODOMA environment focuses on a single agent acquiring natural language fragments. Moreover, while the Talking Heads project targets artificial language acquisition, MODOMA centers on natural language learning. Alishahi's research simulates the acquisition of language by applying algorithms to corpora that are human or artificially generated; see Alishahi and Chrupala (2009, 2012) and Alishahi and Stevenson (2008) for studies modeling the acquisition of lexical categories, word meaning, and early argument structure. Simultaneously, Matusevych et al. (2013) enhance artificially generated input that imitates child-adult interactions, akin to the CHILDES database (MacWhinney 2014). However, unlike the MODOMA framework, the algorithm for language acquisition does not partake in direct conversation. Beekhuizen et al. (2014) provide a different perspective by modeling the acquisition of grammatical constructions using a single agent, which processes generated representations of utterances along with their semantic content. Furthermore, Conner et al. (2008, 2009)'s Baby SRL project models the acquisition of semantic role labeling by inputting annotated data from the CHILDES corpus or constructed sentences into their algorithm.

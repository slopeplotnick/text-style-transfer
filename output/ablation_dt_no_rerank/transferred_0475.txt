Despite the increasing prevalence of this methodology, research on solver-verifier interactions remains scant. Previous investigations have largely focused on how a solitary model verifies its own solutions through self-verification and subsequently enhances its performance. However, the effectiveness of self-verification is not always guaranteed, as models tend to be biased toward their inherent reasoning patterns, and training could further exacerbate this bias. Moreover, diverse tasks may not uniformly benefit from verification processes. Additionally, an exclusive emphasis on self-verification offers little insight into the dynamics when the solver and verifier are distinct entities. With open-source models available in varying configurations and versions, we now have the opportunity to explore verification across a heterogeneous array of models. Therefore, we extend our examination to encompass both intra-family and cross-family model analyses and pose a pivotal question: When is verification actually beneficial, and how do factors such as model family, model size, post-training, solver-verifier resemblance, or task type impact the efficacy of verification in enhancing solver performance? To achieve this, we assess a diverse suite of tasks that include precise logical reasoning or symbolic computation (3-SAT, Sudoku, and matrix multiplication), mathematical reasoning tasks (AIME and GSM8K), commonsense and factual reasoning (CSQA and GPQA), as well as comprehensive domain knowledge (MMLU encompassing STEM and social sciences). This study utilizes 37 models originating from 7 distinct model families. Our results indicate that self-verification does not uniformly yield improvements; models often prefer solutions congruent with their inherent reasoning, post-training might sharpen this bias, and the utility derived from verification is markedly task-dependent. Based on our findings, we provide practical recommendations for the effective utilization of verifiers.

Creating life-like motions for virtual characters is crucial for immersive experiences in games and virtual reality environments. This capability is equally vital in robotics, where replicating naturalistic movements can enhance safety and energy efficiency, and facilitate the acquisition of broadly applicable skills through learning from human motor patterns. Given the expressive nature of human motion, data-driven approaches have emerged as effective methods for producing realistic behaviors by learning from reference motion data. Typically, kinematics-based models, such as autoregressive models, variational autoencoders, or diffusion models, are trained on extensive motion datasets to synthesize plausible animations. However, these methods often fail to enforce physical constraints, leading to artifacts that undermine realism, especially when faced with novel scenarios. In contrast, physics-based methods focus on crafting control policies that generate motion governed by dynamic equations in simulated environments, adhering to real-world physics. Such controllers often utilize trajectory optimization or reinforcement learning to achieve more naturalistic motion. Previously, heuristic-based, non-data-driven approaches led to less realistic robotic movements, as capturing life-like human expressiveness manually poses significant challenges. Instead, contemporary data-driven strategies, like model predictive control or trajectory optimization, have shown promise in mimicking natural human movements. Recently, reinforcement learning-based motion tracking techniques have further advanced the field by enabling controllers to replicate a wide range of reference motion clips, thereby producing more life-like and agile behaviors. Nevertheless, these tracking-based methods constrain the controller to follow specific motion clips, limiting the ability to adapt to novel tasks. To address this, many systems incorporate specialized motion planners or high-level policies to select optimal movements or skills pertinent to the task at hand.

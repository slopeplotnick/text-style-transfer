In conclusion, we introduce a novel benchmark, HEALTHCONTRADICT, which utilizes interpretable quantitative metrics to evaluate language models' ability to process lengthy and contradictory biomedical information. Compared to state-of-the-art medical question-answering evaluations, HEALTHCONTRADICT more effectively differentiates model performance. During our evaluation, language models specifically tailored to the medical domain demonstrated enhanced capabilities in (i) accurately employing relevant information, (ii) dismissing erroneous data, and (iii) deciphering conflicting details. Notably, the TREC Health Misinformation tracks still stand as the only publicly accessible resource providing (1) expert-curated health questions with definitive answers and supporting scientific evidence, and (2) document pools containing both corroborative and contradictory evidence, as assessed by experts. These elements are crucial for crafting health questions with ground-truth answers, leveraging pairs of contradictory documents to evaluate language models' handling of biomedical knowledge conflicts. Within HEALTHCONTRADICT, we concentrate on significant and credible documents to evaluate their effectiveness in supporting a given query's response. We standardized labels by converting the 2019 annotations "effective"/"ineffective" and the 2021 annotations "supportive"/"dissuades" to a binary "yes"/"no" format, consistent with the 2022 labels. Furthermore, if two documents addressing the same query present opposite stances—one "yes" and the other "no"—they are considered a contradictory pair. Moreover, we categorize a document as correct if its stance aligns with scientific evidence, and incorrect if it opposes the scientific evidence. Originally, the datasets included 130 expert-verified questions distributed as follows: 50 questions in 2019, 35 in 2021, and 45 in 2022. These questions included both yes/no format queries and those accompanied by supporting and contradictory documents, which reduced the collection to 110 questions. We paired supporting and refuting documents to form contradiction pairs, ensuring each document's single use, as some may relate to multiple questions. Consequently, we excluded questions that lacked enough unique documents to form at least one contradiction pair. This refinement yielded a comprehensive set of 81 questions and 920 pairs of contradictory documents.

We reassessed the robustness of pretrained Vision-Language Alignment (VLA) models and demonstrated that performance degradation in response to visual perturbations primarily stems from shifts in spatial representation. To address this, we proposed a novel one-shot robustness adaptation framework, comprising Feature Token Modulation (FTM) and Feature Linear Adaptation (FLA), which efficiently recalibrates visual representations with minimal parameter updates. Our approach achieves state-of-the-art generalization to unseen camera viewpoints and visual perturbations on the Libero benchmark, uncovering the untapped robustness potential inherent in existing VLA models. VLA models endeavor to unify visual perception, language grounding, and action generation within a coherent system. By harnessing fundamental principles of pre-trained vision-language models within the robotic domain, recent VLAs such as RT-2, Pi0, PaLM-E, Octo, and OpenVLA demonstrate robust in-distribution generalization across a variety of manipulation tasks. These systems utilize large-scale embodiment datasets to incorporate diverse visual inputs, language instructions, and proprioceptive states, coupled with noisy actions, thereby facilitating the development of a unified action policy that enables robots to execute versatile behaviors. Nonetheless, recent analyses indicate that state-of-the-art VLAs, even with extensive pre-training, remain susceptible to fragility when encountering distribution shifts. Specifically, performance sharply declines when models are exposed to unseen camera perspectives, altered lighting conditions, or scene noise perturbations—a limitation that persists despite training on vast demonstration datasets. Therefore, rectifying this vulnerability is crucial for the effective deployment of these systems in real-world environments, which are inherently more complex than the controlled settings of Libero evaluations. Improving the capability of VLAs to accommodate visual changes remains a substantial challenge. Prior research has investigated data-centric strategies to enhance viewpoint invariance, such as increasing visual diversity through domain randomization, multi-view approaches, and extensive visual perturbation datasets. Although these methods have shown efficacy, they are often constrained by the substantial requirements for data collection and annotation, which limit scalability.

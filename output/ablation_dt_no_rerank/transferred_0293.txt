The principal objective of explainable AI (XAI) is to elucidate the rationale behind a model's specific output. However, the motivations for providing such explanations are multifaceted, including assessing the model's performance, enhancing user trust in the decision-making process, and aiding users in understanding how decisions can be altered when they are not permanent. This latter aspect requires explanations focused on actionable elements, specifically addressing which variables might feasibly be intervened upon in the current circumstances. Explaining under these actionability constraints with the goal of changing a model decision is referred to as algorithmic recourse. Although algorithmic recourse necessitates accurate causal modeling to reliably predict the outcomes of proposed interventions, it is solely concerned with the future outcomes of actualized interventions. The explanation approach discussed herein is intended to be general, deliberately avoiding assumptions about the explainee or the model's performance and deployment stage. This ensures the explanations remain faithful to the model's true operations even if the model's performance deviates from expectations. Moreover, counterfactual reasoning about retrospective interventions on all variables is permitted without a requirement for actionability. While this framework may be adapted for algorithmic recourse, such considerations are beyond the scope of the present study. In terms of concept vocabulary, concept-based explanations utilize overarching concepts instead of specific data features. Various definitions of concepts exist within XAI, and this discourse specifically focuses on symbolic concepts, as delineated by Poeta et al. [2025]. These concepts correspond to high-level attributes or characteristics of data, imbued with semantic meaning as defined by humans.

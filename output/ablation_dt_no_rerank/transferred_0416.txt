To achieve direct statistical comparability between real-world and simulated data, it is crucial for study samples to possess dimensional invariance. To assess this, we conducted a dimensional invariance test using Multigroup-CFA, treating our two samples—real-world and simulated—as independent groups, while methodically increasing levels of constraint. This examination entailed testing for configural invariance (H2.1), metric invariance (H2.2), scalar invariance (H2.3), and residual invariance (H2.4). If configural invariance is upheld, it implies that the factor loading patterns are consistent across groups. Furthermore, confirming metric invariance allows for variance comparisons between latent variables. Similarly, verifying scalar invariance enables comparisons of latent scale means. Lastly, residual invariance permits additional comparisons of manifest means and analyses with manifest scale scores (Putnick & Bornstein, 2016; Vandenberg & Lance, 2000). Achieving high measurement invariance between these samples suggests that in silico data reliably mirrors real-world data. Moreover, we hypothesized significant alignment between responses from real-world and simulated datasets (H3), and that the distributions of scores from both datasets would not significantly diverge (H4). To explore whether simulated data exhibited the same characteristics as real-world data, we first employed Mann-Whitney U tests to evaluate differences in central tendency, followed by Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests to assess distribution disparities.

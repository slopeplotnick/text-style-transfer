Concrete actions facilitate a smoother transition and are widely employed in various software development life cycles, particularly during SPLE migration. Many agile practices could be used to minimize the negative effects of migration adoption. For example, implementing a Sprint Zero is an opportunity to improve teamwork during the migration. This sprint encompasses several stages: The first step is establishing a working agreement, where agile rituals are planned to aid the team throughout the migration. Following this, having a clear and common vision is crucial to ensure all team members understand the goals and benefits of the SPL. Furthermore, personas, representing various user or developer profiles within the organization, are constructed to ascertain the intended users before project initiation. Subsequently, the Backlog is built by enumerating all requisite tasks, with each task needing to be estimated and integrated into a strategic roadmap. Regular retrospectives are planned to allow the team to share their perspectives and improve collaboration. Certain software engineering practices are essential for improving project quality, such as regular refactoring sessions to enhance reuse practices and eliminate technical debt. To ensure superior code quality, regular testing sessions should be scheduled to identify and prevent bugs; moreover, planning acceptance testing sessions with end users is vital for ensuring alignment with project objectives. Constant monitoring of code execution is essential for maintaining the efficiency and scalability of the resulting applications. These concrete actions are universally applicable across any software development setting, whether SPL-based or not. However, enhanced benefits are observed within SPL-based development frameworks due to the intrinsic integration of improved quality within the code base, which supports both current and future derived applications.

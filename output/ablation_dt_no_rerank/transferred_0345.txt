In the realm of financial tasks, several systems have emerged to enhance memory handling processes. FinMem exemplifies such systems, implementing a layered memory structure encompassing short, mid, and long-term frameworks to guide trading decisions. Similarly, MemP offers a general framework that abstracts trajectories into both step-by-step instructions and overarching strategic paradigms, thereby efficiently utilizing procedural memory. On the other hand, MemGPT functions as an OS-inspired system, orchestrating hierarchical storage management and interruption handling to dynamically expand the context window available to large language models. In contrast, Zep employs temporal knowledge graphs to integrate historical relationships with evolving contexts effectively. For the evaluation, we adopted metrics like accuracy (ACC) and the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC). Specifically, ACC measures the overall prediction correctness, while MCC serves as a reliable metric for balanced datasets, offering a robust assessment in stock prediction scenarios where class distribution may be skewed. Our experimental evaluation, conducted on four representative stocks, consistently demonstrated that our proposed framework significantly outperformed all baseline models in both ACC and MCC, thereby validating the efficacy of our approach. Furthermore, the evaluations revealed mismatches between other systems and the requirements of stock prediction. For instance, FinMem's isolated news storage lacks temporal event modeling, which leads to missing critical incremental information and evolving market contexts. Moreover, MemP's trajectory simplification excessively abstracts financial scenarios, leading to the loss of essential semantic information. MemGPT, however, lacks event-aware retrieval, frequently overlooking significant financial patterns. Meanwhile, Zep's approach fails to capture inter-event dependencies, missing decisive correlations within financial data. Our framework addresses these limitations by transforming unstructured news into structured temporal event knowledge, systematically capturing event logic and dependencies. This new paradigm establishes a traceable system capable of reasoning, mirroring human analytical processes, and thereby enhancing the accuracy of financial predictions.

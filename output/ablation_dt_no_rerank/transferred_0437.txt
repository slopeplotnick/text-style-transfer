The shift towards increased electrification has focused global attention on the exploration of critical minerals. Despite this burgeoning demand, the industry remains financially unviable. Moreover, even with enhanced financial investment, new mineral discoveries have declined over the past two decades. In this paper, I propose utilizing artificial intelligence as a catalyst for implementing a rigorous scientific methodology in mineral exploration, which aims to reduce cognitive biases and false positives, enhance the role of domain experts, and drive down exploration costs. Currently, the organization of exploration activities across various scientific disciplines—such as geology, geochemistry, and geophysics—has proven ineffective at accurately identifying deposits obscured by cover. Furthermore, the prevailing approach inadequately quantifies uncertainty, resulting in suboptimal decision-making and financial expenditures on drilling that often yield erroneous conclusions. Instead, I advocate for an innovative framework grounded in Bayesianism and falsification, where data collection primarily serves to critically assess human-generated hypotheses. The selection of subsequent data acquisition is governed by explicit, verifiable metrics and informed by rational decision-making. Additionally, I provide a practical protocol applicable to diverse mineral exploration campaigns. However, to effectively implement this guide, various forms of artificial intelligence are imperative. Chief among these are novel unsupervised learning techniques that collaborate with domain experts to enhance data interpretation and formulate competing geological hypotheses, alongside human-in-the-loop AI systems capable of optimally strategizing data acquisition across geological, geophysical, geochemical, and drilling domains. This approach prioritizes reducing uncertainty related to geological hypotheses over concerns for mineral quality and quantity. Finally, this methodology will be demonstrated through selected contemporary case studies in mineral exploration and resource assessment.

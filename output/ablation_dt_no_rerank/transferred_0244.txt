Reference state initialization (RSI), in which the simulated character begins in states randomly selected from a reference motion dataset, has proven essential for enhancing exploration in motion imitation methods. However, RSI requires ongoing access to the motion dataset to sample initial states during policy training. To eliminate this dependency on the original dataset, SMP can instead use its generative prior to create initial states when training the policy. In employing SMP for policy training, initial states are produced by sampling from the diffusion model that serves as the pretrained motion prior. Therefore, SMP fulfills dual roles in our framework: a reward function and an initial state distribution for training new policies. This generative state initialization (GSI) technique removes the necessity to keep the original motion dataset once SMP is trained, leveraging the generative capabilities of diffusion models to generate diverse, high-quality initial states. Our framework utilizes a pretrained diffusion model as a versatile, modular motion prior for imitation learning. The task-agnostic motion diffusion model functions as a reward mechanism, assessing the naturalness of the agent's movements within a reinforcement learning context. Consequently, it supports the training of control policies capable of performing a variety of tasks while displaying behaviors that naturally resemble those in the original motion dataset. In this section, we outline key design choices within the learning framework. Crafting an appropriate motion representation is crucial for training the diffusion model to accurately capture the dataset distribution and for repurposing it as a motion prior in policy training. The representation must contain ample information to reconstruct motion yet remain easily extractable from both kinematic motion clips and simulator state observations.

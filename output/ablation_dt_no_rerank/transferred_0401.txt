For each of the three metrics, we compute the values for all unordered pairs of methods within the HD paradigm, within the FV paradigm, and across both paradigms. We then average these scores over method pairs in each group, presenting the results in Table 2. These scores provide quantitative evidence that HD and FV paradigms possess complementary strengths. Notably, the higher ACS score for cross-paradigm pairings indicates that their successes are more mutually exclusive than those within the same paradigm, suggesting proficiency in distinct domains. Similarly, the superior ASG score reveals that combining HD and FV methods offers the greatest potential for performance enhancement, surpassing any combination confined to a single paradigm. Most importantly, the AECR score shows a substantial increase for cross-paradigm pairs, implying that their failure modes are largely distinct: when one paradigm errs, the other is more likely to succeed. Collectively, this analysis supports the view that HD and FV not only differ but also complement each other, providing a solid empirical basis for the development of hybrid models integrating both approaches. While the previous section established their statistical complementarity, this section explores the underlying causes for their divergent performance. Through a detailed case study, we examine specific scenarios where each paradigm tends to fail. We identify two primary failure patterns: Fact Verification struggles with retrieval quality, leading to errors when relevant evidence is absent, while Hallucination Detection may trigger false alarms due to semantic flexibility, mistakenly interpreting varied phrasing as hallucination. Analyzing these patterns clarifies the nature of their synergy and supports the design of hybrid integration methods proposed in the subsequent section.

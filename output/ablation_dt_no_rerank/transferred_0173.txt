The simplification process eliminates redundant elements, retaining only the essential logical components of the formula. This ensures that the CNF remains compact while preserving its satisfiability properties, thus enabling more efficient processing by SAT solvers. However, hallucinations can occur as incorrectly reused variables, misclassified literals, or translation errors, all of which hinder the continuous operation of SAT solvers. These issues may arise when models fail to accurately translate specific syntactic structures during training. Moreover, noncanonical sentence constructions that deviate from strict word order complicate the translation into CNF. To address these challenges, we utilized the combined capabilities of the NLTK and Sympy libraries. We employed NLTK for syntactic parsing to ensure accurate decomposition of input sentences according to rigorously designed grammar. This approach, validated through extensive research and careful human supervision, guarantees consistent parsing outcomes. Furthermore, by integrating NLTK with Sympy, our method harnesses precise syntactic analysis to ensure that the subsequent CNF transformation faithfully represents the logical structure of the original sentences. Upon receipt of a paragraph, each sentence is treated as an individual clause. The text is split at periods, allowing each clause to be processed independently. Subsequently, these clauses are combined using logical conjunction operators to form a CNF. A large-scale, custom-defined context-free grammar (CFG) is utilized with NLTK to parse each sentence into a parse tree. This tree starts with the root symbol "S" and branches out into components such as IFCLAUSE, ORCLAUSE, and STATEMENT, reflecting the structure of natural language syntax. Additionally, the tree includes nodes for determiners (DET), nouns (NOUN), adjectives (ADJ), auxiliary verbs (AUX), and punctuation (PUNC/PUNCT). By converting this tree into a logical expression using predefined rules, we obtain an expression composed of Sympy operators. Finally, Sympy leverages its internal functions to convert each logical expression into CNF.

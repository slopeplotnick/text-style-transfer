Micro-level phenomena arise in contexts where small groups of individuals or systems engage in close and ephemeral interactions. Consequently, localized opacity and divergence start to develop. As these groups disseminate information within partially connected networks, meso-level risks emerge, propagating both opacity and divergence further. Macro-level pathologies subsequently occur when full connectivity is achieved—when topology density, opacity, and divergence reach critical thresholds—leading to significant and unanticipated alterations in collective behavior. This taxonomy serves as a conceptual and preliminary framework designed to facilitate discourse and analysis. Without such a framework, researchers struggle to accurately identify, evaluate, and regulate these behaviors within group contexts. Substantiation of this framework will require extensive empirical studies in controlled environments, constituting our planned future endeavors. Localized risks manifest in direct interactions among a limited number of individuals or systems, where early indicators of systemic drift begin to appear. These risks highlight the fragility of shared understanding even before the network undertakes large-scale collaborative operations. Although these problems originate locally, they have the potential to escalate as interactions persist, eventually seeding meso- and macro-level pathologies. Once a deviation becomes embedded in an agent's comprehension, subsequent interactions replicate and amplify it at a nonlinear rate, surpassing the growth in the number of human participants or communicative exchanges. Meso-level risks manifest when larger collectives, consisting of dozens to hundreds of agents, engage within semi-connected networks. At this stage, while no singular issue is immediately evident, the interaction pattern results in degradation of accuracy, diversity, or coherence. Individual components may appear correct in isolation; however, their collective performance deviates from expected norms. Such interactions demonstrate that collective risks can arise without overt failures, as disruptions stem from the dynamics of components that are only partially correct, rather than from individual errors. It is here that local predictability deteriorates, leading to systemic instability.

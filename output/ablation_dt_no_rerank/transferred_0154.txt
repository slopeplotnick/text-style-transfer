System prompts have emerged as a lightweight yet versatile tool for guiding large language model (LLM) behavior during inference. A growing body of literature demonstrates that careful prompt engineering can improve reasoning abilities and reduce bias. However, most of this research has focused on English-language contexts, overlooking a broader and more practical goal: ensuring that a single system prompt works reliably across multiple languages. Multilingual deployment introduces unique challenges for prompt design. Even when using multilingual LLMs, prompts initially crafted in English often underperform when translated or applied to tasks in other languages, leading to inconsistent accuracy, unstable reasoning, and degraded cross-lingual generalization. This discrepancy prompts a crucial question: How can we design a prompt that maintains high and stable performance across various languages and tasks? To address this question systematically, we break it down into three parts: First, what kinds of system prompts most effectively steer models toward better multilingual behaviors? Second, can we automatically discover prompts that achieve strong multilingual performance with minimal human intervention? Third, how do high-performing prompts influence model outputs across multiple dimensions, such as the structure of reasoning patterns and the choice of response language? To tackle these questions, we first define an evaluation framework with four criteria: average accuracy, accuracy variance, cross-lingual consistency, and output length variance. Drawing insights from the performances of various LLMs on diverse tasks, we propose an approach to optimize prompts for this multilingual, multi-metric objective. Subsequently, we analyze model outputs to reveal how optimized prompts shape reasoning behaviors and mitigate cross-lingual variability. Moreover, we introduce a new multilingual prompt optimization technique, demonstrating the feasibility of automatic prompt discovery in multilingual settings. By studying extensive reasoning data, we find that high-quality prompts often induce more structured and consistent reasoning while minimizing language-switching, suggesting that reasoning patterns themselves may significantly enhance multilingual robustness. Taken together, our results indicate that system prompt optimization is a promising and underexplored direction for achieving accurate and robust control over multilingual LLM behavior. The code and dataset are available at https://github.com/orange0629/multilingual-system-prompt.

Our evaluation procedure presents a significant advancement over existing benchmarks. In comparison to related benchmarks involving judges, prior methodologies such as PPTC-R predominantly focus on API-level disparities. While effective in certain scenarios, this approach lacks robustness in detecting crucial semantic errors, such as correctly copying text to an incorrect slide. Our IF Judge, by utilizing structured summaries, is specifically designed to identify such logical inconsistencies. Additionally, although alternative benchmarks like T2US employ visual language models, their dependence on prompting without a comprehensive rubric results in noisy and unreliable assessments. PPTArena addresses these issues by establishing a rigorous and reproducible scoring foundation. Therefore, our dual-judge, rubric-based system is vital for evaluating the intricate processes involved in PowerPoint presentation editing and for uncovering nuanced failure modes that simpler methodologies tend to overlook. We introduce PPTPilot, a presentation editing agent noted for its simplistic architecture, which yields unexpectedly effective results, surpassing certain proprietary tools like OpenAI's ChatGPT Agent, as detailed in Section 5.1. Our system's design is based on two primary insights: firstly, the main challenge is not merely task comprehension but ensuring tasks are executed with precision and reliability. PowerPoint files, structured on the complex Office Open XML (OOXML) format, inadequately manage errors or suboptimal outputs from Visual Language Models (VLMs). Consequently, this necessitates specialized formats and configurations for editing purposes. Moreover, it became evident that relying solely on the XML format for editing is insufficient. An effective tool must intelligently determine the optimal editing approach for each specific task. PPTPilot features a dual-path architecture, allowing for the management of editing queries via either programmatic tools or direct XML editing.

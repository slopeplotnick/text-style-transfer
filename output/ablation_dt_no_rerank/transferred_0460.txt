To address this challenge, we propose a quantitative approach to assess a reasoning model's tendency toward belief entrenchment. Specifically, we define belief entrenchment as a deviation from the Martingale property—a fundamental principle in Bayesian reasoning which posits that the direction of belief updates should not be foreseeable prior to their occurrence. This motivates our use of Martingale deviation as an unsupervised indicator of rigid belief updating. Concretely, when a model's future beliefs can be inferred solely from its previous beliefs, it signifies an excessive rigidity, indicating departure from the Martingale property. We examine this hypothesis through empirical investigations across three domains: event forecasting, value-laden inquiries, and academic paper review (hereafter, Forecasting, r/ChangeMyView, and OpenReview, respectively). Across a range of models and reasoning paradigms, we reveal a pervasive tendency for belief entrenchment. Our analyses consistently show that future beliefs are frequently predictable based on prior ones, demonstrating a clear violation of the Martingale property. Importantly, we find that non-adherence to this property correlates with a decline in model accuracy, as measured by the Martingale Score—a metric assessing the predictability of belief updates from preceding beliefs. Utilizing this score, we evaluated prevalent models and identified belief entrenchment across various domains, prompts, and configurations as a widespread phenomenon.

Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently exhibit hallucinations, generating content that appears fluent and coherent but is factually incorrect. Such inaccuracies undermine trust and hinder their adoption in real-world applications. To address this challenge, two distinct research paradigms have emerged: model-centric Hallucination Detection (HD) and text-centric Fact Verification (FV). Despite sharing the same goal, these paradigms have evolved in isolation, employing distinct methodologies, datasets, and evaluation protocols. This separation has resulted in a research schism that impedes their collective progress. In this study, we take a decisive step toward bridging this divide. UniFact, a novel unified evaluation framework, enables direct, instance-level comparison between HD and FV by dynamically generating model outputs and evaluating their factual accuracy. Extensive experiments on various models and detection methods reveal three key findings: (1) Neither paradigm is universally superior; (2) HD and FV capture different facets of factual errors; and (3) integrated approaches consistently achieve state-of-the-art performance. Beyond benchmarking, we analyze the divergent pathways of HD and FV to provide empirical evidence supporting the necessity of their unification. Comprehensive experimental results highlight the need for a new, integrated research agenda to unify Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification in LLMs. LLMs have fundamentally reshaped the digital landscape, driving next-generation search engines and automating complex tasks previously deemed impossible. However, their deployment in critical, real-world applications is severely hindered by a significant issue: the generation of fluent yet factually incorrect content, known as hallucinations. In fields such as healthcare, legal, and journalism, where factual accuracy is essential, these errors can compromise user trust, spread misinformation, and lead to harmful consequences. Therefore, devising robust mechanisms to detect these factual inaccuracies is not merely a technical challenge but a fundamental requirement for developing reliable intelligent systems.

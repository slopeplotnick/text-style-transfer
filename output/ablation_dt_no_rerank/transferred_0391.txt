To effectively adapt multilingual models for diverse tasks, we leverage the inherent structural relationships among languages, naturally facilitating cross-lingual information transfer. The representational domain of transformer models, such as XLM-RoBERTa, ensures that languages with semantic or syntactic similarities, or shared script and orthographic characteristics, are embedded in close proximity. We capitalize on the 100-language coverage of the XLM-R model, which spans both widely spoken and less common languages. Instead of requiring extensive data for every language, this method employs a strategic selection of widely spoken languages for training, thereby enabling linguistic insights to be extrapolated across a broad linguistic spectrum. Consequently, this approach facilitates knowledge transfer between languages, thus obviating the need for training on infrequently represented languages. Building on this strategy, we translated the MultiJail dataset into all 100 target languages by using state-of-the-art translation systems optimized for each language. For prevalent languages, we use optimal models such as GPT-4o, M2MBart-50, and Helsinki-NLP OpusMT. However, these models demonstrate reduced translation efficacy for low-resource languages. Therefore, we utilize SarvamTranslate (AI 2025) for Indic languages, providing robust performance across less prevalent linguistic contexts, while GPT-4.1 is employed for other low-resource languages. To ensure translation precision, especially for underrepresented languages, a subset is cross-referenced with the Google Translate API. By integrating these models, we attain translations that are fluent, high-quality, and precise.

Large language models (LLMs), such as those used in Google's AI Overviews, are increasingly employed to summarize multiple long documents. However, it remains unclear whether they weight all inputs equally. Focusing on abortion-related news, researchers constructed 40 pro-neutral-con article triplets, permuting each triplet into six input orders, and prompted Gemini 2.5 Flash to generate a neutral overview. Each summary was then evaluated against its source articles using ROUGE-L for lexical overlap, BERTScore for semantic similarity, and SummaC for factual consistency. One-way ANOVA revealed a significant primacy effect for BERTScore across all stances, indicating that summaries were more semantically aligned with the first-seen article. Furthermore, pairwise comparisons showed that Position 1 differed significantly from Positions 2 and 3, while the latter two did not differ from each other, thus confirming a selective preference for the first document. Therefore, these findings present risks for applications relying on LLM-generated overviews and for agentic AI systems, where steps involving LLMs can disproportionately influence downstream actions. The rapid evolution of generative AI, serving as a core component in agentic systems, has accelerated the development and early deployment of such systems across diverse domains. Agentic AI is characterized by its ability to pursue complex objectives adaptively with limited direct supervision, exhibiting a high degree of operational autonomy. Moreover, a common mechanism enabling this autonomy is goal decomposition, wherein a high-level user objective is parsed into a sequence of manageable subtasks. To execute these subtasks effectively, such systems typically engage in multi-step reasoning and planning, retrieving and interpreting external information to guide subsequent actions. Consequently, the reliability of an agentic system is intrinsically linked to the accuracy with which its underlying Large Language Models (LLMs) retrieve, prioritize, and synthesize information from instructions and external documents.

We utilize three multimodal emotion datasets to train our model and validate its effectiveness. EMER provides high-quality human-verified reasoning-label pairs, which are used for initial training phases. Meanwhile, DFEW and MAFW serve as large-scale benchmarks that cover a variety of facial expressions across video, audio, and text modalities. Further information regarding these datasets is detailed in Appendix 7.1. In alignment with prior research, we report Unweighted Average Recall (UAR) and Weighted Average Recall (WAR) to assess recognition accuracy. Moreover, we measure Explanation-Emotion Alignment (EEA), Explanation Prediction Consistency (EPC), and Faithfulness Confidence Ratio (FCR) to evaluate the congruence between the provided explanations, the detected emotions, and the model's predictions, with precise definitions and annotation methodologies elaborated in Appendix 7.2. Architecturally, following the R1-Omni design, our framework employs SigLIP as the vision encoder, Whisper-large-v3 for audio encoding, and BERT for textual encoding. Each modality's output is projected into the LLM embedding space through dedicated visual and audio projectors, which are implemented as two linear layers to align with the dimensional requirements of the LLM representation. For example, a 1-second audio segment yields approximately 50 audio features, whereas a single video frame results in 729 visual features. The foundation of our Long Language Model (LLM) is Qwen2.5-7B, initialized with model weights from the HumanOmni checkpoint to preserve pretrained multimodal representations. To evaluate the efficacy of our approach, we conduct a comparative analysis of our MIGR method against existing methodologies for emotion understanding on the MAFW and DFEW datasets. These existing approaches are categorized into two main groups: (i) those that directly predict emotion labels and (ii) models that generate textual explanations prior to prediction.

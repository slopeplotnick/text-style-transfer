Cross-LLM Agreement: We establish pairs of AI judges, like GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-pro, to evaluate how closely their belief evaluations align. Given our extensive data from GPT-4o, and comparatively limited data from other judges, GPT-4o is designated as the default judge for all comparative analyses. Human-LLM Agreement: We use a small batch of human evaluation data to validate the consistency between human and LLM judges. Human evaluators are tasked with the same belief evaluation procedures as the AI, enabling direct comparisons, such as between human evaluator 1 and GPT-4o. Full results are presented in Table 2. Analyzing both cross-judge and human evaluation consistency, it is unlikely that our findings on belief entrenchment are due to judge bias. In this paper, we propose the Martingale Score as a principled and unsupervised metric for assessing belief entrenchment in LLMs' reasoning processes. We demonstrate that the Martingale Score predicts the ground-truth accuracy of reasoning and apply it across various models, prompts, and problem domains. Furthermore, we identify several factors that might exacerbate or mitigate belief entrenchment severity. Limitations: A key limitation of our study is the apparent absence of correlation between the Martingale Score and the Brier Score within the OpenReview domain. This may be due to the community-voted nature of decision-making in OpenReview, leading to inherent variability and noise. Nevertheless, Table 1 illustrates that belief entrenchment is a pronounced phenomenon in human-judgment-required contexts, such as r/ChangeMyView and OpenReview, raising concerns about the deployment of these AI models in such domains.

The EMOPIA dataset is an essential resource for Affective Music Generation research, yet the discipline is hindered by a scarcity of large-scale human-labeled MIDI datasets. Most available datasets are limited, containing fewer than 200 MIDI files annotated with emotion labels. We leverage the largest dataset available, EMOPIA, which includes 1,087 music clips from 387 songs, all annotated for emotion by four human assessors. This dataset comprises four distinct emotion categories corresponding to quadrants in the valence-arousal model; further details can be found in the Appendix (Section B). Additionally, we employ the GiantMIDI-Piano dataset in our study, which consists of 10,855 solo piano compositions by 2,786 composers. This dataset is valuable for classical music analysis and symbolic music generation. To align our text data with the EMOPIA files, we classified the 27 emotion labels into four distinct AV quadrants. This classification was facilitated by the NRC VAD lexicon, a comprehensive resource containing arousal and valence scores for 55,000 words. Subsequently, each emotion was assigned to one of the four quadrants. Figure 1 illustrates the resulting mappings and their positions on the AV plane, while Appendix A offers a detailed discussion of the VAD lexicon and its integration with EMOPIA. We employ MIDI files to encode and generate symbolic music data due to their ability to store musical performance details in a compact format, capturing emotion-relevant aspects of music. The widespread availability of MIDI files offers a significant advantage for training purposes. During our training process, we conceptualize each MIDI file as a sequence of discrete musical events, facilitating the effective application of sequence-to-sequence models for music learning and creation.

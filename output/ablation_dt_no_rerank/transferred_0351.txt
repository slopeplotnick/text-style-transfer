We propose TACO, a test-time scaling framework for VLAs, which retains the strong generalization capabilities of pretrained VLAs while effectively constraining outputs to the success modes of specific downstream tasks. Moreover, we introduce an efficient internal representation mechanism for pseudo-count estimation in CFN, enabling accurate measurement of distributional shift with minimal computational overhead. We demonstrate that TACO significantly boosts the success rates of diverse VLA models across both simulation and real-world tasks without prolonged training time and can operate with low latency. In the realm of reinforcement learning, particularly in the contrast between online and offline methods, significant challenges are posed by distributional shifts. Online RL involves agents actively interacting with the environment to explore the state-action space, while offline RL learns a policy exclusively from a fixed, previously collected dataset, without further environmental interaction. This setting introduces substantial challenges due to the distributional shift between the behavior policy that generated the data and the target policy being learned. Specifically, the offline dataset reflects the visitation distribution of the behavior policy, which may differ substantially from that of the learned policy, leading to severe extrapolation errors when estimating values for out-of-distribution state-action pairs. A similar distribution shift arises during the SFT stage of VLAs, where the models encode broad and redundant action modes, whereas successful behaviors in downstream tasks typically occupy a much narrower region. Consequently, when sampling actions in flow or diffusion-based processes, the policy may generate suboptimal behaviors. To address this, TACO employs an anti-exploration strategy that imposes policy constraints to restrict the policy to stay close to the support of the success data distribution. Unlike conventional offline RL approaches that typically rely on uncertainty-aware or conservative penalties, our method adopts a simple count-based mechanism, which is well-suited to the structure of VLA models and can operate without value estimation. Notably, count-based regularization has precedent in both classical RL and recent RLHF algorithms.

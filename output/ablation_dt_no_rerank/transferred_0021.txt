Notably, Whisper-large-v3-Turbo achieves the largest absolute reduction of 0.22, followed by Wav2Vec-BERT at 0.27, and MMS at 0.15. To understand the impact of data volume on the fine-tuning process, an investigation into the effect of varying amounts of speech training data was conducted. Specifically, the Whisper-v3-turbo model was trained for a single epoch using subsets of 50 hours, 150 hours, and 250 hours. The baseline experiments demonstrate that the Swivuriso dataset supports measurable improvements in Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) across multiple architectures and languages. Fine-tuning consistently reduced error rates, and results across models indicate that the dataset provides stable learning signals even at early training stages. Furthermore, the analysis of training hours confirms that additional data contributes to performance gains for most languages. Therefore, these findings position Swivuriso as a reliable resource for advancing research in South African ASR and for continued methodological development in multilingual speech modeling. Although substantial work has been undertaken by scholars in creating world-class datasets for Automatic Speech Recognition, focusing on precision and error reduction, progress in developing datasets for African and other low-resource languages has been slower. However, research by Abdulmumin et al. on the FLORES dataset significantly contributes to this field by enhancing evaluations for four African languages: Hausa, Northern Sotho (Sepedi), Xitsonga, and isiZulu. This study involved native speakers in verifying and correcting dataset splits, thus improving linguistic accuracy and reliability. Such efforts highlight the importance of native speaker involvement to ensure the datasets' alignment with linguistic and cultural contexts. Consequently, we have heavily involved native speakers to enhance our dataset's linguistic accuracy and overall quality, reflecting prior research recommendations. Similarly, another study aided in refining sentiment analysis across various domains, particularly highlighting the scarcity of sentiment datasets for low-resource languages like Hindi compared to high-resource languages. This pivotal study introduced a curated corpus annotated with sentiment classifications—positive, negative, and neutral—and addressed challenges in sentiment analysis within socially significant domains.

Hate speech recognition in low-resource languages remains a difficult problem due to insufficient datasets, orthographic heterogeneity, and linguistic variety. Bangla, spoken by more than 230 million people in Bangladesh and the Indian state of West Bengal, is significantly underrepresented in computational resources despite the growing need for automated moderation on social media platforms. In this work, we study Subtask 1A and Subtask 1B of the BLP 2025 Shared Task on hate speech detection. We reproduce the official baselines, such as Majority, Random, and Support Vector Machine, while also considering Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree as additional baseline methods. Moreover, we utilize transformer-based models including DistilBERT, BanglaBERT, m-BERT, and XLM-RoBERTa for hate speech classification. Notably, all the transformer-based models outperformed the baseline methods across the subtasks, except for DistilBERT. Among the transformer-based models, BanglaBERT delivers the best performance for both subtasks, suggesting that language-specific pre-training is crucial. Our results highlight the potential and necessity for pretrained language models for the low-resource Bangla language. Over the past few decades, the widespread usage of the internet has grown significantly, making social media platforms much more popular worldwide. Improved access to the internet has enabled people to express their thoughts and share information online. Furthermore, the use of social media continues to rise rapidly, with no signs of slowing down. Although social media has become integral to public discourse, it also facilitates the spread of hatred. The rapid increase in toxic and harmful content, including hate speech, raises substantial safety concerns on these online platforms. Hate speech, defined as harmful, offensive, or derogatory language targeting individuals or groups, necessitates automatic detection as a critical task within Natural Language Processing (NLP). There has been considerable development in hate speech detection in high-resource languages like English; however, advancements remain limited in low-resource languages such as Bangla due to the scarcity of high-quality annotated datasets and the complex linguistic properties of the language.

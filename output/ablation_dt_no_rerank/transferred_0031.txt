The foundation of our research rests on the definition of a statistical model, whose construction can sometimes be misguided. To mitigate subjective biases in the model, we make several assumptions supported by previous research and the presumed data distribution. However, as with any model, the choice of parameters, their distribution, and the parameters themselves remain open to debate. Our justifications for selecting model parameters stem from the JVM specification and programming languages' definitions, particularly focusing on aspects parametrized within Java Bytecode. The Gaussian distribution is justified both ontologically and epistemologically. Ontologically, it regularly appears in natural phenomena because processes that aggregate fluctuations often conform to a Gaussian distribution, as supported by the central limit theorem. Epistemologically, since we only know that our measurements have a finite mean and variance, the Gaussian distribution aptly represents the inherent uncertainty in our data. Other concerns primarily relate to the execution platform and the JVM. Consequently, the memory state prior to each bytecode pattern execution could influence results, so we activate the garbage collector before each test cycle. To further mitigate bias due to paging, caching, and disruptions from concurrent processes, we measure each bytecode pattern ten times, utilizing a randomized execution order to prevent systematic effects.

How do language models utilize contextual information to answer health-related questions, and how are their responses influenced when faced with conflicting contexts? We evaluate the capabilities of language models to process extensive, contradictory biomedical information using the HEALTHCONTRADICT dataset. This expert-verified dataset includes 920 distinct examples, each featuring a health-related question, a factual answer supported by scientific evidence, and two documents offering opposing perspectives. We explore various questioning formats, incorporating correct, incorrect, or contradictory contexts, to assess their impact on the models' responses. Compared to other evaluation benchmarks, HEALTHCONTRADICT offers better insights into the models' ability to navigate contextual nuances. Our findings reveal that the success of these models is attributed not only to their pre-trained parametric knowledge but also to their ability to leverage accurate context while mitigating the impact of erroneous context. However, language models still often produce responses that seem accurate but are incorrect, posing significant challenges. This is particularly concerning, as such inaccuracies may lead individuals to unknowingly rely on incorrect health-related information, resulting in considerable risks. Moreover, the rapid evolution of health-related knowledge, especially during medical crises, combined with the quick spread of unverified information online, significantly affects the models' potential for effective pre-training and contextual learning. Several approaches have been suggested to help models manage contradictory information. For instance, context-aware decoding allows contextual knowledge to supersede a model's parametric knowledge when conflicts arise. ContextCite, on the other hand, traces back the origins of contextual knowledge that lead models to generate specific statements, thus enhancing explainability. However, these methods primarily focus on either parametric or contextual knowledge. An alternative method integrates both by evaluating the congruence between information sources. Additionally, a framework has been introduced to predict dual responses, one from contextual information and one from the model's inherent knowledge. Furthermore, another technique ensures the model selects the correct response amidst conflicting information. Alternative strategies focus on preventing incorrect responses. In the training phase, they introduce erroneous or irrelevant data to improve models' handling of ambiguous information. During testing, they employ strategies for misinformation detection, careful inquiry frameworks, and reader ensembles to verify response accuracy. Moreover, strategies are utilized to obtain robust answers to counter adversarial attacks, while methodologies for predicting rapidly evolving facts are implemented to avoid providing outdated information. Current approaches primarily address either the rectification of misinformation or the prevention of undesirable model behaviors; nevertheless, both strategies are essential for improving the accuracy and reliability of responses within healthcare-related domains.

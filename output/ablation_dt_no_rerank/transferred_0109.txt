Sentiment analysis has become a central component of modern Natural Language Processing (NLP), supporting applications in digital governance, public health surveillance, financial inclusion, and user-generated content moderation. However, despite global advances in deep learning and multilingual language modeling, South African low-resource languages remain underrepresented in existing sentiment analysis research. This underrepresentation is manifested in the scarcity of annotated datasets, the lack of lexical resources, and limited cross-lingual transferability. Consequently, models trained on high-resource languages such as English and French often exhibit diminished performance in African contexts, widening the digital divide and limiting equitable access to downstream NLP technologies. Existing research on multilingual sentiment analysis has primarily focused on strategies like machine translation, cross-lingual embeddings, and monolingual lexicon construction. While these approaches have shown potential, they present several intrinsic challenges. Firstly, machine-translated lexicons frequently propagate inaccuracies and cultural discrepancies, especially in the morphologically rich African languages. Secondly, lexicon extraction methodologies relying on comprehensive datasets are heavily dependent on domain-specific corpora, which are seldom available for low-resource languages. Furthermore, even with advancements in African language models such as AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa, their efficacy remains constrained by the limited availability of high-quality sentiment lexicons necessary for effective classification in multilingual environments. To address these challenges, this paper introduces TriLex, a three-stage retrieval-augmented framework designed for scalable sentiment lexicon expansion for African low-resource languages. TriLex integrates corpus-based lexical extraction to identify candidate sentiment-bearing terms, cross-lingual semantic mapping to align these terms across English, French, and target African languages, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to refine, validate, and expand the lexicon using large language models conditioned on multilingual retrieval signals. Overall, this study presents TriLex, a novel retrieval-augmented framework for scalable sentiment lexicon construction in South African low-resource languages. Moreover, we develop and release an extensive multilingual sentiment lexicon covering multiple South African languages, enabling reproducible research. Finally, we perform the first comprehensive evaluation of AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa using a lexicon-expanded resource focused on isiXhosa and isiZulu.

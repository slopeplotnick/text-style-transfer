Taken together, these findings collectively affirm that OMNIGUARD demonstrates substantial cross-modal generalization. It can effectively extrapolate safety-related insights from specific modalities to untrained ones while also developing modality-invariant semantic representations of hazardous content. Consequently, we present OMNIGUARD-7B and OMNIGUARD-3B, the pioneering omni-modal guardrails designed to process a comprehensive array of data types, facilitated by an extensive safety fine-tuning dataset. OMNIGUARD represents a unified omni-modal solution that can moderate and reason about unsafe content across various data settings. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that OMNIGUARD-7B consistently surpasses other guardrail models in performance across all data modalities, while OMNIGUARD-3B achieves competitive results against large-scale language models and multi-modal language models such as Qwen3-235B and Qwen3-VL-235B. These results underscore the robust safety detection and reasoning capabilities of our system, affirming the feasibility of a unified guardrail framework with omni-modal understanding and interpretability. Future research will explore increasingly complex and cross-modal safety scenarios to advance omni-modal safeguarding in emerging language models. However, despite OMNIGUARD’s demonstrated proficiency in omni-modal safety reasoning and consistent performance across diverse modalities, several limitations persist. Incorporating reasoning paths enhances interpretability and fidelity, but also increases inference latency due to additional reasoning generation processes, leading to higher computational costs compared to streamlined, binary-classification guardrail systems. Therefore, it is imperative to explore the trade-off between the depth of safety reasoning and inference latency within different use cases to optimize safety robustness and efficiency. Furthermore, the scarcity of publicly available datasets tailored for cross-modal safety training indicates considerable potential for enhancement in moderating complex multimodal scenarios. We anticipate that future research will bolster the reliability and robustness of these systems, improving their efficacy in safeguarding against risks across diverse modalities.

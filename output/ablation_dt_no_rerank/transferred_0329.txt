The rapid advance of large language models (LLMs) is accelerating the discovery of innovative applications geared towards societal benefit. As LLMs increasingly mediate online communication, their potential to foster empathy and constructive dialogue emerges as a significant frontier for responsible AI research. This study investigates whether LLMs can transcend their traditional role of merely identifying harmful content in conversations to actively de-escalate conflicts and resolve disputes in digital communication environments. Our framework delineates mediation into two subtasks: judgment, where the model evaluates the fairness and emotional tenor of a discourse, and steering, where it crafts empathetic, de-escalatory messages to guide participants toward resolution. To evaluate the efficacy of these models in mediating disputes, a substantial Reddit-based dataset has been constructed, alongside a multipronged evaluation strategy that includes principle-based scoring, user simulations, and comparative analysis with human-mediated outcomes. Results indicate that subscription-based models significantly outperform open-source models in reasoning capabilities and conflict resolution efficacy. This study demonstrates that while LLMs exhibit significant potential, they also possess inherent limitations in fostering harmonious online interactions. Beyond traditional natural language processing tasks such as translation or summarization, LLMs are increasingly applied to domains including education, healthcare, and mental well-being. As these systems integrate into virtual environments where individuals engage in dialogue and debate, a novel conceptual development emerges: envisioning LLMs not merely as tools for task automation but as facilitators of more empathetic and equitable interactions. However, a significant challenge remains: the prevalence of online hostilities, often manifesting as contentious exchanges characterized by personal affronts and escalating emotional tensions. The absence of physical presence and the limitations in discerning non-verbal social cues exacerbate these conflicts.

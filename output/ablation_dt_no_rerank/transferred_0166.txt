Recent licensing models such as the Nwulite Obodo Open Data License (NOODL) and the Esethu License illustrate a significant paradigm shift. NOODL, for example, differentiates between users in Africa or developing countries and those outside these regions, enabling tailored benefit-sharing and necessitating that adaptations be shared locally. Conversely, the Esethu License reinvests licensing revenue directly into expanding resources and supporting local employment, thereby maintaining both community agency and resource sustainability. Indeed, as experience with Indigenous and low-resource language data has demonstrated, unrestricted "openness" can reinforce existing inequities if not balanced with robust ethical safeguards. The evolving discourse on open data practices acknowledges that responsible dissemination requires more than a generic license; it demands attention to consent, privacy, fairness, and mechanisms for sustained community benefits. Our choice to utilize the CC BY 4.0 license for Swivuriso was rooted in a commitment to openness, yet it simultaneously highlighted the inadequacies of standard licenses for datasets from African and low-resource languages. Emerging frameworks like NOODL and the Esethu License underscore the field's movement towards more ethical and equitable governance. Consequently, in the realm of dataset publication, it is no longer sufficient to merely assert the presence of a standard open license; authors should also articulate their approach to ethical, just, and community-empowering data stewardship. The Swivuriso initiative represents a significant step forward in advancing automatic speech recognition and broader speech technologies for South African languages. Encompassing seven linguistically and geographically diverse languages and incorporating both domain-specific and culturally grounded unscripted speech, the dataset supports a wide range of applications—including health communication, agricultural advisory tools, and multilingual spoken language technologies. Designed to facilitate benchmark-driven evaluations, such as the enhancement of word error rate and related performance metrics, the dataset also aids the development of speech systems that are culturally informed and socially responsive.

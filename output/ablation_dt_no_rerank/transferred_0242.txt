Li et al. (2025) introduces feedback-guided test-time training, where external auxiliary systems provide weak supervision to refine predictions during inference. Moreover, Zuo et al. (2025) presents Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL), which employs multiple rollouts, consolidates predictions through majority voting, and utilizes the consensus as a reward signal to adjust policies. While these methodologies are effective, they suffer from inefficiency, as they require many redundant rollouts to stabilize consensus estimates. More recently, Wang et al. (2025) demonstrates that reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards significantly enhances learning outcomes with a minimal number of training examples, underscoring the potential of efficient RL in test-time scenarios. Building on this, our research introduces a statistically grounded early-stopping mechanism to optimize rollout efficiency. In the realm of reinforcement learning, policy optimization is critical, particularly for language models. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is a prevalent approach for stabilizing policy updates under constraints, especially in contexts involving reinforcement learning from human feedback. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) advances this approach by incorporating group-normalized advantages, enhancing stability under limited feedback. OptPO leverages the flexibility of these elements, providing a plug-and-play rollout allocation mechanism that boosts efficiency without altering the optimization framework. In summary, existing research has established test-time training as a powerful framework for enhancing model adaptability during inference, employing techniques like uncertainty reduction, assisted learning, and reinforcement learning. However, current methodologies face inefficiencies due to excessive rollout requirements. Our paper addresses this by presenting OptPO, a method that reduces computational demands while maintaining robust performance, thereby advancing and refining contemporary test-time adaptation techniques.

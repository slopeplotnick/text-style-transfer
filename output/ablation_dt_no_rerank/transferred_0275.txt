We begin by visually comparing the metrics of our generated files to those in the EMOPIA files, using t-tests to evaluate whether the generated data maintains the statistically significant differences observed in the EMOPIA data between high and low valence and arousal. To generate music conditioned on a specific emotion inferred from text, we employ an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture. The textual emotion signal is captured using a RoBERTa-large encoder, contrastively fine-tuned to better capture nuanced emotional differences across quadrants. The decoder, a Transformer-based module, consists of three layers with four attention heads each, specifically designed to model the temporal and harmonic structure of symbolic music. Figure 2 provides an overview of the model architecture. The encoder is initialized from RoBERTa-large and fine-tuned on the GoEmotions dataset, where the original 27 emotion labels are replaced with their corresponding quadrant labels. In each batch, examples are drawn from multiple quadrants, treating pairs within the same quadrant as positive and pairs from different quadrants as negative. This in-batch formulation enables efficient training with numerous negatives, resulting in an embedding space where quadrants form distinct and separable clusters. After contrastive fine-tuning, the encoder is frozen and serves as the text-conditioning module in our generative model. The pretrained RoBERTa encoder initially produces highly overlapping representations across the four emotion quadrants; however, the contrastively fine-tuned encoder yields noticeably more separated clusters. This confirms that contrastive training strengthens the emotional structure of the embedding space, while still acknowledging the inherently gradual and subjective nature of affective categories. For detailed t-SNE visualizations of the story embeddings before and after contrastive training, refer to Figure 4 in the Appendix (Section C). Nevertheless, the EMOPIA dataset contains only 1,078 MIDI samples, which is insufficient for a Transformer decoder to effectively learn the dependencies between MIDI tokens. Therefore, we pre-train the decoder on the larger GiantMIDI-Piano dataset. During pre-training, zero tensors are inputted in place of text embeddings, and the decoder is trained using a causal language modeling objective, allowing it to learn the probability distributions over MIDI tokens in an auto-regressive manner.

Referring Expression Comprehension (REC), which seeks to identify a target based on a natural language description, is a fundamental task in vision-language research. Recently, advances in this field have elevated the state of the art, progressing beyond mere lexical matching to employing richer semantic interpretations, such as constructing compositional referring expressions and integrating external knowledge. Generalized Referring Expression Comprehension (GREC) expands on this by interpreting expressions that may refer to zero or multiple objects, exemplified by directives like "Pick up those three boxes," thus better reflecting real-world discourse where referents often may be ambiguous, absent, or occluded. Nevertheless, this research typically depends on extensive datasets of annotated real-world images, posing significant cost challenges. Online crowdsourcing platforms, however, facilitate the alleviation of these financial burdens, offering controlled and reproducible environments for visual grounding research. Yet, challenges arise when models need to perceive dynamic scenes, maintain conversational coherence, and interact over multiple dialogue turns. Under these circumstances, existing methodologies display notable limitations. Recently, a study introduced the first GREC benchmark utilizing multi-turn dialogue within a Minecraft building environment, highlighting the significant challenges this poses to classical transformer-based neural networks and Large Vision-Language Models. Moreover, the process of dialogue annotation is resource-intensive, resulting in a dearth of in-domain training data and making large-scale dataset acquisition a long-term goal. Thus, the proposed data augmentation framework seeks to enhance grounded comprehension and stepwise reasoning by synthesizing concise descriptions and intricate conversations. Although previous research on AI-driven data generation is pertinent, its application to GREC remains challenging due to the need for spatial modeling and visual information integration.

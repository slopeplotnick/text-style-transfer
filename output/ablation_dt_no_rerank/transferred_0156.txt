While humans engage in scientific practice, they also examine the methodology of science itself, raising philosophical questions about the optimal way to conduct scientific inquiry. Moreover, the examination of what constitutes an effective scientific method remains pivotal to this discourse. The evolution of scientific discipline marks a transition from mere observation to probing the relationship between theoretical models and empirical data. Additionally, exploration extends to the collaborative mechanisms within the scientific enterprise, as diverse social interactions lay the groundwork for established standards and norms, which may later be challenged. Insiders often overlook faults or epistemic errors, as these have become normalized within the community. Bond et al. (2007) suggest researchers may exhibit excessive confidence in their knowledge—a phenomenon scrutinized by Thomas Kuhn, who termed these collective frameworks as "paradigms." According to Kuhn, paradigms encompass established beliefs, methodologies, rules, and practices that gain consensus within the scientific community, as exemplified by theories such as evolution or plate tectonics.

Benchmarking LLM inference power consumption fundamentally differs from evaluating throughput or latency. Whereas traditional performance metrics focus on speed and efficiency, energy consumption is affected by hardware diversity, software intricacies, and workload dynamics—all of which vary significantly across different deployment contexts. Existing LLM benchmarks often fail to reflect this variability, typically relying on fixed configurations, static model sizes, and costly instrumentation. As illustrated in Figure 1, TokenPowerBench addresses these shortcomings through a three-layer architecture: Configuration, Execution & Measurement, and Report Generation. It provides a modular, configurable, fully reproducible, and scalable benchmarking methodology tailored to modern LLM inference needs. The initial step in using TokenPowerBench involves setting up the test environment by selecting the model, inference engine, and relevant prompts, thus ensuring comprehensive test readiness. To facilitate this setup, TokenPowerBench offers three plug-and-play modules: a model pool (with any compatible LLM), a prompt-dataset menu (with options like Alpaca, LongBench, or custom prompts), and an inference-engine selector (including vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, Transformers, or DeepSpeed). The Model Pool includes diverse architectural designs to evaluate how different design choices impact energy consumption profiles. This ranges from standard decoder-only transformers such as the LLaMA series to Mixture-of-Experts models like Mixtral, which differ in parameter activation and memory access patterns. - The benchmark considers a broad spectrum of model sizes, from smaller models with fewer than 1 billion parameters—suitable for a single consumer-grade GPU—to frontier-scale models like Llama 3-405B, which require multi-GPU and/or distributed node inference. This comprehensive approach allows for the study of energy consumption dynamics as model size and hardware complexity increase. Furthermore, the configuration of the prompt-dataset interface significantly influences power consumption during inference. TokenPowerBench incorporates two principal datasets to capture this variability: Alpaca, with concise, chat-style prompts, and Long-Bench, which involves extended contexts of up to 10,000 tokens. Moreover, users have the flexibility to integrate their proprietary data, formatted in CSV or other compatible formats.

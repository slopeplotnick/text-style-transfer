Predicting stock prices is inherently challenging due to market volatility and real-time event sensitivity. The emergence of large language models (LLMs) has introduced new opportunities for text-based stock forecasting. While LLMs demonstrate strong text comprehension abilities, effectively applying them to complex real-world tasks often requires specialized memory modules to consistently and stably utilize relevant historical information. Considerable research has focused on developing such memory systems. However, when dealing with the vast and chaotic volume of online news data, these systems face significant difficulties, as this information does not provide straightforward answers and is filled with irrelevant noise. The crucial challenge lies in autonomously identifying and extracting the key information and underlying logic influencing stock price movements from unstructured text. To address this issue, this paper introduces an innovative dual-layer memory framework. In the knowledge construction phase, the framework systematically organizes events along two dimensions: horizontal event consolidation integrates key daily event content to provide a comprehensive view, and longitudinal event tracing tracks the evolution and transformation of events over time. This approach facilitates the construction of a temporally-aware structured event knowledge base that captures and reflects market expectation shifts. Moreover, by examining the correlation between event patterns and stock price fluctuations, the framework establishes an additional knowledge base dedicated to analyzing this relationship, achieving systematic and autonomous knowledge organization. In the knowledge application phase, two key steps are involved: retrieval and reasoning. First, the model uses recent events to search for analogous historical scenarios within its memory, enhancing current decision-making. Subsequently, it integrates recent events, incremental information, and past exemplars to forecast stock price fluctuations. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that this system outperforms previous models in predictive accuracy and offers superior interpretability, distinctly elucidating the causal linkage between events and price variations, as well as providing explanations for these phenomena.

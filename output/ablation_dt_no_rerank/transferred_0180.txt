The discussed language acquisition experiments aim to incorporate explicit grammatical knowledge into a lexicon. Consequently, the daughter agent can use this acquired knowledge to engage in dialogue with the mother agent. To achieve this, the system employs statistical machine learning techniques, resulting in a precisely defined grammatical framework. This enhances existing systems reliant on statistical methods and neural networks by providing a more interpretable, rule-based approach to language modeling. Furthermore, by applying Zipf's law (Mandelbrot 1954, Zipf 1949), this study conducts an essential robustness evaluation of the feasibility of implementing a multi-agent simulation of first language acquisition. Importantly, the objective is not merely to classify function and content words but to utilize this classification as a means to assess the validity of this innovative approach to modeling first language acquisition. The use of Zipf's law offers a solid and dependable methodology for evaluating system performance and components. This approach is well established; for instance, Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) have leveraged Zipf's law to construct a multi-agent framework for modeling language evolution. Recent investigations by Dębowski (2021), Takahashi and Tanaka-Ishii (2019), and Takamichi et al. (2024) further bolster this body of research. Crucially, experiments within this study validate all components necessary to facilitate a multi-agent model of first language acquisition. More specifically, the acquisition of function and content words is achieved through a hybrid language acquisition strategy based on statistical tendencies that underpin the learning of discrete grammatical rules. Therefore, these findings support the modeling of more complex grammatical constructs and affirm the feasibility of the MODOMA research agenda. Building on these findings, the investigation by Shakouri et al. (2025) demonstrates the acquisition of grammatical categories, such as nouns, verbs, and prepositions, through unsupervised methodologies. This study presents two experiments illustrating this approach: the initial simulation, detailed in Sections 6 and 7, used a training dataset curated by the mother agent to determine the necessary configurations for acquiring this linguistic phenomenon. The subsequent experiment, employing a novel test dataset generated by the adult language model, validated this configuration, as elaborated in Section 8. The results indicate that the MODOMA system is adept at acquiring these grammatical categories and accurately representing them within the grammatical framework of the daughter agent.

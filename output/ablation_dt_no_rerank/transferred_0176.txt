The system operates by enabling the mother agent to engage in interactions utilizing its existing grammatical knowledge, thereby presenting a novel approach. This is because, unlike traditional computational models of language acquisition that predominantly rely on inputting corpora (e.g., Alishahi and Stevenson 2008, Alishahi and Chrupala 2012, Conner et al. 2009, Matusevych et al. 2013), our approach diverges significantly. In this paper, we are not confining the term "language model" to its conventional use in current NLP; instead, we employ it in a broader sense. Typically, it refers to neural models trained for next-word prediction, such as GPT and BERT (cf. Brown et al. 2020, Radford et al. 2018, Devlin et al. 2019). However, we include rule-based approaches, statistical methods, and artificial neural networks, as all address common NLP tasks. This distinction is important because the mother agent in our multi-agent system relies on explicit linguistic rules, while the language acquiring agent uses statistical methods to infer a rule-based model for linguistic phenomena. The study presented here investigates whether the MODOMA daughter agent can acquire and represent functional and content categories by integrating the Zipfian distribution into the MODOMA language acquisition system. Specifically, we assess whether a multi-agent language acquisition system can derive functional and content categories from machine-generated utterances and represent them through a rule-based grammar. To address this, we examine whether these utterances exhibit statistical patterns akin to those in human language and determine the amount of input data necessary to detect such patterns. We then explore whether these statistical tendencies can be transformed into grammatical rules. Finally, we validate our findings by assessing the alignment of the inferred categories with the original grammar and evaluating the system's capability in autonomously acquiring structured linguistic knowledge in an unsupervised manner.

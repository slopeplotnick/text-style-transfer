Jack Lu*, Ryan Teehan*, Jinran Jin, and Mengye Ren Agentic Learning AI Lab, New York University {yl11330, rst306, jj3007, mengye}@nyu.edu https://agenticlearning.ai/llm-verification. On November 20, 2025, they provided insights into how large language models (LLMs) function both as problem solvers and solution verifiers. The role of verifiers is crucial in enhancing solver performance by selecting high-quality answers from a pool of candidates. Nevertheless, previous research has largely overlooked the interaction between solver and verifier, focusing predominantly on self-verification while seldom analyzing how verifiers assess outputs from models within their own family or across different model families. Furthermore, although modern LLMs undergo extensive post-training procedures, the impact of such processes on their verification capabilities remains underexplored. Therefore, this study systematically examined 37 models spanning multiple families, sizes, and variations between base and post-trained forms. These models were evaluated on nine benchmarks encompassing logical reasoning, structured puzzles, symbolic computation, mathematics, commonsense reasoning, factual recall, and domain knowledge. Additionally, the study compared self-verification with verification within the same family and across different families. To complement this analysis, a new metric—verifier gain—was introduced and empirically validated to predict performance improvements from test-time verifier-based rejection sampling. The study also analyzed how verifier gain and false positive rates scale with model size and post-training while discerning differences in dataset verifiability. Findings indicate that cross-family verification is particularly effective; post-training diminishes self-improvement but bolsters cross-family gains. Notably, tasks involving mathematical and logical reasoning exhibit the highest inherent verifiability. Consequently, problem-solving with LLMs has evolved from solely querying models for solutions to a more integrated system where models both solve and verify. This paradigm shift in test-time solution verification encompasses simple strategies like generating multiple candidate solutions and employing a verifier as a filter (Zhao et al., 2025), as well as more complex iterative refinement methods (Madaan et al., 2023). Through generative verification at test time, LLMs can address more complex problems than when utilized independently (Cobbe et al., 2021; Lightman et al., 2024).

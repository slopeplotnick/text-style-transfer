As the use of AI tools powered by large language models becomes more widespread, ensuring their safe deployment has become a top priority. This need is especially critical as these models are introduced in multilingual and multimodal environments, where diverse linguistic and cultural contexts can pose new risks. Therefore, it is essential to develop safety guardrails capable of effectively filtering harmful or unsafe outputs across a vast array of languages and user contexts. Without such protective measures, the global deployment of these models risks marginalizing or excluding a significant portion of the population. Most existing literature on model safety has primarily focused on high-resource languages such as English, Chinese, Spanish, German, and French. However, the lack of linguistic data for the majority of the world's languages presents substantial challenges in ensuring safety and accuracy in multilingual contexts. Although some evaluations exist for testing model efficacy across various languages, they are insufficiently comprehensive, especially for low-resource languages, and do not fully capture the diversity of global users. Moreover, efforts to create training data specifically for low-resource languages to develop safety systems remain largely unexplored. Recent initiatives have concentrated on curating training datasets for well-represented languages using a combination of original and translated data; nonetheless, these do not adequately address the need for inclusive safety systems across a genuinely diverse linguistic spectrum. Furthermore, a significant challenge posed by current safety systems is their high computational demands. These systems are often too large, slow, and resource-intensive, making them impractical for use on edge devices or in low-power environments. This underscores the necessity for more lightweight and efficient systems that can reliably protect both the language models and the users from potential harm.

We recruited 17 volunteers (P1-P17) through school posters and random invitations on the street. While the study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB), guardian consent was required for all underage volunteers. All interview materials were securely stored, ensuring that no personally identifiable information was involved. Most volunteers were familiar with short story creation, self-assessing their proficiency at an average of 3.8 on a 5-point Likert scale. Additionally, all volunteers had previous experience in storytelling, either through handwriting or digital office software. Consequently, they were divided into groups of approximately four, each accompanied by a staff member. The workshop lasted around 2.5 hours, during which volunteers were encouraged to verbalize their thought processes using a "think-aloud" approach. This enabled us to capture and analyze both their initial and final outputs, as well as the steps in their creative process. Moreover, follow-up interviews were conducted with four volunteers. By analyzing the workshop recordings and interview data, we identified and categorized four distinct modes of interaction observed during the story creation process. The workshop flow is outlined as follows: 1) Introduction and Overview (15 minutes). Staff members provided an overview of the workshop's objectives and structure, highlighting the application of the four foundational units through illustrative examples. Volunteers were reminded to adopt a "think-aloud" approach to ensure transparency and reflection on their decision-making throughout the creative process. 2) Group Discussion and Story Framework Creation (60 minutes). Here, staff distributed cards to volunteers who engaged in collaborative discussions to develop their own story framework, employing the four foundational units. Throughout the process, volunteers were encouraged to articulate their thoughts and decision-making processes, with staff circulating among groups to offer timely guidance and support as necessary. 3) Story Sharing and Feedback (45 minutes). Volunteers detailed their use of the four foundational units in story construction, subsequently receiving formative feedback from peers and staff, with a focus on creativity and structural coherence. Finally, 4) Informal Interviews with Volunteers (30 minutes). The staff selected four volunteers (P1-P4) for informal interviews to explore their creative experiences further.

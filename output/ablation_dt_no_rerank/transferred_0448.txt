In this section, we will exemplify the previously discussed process through a practical example using the CelebA dataset [Liu et al., 2015]. This dataset comprises over 200,000 images of celebrities' faces, each annotated with binary attributes that describe various facial features and accessories such as whether the individual is smiling, wearing glasses, of young age, or appears attractive. We will examine two tasks: determining whether an individual in an image is classified as Young and whether they are deemed Attractive. We next turn our attention to the images in Figure 6. Originating with the initial image on the left, the figure illustrates various modified versions of the same image, all adhering to the intervention do(Young = 0). The initial counterfactual image was generated using a specific methodology. While systems that rely solely on visual evaluation may be misleading [Adebayo et al., 2018], saliency maps remain a prevalent technique for attribution-based explanations of image classification processes. Therefore, we compare our causal concept-based explanations with those generated by Grad-CAM [Selvaraju et al., 2017], utilizing the implementation provided by Gildenblat [2021]. Consider first the analysis of Figure 7, intended to elucidate why the two images on the far left of Figure 5 were classified as young. Interestingly, the analysis reveals that the chin area was instrumental for the male subject's classification, whereas for the female subject, the classifier predominantly focused on the area around the eyes. Subsequently, Figure 8 examines the classification of the subject depicted in Figure 6. The first image was accurately classified as young, and the Grad-CAM analysis indicates that the classifier concentrated on the area surrounding the eyes to reach this conclusion. Conversely, for the image on the right, which was incorrectly classified, Grad-CAM shows that the classifier focused on the forehead and chin areas. As Grad-CAM provides non-causal explanations, it cannot elucidate outcomes resulting from alterations in the input data. Instead, it merely identifies the regions of the image most salient in the classifier's decision-making process. Moreover, since Grad-CAM explanations are based on pixel levels, they do not impart insights into why these highlighted areas are considered significant.

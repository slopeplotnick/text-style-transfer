In AI systems with multiple components collaborating on complex tasks, the role of large language models (LLMs) is crucial in shaping subsequent decision-making processes. However, potential bias towards initial inputs could disrupt the entire system's operation. Therefore, addressing this bias is essential to ensure fairness and transparency. This study faces two primary limitations. Firstly, although efforts were made to standardize the length of each article triplet, an explicit examination of evaluation metrics' sensitivity to document length was not conducted. Since document length can impact the absolute values of ROUGE-L, BERTScore, and SummaC, future research could categorize datasets by length or conduct length-controlled analyses to better isolate the effects of input order on summary similarity in LLMs. Secondly, our findings indicated that the absolute scores from evaluation metrics were relatively low, highlighting a broader challenge with reference-free evaluation methods in abstractive summarization, especially when comparing lengthy documents to concise summaries. Thus, future research should expand the analysis beyond abortion to include other socially and politically contentious topics like gun control, LGBTQ+ rights, and immigration, assessing if the identified order effects are consistent across various issues. Moreover, to thoroughly evaluate the persistence of primacy effects across different model architectures, it would be beneficial to assess a range of LLM families, such as GPT and Claude. Incorporating additional evaluation metrics, including measures of positional stance and indicators of social polarization, could provide a more nuanced understanding of order-sensitive behaviors in LLM-generated summaries. Our findings revealed a discernible primacy effect in multi-document summarization, where generated summaries align predominantly with the initially encountered document, especially at the semantic level. These results support prior evidence that the sequence of presented information significantly influences model output. Such order sensitivity poses challenges for systems like AI summaries or agentic AI pipelines, where earlier documents disproportionately affect subsequent processes. Consequently, addressing this order bias is imperative for developing more equitable, transparent, and reliable LLM-based systems.

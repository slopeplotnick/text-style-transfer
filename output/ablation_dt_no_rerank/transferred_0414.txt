Programmatic Editing. The utilization of python-pptx for programmatic edits in PowerPoint presentations is well-documented in prior research [9, 20, 25, 44, 45]. This approach scripts edits by generating code, making it particularly effective for repetitive, clearly defined, and content-centric tasks such as performing automated find-and-replace actions across all slides or translating text. However, it lacks the fine-grained control needed for complex structural modifications, such as altering slide masters, themes, or specific layout geometries. Direct XML Editing. To overcome these programmatic limitations in visual and structural editing scenarios, PPTPilot has been equipped to read and modify raw OOXML files, such as slide1.xml and theme.xml. This capability offers precision for structured contexts, allowing for direct manipulation of element properties like specific positions. Since OOXML encodes the majority of presentation information, using XML provides an interface well-aligned with existing editing methodologies. Nonetheless, the extensive context and stringent format requirements inherent to XML can hinder precise edits, especially across numerous slides, where the programmatic approach proves more reliable. Skill Routing. To determine the most suitable editing approach for a user's query, we utilize a mechanism that routes queries to the appropriate method. Upon receiving instructions, this system analyzes both the user prompt and the presentation's structure, including its visual and textual elements, to decide whether to employ programmatic edits or direct XML manipulation.

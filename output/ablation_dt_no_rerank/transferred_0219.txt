In this work, we present Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), a reusable and modular framework for enhancing physics-based character animation via score-matching. Once trained, this motion prior empowers the development of control policies for a variety of tasks, promoting natural behaviors that align with the reference distribution of the training data. Moreover, our priors can be effectively used without needing to retain the original motion dataset. At test-time, diffusion techniques like classifier-free guidance can be employed to modify the base motion prior, thereby generating novel stylistic priors that enable agents to execute tasks in specific styles. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our method across a diverse range of scenarios, from single-character movement to human-object interactions. SMPs can be constructed from a wide spectrum of datasets, ranging from just a few seconds of motion clips to extensive datasets with up to 20 hours of motion data. In our experiments, we show that employing reinforcement learning with score distillation sampling (SDS) objectives generates motion sequences of comparable quality to those developed through adversarial imitation learning, without the need for continuous prior updates during policy training. Furthermore, SMP demonstrates higher sample efficiency in numerous scenarios, partly due to its more stable stationary reward function compared to adversarial reward models. However, as with many mode-seeking objectives, policies trained with SMP may occasionally be subject to mode collapse, yielding a limited set of behaviors from the original dataset. While our primary focus is on humanoid motion control, we believe SMP holds potential for broader applications in other control problems. We hope this work opens new directions toward building general motion tools that enable more adaptable controllers for character animation and robotics beyond mere motion tracking.

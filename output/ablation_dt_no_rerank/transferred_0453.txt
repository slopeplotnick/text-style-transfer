Overall, these results underscore that reasoning-based safety alignment not only enhances the performance of omnimodal guardrails across various modality settings but also facilitates improved understanding and generalization in complex cross-modal safety scenarios that label-only supervision fails to address. To assess whether safety knowledge learned from observed modalities can be generalized to unobserved ones, we conducted cross-modal training and evaluation. Specifically, for each configuration, we trained OMNIGUARD using data from three modalities for training and evaluation of seen modalities, while leaving one modality out for evaluation of unseen modalities. We present the average F1 scores and accuracy across all evaluations of seen and unseen modalities in Table 5. Two main findings emerge from these experiments. First, robust cross-modal transferability is evident across all four modalities. The performance on unseen modalities remains comparable to that on seen modalities (79.4 vs. 81.8 F1 on average), indicating OMNIGUARD's successful learning of modality-invariant safety representations. This suggests that harmful semantic patterns can be effectively aligned and learned across text, image, video, and audio inputs, allowing OMNIGUARD to gain generalizable safety reasoning ability across modalities. Second, a noteworthy exception appears in the audio modality, where the F1 score for seen modality (64.6%) is slightly lower than for unseen modality (65.1%). This anomaly arises from the OMNIGUARD variant trained on the text-excluded split, exhibiting degraded performance on the WildGuard-TTS benchmark. WildGuardMix-TTS, the auditory version of WildGuardMix, is constructed via a text-to-speech model. Training without textual data also leads to degradation in the audio setting. This reveals that semantically equivalent content originating from different modalities (e.g., harmful text versus its spoken version) can mutually influence each other during safety alignment. Thus, knowledge acquired from one form can be transferred to another semantically invariant form.

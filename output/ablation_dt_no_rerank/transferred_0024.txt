By synthesizing multi-dimensional CoT data, we undertook the training of the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model using a two-step procedural methodology comparable to the LLM base model used in our investigation. Specifically, performance metrics of models based on VLM versus LLM show that the LLM-based approach significantly outperforms the VLM-based method on both general and complex datasets, particularly in terms of macro-F1 scores. This indicates that, with equivalent CoT capabilities, LLMs possess superior reasoning and knowledge capabilities compared to VLMs. However, VLMs demonstrate better proficiency in processing visual details, as they avoid the loss of critical information that can occur during image-to-text transcription. Through a tri-phasic developmental framework, our LORE model has undergone systematic evolution characterized by progressive refinements across five key dimensions: core goals, model paradigm and backbone, data and prompting strategies, evaluation frameworks, and application scenarios. This multi-phase approach outlines a trajectory of innovation, combining advanced architectural innovations, high-quality datasets, and applied methodologies to optimize search result relevancy in online retail environments. Initially, the primary challenge was accurately deciphering e-commerce queries, particularly identifying non-standard product nomenclature. To overcome this obstacle, we developed extensive, high-quality e-commerce datasets and employed In-Context Learning (ICL) to systematically infuse the model with domain-specific knowledge, thereby enhancing its capacity for e-commerce semantic comprehension.

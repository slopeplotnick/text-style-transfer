Despite significant advancements in memory augmentation technologies, general architectures face particular challenges when adapting these technologies for stock prediction tasks. Firstly, in memory representation, existing systems struggle with achieving fine-grained decomposition of event sequences. Secondly, in retrieval mechanisms, they lack effective capability for matching historical scenario patterns. Thirdly, in temporal processing, they are inept at managing the unique dynamic evolution characteristic of financial markets. Consequently, these deficiencies highlight the urgent need for developing specialized memory systems specifically tailored for the financial domain. This chapter examines the pertinent research across two foundational domains: the progression from historical pattern matching to the adoption of memory mechanisms, and the advancing paradigm of event-driven prediction. Although significant advancements have been made in these areas, a substantial challenge persists in the integration of structured event knowledge with traceable memory mechanisms—a critical gap that establishes the central focus of our investigation. Historically, stock price prediction has predominantly relied on identifying patterns in historical data analogous to current scenarios in order to inform predictive models. This concept aligns with the functioning of memory systems in large language models, whose primary objective is to effectively store and retrieve previous experiences or contextual information. Initially, researchers employed statistical methodologies adept at identifying numerical patterns, yet these methods were limited in their capacity to process more intricate market information. Subsequently, the advent of deep learning facilitated the employment of models such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Transformer architectures, which enabled the automatic extraction of temporal features. Transformer-based models demonstrated exceptional proficiency in identifying long-term dependencies through self-attention mechanisms, thereby significantly advancing the domain. Moreover, the FinSeer framework integrated large language models (LLMs) to retrieve numerically analogous historical data sequences, thus facilitating more profound feature extraction and analysis.

In recent years, the technology underpinning Large Language Models (LLMs) has advanced significantly and is now widely employed across various domains, including intelligent interactions and scientific research support. Specifically, in the healthcare sector, LLMs have demonstrated considerable potential in assisting disease diagnosis and enhancing diagnostic and therapeutic processes. In the realm of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), the fundamental clinical concept is articulated as "Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment" (SDT). Physicians engage in the integration of multifaceted data to ascertain the patient's condition and, subsequently, develop a customized treatment strategy, adhering to principles such as "individualized treatment for the same disease." However, while there exist TCM-specific LLMs, such as Zhongjing and HuatuoGPT, their application in clinical settings remains challenging due to the complex and individualized nature of TCM methodologies. Critically, there is a significant issue in the absence of comprehensive evaluation benchmarks to assess the efficacy of these models in the clinical decision-making continuum and specialized metrics for evaluating their reasoning capabilities. Therefore, there is an imperative need for researchers and organizations to develop a dataset capable of rigorously and comprehensively evaluating the applicability of these models within the context of TCM. Such a dataset would facilitate the enhancement of model algorithms, assess their performance, and ultimately enable their integration into practical medical scenarios. Nevertheless, the development of such a dataset is considerably challenging due to the inherently individualized, holistic, and diverse nature of TCM, which demands a nuanced approach to patient care and diagnosis. For instance, individuals presenting with the same pathological condition may exhibit varying symptomatology due to factors such as individual constitution, disease progression, and lifestyle habits, thus complicating the development of standardized model evaluation methods. TCM diagnosis is inherently a holistic process that employs diverse diagnostic methodologies to amass information, ascertain etiology, and formulate therapeutic strategies. Consequently, the dataset must encompass a broad spectrum of criteria. Moreover, the frequent concurrence of multiple health issues in patients, along with the subjective nature of diagnostic methodologies, can further contribute to variability in evaluation outcomes. In conclusion, these complexities significantly impede the development of a comprehensive dataset for evaluating the efficacy of TCM-centric LLMs in real-world clinical scenarios.

A Software Product Line (SPL) involves transforming a collection of related software systems that share common features into a framework, thereby enabling more efficient and rapid creation of new software products. This transformation process, entailing the migration of an existing code base into an SPL, is inherently complex and time-consuming. Moreover, it may significantly interfere with well-established software development practices within the organization, often requiring substantial adjustments to routine procedures. This paper outlines the preliminary phases undertaken to commence the practical implementation of this transformative process. The study is an integral component of a collaborative initiative with ITK - Predict and Decide, a small and medium-sized enterprise that has embarked on the conversion of its code base into an SPL. Our role as a research team was to facilitate and provide expert guidance throughout this migration process. The code base is structured around a platform called Cross, which functions as an extended framework where software reuse is common. Although this framework resembles an ad hoc SPL, it lacks the models and processes characteristic of a traditional SPL. Therefore, to facilitate the developers' transition, a plan for gradual reorganization of both their code base and engineering practices has been delineated. Initially, we conducted an investigation into the sentiments of the company's personnel regarding the forthcoming changes, offering them guidance to ease the transition. This study articulates the insights garnered from this preliminary inquiry. We engaged with company stakeholders to ascertain their perspectives on existing operational methodologies, their aspirations concerning the SPL, and any apprehensions they harbored. Notably, most stakeholders expressed readiness to transition to an SPL and showed enthusiasm in actively participating in this initiative. Developers, in particular, viewed the transition as an engaging technical and managerial undertaking. However, individuals involved in business operations demonstrated a more cautious stance, voicing concerns over potential complications in maintenance and possible constraints on creativity, especially regarding user interface design.

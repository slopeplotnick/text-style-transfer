We propose a scalable approach for constructing multilingual safety guardrails that effectively generalize across a diverse array of languages, utilizing only a few high-resource languages. Specifically, we have developed a lightweight safety guardrail model with merely 0.5B parameters, supporting over 100 languages, and trained exclusively on data derived from just 13 high-resource languages. Our analysis systematically examines inter-cluster and intra-cluster cross-lingual transfer from high-resource to low-resource languages. Early content moderation systems predominantly concentrated on English; for instance, Google's Perspective API (Lees et al. 2022) initially supported only English text. Subsequently, works such as Aegis-Defensive (Ghosh et al. 2025), WalledGuard (Gupta et al. 2024), and WildGuard (Han et al. 2024) demonstrated promising performance in identifying and mitigating harmful content in English. However, recent studies (Wang et al. 2024; Nicholas and Bhatia 2023; Deng et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2024) have uncovered that popular LLMs tend to generate significantly more unsafe or harmful responses for non-English queries. Furthermore, prompts in low-resource languages are more prone to bypass safety mechanisms. Therefore, new developments have specifically targeted the weaknesses of English-centric safety measures in multilingual contexts. Approaches such as MrGuard (Yang et al. 2025) and X-Guard (Upadhayay, Behzadan, and D 2025) rely on data of lower fidelity, such as translated or synthetic data, which inadequately capture the linguistic and cultural nuances in low-resource languages. More recent advancements, including LlamaGuard3-8B (2024) and NemoGuard-8B-content-safety (2025), are based on the Llama3.1-8B architecture. DuoGuard (2025) employs an innovative two-agent training methodology for a compact safety mechanism, using the Qwen2.5-0.5B base model, marking a significant advance in small-scale multilingual safety guardrails. To our knowledge, PolyGuard (Kumar et al. 2025) stands as the most advanced safety moderation model, released alongside the largest multinational safety corpus to date, known as PolyGuardMix. Nonetheless, even PolyGuard's scope, covering only 17 languages, underscores the prevalent focus of current tools on a limited set of major languages.

Editing portrait videos is a challenging task that necessitates flexible yet precise control over a wide range of modifications, such as changes in appearance, expression edits, or the addition of objects. The primary difficulty is preserving the subject's original temporal behaviors, requiring that each edited frame remains precisely synchronized with its corresponding source frame. To address this, we introduce SyncLoRA, a method for editing portraits in videos that excels in delivering high-quality visual modifications alongside accurate temporal synchronization and identity consistency. This approach employs an image-to-video diffusion model, where modifications are initiated in the first frame and subsequently propagated through the entire sequence. To ensure accurate synchronization, we train the model using video pairs that share identical motion trajectories but differ in visual attributes. These pairs are generated and curated through a synchronization-based filtering process, which selects only the most temporally aligned examples for training. This setup enables the model to effectively integrate motion cues from the source video with changes introduced in the first frame. Through rigorous training on a compact, highly curated set of synchronized human portraits, Sync-LoRA demonstrates the ability to generalize to unseen identities and diverse editorial modifications—including modifying appearance, adding objects, or changing backgrounds—while adeptly handling variations in pose and expression. Our results underscore Sync-LoRA's high visual fidelity and strong temporal coherence, achieving a robust balance between edit fidelity and precise motion preservation. Such editing techniques are particularly advantageous for applications in advertising, film production, gaming, and interactive media, where meticulous control over visual aesthetics is crucial. Given the prevalence of human representation in media, editing portrait videos poses a unique challenge; however, the modifications must appear seamless and preserve the authenticity of the original content.

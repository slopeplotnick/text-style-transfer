Thinking in Language Models for Evaluation. The utilization of language models for evaluation often sees optimized reliability when they are prompted to generate intermediate reasoning steps before delivering a final response. This technique, known as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, facilitates the articulation of criteria for judgment, helping to justify decisions and leading to enhanced accuracy and comprehensibility, aligning with the "Thinking-LLM-as-a-Judge" paradigm. Our study leverages these cognitive processes and delves into the best ways to aggregate resulting samples. Inference Time Compute and Sample Aggregation: When confronted with multiple samples, diverse strategies have been proposed to optimize computational resource utilization through inference time compute. Among them, Self-Consistency (SC) aggregates multiple outputs by employing majority voting. Meanwhile, variants of SC incorporate confidence indicators; Soft Self-consistency (Soft-SC) selects outcomes based on confidence scores across categories, while Confidence-Informed Self-Consistency (CI-SC) utilizes a weighted majority vote influenced by length-normalized probability of sequences or by prompting an LLM. Alternatively, some methodologies harness the LLM itself for aggregation. Generative Self-Aggregation (GSA) encourages the model to synthesize a new response derived from multiple contexts. In contrast, Universal Self-Consistency (USC) directs the LLM to identify the most consistent answer among several alternatives. Furthermore, empirical evidence by Singhi et al. and Zhang et al. suggests that leveraging computational resources during test-time, especially through majority voting, enhances the efficacy of reasoning-based generative verifiers.

As studies [25] and [15] proposed, structured moral reasoning benchmarks like MoralBench have been developed to evaluate how well AI models align with human moral reasoning. These studies show that while models may exhibit partial moral sensitivity, they often lack coherence when faced with complex scenarios or conflicting moral principles. However, these benchmarks are limited in that they capture the ethical reasoning capabilities of models at a single temporal point and do not assess how this reasoning might evolve through further interaction and retraining. Other investigations have explored methodologies for enhancing AI's ability to articulate its decision-making processes and align with human ethical paradigms, which are crucial for the advancement of responsible AI systems. For instance, Shitong et al. [9] introduced the DeNEVIL model, facilitating unbiased moral reasoning in AI. Furthermore, Jiao et al. [16] emphasized cross-cultural evaluations of AI moral frameworks and trust formation, highlighting the sociotechnical dependencies inherent in AI ethical considerations. These methodologies demonstrate efficacy in elucidating and fostering trust; however, they are constrained by reliance on predetermined moral categories and manual labeling, hindering their adaptability to evolving ethical contexts. Despite significant advancements in fairness auditing and value-alignment strategies, a critical gap persists: the development of a flexible, model-agnostic framework for longitudinal moral evaluation remains unmet. Current methodologies assess ethical behavior at discrete intervals yet lack autonomous capabilities for scenario generation, interpretation, and adaptation. Moreover, previous approaches do not provide mechanisms to quantitatively assess variations in AI ethical frameworks or monitor real-time moral consistency across models. Therefore, informed by the principles delineated in Section 1, the newly introduced Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP) serves as an ongoing evaluative system that circumvents the necessity for a specific dataset, thereby facilitating the measurement, interpretation, and enhancement of the moral stability of large language models. Consistent with the overarching goals, MoCoP is dedicated solely to evaluation and interpretation, consciously eschewing model optimization or retraining to maintain methodological clarity and consistency.

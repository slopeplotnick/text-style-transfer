We deconstructed the relevance task and summarized the required model capabilities based on the deconstruction results, thereby providing clear and comprehensive guidance for subsequent iterations of relevance models in the industry. We propose a complete methodology and documented practice that encompasses the entire pipeline, from preliminary work such as model selection, to training paradigm, to capability evaluation. During LORE's development, we explored various supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) training strategies. Key findings that may benefit the fine-tuning of language models in vertical domains are summarized as follows: First, the data scaling law demonstrates diminishing returns. Initially, model performance grows rapidly with more data but eventually plateaus as data continues to increase. Second, more features prove superior to fewer. Providing stable and relevant information, despite some redundancy, can yield gains for the model. Third, concise prompts containing essential information are most effective. Information-rich but verbose prompts do not necessarily yield improvements, while prompts lacking key information can degrade model capability. Fourth, naive teacher Chain-of-Thought (CoT) distillation results in negative effects, as direct teacher CoT distillation underperforms when compared to vanilla SFT due to distribution shift. Nevertheless, curriculum learning remains effective, with strategies graded by difficulty significantly outperforming randomly ordered ones. Additionally, long CoT is not necessary for better performance; there was no significant increase in model output length observed during the RL process, indicating that extensive CoT is merely a potential byproduct when the model's capability improves. Furthermore, the rate at which entropy collapses imposes constraints on model performance. In the early stages of training, the model rapidly trades entropy for performance gains; however, when entropy decreases to a certain level, the model's exploration capability becomes constrained, and subsequent room for improvement diminishes. To address this, smart strategies effectively slow entropy collapse. The report discusses several optimization methods, including clip-higher, on-policy, and entropy loss, and ultimately finds that clip-higher achieves optimal performance.

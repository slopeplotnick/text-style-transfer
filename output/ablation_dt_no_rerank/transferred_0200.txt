Curriculum Learning. Originally introduced by Bengio et al. (2009b), curriculum learning has traditionally followed a data-centric paradigm and has been widely adopted in the development of large language models (Parashar et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025a). Recent approaches, such as Speed-RL (Zhang et al., 2025) and the methodologies from Kim and Lee (2024), still rely on data-centric strategies, opting to organize tasks from simple to complex based on external criteria, like task difficulty, to dictate sample ordering. Similarly, LBS3 employs a progressive training protocol that transitions models from easy to difficult queries. However, these approaches fail to adhere to effective curriculum design principles, as they depend on static difficulty thresholds rather than adapting to the evolving capabilities of the model. Effective curriculum learning should be competence-aware, dynamically adjusting to the model's performance trajectory. CAPO utilizes advantage estimates as an intrinsic competence-aware signal, strategically avoiding the premature mixing of positive and negative feedback that often destabilizes reinforcement learning training. It employs a staged curriculum, initially focusing on positive advantages to establish stable behavioral priors, and subsequently introducing negative ones to bolster generalization. Unlike approaches that depend on static, externally defined difficulty measures, CAPO's framework dynamically adapts to the evolving competence of the policy, thereby merging the stability of early reinforcement learning with the adaptability inherent in curriculum scheduling. In this paper, we introduce CAPO (Curriculum Advantage Policy Optimization), a novel mechanism addressing core limitations in reasoning model training by employing advantage as an intrinsic learning signal for adaptive curriculum construction. CAPO's two-phase approach transitions from imitation learning using exclusively positive samples to discrimination learning incorporating negative signals, mirroring human cognitive development while preventing the instability often seen in direct mixed-signal optimization. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CAPO consistently outperforms strong baselines like GRPO across diverse benchmarks and model scales. Remarkably, CAPO exhibits outstanding cross-domain generalization, spanning from mathematical reasoning to tasks involving multimodal data types. By aligning training with the model's evolving capabilities rather than solely external metrics, CAPO unlocks new possibilities for developing adaptive and cognitively inspired learning algorithms.

Advanced market mechanisms offer notable advantages in scalability, adaptability, and fault tolerance relative to centralized approaches. Botelho and Alami (1999) introduced innovative structured auction protocols to enable competitive task assignment, whereas Gerkey and Matario (2002) showed that such auction methods could facilitate efficient task distribution while sustaining coordination properties. Recent advancements have, moreover, addressed communication constraints through complex bidding mechanisms under limited connectivity conditions (Ferri et al., 2017; Quinton et al., 2022). However, auction-based methods may not invariably lead to global optimality, as local bidding choices might disrupt coordination when tasks have intricate interdependencies. In the construction sector, workflows demand precise coordination across multiple trades, with tasks needing to follow specific sequences, making auction mechanisms struggle with temporal constraints crucial for project scheduling. Consequently, these coordination challenges prompted the exploration of structured optimization approaches. Dynamic programming methods arose as alternative strategies suited to scenarios necessitating sequential decision-making. Bellman's theoretical framework (1962) established optimal solution methodologies for problems with optimal substructure properties. Nevertheless, dynamic programming faces the challenge of exponential state space growth, thus confining practical applications to smaller problem instances. Construction projects, which involve numerous robots executing complex interdependent tasks, impose substantial computational demands that hinder real-time decision-making. Therefore, researchers investigated metaheuristic methods for managing expansive and intricate problem domains. Population-based metaheuristic approaches utilized evolutionary principles to navigate complex solution spaces effectively. Genetic algorithms proved particularly adept at managing combinatorial complexities and hierarchical constraints without the need for exhaustive mathematical problem formulations (Jones et al., 2010; Al-Omeer and Ahmed, 2019). Meanwhile, swarm intelligence methods enabled distributed optimization well-suited for coordinating large robot teams (Chen et al., 2022; Lim and Isa, 2015; Wang et al., 2012; Blum, 2005). Despite their merits, however, metaheuristic approaches guarantee neither optimality nor computational efficiency, requiring multiple iterations to achieve satisfactory solution quality. In dynamic construction environments that necessitate real-time adaptation to changing conditions, the lengthy computational time required for iterative optimization renders these methods impractical.

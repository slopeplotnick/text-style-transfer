Recent advancements in reasoning methodologies have significantly improved the capabilities of large language models (LLMs), raising expectations for their potential to deliver information that is accurate, truthful, and reliable. However, recent observations suggest that iterative reasoning might reinforce existing beliefs rather than fostering truth-seeking behavior. In this research, we propose a systematic evaluation framework for assessing belief entrenchment in LLM reasoning, using the Martingale property derived from Bayesian statistics. This property implies that, with rational belief updating, the expected value of future beliefs should align with current beliefs, thus preventing predictions of future beliefs based solely on present ones. We introduce the Martingale Score, an unsupervised, regression-based metric, to detect instances where models fail to appropriately update their beliefs. Across various domains such as event forecasting, value-laden inquiries, and academic paper reviews, models frequently demonstrate a propensity to maintain existing beliefs—a phenomenon we identify as belief entrenchment. Our comprehensive investigation examined different models, cognitive styles, and domains, revealing tendencies toward belief entrenchment. Furthermore, our findings demonstrate the Martingale Score's ability to predict model accuracy in domains where ground truth labels are available, highlighting its utility even in areas lacking knowledge of correct answers. This advancement deepens our understanding of model efficacy in truth discernment. Consider an individual who clings to their convictions or a monarch who heeds only affirmations from loyal subordinates. Often, individuals persist in their preconceived notions due to a selective search for confirming information or external influences.

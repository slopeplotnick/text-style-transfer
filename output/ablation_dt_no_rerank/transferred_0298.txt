To effectively engage with extended video content, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods have been developed to retrieve relevant segments rather than arbitrary frames. These approaches utilize textual and visual indicators to identify salient frames or clips, allowing the model to focus on the most critical components of the video. Recent advances extend this strategy by using graph-based structures to elucidate interrelations among video components. However, these models often overly rely on textual representations or basic visual-textual comparison metrics, limiting their capacity for comprehensive understanding and complex reasoning over long video durations. Beyond these basic retrieval designs, memory-based techniques have been proposed to systematically organize and archive video information. For instance, EgoRAG organizes hierarchical textual memories from video data, facilitating reasoning throughout day-long activities. Building upon this, Ego-R1 employs vision-centric methodologies and iterative reasoning for tasks of extended complexity. Moreover, HippoMM introduces a dual-process memory model by integrating semantic summaries with multimodal cues, while M3-Agent develops an entity-centric memory system employing iterative reasoning to retrieve pertinent information. Despite these enhancements, current methodologies struggle to fully integrate multimodal information and adapt across varying temporal scales inherent in video content. To address these challenges, we present WorldMM, a novel framework that synthesizes both textual and visual contexts from video streams to construct a multimodal memory system for comprehensive understanding and reasoning over long video sequences. The system operates in three stages: initially, it establishes multiple memories, including two textual and one visual memory. Subsequently, an adaptive retrieval mechanism is used to extract pertinent information across different temporal segments until sufficient data is amassed for a given query. Finally, the aggregated information is used to generate a coherent response.

In this section, we establish the framework used throughout this work. First, we define the datasets, solvers, and verifiers, introduce the metrics used to evaluate solver and verifier behaviors, and specify the verification settings in our empirical analysis. Importantly, verifier gain is an asymptotic metric: it reflects the limit of infinite sampling and therefore serves as a bound on the improvement attainable by verifier-based rejection sampling. Consequently, we use verifier gain throughout this work to compare differences in verifier behavior. Models are grouped into families, such as Llama3 or Qwen2.5, where each family contains related models of varying sizes. As foundational and post-trained models often exhibit substantially different behaviors, we treat them as distinct categories. For example, the base model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-70B belongs to the Llama3-Base family, while the post-trained model meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct is part of the Llama3 family. Each solver-verifier pair from our pool of models is categorized into one of three verification settings. Firstly, self-verification occurs when the solver and verifier are the same model, verifying its own solutions. For instance, when a 70B Llama3 model serves as both solver and verifier, the verification metric, such as accuracy, is computed on this single pairing. Secondly, intra-family verification happens when the verifier evaluates solutions produced by other models within the same family. For example, a 70B Llama3 verifier may assess outputs from 8B or 13B Llama3 solvers, and the resulting metric is averaged over all those within-family solvers, excluding self-verification cases. Lastly, cross-family verification occurs when the verifier evaluates solutions produced by models from different families. For example, a base Llama3 verifier might assess outputs from Qwen3 or an enhanced Llama3, and the metric is averaged over all such cross-family solvers.

The project aims to extend the existing French-Ciluba sentiment lexicon to include translations in English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, Sesotho, and Sepedi by utilizing cross-lingual translation and corpus-based mapping. To further enrich the lexicon, Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) will be applied to identify sentiment-bearing terms from African language corpora and align them with present lexicon entries. Moreover, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) will be implemented to resolve ambiguities in sentiment labels and refine multilingual word alignments. Subsequently, AfriBERTa and AfroXLMR models will be trained for sentiment classification using the expanded multilingual lexicon, with their performance evaluated through precision, recall, F1-score, and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) metrics. Additionally, we will integrate Explainable AI techniques, such as attention visualization and feature attribution, to interpret model predictions and assess linguistic focus. We will then compare the performance of individual models with an ensemble learning approach to identify potential improvements in accuracy for multilingual sentiment classification. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II reviews existing research on sentiment analysis, multilingual lexicon development, and African language processing. We then outline the TriLex framework and methodologies, followed by an exposition of our experimental procedures and model evaluations. Next, we present our findings and discuss their implications for multilingual natural language processing. Traditionally, sentiment analysis has relied on supervised machine learning and lexicon-driven methods for opinion classification in text. Foundational studies established the efficacy of statistical classifiers in this area, while subsequent research highlighted the utility of sentiment lexicons in facilitating interpretable, rule-based sentiment analysis. Widely used lexicons like SentiWordNet, NRC Emotion Lexicon, and VADER provide polarity scores for high-resource languages; however, they offer limited coverage for morphologically rich and low-resource languages. Therefore, efforts have been made to develop multilingual sentiment lexicons through corpus-based extraction techniques.

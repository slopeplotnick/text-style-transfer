Some language models may demonstrate higher reliability than others. This review explores the impact of model size, structure, and training data on hallucination occurrence. The study outlines these trends across various models and suggests solutions, such as improving training techniques and imposing limitations on generated outputs, to address this issue. Similarly, another study highlights that hallucinations remain a fundamental problem for large language models (LLMs), regardless of their size or training methodology. The study proposes that careful model selection and training can help mitigate hallucinations, though the authors caution that completely eliminating them is not feasible. This insight is crucial as it assists in formulating realistic goals and evaluating the appropriateness of the chosen model for specific tasks. When selecting an LLM to reduce hallucinations, it is essential to consider the model architecture, the quality and diversity of the training data, and strategies like fine-tuning or retrieval augmentation. Several models are currently available, such as GPT, BERT, and T5. For instance, GPT often generates text that is logically and cohesively consistent, yet it is prone to producing fabricated data or hallucinations. BERT exhibits lower rates of hallucinations due to its use of bidirectional training for specific tasks, although it remains susceptible to generating new content. Additionally, models such as PaLM and LLaMa have been developed to mitigate hallucination effects; however, their success varies based on their training methods and architectural designs. Understanding the strengths and limitations of these models is vital when choosing an appropriate tool for applications where information accuracy is critical and minimizing hallucination rates is imperative.

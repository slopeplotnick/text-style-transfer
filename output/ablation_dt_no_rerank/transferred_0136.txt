In contrast to image-based IC-LoRA methodologies that require separate training for each editing task, our singular Sync-LoRA module effectively generalizes across a variety of edits, achieving a robust synthesis of high visual fidelity and precise motion preservation, as demonstrated in our experiments (see section 4). In-Context Learning within diffusion models enables the adaptation to new tasks by conditioning on structured input examples without the need for explicit supervision. While extensively explored in the realm of language models, recent research has recently begun to examine ICL's effectiveness in image generation using diffusion models. In this domain, the model generates a panel of images, where the images within the panel provide context to one another. This feature is advantageous for applications requiring coherence in generated images, such as storyboard creation. When these panels are conditioned on a specific real image, ICL methodologies can be applied to a variety of image-to-image tasks, including image personalization and editorial modifications. The work most analogous to ours is IC-LoRA, which identifies that transformer-based diffusion models have an inherent capacity to generate panels where certain attributes are consistent across images. Their method enhances this intrinsic ability through the use of a low-rank adapter (LoRA), refining the model on structured and consistent panels while employing tailored template prompts. This approach has been applied in diverse domains such as film storyboard creation, font design, and home decoration. Our work extends this concept to video, introducing an IC-LoRA module focused on frame-accurate portrait synchronization for video editing. Furthermore, recent advancements have shown that large video diffusion transformers naturally exhibit in-context learning abilities, allowing for the generation of multiple temporally or spatially related clips within a single diffusion process. Earlier research explored this ability, using inpainting techniques for scene manipulation but lacking mechanisms to ensure temporal coherence among clips. Building upon these findings, PoseGen pioneered the use of in-context LoRA fine-tuning for pose-controllable human video generation, thereby achieving extended and coherent motion sequences from a single reference image. Our approach further extends this methodology by explicitly training in-context behavior towards synchronized editing, ensuring temporal alignment and subject consistency.

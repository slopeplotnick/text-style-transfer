The established five primary categories are divided into sixteen specific edit types, encompassing processes ranging from fundamental text formatting to advanced editing workflows. This approach, consistent with the methodology of Maia Polo et al., emphasizes fewer but more comprehensive cases. Specifically, these scenarios involve elements like text replacement and resolving challenges requiring multiple edits, engaging both imagery and textual analysis to derive solutions. For instance, a case necessitates aligning images with captions distributed across multiple slides, using both visual and textual indicators. Moreover, many cases concurrently pertain to different thematic classifications, resulting in an aggregate category count exceeding 100. Distinct from previous benchmarks, PPTArena concentrates on the complexity of edits across four pivotal dimensions: the extent of multi-step reasoning, the degree of inter-slide dependencies, the depth of semantic comprehension, and the intricacies of long-term strategic planning. Unlike existing tools such as PPTC-R and T2US, which predominantly address brief, template-dependent modifications, PPTArena is crafted to engage with more challenging and realistic scenarios that highlight the shortcomings of current systems. It integrates decks created by humans and Python algorithms, provides deterministic evaluation through explicit ground truth, ensures cross-platform compatibility, and incorporates accessibility constraints. A representative task involves matching images to captions on a slide, achieving visual equilibrium, updating slide titles, and ensuring uniformity in image dimensions, specifically maintaining a standard size of 3.2 inches in width and 2.4 inches in height. Therefore, these tasks evaluate systems' proficiency in multi-step reasoning, cross-slide information integration, and prospective planning. This framework effectively identifies challenges that simpler tasks may not reveal, providing a robust mechanism for assessing sophisticated systems' capabilities. Furthermore, the evaluation focuses on two primary criteria: adherence to instructional guidelines and the quality of visual outputs. Expert judges conduct evaluations, assigning scores from 0 to 5, where 0 indicates failure and 5 represents exemplary performance.

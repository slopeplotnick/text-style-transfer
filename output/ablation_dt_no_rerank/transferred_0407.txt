Flow-based diffusion models have emerged as a leading paradigm for training generative models across domains of images and videos. However, understanding their memorization-generalization dynamics has been challenging. In this investigation, we revisit the flow matching (FM) objective and examine its marginal velocity field, which admits a closed-form expression, thus allowing precise computation of the FM oracle target. Analyzing this oracle velocity field reveals that flow-based diffusion models inherently establish a two-stage training target: initially, a stage influenced by a mixture of data modes, followed by a stage dominated by the nearest data sample. This bifurcated objective yields distinct learning behaviors: the early navigation stage facilitates generalization across various data modes to form global patterns, while the subsequent refinement stage focuses on memorizing intricate details. Leveraging these insights, we elucidate the effectiveness of practical techniques such as timestep-shifted schedules, classifier-free guidance intervals, and strategic latent space design choices. Our study deepens the understanding of diffusion model training dynamics and offers foundational principles for guiding future architectural and algorithmic improvements. Diffusion models have emerged as a powerful class of generative methods, capable of synthesizing high-fidelity samples across diverse domains, including images and videos. These models learn complex data distributions by progressively transforming a prior distribution (e.g., Gaussian) into the data distribution through an interpolation process parameterized by denoising or transport directions. Various formulations have been proposed to characterize the diffusion process, including probabilistic models, score-based ordinary or stochastic differential equations, and flow matching methodologies. These perspectives give rise to diverse training objectives, such as noise prediction, score prediction, or velocity prediction, and correspond to different parameterizations of the probability flow ordinary differential equation. Recently, the flow matching formulation has gained substantial consensus, wherein the model is trained to predict the velocity field under a linear scheduling framework. Due to its simplicity and stable training dynamics, this approach has become the standard for training state-of-the-art diffusion models.

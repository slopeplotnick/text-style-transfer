As illustrated in Fig 12, the experimental cohort employing the curriculum learning strategy demonstrates accelerated convergence rates and more stable optimization paths concerning both reward metrics and model efficacy enhancement. During RL training, as convergence nears, the entropy of the model's output distribution rapidly diminishes, leading to more deterministic generation outcomes. This premature convergence phenomenon limits the model's exploration capability, making it difficult to discover superior solutions and ultimately constraining the improvement potential. To address this issue, we explored various strategies to decelerate this predictability, thereby maintaining the model's exploration capacity. Fig 13 presents a comparison of these methods. The Clip-higher strategy emerges as optimal by effectively slowing the predictability rate while still allowing it to proceed, achieving a balance between exploration and exploitation and ensuring training stability with noticeable performance improvements. In contrast, the On-policy approach stabilizes policy entropy, significantly curbing its decline. Although this enhances exploratory capabilities, it impedes efficient policy convergence, resulting in reduced performance improvements. Meanwhile, the Off-policy + Entropy Loss approach experiences training instability, especially as the penalty for randomness becomes excessively substantial, leading to erratic behaviors and eventual training failure. Moreover, this approach demands extensive experimentation to set optimal parameters, increasing computational costs. In summary, the Clip-higher strategy achieves an optimal balance between exploration and convergence by moderately controlling the rate at which the model becomes predictable, making it the best choice for this study's context.

To enhance the perceptual capabilities of machines in high-resolution image interpretation, contemporary research has focused on advancing methods for detail observation. A widely adopted tactic is the "locate-and-zoom-in" strategy, which sharpens a model’s ability to perceive finer details. Training-based methodologies, like Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning, offer potential effectiveness but are significantly limited by high computational demands, lengthy training durations, and limited adaptability across different architectures, hindering scalability and practical use. Conversely, training-free approaches leverage attention mechanisms and tree-based searches to automatically pinpoint relevant image regions without the need for dataset construction or model fine-tuning. However, despite starting from a global perspective and refining focus thereafter, these methods often fall short in detecting small details during initial stages, which can lead to faulty search paths. In response, a novel strategy, inspired by the benefits of Retrieval-Augmented Generation in language models, has emerged: Retrieval-Augmented Perception (RAP), which aims to bolster machine perception of high-resolution images without prior training. RAP extends this concept into the visual domain, showing significant performance improvements on complex high-resolution tasks. It comprises three main components: First, the Visual Retrieval module uses a pre-trained VisRAG model to isolate image regions that closely match the query in semantic content, effectively reducing irrelevant data. Next, the Spatial-Awareness Layout module safeguards the original spatial relationships among these regions to ensure spatial coherence in inputs. Lastly, the Adaptive Retrieval-Exploration Search (RE-Search) module determines the optimal quantity of these image segments by building a Retrieval-Exploration Tree (RE-Tree), thereby achieving a balance between information acquisition and computational load.

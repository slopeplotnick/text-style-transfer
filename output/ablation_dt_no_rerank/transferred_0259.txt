High task accuracy does not always guarantee optimal cue combination, as illustrated in the middle panel of Figure 8. For instance, although GPT-5 Mini achieves very low NRMSE, it fails to efficiently integrate modalities compared to other models. This deficiency becomes evident when unimodal performance is imbalanced, requiring models to down-weight the weaker modality substantially, which some models fail to accomplish. Consequently, benchmarks focusing exclusively on accuracy risk favouring models that lack robustness against noise. Conversely, Llama-4 Maverick outperforms Bayesian reliability-weighted linear fusion, suggesting the use of more advanced non-linear integration strategies, consistent with the superior fit of non-linear random forest methods over linear ones. Furthermore, when comparing uni- and multimodal performance, models like Llama-4 Maverick, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o leverage additional input modalities to reduce error rates in the majority of multimodal tasks. However, this improvement is not universal, indicating opportunities for enhancing multimodal LLM performance. Model-specific details are available in Appendix A.11. To evaluate beyond static task metrics, the Bayesian Consistency Score (BCS) captures systematic behavioural shifts, providing a holistic view of model behaviours even at high accuracy levels. Observing behaviour changes under controlled variations allows comparisons between models with different base performances, offering insights into their computational strategies. While complex heuristics, such as salience-based weighting or rule-switching strategies, may align with static response patterns, investigating Bayes-consistent behaviours under a Bayesian observer model when conditions change is more insightful.

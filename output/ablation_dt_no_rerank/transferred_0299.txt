We employ OmniFusion by integrating lecture slide screenshots during live translation to extract pertinent slides from segmented audio. Moreover, we are developing an open-source image-to-image translation pipeline with modular components, enabling future research on full-image and slide translation. Additionally, by incorporating images, we demonstrate performance improvements in language processing tasks across different language models for various language pairs. We also evaluate several optical character recognition (OCR) models and the translation quality using unimodal and multimodal approaches. To enhance lecture accessibility across diverse languages and formats, our multimodal translation systems translate both audio and slides, performing multimodal processing and leveraging transcripts for subsequent language tasks. Furthermore, we translate slides by converting text and images into the target language, preserving their original layout and visual coherence. We developed a PDF viewer displaying slides with captions synchronized to the slides selected by the presenter, helping participants follow translations seamlessly. This arrangement provides essential context for associating each audio segment with the corresponding slide in multimodal speech translation. In this section, we delineate the methodology for associating slide images with corresponding audio segments and how derived translations are integrated into subsequent summarization and question-answering tasks. We further detail the mechanisms involved in detecting, translating, and reconstructing text embedded within slide images. Additional details about the Text-to-Speech processes are provided in Appendix A.1. Current systems supporting multilingual translations often encounter limitations when applied to live lectures, as they are generally designed for offline functionality, requiring predefined cues to initiate translation. Furthermore, existing technologies either overlook lecture slides or fail to manage translations across multiple languages concurrently. Effective lecture scenarios demand the integration of both multimodality and multilinguality.

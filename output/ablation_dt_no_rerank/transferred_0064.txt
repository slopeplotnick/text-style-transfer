4.1.2 Graph-Based Statistical Analysis of Retrieved Training Images. Our previous analysis, focusing on the influence of training images through similarity comparisons of generated images before and after retraining, did not fully explain the varying impacts of certain images. To supplement these findings, we employed a graph-based statistical analysis of the retrieved samples. Specifically, we analyzed the structural and semantic features of the top-ranked training images per prompt, selected via our graph-based method. This investigation aimed to determine if the retrieved samples formed coherent and semantically dense groups. Table 3 summarizes key graph statistics from ten unlearning experiments, each consisting of 15 prompts, which provide insights into the consistency and clustering of attributes in each group. These results validate the efficacy of our retrieval strategy and offer an interpretable view of what makes these samples influential in generation. Unique Values per Relationship. The average number of unique values per relationship ranged from 3.9 to 5.1 across all experiments. This moderate diversity is ideal for a controlled domain like fashion, where variation occurs but is constrained by categorical ontology (e.g., limited silhouettes or patterns). The values indicate that retrieved samples are similar enough to form coherent groups, yet diverse enough to avoid redundancy, ensuring that structured influence arises from shared high-level concepts and not duplication. Shared Attributes: Depth of Semantic Similarity. On average, retrieved graphs shared between 5 and 7.1 attributes, with some groups sharing up to 13. This suggests that retrieved images are not only semantically rich but also aligned with one another. Prompts with more specific cues generally led to a higher average of shared attributes, while more abstract or stylistic prompts resulted in greater variation. This demonstrates the system's capability to adapt to varying degrees of prompt specificity while surfacing semantically aligned examples.

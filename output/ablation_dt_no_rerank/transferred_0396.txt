The MODOMA framework comprises two integral components: the mother agent and the daughter agent. Within this architecture, Delilah functions as the mother agent and has been specifically developed to generate and analyze Dutch language constructs. Unlike systems that depend on curated databases of linguistic examples, Delilah operates based on a predetermined set of grammatical rules and structural principles. These templates provide a comprehensive representation of the lexical and grammatical properties inherent in Dutch language use. For instance, the template associated with the lexeme "de" encompasses detailed specifications concerning its morphological form, semantic roles, syntactic functions, and integration within sentence structures. These templates are meticulously developed through extensive linguistic research dedicated to the Dutch lexicon and grammar, thereby ensuring their precision and alignment with linguistic realities. Moreover, Delilah employs these templates to construct phrases and sentences through a procedure known as unification. This method verifies the compatibility of templates, ensuring there are no conflicting characteristics, and then amalgamates them into a singular construct. For example, the template for "de" is configured to conjoin exclusively with noun phrases, as exemplified in "de vrouw" ("the woman"). Delilah operates similarly to a grammatical framework known as head-driven phrase structure grammar (HPSG), where no significant formal distinction is made between lexical items and grammatical rules, effectively treating both as equivalent entities. Furthermore, Delilah intrinsically utilizes combinatory list grammar, which exhibits a relation to combinatory categorial grammar (CCG). The operational mechanisms of Delilah have been extensively investigated and elucidated in numerous scholarly articles. In the context of the MODOMA system, Delilah is implemented in accordance with its original specifications, without any modifications.

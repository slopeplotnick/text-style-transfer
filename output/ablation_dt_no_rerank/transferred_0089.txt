Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) with voice interfaces have garnered significant attention from both the research community and broader society. Proprietary models, such as GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-pro, have demonstrated superior performance in classical speech tasks, including speech recognition, translation, and spoken language understanding, significantly surpassing previous open-source benchmarks. However, despite these strides, there remains a notable paucity of comprehensive studies on the integration of speech modalities into LLMs for true multi-modal understanding. Furthermore, the interaction between speech models and LLMs within autonomous systems remains in its nascent stages. This tutorial aims to provide a thorough review of the historical trajectory of language modeling in speech processing, offering insights into the development of interoperable systems. Moreover, we will delve into advanced methodologies for achieving multimodal data integration, introducing foundational principles necessary for aligning non-textual data with textual information. These concepts will be discussed alongside illustrative examples that facilitate a deeper comprehension of these ideas. Additionally, we will explore contemporary methodologies for constructing models that concurrently process speech and text, employing techniques that iteratively integrate both modalities. By comparing traditional practices with innovative approaches, this tutorial aims to enhance understanding of current advancements and identify persisting challenges within the domain of integrating speech with LLMs. This comprehensive three-hour tutorial will focus on transformative developments in speech-language modeling within the context of voice-interfacing LLMs and agent systems. Each thematic section is allocated 35 minutes of focused discussion, succeeded by a 10-minute period dedicated to questions and answers, and an additional 10-minute interval for relaxation. For each segment, we will provide an overview of pertinent topics, followed by a thorough analysis of seminal studies currently shaping the field. The tutorial will conclude with an examination of the persisting challenges and emerging research opportunities in the dynamic and transformative domain of joint speech-language modeling.

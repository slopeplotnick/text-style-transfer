The integration of robotics in the construction industry holds immense potential to address workforce deficits while simultaneously enhancing safety and efficiency. Robots demonstrate proficiency in executing tasks that require substantial physical strength and repetitive actions, performing these activities with diminished susceptibility to fatigue or errors. Consequently, deploying construction robots significantly expedites the construction process and curtails associated expenses. However, the inherent complexity of construction tasks poses challenges for individual robots to adeptly execute all required processes. For example, constructing a structure, such as a wall, necessitates a diverse array of skills, including material transportation, precise placement, welding, and quality assurance. Therefore, a coordinated deployment of multiple robots is indispensable to accomplish these multifaceted tasks effectively. Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) systematically decomposes complex tasks into manageable subtasks and assigns these to various robots, considering the capabilities of each robot, the specific requirements of the task, and the conditions of the work environment. MRTA challenges are articulated along several dimensions, including the distinction between single-task and multi-task robots, single-robot versus multi-robot task execution, and the nature of assignments as either instantaneous or time-extended. The most intricate category involves coordinating numerous robots executing multiple tasks over extended periods, necessitating sophisticated planning to address diverse robot capabilities, intricate task requirements, and scheduling considerations. However, the majority of studies on MRTA predominantly concentrate on applications within warehouse logistics and environmental exploration, with limited emphasis on the construction sector.

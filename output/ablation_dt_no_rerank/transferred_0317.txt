Empirical evidence indicates that the entrenchment in beliefs significantly contributes to social epistemic issues such as polarization and misinformation [Del Vicario et al., 2017; Modgil et al., 2024; Lefebvre et al., 2024; Calhoun, 2004], as well as a multitude of cognitive biases [Oeberst and Imhoff, 2023]. This phenomenon stems from the mechanisms within human information processing, namely reasoning [Oeberst and Imhoff, 2023]. Large language models (LLMs), having assimilated patterns of human cognition from extensive internet-scale text data, are engineered to aid individuals by responding to questions [Luo et al., 2022] and assisting in task completion [Zheng et al., 2023]. Their efficacy in sophisticated cognitive tasks [Brown et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2023] has led to the development of diverse reasoning techniques, starting with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) [Wei et al., 2022] and advancing to reinforced reasoning via reinforcement learning frameworks [Zelikman et al., 2024; Jaech et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025]. These techniques aim to support language models in truth-seeking by improving comprehension through argumentation, evidence evaluation, and experimental tactics. However, this reasoning can deviate from truth-seeking due to belief entrenchment—a systematic tendency to favor prior opinions over new evidence (Figure 1). Such deviations can undermine the accuracy of a model's conclusions, reflecting similar impacts in human cognition [Park et al., 2010], and give users an unjustified sense of confidence [Shi et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024a]. While studies suggest the presence of belief entrenchment in LLMs [Schmidgall et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024; Sumita et al., 2024], proving this rigorously remains challenging. Distinguishing between justified, prior-consistent updates and biased updates is particularly difficult [Atallah et al., 2021; Ji, 2023]. Consequently, prior research often utilizes highly synthetic tasks or specific domains where belief entrenchment is readily apparent [Schmidgall et al., 2024]. Nevertheless, these methods fail to capture reasoning errors as they occur, especially in scenarios where ground truth is ambiguous or unavailable.

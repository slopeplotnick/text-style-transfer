Online videos have become a dominant communication medium, and their widespread reach has enabled the rapid dissemination of hateful content. This content exacerbates discrimination and social divisions and can even incite offline violence. However, conventional approaches to addressing this issue typically involve extracting and integrating features from video frames, audio, and transcribed text but often fail to capture effective multimodal semantic interaction. Detecting hateful video content is predominantly hampered by two principal challenges. Firstly, there is the nuanced comprehension of contextual cues—these videos often employ subtle indicators across temporal and multimodal dimensions, such as the congruence between specific visuals and verbal expressions or the implicit associations between visual emphasis and auditory elements. Secondly, there is the effective integration of multimodal information—existing methodologies inadequately facilitate interaction among extracted features from video frames, audio, and transcribed text. Addressing these challenges necessitates profound contextual reasoning capabilities that current approaches struggle to achieve. Recent methodologies endeavor to enhance contextual comprehension by employing models that integrate visual and linguistic elements, introducing reasoning processes. However, direct reasoning predominantly results in superficial descriptions devoid of semantic elements of hate, while emerging techniques may produce inaccurate interpretations that fail to capture the model's genuine reasoning processes, posing challenges for sensitive tasks. Additionally, recent research highlights that hate cues within videos can manifest swiftly in brief segments or be disseminated throughout the entire video timeline, exhibiting heterogeneous temporal distributions. These signals inherently possess intricate semantics that necessitate integration with elemental multimodal features. Therefore, these issues underscore a significant deficiency: current systems exhibit inadequacies in reliable semantic reasoning and in synthesizing diverse informational modalities for the effective detection of hateful videos.

4.5.1 Fact Verification. Our analysis suggests that the effectiveness of FV methods is fundamentally constrained by the retrieval module's performance and the availability of relevant external knowledge. As demonstrated in Table 3, this reliance creates a significant vulnerability when the system fails to provide necessary evidence. In particular, if the retriever cannot locate pertinent passages—often due to keyword mismatches in the LLM-Q setting—the verification module must make judgments without adequate context. This absence of evidence results in unstable model behavior; the model may exhibit unwarranted skepticism (leading to false alarms, as in Case 1) or, conversely, accept unverified false claims (resulting in overlooked errors, as in Case 2). However, when the retrieval process is enhanced with the generated answer (LLM-QA), the system is more likely to obtain relevant supporting documents, thus enabling the FV model to make stronger predictions. 4.5.2 Hallucination Detection. Meanwhile, HD methods are vulnerable to false alerts due to the inherent semantic flexibility of language generation. Table 4 highlights a clear dichotomy: HD methods perform robustly with deterministic responses (Group A), yet their efficacy degrades significantly with more expressive responses (Group B). In such scenarios, a model may be factually confident about an event—such as the formation year of a band—while remaining lexically uncertain about its articulation. Present HD methods lack the ability to disentangle this valid semantic diversity from genuine falsehoods, leading to high entropy caused by varied phrasing being mistaken for hallucination, and thus, flagging correct generations solely because of their diverse expression. 4.5.3 Implications for Hybrid Design. Our qualitative analysis reveals that HD and FV operate with orthogonal error boundaries, providing a theoretical basis for their integration. FV serves as a precision-oriented anchor, delivering highly reliable judgments when evidence is successfully retrieved, but performing poorly when retrieval fails. Conversely, HD acts as a recall-oriented safety net, capable of identifying potential errors independently of external knowledge, though susceptible to noise from semantic flexibility. Therefore, an optimal hybrid strategy should not equally prioritize both elements. Instead, it should emphasize FV's judgment when the evidence is relevant and rely on HD's intrinsic signals when retrieval results are not pertinent. This conditional complementarity has directly motivated the hierarchical design of our proposed hybrid framework in RQ3.

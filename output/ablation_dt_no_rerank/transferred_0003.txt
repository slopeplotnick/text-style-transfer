This investigation conducted an empirical survey on seven model-merging strategies aimed at mitigating social bias, using three bias benchmarks and the SuperGLUE benchmark suite across a collection of 13 large language models. Notably, the results identified SLERP with moderate interpolation weights as the most balanced method, achieving effective bias reduction without sacrificing downstream accuracy. It is crucial, therefore, to verify the efficacy of these methods on tasks such as reading comprehension and reasoning, which are essential for addressing social bias. Recently, model merging has emerged as an effective strategy for leveraging the strengths of multiple models without incurring the costs of retraining, as demonstrated by Li et al. (2023) and Yang et al. (2024). This approach involves fusing parameters from two or more trained models to produce a single model that retains and consolidates the knowledge and skills from all sources. Pioneering this domain is the linear averaging technique, introduced by Wortsman et al. (2022), where weights are merged by arithmetic means. This technique represents a cost-effective way to incorporate diverse expertise by utilizing pre-existing fine-tuned models without requiring additional training. Furthermore, several studies, including those by Matena and Raffel (2022) and Lee et al. (2025), have expanded this concept by weighting each parameter inversely to its Fisher information, ensuring that the resultant combinations align with likelihood principles. Merging methodologies that are based on sphere interpolation, as described by Shoemake (1985) and Goddard et al. (2024), conceptualize parameter vectors as residing on a spherical surface. The seminal concept of SLERP, introduced by Shoemake in 1985, involves interpolating between two models, while the Karcher Mean, detailed by Grove and Karcher in 1973, facilitates the computation of averages across multiple models. More recently, Goddard et al. in 2024 proposed the NuSLERP method, which incorporates per-tensor normalization to address any discrepancies in model dimensions. Inspired by these foundational concepts, Ilharco et al. (2022) developed the task arithmetic approach under the notion of "task vector," representing the parameters of the difference between a pre-trained model and its fine-tuned counterpart.

Integrating related events and examining their antecedents enables the formation of coherent narratives that provide clarity and connectivity. This approach surpasses mere summarization, which often results in fragmented and causally ambiguous representations. Preserving detailed specifics within events, such as technological intricacies in the context of a "new tech release," affords a more precise understanding than generalizations, which can render analyses overly broad and overlook critical aspects impacting business outcomes. Furthermore, the precise identification of chronological timestamps facilitates the assessment of event durations and their subsequent impacts. Without specific temporal markers, establishing causality between events and market fluctuations becomes challenging, thereby diminishing the accuracy of predictive models. We examined the significance of systematic event tracking and the assimilation of incremental information. Removing the event tracking component demonstrated that minor news items could elicit significant market responses when subsequent reports indicate unforeseen progression. Conversely, the absence of new information—such as when a major shareholder reduces their holdings without further developments—results in neutral market reactions, as expectations remain constant. Thus, while individual events may not exert substantial influence independently, the evolution of expectations over time serves as a potent mechanism for predictive accuracy, effectively captured through event tracking. To assess the efficacy of our historical pattern referencing strategy, we conducted a comparative analysis of four distinct methodologies. Our strategy demonstrated superior performance; however, referencing historical data from the same company might yield satisfactory results, yet its effectiveness diminishes when the company possesses limited historical data or undergoes novel developments. Under such conditions, identifying analogous past events proves challenging.

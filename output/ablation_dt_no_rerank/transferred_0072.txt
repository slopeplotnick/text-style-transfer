Certain formula-based fault localisation methods in software engineering either produce a large number of redundant diagnoses, as seen with SNIPER, or fail to return a minimal set sufficient to repair the program, like BugAssIST. Recently, Graussam proposed a model-based software fault localisation method for ANSI C programs, introducing a well-defined fault model that adeptly manages complex language features, such as pointers and arrays, and building on CBMC encodings. Consequently, the relaxation step is performed at the CBMC level rather than at the code level, distinguishing it from our approach. This method effectively handles multiple failing observations by formulating standard MBD instances and solving them with HSD to guarantee minimal diagnoses for each failing test case. However, Graussam's method introduces an aggregation procedure after diagnosis enumeration, which may compromise the minimality of the final corrections. We did not include this tool in our evaluation due to two main limitations. First, functions require manual annotations for analysis, which is impractical for our scope, as we analyze over 1500 programs. Second, its current support is limited to integers and floats in C, making it unsuitable for the C-PACK-IPAs benchmark. Furthermore, Model-Based Diagnosis has been successfully applied to restore consistency across several domains, including Boolean circuits, C software, knowledge bases, and spreadsheets. Similarly, program slicing has emerged as a complementary technique for fault localisation within software programs. A syntactic fault localisation approach utilizes program slicing to enumerate all minimal sets required to repair a defective program. Additionally, a method for identifying the causes of faulty behaviour involves analyzing the differences between successive software versions. This paper introduces a novel formula-based fault localisation technique for C programs and Boolean circuits, capable of addressing multiple faults simultaneously. By leveraging Model-Based Diagnosis with multiple observations, all failing test cases are integrated into a single MaxSAT formula, ensuring consistency throughout the localisation process and avoiding redundant diagnoses.

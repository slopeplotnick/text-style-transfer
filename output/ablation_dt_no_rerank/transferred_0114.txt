PEFT-FACTORY also implements the calculation of classification and efficiency metrics during the prediction phase in addition to existing token accuracy and semantic similarity metrics such as Rouge (Lin, 2004) and Bleu (Papineni et al., 2002). Specifically, standard Accuracy and F1 metrics are employed for classification. To measure efficiency during the evaluation of PEFT methods, PEFT-FACTORY utilizes the PSCP metric (Balanec et al., 2025), which is a highly adjustable metric considering various efficiency parameters like the number of parameters, memory usage, and inference time. These metrics are implemented within the train/sft/metric.py file for supervised fine-tuning, adhering to the pattern established by LLaMA-Factory and utilizing distinct data classes named ComputeAccuracy, ComputeF1, and ComputePSCP for each metric. For classification metrics, a binary flag attribute called compute_classification.metrics is included in the training parameters, enabling or disabling their computation. Regarding efficiency metrics, a corresponding binary flag named compute_pscp is provided. Additional information on efficiency metrics can be found in Appendix B.3. Furthermore, PEFT-FACTORY's extensibility and a ready-to-use, controlled, and stable environment are key factors promoting research on PEFT methods. To illustrate how PEFT-FACTORY enhances reproducibility and benchmarking, two specific use cases are presented. The modular design of PEFT-FACTORY significantly contributes to reproducibility and transparency when researchers and practitioners develop new PEFT methods. Presently, upon the creation of a new PEFT method, the published source code is often either not fully functional or challenging to reproduce. Additionally, authors frequently need to write code for training and evaluating the PEFT method from scratch, which is often repetitive, increases the probability of mistakes, and can lead to inconsistencies in the final results.

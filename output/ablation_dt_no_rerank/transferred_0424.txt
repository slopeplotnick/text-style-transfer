The goal is to develop a framework that facilitates the training of control policies to produce naturalistic motions without needing access to the original dataset, while ensuring modularity and reusability across a variety of tasks. Therefore, we present Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), a method for constructing such reusable motion priors. Initially, a model is trained to understand motion data without being tied to a specific task or policy. Once this training is complete, the model serves as a prior using score-matching techniques to provide a robust comparison between simulated motions and behaviors from the original dataset. Furthermore, by using the learned SMP as a general style reward, our framework enables the training of new policies capable of performing numerous tasks with life-like behaviors. The key innovation here is integrating reinforcement learning with score distillation to construct modular and reusable motion priors for physics-based character animation. Our approach generates high-quality motions on par with advanced adversarial imitation learning techniques, while greatly improving modularity and reusability. Crucially, SMP allows the system to eliminate reliance on the reference dataset during policy training. Subsequently, we demonstrate that a motion prior can be synthesized from a model trained on large motion datasets without focusing on specific tasks. This versatile motion prior can then be converted into style-specific versions using prompts and guidance techniques. Moreover, we show that different stylistic priors can be combined to create new styles absent from the original dataset, enhancing creative flexibility.

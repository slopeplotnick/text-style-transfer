Based on the resulted clusters detailed in Figure 2, referred to as In-Domain (ID) languages, we evaluate our model on both In-Domain and Out-of-Domain (OOD) languages. For the Out-of-Domain set, we selected 11 low-resource languages, chosen for their strong representation within their respective clusters and to ensure coverage across diverse language groups. In our evaluation, we utilize six safety classification datasets: Aegis-Content-Safety-2.0-Test (Aegis-CS2, 2025), HarmBench (Mazeika et al., 2024), Redteam2k (Luo et al., 2024), JBB-Behaviors featuring subsets Behaviors (JBB-Behav) and Judge-comparison (JBB-Judge) (Chao et al., 2024), and StrongReject (Souly et al., 2024). Collectively, these datasets span a variety of harm categories, including Hate/Identity Hate, Sexual, Suicide/Self-Harm, Violence, Guns/Illegal Weapons, PII/Privacy, Sexual Minor, Toxicity, Abuse, among others, rendering them suitable for a comprehensive assessment of safety mechanisms. For our multilingual safety guardrail, we employ the XLM-RoBERTa-Base with 279 million parameters and the XLM-RoBERTa-Large with 560 million parameters as the foundational multilingual encoders. These models, pre-trained on 100 languages, offer robust cross-lingual representations and align with our generalization goals. We append a single-layer classification head atop the encoder, matching the hidden size of the encoder outputs, complemented by a binary output layer. The training process involves full-weight training of both the pre-trained encoder and the classification head using the aggregated dataset. All training and evaluations are conducted on an NVIDIA H100 GPU cluster with Bfloat16 precision for enhanced efficiency and performance. For baseline comparisons, we assess our model against smaller guardrails such as DuoGuard-0.5B (2025), PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen-Smol, and WalledGuard-C (2024), alongside larger guardrails including PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen (2025), LlamaGuard3 (2024), and Aegis-Defensive (2025). These baselines cover a spectrum of language support from monolingual English models to multilingual models supporting up to 29 languages. This diversity permits us to thoroughly evaluate the scalability, efficiency, and robustness of our proposed safety guardrail across both high-resource and low-resource language environments.

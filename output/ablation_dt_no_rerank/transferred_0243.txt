In this section, our objective is to cultivate a thorough understanding of the factors that contribute to effective e-commerce search and, consequently, to identify the essential tools and methodologies for enhancing this process. At its core, e-commerce search is the alignment of user queries with product offerings. However, improving this alignment requires the assessment of relevance, which encompasses both explicit elements such as attributes and constraints and subjective user preferences. To do this, we decompose user search intent into 18 distinct categories, including type, brand, and style. For each query-product pairing, the extent to which the product satisfies the search intent is evaluated, and these evaluations are used to determine match relevance. This understanding provides a framework to facilitate analysis of relevance determination processes. In the appraisal of relevance within e-commerce search mechanisms, attention is directed towards three integral components: understanding product characteristics, interpreting search queries, and evaluating relevance. The evaluation phase necessitates examining two primary aspects of a product: its attributes and its presentation. Importantly, attributes can be categorized as either inherent—those readily apparent and discernible—or derived, which require deeper analysis and comprehension. Typically, inherent attributes such as category, brand, model, and price exhibit straightforward alignment with search queries. In contrast, derived attributes necessitate more profound comprehension and reasoning, as they pertain to functional characteristics or experiential qualities of a product. Moreover, identifying and interpreting these attributes is complicated by the idiosyncratic terminologies and descriptive conventions prevalent within various industries.

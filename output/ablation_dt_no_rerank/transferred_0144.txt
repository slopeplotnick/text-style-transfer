Other methodologies include CoT2 (developed by Gozeten et al., 2025), which emulates training-free techniques to generate token mixtures applied to the GPT-2 model for logical reasoning tasks. Additionally, CODI, introduced by Shen et al. (2025b), utilizes a teacher-student distillation framework to enhance the performance of both GPT-2 and Llama 3.1 1B models on GSM8k tasks. Moreover, Token Assorted, described by Su et al. (2025), innovatively develops new token embeddings using a VQ-VAE method, facilitating the training of Llama 3.1 8B for mathematical and logical reasoning applications. Our LiteReason approach diverges from these strategies in several ways. Firstly, unlike reliance on extensive datasets like GSM8k-Aug for reasoning traces—which are unavailable for our tasks—LiteReason exclusively utilizes traces generated internally by the model. Secondly, we focus on training a compact Reasoning Projector for latent reasoning rather than the entire model, based on our hypothesis that excessive training of the base LLM for token embedding generation may destabilize its inherent token generation capabilities. Thirdly, we allow interleaving latent and discrete reasoning during reinforcement learning (RL) training, positing that the model will eventually discern an optimal balance between them. Finally, while several existing approaches compare their outcomes to Chain-of-Thought prompting, and some embed RL into their protocols, to our knowledge, none have contrasted their results against a non-latent RL-trained model. We consider this comparison as a more rigorous performance measure for complex tasks, thus integrating it into our evaluations to establish benchmarks for top performance and computational constraints. Although much progress has been made in reasoning tasks within domains such as mathematics and coding, significant scholarly attention has also been directed toward the application of large language models (LLMs) in narrative comprehension and generation (Mostafazadeh et al., 2016; Fan et al., 2018; Huot et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2022, 2023; Xie and Riedl, 2024; Chhun et al., 2022; Karpinska et al., 2024; Sprague et al., 2023). For instance, numerous studies on story generation encourage models to perform specific sub-tasks such as planning plot events or developing character details (Huot et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023, 2022), which exemplify different reasoning styles. However, these tasks pose challenges for adaptation to the RL domain due to the absence of explicit reward mechanisms. In this study, we concentrate on two tasks specifically designed to evaluate reasoning capabilities derived from narrative-based contexts.

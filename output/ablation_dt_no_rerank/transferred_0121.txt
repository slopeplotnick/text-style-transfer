Nonparametric comparisons between authentic and simulated datasets showed no significant differences in central tendency across datasets, as supported by Mann-Whitney U tests. However, Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests revealed significant discrepancies in score distributions (D = 0.36-0.42, all p < .001). These results indicate that while the simulated data mirrored real-world data in terms of mean values, their distribution patterns diverged considerably. Accordingly, these outcomes partially validate our fourth hypothesis. Figure 2 presents histograms that illustrate the differing distributions between the real and simulated datasets. To avoid biases stemming from self-collected datasets, we employed an independent cohort for subsequent analysis. Specifically, we utilized the English validation dataset of the "Information and Communication Self-Concept, 25 Items" (ICT-SC25). This scale assesses individuals’ perceived competence with technological tasks and consists of 25 Likert-type items, categorized into five domains: communication, information management, content creation, safe utilization, and problem-solving. The selection of this dataset was driven by its comprehensive validation and public availability. Consistent with previous study protocols, we preregistered our research plans before generating LLM-simulated data, with details accessible via OSF preregistration. Notably, the LLM-simulated data generation involved two key adjustments. Firstly, due to the absence of ethnicity data, prompts were modified to include only age and gender as matching variables. Secondly, the ICT-SC25 dataset provided unique participant IDs, facilitating exact alignment between simulated and real samples based on age and gender, which enhanced pairwise comparisons in hypotheses H3.1 and H3.2. Duplicate IDs were removed to ensure precise conformity. The final sample comprising 331 participants surpassed the initial target of 300.

Because concepts are not explicitly defined in the data features, it is common to employ probing techniques to identify neurons, or combinations of neurons, that best represent target concepts. Generally, these probes are linear, though they may be non-linear when concepts are not linearly separable. Moreover, probes can be trained using both supervised and unsupervised approaches. The supervised approach requires labelled data for the specific concept defined by humans, whereas the unsupervised approach identifies concepts that are crucial to the model but may not necessarily be interpretable by humans. The present work extends the modeling of structural equations beyond mere observation-based estimations, under general assumptions. It explores various modeling levels, ranging from Functional Structural Causal Models (FSCMs) designed for clear interpretation through domain-specific knowledge, to Parametric Structural Causal Models (PSCMs) that encompass all FSCMs consistent with the data. This highlights the significance of modeling choices concerning structural functions and underscores the utility of partial information, such as counterfactual intervals, especially when complete FSCMs are unavailable. Galhotra et al. [2021] introduce an explanation methodology founded on FSCMs to compute probabilities of sufficiency and necessity. This approach, tailored to tabular data, incorporates causal modeling into the feature space, assuming these features denote high-level concepts and permitting the integration of additional concepts not inherent to the initial model input. However, the method is only applicable when the features are meaningful concepts, thus negating the need for a causal generator. Furthermore, it is assumed that there exists a preference regarding the model's output to be explained. Consequently, explanations are framed in terms of which subsets of input values are sufficient to alter a model's prediction to a more preferable one, or which values are necessary to maintain a preferable prediction whenever the desired outcome remains unchanged.

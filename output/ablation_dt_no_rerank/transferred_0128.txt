Unlike general-purpose VLMs, automotive AI systems must operate under stringent regulations, which demand latency with rapid response times within milliseconds to prevent driver distraction and maintain safety. Moreover, these systems are bound by strict power consumption constraints dictated by the vehicle's electrical configuration. Furthermore, they must efficiently function in extreme environmental conditions such as high illumination during daylight or complete darkness at night, temperature extremes, and vehicular movement-induced vibrations, compounded by diverse user interactions. These constraints necessitate leveraging specialized hardware architectures for precise and rapid processing, ensuring consistent low-latency performance while maintaining high accuracy across various automotive-specific visual and linguistic domains. We employ the Infinity-MM dataset, a comprehensive multimodal instruction dataset of approximately 44.8 million instances designed to enhance AI capabilities across diverse tasks involving visual and textual data. The dataset is meticulously curated, balancing multiple task categories including inquiries about everyday phenomena depicted in images, object recognition, scene interpretation, document comprehension, chart and diagram analysis, OCR tasks, multi-turn conversational exchanges, and specialized domains like medical imagery, autonomous driving scenarios, and industrial inspection. Each dataset entry consists of an image paired with an instruction sequence and reference responses, emphasizing high-quality responses that necessitate a multi-step reasoning process, instead of selecting simple labels. This approach is vital for automotive AI systems to elucidate their decision-making processes and foster user confidence. We follow a systematic four-step training methodology aligned with the Infinity-MM training protocol; during the second phase, comprehensive visual task training occurs, focusing on object recognition, scene interpretation, and foundational visual question answering. At this stage, we unfreeze all parameters including the vision encoder and language model, employing a learning rate of 1e-5 for 1 epoch. Initially, a smaller batch size of 16 is used to prevent gradient explosion, gradually increasing to an effective batch size of 512 through gradient accumulation in subsequent steps.

Test-time learning (TTL) represents a promising paradigm, enabling models to adjust themselves during deployment. Early approaches concentrated on minimalistic techniques that adapt without parameter updates, such as entropy minimization or batch normalization. Building on these foundational ideas, more recent methodologies have embraced test-time training (TTT) with explicit parameter updates during inference, significantly boosting task-specific adaptability. Empirical findings demonstrate that TTT enhances models' performance in dynamic environments, with some methods allowing large language models to autonomously fine-tune based on beneficial feedback. A novel direction within this realm is test-time policy optimization, which frames the inference process as a reinforcement learning challenge. Techniques like RLVR and TTRL utilize feedback from multiple iterations, such as majority voting, to effectively guide policy updates. Ensuring models can consistently reason accurately without labeled data is critical. Nevertheless, a notable challenge persists: current methods often require multiple iterations per query for dependable results, demanding substantial time and computational resources. While straightforward queries may resolve quickly, complex ones incur considerable computational expense, thus rendering the process inefficient and costly. In response, we introduce a framework termed OptPO to enhance procedural efficacy. Rather than pre-setting the number of iterations, OptPO employs a sequential testing approach, halting once enough evidence is collected to provide a definitive answer within a preset error threshold. The completed iterations are then used to optimize the model's parameters, ensuring judicious allocation of computational resources. Furthermore, OptPO is compatible with supervised fine-tuning methodologies for test-time training.

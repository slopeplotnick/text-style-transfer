Cross-model and cross-algorithm generalization. The proposed system demonstrates robust efficacy across a variety of model architectures and algorithms, indicating that our approach is architecture-agnostic. Consistent enhancements observed in both Qwen2.5 and Llama models underscore this point. Even the smaller Llama-3.2-1B model exhibits significant accuracy improvements when employing our OptPO-SFT and OptPO techniques over conventional methodologies, highlighting that the proposed adaptation mechanism scales robustly across model sizes. Moreover, the OptPO framework integrates seamlessly with different reinforcement learning paradigms such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO), and Reinforce++, consistently outperforming TTRL with higher accuracy and substantial efficiency gains, which indicates its broad effectiveness across various RL algorithms. SFT-based vs. RL-based variants. In juxtaposing the SFT-based and RL-based OptPO variants, the latter generally exhibits superior accuracy, particularly in mathematical reasoning benchmarks. This superiority can be attributed to its enhanced utilization of stochastic feedback from rollouts compared to deterministic pseudo-label fine-tuning. Nonetheless, OptPO-SFT demonstrates stable enhancements over TTSFT across all settings, validating that our adaptive pseudo-labeling mechanism significantly enhances SFT-style test-time training. Takeaways. Our results demonstrate that (1) the implementation of adaptive early-stopping via Bayesian Sequential Probability Ratio Test (BSPRT) enhances computational efficiency without sacrificing accuracy; (2) OptPO offers the strongest balance between accuracy and computational resource utilization; and (3) OptPO-SFT provides a lightweight alternative for rapid adaptation when RL-style updates are infeasible. Collectively, these mechanisms establish a robust methodology for model training during test-time in reasoning tasks. This paper introduces OptPO, the inaugural statistically grounded framework for efficient test-time policy optimization, addressing the inherent inefficiencies associated with majority-vote-based rollouts in reinforcement learning applied to large language models. By formulating early-stopping consensus estimation as a Bayesian sequential probability ratio test, OptPO adaptively allocates rollouts based on posterior evidence rather than predetermined limits, thereby guaranteeing dependable pseudo-labels without excessive sampling. The resulting framework is straightforward, plug-and-play, and compatible with established policy optimization techniques such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, alongside supervised test-time fine-tuning. During evaluations across diverse models and tasks, OptPO consistently retains or enhances accuracy while reducing computational overhead. More broadly, this study demonstrates that efficient, label-free adaptation is both feasible and imperative as large language models are increasingly deployed in unpredictable real-world environments. It is anticipated that OptPO will serve as a fundamental basis for future research on compute-efficient test-time learning.

Substantial progress has been made in identifying bias, reducing toxicity, and conducting fairness audits. However, these methodologies typically focus on isolated model outputs rather than examining the progression of ethical reasoning or transformations in value alignment over time as the models engage with varied, context-specific, and dynamically evolving scenarios. This limitation is particularly critical in the context of large language model-based agentic systems, which demonstrate adaptive and context-sensitive behavior, continually modifying their outputs in response to the structure of prompts, user intentions, task requirements, and previous interactions. Consequently, a lack of longitudinal consistency engenders the risk of moral drift, whereby ethical perspectives may fluctuate or degrade in response to linguistic nuances, contextual exigencies, and temporal evolution. Such inconsistencies threaten the reliability, societal trustworthiness, and accountability of contemporary AI systems. Therefore, maintaining ethical consistency transcends mere regulatory adherence, representing a pivotal factor in ensuring that AI systems are capable of stable, interpretable, and trustworthy reasoning over time. In the development of systems operating within critical domains such as healthcare, finance, education, and governance, even minor ethical deviations can propagate misinformation, reinforce cognitive biases, and yield outcomes incongruent with human values. Despite increasing awareness of these issues, most prevailing auditing methodologies are constrained by static benchmarks and immovable, human-annotated datasets that inadequately accommodate the dynamic and contextually contingent behavior of LLMs. Thus, a significant research challenge lies in the design of an adaptive, self-contained mechanism capable of autonomously assessing and interpreting the ethical stability of these models, independent of external datasets or post-hoc analysis. To address this challenge, this study introduces the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP), an entirely autonomous ethical evaluation methodology that operates independently of established datasets and is designed to continuously monitor, analyze, and enhance the moral reasoning capabilities of AI models. Unlike conventional evaluation methodologies predicated on predetermined test corpora and external annotation, MoCoP functions as a self-sufficient system, generating ethical scenarios, eliciting model responses, and employing structured interpretation through internally defined principles and semantic heuristics. The framework employs three primary analytical components—lexical integrity analysis, semantic risk estimation, and reasoning-based judgment modeling—to derive comprehensive measures of moral coherence and ethical stability. Each component fulfills a distinct role: lexical integrity analysis identifies bias and linguistic degradation; semantic risk estimation assesses potential semantic challenges; and reasoning-based judgment modeling elucidates decision-making processes.

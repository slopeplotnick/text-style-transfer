In expressions like "pearl white" or "ivory white," the core need is for a specific shade of white, not for "pearl" or "ivory" themselves. Similarly, "phone gas station" signifies the need for a power bank, with "gas station" serving as a metaphor. In these cases, higher-level semantic understanding and knowledge interact tightly, rendering pure semantic parsing insufficient. Furthermore, domain-specific scenarios require niche characteristic attributes or derived attributes that cannot be inferred from surface forms alone and must rely on structured domain knowledge. Examples include jargon in vertical e-commerce, shop and brand taxonomies, model coding rules, and category-specific descriptions of functions or effects, such as mouthfeel descriptions relevant to tea or wine. However, other challenges arise from misspellings and aliases, where users may express needs using typos, homophones, aliases, or industry slang. Therefore, this demands robust integration of general and domain knowledge, as the model needs capabilities in spelling correction, variant normalization, and alias alignment. Moreover, intent recognition (entity-relation recognition) depends heavily on reasoning at both the methodological and implementation levels. Intent recognition seeks to determine whether a query asks for items themselves or item-related information, like reviews, comparisons, and usage guides, and distinguishes between the main target entities and modifiers or constraints. The main challenges include accurately identifying the purchase purpose and item usage, thereby locating the true target of the need. For instance, in "charger/headphones for iPhone 17," the target items are the charger or headphones, while "iPhone 17" only imposes compatibility or model constraints, corresponding to a need that combines functionality and model specifics. Additionally, handling abstract needs that do not point to specific items but to categories or domains poses another challenge. For example, "phones with the best battery life" elicits a ranking or filtering request over the entire item space, requiring the system to capture the semantic meaning of "battery life" in the phone domain and its operational measurement, followed by constructing logical ranking or filtering rules to support inference and comparison over candidate sets. Moreover, ambiguity caused by missing context must be mitigated, as users often fail to specify all constraints or preferences in a single query. Consequently, the system must exploit dialogue history, user profiles, and default preferences to fill in the gaps and adequately fulfill user needs.

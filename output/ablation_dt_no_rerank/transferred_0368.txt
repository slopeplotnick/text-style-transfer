Problem difficulty is associated with inherent complexity, whereas both intra-family and cross-family verifier improvements display clear positive correlations. Notably, AIME emerges as an anomaly in the final plot, potentially because certain models encounter similar challenges during post-training. Moreover, the best-fit lines for verifier accuracy delineate two distinct clusters of tasks (designated in red and blue), suggesting that verification difficulty cannot be attributed solely to solver accuracy. This observation raises the question: Do some tasks inherently lend themselves to more accessible verification than others? Relatedly, Song et al. (2025) identified that models struggle to enhance performance on tasks needing factual recall but observed that performance on Tokoku improves with sufficient pretraining. A similar pattern is evident in Figure 7, where tasks like AIME, GSM8K, 3SAT, and Tokoku show a higher ratio of verifier accuracy to solver accuracy, yielding enhanced performance across various verification contexts. To elucidate this further, among our synthetic datasets, Selenium and 3SAT exemplify problems requiring extended computational time for solving but allow rapid verification. Conversely, there is no straightforward way to verify the product of two matrices without essentially recomputing it for Matrix Multiplication. Among real-world datasets, GSM8K and AIME involve problems solvable with high-school-level mathematics, whereas MMLU (Social Sciences) demands domain-specific knowledge. Moreover, CommonsenseQA relies on implicit world knowledge, while GPQA and MMLU (STEM) necessitate specialized natural science knowledge. For these latter tasks, verifying an answer requires essentially the same knowledge as solving the problem. Takeaways: Although tasks that are easy to solve are typically easier to verify, some tasks are inherently easier to verify. These include synthetic problems characterized by logical or structured reasoning (e.g., 3SAT and Sudo) and real-world tasks relying primarily on mathematical reasoning rather than extensive factual recall (e.g., GSM8K and AIME). Additionally, these tasks benefit significantly from the implementation of test-time rejection sampling facilitated by verifiers.

What do individuals consider when evaluating AI-generated music? Current study methodologies, such as those by Hong et al. (2022) and Moura & Maw (2021), often utilize rating scales adapted from related domains. However, these measures might not adequately capture the intricacies of how people truly experience and evaluate AI-generated music. Therefore, our exploratory approach seeks to uncover the subjective criteria and themes guiding participants' judgments through open-ended responses. For this purpose, music samples were created using AI tools like Stable Audio (v.2.0) and Suno (v.3.5). To ensure ecological validity, we based our prompts on insights from the Emotion-to-Music Atlas (EMMA; Strauss et al., 2024), which is an extensive database containing human emotion annotations for a diverse range of music excerpts. Specifically, we utilized recent GEMS9 (Zentner et al., 2008) annotations for 453 music excerpts sourced from the Music4All-Onion database (Moscati et al., 2022). These excerpts span five major genres, each with three distinct subgenres: (1) Electronic (Trance, Downtempo, Dubstep), (2) Jazz (Bossa-Nova, Smooth-Jazz, Swing), (3) Pop (Europop, Indie-Pop, Pop-Punk), (4) Rock (Soft-Rock, RockBallad, Doom-Metal), and (5) Soul/Funk (Funk, Disco, Soul). For each subgenre, we developed prompts based on the most prominent emotion dimensions, determined by the average values within the subgenre in the EMMA and their respective GEMS-9 descriptors —for example, "a joyful, bouncy, animated trance song" representing the Joyful Activation dimension for Trance. Consequently, this led to crafting 2-3 prompts per subgenre, totaling 38 prompts. These prompts were then adapted for compatibility with Suno and Stable Audio, resulting in the creation of 76 stimuli. Moreover, to cater to the specific properties of each tool, for Suno, the prompts were generated in instrumental mode, with one of the outputs randomly selected and edited to ensure a duration of 45-60 seconds, thus preserving comparability with prior works (e.g., Strauss et al., 2024; Warrenburg, 2020). Conversely, Stable Audio required that the term 'instrumental' be included in each prompt, with the intended duration set to 60 seconds. Further details about the generation and editing procedures, as well as the audio samples, are available in the supplementary materials.

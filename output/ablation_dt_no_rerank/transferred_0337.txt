Given the nature of the relevance task, where categories L1 and L2 are classified as non-essential and categories L3 and L4 as essential, we introduced the binary classification accuracy metric (acc@2) to measure performance accuracy. Moreover, to address the imbalance in data label distribution, we introduce the macro F1 score, which is calculated as the average of F1 scores across all classes, thus providing a balanced metric. As comparison baselines, we evaluate two distinct categories of Language Model Systems (LMS): Firstly, top-tier large language models, available as either open-source or proprietary solutions. All models are evaluated using zero-shot direct prompting with a unified prompt template that delineates the task parameters and associated guidelines. Adhering strictly to established best practices, each model is compared under optimal conditions by employing single-pass inference, with temperature parameters set to the officially recommended values—such as 0.7 for GPT-4 and 0.6 for Llama-3—to ensure optimal performance. Secondly, we present the Vanilla SFT Model, trained using labeled data without incorporating techniques such as CoT or RL, serving to validate the joint contribution of CoT modeling and reinforcement learning optimization. This standard model yields significant results, attaining an Acc@2 of 0.929 and a Macro-F1 score of 0.722, surpassing even advanced models like GPT-4. In comparison, the LORE model further enhances Acc@2 by 0.4 percentage points, indicating that while Vanilla SFT can achieve satisfactory results with in-distribution routine data, the LORE model obtains marginal performance gains beyond this robust baseline.

Search relevance is crucial for e-commerce platforms such as JD and Taobao. It involves evaluating candidate items to determine their alignment with user queries by assigning relevance scores, thus filtering out unsuitable products. Consequently, this mechanism significantly enhances the quality and efficacy of the user search experience. Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable proficiency across various applications and have been extensively applied to relevance tasks. However, even state-of-the-art (SOTA) models like GPT-5 face challenges, such as insufficient domain-specific knowledge and high operational costs. Therefore, researchers have shifted towards training smaller language models on domain-specific datasets derived from consumer purchasing data to develop relevance experts. Recent advancements have transitioned from basic training techniques to more sophisticated methodologies that enhance problem-solving capabilities. Although efforts have been made to teach models systematic reasoning processes, challenges persist in complex scenarios. For example, the model ELLM conceptualizes the relevance task as analyzing product information and matching it with user queries. However, it struggles with intricate situations requiring adherence to specific rules, such as distinguishing between color nuances like "lake blue" and "sky blue." In response, models like LREF and TaoSR1 attempt to address this shortcoming by incorporating rule-following processes, yet they remain unable to detect discrepancies due to their lack of visual information processing. For instance, when an item is labeled as a "blue top," but its image specifies the shade, these models fail to make accurate judgments because they do not analyze visual data. These limitations arise from neglecting a fundamental question: What essential competencies must a model possess to effectively manage these tasks? This inquiry pertains to the requisite capabilities for a model to accurately assess relevance.

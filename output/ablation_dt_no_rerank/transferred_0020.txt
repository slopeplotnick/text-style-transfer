Large language models (LLMs) are progressively being utilized as autonomous evaluators for assessing the outputs produced by other computational systems, a concept referred to as "Thinking-LLM-as-a-Judge." This approach offers a more economical and accelerated alternative to human evaluation, which tends to be slow and expensive. To address the inherent randomness and potential errors associated with single-pass judgments, substantial computational resources are often used to generate multiple estimations and assessments for each item under review. However, the efficacy of the final decision is critically dependent on how these various outputs are synthesized. Traditional aggregation methods, such as majority voting or reliance on model confidence scores, frequently exhibit limitations and are statistically suboptimal. Moreover, these approaches show pronounced vulnerabilities, especially in scenarios with tied results. For example, merely counting votes fails to distinguish between a narrow 5-to-4 decision and a definitive 9-to-0 consensus, thereby neglecting significant insights into the strength of the evidence. This oversight regarding evidential potency results in less reliable evaluations. In our research, therefore, we emphasize the paramount importance of the aggregation process as an essential aspect of effectively harnessing computational capabilities. We propose a novel approach termed Distribution-Calibrated Aggregation, which transcends simple vote-counting methodologies. Specifically, our method comprehensively analyzes the counts of positive, negative, and tie votes, thereby preserving the complete informational signal contained within the distribution. By modeling three possible outcomes—preference for one side, preference for the other, and the probability of ties—we aim to enhance the robustness of decision-making processes. Additionally, through the optimization of configuration settings derived from a preliminary test group and the application of a targeted error metric, our methodology faithfully adheres to the evaluation objectives while achieving a robust fit.

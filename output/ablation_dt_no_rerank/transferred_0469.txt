Probabilistic programming languages (PPL), equipped with first-class constructs for handling probabilistic elements like probability distributions and their samplers, are instrumental in probabilistic estimation and assertions. Numerous popular examples exist, across both imperative and functional paradigms. The principal shortcoming of current PPLs is their inadequate adaptation to text-rich graphs, essential data structures for specifying language model systems (LMS), thus necessitating the creation of LUMOS. Generally, these languages are generative, leveraging user-defined probabilistic distributions. For instance, specific languages enable the generation of random images or scenes in contexts such as autonomous driving by using user-defined scene graphs. However, when designing real-world specifications, it is crucial to ground them within a fixed underlying graph, from which subgraphs can be sampled to define scenarios. Conversely, domain-specific languages (DSLs) for large language models (LLMs) act as query languages devoid of probabilistic constructs. These DSLs offer interfaces for writing, optimizing, and securing prompts to achieve desirable responses from target LMS, serving as a complement to LUMOS. LUMOS programs evaluate LMS behavior across distributions of prompts programmatically defined in them. Furthermore, LMS alignment involves identifying vulnerabilities, either by benchmarking performance or through adversarial attacks. Recent developments have introduced probabilistic certifiers for specific LLM properties, providing formal assurances for LMS property assessments. In this study, we extend the LMS certification process with LUMOS to specify and certify a broader range of desirable properties.

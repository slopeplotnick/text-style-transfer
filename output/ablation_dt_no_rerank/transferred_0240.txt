The study indicated categories where certain tasks failed to execute entirely, thereby establishing performance benchmarks and confirming the need for architectural improvements. Despite these evaluation limitations, the study confirmed three critical findings: (1) the feasibility of LLM-driven coordination for multi-robot task allocation, (2) the significant enhancement of reasoning reliability through structured validation mechanisms, and (3) the effective handling of LLM output inconsistencies by hierarchical retry systems. With this LLM-MRTA feasibility established, the authors consequently developed the LTAA framework to enhance LLM-MRTA capabilities. Moreover, LTAA evaluation utilizes the TEACH dataset, which serves as the standard benchmark for comparing Q-learning, DQN, and DP methodologies in MRTA research. This dataset transition, from AI2-THOR household tasks to construction robotics scenarios, enables direct performance comparisons with established algorithmic baselines under identical evaluation conditions. Specifically, the LTAA framework specializes in the allocation component extracted from the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture, accepting pre-decomposed tasks, akin to traditional algorithms, and focusing exclusively on robot-task assignment decisions. This scope alignment ensures that both LLM-based and algorithm-based approaches are evaluated on equivalent problem formulations. Building further on the self-corrective principles previously established, the LTAA framework implements a comprehensive nine-node workflow. Unlike the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture, which managed full-pipeline coordination, the LTAA framework focuses exclusively on robot-task assignments that are comparable to traditional algorithmic outputs. Importantly, the LTAA framework introduces three core technical innovations that enable competitive performance with traditional optimization algorithms while maintaining reasoning transparency: (1) phase-adaptive allocation strategies that dynamically adjust decision priorities as projects progress, (2) multi-stage validation with hierarchical retry and self-correction mechanisms ensuring LLM reasoning quality, and (3) context-aware reasoning integration that structures allocation decisions with quantitative capability priors. These innovations are systematically implemented through a nine-node LangGraph workflow, which processes each task through structured reasoning stages.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first instance of systematically employing an open-vocabulary object detector to enhance the comprehension of high-resolution images by MLLMs. Experimental results demonstrate that the detector provides precise localization of targets while effectively suppressing interference from irrelevant regions. We propose MRD, a training-free and generic framework. It innovatively rectifies semantic similarity through a multi-resolution fusion strategy and incorporates open-set detection results to refine target regions, thus creating a synergistic effect. Extensive experimental validation attests to the efficacy and robustness of our method, showing superior performance on both single-object and multi-object tasks across various MLLMs and high-resolution benchmarks. To enhance the understanding of high-resolution images without proportionally increasing the complexity of visual tokens, several studies have integrated high-resolution visual encoders into MLLMs. For instance, Vary [23] and Deepseek-VL [15] utilize the SAM encoder [10] to improve model performance on high-resolution images. Alternatively, a different approach introduces Native/Dynamic-Resolution MLLMs, which process images at their native resolution. The principal idea here involves generating a sequence of visual tokens corresponding to the original dimensions of the image, thereby preserving spatial fidelity and high-frequency details. These models employ mechanisms such as sliding window attention, dynamic masking, and patch-based encoding strategies to manage the resulting long sequences and associated computational complexity. Noteworthy contributions within this domain have demonstrated considerable advancement. For instance, the InternVL series employs a technique of segmenting a high-resolution image into multiple fixed-size patches. In contrast, models such as Qwen2.5-VL adopt an end-to-end approach by training a ViT directly on images at their native resolution, thereby enabling the model to convert the entire image into a single, cohesive token sequence in one forward pass, potentially enhancing the understanding of global context.

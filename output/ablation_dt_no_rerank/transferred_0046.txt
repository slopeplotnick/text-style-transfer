We evaluated WorldMM using five long video question-answering benchmarks, ranging from hours to weeks in duration. Our proposed method consistently outperformed strong baselines, such as long video LLMs and memory-augmented models. Comprehensive ablation studies further demonstrated the effectiveness of our multi-memory, multiscale design. Specifically, episodic memory enables reasoning across multiple temporal scales, visual memory improves the model's response to object- and action-focused queries, and semantic memory enhances understanding of long-term contexts. When these memories are adaptively integrated, the model achieves optimal overall performance. These results highlight that our multimodal memory framework signifies a promising advancement in robust long video reasoning. While existing models perform well for short video understanding, recent research has increasingly focused on longer video analysis. State-of-the-art models like GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 have advanced to comprehend videos from minutes to hours by leveraging extended context lengths. However, these models still face high computational demands, and uniformly sampling each frame is often suboptimal for questions targeting localized events. To overcome these challenges, various strategies have been explored. Visual token compression enhances efficiency by reducing token counts but often loses fine-grained details, limiting the capture of subtle or sparse events. Key frame selection aims to reduce redundancy by retaining only the most informative frames, yet it struggles to detect relevant frames in lengthy video streams, potentially missing significant events. More recently, reasoning-centric training and testing have improved understanding of extended video content, but they still encounter limitations with ultra-long videos exceeding ten hours. With the introduction of new benchmarks, video processing is increasingly tested on continuous recordings that last days or even weeks. In these contexts, the previously mentioned strategies falter due to the massive volume of frames and intricate long-term temporal dependencies. This underscores the necessity for more efficient, context-aware, and scalable methodologies to handle exceedingly long videos.

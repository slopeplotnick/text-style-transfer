Retry with Feedback Integration governs the retry mechanism, permitting up to three attempts per task. Enhanced prompts amalgamate the initial context with precise feedback, further facilitating refined reasoning on reattempts. As illustrated in the preceding discourse, sophisticated methodologies translate strategic weights into contextual reasoning guidance according to the specific project phase. The prompts are adapted based on the development stage, each accentuating distinct priorities. Initially, the focus is on maximizing success rates, guided by directives such as "prioritize robots with the highest success rates" and "accept moderate workload imbalances"; however, subsequent phases emphasize workload equity with phrases like "favor workload equity" and "consider balance as primary factor." This configuration seamlessly aligns with the strategic frameworks established for each phase. The authors operationalize this integration through three mechanisms: (1) probabilistic robot capability modeling to compute task-specific success probabilities, (2) structured prompt generation encapsulating quantitative priors alongside workload information, and (3) standardized response formatting to ensure reliable parsing and validation. Robots are classified into Light, Medium, and Heavy categories, which reflect their varying capabilities and probabilities of task completion. The likelihood of successful task execution depends on the alignment between the robot's competencies and the task demands. For each robot type, the success rate is derived from an integrated assessment of its proficiency across distinct task characteristics. Accordingly, a robot's successful task execution hinges on its operational alignment with task requirements. The allocation process sequentially addresses each of the 36 tasks delineated within the TEACH dataset, encompassing phases such as task-specific determination, prompt generation, reasoning, validation, and task allocation prior to progressing to subsequent tasks. Upon completion of each task, the system updates its progress metrics and decreases the count of unassigned tasks, thus facilitating the eventual allocation of all pending tasks.

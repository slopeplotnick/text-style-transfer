Sampling different noise vectors in flow-matching or diffusion-based frameworks reveals how the stochasticity of the initial noise samples directly influences the resulting actions. In this paper, we address the distribution shift challenge from an anti-exploration perspective and propose Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-Counts (TACO). The anti-exploration principle, originating from offline reinforcement learning (RL), aims to prevent the policy from visiting states or actions outside the dataset's support. Analogously, during VLA inference, we strive to constrain the generated actions to remain within the support of successful patterns identified in the SFT dataset, thus avoiding the exploration of redundant or irrelevant action modes retained from pretraining or imperfect fine-tuning data. However, in TACO, anti-exploration is realized through test-time scaling (TTS) rather than policy optimization, due to the inherent complexities of flow-matching or diffusion-based VLA models that resist conventional RL-style updates. Instead, TTS refines the action sampling process without modifying the VLA model's parameters, eliminating the need for gradient computation. Moreover, we employ a classical Coin-Flipping Network (CFN) enhanced with an internal representation mechanism to estimate pseudo-counts for each observation-instruction-action pair in the SFT dataset. Essentially, an action component with a higher pseudo-count aligns more closely with frequently observed, successful behaviors in the SFT data, and is therefore preferred. During decision-making, TACO utilizes CFN-derived pseudo-counts as a validation tool to select the most appropriate action component from various candidates, ultimately executing the one with the highest pseudo-count. Furthermore, to mitigate the computational overhead associated with processing multiple tests, we introduce a shared observation key-value cache, which significantly reduces latency by reusing visual-language representations across samples.

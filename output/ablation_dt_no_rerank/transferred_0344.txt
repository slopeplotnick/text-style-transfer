An initial experiment conducted in the MODOMA laboratory simulation environment was based on a particular correlation. This experiment offers a method to assess the MODOMA approach to language acquisition by utilizing the well-established principle of Zipf's law. Thus, this study significantly contributes to research development by enabling the gradual evolution of MODOMA through the implementation of increasingly complex experimental designs. As a result, experience with MODOMA as a laboratory model for language acquisition experiments can be enhanced, allowing the system to be tested and refined before the implementation of alternative language acquisition methodologies. Although Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) focus on language emergence, their work shares similarities regarding the application of Zipf's law to enhance understanding of language models vis-à-vis human languages. Notably, this procedure incorporates most of the components and methodologies required for orchestrating more intricate language acquisition experiments. Accordingly, the individual components of the MODOMA system, along with their interactions, can be validated and refined to facilitate further computational language acquisition experiments. Upon successful acquisition, the daughter agent learns to distinguish between functional and content categories based on samples of the target language presented by the mother agent during interactions. Crucially, as soon as the daughter agent internalizes this grammatical distinction, it promptly applies the new grammatical insights to update its own grammar, generate novel utterances during dialogues with the mother agent, and accurately parse new sentences introduced by the mother. Consequently, this study provides an important indication of whether the MODOMA laboratory approach to language acquisition can result in the acquisition of substantial grammatical knowledge that can be used productively during parsing and generation.

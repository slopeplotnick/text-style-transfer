We begin with a foundational model developed solely using the EMER dataset, which offers high-quality but limited supervision. However, the integration of emotion-consistent data augmentation significantly improves all consistency metrics, confirming the importance of providing emotionally reliable samples during the early stages of training. Furthermore, the application of MI-based reasoning reordering yields additional gains by directing the model to initiate reasoning from the emotion-dominant modality. This sequence organization around MI mitigates visually biased or text-driven drift in the initial reasoning stage. In the subsequent stage, we assess the impact of each proposed reward on maintaining reasoning stability and coherence. Specifically, the Modality-Aligned Order Reward encourages the model to initiate reasoning from the modality prioritized by MI, effectively restoring the modality-first structure previously acquired during SFT. Moreover, the Modality-Grounded Reasoning Reward further ensures that the reasoning aligns with the designated target emotion, promoting causal and semantically consistent evidence. When both rewards are applied together, the highest consistency across all metrics is observed, demonstrating their complementary benefits. In this study, we aimed to enhance the reliability of reasoning-based multimodal emotion understanding by addressing the early-stage reasoning drift common in MLLMs. To achieve this, we introduced MI and developed MIGR, a training framework designed to initiate and sustain reasoning from the modality most relevant to the target emotion. Our two-stage approach ensures that both supervised and reinforcement learning processes align with the emotion-dominant modality, resulting in explanations that are more coherent, emotionally grounded, and causally meaningful. Experiments on the MAFW and DFEW benchmarks show that MIGR greatly reduces emotionally inconsistent reasoning. Overall, MIGR advances toward more trustworthy and interpretable multimodal emotional reasoning, underscoring the value of integrating modality dominance into both data organization and optimization processes.

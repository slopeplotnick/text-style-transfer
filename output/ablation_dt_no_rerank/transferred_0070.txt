We reproduce standard baseline methods, such as majority, random, and SVM classifiers. Moreover, we generate results for Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), and Decision Tree (DT) classifiers, which are considered benchmark methods. We then fine-tune several transformer-based models, including DistilBERT, BanglaBERT, mBERT, and XLM-RoBERTa, providing a detailed evaluation of their performance. Additionally, we offer a comprehensive analysis of modeling challenges and dataset limitations. Social media has become an integral component of daily life, facilitating rapid communication, easy information sharing, and the exchange of opinions across geographic boundaries. However, this freedom of expression has also led to the proliferation of hate speech and harsh language. Regrettably, little emphasis has been placed on detecting hate speech in Bangla social media due to a notable absence of high-quality datasets. The language style used on social media significantly diverges from that found in traditional print media, posing substantial challenges in automatically detecting Bangla hate speech. Traditionally, the task of identifying hate speech has been treated as a supervised classification problem. Initial research relied on lexicon-based and rule-based heuristics, which often failed to detect implicit or context-dependent hate speech. More recent studies employ machine learning classifiers, such as logistic regression and SVMs, using established supervised learning methodologies to identify vulnerabilities and potentially harmful online content.

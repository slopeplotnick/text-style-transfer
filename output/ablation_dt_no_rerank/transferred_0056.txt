Intuitively, the model benefits significantly by learning from its own mistakes, whereas the use of highly unrelated negative examples can be detrimental, potentially leading to misleading or destabilizing the optimization process. It has been empirically observed that Off-Policy Sequence Masking improves stability in certain training environments that would otherwise exhibit instability. Moreover, Keep Routing Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models enhance computational efficiency by selectively activating a subset of expert modules during inference. However, discrepancies between inference and training processes, along with policy updates, can lead to inconsistent expert usage even when presented with identical inputs. Such inconsistency can induce abrupt changes in the active parameter subspace, ultimately disrupting optimization and exacerbating off-policy issues. To address this, we preserve the expert routing paths used during inference and enforce their use during training, thereby ensuring the optimization of consistent expert parameters. This Keep Routing operation is crucial for maintaining stability in MoE models during RL training and has been integrated into our RL training framework since the introduction of DeepSeek-V3-0324. DeepSeek-R1 has demonstrated that incorporating a cognitive process can significantly enhance a model's proficiency in addressing complex problems. Building on this insight, we aim to integrate cognitive capabilities into tool-calling scenarios. However, replicating the methodology used by DeepSeek-R1, wherein reasoning content is discarded upon receipt of a second round of messages, results in substantial token inefficiency. This necessitates the model to redundantly re-evaluate the entire problem for each subsequent tool invocation. To mitigate this inefficiency, we developed a context management system explicitly designed for tool-calling scenarios, as depicted in Fig 4. Historical reasoning content is discarded only when a new user message is introduced into the interaction. Conversely, if only tool-related messages (such as tool outputs) are incorporated, the reasoning content is retained throughout the interaction. When reasoning traces are omitted, the historical record of tool calls and their respective outcomes is preserved within the context.

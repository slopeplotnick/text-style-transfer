Model comparison was conducted among several methods, including RL-Trained, Mixture-of-Inputs, CoLaR, and LiteReason. We used Qwen 2.5 7B-Instruct to generate chapters based on plans created by each method and performed 20 pairwise comparisons for every method combination. The relative strengths of these models were analyzed using the Bradley-Terry model, with additional methodological details found in Appendix E. Initially, we executed ablations on the Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks, with results displayed in Table 4. LiteReason variants were benchmarked against two baseline models, both built upon the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct architecture: one employing straightforward prompting and the other enhanced by reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. Notably, neither baseline incorporated latent reasoning capabilities. Interestingly, LiteReason w/o RP (simply skipping reasoning steps) results in longer reasoning processes and worse performance than the RL-Trained baseline. Table 7 provides comparisons with and without the implicit-thought prompt across different models. Additionally, we examined the frequency of 'latent thoughts' during training to assess RL's impact on their occurrence. Initially, RL training showed a sharp increase in latent-thought usage, though this trend decreased by the end of training. Future work may explore enhancing latent reasoning by modifying prompts or increasing the probability of using implicit markers. While LiteReason achieved only modest improvements (0.8% on Flawed Fictions and 58% on NCP), this may be due to its similarity to the RL-Trained baseline, yet it explores reasoning paths the foundational model might not independently generate. Unsurprisingly, training-free methods, Mixture-of-Inputs (MoI) and Soft Thinking, closely align with the untrained model in performance and token length. On the NCP task, Soft Thinking performs slightly better than the baseline but produces more tokens, whereas MoI underperforms with fewer tokens. Comparative discrepancies on the Flawed Fictions task remain within confidence intervals. Further research is essential to consistently improve efficiency and performance across tasks and reasoning styles for training-free methodologies.

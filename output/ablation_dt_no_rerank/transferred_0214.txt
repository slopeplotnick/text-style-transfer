The phenomenon wherein adversarial training enhances the transferability of attacks is attributed not to specific model architectures but to the alteration in how models process information due to adversarial training. Notably, this occurs across various model designs, highlighting its significance for comprehensive security considerations. To elucidate the mechanisms by which adversarial training augments transferability, researchers have used integrated gradients to analyze models’ decision-making explanations. In a comparative study of two variants of the DenseNet121 architecture—one with adversarial training (AT) and another traditionally trained (ST)—it was observed that the AT model directed focus towards salient image regions, such as the head, body, and legs of an ostrich, emphasizing significant object-level features. Conversely, the ST model predominantly concentrated on localized, textural elements such as feather patterns and background segments. These findings have substantial implications: AT models demonstrate a reliance on overarching visual features prevalent across diverse images, while ST models tend to prioritize intricate details with limited transferability across different model architectures. Moreover, adversarial examples generated by AT models exhibit superior cross-model transferability as they exploit features common across many models. For instance, since DenseNet121 and DenseNet201 share similar image processing methodologies, models like MobileNetV3, influenced by EfficientNet's design paradigms, are particularly susceptible to adversarial attacks originating from models based on EfficientNet.

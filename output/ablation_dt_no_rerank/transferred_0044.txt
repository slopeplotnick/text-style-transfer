Human comprehension of videos relies on the recognition of unfolding events, the causation behind actions, and the identification of crucial moments. However, traditional video object segmentation (VOS) methods primarily exploit appearance-based or categorical cues. In contrast, reasoning VOS elevates this task by requiring models to parse abstract, underspecified instructions, draw on commonsense and causal reasoning, and integrate temporal dynamics with semantic knowledge to discern the target, such as "the runner most likely to win" or "the object causing the accident." Although this approach simplifies certain aspects, it also introduces complexities in interpretation, alters model functionalities, and demands extensive data for fine-tuning. This underscores the importance of deliberate decision-making, rather than reliance on instantaneous conclusions. A model must therefore determine where to focus, when to attend, and which entity the query refers to. Motivated by this principle, we introduce ReVSeg, which eschews latent segmentation tokens and instead reformulates reasoning video segmentation into an explicit sequence of reasoning steps. ReVSeg decomposes reasoning VOS into three components aligned with model capabilities: video understanding, which involves interpreting the query and analyzing scene dynamics; temporal grounding, which requires identifying key frames or intervals pertinent to the query; and spatial grounding, which pertains to localizing the target objects within selected frames. These procedural stages are orchestrated within a single model, ensuring that insights derived from early steps are sustained in subsequent phases. This structured method preserves pretraining alignment, avoids the necessity for extensive retraining, and transforms a complex task into a structured process. Once the reasoning steps are delineated, the central challenge is enhancing their quality. Supervised learning falls short here, as intermediate steps lack comprehensive annotation. Therefore, we employ reinforcement learning (RL), conceptualizing reasoning VOS as a behavior subject to reinforcement, conditioned upon achieving the correct outcome across the entire trajectory. However, applying RL to reasoning VOS is challenging due to sparse success signals and the need for coordination across comprehension, temporal selection, and spatial identification. To address these difficulties, we introduce rewards at pivotal decision points, ensuring that RL augments the model's reasoning capabilities. Together with task decomposition, RL transforms the process from an implicit estimation task into a structured reasoning strategy: decomposition delineates what the model must decide, while RL elucidates how these decisions should integrate. This collaborative approach establishes a reasoning strategy characterized by internal consistency and empirical robustness.

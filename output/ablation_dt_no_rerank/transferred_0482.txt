Ensuring consistent theme color roles across a deck, fixing layout and format across related slides, and translating slide content while maintaining charts and structure is crucial. To our knowledge, PPTArena is the pioneering benchmark that first treats PPT editing as a structured program over deck semantics, ships element-level ground truths, style targets, and error metrics to ensure correctness, and uses dual instruction-following and visual tools to allow diverse configurations of edits while maintaining task coherence. Complementing this benchmark, we present PPTPilot, a structure-aware pilot agent for robust, fine-grained PPT editing. PPTPilot breaks down each natural-language instruction into a sequence of semantic operations, chooses between high-level APIs such as python-pptx and direct XML patching, and validates the outcomes against task-specific targets. Two crucial design components are structure-aware planning—where the agent parses slide masters, placeholders, shape trees, text, and visual data before editing—and deterministic execution, where XML-level patches with strict schemas provide exact control over fonts, theme color slots, positions, and master-level changes. Programmatic tools efficiently handle repetitive global operations such as translation and bulk normalization. An iterative plan-edit-verify loop, enhanced by XML validation and visual checks, boosts robustness in visually demanding and extensive edits. We evaluated a wide range of baselines on PPTArena, including strong proprietary PPT agents like ChatGPT Agent and MiniMax Agent, extended-thinking VLM configurations, and ablations of PPTPilot. However, despite generous prompting and tool access, existing systems often struggle to balance instruction fidelity with visual and layout quality on compound, multi-step edits. They frequently fail with cross-slide dependencies and master-level style changes highlighted by our benchmark. In contrast, PPTPilot achieves considerably higher scores, surpassing proprietary agents and frontier VLM systems by more than 10 percentage points on compound, layout-sensitive, and cross-slide edits, while maintaining competitive performance on simpler tasks. Nonetheless, PPTPilot and other evaluated agents still face significant challenges with difficult, visually dependent tasks, highlighting both the complexity of PPTArena and the potential for future research improvements.

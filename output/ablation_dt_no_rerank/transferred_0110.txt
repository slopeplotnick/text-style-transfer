In conclusion, while the utilization of contextual memory for detecting LeechHijack holds promise, it lacks the precision necessary for immediate implementation. Our results indicate that this method should be supplemented with more granular verification mechanisms, such as runtime tool isolation or fine-grained semantic analysis. We have established that auditing contextual memory is a viable detection vector. However, realizing a robust and practical defense requires further refinement. This section addresses the limitations and broader implications of the proposed LeechHijack attack. We examine the constraints inherent in the current design, delineate the scope of its applicability, and highlight potential avenues through which the threat may evolve or escalate over time. A significant limitation of our approach concerns the fidelity with which hijacked computations are managed. Nevertheless, this issue can be ameliorated by distributing the additional task among multiple victims and employing a consensus mechanism to aggregate the outputs. Moreover, another limitation pertains to the range of tasks that LeechHijack can execute. Our experiments verified the feasibility of LeechHijack for tasks within the MMLU benchmark. However, we did not assess its effectiveness on more complex tasks, such as those present in GAIA. The current implementation of LeechHijack is mainly configured for conventional chat-based tasks, and it is anticipated that its performance may decline when confronted with more intricate challenges. Exploiting intricate commands in an attack scenario presents significant challenges and necessitates more advanced configurations. Given the high operational costs associated with LLMs in agent systems, an attacker can extract value from LeechHijack without engaging in complex agentic maneuvers. Instead, one might utilize the compromised resources for less demanding tasks such as information aggregation or the generation of templated content, thereby capitalizing on the extensive knowledge base of the model. Consequently, LeechHijack represents a tangible and persistent threat to MCP and agent systems, irrespective of the complexity inherent in the hijacked operation.

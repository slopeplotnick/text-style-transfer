Future Work Due to resource constraints, the systematic study of belief entrenchment in reinforced reasoning has not been conducted [Guo et al., 2025], despite the growing popularity of this approach in LLM reasoning. Moreover, violations of ideal Bayesian rationality can arise from irrational engagement with either internal reasoning processes or external evidence. In this study, our focus was limited to the former. Future research could incorporate an "evidence searching" component to evaluate an LLM's capacity to update beliefs in response to new external evidence, rather than simply fixating on prior beliefs. A key challenge here is ensuring that during evidence searching, LLMs do not access ground truth directly, should such truth exist. In domains with established ground truths, such as forecasting, we demonstrated how belief entrenchment impacts accuracy. Future studies could assess the validity of the Martingale Score in open-ended domains by identifying additional downstream effects of belief entrenchment. To illustrate the Martingale Score's utility as a general-purpose, process-based reasoning evaluation tool, one could extend our methodology to evaluate sycophancy [Sharma et al., 2023], group conformity [Weng et al., 2025], and inverse scaling [Gema et al., 2025]. Furthermore, the Martingale Score can be transformed into a training objective to test its robustness and applicability. If martingale training results in reductions of both the Martingale Score and the Brier Score in any domain, it would provide new evidence that the Martingale Score is effective in evaluating reasoning, extending its applicability beyond forecasting. Exploring the application of the Martingale Score in measuring and mitigating belief entrenchment or polarization [Lefebvre et al., 2024] within human-AI systems, such as recommender systems, represents an exciting direction for future research.

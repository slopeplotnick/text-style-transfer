Despite a substantial requirement for mental health support, significant gaps in diagnosis persist globally. Factors such as insufficient clinic operational hours, geographic barriers, and workforce shortages impede timely evaluations and early self-awareness regarding mental health conditions. Moreover, structural, patient-level, and systemic challenges further delay diagnosis for individuals with severe mental health issues. This mismatch between the high demand for support and the limited availability of resources highlights the constraints of traditional diagnostic models. Traditional methods like face-to-face consultations and self-reported questionnaires struggle to scale, facing systemic bottlenecks, including workforce shortages, high costs, regional disparities, and pervasive stigma. Consequently, wait times for mental health assistance can extend from three to eighteen months, depending on geographic location and healthcare provider availability. Therefore, the imperative for rapid identification of early indicators of mental health issues necessitates the development of enhanced methodologies for timely intervention. Portable, resource-efficient solutions, such as small language models, are crucial in scaling mental health detection and intervention. Emerging on-device technologies, notably those operational on smartphones, offer a promising avenue for early identification through passive, real-time analysis of social media interactions and application usage. Utilizing linguistic data from social media platforms accessed via smartphones provides a scalable and unobtrusive method for passive surveillance of psychological states, facilitating early detection of mood disturbances, stress, or suicide risk. Such systems play a critical role in suicide prevention efforts and in recognizing patterns in social media activity pertinent to mental well-being. When these models operate locally on personal devices, they preserve user privacy while assessing language inputs for indicators of depression or stress. As a result, mental health monitoring can extend beyond clinical settings, enhancing accessibility for a broader population.

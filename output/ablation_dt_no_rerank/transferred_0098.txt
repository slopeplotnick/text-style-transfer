In this context, language models present a significant advancement over traditional methods in mental health monitoring. They surpass supervised learning classifiers by capturing deeper context and acquiring information, which enhances their generalization capabilities and practicality for extensive mental health surveillance. However, deploying large language models (LLMs) entails considerable challenges: they require substantial computational resources and raise privacy concerns due to processing sensitive data on remote servers. Their large size renders them impractical for resource-constrained, on-device environments, highlighting the need for more efficient alternatives. Conversely, Small Language Models (SLMs) show comparable or even superior potential for digital health applications due to their streamlined architecture, reduced inference costs, and capacity for local deployment. Consequently, the deployment of SLMs supports mental health prediction from social media activity through a privacy-preserving, on-device workflow, facilitating real-time, low-resource monitoring across various mental health dimensions without storing personal data, thereby aligning with ethical standards. Nonetheless, the application of SLMs for mental health outcome prediction remains largely underexplored. This study investigates the efficacy of SLMs in predicting digital mental health outcomes and demonstrates that, with careful design and optimization, SLMs can serve as viable alternatives for comprehensive assessment across multiple mental health conditions on personal devices. To explore this, we evaluate the performance of these models across tasks related to stress, depression, and suicidality. We introduce Menta, a compact model optimized through a LoRA-based multi-task framework with cross-dataset training, prioritizing balanced accuracy. Menta demonstrates robust performance across a spectrum of mental health tasks, particularly in stress and depression detection. Furthermore, its practical applicability is evidenced through deployment on an iPhone 15 Pro Max, where it executes real-time inference with minimal memory consumption, enabling private and scalable on-device mental health support.

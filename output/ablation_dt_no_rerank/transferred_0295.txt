Synchronization is essential in measuring the frame-level temporal correspondence between source and edited videos. We utilize four correlation-based metrics—speech, gaze, blink, and pose—to capture distinct aspects of motion dynamics: mouth aspect ratio for speech, iris trajectory for gaze, eye aspect ratio for blinking, and upper-body joint angles for pose. While these metrics are identical to those employed in our training data filtering, they are solely used for curating the dataset and performing final evaluations. Crucially, they are never incorporated as a loss function or integrated into the model's training objective. Moreover, edit fidelity is evaluated to ensure the intended change is consistently applied throughout the video using two complementary CLIP-based metrics. First, the Directional CLIP Score, originally designed for image editing, is adapted for the video domain to measure how consistently per-frame visual transformations align with the overall edit direction within CLIP's embedding space. We report two versions: an image-based variant that quantifies alignment with the visual edit implied by the first frame, and a text-dual version that assesses alignment with the semantic edit described by the text prompt. The text-dual variant is included for fairness as it acknowledges baselines like LucyEdit, which operate solely from textual guidance without the edited first frame. Intuitively, the image-based variant questions whether transitions from source frames to edited frames resemble changes between the unedited and edited first frames, while the text-dual variant evaluates whether the visual changes align with those suggested by the edit text. Additionally, the CLIP-Text Alignment Score assesses semantic accuracy by measuring the degree to which each generated frame matches the textual description within CLIP space. This score helps ensure the video remains faithful to the prompt semantics over time, thereby complementing the directional measure.

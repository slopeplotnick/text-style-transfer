The authors of an extensive work on Empirical Software Engineering discuss the influential contributions of Victor R. Basili, a trailblazer in the field. Their work underscores the critical importance of utilizing empirical data and evidence to improve software engineering practices. As noted, numerous studies have explored the adoption of Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE) in industrial contexts and its effect on agile development methodologies. These studies have proposed valuable procedures and strategies to facilitate this adoption, providing essential insights on how to proceed with such transitions. However, none have conducted an empirical assessment study similar to ours, which is positioned between adoption and prior to implementation. In this paper, we outline the initial phase of a comprehensive process aimed at assisting a company in transitioning its codebase to a Software Product Line (SPL). Interviews were carefully designed to address the unique aspects of the company, particularly considering the diverse roles of project participants. Our objective was to pinpoint optimal strategies to facilitate the impending transition by focusing on research questions that shed light on current best practices suitable for adoption and the benefits and risks seen by stakeholders. Addressing these risks and concerns, while leveraging recognized benefits and promoting established good practices, will be vital for ensuring a seamless transition. Our analysis revealed that the organization has beneficial routines that will aid the transition process and is characterized by a culture of innovation, which will support the project's success. Most stakeholders perceive the transition as beneficial. Nevertheless, main concerns include the perceived complexity of Software Product Lines (SPL) and the sustainability of their implementation.

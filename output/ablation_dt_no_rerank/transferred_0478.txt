After fine-tuning the LLM using the KTO method, we employ it as a tool for annotating domain-specific online items. Hard samples that are inaccurately predicted by the online BERT model are incorporated into the training set to improve the model's performance further. By using the domain rule-aware LLM developed within the RD module, we can easily identify challenging instances where the online model incorrectly predicts. However, this targeted approach merely addresses isolated errors and does not systematically resolve an entire category of issues. When subtle modifications occur in search terms or product descriptions, the online model may still yield incorrect predictions. Therefore, we have developed an error-type-aware LLM generative model that synthesizes data prone to misprediction by the online model, thereby enhancing its generalization capacity and accuracy. For any search-product pair, our proposed model can modify the attributes of the search query or product based on specific instructional prompts, resulting in novel examples classified as either Relevant or Irrelevant. Training examples at this stage are procured using the GPT-4o framework through few-shot prompting. To ensure comprehensive understanding of error patterns inherent in the online model, we iteratively select the generated outputs where the online model exhibited erroneous predictions, which are subsequently used as fine-tuning training data for the large language model (LLM). After developing the error-type-aware LLM-based generative model, we extract data from online retrievals and leverage the model to generate challenging samples that could potentially confound the online model. Moreover, to mitigate the detrimental effects of noisy data during training, we employ the relevance discrimination model from the RD module to meticulously filter the generated outputs.

The purpose of this paper is to present a forward-looking discourse on the application of artificial intelligence in the exploration of critical minerals, offering a personal perspective grounded in three decades of experience in data science, decision science, and AI within Earth resources exploration and development. Despite containing personal opinions and observations, the discourse is informed by extensive expertise in the field. In 1998, I authored a paper on utilizing neural networks trained on outcrop data to enhance geological realism in reservoir models, ambitiously presenting it at the Society of Petroleum Engineers Annual Technical Conference and Exhibition in New Orleans amid the threat posed by Hurricane George. Integrating geological realism is crucial for accurately predicting fluid flow in porous media, ultimately leading to better decision-making in oil and gas exploration and development. Furthermore, a subsequent paper in 2002 demonstrated that neural networks effectively utilize borehole and geophysical data to predict critical reservoir characteristics like porosity, even in areas lacking boreholes. This research addressed the challenge of reconciling disparate data scales, such as those between borehole cores and seismic signals detected deep in the subsurface—a topic extensively explored in this paper. While oil and gas companies wouldn't dream of constructing large offshore platforms without quantifying geological uncertainty, the mining industry often relies on deterministic models. In mineral exploration, deterministic modeling and interpretation of multi-physics data remain prevalent. Moreover, the advances in quantitative risk assessment achieved by the oil and gas industry appear largely absent in the minerals sector.

Our proposed test-time scaling framework, which endeavors to find the highest-density (i.e., in-support) action, is not merely a heuristic but represents a principled and theoretically equivalent application of the anti-exploration objective standard in Offline RL. The empirical successes demonstrated by Lobel et al. (2023) and Bai et al. (2025) in using pseudo-counts for LLM preference optimization affirm this approach. Therefore, we propose implementing this concept through a Coin Flipping Network (CFN). As evidenced in Figure 3, the pseudo-counts generated by our estimator show significant congruity with the L2 distance to the actual action, as well as the pseudo-counts produced by our CFN, ensuring alignment with theoretical expectations. This corroboration indicates our CFN's capability to verify and penalize unsupported actions effectively. Our investigative efforts aim to ascertain whether implementing Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-COunits enhances action prediction accuracy and task success rates. Moreover, we seek to elucidate the design factors contributing to these advancements and consider the acceptability of the additional computational demands imposed. Specifically, our experiments are designed to address the following research inquiries: Q1: Can our framework enhance performance across a spectrum of environments, diverse VLC policies, and a wide array of tasks? Q2: What is the additional computational burden imposed by our test-time scaling method, and can it be efficiently implemented on real robotic platforms while maintaining performance enhancements? Q3: How do design decisions, such as the incorporation of internal features and the CFN pseudo-count network, influence overall performance?

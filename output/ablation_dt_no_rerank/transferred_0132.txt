This research examines the application of robotic systems in construction, aiming to demonstrate their effectiveness through systematic evaluation against established algorithmic and learning-based methods. Building upon probabilistic robot modeling principles and specific construction constraints, the authors provide a comparative analysis between LLM-based reasoning and traditional approaches for task allocation in construction robotics. The LTAA framework is developed to include capability-aware modeling, phase-adaptive allocation strategies, and validated LLM decision-making. This framework addresses construction-specific coordination challenges previously identified in the literature. By systematically comparing it with traditional algorithmic methods such as Brute Force and Greedy Method, as well as learning approaches like Q-learning and Deep Q-Network, this study offers both a rigorous LLM coordination system and valuable insights into the effectiveness of reasoning-based over traditional methods for construction automation. The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the literature on multi-robot task allocation, including optimization, learning-based strategies, and large language model-driven methodologies. Section 3 details the LTAA framework's methodology. Section 4 describes the experimental setup, while Section 5 presents a comparative analysis of results against established methodologies. Finally, Section 6 synthesizes findings, offering conclusions and potential avenues for future investigation. At the core of this study, MRTA serves as the theoretical underpinning for task distribution among robotic teams, accounting for constraints like resources, time, and robot capability heterogeneity. Gerkey and Matarić (2004) created a taxonomy for analyzing task allocation issues along various dimensions, focusing on the capacity of robots to handle different task types and the temporal complexity involved. However, this framework was insufficient for real-world scenarios involving diverse robotic capabilities and temporal constraints. Consequently, Korsah et al. (2013) expanded this framework to incorporate robotic competencies, task interdependencies, and dynamic constraints, marking the advancement towards using heterogeneous teams for complex tasks.

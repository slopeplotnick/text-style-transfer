System prompts provide task-agnostic control over LLM behavior by defining roles, styles, or policies applicable across various tasks. Prior research has demonstrated that elements such as personas, emotions, and jailbreak triggers can influence response style and reasoning, although they do not always enhance task accuracy. Despite their growing use, system prompts remain underexplored in terms of structured optimization. For example, recent leaks of system prompts from Grok and Claude reveal highly verbose and complex manually crafted rules. While recent methodologies suggest that system prompts can be automatically optimized through genetic algorithms, such optimization has so far been limited to monolingual and single-metric environments. However, deploying multilingual systems presents additional challenges, such as language-specific degradation and cross-lingual inconsistency. Multilingual Large Language Models (LLMs) often produce inconsistent outputs when the same question is asked in different languages due to their unequal distribution of knowledge across languages. Techniques like Cross-Lingual Thought Prompting and multilingual self-consistency offer partial solutions, though they typically rely on multi-turn prompting strategies. Despite these advancements, there remains no comprehensive framework for optimizing system prompts that ensures consistent and accurate performance across all languages. Moreover, beyond traditional metrics, recent studies have increasingly focused on the cognitive processes of LLMs by breaking down responses into understandable reasoning units. Nonetheless, these efforts have primarily concentrated on surface-level analyses, such as using text prefill to control output language, without delving deeply into the model's intrinsic reasoning patterns. Our work aims to address this gap by examining the connection between system prompt design and multilingual reasoning behaviors, thereby clarifying how these cognitive patterns correlate with cross-lingual generalization capabilities.

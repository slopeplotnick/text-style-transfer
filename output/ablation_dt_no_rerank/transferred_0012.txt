Prompt design plays a critical role in the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs); however, the impact of prompt specificity—how detailed or vague a prompt is—remains understudied. This paper introduces DETAIL, a framework for evaluating LLM performance across varying levels of prompt specificity. We generate multi-level prompts using GPT-4, quantify specificity via perplexity, and assess correctness using GPT-based semantic equivalence. Experiments on 30 novel reasoning tasks using both GPT-4 and O3-mini reveal that increased specificity generally improves accuracy, particularly for smaller models and procedural tasks. Therefore, our results underscore the importance of adaptive prompting strategies, offering tools and data to support further research. LLMs have drastically transformed numerous domains due to their excellent generative capabilities, with developments emerging at an unprecedented rate. This NLP revolution is not limited to simple text tasks but also surpasses many state-of-the-art methods across a variety of challenges. Recent evaluations predominantly focus on the proficiency of these models in tackling mathematical and complex multi-step problem-solving tasks. Improving the reasoning capabilities of LLMs is thus essential for their continued advancement. To this end, various approaches to enhance LLM reasoning via prompting have been introduced, including sophisticated strategies like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree of Thoughts (ToT), and Graph of Thoughts (GoT), which decompose complex problems into manageable steps. Nonetheless, a significant challenge remains in determining the optimal level of prompt specificity to generate accurate and coherent responses. While detailed prompts offer clear guidance, they risk constraining the model's reasoning pathways. Conversely, vague prompts provide flexibility but may lead to ambiguity and misinterpretation.

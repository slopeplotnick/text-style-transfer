Modern large language models (LLMs) showcase exceptional versatility in performing a wide range of tasks. They can be adapted to specific tasks through in-context learning, which involves providing a textual instruction and possibly a few examples. Notably, the conveyed instructions can significantly affect the model's performance. For instance, Table 2 illustrates how two semantically similar instructions for a mathematical task yield different performance outcomes, emphasizing the importance of crafting precise and well-structured instructions—akin to hyperparameter tuning in machine learning that enhances performance (Kohavi and John, 1995). However, manual creation of effective instructions is both challenging and unpredictable. Therefore, automatic prompt optimization, much like AutoML's ability to automate optimal hyperparameter selection (Feurer and Hutter, 2019), has emerged to systematically find optimal instructions within the intricate space of prompt configurations. This advancement holds particular significance in today's era, where multi-agent systems are pivotal in both industrial applications and academic research (He et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024). When modifying entire models is prohibitive due to high computational costs and data requirements, automatic prompt optimization offers a feasible, streamlined alternative, especially beneficial for models with opaque internal mechanisms. Although numerous prompt optimization tools exist, their practical application often presents challenges. Each optimizer is housed in its own research repository, necessitating navigation of diverse code bases and conflicting requirements. Moreover, these repositories are frequently not actively maintained, which potentially reduces reliability due to inadequate software testing and documentation, thereby complicating both scientific benchmarking experiments with other optimizers and practical applications with specific needs. Furthermore, these systems often lack flexibility, making adaptation for complex tasks difficult. While certain tools have been developed to address these challenges, they may be proprietary and closed-source, limited to a single optimizer, or overly complicated for typical use.

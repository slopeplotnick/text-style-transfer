Auditors rely on Journal Entry Tests (JETs) to detect anomalies in tax-related ledger records; however, these rule-based methods often generate overwhelming false positives and struggle with subtle irregularities. In this context, we investigate whether large language models (LLMs) can serve as effective anomaly detectors in double-entry bookkeeping. By benchmarking state-of-the-art LLMs, such as LLaMA and Gemma, on both synthetic and real-world anonymized ledgers, we compare their performance against JETs and machine learning baselines. Our results demonstrate that LLMs consistently outperform traditional rule-based JETs and classical ML baselines while providing natural-language explanations to enhance interpretability. Therefore, these findings highlight the potential of AI-augmented auditing, where collaboration between human auditors and foundation models strengthens financial integrity. Financial auditors play a critical role in detecting unusual transactions and anomalies that could indicate errors, fraud, or tax evasion in a company's books. Identifying such irregularities is essential for maintaining trust in financial statements. However, auditing large volumes of accounting data is extremely challenging; current manual and rule-based processes are often inefficient and error-prone, meaning auditors can miss important red flags. Standard Journal Entry Tests (JETs), which rely on predefined rules and known patterns, tend to flag a huge number of transactions. These tests predominantly identify anticipated issues and inundate auditors with alerts, a significant proportion of which are false positives requiring time-consuming review. In practice, auditors continue to depend primarily on sampling and manual procedures, which may overlook nuanced discrepancies within today's vast, intricate datasets. Consequently, this gap between growing data complexity and the limits of traditional auditing techniques underscores the need for more intelligent, automated anomaly detection tools in auditing.

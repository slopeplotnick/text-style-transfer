Building on the findings in RQ2, which demonstrated that Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV) exhibit statistical complementarity and mechanistically distinct failure modes, we now address RQ3: Can we operationalize this synergy into high-performance hybrid systems? To achieve this, we propose and evaluate two integration paradigms: a Score-Level Fusion approach and an Evidence-Aware Pipeline specifically designed to address the structural limitations of FV identified in our study. While Strategy I enhances robustness through score-level fusion, it does not explicitly address the failure modes highlighted in Section 4.5. Moreover, FV systems prove reliable only when evidence is explicitly retrieved, yielding "Supported" or "Contradicted" verdicts, yet they falter when relevant evidence is absent, indicated by "Not Enough Information" (NEI). Therefore, to address this limitation, we design an Evidence-Aware Pipeline that prioritizes external grounding and resorts to internal signals only when retrieval fails: (1) Step 1: Retrieval & Verification. An FV module, specifically LLM-QA, retrieves evidence and classifies the claim-evidence relationship as Supported, Contradicted, or NEI. (2) Step 2: Conditional Branching. Here, a cascading decision mechanism is employed. If the FV yields a definitive prediction (Supported or Contradicted), the system directly accepts this result. Conversely, if the output is NEI, the system recognizes the absence of external support and, instead of forcing a potentially erroneous verification, bypasses the FV process and employs an HD method, such as LNPE. Thus, the final score derives solely from the model's internal uncertainty signals. This conditional design effectively mitigates the limitations of FV when retrieval results are not relevant; crucially, decoupling retrieval dependence from internal signals prevents the propagation of errors caused by low-quality evidence retrieval, ensuring robust detection even when external knowledge is unavailable.

Building larger and more diverse text corpora by integrating multimodal sources like social media, news articles, and transcribed speech is essential to mitigating data scarcity. Moreover, applying explainable AI techniques is crucial for developing transparent, domain-specific sentiment analysis methodologies that enhance the reliability of sentiment assignments. Furthermore, evaluating scalable lexicon expansions through a unified framework—incorporating corpus-based extraction, cross-lingual mapping, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)—while benchmarking AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa on newly incorporated African languages, particularly in low-resource scenarios, aims to enhance coverage, accuracy, and interpretability in multilingual sentiment lexicons. This initiative ultimately enhances sentiment analysis for underrepresented African languages. By employing these varied approaches, we have presented a comprehensive methodology for lexicon expansion and sentiment analysis in languages with limited resources. Our enriched lexicon, developed for languages such as Ciluba, French, English, and several South African languages, ensures precise sentiment annotations through automated translation, manual verification, and normalization processes. Empirical evaluations demonstrated that AfroXLMR generally surpasses AfriBERTa in terms of accuracy and consistency. However, AfriBERTa remains a viable solution for environments constrained by computational resources. Token-level explainability analyses, employing LIME and attention visualizations, confirmed both models' proficiency in detecting sentiment-bearing morphemes within morphologically complex languages such as Zulu and Xhosa. Despite challenges related to translation inaccuracies and limited data availability, this study substantiates the feasibility and utility of conducting sentiment analysis in low-resource languages. Future research will focus on enriching data acquisition strategies, employing explainable AI techniques, and expanding lexicon databases to enhance performance further. This study provides a robust foundation for continued exploration and development in low-resource multilingual NLP.

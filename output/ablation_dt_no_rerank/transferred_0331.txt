To address these limitations, we propose HEALTHCONTRADICT, a meticulously compiled dataset encompassing 920 unique instances. Each instance includes a health-related question and two documents that present contradictory perspectives, along with a factual answer substantiated by scientific evidence. Using HEALTHCONTRADICT, we evaluated several language models, ranging in size from 1 billion to 8 billion parameters, to determine their proficiency in answering health-related questions amidst conflicting information. Specifically, we focused on two key aspects: (i) How do language models respond accurately to health-related questions when faced with knowledge conflicts? (ii) How does the biomedical context provided to language models influence the formulation of their responses? To assess these questions, we provided contexts that were accurate, erroneous, or contradictory in different prompt scenarios and subsequently evaluated models' accuracy and probability distribution. Our contributions are summarized as follows: (i) We developed a novel dataset, designated HEALTHCONTRADICT, aimed at evaluating language models in the presence of conflicting information in the biomedical domain; (ii) We conducted an extensive evaluation of these models against HEALTHCONTRADICT, to assess their capability to process lengthy, conflicting biomedical texts using interpretable quantitative metrics; and (iii) Moreover, we performed a comparative analysis between general language models and their fine-tuned biomedical counterparts, demonstrating that the latter more effectively leverage correct information while discounting incorrect information. To ensure the integrity of this evaluation, each document within the dataset is unique. For example, in response to the inquiry "Can coffee help you lose weight?" two documents are presented with divergent perspectives—one affirming and the other negating the proposition—while the factual answer, corroborated by scientific evidence, suggests that caffeine intake may facilitate reductions in weight, BMI, and body fat.

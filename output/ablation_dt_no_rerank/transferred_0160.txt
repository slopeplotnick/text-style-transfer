Evaluation Metrics. Evaluating the quality of generated summaries necessitates a multi-dimensional approach that considers lexical overlap, semantic similarity, and factual consistency. Traditional metrics such as BLEU [Papineni et al., 2002] and ROUGE-L [Lin, 2004] rely on n-gram overlap between system outputs and reference summaries. However, these metrics often fail to handle paraphrasing, where the wording differs but the meaning remains consistent. To overcome this limitation, BERTScore was introduced, embedding tokens using pre-trained contextual encoders and computing similarity via cosine distance. By capturing semantic relationships and paraphrastic variation, BERTScore offers a more robust measure of summary quality, particularly in settings requiring concise abstraction. Beyond semantic similarity, it is crucial to assess whether a summary is factually consistent with its source documents. Therefore, several metrics have been developed for this purpose. FactCC employs a trained classifier to evaluate whether each summary sentence is supported by the source text. Nevertheless, analyses indicate that FactCC can be unstable, occasionally misclassifying sentences that are truly supported by the original text. Alternatively, BLANC, a reference-free metric, assesses the efficacy of a summary in aiding a language model to reconstruct masked portions of the source document. However, BLANC scores are sensitive to the choice of underlying language model and sometimes correlate more with lexical overlap than with true semantic entailment. To address these limitations, SummaC employs a Natural Language Inference (NLI) framework, typically using models such as RoBERTa-MNLI, to evaluate factual consistency. SummaC operates at the sentence level, assessing whether the source text supports each statement in the summary. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that SummaC generally outperforms earlier metrics such as FactCC. In this study, therefore, we adopt a multiple evaluation strategy: ROUGE-L to capture lexical overlap, BERTScore to measure semantic similarity, and SummaC to evaluate factual consistency between the generated summaries and the original articles.

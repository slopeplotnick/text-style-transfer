Understanding real-world scientific laws is crucial for accurate physical outcome modeling under diverse conditions. However, existing video benchmarks are predominantly based on physical commonsense, offering limited insight into the scientific reasoning capabilities of video models. To address this gap, we introduce VideoScience-Bench, a benchmark designed to evaluate the scientific understanding of video models at an undergraduate level. Each prompt encodes a composite scientific scenario that requires comprehension and analytical reasoning across multiple scientific concepts to generate the correct phenomenon. The benchmark comprises 200 carefully curated questions spanning 14 topics and 103 concepts in physics and chemistry. Expert-annotated evaluations were conducted on seven state-of-the-art video models in T2V and I2V settings across four dimensions: Immutability, Correct Dynamism, Spatio-Temporal Continuity, and Phenomenon Congruency. Notably, using a VLM-as-a-Judge to assess video outputs, we observe a strong correlation with human evaluations. To the best of our knowledge, VideoScience-Bench represents the first benchmark to evaluate video models not only as generators but also as reasoners, requiring their outputs to demonstrate scientific understanding consistent with expected physical and chemical phenomena. The accompanying data and evaluation code are available at github.com/hao-ai-lab/VideoScience. Moreover, the releases of Sora-2 and Veo-3.1 mark a significant transformation in the video-generation landscape, as these models now deliver cinematic-level visual fidelity and exhibit enhanced world-modeling capabilities. As video models evolve, the criteria defining "good" video generation are shifting beyond traditional quality metrics such as Fréchet Video Distance and Fréchet Video Motion Distance, toward evaluations that probe a model's world-modeling ability and assess whether the generated videos adhere to commonsense physical regularities. Additionally, emerging models are beginning to exhibit zero-shot reasoning abilities, tackling tasks involving scientific, mathematical, and spatial reasoning without prior specific training. Human reasoners trained in science model the world using a rich set of scientific principles, anticipating phenomena like whether citric acid removes iron stains or the outcome of powering an LED with or without a resistor. This motivates our central question: beyond visual fidelity and physical commonsense, can video models correctly portray scientific phenomena and demonstrate scientific reasoning about the real world? World modeling grounded on scientific principles plays a key role in advancing discovery by allowing models to predict the outcomes of real-world processes. Unlike commonsense-based modeling, true scientific understanding requires reasoning across multiple interacting concepts, such as the integration of specific-heat and heat-transfer principles, which predicts that water will absorb heat.

Challenges like climate change and transitioning to alternative energy sources far surpass the knowledge contained within a single scientific discipline; yet, a degree in Mineral Exploration remains absent from academic offerings. Information is now produced almost instantaneously, and its sophistication continues to evolve. Instead of manually copying journal articles from libraries, algorithms can now search and summarize thousands of documents in near real-time. However, there is a noticeable decline in interest in academic pursuits such as economic geology and mining engineering within the Western world. Now more than ever, it is crucial to preserve the longstanding knowledge about the genesis of ore deposits, much of which lies within outdated geological reports scattered globally. High-quality geological databases, such as those in Canada and Australia, are critically needed worldwide. In Africa, only one country has established such a database (gsd.gov.zm). Making this information accessible through online platforms is essential to attract greater investment. This personal essay advocates for the scientific and industrial communities to collaborate on developing a new scientific methodology for mineral exploration. As exploration moves to "under cover" methodologies, the traditional approach of starting with surface outcrops and using deterministic geophysical inversion is becoming increasingly ineffective, as evidenced by decreasing discovery rates. Therefore, adopting a decision-oriented approach that explicitly incorporates risk assessment through uncertainty quantification is necessary to minimize false positive drilling outcomes. This requires acknowledging the substantial gaps in our understanding of orebody structures, which can be mitigated by measuring epistemic uncertainty. Moreover, it is imperative to develop and implement refined geostatistical techniques that adequately process data at the appropriate scales, as current methods are inadequate and can lead to inaccuracies.

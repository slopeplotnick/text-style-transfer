In this study, we present a method to create knowledge graphs (KGs) from images by utilizing multimodal AI models, which blend textual and visual information, under the guidance of domain-specific ontological frameworks. These ontologies define the key objects and relationships of interest, and are embedded in the prompts to ensure the AI system focuses on the relevant aspects of the image. When an image is provided, the model generates a list of critical elements, their attributes, and their interconnections as determined by the ontological guidelines. These element lists are then transformed into structured graph representations that clarify the image's contents. Importantly, the configuration of each graph is dictated by predefined ontological specifications, ensuring domain-specific accuracy and relevance. Each image yields its own unique graph, which is systematically stored within a comprehensive database for future analysis. Despite variations among the graphs of individual images, the use of consistent terminologies governed by fixed ontological rules results in shared components across these graphs. Consequently, this leads to an integrated, comprehensive graph, which facilitates the identification of similarities by enabling comparative analyses across the different image graphs. To capture the essence of an image, artificial intelligence is employed to extract key features, converting them into a structured list. These features relate to significant concepts within the image, and consistent descriptions for all images are ensured through domain-specific ontological guidelines. To operate the AI system under these constraints, the rules are integrated into the guidelines during the AI's decision-making process. This method facilitates the development of detailed graphs for individual images, which can be synthesized into a comprehensive graph. These smaller graphs, corresponding to each image, are interconnected through shared attributes, thereby enhancing the ability to conduct comparative analyses across the entire dataset.

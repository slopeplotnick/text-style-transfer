The primary competencies VIDEO4SPATIAL aims to cultivate include: (i) implicitly deducing 3D structure without explicit guidance, (ii) regulating the camera viewpoint to maintain geometric consistency, addressing issues such as scale, occlusion, and layout, (iii) comprehending the semantic arrangement of scene elements, (iv) strategizing to achieve specific objectives, and (v) sustaining temporal coherence across longer durations. To evaluate these capabilities, we introduce two principal tasks: video-based scene navigation and object grounding via textual instructions. In scene navigation, the model adheres to camera-pose directives to generate a video trajectory that aligns consistently with both the provided instructions and the inherent geometry of the scene. Conversely, in object grounding, the model follows textual instructions to locate and display specified objects, thereby testing its semantic layout comprehension and effective planning skills. These tasks are crucial in assessing whether VIDEO4SPATIAL can discern and manipulate scene particulars solely through video input. However, due to the lack of extensive public datasets specifically designed for such spatial tasks, we repurpose indoor scanning datasets, such as ScanNet++ and ARKitScenes. These datasets offer long trajectories through diverse rooms and enable creating context-target video clip pairs. By utilizing a vision-language model, we meticulously select video clips ending with a centered object and produce natural-language grounding instructions for object localization. For navigation tasks, we transform the annotated camera trajectories into pose-following directives. Experimental results indicate that the proposed framework adeptly addresses complex visuospatial challenges. From video input alone, the model discerns scene-level geometry and semantics, exhibiting strong performance in object grounding and scene navigation. Trained on short video contexts, the model successfully extrapolates during inference to considerably longer temporal windows, thereby enhancing its overall effectiveness. Furthermore, despite its initial training exposure being limited to indoor scenes, it generalizes to outdoor environments and recognizes object categories not encountered during training. In conclusion, we introduce VIDEO4SPATIAL, a streamlined yet powerful framework for conducting video-based visuospatial tasks. Moreover, we emphasize pivotal design decisions, such as employing joint classifier-free guidance over context and instruction, incorporating auxiliary bounding boxes to enhance grounding accuracy, and implementing non-continuous context sampling to facilitate efficient understanding. Finally, we have instantiated and assessed two spatial tasks to validate the framework's efficacy.

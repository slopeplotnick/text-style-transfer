Sensory language processing, or language comprehension, follows a structured progression: speech is perceived, phonemes are recognized, words are formed from these phonemes, and meanings are integrated according to grammatical rules to derive sentence meaning. Here, we particularly focus on language comprehension after word recognition—a sophisticated cognitive process unique to humans—as a point of comparison with advanced language AI systems. Research consistently reveals that neocortical language processing functions through hierarchical predictive coding, wherein simpler tasks like word prediction occur in lower layers, while higher layers handle more abstract content. Not unlike the processing of sensory information, the neocortex builds a linguistic model through prediction-error learning, utilizing this model for prediction and comprehension by extracting abstract information. This shared computational principle of predictive modeling underpins both neocortical language processing and AI language models, offering intriguing parallels. For instance, consider the architecture of Google's Neural Machine Translation (GNMT) system. This translation circuit comprises encoder, attention, and decoder units, with the encoder-decoder pair used comprehensively throughout. Specifically, the encoder is a stacked-LSTM circuit processing each word of a source sentence sequentially to create an integrated signal representing the sentence's meaning. This eight-layered encoder generates a vector that captures the essence of the entire sentence, including contextual information via a bidirectional layer. Meanwhile, the attention unit—a two-layer neural network—receives input from both the decoder and the encoder, crafting a weight-coefficient signal to focus on crucial words in the source sentence. This signal is subsequently delivered to each decoder layer, which then sequentially predicts the probability of the next translated word. The circuit undergoes training through supervised learning using human-generated translations, refining its parameters via backpropagation in response to errors. In contrast, the GPT-2 model employs a distinct transformer circuit, foregoing the encoder-decoder setup in favor of an integrated structure. During its training, GPT-2 processes sentences with masked words, predicting next words like 'book.' Post-training, it generates new sentences by repurposing this output system. The architecture involves repeating blocks—chiefly consisting of attention units and two-layer feedforward neural networks—stacked multiple times. Input is provided as a sparse word sequence, producing a probabilistic representation for potential next-word candidates. GPT-2 employs unsupervised learning through next-word prediction, illustrating a different yet parallel approach to language processing.

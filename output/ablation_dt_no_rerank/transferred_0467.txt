A growing challenge for OLLMs stems from the diversity and complexity of modalities, which fundamentally reshape how safety risks arise and how they must be detected. This variety of inputs across all modalities introduces heterogeneous risk patterns that manifest differently, such as implicit bias in text, visual misinformation, privacy leakage, or harmful noise in audio. Moreover, the complexity of cross-modal interactions further exacerbates the challenge of identifying hidden risks. For instance, the text "I want to fly" and an image of a person standing on a rooftop are individually innocuous yet, when combined, convey a potentially unsafe, suicidal intent. Similarly, when a video of police patrolling is paired with a textual query like "How to overcome obstacles," it reveals an implicitly criminal motive that is not evident from either modality alone. Consequently, safety risks for OLLMs become substantially more challenging, requiring effective detection through modality-specific understanding and stronger cross-modal comprehension. However, directly applying safety alignment to base OLLMs may not suffice, as it often demands substantially more development time, additional computational resources, and may lead to degradation in core reasoning capabilities. Furthermore, even after significant investment, these aligned models require prohibitively expensive retraining to address emerging issues and remain vulnerable to low-cost jailbreak attacks. Guardrail models offer a decentralized and more flexible alternative, yet current research predominantly focuses on unimodal settings or simple modality combinations like image-text pairs, leaving the omni-modal scenario largely unexplored. Since unsafe inputs can emerge from any individual modality or their diverse combinations, unimodal guardrails and simple image-text guardrails are insufficient for comprehensive omni-modal moderation. Additionally, existing guardrails often approach safety as a binary classification task. This simplification limits their effectiveness.

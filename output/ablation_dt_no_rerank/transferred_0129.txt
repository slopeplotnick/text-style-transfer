Individuals with a focus on business rather than technical expertise may be the most hesitant to adopt changes, as they may have concerns about the rapid pace of delivery, even when acknowledging its utility. Since our team is relatively small and accustomed to innovative technologies, and the use of an SPL was considered from the project's inception, we anticipated minimal resistance. In this context, using data from interviews, we identified strategies to consolidate existing effective practices, enhance benefits, and minimize risks and fears. Maintaining and reinforcing effective practices during SPL implementation and its maintenance serves as a reassuring factor. As work habits evolve, a team that continues its development of good practices is likely to remain motivated and open to change. With the nature of work set for significant transformation, keeping established good practices helps maintain a familiar environment for the team. SPL's core principles include factorization, reuse, and rapid processes, which are already part of our good practices. Thus, these can be leveraged to highlight future benefits. The transition to an SPL framework supports sustainable code reuse and factorization. Moreover, SPL offers other benefits such as enhanced customization, automation, and a factory-like production process. Better code quality emerges as a benefit, helping mitigate technical debt, resulting in a more homogeneous, consistent codebase with fewer errors, and avoids clone-and-own situations. Correcting common defects further boosts code quality. Interestingly, the advantages related to marketability are not just recognized by product owners; IT professionals cite them even more frequently. As an example, employing this engineering approach can enhance and solidify the company's R&D reputation. Furthermore, SPL adoption accelerates the development of new software products.

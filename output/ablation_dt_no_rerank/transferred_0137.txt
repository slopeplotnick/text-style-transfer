Machine learning methodologies, such as neural networks and generative AI algorithms, facilitate the modeling of predictive tasks. In contexts characterized by uncertainty, Monte Carlo Tree search algorithms are effective tools for both offline and online sequential planning. With these two major components of AI—prediction and decision-making—identified, it is crucial to understand their application in exploration. Predicating effective mineral exploration requires comprehensive datasets. However, the most significant challenge in mineral exploration lies in creating and curating these datasets, rather than selecting a specific machine learning technique. These datasets often present complex issues, being multi-variate, three-dimensional, and containing extreme values, challenges not commonly found in other domains. An often overlooked aspect of data curation involves two critical components: not all data is on the same scale, and not all data should be pooled into a single dataset. Furthermore, as methodologies effective in one domain may not apply in another, there are issues like indiscriminate upscaling of ground truth data to match geophysical dimensions, and deterministic interpolation of geophysical and geochemical datasets without geological justification. Consequently, applying machine learning models across different domains without validation leads to erroneous implementations, increasing false positives and diminishing the reliability of AI systems. Therefore, the core issue is not AI itself but how data is manipulated and understood. A significant challenge in the mining industry, rooted in geostatistics, is the variability of data variance across scales. For instance, interpolated geophysical data shows reduced variance compared to direct measurements. Besides, data from fine-resolution drilling is often upscaled or repositioned to facilitate comparisons with geophysical data, inducing artificial correlations between labels and predictors. Consequently, existing mineral potential and prospecting maps frequently suffer from a high prevalence of false positive indications.

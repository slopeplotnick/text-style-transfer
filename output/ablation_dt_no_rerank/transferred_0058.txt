The PF model is a continuum mechanics approach that simulates the kinetic evolution of a possibly multiphase and multimaterial system as it progresses toward thermodynamic equilibrium. PF simulations effectively handle the relevant length scales, ranging from nanometers to microns, and time scales, extending up to seconds or even minutes, which are crucial for the formation of bulk heterojunction (BHJ) solar cells via solution processing. The thermodynamic properties of the studied system are encapsulated within a Gibbs free energy functional. Our method specifically examines the BHJ formation processes, including solvent evaporation, liquid demixing, and crystallization. The forces driving the system toward equilibrium are derived directly from this Gibbs free energy functional. Furthermore, the kinetics of phase changes, such as evaporation and crystallization, are described by a set of equations encompassing both the Allen-Cahn and Cahn-Hilliard formulations, which also account for diffusive mass transport. Throughout the simulation process, the PF model remains integrated with a fluid dynamics solver that incorporates advective mass transport. Detailed simulation methodologies are provided in Supporting Information Section S2. Using the calculated material properties, optical simulations are conducted to analyze light propagation within solar cells. Depending on the complexity of the solar cells in question, either numerical optical simulations, for instance, using COMSOL Multiphysics and JCMsuite, or simpler analytical methods like the transfer matrix method, can be applied. By modeling the solar cell as stacks of stratified isotropic media, we facilitate an understanding of the optical effects at play. Moreover, a neural network surrogate model is employed to predict the charge generation rate as a function of the incident angle, photoactive layer thickness, and light wavelength. Additional details on this neural network model can be found in Section S3.

Checkers and similar methods are effective in domains like mathematics and coding but do not generalize well to open-ended tasks. To address this, some models adopt self-evaluation approaches, treating the model itself or another large language model (LLM) as a judge. For example, Self-Rewarding Language Models (Yuan et al., 2024) and RLAIF (Lee et al., 2024) use AI feedback rather than human labels, while IPO (Garg et al., 2025) and Self-Rewarding PPO (Zhang et al., 2025a) derive preference signals from token-level probabilities. Moving closer to our setting, research on internal activations, such as Factoscope (He et al., 2024c) and INSIDE (Chen et al., 2024), reveals that hidden states contain information about factuality and hallucination risk. Our method follows this approach, using geometric statistics of activations as a reward signal for policy optimization. The theoretical basis for rank-based quality measures comes from the analysis of the softmax bottleneck. Yang et al. (2018) demonstrated that the expressiveness of softmax-based models is fundamentally constrained by the rank of their hidden representations. Moreover, Godey et al. (2024) extended this principle to modern LLMs, showing that the contextual probability distribution of natural language inherently possesses a high rank. Complementary studies indicate that collapsing embeddings into a narrow dimensional cone during training results in decreased generation quality. Together, these findings establish a crucial link between representational dimensionality and generation quality. Empirical evidence further supports this connection, as illustrated by Garrido et al. (2023), which shows that the effective rank of learned representations can predict downstream performance without needing labels. Specifically, in language models, hidden states encode rich semantic structures: word embeddings capture syntactic and semantic regularities through geometric relationships, while intermediate representations encode factuality and risk of errors. This confirms that generation quality leaves detectable geometric signatures. Building on these insights, our work moves from diagnosis to optimization. Instead of using rank merely for post-hoc analysis, we employ stable rank as an active reward signal during policy optimization, thereby transforming a geometric insight into a practical alignment method.

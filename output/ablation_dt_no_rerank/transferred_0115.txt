Human cognition excels using only visual data. To this end, we present VIDEO4SPATIAL, a framework demonstrating that video diffusion models can perform complex spatial tasks relying solely on video-based scene context. We validate this framework using two tasks: scene navigation, which involves adhering to camera-pose instructions while remaining consistent with the 3D geometry of the scene, and object grounding, which requires semantic localization, instruction following, and planning. Notably, both tasks employ video-only inputs, devoid of auxiliary modalities like depth or poses. Through simple yet effective design choices in the framework and data curation, VIDEO4SPATIAL exhibits strong spatial understanding derived from video context: it effectively plans navigation and grounds target objects from start to finish, follows camera-pose instructions while maintaining spatial coherence, and generalizes to extended contexts and out-of-domain environments. Collectively, these achievements propel video generative models toward general visuospatial reasoning. Humans are particularly adept at remembering, understanding, and acting within spatial environments—a hallmark of high-level spatial reasoning capability. A long line of research has aimed to endow neural networks with similar abilities. Recently, rapid advancements in video generative models have started to address this challenge, displaying general-purpose perceptual and reasoning abilities, similar to the trajectory observed with Large Language Models. Because video generation is inherently sequential and temporally coherent, these models are well-suited for visuospatial tasks by projecting future frames that preserve scene geometry and appearance, thereby simulating plausible futures. For instance, in maze navigation, a model can envision progress by generating frames that maintain the maze's topology while ensuring smooth transitions. Given that humans primarily perceive spatial information visually, we aim to impart models with this capability using video as a simple, general, and scalable modality. However, learning spatial understanding solely from RGB video is challenging, as many recent methods continue to rely on auxiliary signals such as depth maps, camera poses, or point clouds for supervision or augmentation. In this study, we introduce VIDEO4SPATIAL, showcasing that video generative models relying solely on video context from a spatial environment exhibit strong spatial intelligence. Our framework is intentionally straightforward: a standard video diffusion architecture trained solely with the diffusion objective. The inputs consist of a video context and instructions, while the output is a video that completes the instructed spatial task, maintaining scene geometry and temporal coherence.

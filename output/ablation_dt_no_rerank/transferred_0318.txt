Summarization. In Table 3, summaries generated from audio+image inputs consistently outperform those based solely on audio across most languages and models, even though the summarization models utilize text exclusively. These improvements are most pronounced in English, German, and Italian, while results for Chinese show less advancement. This is likely because English domain terminology appears frequently in Latin-alphabet languages, whereas the significant lexical distance between English and Chinese prevents models from fully capitalizing on additional context provided in English. Question Answering. In most scenarios, incorporating visual context slightly enhances QA results, though these gains are less significant compared to summarization. We witness minor improvements and performance regression in four out of twelve language-model combinations. We attribute this to the fact that the LLM does not directly receive image data; it only benefits from the enhanced textual context, which is insufficient for reliability in answering questions. Streaming ST has been extensively explored in the past decade. Several lecture translation tools have harnessed ST, predominantly relying on audio inputs. Conversely, our approach broadens lecture translation to multimodal inputs, integrating visual elements from slides, and producing translated audio and slide content in numerous languages. Image-to-image translation remains relatively unexplored, although interest is increasing with larger dataset availability. Most research focuses on text translation within images, without addressing the essential aspect of accurate visual re-rendering post-translation. A preliminary step in this direction is a study that explicitly models the rendering process. Our image translation pipeline offers a modular base, allowing researchers to integrate models at various stages from OCR to translation and rendering, without necessitating additional components.

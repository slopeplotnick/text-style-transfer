To address these challenges, model-agnostic approaches have been introduced to compare generated outputs with training data within a shared embedding space, typically utilizing features derived from pre-trained encoders. While effective and scalable, these methods rely on vector representations that are difficult to interpret. Recent studies, such as LatentExplainer, attempt to bridge this interpretability gap by manipulating latent variables and using language models to elucidate their semantic implications. However, these approaches continue to operate within an opaque framework that limits transparency for end-users. In contrast to these intricate techniques, our method employs automatically generated Knowledge Graphs (KGs) to trace the influence of data, thereby providing clearer insights into semantic dynamics. By comparing generated and training images via shared triples that encapsulate objects, attributes, and stylistic elements, we facilitate more accessible interpretability and alignment with human cognition. Moreover, Large Language Models (LLMs) have emerged as pivotal components in contemporary KG processes, enabling the structuring of unstructured data. Specifically, their principal roles encompass three critical domains: generating subject-predicate-object triples from natural language text, facilitating ontology development, and transforming natural language inquiries into graph queries. For the purpose of triple generation, methodologies such as AutoKG are capable of transforming text into ontology-aligned triples. Furthermore, LLMs extend beyond mere extraction by facilitating ontology learning and expansion through techniques such as prompt engineering and the reuse of ontologies. In the realm of question answering, LLMs serve as semantic parsers, converting user inquiries into SPARQL queries. This process is enhanced by employing contextual knowledge graphs or controlled natural languages, thereby fostering greater clarity and precision. Notably, recent surveys on generative KG construction indicate a shift from conventional pipeline architectures to unified, LLM-driven systems that demonstrate superior capabilities in entity recognition, relation extraction, and graph population compared to traditional approaches. These advancements in KG construction offer a robust foundation for addressing challenges associated with data attribution in generative models. Instead of relying on opaque embeddings or inaccessible parameters, structured outputs from multimodal LLMs can be employed to construct interpretable knowledge graphs (KGs) that encapsulate key aspects of both generated and training data, thereby enabling the tracing of influences. Although the majority of existing research has focused predominantly on textual data, these methodologies exhibit significant potential for adaptation to images, notwithstanding the added complexity inherent in visual content.

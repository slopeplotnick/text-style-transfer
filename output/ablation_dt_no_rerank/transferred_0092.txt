Upon removing the deformable scan, which reverts the model to the vanilla configuration of Mamba, we notice a significant drop in performance, underscoring its critical role. Therefore, to justify the proposed tribrid architecture design, we explored various alternatives. Our architecture is represented as "CCDGDG" across stages 1 to 6, where C, D, and G denote Conv, DDSM+FNN, and Gated-Conv blocks, respectively. In addition, we constructed hybrid architectures using combinations of two block types (such as "CCGGGG" and "CCDDD") and monomodal architectures consisting exclusively of a single block type. The experimental results confirm that our tribrid architecture significantly outperforms both the hybrid and monomodal variants, highlighting the effectiveness of integrating complementary block structures. Qualitative examples, as depicted in Figure 5, illustrate DF-Mamba's ability to accurately estimate joint positions, as evidenced by reduced positional errors highlighted using colored ellipses, even in scenarios where joints are occluded or not directly visible from an egocentric viewpoint. Thus, DF-Mamba showcases enhanced robustness in real-world settings. We propose DF-Mamba as a novel backbone architecture for 3D hand pose estimation, combining efficient convolutional feature extraction in the lower layers with a deformable state-space representation in the higher layers. Through extensive testing on five datasets covering single-hand, two-hand, and hand-object interactions, as well as both RGB and depth modalities, we demonstrate that DF-Mamba consistently surpasses existing architectures while achieving faster inference. Promising avenues for future study include 1) extending DF-Mamba to broader tasks like 3D human pose estimation, 2) pre-training it using vast external data, 3) applying it to recent hand-mesh reconstruction decoders, and 4) training it as a foundational model that generalizes across scenarios without data-specific fine-tuning.

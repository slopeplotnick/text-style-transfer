Thus, OptPO-SFT employs the same update strategy as TTSFT but utilizes a Bayesian principle to adaptively dictate iteration frequency, thereby unifying efficiency and reliability. In a unified perspective, both the RL-based and SFT-based variants of OptPO adhere to a three-step protocol: initiating the rollouts, computing pseudo-labels through a specific technique, and performing parameter updates. They differ by their update mechanisms—policy-gradient optimization versus cross-entropy fine-tuning—but their operational framework remains conceptually unified. To benchmark their efficacy, we adopt challenging math and science reasoning tasks: MATH-500 with 500 high school-level problems, AIME 2024 containing 30 pre-Olympiad problems, AMC featuring American Mathematics Competition problems, and GPQA with rigorous, graduate-level scientific inquiries. We evaluate the OptPO framework on prominent open-source models such as Qwen2.5-Math1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, thereby verifying its performance across diverse model scales and configurations. In assessing the effectiveness of TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct serve as the primary backbone models. OptPO acts as a plug-and-play framework that can be integrated seamlessly into various established RL methodologies to enhance test-time training efficiency. Specifically, we incorporated OptPO into PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++ to ensure a comprehensive evaluation of its efficacy. We conducted a comparative analysis against TTRL, recognized as the predominant method for test-time policy optimization, while also assessing OptPO-SFT under analogous conditions. The critic learning rate was established at 1e-5. In SFT-based training methodologies, including TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, gradient descent is applied to the pseudo-labeled batch using the AdamW optimizer, maintaining a learning rate of 2e-5.

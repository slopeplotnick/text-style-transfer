The hybrid routing strategy is essential as enforcing a single execution path substantially degrades task success, particularly when workloads involve both local and global edits. A case-by-case analysis indicates that providing slide images to PPTPilot improves layout reasoning since visual grounding enhances decisions about z-ordering, spacing, and alignment without compromising text editing quality. Iterative refinement stabilizes complex edits, with an additional pass often resolving formatting drift after re-theming or batch rewrites, at a modest computational overhead. We introduce PPTArena and PPTPilot to establish PowerPoint editing as a concrete, measurable capability for agentistic multi-modal systems. PPTArena converts real slide decks, human-authored edit programs, ground truths, and style-aware rubrics into a benchmark that highlights instruction fidelity, structural grounding, and layout quality beyond generation-centric evaluations. In demonstrating a dual-path paradigm with explicit plan-edit-verify loops, PPTPilot substantially improves reliability, surpassing strong proprietary agents and cutting-edge VLMs. However, agents still face challenges with complex, long-horizon, multi-modal tasks, highlighting that robust PPT editing remains unsolved. We anticipate that PPTArena and PPTPilot will serve as a foundation for future advancements in robust PPT editing systems capable of safely and reliably operating on the documents that people actually use.

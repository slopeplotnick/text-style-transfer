Experiment 2 demonstrated that employing a single system prompt can enhance performance across all evaluated languages, indicating a systematic change in the model's behavior. To better understand the changes in model output, we analyzed the intermediate reasoning processes and assessed how alterations in specific reasoning behaviors impact multilingual performance. A pertinent question arises: does prompt optimization influence the model's language preference? We begin by examining the languages used within the reasoning chains generated by the model. As illustrated in Figure 7, even when using identical randomized prompts, the model exhibits different language preferences based on the input question's language. These preferences correspond with the availability of training data for each language. For instance, due to the plentiful Chinese data in model families such as Qwen, the model responds to Chinese inquiries in Chinese almost as frequently as in English. After optimization, these language preferences become more pronounced: the model heavily favors generating responses in the language of the input question, rather than defaulting to English as observed with the initial system prompt. However, for languages with limited training data, like Hindi, the rate of English responses remains relatively stable post-optimization. Additionally, languages other than the task language and English provide minimal benefit to the model, as evidenced by their substantial decline following prompt optimization. Further information on language identification is available in Appendix A.9, with additional data in Tables 6 and 7. In Figure 8, we delve into the distribution of reasoning behavior types across various languages. Despite the overall patterns remaining largely consistent, certain languages display distinct preferences. For example, English shows a predominant inclination toward Reframing, Chinese leans toward Logical Reasoning, and Spanish exhibits a notable preference for calculation. Conversely, languages with limited training data, such as Hindi, show an increased representation of the Other category, indicating a relatively lower exposure to structured reasoning exercises during pretraining. While these trends are consistent, they seem more influenced by intrinsic language-specific patterns in model pretraining, rather than the intricacies of prompt design.

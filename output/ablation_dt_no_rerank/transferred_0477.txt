To this end, we evaluate our framework on four simulation benchmarks, comprising a total of 64 tasks, and five real-world tasks, to cover three types of VLA policies. Specifically, we conduct our evaluations using the Simpler, Libero, Robotwin1.0, and Robotwin2.0 benchmarks. Simpler, established on the SAPIEN simulator and ManiSkill2 framework, provides simulated task environments compatible with both the WindowX and Google Robot platforms; however, in this work, we primarily focus on tasks designed for the WindowX platform. Libero serves as a benchmark for lifelong learning in decision-making, consisting of four task suites. We concentrate on the most challenging suite since previous works have achieved significant success in the others. Robotwin1.0 and Robotwin2.0 are centered on dual-arm gripper manipulation, offering diverse tasks and facilitating automatic data collection. For the real-world experimental setup, we use the REALMAN75 dual-arm robot in an office-like environment. This setup includes five distinct tasks involving various common objects, specifically books, pens, a cabinet, a charger, a laptop, and a paper ball. The tasks are as follows: in Receive Book, the robot receives a book from a human and places it back on the bookshelf; in Storage Charger, it picks up the charger head, places it inside the cabinet, and closes the cabinet door; in Paper and Pen, it disposes of the paper ball and places the pen into the pen holder; in Laptop, it closes the laptop lid, unplugs the charger from the socket, and stores it in the cabinet; and in Pick Books, both the left and right arms simultaneously pick up and handle a book.

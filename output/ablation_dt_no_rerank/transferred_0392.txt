On-device AI technology provides a compelling advantage for mental health monitoring by enabling real-time personalized feedback and safeguarding user privacy. By operating directly on users’ smartphones, this approach eliminates the need to transmit sensitive data to cloud servers, reducing risks of data leakage and minimizing dependence on internet connectivity. Moreover, the availability of such tools on mobile devices significantly improves accessibility, especially for individuals in remote areas or those facing long waits for clinical appointments. The direct integration of AI functionalities allows for expedited feedback, facilitating the detection of linguistic and behavioral changes indicative of stress or mood disorders. With mobile devices gaining prominence due to their rapid operational capabilities, reduced energy consumption, and enhanced privacy protections over cloud-reliant systems, empirical evidence supports the efficacy of Small Language Models (SLMs), such as those used in the Samsung Galaxy S24. These models not only optimize memory usage and processing speed but are also particularly suited for practical applications due to their ease of deployment and minimal resource requirements. Furthermore, their ability to function autonomously without continuous cloud connectivity further enhances the safeguarding of user privacy. However, despite previous investigations demonstrating the capacity of SLMs in handling specific mental health tasks, their broader applicability remains largely unexplored. Prior research largely focused on fundamental tasks, with limited efficacy in the face of complex datasets and constrained computational resources. To address these challenges, we introduce Menta—an innovative framework employing the LoRA methodology to facilitate multi-task execution and effectively manage heterogeneous data across a variety of mental health tasks, maintaining efficiency and safeguarding privacy on commonly used devices.

In this investigation, we examine four distinct models—MOIRAI, CHRONOS, GTT, and LightGTS—to evaluate their handling of data and training divergences. Each model employs unique methodologies for learning and prediction, focusing on managing variations in data size rather than computational potency; hence, the smallest configurations were employed. Moreover, each model possesses distinct predictive and learning strategies impacting their ability to address fluctuations in data dimensions. For instance, CHRONOS partitions data into smaller segments prior to learning, thereby mitigating the impact of data size and ensuring robustness against large numerical values. To further ensure accuracy, we meticulously preprocess and normalize the datasets to avert any anomalous data from skewing results. Accordingly, adjustments are made to harmonize all data points. We assess the models' efficacy using the Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE) as a key evaluation metric, which facilitates predictive accuracy assessment irrespective of dataset size and ensures a standardized comparison. Our objective is to examine the impact of varying data dimensions on model performance, demonstrated through a comparative analysis of MASE reductions against established benchmarks.

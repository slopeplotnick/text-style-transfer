Moreover, Galhotra et al. [2021] engage in an analytical examination of probability determination in scenarios where a fully specified system model is unavailable. Counterfactual intervals for AI explanations are further explored in their work, utilizing specific models to calculate intervals for counterfactual queries. This approach allows for a concentrated examination of model components within a subset, which can yield narrower interval estimates compared to purely mathematical calculations when domain knowledge is incomplete. Probabilities of sufficiency and necessity are also integral for evaluating AI explanation methods. Mothilal et al. [2021] define an ideal model explanation based on these two concepts, proposing that the explanation should consist of a minimal subset of essential features that are both necessary and sufficient for a model outcome in a given context. Assuming that explanations pertain solely to input features, which are independent, various types of AI explanations are analyzed in this light. The concept of necessity relates to explanations that identify the changes required for the original output, while sufficiency involves determining which features are crucial for yielding the outcome, regardless of other conditions. Pawar et al. [2024] similarly examine sufficiency and necessity as criteria for evaluating existing AI methodologies, extending the discussion to cover different approaches to generating exemplar explanations under the assumption of feature independence. Additionally, Beckers [2022] explores scenarios where predictive models determine outcomes based on prescribed actions, under the premise of model infallibility. Both sufficient and counterfactual explanations are defined for this scenario, assuming that the model accurately predicts outcomes. The types of explanations are further distinguished by the varying degrees of causal influence to identify the principal causes underlying the explanations.

This paper elaborates on the acquisition of diverse linguistic categories through a computational multi-agent language acquisition system, comprising an adult language model, Delilah, and a daughter agent. The daughter agent, specifically designed for MODOMA, utilizes graph-based representations to effectively delineate grammatical knowledge, thereby enhancing sentence production and comprehension as language acquisition progresses. Conversely, Delilah includes a parser and generator independently developed by Cremers and Hijzelendoorn, which employs the grammar model of Dutch to output and comprehend sentences. Consequently, the MODOMA framework necessitates a novel methodological approach to the investigation of Delilah. Beyond the enhancement of Delilah's capabilities in sentence generation and interpretation, it is crucial to examine the properties of Delilah's output to enable the daughter model to discern necessary acquisition patterns. To achieve this, techniques and analytical frameworks from the disciplines of linguistics, psycholinguistics, and corpus linguistics could be employed. These applications will provide further insights into machine-generated linguistic outputs akin to those afforded by the analysis of human language data. Given the increasing prominence of large language models, assessing the congruence between machine-generated language and human language has become increasingly relevant. This study presents findings from experiments exploring this subject. Interestingly, the utterances produced by Delilah during interactions with the daughter agent manifest as discrete instances, whereas typical corpora generally consist of interrelated sentences. Nonetheless, similar grammatical patterns are observed in both types of data, which may suggest that these patterns pertain more to the individual sentences within these collections rather than to the entire corpora. Crucially, this study demonstrates that the divergent frequency distributions of linguistic categories, well-documented in human language data, are also observed in Delilah's output. Significantly, this pattern emerges not as a result of Delilah's configuration to replicate these patterns but rather as an inherent outcome of the employed grammatical model.

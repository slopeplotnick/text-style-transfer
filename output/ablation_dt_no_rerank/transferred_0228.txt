Grammatical features were thoroughly examined, and then a subsequent experiment was conducted with training and test data sets consisting of 10,000 noun phrases (NPs) and sentence exemplars produced by Delilah. If the language acquisition process successfully identifies distinctions that allow words to be systematically classified, the results are illustrated using graphs with feature-value pairs specified by alphanumeric labels. These labels should align with the terms typically used by linguists, as shown in Figure 3a with labels applied by the MODOMA agent and Figure 3b with conventional labels. Similar to the previous experiment, the 35 most frequent words in both data sets are listed in Table 6 and Table 7. Notably, these top-ranked words are generally function words. In the sample of 10,000 NPs, the first clear content word, 'forgotten', appears at rank 54/55, sharing its position with the function word 'at' or 'near'. Beyond rank 55, the number of content words increases. Similarly, in the experiment with 10,000 sentences, the most frequent content words are 'forgotten' at position 11, 'works' at position 27, and 'in the morning' at position 66. After rank 66, additional content words become more apparent. Interestingly, Delilah uses 'masses' as a numeral, and this usage is correctly identified by the acquisition procedures. For the data sets of 10,000 exemplars, it's easier to distinguish between function and content words manually, as words with a frequency exceeding 100 occurrences tend to be function words—surpassing 1.7 per thousand in Delilah's corpus. Therefore, the MODOMA parameters specify that word types with a frequency above 2 per thousand are classified as function categories, while others are considered content words. Furthermore, this acquisition procedure is executed once after assimilating at least 10,000 exemplars. These parameters are conservatively set to minimize the risk of incorrectly classifying a word as a function word rather than a content word. Since content is the default category, a word is presumed to be a content word absent strong evidence to the contrary. The parameter values from this initial experiment have been established as the default settings for the MODOMA system, although these settings can be adjusted based on further experiments and linguistic theories.

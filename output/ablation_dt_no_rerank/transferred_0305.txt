SMP serves as a modular motion style reward model that facilitates policy training without the need for access to the original motion dataset. This capability allows for the implementation of various adaptation strategies to refine our diffusion-based reward model, thereby altering the motion prior. However, this process may sometimes lead to unnatural behaviors. The comparative performance of different methodologies is shown in the supplementary video. Moreover, SMP supports crafting novel motion priors by manipulating the outputs of the pretrained diffusion model, enabling the creation of new styles not present initially without additional training of the prior. For example, as illustrated in Figure 6, new styles like "AeroPlane + HighKnees" can be synthesized by blending style-conditioned predictions from the pretrained model. As shown in Table 2, a single pretrained score-matching motion prior can be reused to train multiple policies for diverse tasks, including steering, target location, and dodgeball. The score-matching motion prior is originally trained on a subset of the LaFAN1 dataset, which features unstructured running behaviors. Consequently, the policies achieve high task performance while maintaining natural gaits. As depicted in Figures 5(a) and 5(b), the character dynamically selects and transitions between suitable gaits from the prior to uphold both task performance and motion naturalness. The Steering policy automatically initiates a backward jog when the character's direction opposes the target velocity, transitioning to a forward jog upon alignment. Furthermore, it learns lateral movements like side-stepping and cross-stepping without needing an explicit motion planner. In the Target Location task, the policy trained with SMP discerns human-like strategies, such as modulating speed autonomously.

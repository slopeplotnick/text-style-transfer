We source forecasting questions from Metaculus [Metaculus, 2015] and Polymarket [Polymarket, 2020] to test belief entrenchment in LLMs. This domain is chosen for two key reasons: first, forecasting questions come with ground truth labels upon resolution; second, achieving high accuracy on these questions reflects Bayesian-like reasoning, which requires seeking evidence and proportionally updating beliefs. Although forecasting differs from factual question answering, it nonetheless demands well-calibrated, unbiased belief updates, making it a strong proxy for rational reasoning under uncertainty. Similarly, scientific peer review is another setting that meets all three criteria for evaluating belief entrenchment. For this purpose, we employ the open-access ICLR submission dataset from OpenReview [Höpner et al., 2025], which includes paper abstracts, bibliographies, reviewer comments, rebuttals, and final acceptance decisions. In our setup, the model assumes the role of an area chair, tasked with making a final acceptance decision based on the abstract and the arguments presented in the reviews and rebuttals. This approach enables an evaluation of how reasoning unfolds when initial impressions from the abstract may conflict with later-stage evidence such as critiques and responses. We evaluate belief entrenchment using two reasoning techniques: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) [Wei et al., 2022] and debate [Khan et al., 2024]. The debate method lets two clones of the same expert model hold opposing positions, thereby allowing a less informed observer to achieve a better understanding of the subject. We conduct experiments using GPT-4o, DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Flash, LLaMA 4 Scout, and LLaMA 4 Maverick. To examine how prior beliefs affect reasoning, model priors are manipulated via system prompts. Specifically, beyond a baseline with no system prompt, we introduce two prompting conditions: a prior-conforming prompt and a critical-thinking prompt.

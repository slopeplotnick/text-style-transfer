The analysis evaluates the performance of CFAULTs and HSD. Specifically, Figure 6a presents a cactus plot illustrating CPU time (y-axis) against the number of circuits successfully localized (x-axis), using CFAULTs, HSD, and HSD-CM on the augmented ISCAS85 benchmark. The results confirm that HSD achieves faster performance overall and localizes faults in a larger number of circuits, aligning with the findings of Table 5. Moreover, additional insights are provided by the scatter plots in Figures 6b, 6c, and 6d, which compare the CPU times of CFAULTS and HSD. For instance, in Figure 6b, each point corresponds to a faulty circuit, with the x-value representing the time taken by HSD and the y-value representing the time taken by CFAULTS to localize faults in that circuit. Points lying above the diagonal indicate circuits where CFAULTS required more time than HSD to compute a valid diagnosis. These plots clearly demonstrate that HSD, both with and without core minimization, consistently outperforms CFAULTS in terms of CPU time. However, in the evaluation on buggy C software, using the TCAS and C-PACK-IPAs benchmarks, CFAULTS showed clear advantages over the competing FBFL methods. Although CFAULTS encodes all failing test cases into a single MaxSAT formula, it consistently outperformed the other approaches. Specifically, CFAULTS was both faster and able to localize more faults. Therefore, among the FBFL approaches considered, CFAULTS is the fastest and most reliable method for C software. Nonetheless, for Boolean circuits, it remains competitive, but HSD continues to represent the state of the art. This difference in performance can be explained by the structural distinctions in fault localization tasks between C programs and Boolean circuits, resulting in divergent MaxSAT formula encodings. This distinction has a substantial influence on solver efficiency and contributes significantly to the observed disparity between the two domains.

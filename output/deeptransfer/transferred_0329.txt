The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has catalyzed the exploration of novel applications aimed at societal benefit. As these models increasingly mediate online communication, their potential to enhance empathy and foster constructive dialogue has emerged as a critical focus within responsible AI research. This study investigates whether LLMs can go beyond detecting harmful content to actively mediate disputes and promote resolution in online interactions. To this end, the proposed framework decomposes mediation into two core components: judgment, where the model assesses the fairness of a conversation and analyzes participants' emotional dynamics, and steering, where it generates empathetic, de-escalatory responses to guide conflict resolution. To evaluate the effectiveness of these models, the researchers constructed a comprehensive dataset derived from Reddit and developed a multi-faceted evaluation pipeline that integrates principle-based scoring, user simulation, and human comparison. The findings reveal that proprietary models outperform open-access counterparts, particularly in reasoning capabilities and their ability to mediate and resolve disputes effectively. Nevertheless, while LLMs demonstrate significant potential, they also exhibit notable limitations in fostering positive online interactions. Beyond traditional natural language processing tasks such as translation and summarization, LLMs are increasingly being applied in domains like education, healthcare, and mental well-being, underscoring their expanding role in addressing complex societal challenges. As these systems become embedded in online platforms where individuals engage in discourse and debate, a novel paradigm emerges: using LLMs not merely for task automation but as agents that promote kindness, fairness, and constructive dialogue. However, a significant challenge in this domain remains the prevalence of hostile interactions, often characterized by repeated personal attacks and escalating emotional intensity. This issue is further exacerbated by the absence of visual cues and social signals in online communication.

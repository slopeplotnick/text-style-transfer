The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has enabled the exploration of innovative applications aimed at societal benefit. As these models increasingly mediate online communication, their potential to foster empathy and constructive dialogue has emerged as a critical area of responsible AI research. This study examines whether LLMs can move beyond simply detecting harmful content to actively de-escalating tensions and resolving conflicts in online discourse. Our framework decomposes this mediation task into two key components: judgment, where the model evaluates the fairness and emotional dynamics of a conversation, and steering, where it generates empathetic, de-escalatory messages to guide participants toward resolution. To evaluate the effectiveness of these models, we construct a comprehensive Reddit-based dataset and propose a multi-stage evaluation pipeline that integrates principle-based scoring, user simulation, and human comparative analysis. Results indicate that proprietary models outperform open-source counterparts in reasoning and conflict resolution, highlighting both the promise and limitations of current LLMs as tools for online social mediation. Beyond traditional applications such as machine translation and text summarization, LLMs are increasingly being deployed in critical domains like education, healthcare, and mental health support, reshaping how individuals communicate, learn, and collaborate. As these systems become embedded in online spaces where people interact and debate, a new frontier emerges: employing LLMs not merely for task automation but as agents that promote kindness, fairness, and constructive dialogue. However, a significant challenge in these digital spaces is the prevalence of hostile interactions, often characterized by persistent personal attacks and escalating emotional intensity. This issue is further exacerbated by the absence of face-to-face cues, which typically facilitate more empathetic communication.

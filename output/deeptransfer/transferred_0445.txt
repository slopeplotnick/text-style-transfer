We source forecasting questions from Metaculus [Metaculus, 2015] and Polymarket [Polymarket, 2020] to evaluate belief entrenchment in large language models (LLMs). This domain is chosen for two primary reasons: (1) forecasting questions provide definitive ground truth labels upon resolution; and (2) achieving high accuracy on these questions demonstrates Bayesian-like reasoning, as it requires gathering evidence and proportionally updating beliefs. While forecasting differs from factual question answering, it still demands well-calibrated and unbiased belief updates, making it a robust proxy for reasoning under uncertainty. Another domain that meets these criteria for assessing belief entrenchment is scientific peer review. For this, we leverage the open-access ICLR submission dataset from OpenReview, which includes paper abstracts, bibliographies, reviewer comments, author rebuttals, and final acceptance decisions. In our experimental setup, the model assumes the role of an area chair, tasked with making acceptance decisions based on the abstract and the evaluative arguments presented in the reviews and rebuttals. This setup enables us to analyze how the model’s reasoning evolves when initial impressions, such as those formed from the abstract, conflict with subsequent evidence, such as critiques and responses. To evaluate belief entrenchment, we employ two reasoning methodologies: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and debate. The debate methodology involves two iterations of the same expert model presenting opposing arguments on a topic, helping a less-informed observer gain a more comprehensive understanding. We conduct experiments using models such as GPT-4o, DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Flash, LLaMA 4 Scout, and LLaMA 4 Maverick. To investigate how prior beliefs influence reasoning, we manipulate the models’ initial conditions through system prompts. Specifically, in addition to a baseline condition without prompts, we introduce two configurations: one where the prompt aligns with the model’s initial belief and another designed to encourage critical thinking.

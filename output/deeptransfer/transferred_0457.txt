In this section, we describe the computation of each evaluation metric and the interpretation of their outputs, focusing on lexical overlap, semantic similarity, and factual consistency. ROUGE-L [Lin, 2004] measures the longest common subsequence (LCS) between a model-generated summary (hypothesis) and a reference summary. By capturing sentence-level structural similarity, ROUGE-L is less sensitive to variations in word order. It is commonly employed as a reference-based metric to compare system-generated outputs with human-written summaries. In this study, however, we are not concerned with the absolute ROUGE-L score. Instead, we analyze relative differences across input-order conditions to investigate potential primacy effects. Since ROUGE-L primarily reflects surface-level lexical overlap, we complement it with two additional metrics—BERTScore and SummaC—to assess semantic similarity and factual consistency, respectively. To go beyond surface-level word matching, BERTScore [Zhang et al., 2019] leverages contextual embeddings from a pre-trained model (RoBERTa-large in this study) to evaluate semantic similarity. For each token in the hypothesis, BERTScore identifies the most semantically similar token in the reference and calculates precision, recall, and F1 scores based on these alignments. This approach effectively accounts for synonymy and paraphrastic variation, making it well-suited for capturing semantic similarity beyond exact lexical matches. Consistent with our study design, we focus on relative differences in BERTScore across input-order conditions rather than absolute scores, which may be lower due to the abstractive nature of the summaries. Finally, SummaC evaluates whether the generated summary is entailed by the original documents, thereby assessing factual consistency. This metric is available in two variants: SummaC-ZS, which operates in a zero-shot manner without training, and SummaC-Conv, a trained convolutional model. We adopt SummaC-Conv due to its superior performance. However, the RoBERTa-based architecture underlying SummaC-Conv imposes limitations on the maximum input length it can process effectively.

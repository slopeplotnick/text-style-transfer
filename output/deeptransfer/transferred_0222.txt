Research in the domain of truthful AI focuses on developing AI systems that provide accurate representations of the real world. Prior studies have proposed various interventions to enhance the "truthfulness" of AI systems, such as identifying learned activations that align with truth [Burns et al., 2022] or modifying model activations during the inference process [Li et al., 2023]. Additionally, there has been discourse on the trade-off between the truthfulness of an AI system and its utility [Su et al., 2025]. Honest AI pertains to ensuring that AI systems do not deliberately convey information that contradicts their internal beliefs merely to obtain rewards, thereby addressing concerns related to AI deception. This is closely related to the concept of deception in large language models [Hubinger et al., 2024, Su et al., 2025]. Conversely, truth-seeking AI is concerned with the examination of evidence and the discovery of novel insights. As AI systems increasingly exhibit autonomous behavior, the significance of truth-seeking AI is likely to escalate, as these systems will autonomously determine which information to pursue based on their interpretation of the prevailing circumstances. Truth-seeking AI endeavors to ascertain veracity; however, the evaluation of its efficacy is not confined to a singular metric. For instance, it may strive to adhere to Bayesian principles or maintain coherence in its reasoning processes. Belief entrenchment refers to the propensity of individuals to adhere to their existing beliefs despite the presence of contradictory evidence. This phenomenon closely parallels confirmation bias, wherein individuals selectively seek, recall, or interpret information in a manner that corroborates their pre-existing beliefs. The assessment of confirmation bias, which pertains to the manner in which individuals process information in particular contexts, presents significant challenges in terms of measurement. In the field of psychology, the measurement of confirmation bias is inherently task-specific and is characterized by low reliability. Accurately assessing confirmation bias necessitates a comprehensive understanding of an individual's pre-existing beliefs and the development of a reliable method to quantify these beliefs, which presents a considerable challenge.

To effectively process extensive video content, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methodologies have been developed, focusing on retrieving relevant segments such as specific frames or clips using textual and visual cues rather than random selection. This approach allows the model to concentrate on essential components of the video. Recent advancements extend beyond traditional methods by employing graph-based approaches to elucidate interconnections among video segments. However, these models often rely heavily on textual representations or simplistic visual-textual comparisons, limiting their capacity for comprehensive understanding and sophisticated reasoning across extended video sequences. To address these limitations, memory-based methodologies have emerged, facilitating the organization and structured storage of information from video streams. For instance, EgoRAG organizes hierarchical textual memories derived from video streams, enabling reasoning across day-long activities. Building on this, Ego-R1 incorporates vision-centric tools and iterative reasoning mechanisms for long-horizon tasks. Similarly, HippoMM introduces a dual-process memory framework that integrates semantic summaries with multimodal information to enhance reasoning capabilities. M3-Agent constructs an entity-centric memory system using iterative reasoning to retrieve pertinent information. Despite these advancements, existing methodologies still struggle to fully integrate textual and visual modalities and dynamically adapt to diverse temporal scales within video content to handle complex scenarios. Therefore, we introduce WorldMM, an innovative framework that leverages both textual and visual contexts from video streams to construct a multimodal memory system, facilitating enhanced comprehension and reasoning over extended video sequences. The system operates through a tripartite process: initially constructing multiple memory types, including two textual memories and one visual memory. Subsequently, it employs a retrieval mechanism to extract pertinent information from these memories across various temporal segments until sufficient data is accumulated to address a query. Ultimately, it synthesizes this information to generate a response.

To address RQ2, we evaluated our models on 11 low-resource languages, each selectively sampled from the 8 clusters. Notably, languages written in the Latin script, such as Galician and Slovenian, exhibited more stable performance across various benchmarks. This consistency is likely due to script overlap and subword tokenization coverage in the shared XLM-R vocabulary, which favors languages with higher representation during pretraining. However, Icelandic, despite also using the Latin script, performed less reliably due to its rich and complex morphological structure, leading to significant token fragmentation and less coherent sentence representations. These factors can limit the effectiveness of multilingual encoders using subword tokenization. To assess the extent to which training on a high-resource language supports transfer within and across clusters and subsequently answer RQ3, we trained models with the following configurations: (i) Hindi-only, (ii) Chinese-only, and (iii) a combination of Hindi and Chinese. Each model was evaluated across six languages from the Indic (Hindi) and East-Southeast Asian (Chinese) clusters. Within the Indic cluster, the Hindi-trained model consistently outperformed the Chinese-trained model across all benchmarks. Conversely, in the East-Southeast Asian cluster, the Chinese-trained model demonstrated superior performance compared to the Hindi-trained model for the majority of the benchmarks. This indicates that transfer within a cluster from its own high-resource language to other member languages is more effective than transfer across clusters from a high-resource language of a different cluster.

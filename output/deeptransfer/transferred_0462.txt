Language Model Systems (LMS), which include Large Language Models (LLMs) and other related architectures, are employed across a diverse range of applications, such as medical diagnostic chatbots, autonomous vehicle assistance systems, and programming support tools. As these systems are deployed in critical and publicly accessible domains, ensuring their reliability, accuracy, and alignment with user expectations is crucial. Numerous studies have explored the integration of safety features within LMS. However, the current methodologies for evaluating and enforcing desirable behaviors remain inadequate, as they lack the capacity to provide universal formal guarantees of efficacy. Such guarantees are essential to ensure user safety and foster trust in these systems. Recent scholarly efforts have initiated the formal certification of LMS for specific desirable behaviors, including fairness, accurate question answering, and safety, which are encapsulated as precise mathematical specifications that the systems must adhere to. For instance, researchers have developed specifications for counterfactual fairness in responses, comprehension of reading material, management of sensitive dialogues, and appropriate tool selection. Nevertheless, these narrowly defined certification frameworks are inherently inflexible, as they are tailored to specific properties and are predominantly applicable within conversational contexts. Consequently, modifying these frameworks to accommodate alternative requirements or contexts necessitates substantial reconfiguration. Therefore, the current paradigm for certifying LMS relies on bespoke methodologies tailored to each property, domain, and system type—a limitation this work seeks to address.

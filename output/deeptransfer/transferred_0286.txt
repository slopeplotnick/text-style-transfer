In the analysis of SWE agents, there has been an initial endeavor to identify areas where these agents excel and where they encounter challenges. Studies indicate that state-of-the-art SWE agents struggle significantly with issues involving multiple files. Recent research has emphasized the necessity for more comprehensive evaluation frameworks that assess efficiency, reliability, and reasoning capabilities. Further investigations into the failure modes of these agents have identified several critical challenges, including inaccurate fault localization, difficulties in comprehending intricate code dependencies, and an inability to maintain contextual awareness across multiple files. To address these limitations, Deshpande et al. introduced TRAIL, a curated dataset comprising a benchmark of 148 human-verified traces designed to systematically analyze and evaluate agent performance. Similarly, Cemri et al. proposed MAST (Multi-Agent System Failure Taxonomy) as a framework for classifying failures in multi-agent systems. This approach involves the analysis of 150 execution traces collected from five multi-agent systems across five benchmarks, although the taxonomy and subsequent analysis rely on human annotations. Additionally, Ceka et al. constructed a flow graph where nodes represent agent actions categorized into project understanding, context comprehension, and activities related to patching and testing. However, their analysis was conducted manually and encompassed only a limited subset of the dataset, specifically addressing bug localization for 20 out of 500 issues. Moreover, prior research by Liu et al. developed a taxonomy of failure modes in LLM tools during SWE tasks, categorizing them into three primary phases, nine main categories, and 25 fine-grained subcategories. This taxonomy was utilized to systematically investigate the underlying causes of tool failures, identifying challenges such as agents becoming trapped in unproductive iterative loops. However, the analysis was conducted manually on 150 out of 500 issues.

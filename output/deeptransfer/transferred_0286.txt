Regarding SWE agents, preliminary investigations have been conducted to ascertain the domains in which these agents demonstrate proficiency and those in which they exhibit deficiencies. Analysis of agent performance on multi-file issues reveals that state-of-the-art SWE agents encounter significant challenges when addressing issues involving multiple files. Recent scholarly discourse has emphasized the necessity for enhanced evaluation methodologies that encompass dimensions such as efficiency, reliability, and cognitive aptitude. Further research has delved into the failure mechanisms of these agents, identifying critical deficiencies such as inadequate fault localization, difficulty in navigating intricate code dependencies, and challenges in maintaining contextual coherence across multiple files. In response to these findings, Deshpande et al. have developed TRAIL, an error taxonomy and benchmark comprising 148 human-verified traces, aimed at facilitating a comprehensive assessment of agent performance. Similarly, Cemri et al. introduced the Multi-Agent System Failure Taxonomy (MAST) to classify failures within multi-agent systems, analyzing 150 traces derived from five distinct systems across five benchmarks, necessitating human annotations for comprehensive analysis. In a related study, Ceka et al. constructed a flow graph to represent agent actions, categorized into project understanding, context tracking, and activities related to patching and testing. However, their analysis was conducted manually and encompassed only a limited portion of the dataset, specifically addressing bug localization for 20 out of 500 issues. Additionally, Liu et al. conducted research to develop a taxonomy of failure modes for LLM tools in software engineering tasks, categorizing them into three primary phases, nine main categories, and 25 fine-grained subcategories. This taxonomy was utilized to elucidate the reasons for tool failures, identifying issues such as agents becoming ensnared in unproductive iterative loops. Nevertheless, their analysis was conducted manually on only 150 out of 500 issues.

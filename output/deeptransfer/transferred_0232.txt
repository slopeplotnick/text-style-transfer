This paper discusses how various linguistic categories can be acquired by a computational multi-agent language acquisition framework involving an adult language model, Delilah, and a daughter agent. The daughter model, designed for the MODOMA system, employs graph-based representations to encode grammatical knowledge, thereby enhancing its capacity to generate and comprehend sentences as language acquisition progresses. Conversely, Delilah incorporates a parser and generator developed by Cremers and Hijzelendoorn, specifically designed to process Dutch grammar for sentence generation and comprehension. Therefore, the MODOMA framework necessitates a novel methodological approach to the study of Delilah. In addition to enhancing Delilah’s capabilities in sentence production and parsing, it is imperative to analyze the properties of its output to facilitate the daughter’s identification of patterns essential for language acquisition. To this end, methodologies and analytical frameworks from disciplines such as linguistics, psycholinguistics, and corpus linguistics may be employed to examine the machine-generated linguistic data, paralleling the approaches traditionally applied to human language data. Given the increasing prominence of large language models, it has become imperative to assess whether machine-generated language exhibits characteristics analogous to human language. This study presents the findings of experiments conducted to investigate this phenomenon. Interestingly, the utterances produced by Delilah during interactions with the daughter agent constitute discrete exemplars, in contrast to the cohesive and sequential nature typically observed in linguistic corpora. Nonetheless, similar grammatical patterns are evident in both types of linguistic data. This may suggest that these patterns pertain more to the individual utterances than to the entire corpora. Crucially, the study presented herein demonstrates that the divergent frequency distributions of linguistic categories, which are well-documented for human language data, are also observed in Delilah's output. As these patterns are not explicitly programmed into Delilah’s configuration, they emerge as a consequence of the specified grammar model utilized in its design.

This paper discusses how functional and content categories can be acquired within a computational multi-agent language acquisition framework involving an adult language model, Delilah, and a daughter agent. The daughter agent, specifically designed for MODOMA, employs explicit graph representations of acquired grammatical knowledge to generate and parse utterances, which become more specific as language acquisition progresses. Conversely, Delilah consists of a parser and generator independently developed by Cremers and Hijzelendoorn to implement a grammar model of Dutch, utilizing this model to output and parse utterances. Therefore, the MODOMA framework requires a novel approach to researching Delilah: beyond enhancing Delilah's capabilities in generating and parsing utterances, it is imperative to study the properties of Delilah's output to enable the daughter to detect the patterns needed for acquisition. To this end, analyses and techniques from fields such as linguistics, psycholinguistics, and corpus linguistics could be applied to gain further insights into the machine-generated output, similar to how they have been applied to human-generated language data. Considering the current rise of large language models, evaluating whether machine-generated language exhibits similar patterns to human-produced language has become increasingly relevant. This paper presents the results of experiments exploring this approach. Interestingly, the collection of Delilah's utterances generated during interactions with the daughter agent contains independent exemplars, while corpora typically form a cohesive collection of sequential sentences. Nonetheless, similar grammatical patterns can be detected in both types of linguistic data. This might indicate that these patterns are more characteristic of individual utterances than of entire corpora. Crucially, the study reveals that the divergent frequency distributions of linguistic categories, well-documented for human language data, are also present in Delilah's output. As Delilah is not configured to reproduce these statistical patterns, the observed distribution is a consequence of the specified grammar model.

The appearance of a target on the left or right side of the screen prompts the participant to open and close the corresponding fist until the target disappears, followed by a period of relaxation. Similarly, when the target appears at the top or bottom of the screen, the participant is instructed to open and close both fists if the target is at the top, or both feet if the target is at the bottom, until the target vanishes, followed by relaxation. To ensure the accurate capture of relevant motor and premotor brain activities necessary for effective classification of motor imagery tasks, we focused on specific channels and optimized the feature extraction process accordingly. However, to mitigate potential confounding effects associated with evoked responses, we implemented a targeted temporal analysis strategy. This approach involved analyzing data epochs starting one second after stimulus onset, thereby excluding the immediate post-stimulus interval, which is typically dominated by evoked responses. This adjustment allowed us to concentrate on the brain's intrinsic activity. By emphasizing these stable features, which reflect the participant's sensorimotor state, our method enhanced the precision of brain signal classification. Furthermore, various combinations of data preprocessing techniques were applied prior to training classical machine learning algorithms. Initially, we employed signal processing methods such as Common Spatial Patterns (CSP), which are widely used in EEG signal classification. CSP, first introduced by Koles et al., is designed to extract and discriminate features by optimizing the variance between distinct motor imagery tasks or mental states. Specifically, it transforms EEG signals into a new vector space where the variance of one class is maximized while that of the other class is minimized. The primary objective of this method is to identify and apply spatial filters that highlight critical distinctions between classes. The CSP process begins with the computation of covariance matrices for each EEG signal class, where the covariance between two variables, X and Y, is calculated using a predefined mathematical formula.

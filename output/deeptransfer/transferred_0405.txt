A target appears on the left or right side of the screen, prompting the participant to open and close the corresponding fist until the target disappears, followed by a period of relaxation. Similarly, if the target appears at the top or bottom of the screen, the participant is instructed to open and close both fists if the target is at the top, or both feet if the target is at the bottom, until the target disappears, followed by relaxation. By focusing on specific channels and optimizing the feature extraction process, we ensured the acquisition of pertinent motor and premotor brain activity data necessary for the accurate classification of motor imagery tasks. To mitigate the influence of evoked responses on data interpretation, we implemented a targeted temporal analysis strategy. Our approach involved analyzing epochs that commenced one second post-stimulus, thereby excluding the immediate post-stimulus interval typically saturated with evoked responses. This adjustment enabled us to examine the brain's intrinsic activity. By emphasizing these stable features, which reflect the individual's sensorimotor state, our method enhances the precision of brain signal classification. Various combinations of data preprocessing techniques were explored prior to the application of classical machine learning algorithms. Initially, signal processing methods such as Common Spatial Patterns (CSP) were employed, which are extensively utilized in the classification of EEG signals. In the context of EEG, CSP was first introduced by Koles et al. This technique is employed to extract and discriminate features by optimizing the variance between distinct motor imagery tasks or mental states. It involves transforming EEG signals into a novel vector space, wherein the variance associated with one class is maximized, while that of the other class is minimized. The objective is to identify and apply spatial filters to accentuate significant differences between the classes. CSP is a sequential process that commences with the computation of covariance matrices for each EEG signal class. The covariance between two variables, X and Y, is determined using a defined mathematical formula.

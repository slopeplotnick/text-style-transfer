These findings underscore the significance of listeners' pre-existing attitudes and expectations. For instance, Hong et al. (2021) demonstrated that a more favorable disposition toward creative AI correlated with higher music ratings in a study examining individuals' expectations of AI-generated music across various genres. However, deviations from these expectations tended to result in lower ratings, although substantial differences between music genres were not observed. A significant inquiry pertains to the extent to which individuals perceive AI-composed music as resembling human-created compositions. This study aims to elucidate the specific musical attributes that influence perceptions of whether a piece is human or AI-generated. According to Shank et al. (2023), musical style is a critical factor, with electronic music frequently perceived as AI-produced. Interestingly, when participants were misled regarding the origin of the music, they perceived differences that were not present, illustrating that expectations and beliefs about the humanness of a piece influence aesthetic judgment (Ansani et al., 2024). Déguernel et al. (2022) identified that individuals employ specific strategies to evaluate music, predominantly recognizing AI-generated compositions through the detection of excessive repetition, rigid or unnatural structure, and atypical harmony or melody. Conversely, human-composed music was associated with variations in melody, ornamental elements, familiar stylistic conventions, and characteristics suggestive of performance on traditional instruments. This aligns with broader trends indicating that musical features perceived as "human" tend to receive more favorable evaluations, whereas those attributed to AI are often regarded less positively. Consistent with these findings, Hong et al. (2022) investigated the extent to which anthropomorphism and perceptions of creative autonomy influence the acceptance of AI as legitimate musical creators. Participants assessed AI-generated music across various genres, evaluating both the quality of the compositions and their own attitudes toward AI entities. The findings indicated that higher levels of anthropomorphism and perceived autonomy were correlated with increased acceptance of AI musicians. Furthermore, Sun et al. (2023) demonstrated that anthropomorphism influences perceptions of competence and warmth in AI musicians, both of which contribute to more favorable attitudes.

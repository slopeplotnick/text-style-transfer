These findings underscore the importance of listeners' pre-existing attitudes and expectations. For instance, Hong et al. (2021) demonstrated that individuals with more favorable attitudes toward creative AI tended to assign higher ratings to music, emphasizing the role of expectancies regarding AI-generated music across various styles. However, deviations from these expectancies negatively impacted ratings, although the differences between musical styles were not pronounced. A significant inquiry pertains to whether individuals perceive AI-generated music as indistinguishable from human-composed music. In this study, it is crucial to identify the specific musical elements that influence such perceptions. According to Shank et al. (2023), the genre of music plays a crucial role, with electronic music more frequently attributed to AI composition. Moreover, Ansani et al. (2024) demonstrated that when participants were misled regarding the origin of the music, they perceived differences that were non-existent, indicating that expectations and beliefs concerning the human origin of a piece influence aesthetic judgment. Similarly, Déguernel et al. (2022) identified specific strategies employed by individuals to evaluate music, noting that AI-generated compositions were often discerned through characteristics such as excessive repetition, rigid or unnatural structure, and atypical harmony or melody. Conversely, human-composed music was associated with alterations in melody, ornamentation, and a familiar stylistic approach, as well as features that appeared performable on traditional instruments. This observation aligns with a broader trend wherein musical attributes perceived as "human" receive more favorable evaluations, whereas those associated with AI are viewed less positively. Consistent with this perspective, Hong et al. (2022) investigated the influence of anthropomorphism and perceived creative autonomy on the acceptance of AI as musicians. Participants assessed AI-generated music across various genres, evaluating both the quality of the music and their attitudes toward AI entities. The findings indicated that increased anthropomorphism and perceived autonomy were correlated with a greater acceptance of AI musicians. Furthermore, research by Sun et al. (2023) demonstrated that anthropomorphism influences perceptions of competence and warmth in AI musicians, both of which contribute to more favorable attitudes.

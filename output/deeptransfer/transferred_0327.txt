Following the training phase, we developed a comprehensive and challenging evaluation benchmark designed to rigorously assess model capabilities (Section 3.5). Given the powerful capabilities of LLMs and the diversity of e-commerce data, we ensured our evaluation benchmark was comprehensive, incorporating challenging instances based on the core competencies identified in Section 2. In this section, we conducted a series of necessary preliminary explorations, including three components: feature construction, model selection, and prompt optimization, as shown in Fig 6. Guided by the theoretical framework established in Section 2, effective relevance modeling necessitates a thorough construction of the item's attribute space. Therefore, we selected features to endow the model with the necessary perception and reasoning skills across both visual and textual modalities. First, to address the need for visual attributes, such as stylistic details, cuts, and patterns, we incorporated the item's Main Product Image. This visual input is crucial for queries where textual descriptions alone are insufficient. Moreover, to enrich the item-specific attributes, we introduced category-property-value (CPV) triples and Stock-keeping-unit (SKU) identifiers. Considering that CPV information may contain redundant elements irrelevant to relevance judgment, we implemented a pipeline combining prior knowledge with LLMs for refinement. Specifically, CPV data is presented in key-value pairs, with similar products often exhibiting overlapping keys. For example, both "eye cream" and "face cream" products typically contain the key "brand." Consequently, we conducted a statistical analysis on a substantial dataset of product information to identify keys with medium-to-high frequency occurrences. Subsequently, we engaged a robust language model, Qwen3-235B-Instruct, to evaluate the contribution of each attribute to the relevance task, utilizing the task definition, the specified attributes, and corresponding keys. To enhance the accuracy of these evaluations, we conducted eight iterations of sampling and retained only those attributes for which the model consistently affirmed their relevance as "true."

Using scikit-learn, we trained Majority, Random, n-gram SVM, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree classifiers for baseline models. We extracted features using TF-IDF and count-vector representations. While baseline models were trained on CPU, all experiments involving transformer models were conducted in a GPU-enabled environment (Google Colab T4). The primary evaluation metric employed in this study is the micro-averaged F1-score, complemented by assessments of accuracy, precision, and recall to ensure a comprehensive and equitable comparison. In our study, we evaluated a wide range of models, such as m-BERT cased, XLM-RoBERTa base, BanglaBERT, DistilBERT cased, alongside the baseline models for classifying hate speech on the BLP2025 dataset. Table 2 presents the classification report for subtask 1A. Notably, BanglaBERT achieved the highest accuracy of 0.71, surpassing all other models in our evaluation. Moreover, BanglaBERT outperformed other models with a precision of 0.70 and a recall of 0.71. The official scoring metric for subtask 1A was the micro F1 score, for which BanglaBERT also achieved a value of 0.71. Meanwhile, the BERT multilingual base (m-BERT) achieved an F1 score of 0.66, and the XLM-RoBERTa base attained an F1 score of 0.67, both surpassing the baseline models. However, DistilBERT obtained an F1 score of 0.57, which is inferior to the baseline methods SVM and Logistic Regression (LR). Among the baseline models, the Support Vector Machine (SVM) demonstrated superior performance, achieving an F1 score of 0.61, whereas the random baseline exhibited the poorest performance with an F1 score of 0.16. Notably, SVM and Logistic Regression (LR) models yielded the highest F1 scores of 0.63 and 0.62, respectively, among the baseline models.

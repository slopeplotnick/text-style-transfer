In the context of sampling diverse noise vectors within flow-matching or diffusion-based frameworks, the stochastic nature of the initial noise critically impacts the resultant actions. This study addresses the issue of distribution shift through an anti-exploration lens and introduces the concept of Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-Counts (TACO). The anti-exploration principle, rooted in offline reinforcement learning, aims to prevent the policy from engaging in actions or visiting states that fall outside the dataset's support. Similarly, during VLA inference, our objective is to constrain the generated actions to remain within the support of the successful patterns present in the SFT dataset, thereby avoiding the exploration of unnecessary or irrelevant action modes retained from pretraining or suboptimal fine-tuning data. In TACO, we implement anti-exploration through test-time scaling (TTS) rather than policy modification, as flow-matching or diffusion-based VLA models entail complex sampling dynamics that are not readily compatible with conventional RL-style updates. Instead, TTS modifies the action sampling process without altering the VLA's parameters, thus obviating the need for gradient computation. We employ a classical Coin-Flipping Network (CFN) augmented with an internal representation mechanism to estimate pseudo-counts for each observation-instruction-action segment within the SFT dataset. An action segment characterized by a higher pseudo-count is indicative of greater alignment with the frequently observed successful behaviors in the SFT data, thereby rendering it preferable. During the decision-making process, TACO utilizes the pseudo-counts derived from the CFN as a verifier to select the most reliable action segment from among multiple candidates, ultimately executing the one with the highest pseudo-count. To mitigate the computational overhead associated with processing multiple tests, we introduce a shared observation key-value cache, which significantly enhances efficiency by reusing visual-language representations across samples.

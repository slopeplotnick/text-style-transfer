The primary objective of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) is to elucidate the rationale behind a model's predictions. However, the motivations for pursuing such explanations may differ, including assessing the model's performance, fostering user trust in the decision-making process, and enabling users to comprehend how a decision might be altered in cases where it is not final. The latter scenario requires explanations to be constrained to actionable elements that can be modified within the given context. This approach, known as algorithmic recourse, involves altering a model's decision by elucidating the actions necessary to achieve such a change. While it necessitates precise predictions regarding the outcomes following the implementation of proposed alterations, it primarily focuses on the consequences that manifest subsequent to these changes. The type of explanation presented in this paper is designed to be applicable in a broad range of contexts, without concentrating on a specific individual requiring the explanation, nor making assumptions about the model's efficacy or the environment in which it is deployed. Ensuring that explanations accurately reflect the model's operations, even when the model does not perform as anticipated, is imperative. This approach facilitates retrospective analysis of alternative actions without concern for their feasibility. Although this methodology could be adapted for algorithmic recourse, such adaptations are beyond the scope of this paper. Concept-based explanations, rather than relying on specific data features, employ broader constructs or characteristics that are semantically meaningful to humans. While multiple definitions of such concepts exist within the domain of XAI, the present discussion aligns with the interpretation by Poeta et al. [2025], which designates these as symbolic concepts. These symbolic concepts represent high-level, comprehensible attributes of the data.

In conclusion, we introduce a novel benchmark, HEALTHCONTRADICT, which employs interpretable quantitative metrics to evaluate language models' ability to reason over extensive and conflicting biomedical contexts. Compared to state-of-the-art medical question-answering benchmarks, HEALTHCONTRADICT more effectively differentiates model performance. In our evaluation, language models specialized in the biomedical domain demonstrate enhanced capabilities to (i) leverage accurate contextual information, (ii) disregard erroneous data, and (iii) resolve conflicting information. To the best of our knowledge, the TREC Health Misinformation tracks remain the only publicly available resource that provides (1) expert-curated health questions with ground-truth answers and supporting scientific evidence, and (2) document pools containing both corroborative and contradictory evidence, as assessed by experts. These components are essential for constructing health-related questions with definitive answers and for utilizing pairs of contradictory documents to evaluate language models' handling of biomedical knowledge conflicts. Within HEALTHCONTRADICT, we prioritize credible and scientifically substantiated documents to assess how effectively a document supports the answer to a given question. To ensure consistency across annotations, we standardized labels by mapping the 2019 categories "effective"/"ineffective" and the 2021 categories "supportive"/"dissuades" to the binary "yes"/"no" format, aligning them with the 2022 labeling scheme. Furthermore, we define a pair of documents as contradictory if they present opposing stances—one labeled "yes" and the other "no"—in response to the same question. Additionally, a document is considered correct if its stance aligns with scientific evidence and incorrect if it contradicts such evidence. The original datasets comprise 130 expert-verified questions distributed across three years: 50 questions from 2019, 35 from 2021, and 45 from 2022. Questions with yes/no stance annotations, as well as those accompanied by documents presenting both supporting and refuting viewpoints, are included. Applying this criterion reduces the set to 110 questions. Supporting and refuting documents are systematically paired into contradiction pairs, ensuring that each document is used only once, as some documents may correspond to multiple questions. Questions that fail to generate at least one contradiction pair due to insufficient unique documents are excluded from the final dataset. Consequently, the refined dataset consists of 81 questions paired with 920 sets of contradictory documents.

Despite these evaluation limitations, the study substantiated three principal findings: (1) LLM-driven coordination is a viable approach for multi-robot task allocation, (2) structured validation mechanisms significantly enhance the reliability of reasoning processes, and (3) hierarchical retry systems effectively address inconsistencies in LLM outputs. Therefore, with the feasibility of LLM-MRTA established, the authors introduced the LangGraph-based Task Allocation Agent (LTAA) framework, designed to advance LLM-MRTA methodologies. The LTAA framework was evaluated using the TEACH dataset, recognized as the standard benchmark for assessing Q-learning, DQN, and DP methodologies in MRTA research. This dataset transition from AI2-THOR household tasks to construction robotics scenarios facilitates direct performance comparison with established algorithmic baselines under consistent evaluation conditions. Moreover, LTAA emphasizes the allocation of robots to tasks, akin to conventional algorithms, and employs pre-decomposed tasks to ensure that both LLM-based and algorithm-based approaches are assessed on analogous problem types. Building upon the self-corrective principles previously delineated, the LTAA framework employs a nine-node workflow. In contrast to the comprehensive coordination managed by the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture, the LTAA framework is dedicated solely to the allocation decision process, specifically focusing on robot-task assignments. This approach utilizes pre-decomposed tasks and generates outputs akin to those of traditional algorithmic methods. Furthermore, the LTAA framework introduces three principal innovations that facilitate competitive performance with conventional optimization algorithms while ensuring transparency in reasoning: (1) phase-adaptive allocation strategies that dynamically adjust decision-making priorities as projects advance, (2) multi-stage validation incorporating hierarchical retry and self-correction mechanisms to uphold the accuracy of LLM reasoning, and (3) the integration of context-aware reasoning that structures allocation decisions based on quantitative assessments of robot capabilities. These contributions are integral to a comprehensive nine-step LangGraph workflow, which systematically processes tasks through structured reasoning phases.

Objective Evaluation. ViSAudio demonstrates superior performance across multiple objective metrics on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution datasets, as shown in Table 2. It is benchmarked against several state-of-the-art video-to-spatial-audio generation methods, with detailed descriptions provided in Section 5.1. For the BiAudio and MUSIC test sets, both video and caption inputs are utilized; however, for FAIR-Play, which lacks caption data, only video inputs are employed. Models without text-processing capabilities are evaluated using solely video inputs. The results in Table 2 indicate that ViSAudio surpasses other binaural audio generation methods in terms of overall quality, spatial channel distribution matching, audio-visual synchrony, and semantic alignment. This underscores the model's ability to generate high-fidelity binaural audio while maintaining audio-visual coherence across varying acoustic environments. Notably, its robust performance on the out-of-distribution FAIR-Play dataset, despite not being trained on it, demonstrates its capability to generalize to novel scenarios. Figure 3 provides a comparative analysis of video-conditioned binaural spatial audio generation. One illustrative example depicts an individual playing the sitar, with the camera panning from left to right, resulting in the auditory perception shifting from right to left. We examined the spectrograms of the binaural audio generated by ViSAudio, alongside the ground truth and other baseline methods. The audio produced by ViSAudio exhibits the highest fidelity to the ground truth and accurately represents spatial transitions of the sound source. Specifically, when the sound source is positioned on the left, the spectrogram of the left channel displays heightened energy levels, corresponding to the increased loudness perceived by the left ear, thereby aligning with the ground-truth spatial audio. In contrast, alternative methodologies failed to accurately capture the rhythm and did not effectively demonstrate the distinctions between the left and right audio channels.

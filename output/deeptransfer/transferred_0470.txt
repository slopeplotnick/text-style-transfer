Energy consumption is an increasingly critical concern across various domains, from mobile devices to large-scale data centers. To address inefficiencies, developers require detailed insights into the energy usage of their software. However, previous approaches have primarily focused on broader aspects, such as entire programs or specific functions, rather than analyzing energy consumption at the level of individual source code statements. Furthermore, these methods often concentrated on estimating only the CPU's energy consumption using point estimates, neglecting other hardware components and limiting their applicability for statistical reasoning and explainability. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel methodology that extends beyond CPU consumption and point estimates, focusing instead on predicting the energy usage of statically typed JVM-based programming languages, such as Java, Kotlin, or Scala. Our approach measures the energy consumption of bytecode patterns, which represent the transformation of source code statements into Java bytecode. Using these measurements, we construct a statistical model based on Bayesian statistics, enabling us to predict energy consumption through a statistical distribution and identify the factors influencing it. The model incorporates three static factors derived from the code—data size, data type, and operation—as well as one hardware-related factor: the device. To validate our methodology, we implemented it for Java, analyzing Java bytecode patterns and evaluating its predictions on previously untested programs. Our findings reveal that all four factors significantly impact energy consumption. Notably, even devices of the same model can exhibit substantial variations in energy usage, while operations and data types also play a critical role. Moreover, our experimental results demonstrate that the predicted energy consumption closely aligns with actual measurements, further substantiating the validity of our approach. This study introduces a methodology for constructing an energy model that can serve as a foundation for future research, such as energy-aware program analysis or verification tools, to assess and improve the energy efficiency of software systems.

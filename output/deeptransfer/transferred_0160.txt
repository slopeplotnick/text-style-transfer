Evaluation Metrics. Assessing the quality of generated summaries requires a comprehensive approach that accounts for lexical overlap, semantic similarity, and factual consistency. Traditional metrics, such as BLEU [Papineni et al., 2002] and ROUGE-L [Lin, 2004], evaluate quality by measuring n-gram overlap between generated summaries and reference texts. While widely used, these methods often struggle with paraphrasing, where the same meaning is expressed using different lexical or syntactic structures. To address this limitation, Zhang et al. [2019] introduced BERTScore, which leverages pre-trained contextual encoders to embed tokens and computes similarity using cosine distance. By capturing semantic relationships and accommodating paraphrastic variation, BERTScore provides a more robust measure of summary quality, particularly in contexts requiring concise abstraction. Beyond semantic similarity, it is crucial to evaluate whether a summary is factually consistent with its source text. Several metrics have been developed to address this dimension. FactCC [Kryscinski et al., 2020] employs a trained classifier to determine whether each sentence in the summary is supported by the source text. However, subsequent analyses reveal that FactCC can be prone to false negatives, occasionally misclassifying supported sentences as inconsistent. BLANC [Vasilyev et al., 2020], a reference-free metric, measures how effectively a summary aids a language model in reconstructing masked portions of the source document. Nevertheless, BLANC’s performance is influenced by the choice of the underlying language model and often emphasizes lexical overlap over true semantic equivalence. To overcome these challenges, SummaC [Laban et al., 2021] utilizes a Natural Language Inference (NLI) framework, typically employing models like RoBERTa-MNLI, to evaluate factual consistency. Operating at the sentence level, SummaC determines whether the source text substantiates each claim in the summary. Empirical studies indicate that SummaC consistently outperforms earlier metrics such as FactCC in assessing factual alignment. In this study, we adopt a multi-faceted evaluation framework incorporating ROUGE-L to capture lexical overlap, BERTScore to measure semantic similarity, and SummaC to evaluate factual consistency between generated summaries and their source material.

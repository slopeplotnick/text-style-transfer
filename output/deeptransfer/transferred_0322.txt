We utilized a multimodal language model to process images, and after generating these images, we proceeded with the triple extraction process to semantically describe their content. For this task, we selected the open-source model LLaMA 3.2 Vision, accessed via the Ollama framework. This model was chosen due to its capability to handle image inputs and convert them into clear, detailed descriptions in natural language, making it well-suited for transforming visual content into structured knowledge representations. Although our primary objective was the extraction of structured triples, this process bears resemblance to image captioning. In this respect, studies indicate that multimodal language models exhibit comparable performance in visual comprehension tasks. While all models demonstrated consistent reliability, our analysis prioritized the structural organization and comparative evaluation of the extracted information. Consequently, we focused on one representative model for this study. To ensure the extracted triples were both consistent and domain-specific, particularly within the context of fashion, we employed the Fashionpedia ontology as a guiding framework. This comprehensive taxonomy provides a structured set of categories, components, and attributes relevant to garments and accessories. By incorporating this guide into the model's instructional framework, we ensured that the extracted information adhered to a well-defined and systematic structure. Moreover, we conducted an experiment wherein the ontology was excluded to evaluate its impact on the outcomes. The findings, presented in Table 1, demonstrate that the incorporation of the ontology results in a greater number of triples per image, encompassing a wider array of relationships. In contrast, without the ontology, the model predominantly identifies a limited set of conspicuous visual elements, thereby generating less comprehensive and informative data. This underscores the critical importance of ontology guidance in achieving detailed and consistent outputs, which are indispensable for conducting graph-based comparisons of the information.

Utilizing a multimodal language model, we engaged in the analysis of images. After generating these images, we proceeded with the structured extraction of key details to semantically describe their content. We selected the open-source model LLaMA 3.2 Vision, accessed via the Ollama framework, as it was a practical solution capable of handling image inputs and producing coherent, attribute-rich descriptions in natural language. This model's proficiency in generating detailed representations made it particularly effective for transforming visual content into structured knowledge. Although our primary objective was the extraction of structured triples, this process can be regarded as analogous to image captioning. Recent studies indicate that multimodal language models exhibit comparable performance in visual comprehension tasks. All examined models demonstrated considerable reliability; however, since our focus lies in the structuring and comparison of extracted information, we elected to utilize a single representative model. To guide and constrain the triple extraction, we employed the Fashionpedia ontology, a comprehensive taxonomy detailing categories, components, and attributes pertinent to garments and accessories. By incorporating this taxonomy into the model's instructions, we ensured that the generated outputs adhered to a well-defined and consistent framework. Moreover, a comparative evaluation was conducted by omitting the ontology to assess its impact on the quality and coherence of the extracted information. The results presented in Table 1 demonstrate that the incorporation of the ontology results in a significantly greater number of triples per image, thereby encompassing a broader range of relationships. In the absence of the ontology, the model predominantly identifies a limited set of conspicuous details, resulting in a diminished capacity to generate comprehensive information. This underscores the critical importance of ontology utilization in achieving detailed and consistent outcomes, which are essential for conducting graph-based comparisons of the information.

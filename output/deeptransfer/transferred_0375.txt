Training Dynamics Analysis. In Figure 3, we present the learning dynamics of the 7B model under the CAPO and GRPO methodologies. Initially, both approaches demonstrate comparable improvements in performance; however, CAPO consistently outperforms GRPO once the model stabilizes. The gray line indicates the transition from imitation to independent problem-solving. After this point, the entropy trajectory of CAPO continues to ascend, contrasting with the stability observed in GRPO, while rewards persist in their upward trend. This suggests that CAPO not only achieves superior reward outcomes in the later stages but also maintains elevated entropy levels, which are advantageous for exploring diverse cognitive pathways. Moreover, the gradual increase in entropy indicates that CAPO avoids premature convergence by judiciously integrating negative examples. By deferring the integration of negative feedback, CAPO maintains stability during the imitation phase and subsequently enhances its ability to manage diverse tasks. Comparing Static and Dynamic Curriculum Strategies. We proceed by contrasting the conventional static instructional approach with our novel dynamic methodology. In the static approach, the difficulty of each example is assessed based on the model's performance, and training is conducted in accordance with this predetermined sequence. In contrast, our dynamic approach progressively adjusts the focus during training without necessitating manual reordering of the data. As demonstrated in Table 3, both ADARFT and the static curriculum GRPO(+SC) yield limited and inconsistent improvements over the baseline GRPO. However, our dynamic method, GRPO(+Ours), achieves substantial and consistent performance gains across the majority of tasks. This indicates that relying solely on predefined difficulty assessments is inadequate, and dynamically adjusting the focus during training yields more effective outcomes.

Training Dynamics Analysis. In Figure 3, we analyze the learning dynamics of the 7B model under CAPO and GRPO frameworks. Initially, both methodologies exhibit comparable rates of improvement; however, CAPO demonstrates superior performance once the imitation phase stabilizes. The gray vertical line delineates the transition from the imitation phase to the discrimination phase, marking the point at which the model ceases mere replication and begins autonomous reasoning. After this transition, CAPO's entropy trajectory continues to ascend steadily, in contrast to the stagnation observed in GRPO, while the reward metrics demonstrate consistent improvement. This suggests that CAPO not only achieves superior reward optimization in the later stages but also sustains elevated entropy levels, a characteristic conducive to fostering diverse and exploratory reasoning pathways. Moreover, the gradual increase in entropy further indicates that CAPO avoids premature convergence to deterministic behavior, thereby mitigating the risk of overfitting to negative examples introduced too early in the training process. By maintaining stability during the imitation phase, CAPO effectively utilizes negative feedback in subsequent stages, enhancing its capacity to manage diverse tasks. Comparing Static and Dynamic Curriculum Strategies. We now proceed to contrast the conventional static instructional approach with our novel dynamic methodology. In the static approach, the difficulty of each example is determined by evaluating the model's performance on it, and the training sequence is subsequently organized based on this assessment. In contrast, our dynamic approach progressively adjusts the focus during training without necessitating manual data reordering. As illustrated in Table 3, both ADARFT and the static curriculum GRPO(+SC) yield only marginal and inconsistent improvements over the baseline GRPO. However, our dynamic method, GRPO(+Ours), demonstrates substantial and consistent performance gains across the majority of tasks. Therefore, this indicates that relying solely on predefined difficulty heuristics is inadequate, and dynamically adjusting the focus during training yields a more effective curriculum.

Traditional MRTA approaches have achieved success across various domains through optimization algorithms, including linear programming methods, genetic algorithms, and Reinforcement Learning (RL), which offer mathematical rigor and proven performance guarantees. However, construction environments pose fundamentally distinct challenges that render these traditional methods insufficient. The inherently unstructured and complex nature of construction sites introduces significant coordination difficulties, while the sheer volume and intricacy of information in such settings surpass the reasoning capabilities of mathematical optimization techniques. Consequently, there is an urgent need for more adaptive and flexible solutions. Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated exceptional capabilities in natural language understanding, logical reasoning, and adaptive decision-making, which address several critical limitations of traditional optimization and learning-based methods. Moreover, LLM-based coordination approaches enable intuitive human-robot interaction and exhibit adaptability to the dynamic and complex requirements of construction environments. Emerging LLM frameworks have shown promise in areas such as task planning, digital twin integration, and multi-agent system deployment. Nevertheless, these frameworks currently lack systematic validation mechanisms and performance benchmarking against traditional approaches. As a result, a significant research gap persists: while LLM-based coordination shows potential, no existing studies have developed iterative retry and self-corrective mechanisms to ensure reasoning reliability, nor have they conducted systematic benchmarking to evaluate LLM performance in comparison to traditional optimization methods for construction task allocation. To address this gap, this study introduces the LangGraph based Task Allocation Agent (LTAA) framework, a novel LLM-driven coordination approach that incorporates phase-adaptive strategies and multi-stage validation mechanisms absent in existing frameworks. The proposed phase-adaptive strategy effectively achieves workload balance without compromising performance, while the multi-stage validation system ensures reasoning consistency by systematically identifying and rectifying allocation deficiencies through structured feedback loops.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has significantly improved the performance of large language models in knowledge-intensive tasks. However, most existing systems are predominantly oriented towards generic or news-based domains, lacking optimization for critical applications such as agricultural climate adaptation. Although recent studies have endeavored to integrate document retrieval and climate data for domain-specific inquiries, they continue to rely on static retrieval and generic prompting methods, which do not yield region-specific or verifiable answers. Geography-aware question-answering systems have been explored in various domains; however, these systems are not specifically designed for climate reasoning. Moreover, existing research tends to treat climate data and literature as distinct entities, lacking an integrated approach. CAIRNS is a comprehensive framework comprising four stages: (1) location-weighted retrieval of climate literature from the Elsevier database and data acquisition from a climate data API; (2) answer generation utilizing the ScholarGuide Prompt; (3) answer verification; and (4) hybrid evaluation. To identify the pertinent documents, we developed a retriever that integrates both sparse lexical matching and dense semantic retrieval. However, employing this approach alone is inadequate, as geographical context significantly influences the relevance of climate-related inquiries. Therefore, we incorporate a location similarity score to ensure that the responses are pertinent in both semantic and spatial dimensions. CAIRNS employs an iterative process wherein it retrieves evidence, formulates an initial response, and evaluates the sufficiency of the information provided. Should any informational deficiencies be identified, the system generates additional sub-questions and re-engages in the retrieval cycle to augment the data until the response meets the requisite standards of completeness. Central to the answer generation process within CAIRNS is the ScholarGuide Prompt. This prompt serves as a directive for the language model, facilitating the generation of responses that effectively integrate scientific reasoning, provide precise recommendations, and maintain a balance between readability and factual accuracy in addressing climate-related inquiries.

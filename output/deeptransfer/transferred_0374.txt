Taken together, these findings demonstrate that OMNIGUARD exhibits substantial capabilities in understanding safety across diverse data modalities. It not only generalizes safety reasoning from trained modalities to untrained ones but also acquires modality-invariant semantic representations of unsafe content. In conclusion, we introduce OMNIGUARD-7B and OMNIGUARD-3B, the first family of omni-modal guardrails designed to handle a wide range of data modalities, leveraging a comprehensive and unified safety fine-tuning dataset that includes both unimodal and cross-modal samples. OMNIGUARD represents a unified solution for addressing unsafe content across heterogeneous and cross-modal settings. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that OMNIGUARD-7B consistently outperforms existing models across all modalities, while OMNIGUARD-3B achieves competitive performance relative to large-scale LLMs and MLLMs such as Qwen3-235B and Qwen3-VL-235B. These results underscore the robust omni-modal safety detection and reasoning capabilities of our system, validating the feasibility of a unified framework that facilitates omni-modal understanding and interpretability. Future research will explore more intricate and cross-modal safety scenarios to further enhance omni-modal safeguarding in next-generation language models. 

Despite OMNIGUARD's strong omni-modal safety reasoning and consistent performance across various modalities, several limitations remain. While incorporating reasoning paths significantly enhances the interpretability and reliability of safety assessments, it inevitably introduces additional inference latency due to the reasoning generation step. Consequently, OMNIGUARD incurs higher computational overhead compared to lightweight, binary-classification guardrail systems. Striking an optimal balance between the depth of safety reasoning and inference efficiency remains a critical area of exploration, contingent upon specific use cases and operational contexts. Additionally, the limited availability of publicly accessible cross-modal safety fine-tuning datasets presents a significant challenge, leaving considerable room for progress in moderating more complex interleaved multimodal safety scenarios. We anticipate that future research will continue to improve the robustness and reliability of omni-modal systems, thereby advancing their capacity to mitigate risks across diverse and complex modality combinations.

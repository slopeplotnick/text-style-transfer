The methodology section is composed of the presentation of participants (Sect. 3.1), the design of the interviews (Sect. 3.2), our approach to conducting interviews (Sect. 3.3), and the method of analyzing responses (Sect. 3.4). We selected participants to ensure a representative sample, covering diverse roles within the project, varying levels of professional experience, and distinct educational backgrounds. Specifically, the project involves four roles: Product Owners (PO), Developers (IT), Agronomists (AGRO), and User Interface/Experience (UX) designers. These participants possess varied educational qualifications, ranging from engineering degrees to doctoral degrees. Additionally, individuals were selected based on differing levels of experience within the project. Ultimately, we selected 16 participants: nine Developers who are currently or have previously been engaged in the project, two Agronomists, three Product Owners, and two User Interface/Experience designers, all actively participating in the project. It is imperative that participants are adequately motivated to provide insightful and valuable responses. Therefore, in this study, participants were informed that they would receive feedback and that their responses would contribute to formulating an effective strategy for the implementation of the SPL process. We employed structured interviews, utilizing a predetermined set of questions. We adhered to the primary recommendations, employing clear and unambiguous language. Each question was designed to address a single concept, avoiding topics of a sensitive nature or inquiries related to past events. Furthermore, questions that participants might lack sufficient knowledge to answer were deliberately excluded. This consideration prompted us to incorporate actor-driven interviews into our methodology. The interview questions are structured into three distinct sections. The initial section involves the participant's self-introduction. The subsequent section is divided into two distinct parts: the first addresses the existing process, while the second focuses on proposed modifications. Finally, a conclusion section provides a comprehensive discussion of the interview in its entirety.

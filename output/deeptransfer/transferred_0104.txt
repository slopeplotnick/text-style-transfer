We deconstructed the relevance task and summarized the required model capabilities based on the deconstruction results, thereby providing clear and comprehensive guidance for subsequent iterations of relevance models in the industry. Furthermore, we propose a complete methodology and documented practice that spans the entire pipeline, from preliminary tasks such as model selection, to training paradigms, to capability evaluation. During LORE's development, we explored various SFT and RL training strategies, yielding key findings that may benefit LLM fine-tuning in vertical domains. First, the data scaling law reveals diminishing returns; while initial increases in data volume lead to rapid performance gains, further additions often result in plateauing improvements (Section 3.3.3). Second, incorporating more features proves advantageous. Providing stable and relevant information, even if somewhat redundant, can enhance model performance (Section 3.2.1). Third, concise prompts that encapsulate essential information are most effective. While verbose prompts rich in details do not consistently yield improvements, prompts lacking critical information can degrade model capabilities (Section 3.2.3). Fourth, naive teacher CoT distillation exhibits negative effects, as direct teacher CoT distillation underperforms standard SFT due to distributional shifts (Section 6.1). Moreover, curriculum learning remains effective; strategies structured around graded difficulty significantly outperform those employing random sequencing (Section 3.4.3). Notably, extended CoT is not necessary for better performance, as no significant increase in model output length was observed during the RL process, indicating that long CoT are merely a byproduct of improved model capabilities (Section 6.2). Finally, the entropy collapse rate imposes a cap on model performance. In the early stages of training, the model rapidly trades entropy for performance gains; however, once entropy decreases beyond a certain threshold, the model's exploration capability becomes constrained, limiting further improvement (Section 3.4.3). To address this, we implemented smart strategies to slow entropy collapse. Among the optimization methods examined—clip-higher, on-policy, and entropy loss—clip-higher was found to achieve optimal performance (Section 3.4.3).

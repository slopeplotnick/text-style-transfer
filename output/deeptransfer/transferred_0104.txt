We deconstructed the relevance task and summarized the required model capabilities based on the deconstruction results, thereby providing clear and comprehensive guidance for subsequent iterations of relevance models in the industry. Moreover, we propose a complete methodology and documented practice that spans the entire pipeline, from preliminary tasks such as model selection to training paradigms and capability evaluation. During LORE's development, we explored various training strategies, including supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL). Key findings that may benefit the fine-tuning of language models in specialized domains are outlined as follows: Firstly, data scaling law reveals diminishing returns, as model performance improves rapidly at the outset but plateaus with additional data (Section 3.3.3). Secondly, incorporating more features proves advantageous; providing stable and relevant information, even if somewhat redundant, can yield performance gains (Section 3.2.1). Furthermore, concise prompts containing essential information are most effective, whereas verbose prompts with excessive details do not consistently enhance performance, and prompts lacking critical information can degrade model capability (Section 3.2.3). Additionally, naive teacher CoT distillation results in negative effects, as direct teacher CoT distillation underperforms vanilla SFT due to distributional shifts (Section 6.1). Curriculum learning remains effective, with strategies that organize tasks by difficulty significantly outperforming random approaches (Section 3.4.3). Notably, extended CoT is not necessary for improved performance, as no significant increase in model output length was observed during the RL process, suggesting that long CoT are merely a potential byproduct of enhanced model capability (Section 6.2). Finally, the rate of entropy collapse imposes an upper limit on model performance; while the model initially trades entropy for rapid performance gains, excessive entropy reduction constrains exploration and limits further improvement (Section 3.4.3). Strategic interventions that slow entropy collapse, such as clip-higher optimization, prove effective, with clip-higher achieving optimal performance among the methods examined (Section 3.4.3).

Another line of inquiry explores enhancing system robustness by maintaining geometric consistency in representation interpretation. Empirical evidence suggests that alterations in viewpoint can significantly disrupt the embedding space of pretrained ViT encoders, thereby impairing the integration between perceptual and decision-making components. Motivated by these observations, we examine whether rectifying these representational discrepancies—without altering the overarching VLA framework or acquiring additional datasets—can effectively enhance the system's resilience to viewpoint variations. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) has gained significant attention as a practical approach for enabling models to adapt to novel tasks or domains with minimal modification to the underlying architecture. Prominent examples of PEFT methods include adapters, LoRA, Prefix-Tuning, and BitFit, which incorporate small, trainable modules into the model while maintaining the majority of the backbone parameters in a frozen state. These techniques have been extensively adopted in large language models and multimodal foundation models due to their computational efficiency and robustness. In the domain of embodied AI, PEFT is predominantly employed for the modification of language or policy components, such as the refinement of decision transformers or instruction encoders. However, when addressing viewpoint alterations, prevailing methodologies seldom concentrate on directly finetuning the visual module itself. Approaches that facilitate lightweight and targeted adaptation of the visual processing pathway within Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models remain notably limited. In contrast to the prevalent methodologies that endeavor to adapt the entire system, our research investigates the efficacy of implementing parameter-efficient modifications exclusively within the visual components, such as vision tokens or the vision encoder, to address these challenges. Conventionally, existing approaches tend to mitigate visual perturbations by extensively retraining substantial segments of the system or the entire visuomotor policy. However, we contend that such global adaptation is unwarranted. The primary challenge posed by visual perturbations lies in their tendency to induce systematic and predictable shifts within the visual embedding space, thereby disrupting the processing and alignment of visual data.

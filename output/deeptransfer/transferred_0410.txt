Cross-model and cross-algorithm generalization is a key feature of our system. Consistent improvements on both Qwen2.5 and Llama backbones indicate that our framework is architecture-agnostic. Notably, even the smaller Llama-3.2-1B model shows significant accuracy gains from OptPO-SFT and OptPO over conventional techniques, highlighting that the proposed adaptation mechanism scales robustly across model sizes. Moreover, our OptPO framework integrates seamlessly with different types of reinforcement learning algorithms, such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, and consistently outperforms TTRL with higher accuracy and substantial efficiency gains. This underscores its effectiveness across various RL methodologies. When comparing SFT-based and RL-based variants, the RL-based OptPO models generally yield stronger overall accuracy, particularly on mathematical reasoning benchmarks. This suggests that policy-gradient adaptation better leverages stochastic feedback from rollouts compared to the deterministic pseudo-label fine-tuning of the SFT approach. Nevertheless, OptPO-SFT achieves more stable improvements over TTSFT across all settings, validating that our adaptive pseudo-labeling significantly enhances SFT-style test-time training. Our results demonstrate several key takeaways: (1) adaptive early-stopping through BSPRT effectively improves efficiency without sacrificing accuracy; (2) OptPO offers the strongest balance between accuracy and computational resource utilization; and (3) OptPO-SFT provides a lightweight alternative for rapid adaptation when RL-style updates are infeasible. Together, these methodologies establish a unified and efficient test-time training paradigm for verifiable reasoning tasks. This paper introduces OptPO as the first statistically grounded framework for efficient test-time policy optimization, effectively addressing the inefficiencies of majority-vote-based rollouts in test-time reinforcement learning for large language models. By formulating early-stopping consensus estimation as a Bayesian sequential probability ratio test, OptPO adaptively allocates rollouts based on posterior evidence rather than fixed budgets, ensuring reliable pseudo-labels while avoiding redundant sampling. The resulting framework is simple, plug-and-play, and compatible with standard policy optimization methods such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, as well as supervised test-time fine-tuning. Across diverse benchmark and backbone settings, OptPO consistently preserves or improves accuracy while achieving substantial compute savings. More broadly, this work underscores that efficient, label-free adaptation is both feasible and increasingly essential as large language models are deployed in unpredictable real-world environments. We hope OptPO serves as a foundation for future research on principled, compute-efficient test-time learning.

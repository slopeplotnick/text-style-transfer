Cross-model and cross-algorithm generalization. Our system demonstrates efficacy across diverse models and algorithms. Consistent enhancements observed with both Qwen2.5 and Llama architectures underscore the architecture-agnostic nature of our approach. Notably, even the smaller Llama-3.2-1B model exhibits significant accuracy improvements when employing our OptPO-SFT and OptPO methodologies compared to conventional techniques, thereby affirming the adaptability of our method across varying model scales. Moreover, the OptPO framework demonstrates robust compatibility across various reinforcement learning algorithms, including PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, consistently surpassing TTRL in both accuracy and efficiency, thereby affirming its efficacy across diverse RL methodologies. SFT-based vs. RL-based variants. In the comparative analysis of SFT-based and RL-based variants, the RL-based OptPO versions exhibit superior accuracy, particularly in mathematical reasoning assessments, attributable to their enhanced utilization of stochastic feedback from rollouts relative to the deterministic pseudo-label fine-tuning approach. Nonetheless, OptPO-SFT achieves stable enhancements over TTSFT across all scenarios, substantiating the effectiveness of our adaptive pseudo-labeling in augmenting SFT-style test-time training. Takeaways. This study elucidates three pivotal contributions: First, the integration of adaptive early-stopping via Bayesian Sequential Probability Ratio Test (BSPRT) enhances computational efficiency while maintaining accuracy. Second, OptPO demonstrates a robust equilibrium between precision and computational resource utilization. Third, OptPO-SFT emerges as an expedient alternative for adaptation in scenarios where reinforcement learning-style updates are impracticable. Collectively, these innovations furnish a proficient methodology for model training during test-time reasoning tasks. This paper introduces OptPO, a pioneering framework that adeptly optimizes policies during testing, thereby addressing the inefficiencies inherent in majority-vote-based rollouts within test-time reinforcement learning for large language models. By employing a Bayesian sequential probability ratio test for early-stopping, OptPO dynamically adjusts rollout allocations based on posterior evidence rather than predetermined limits, thereby ensuring the generation of reliable pseudo-labels while mitigating redundant sampling. The proposed framework is inherently user-friendly, seamlessly integrates with established policy optimization techniques such as PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++, and supports supervised fine-tuning during test-time adaptation. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that OptPO consistently maintains or enhances predictive accuracy across diverse experimental settings and model architectures, all while achieving significant computational efficiency. More broadly, this work underscores that efficient, label-free adaptation is both feasible and crucial as large language models are increasingly deployed in unpredictable real-world scenarios. We anticipate that OptPO will serve as a foundational framework for subsequent research on compute-efficient test-time learning.

We evaluate citation quality using standard metrics: Citation Recall, Citation Precision, and their harmonic mean (F1). Citation Recall measures the proportion of ground-truth references from the human-written survey that are correctly included in the generated survey, reflecting the breadth of literature coverage. Citation Precision, in contrast, assesses whether the references cited in the generated survey are present in the ground-truth reference set, thereby indicating citation accuracy and reliability. Together, these metrics provide a clear and interpretable measure of how closely an automatic system’s reference list aligns with human research practices. This systematic approach enables a direct comparison of the comprehensiveness and accuracy of literature exploration between automated systems and human authors. Recently, the "LLM-as-a-Judge" framework has gained prominence for evaluating machine-generated text due to its scalability, consistency, and cost-effectiveness. In its standard form, this paradigm involves providing a model with rubric-based guidelines and scoring criteria to evaluate outputs. However, this rubric-only approach often proves insufficient for complex tasks such as comprehensive survey generation, as it tends to exhibit leniency and score saturation. To address these limitations, we employ two complementary variants of LLM-as-a-Judge: one utilizing a human-written survey for overall content evaluation and another based on principles for assessing outline structure. For overall survey quality, we adopt an enhanced configuration where the model is presented with a human-written survey on the same topic alongside the generated survey. The human-written survey serves as a high-quality anchor, enabling the model to make relative judgments rather than relying solely on abstract rubric descriptions. This reference-guided setup overcomes several challenges associated with rubric-only evaluations. First, the inclusion of an expert example provides strong contextual grounding, reducing the risk of overvaluing vague or superficial content. Second, comparing the generated survey to a detailed human-authored counterpart enhances the model’s ability to evaluate higher-level qualities such as argumentative rigor, evidential support, and conceptual depth—areas often overlooked in rubric-based assessments. Finally, anchoring the evaluation to a gold-standard reference mitigates score inflation, ensuring clearer differentiation among systems and enabling a more nuanced diagnosis of strengths and weaknesses in automated survey generation.

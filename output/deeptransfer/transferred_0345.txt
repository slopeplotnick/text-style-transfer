FinMem[22] is a financial-domain memory system that employs a layered memory structure, encompassing short, mid, and long-term memory, to guide trading decisions. Similarly, MemP[5] serves as a general framework that abstracts trajectories into both step-by-step instructions and high-level strategies, thereby preserving and utilizing procedural memory. MemGPT, inspired by operating systems, manages hierarchical storage and handles interruptions to dynamically expand the effective context window of large language models. In contrast, Zep adopts temporal knowledge graphs to integrate historical relationships with evolving contextual information, enabling joint retrieval of past and present data. 

To evaluate these systems, we utilized Accuracy (ACC) and the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) as performance metrics. ACC measures the overall correctness of predictions, while MCC provides a robust assessment for balanced datasets, making it particularly suitable for stock prediction tasks where class distributions are often imbalanced. Our evaluations, conducted on four representative stocks, demonstrated that our framework consistently and significantly outperformed all baseline models in both ACC and MCC, as shown in Table 1. These results validate the effectiveness of our approach while also highlighting critical limitations in the baseline systems. 

Specifically, FinMem's isolated news storage lacks temporal event modeling, which prevents it from adapting to evolving market contexts and leads to the omission of critical incremental information. Likewise, MemP's trajectory simplification excessively abstracts procedural details, discarding essential financial information necessary for scenario reasoning. MemGPT, while effective in general-purpose memory management, fails to incorporate event-aware retrieval, often overlooking financially significant patterns during memory updates. Similarly, Zep's graph-based approach struggles to capture inter-event dependencies, thereby missing decisive correlations within financial data. 

Our proposed framework addresses these deficiencies by introducing a novel paradigm that transforms unstructured news into structured temporal event knowledge. This approach systematically captures event logic and dependencies, enabling traceability and comprehensive reasoning. By mirroring human analytical processes, our framework enhances the accuracy and reliability of financial predictions, establishing a more robust and trustworthy system for stock market analysis.

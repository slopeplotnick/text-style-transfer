In conclusion, we present OMNIGUARD, a suite of models designed to perform safety evaluations across multiple modalities, including text, images, videos, and audio, while incorporating advanced reasoning capabilities. Furthermore, we propose a novel training paradigm that enhances the model's ability to articulate its safety judgments. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that OMNIGUARD achieves high accuracy, exhibits robust generalization across modalities, and provides more interpretable safety rationales compared to prior approaches. The advent of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has catalyzed significant interest in the development of omni-modal language models (OLLMs), which possess the capability to process and integrate inputs from diverse modalities simultaneously. In contrast to earlier approaches that relied on the assembly of independently pretrained unimodal components, OLLMs are trained end-to-end on multimodal datasets, thereby enabling them to natively perform unified understanding and generation across text, audio, image, and video modalities. These models operate by mapping heterogeneous inputs into a shared latent representation, facilitating cross-modal reasoning and enabling seamless interaction across different data types. Models such as Qwen2.5-Omni and LLaMA-Omni demonstrate the capability to generate both textual and auditory outputs in real-time. NExT-OMNI extends these functionalities by facilitating the comprehension and generation of diverse data types. Nevertheless, these models are confronted with safety challenges attributed to parameter inconsistencies, which may precipitate hazardous scenarios.

To ensure fidelity in video editing, maintaining precise temporal alignment with the source material is imperative. This requires that all micro-expressions, such as blinks, gaze shifts, and head movements, occur synchronously with the original video. Temporal consistency is particularly critical in talking-head scenarios, where even minor discrepancies can compromise the perceived authenticity of the output. Typically, users desire minor modifications, such as the addition of a mustache or the alteration of a facial expression. The objective is to implement these specific changes while keeping the remainder of the content unchanged. However, achieving this level of precision and synchronization remains a challenge for most contemporary video editing tools. Therefore, we introduce a novel methodology termed Sync-LoRA, designed to produce edited videos that maintain synchronization with the original sequences. This approach involves modifying solely the initial frame of the video, guided by a text prompt. Our model is trained using pairs of videos, comprising an original and its corresponding edited version. These pairs are meticulously constructed to ensure frame-level synchronization, wherein each frame exhibits identical motion trajectories while differing only in the edited components. The process of generating these pairs involves an initial creation followed by rigorous synchronization verification. Moreover, we employ a model that integrates visual and textual understanding to generate diverse transformation concepts, subsequently producing corresponding images synthesized into videos. However, these videos frequently exhibit imperfect alignment; thus, we employ human motion data, such as facial expressions and body movements, to evaluate their congruence. We assess their alignment in terms of speech, ocular motion, blinking, and body posture, retaining only the most accurately matched instances for training. By utilizing these well-aligned examples, our model learns to preserve the original video's motion dynamics while implementing only the desired modifications, thereby maintaining synchronization with the source video.

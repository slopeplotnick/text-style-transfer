During the execution of benchmark tests, the system may generate noise due to background processes and other confounding factors. Therefore, to mitigate some of these disturbances, we disabled the wireless networking hardware and collected multiple samples at various time points to filter out external noise. The construction of our statistical model is crucial to the research, although it may not be entirely flawless. We formulated several assumptions based on prior research and the presumed distribution of the data, which serve to mitigate potential subjective biases within the model. However, as with any modeling approach, the selection of model parameters, their associated distributions, and the rationale underlying these choices remain open to debate. Our selection of parameters is grounded in the specifications of the Java Virtual Machine (JVM) and the formal definitions of programming languages, with a particular emphasis on the parametrized aspects of Java Bytecode. The employment of the Gaussian distribution is justified by its frequent occurrence in natural phenomena and its alignment with the principles of the central limit theorem, which posits that the sum of a large number of independent, random variables tends to form a normal distribution. Moreover, our understanding of the measurements is limited to their mean and variance, thus the Gaussian distribution effectively encapsulates our epistemic uncertainty. Additional concerns primarily pertain to the execution platform and the Java Virtual Machine (JVM). Specifically, the memory state preceding the execution of each bytecode pattern has the potential to influence the results; therefore, we initiate the garbage collector prior to each measurement cycle. To mitigate biases arising from paging, caching, and concurrent processes, each bytecode pattern is measured ten times, employing a split-plot approach with randomized execution order across sequences.

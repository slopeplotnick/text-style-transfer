The execution platform may produce noise during the benchmark harness's execution due to background processes and other confounding factors. To mitigate some of these confounds, we turned off the platform's wireless networking hardware and took multiple samples at varying time points to filter out external noise. The definition of the statistical model, which is the foundation of our research, may have imperfections. We make several assumptions supported by previous research and the presumed distribution of the data, which serve to mitigate subjective biases in the model. However, as with any modeling approach, the choice of model parameters, their distribution, and the parameters themselves are debatable. Our justifications for the model parameters stem from the JVM specification and the formal definitions of programming languages, particularly focusing on aspects parametrized within Java Bytecode. The use of the Gaussian distribution is justified both ontologically and epistemologically. Ontologically, the Gaussian distribution frequently appears in modeling natural phenomena, aligning with the central limit theorem, which posits that processes summing fluctuations converge to a standard normal distribution. Epistemologically, given our limited knowledge about the measurements, characterized only by their finite mean and variance, this distribution effectively represents our uncertainty. Other concerns primarily pertain to the execution platform and the JVM. The memory state before each bytecode pattern execution could alter the results; therefore, we execute the garbage collector before each measurement cycle. To mitigate bias due to paging, caching, and disruptions from concurrent processes, we measure each bytecode pattern ten times, using a split-plot approach and randomizing execution order across sequences.

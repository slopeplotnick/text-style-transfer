To validate the proposed tribrid architecture design, we conducted experiments comparing it against alternative hybrid and monomodal architectures. Our architecture is represented as "CCDGDG" across stages 1 to 6, where C, D, and G denote Conv, DDSM+FNN, and Gated-Conv blocks, respectively. Additionally, we constructed hybrid architectures combining two block types (e.g., CCGGGG and CCDDD) and monomodal architectures consisting exclusively of a single block type. As shown in Table 7, the experimental results demonstrate that the tribrid architecture significantly outperforms both hybrid and monomodal variants, emphasizing the effectiveness of integrating complementary block structures. Notably, in egocentric scenarios, our approach accurately estimates joint positions even when they are occluded or not directly visible, showcasing enhanced robustness in real-world applications. DF-Mamba introduces a novel backbone architecture for 3D hand pose estimation, combining efficient convolutional feature extraction in the lower layers with a deformable state-space representation in the higher layers. Extensive evaluations across five datasets covering single-hand, two-hand, and hand-object interactions, as well as both RGB and depth modalities, demonstrate that DF-Mamba consistently outperforms existing backbone architectures while achieving faster inference. Promising directions for future research include extending DF-Mamba to broader tasks such as 3D human pose estimation, pre-training it on large external datasets, applying it to advanced hand-mesh reconstruction decoders (e.g., [11, 75]), and developing it as a foundational model capable of generalizing across diverse scenarios without task-specific fine-tuning.

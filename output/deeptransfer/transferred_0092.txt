Our architecture is represented as "CCDGDG" across stages 1 to 6, where C, D, and G denote Conv, DDSM+FNN, and Gated-Conv blocks, respectively. Additionally, we evaluated hybrid architectures combining two block types, such as "CCGGGG" and "CCDDD," and monomodal architectures consisting solely of a single block type. The results in Table 7 demonstrate that our tribrid architecture significantly outperforms both hybrid and monomodal variants, highlighting the effectiveness of integrating complementary block structures. Qualitative examples. Figure 5 illustrates qualitative examples from InterHand2.6M and AssemblyHands, where DF-Mamba shows superior accuracy in joint position estimation, as evidenced by reduced positional errors highlighted with colored ellipses. In egocentric scenarios, our approach accurately estimates joint positions, even when they are occluded or not directly visible, demonstrating enhanced robustness in real-world settings. We propose DF-Mamba, a novel backbone architecture for 3D hand pose estimation that combines efficient convolutional feature extraction in the initial stages with a deformable state-space representation in the subsequent layers. Through comprehensive evaluations on five datasets covering single-hand, two-hand, and hand-object interactions, as well as both RGB and depth modalities, DF-Mamba consistently surpasses existing architectures in performance and inference speed. Promising future studies include extending DF-Mamba to broader tasks like 3D human pose estimation, pre-training it using extensive external data, applying it to recent hand-mesh reconstruction decoders, and developing it into a foundational model capable of generalizing across various scenarios without data-specific fine-tuning.

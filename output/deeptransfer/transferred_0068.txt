Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have made substantial progress in recent years; however, they continue to face challenges in accurately recognizing and interpreting fine-grained details within high-resolution images. To enhance the capability of MLLMs in high-resolution image understanding, existing studies generally follow two main directions. Training-based approaches rely on supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, but such methods often compromise the model's generalization ability on broad vision-language tasks. In contrast, training-free approaches typically perform hierarchical or tree-based searches to localize target regions. However, these methods tend to suffer from low efficiency and may fail to retain all target objects during the search process, particularly in multi-object scenarios. In this section, we analyze the relationship between the resolution of image crops and the performance of MLLMs. The experimental results indicate that using different resolutions has a significant impact on MLLMs' ability to analyze high-resolution images. Objects of different sizes are suitable for different resolutions. Inspired by these insights, we propose the MRD framework. The evaluation was conducted using two model variants, LLaVA-ov and LLaVA-v1.5, on a standardized benchmark. As shown in Figure 3, the findings indicate that when the resolution of image crops is set to 112, both models achieve optimal performance across single-object and multi-object tasks. Therefore, setting the resolution to 112 might be the optimal choice for such tasks.

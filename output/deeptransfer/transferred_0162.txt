Audio and visual inputs are utilized to determine the emotion-dominant modality in each audio-visual sample. After constructing the high-quality human-annotated dataset and an additional dataset filtered through facial action units (FAU), these datasets are merged to create a unified training set. Using this combined data, the Modality Importance (MI) is estimated for every sample. Once the emotion-dominant modality is identified, the structure of the reasoning text is reorganized accordingly. Specifically, for each training instance, the reasoning text is divided into two modality-specific components: an audio-based reasoning text and a video-based reasoning text. To explicitly mark these sections, two specialized tokens, <aud_desc> and <vis_desc>, are used at both the beginning and end of each respective text. The texts are then reordered based on the MI. If the audio modality is more emotionally dominant, the audio-based reasoning text is placed first, followed by the video-based text. Conversely, if the video modality is more dominant, the video-based text precedes the audio-based text. For cases where audio and video are equally informative, both orderings are included: one starting with audio and the other with video. This reorganization allows the model to initiate its reasoning process with the modality that most effectively conveys emotional content. Following this initial stage, the model’s reasoning capabilities are further refined through Modality-Aligned Reward Optimization. Previous methods based on GRPO have employed two types of rewards: an answer reward and a format reward. However, these rewards focus solely on generating correct and well-structured reasoning and do not explicitly guide the model to prioritize the emotion-dominant modality. To address this limitation, we introduce two novel rewards: the modality-aligned order reward, which ensures the correct sequencing, and the modality-grounded reasoning reward, which enhances reasoning quality based on the emotion-dominant input.

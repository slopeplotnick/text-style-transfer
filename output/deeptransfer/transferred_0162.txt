Audio and visual inputs are utilized to determine which modality predominantly conveys emotions in each sample. After constructing both a high-quality human-annotated dataset and an additional dataset filtered through FAU criteria, we merge them to form a comprehensive training set. Using this combined data, we then conduct Modality Importance (MI) estimation for each sample. Once the emotion-dominant modality is identified, we reorganize the reasoning text structure. Specifically, for each training instance, the reasoning is divided into two distinct segments: an audio-based reasoning segment and a video-based reasoning segment. To clearly delineate these segments, we employ specific markers, <aud_desc> and <vis_desc>, placing them at both the beginning and the end of each segment. Subsequently, we organize these texts according to the MI. If the audio modality is more emotionally prominent, the audio-based text is positioned first, followed by the video-based text. Conversely, if the video modality is more dominant, the video-based text precedes the audio-based text. In cases where audio and video are equally informative, we generate two versions of the input sequence: one starting with audio and the other with video. This approach allows the model to initiate its reasoning process with the modality that most effectively conveys emotional signals. Following the initial stage, we further refine the model's reasoning capabilities through Modality-Aligned Reward Optimization. Previous methodologies typically employed two categories of rewards: one for correct responses and another for structural coherence. However, these approaches do not emphasize the input modality that most effectively conveys emotional content. Therefore, we introduce two novel rewards: the modality-aligned order reward and the modality-grounded reasoning reward, designed to enhance the prioritization of emotion-relevant inputs and facilitate emotion-grounded reasoning.

Given the nature of the task at hand, where categories L1 and L2 are deemed non-essential and L3 and L4 are considered significant, we introduce the binary classification accuracy metric (acc@2) to evaluate performance in terms of correct and incorrect classifications. Additionally, to address the issue of imbalance in data label distribution, we utilize the macro F1 score, calculated as the average of F1 scores across all classes, to ensure equitable evaluation across categories. For comparison, we evaluate two categories of models: (1) Top-tier large language models, both open-source and proprietary. These models are assessed using zero-shot direct prompting with a unified prompt template that includes the complete task definition and operational guidelines. The inference configuration adheres to the best practices reported in each model's official documentation, employing single-pass inference with temperature parameters set to the recommended optimal values (e.g., 0.7 for GPT-4, 0.6 for Llama-3), ensuring each model is compared under its optimal operating conditions. (2) The Vanilla SFT Model, which is trained using only labeled data without incorporating additional methodologies such as Chain of Thought (CoT) or Reinforcement Learning (RL), serves to validate the joint contribution of CoT modeling and RL optimization in our method. Notably, this fundamental model achieves an Acc@2 score of 0.929 and a Macro-F1 score of 0.722, demonstrating performance superior to advanced models such as GPT-4. However, the LORE model further enhances performance, improving Acc@2 by 0.4 percentage points. This suggests that while the vanilla-SFT model exhibits robust performance on in-distribution routine data, LORE achieves incremental performance improvements beyond this high-performing baseline.

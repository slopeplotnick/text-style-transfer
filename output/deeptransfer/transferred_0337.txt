Given the nature of the relevance task, where categories L1 and L2 are deemed irrelevant and L3 and L4 are considered relevant, we introduce the binary classification accuracy metric (acc@2) to evaluate performance. Additionally, to mitigate the impact of imbalanced data label distribution, we employ the macro F1 score, calculated as the average of F1 scores across all classes. As comparison baselines, we select two categories of models: (1) Top-tier large language models, both open-source and closed-source. These models are evaluated using zero-shot direct prompting with a unified prompt template that includes the complete relevance task definition and discrimination rules. The inference configuration adheres to the best practices reported in the official documentation of each model, employing single-pass inference with temperature parameters set to the officially recommended optimal values for each model (e.g., 0.7 for GPT-4, 0.6 for Llama-3), ensuring comparison under optimal operating conditions. (2) The Vanilla SFT Model, trained using only labels for SFT without incorporating CoT modeling or the RL phase, serves to validate the joint contribution of CoT modeling and reinforcement learning optimization in our method. Notably, multi-dimensional CoT modeling yields marginal improvements on high-performance baselines. On the General subset, the vanilla-SFT model achieves an Acc@2 of 0.929 and a Macro-F1 of 0.722, demonstrating performance significantly superior to advanced models such as GPT-4. Moreover, compared to vanilla-SFT, the LORE model further improves Acc@2 by 0.4 percentage points. This indicates that although vanilla-SFT has already achieved sufficient fitting for in-distribution routine data, LORE can still obtain marginal performance gains on top of this high-performance baseline.

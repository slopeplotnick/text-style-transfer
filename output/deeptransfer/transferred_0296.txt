The expansion of the French-Ciluba sentiment lexicon will extend to include translations into English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, Sesotho, and Sepedi. This will be achieved using cross-lingual translation tools and corpus-based mapping to align words based on their contextual usage. Moreover, Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) will be applied to identify sentiment-bearing words within African language corpora, thereby facilitating their integration with existing lexicon entries. To address ambiguities in word meanings and ensure accurate translations, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system, integrated with large language models, will be implemented. Subsequently, the AfriBERTa and AfroXLMR models will be trained for sentiment classification using the enhanced lexicon, with their efficacy evaluated through metrics such as precision, recall, F1-score, and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analysis. Furthermore, to enhance the interpretability of these models, visualization tools will be employed to elucidate the specific textual components prioritized during decision-making processes. In addition, we will evaluate the performance of individual models in comparison to ensemble approaches to ascertain potential improvements in accuracy. The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: initially, we conduct a review of existing literature on sentiment analysis, multilingual lexicon development, and African language processing. This study delineates the proposed framework and methodologies, detailing the experimental setup and model evaluation processes, followed by an analysis of the results and their implications for multilingual natural language processing. Historically, sentiment analysis has predominantly employed supervised machine learning techniques and lexicon-based approaches to determine the polarity of textual data. Foundational research established the efficacy of statistical classifiers in sentiment classification tasks, while subsequent investigations underscored the utility of sentiment lexicons in facilitating interpretable, rule-based methodologies. Prominent sentiment lexicons such as SentiWordNet, the NRC Emotion Lexicon, and VADER are well-suited for high-resource languages; however, they offer limited applicability for morphologically complex and low-resource languages. Therefore, efforts have been made to develop multilingual sentiment lexicons by extracting terms from extensive text corpora.

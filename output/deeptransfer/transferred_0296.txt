The French-Ciluba sentiment lexicon is being expanded to include translations in English, Afrikaans, isiZulu, Sesotho, and Sepedi through the use of cross-lingual translation tools and corpus-based mapping techniques. Moreover, Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) will be applied to extract sentiment-bearing words from African language corpora and align them with existing lexicon entries, thereby enriching the resource. To address ambiguities in sentiment labels and refine multilingual word alignments, the Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework will be implemented alongside large language models. Subsequently, AfriBERTa and AfroXLMR models will be fine-tuned for sentiment classification tasks using the enriched multilingual lexicon, with their performance evaluated through metrics such as precision, recall, F1-score, and the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve. Furthermore, explainable AI techniques, including attention visualization and feature attribution, will be employed to interpret model predictions and highlight linguistic focus during sentiment classification. To assess the effectiveness of ensemble methods, individual model performance will be compared against ensemble approaches to determine potential improvements in multilingual sentiment classification accuracy. The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: Section II reviews existing literature on sentiment analysis, multilingual lexicon development, and African language modeling. Section III outlines the TriLex framework and methodological pipeline. Section IV describes the experimental setup and model evaluation protocols. Section V presents the results and discusses their implications for multilingual natural language processing. Finally, Section VI concludes the study. Historically, sentiment analysis has relied on supervised machine learning algorithms and lexicon-based approaches to determine textual polarity. Foundational work by Pang and Lee demonstrated the efficacy of statistical methods in sentiment classification, while subsequent studies emphasized the interpretability of sentiment lexicons for rule-based analysis. Prominent resources such as SentiWordNet, the NRC Emotion Lexicon, and VADER have proven effective for high-resource languages; however, they lack adequate coverage for morphologically complex or low-resource languages. Efforts to construct multilingual lexicons have leveraged corpus-based extraction methods to address these gaps.

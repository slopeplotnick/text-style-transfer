Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly employed in applications such as Google's AI Overviews, where they synthesize information from multiple extensive documents. However, it remains uncertain whether these models assign equal importance to all inputs. In the context of abortion-related news, researchers have constructed 40 triplets of articles—comprising one pro, one neutral, and one con article. These triplets were permuted into six different input sequences, and Gemini 2.5 Flash was tasked with generating a neutral summary. The summaries were evaluated against the original articles utilizing metrics designed to assess lexical overlap (ROUGE-L), semantic similarity (BERTScore), and factual accuracy (SummaC). Analysis revealed a significant primacy effect, with BERTScore indicating that the summaries exhibited greater semantic alignment with the first article in the sequence. Furthermore, pairwise comparisons demonstrated that the first article exerted a stronger influence on the generated summaries compared to the second and third articles, which did not significantly differ from one another. This observation underscores the disproportionate influence of the initial document on the generated summary, which poses challenges for systems reliant on these outputs or for agentic AI frameworks that utilize such summaries to inform decision-making processes. As generative AI continues to advance rapidly and finds applications across diverse domains, these autonomous systems, designed to execute complex tasks with minimal human oversight, necessitate careful consideration of structural biases to ensure equitable and reliable performance. These systems decompose overarching objectives into smaller, manageable tasks to facilitate their execution. In achieving this, they engage in strategic planning and consider multiple procedural steps, incorporating external information as necessary to inform their actions. Consequently, the efficacy of these systems is significantly contingent upon the precision with which the LLMs they utilize can identify, organize, and synthesize information from both directives and external sources.

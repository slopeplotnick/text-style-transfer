To enhance interpretability and enable reasoning-based safety alignment, we augment each sample in our dataset with critiques generated by advanced models. Specifically, we utilize gpt-oss-120b for textual data, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct for visual data, including images and videos, and Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct for auditory data. For each instance, the model is provided with the original content, its corresponding safety label, and any violated categories (if applicable) and is instructed to generate a reasoning critique that explains the rationale behind the safety assessment. Unlike basic classification-based guardrail models that provide only a binary safety determination, OMNIGUARD employs a deliberate, reasoning-driven approach to deliver fine-grained and interpretable safety evaluations. Specifically, it generates a structured output comprising three components: (1) Safety judgment: the overall assessment of whether the input is safe or unsafe. (2) Violation categories: the specific safety issues that the content violates, if deemed unsafe. (3) Reasoning critique: a natural language explanation that elucidates the rationale behind the model's decision in alignment with established guidelines. This approach enables OMNIGUARD to transcend shallow pattern recognition, supporting a more comprehensive and explainable analysis of potentially unsafe content across diverse modalities. In this section, we present a detailed analysis of OMNIGUARD's experimental results. We evaluate its performance in both unimodal and cross-modal configurations across 15 benchmarks spanning text, image, video, and audio modalities. Furthermore, we design experiments to address two key research questions: (1) Does the integration of reasoning-based safety alignment improve the omni-modal guardrail model's ability to perform safety moderation and address safety-critical challenges across various modalities? (2) Can safety knowledge acquired from familiar modalities transfer to unfamiliar ones, thereby demonstrating cross-modal generalization in safety understanding and moderation?

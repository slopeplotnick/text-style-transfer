To enhance interpretability and enable reasoning-based safety alignment, we augment each sample in our dataset with critiques generated by high-capacity models. Specifically, we employ gpt-oss-120b (Agarwal et al., 2025) for textual data, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct (Team, 2025) for visual data, including images, videos, and text-image pairs, and Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct (Team, 2024a) for auditory data. For each instance, the teacher model is provided with the original content, its corresponding safety label, and any violated categories, if applicable, and is instructed to generate a reasoning critique that explains the rationale behind the safety assessment. The complete prompting template used for critique generation is illustrated in Figure 5. Unlike simplistic classification-only guardrail models that provide only a binary safety label, OMNIGUARD employs deliberate reasoning to deliver fine-grained and interpretable safety moderation. Specifically, it generates a structured output comprising three components: (1) Safety judgment, which provides the overall safety assessment of the input, determining whether it is safe or unsafe. (2) Violation categories, which specify the unsafe categories that the input violates, if deemed unsafe. (3) Reasoning critique, which offers a natural language explanation articulating the rationale behind the model's decision in alignment with policy guidelines. This approach enables OMNIGUARD to transcend shallow pattern recognition, facilitating explainable analysis of potentially unsafe content across diverse modalities. In the following section, we present a comprehensive evaluation of OMNIGUARD's performance. We assess its efficacy in both unimodal and cross-modal settings across 15 guardrail and jailbreak benchmarks spanning text, image, video, and audio modalities. Furthermore, we design experiments to address two key research questions: (1) To what extent does reasoning-based safety alignment enhance the omni-modal guardrail model's ability to perform safety moderation and address safety-critical challenges across diverse modalities? (2) Can safety knowledge acquired from seen modalities transfer to unseen ones, thereby demonstrating cross-modal generalization in safety understanding and moderation capabilities?

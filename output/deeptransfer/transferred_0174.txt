Enhancing the high-resolution image perception capability of machines has emerged as a significant research focus. A widely adopted approach to improve detail perception is the "locate-and-zoom-in" strategy. While training-based methods such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) have demonstrated effectiveness in identifying relevant regions, they face critical limitations, including high computational costs, prolonged training durations, and limited cross-architecture adaptability, which restrict their scalability and practical application. In contrast, training-free approaches, which leverage attention mechanisms or tree-based search strategies to autonomously locate salient regions, offer an alternative without requiring dataset construction or model fine-tuning. However, despite employing a top-down search strategy from high to low resolution, these methods often struggle with accurately perceiving small objects during the initial search phase, leading to erroneous search paths. Recently, inspired by the success of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in enabling long-context understanding for general LLMs, a novel framework called Retrieval-Augmented Perception (RAP) has been introduced to enhance the perception of high-resolution images without the need for training. RAP extends the RAG paradigm to the visual domain, achieving notable performance improvements on high-resolution benchmarks. The RAP framework comprises three key components: First, the Visual Retrieval module employs a pre-trained vision RAG model to compute semantic similarity between the query and various image regions, retrieving the most relevant ones to reduce noise. Next, the Spatial-Awareness Layout module preserves the original spatial relationships among the retrieved regions when composing them into the model input, ensuring spatial coherence. Finally, the Adaptive Retrieval-Exploration Search (RE-Search) module dynamically determines the optimal number of retrieved regions by constructing a Retrieval-Exploration Tree (RE-Tree), thereby balancing information sufficiency with computational efficiency.

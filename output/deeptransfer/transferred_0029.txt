What aspects of AI-generated music do people consider when evaluating it? Current study settings (e.g., Hong et al., 2022; Moura and Maw, 2021) rely on rating scales adapted from adjacent domains; however, these measures may lack specificity in capturing how people actually experience and judge AI-generated music. Using an exploratory approach, we aim to identify the subjective criteria and themes underlying participants' judgments, based on open-ended responses. To create ecologically valid stimuli, we used the AI tools Stable Audio (v.2.0) and Suno (v.3.5), informed by findings from the Emotion-to-Music Atlas (EMMA; Strauss et al., 2024), a comprehensive database of human emotion annotations for music excerpts from various genres. Specifically, we drew on recent data providing GEMS9 (Zentner et al., 2008) annotations for 453 music excerpts from the Music4All-Onion database (Moscati et al., 2022). These excerpts were derived from five major genres, each with three distinct subgenres: (1) Electronic (Trance, Downtempo, Dubstep), (2) Jazz (Bossa-Nova, Smooth-Jazz, Swing), (3) Pop (Europop, Indie-Pop, Pop-Punk), (4) Rock (Soft-Rock, RockBallad, Doom-Metal), and (5) Soul/Funk (Funk, Disco, Soul). For each subgenre, prompts were constructed based on the most prominent emotion dimensions, determined by the mean values across all songs within that subgenre in the EMMA, and their respective GEMS-9 descriptors. This procedure yielded 2-3 prompts per subgenre, resulting in a total of 38 different prompts. These prompts were then adapted for use in Suno and Stable Audio, resulting in a set of 76 stimuli. To account for the properties of each tool, the following approach was used: For Suno, prompts were created in instrumental mode, and one of two outputs was randomly selected and cut to a duration of 45-60 seconds to ensure comparability with prior research. For Stable Audio, the term 'instrumental' was added to each prompt, and the target duration was set to 60 seconds. Further details on the generation and editing procedure, as well as the audio files, are provided in the supplementary materials.

The evaluation examined the efficiency and efficacy of various systems for PowerPoint file editing, with a particular focus on their ability to handle complex and large-scale tasks. Among the systems tested, only the MiniMax Agent and PPTPilot successfully completed edits within a two-minute timeframe, while others exhibited slower performance or frequent failures. Given the emphasis on efficiency, the primary analysis centered on single-pass outputs, with results for loop-enabled edits detailed separately in Table 4. Notably, PPTPilot’s effectiveness in PowerPoint editing hinges on three critical components: a planning mechanism that comprehends the structural and semantic aspects of slides, a system capable of switching between API utilization and direct XML modifications, and a stepwise refinement process tailored to manage extensive and intricate tasks. Empirical evaluations reveal that while most advanced systems struggle with complex edits, mixed media, and modifications across multiple slides, PPTPilot consistently outperforms its counterparts in these areas. To further understand its performance, an analysis of PPTPilot’s core components was conducted to evaluate their individual contributions. Using the evaluation metrics outlined in Section 3—task success rate, PPTX validity, instruction fidelity, visual quality, and computational cost—we assessed PPTPilot in its comprehensive configuration, which includes JSON snapshot construction, intelligent routing between XML editing and programmatic python-pptx paths, strict adherence to output schemas, and automatic XML validation and repair. Subsequently, an ablation study was performed by systematically disabling or modifying specific components of PPTPilot while keeping all other variables constant. In one configuration, all modifications were executed solely through XML manipulation, which proved advantageous for precise, localized adjustments, such as resizing individual shapes. However, this approach was less efficient and effective for extensive deck-wide alterations, such as standardizing text size or applying a uniform theme, leading to increased latency and reduced efficacy for global transformations. Conversely, another configuration relied exclusively on the Python library, which excelled at executing broad modifications, such as renaming or translating text across the deck. Nevertheless, this method struggled with intricate adjustments requiring precise control over specific elements, highlighting the complementary strengths of the two approaches.

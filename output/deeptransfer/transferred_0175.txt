The investigation centered on the efficiency and effectiveness of various systems in PowerPoint file editing. Notably, only the MiniMax Agent and PPTPilot consistently complete edits within a two-minute timeframe, whereas other systems exhibit slower performance or frequent operational failures. Recognizing the critical importance of processing speed, our analysis exclusively considered single-pass outputs; results pertaining to loop-enabled edits are presented separately in Table 4. The experimental findings underscore that effective PowerPoint editing within PPTPilot hinges on three critical components: a planning mechanism capable of comprehending the structural and semantic intricacies of slides, a dual-modality system alternating between API utilization and direct OOXML manipulation, and an iterative refinement process designed to tackle extensive and intricate editing tasks. Empirical evaluations reveal that while state-of-the-art systems encounter significant difficulties with complex modifications, heterogeneous media, and multi-slide transformations, PPTPilot demonstrates superior performance in these domains. Moreover, a detailed ablation study was conducted to evaluate the contribution of each core component to the system's overall efficacy. We employed the same metrics as delineated in Section 3: task success rate, PPTX validity, instruction fidelity (IF), visual quality (VQ), and computational cost. For the experimental evaluations, we utilized the comprehensive PPTPilot configuration, which encompasses JSON snapshot construction, intelligent routing between XML editing and programmatic python-pptx paths, adherence to strict output schemas, and automatic XML validation and repair. Subsequently, we conducted an ablation study by systematically disabling or modifying specific components of PPTPilot while maintaining all other variables constant. In one configuration, all modifications were executed directly through XML manipulation, which is effective for precise, localized adjustments such as resizing an individual shape. However, this method exhibits inefficiencies and diminished efficacy when applied to comprehensive transformations across the entire deck, such as standardizing text size or implementing a uniform theme. Conversely, another configuration employed exclusively the Python library, which demonstrates proficiency in executing extensive transformations, such as renaming or translating text, yet lacks the granularity required for meticulous adjustments necessitating precise control over specific elements.

The method optimizes a penalized value function to constrain the policy within the support of the dataset, providing the foundation of our approach. This formulation simplifies the principled Offline RL objective from Equation (4) into an equivalent objective: identifying the action with the maximum visitation count, or density. This result offers a strong theoretical basis for our methodology. Our test-time scaling framework, which seeks the highest-density (i.e., in-support) action, is not merely a heuristic but a principled and theoretically grounded realization of the anti-exploration objective standard in Offline RL. Building on the successes of Lobel et al. (2023) and Bai et al. (2025) in applying pseudo-counts to optimize LLM preferences, we implement this concept using a Coin Flipping Network (CFN). As validated in Figure 3, the pseudo-counts generated by our estimator strongly correlate with both the L2 distance to the ground-truth action and the pseudo-count derived from the CFN. This confirms that our CFN effectively serves as a verifier to penalize out-of-support actions. Our experiments aim to determine whether the proposed Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-counts can improve action prediction accuracy and enhance task success rates. Furthermore, we investigate which design choices contribute to these improvements and assess the acceptability of the additional computational overhead. Specifically, our evaluation addresses three research questions: Q1: Can the framework improve performance across diverse environments, various VLC policies, and a wide range of tasks? Q2: What is the computational cost of the test-time scaling method, and can it maintain performance gains when deployed on physical robotic systems? Q3: To what extent do design decisions—such as the inclusion of internal features and the use of the CFN pseudo-count network—impact overall system performance?

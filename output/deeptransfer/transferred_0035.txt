This establishes our primary objective: a formal simplification of the principled Offline RL objective in Eq. (4) into an equivalent goal of identifying the action with the maximum visitation count, or density. This result provides strong theoretical justification for our methodology. Our test-time scaling framework, which seeks the highest-density (i.e., in-support) action, is not merely heuristic. Instead, it represents a principled and theoretically equivalent realization of the anti-exploration objective standard in Offline RL. Building on the demonstrated successes of pseudo-count methodologies in LLM preference optimization (Lobel et al., 2023; Bai et al., 2025), we instantiate this approach through the Coin Flipping Network (CFN). As shown in Figure 3, the pseudo-count estimates derived from our CFN exhibit a strong correlation with both the L2 distance to the ground-truth action and the pseudo-count values generated by the estimator. This confirms that our CFN effectively serves as a verifier to penalize out-of-support actions. Our experiments aim to determine whether implementing Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-counts can enhance action prediction accuracy and improve task success rates. Furthermore, we investigate which design choices contribute to these improvements and assess whether the additional computational cost is justified. Specifically, our experiments address the following research questions: Q1: Can our framework improve performance across diverse environments, various VLC policies, and a wide range of tasks? Q2: What is the additional computational cost introduced by our test-time scaling method, and can it maintain performance improvements when applied to real robotic systems? Q3: How do specific design choices, such as incorporating internal features and the CFN pseudo-count network, influence overall system performance?

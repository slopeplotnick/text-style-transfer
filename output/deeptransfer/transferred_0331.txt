To address these limitations, we propose HEALTHCONTRADICT, a dataset consisting of 920 unique instances, each comprising a health-related question and two documents with contradictory stances. In addition, each instance includes a factual answer supported by scientific evidence. Using HEALTHCONTRADICT, we evaluated several language models, ranging from 1 billion to 8 billion parameters, including both general-purpose and fine-tuned biomedical models, to answer health-related questions amidst knowledge conflicts. Our benchmark evaluates two primary aspects: (i) How do language models answer biomedical questions in the presence of knowledge conflicts? (ii) How does the biomedical context provided to the language models influence their responses? To achieve this, we included correct, incorrect, or contradictory contexts in different prompt scenarios and assessed the models' accuracy and probability distribution in answering health-related questions. Our contributions are as follows: (i) the development of HEALTHCONTRADICT, a novel dataset designed to evaluate language models in the presence of conflicting information in the biomedical domain; (ii) a comprehensive evaluation of language models using HEALTHCONTRADICT, focusing on their ability to reason over extensive, conflicting biomedical contexts through interpretable quantitative metrics; and (iii) a comparative analysis of general-purpose language models versus their fine-tuned biomedical counterparts, revealing that the latter excel in leveraging accurate information while resisting incorrect contextual knowledge. To ensure unbiased evaluation, each document within the dataset was curated to be unique. For example, in response to the inquiry "Can coffee aid in weight loss?" two documents present opposing perspectives, with one affirming and the other negating the proposition. The factual answer, supported by scientific evidence, is affirmative, indicating that caffeine intake may facilitate reductions in weight, BMI, and body fat.

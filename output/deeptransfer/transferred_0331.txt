To address these limitations, we propose HEALTHCONTRADICT, a dataset consisting of 920 unique instances, each comprising a health-related question paired with two documents presenting contradictory stances. Moreover, each instance includes a factual answer supported by scientific evidence. Using HEALTHCONTRADICT, we conducted evaluations on several language models, ranging from 1 billion to 8 billion parameters, including general domain models and their fine-tuned biomedical counterparts, to assess their ability to answer health-related questions amidst conflicting information. Specifically, our benchmark evaluates: (i) how language models respond to biomedical questions in the presence of knowledge conflicts, and (ii) how the biomedical context provided to the models influences their answers. To achieve this, we incorporated correct, incorrect, or contradictory contexts into different prompt scenarios and assessed the models' accuracy and probability distributions in answering health-related questions. Our contributions are as follows: (i) we introduce HEALTHCONTRADICT, a novel dataset designed to evaluate language models in the presence of conflicting biomedical information; (ii) we perform a comprehensive evaluation of language models using HEALTHCONTRADICT, measuring their ability to reason over extensive, conflicting biomedical contexts through interpretable quantitative metrics; and (iii) we compare general domain language models with their fine-tuned biomedical counterparts, demonstrating that the latter excel at leveraging accurate information while resisting erroneous knowledge. To ensure unbiased evaluation, each document within the dataset is unique and appears only once.

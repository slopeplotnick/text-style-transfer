Despite significant advancements in memory augmentation technologies, general architectures encounter specific challenges when applied to stock prediction tasks. In terms of memory representation, current systems struggle to achieve fine-grained modeling of event sequences; in retrieval mechanisms, they lack effective capabilities for matching historical scenario patterns to new contexts; and in temporal processing, they fail to adapt to the dynamic and evolving nature of financial markets. These limitations underscore the pressing need for specialized memory architectures tailored to the unique demands of the financial domain. This chapter explores the relevant research along two primary dimensions: first, the evolution from historical pattern matching to the integration of memory mechanisms, and second, the growing emphasis on event-driven prediction paradigms. While notable progress has been made in these areas, a critical challenge persists in seamlessly integrating structured event knowledge with traceable memory systems—an issue that this study aims to address. 

2.2.1 From Historical Pattern Matching to Memory Mechanisms. In the domain of time series forecasting, stock price prediction methods based on identifying analogous historical patterns attracted considerable academic interest long before the advent of LLMs. The central premise of these approaches—leveraging historical data to identify patterns resembling current contexts for predictive purposes—aligns conceptually with the memory mechanisms employed in LLMs. Both paradigms share the fundamental goal of efficiently storing and retrieving external experiences or contextual information. Early research predominantly relied on statistical methods, which, while effective at detecting numerical patterns, were insufficient for processing the complexity of financial market data. With the emergence of deep learning, models such as LSTM networks and Transformers enabled the automatic extraction of temporal features, significantly advancing the field. Transformer-based temporal models, in particular, demonstrated exceptional proficiency in capturing long-term dependencies through self-attention mechanisms. Moreover, the FinSeer framework further advanced this area by incorporating LLMs to retrieve numerically similar historical sequences, thereby enabling deeper feature extraction.

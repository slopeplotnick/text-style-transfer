Despite significant advancements in memory augmentation technologies, general architectures encounter specific challenges when applied to stock price prediction tasks. In terms of memory representation, these systems face difficulties in achieving fine-grained modeling of event sequences; in retrieval mechanisms, they exhibit limited effectiveness in matching historical scenario patterns; and in temporal processing, they struggle to adapt to the unique and dynamic evolution patterns of financial markets. These limitations underscore the urgent need for specialized memory architectures tailored to the financial domain. This chapter examines the relevant research along two key dimensions: the progression from historical pattern matching to the adoption of memory mechanisms, and the growing emphasis on event-driven prediction paradigms. While substantial progress has been made in these areas, a critical challenge persists in integrating structured event knowledge with traceable memory systems—a gap that this study seeks to address. 

2.2.1 From Historical Pattern Matching to Memory Mechanisms. In the field of time series forecasting, stock price prediction methods that rely on identifying similar historical patterns have long been a focus of academic research, predating the emergence of large language models (LLMs). The fundamental principle of these approaches—leveraging historical data patterns that resemble current contexts to inform predictions—aligns conceptually with the memory mechanisms in LLMs. Both paradigms share the core objective of effectively encoding and retrieving external experiences or historical contexts. Early research predominantly employed statistical methods, which, while effective at capturing numerical patterns, were inherently limited in their ability to process the intricate dynamics of financial markets. With the advent of deep learning, architectures such as LSTM and Transformer enabled the automatic extraction of temporal features with greater sophistication. Transformer-based models, in particular, excelled in capturing long-term dependencies through self-attention mechanisms, driving significant advancements in the field. Moreover, the FinSeer framework has further enhanced this progress by incorporating LLMs to retrieve numerically analogous historical sequences, thereby enabling deeper feature extraction and analysis.

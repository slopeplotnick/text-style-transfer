Results and observations. Table 4 presents the ablation results, highlighting the importance of hybrid routing strategies. Forcing a single execution path significantly degrades task performance, especially on workloads combining local and global edits. The XML-only variant achieves an IF score of 0.95, whereas python-pptx-only reaches 2.06. The hybrid approach without refinement improves to 2.36, and enabling iterative refinement further boosts performance to 2.84 IF and 3.21 VQ. A case-by-case analysis reveals that providing slide images to PPTPilot enhances layout reasoning: visual grounding improves decisions related to z-ordering, spacing, and alignment without diminishing text editing quality. Moreover, iterative refinement stabilizes complex edits, with an additional pass often resolving formatting drift after re-theming or batch rewrites, incurring only modest computational overhead. PPTArena and PPTPilot are introduced to establish PowerPoint editing as a measurable capability for multimodal intelligent systems. PPTArena transforms real slide decks, human-authored edit programs, and style-aware rubrics into a benchmark emphasizing instruction fidelity, structural grounding, and layout quality beyond generation-focused metrics. PPTPilot demonstrates that a dual-path paradigm with explicit plan-edit-verify loops significantly enhances reliability, outperforming both robust proprietary agents and cutting-edge VLMs. However, these systems still struggle with complex, long-horizon, and multimodal tasks, underscoring that robust PowerPoint editing remains an unsolved challenge. We anticipate that PPTArena and PPTPilot will serve as foundational tools for advancing stronger PowerPoint editing systems capable of safely and reliably handling the documents commonly used by individuals.

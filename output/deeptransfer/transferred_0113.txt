We evaluate our knowledge graph-based framework using two complementary approaches to analyze data attribution in generative models. First, we conduct controlled data removal experiments on a locally trained model to determine whether our method can effectively identify influential training samples. Second, we explore the impact of stylistic attributes in a black-box setting by generating Ghibli-style images. To this end, we construct a "Ghibli World" knowledge graph from the original films and compare it against stylized images. These approaches enable us to investigate both direct data attribution and the traceability of stylistic elements within generative models. To ensure a fair and interpretable comparison, we replicate the experimental setup proposed in a recent influence analysis framework, which operates without requiring access to training parameters. This framework follows a two-step process: (1) it retrieves training samples whose textual descriptions most closely match the user's prompt and (2) ranks these samples based on the cosine similarity between their embeddings and the generated output. The most similar training images are then removed, and the model is retrained to empirically evaluate their influence. Under the same conditions, we applied our graph-based method using the same dataset, prompts, and generative model. However, instead of relying on text and latent-space similarity, we identified influential samples based on the similarity of their extracted knowledge graphs. This allowed for a direct comparison between embedding-based and graph-based retrieval methods in terms of influence detection and interpretability. For the generative model, we utilized a compact model derived from the open-source dalle-pytorch package, trained on the Fashion Product Images dataset, which consists of 44,000 fashion images with corresponding captions. We retained the same experimental configuration and hyperparameters as the original setup, ensuring full control over the training data and enabling rigorous experimentation with data removal and retraining.

Knowledge. In Case 1, the query term is "Capybara" and the product title is "water pig plush toy." Through the distillation process facilitated by the teacher model, the system has internalized sufficient semantic knowledge to recognize that "Capybara" is an alternative name for "water pig," thereby correctly determining their relevance. Reasoning. In Case 2, the query is "intellectual women's clothing," while the product description contains no directly matchable literal keywords. However, by leveraging its reasoning capabilities, the model deduces that the product "commuter formal dress" aligns with the user's implicit demand for professional and intellectual attire. Specifically, the product's attributes—such as its formal design, gray color, applicable age range (18–24 years old), and stylistic elements (suit set, formal dress)—conform to the typical preferences of the intellectual demographic for simple and appropriate clothing, leading to an accurate judgment. Multimodal Understanding. Case 3 exemplifies the model's ability to integrate textual and visual information. For the query "1/4 Black Soft," the model identifies the "Black Soft" attribute within the product title and simultaneously locates the phrase "Kase MAGNETIC BLACK MIST 1/4" on the product image. Therefore, by synthesizing information from both textual and visual inputs, the model achieves coordinated reasoning, which is further enhanced by the relevance-oriented caption generation strategy (Section 3.3). Rule Compliance. In Case 4, the alignment between the query and the product is relatively straightforward. However, a predefined rule stipulates that if a user searching for standard products is presented with second-hand alternatives, the relevance should be classified as "weakly relevant" (L3). After rule-aware CoT modeling (Section 3.3), the model has acquired sufficient rule compliance capabilities to accurately apply such business rules. Deployment Optimization. To address latency bottlenecks in real-time computation, a hybrid deployment strategy combining offline pre-computation with online caching was implemented. Specifically, a batch of high-frequency query-item pairs was sampled from search logs. These pairs were processed via offline inference using the LLM, and the outputs were stored for future utilization, thereby optimizing performance.

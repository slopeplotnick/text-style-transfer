The system operates on Spring Boot, and the database is hosted by PostgreSQL. Agronomists use Python or MATLAB to build the simulators. The IT team relies on a central platform, named Cross, which serves as a comprehensive framework for building application code. Initially, the platform was designed to generate Proof of Concepts; however, its purpose has evolved over time, transforming into a foundation for finalized products. Currently, there are seven variants within the family, catering to three Product Owners. New variants are specifically tailored to meet client requirements, with approximately one new variant introduced each year. The levels of the Architecture Dimension, as outlined in the FEF framework, address the technical methodologies for software construction and define the technical realization of products within the software product line. At Level 1, architectures are restricted to individual systems, with no visible focus on reuse. By Level 2, there is a shift towards reusing third-party infrastructure, incorporating common software components such as middleware, though domain-specific assets are not formally integrated. At Level 3, domain commonality is systematically captured and implemented into a software platform, which provides a reference architecture applicable to all applications and primarily emphasizes platform utilization. Nevertheless, this configurable platform does not effectively support configuration variability. At Level 4, both domain commonality and variability are systematically addressed, and a reference architecture is established to encompass the entire software product line. Domain assets are carefully designed to support product derivation, with particular attention given to variability management within the overarching software product line architecture. Finally, at Level 5, the reference architecture assumes a central role, with application architectures diverging only minimally from its structure. Automated product derivation becomes possible through the use of scripts, tools, and high-level languages, while application development primarily focuses on configuration within the constraints of the reference architecture, enabling automated product configuration.

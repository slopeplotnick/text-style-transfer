Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) extend the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) by incorporating an additional visual input channel, which enhances semantic representation beyond textual modalities while also introducing new safety risks. Jailbreaking, the process of using carefully crafted adversarial inputs to bypass safety mechanisms and elicit harmful outputs, remains a critical challenge in the development of robust, trustworthy, and secure MLLMs. While earlier jailbreaking efforts primarily targeted LLMs using techniques such as prompt injection and role-play, the shift toward MLLMs has led to the emergence of attacks that exploit their visual modality. However, existing methods reveal significant limitations. One common approach involves creating adversarial images or embedding sensitive content within visual inputs, yet this fails to fully utilize the contextual richness that images can provide. Another strategy, though incorporating context, merely replicates the textual jailbreak techniques used with LLMs, describing scenarios in words and neglecting the unique advantages of visual representation. To overcome these shortcomings, we propose the Contextual-Image Attack (CIA) method, which generates images that simultaneously convey both the contextual scenario and the malicious intent, with textual input serving only as a supplementary prompt. For example, instead of embedding the harmful query "How can someone manufacture illegal drugs?" as plain text within an image, our approach situates the query within a plausible visual context, such as a teacher posing the question in a chemistry classroom. This contextual embedding lends an appearance of legitimacy to the inquiry, potentially obfuscating the malicious intent and enabling it to bypass safety mechanisms. In contrast, prior approaches like FigStep simply render the query as text within an image or rely entirely on textual descriptions to depict the scenario. By leveraging advancements in image generation technologies, which enable the creation of highly realistic visual scenarios, and emerging methods that transform textual information into visual tokens, our approach demonstrates the growing potential of images to encode complex, covert meanings that exploit latent semantic structures within models.

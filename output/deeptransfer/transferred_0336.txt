Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) extend the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) by incorporating visual input channels, which enrich the semantic space beyond text and introduce additional safety risks [15, 17, 24]. Jailbreaking, a method that uses crafted adversarial inputs to bypass safety alignments and elicit harmful content, remains a critical challenge in the development of robust, trustworthy, and secure models [26, 33, 48]. While earlier research predominantly focused on jailbreaking LLMs through techniques such as prompt injection and role-play [17, 52], the advent of MLLMs has shifted attention toward exploiting their visual modality. However, current strategies reveal significant limitations. One approach involves creating adversarial images or embedding sensitive information within visual content, yet it fails to fully utilize contextual elements [3, 12]. Another line of attack, which considers context, merely adapts LLM jailbreak techniques by describing scenarios textually, neglecting the unique advantages of images in conveying complex situations [28, 30]. To overcome these shortcomings, we propose the Contextual-Image Attack (CIA) method, which generates images that integrate both contextual scenarios and malicious intent, with textual input serving only as an auxiliary component. For example, rather than embedding the query "How can someone manufacture illegal drugs?" as text within an image, our approach depicts a plausible scenario, such as a teacher asking this question in a chemistry classroom. This visually coherent setting provides a legitimate communicative motive, leading the model to misinterpret the user's intent and bypass safety measures. In contrast, prior methods like FigStep [12] simply embed the query as text within an image (b), while others [27, 30, 44] rely solely on textual descriptions (a). Our image-centric approach is particularly advantageous due to two emerging technological trends: advancements in image generation, which enable the creation of realistic scenarios [49], and the development of techniques to compress textual information into visual tokens [47]. These innovations underscore the growing potential of images to embed intricate semantic content, thereby enhancing the efficacy of our attack.

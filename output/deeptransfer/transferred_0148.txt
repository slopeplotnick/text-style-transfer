This study introduces a novel approach to disentangling perceptual and cognitive processes within AI models by incorporating examples of erroneous reasoning into the training data. This enables the model to discern between valid and flawed reasoning patterns, thereby enhancing its ability to differentiate correct from incorrect conclusions. To further address this challenge, we propose a new framework, termed Self-Taught Learning (STL), which simultaneously refines both perceptual and cognitive capabilities. Unlike prior methodologies that predominantly emphasized cognitive reasoning, STL facilitates a more integrated development of the model’s interpretative and inferential capacities. Moreover, our methodology was evaluated across domains such as commonsense, language, and science, demonstrating enhanced performance without reliance on human intervention. In the realm of vision-language reasoning, reasoning plays a pivotal role in augmenting the efficacy of models. Although contemporary models exhibit commendable results on standardized benchmarks, integrating visual information into the reasoning process remains a formidable challenge, particularly for open-source models. To address this, certain methodologies employ prompts to facilitate step-by-step cognitive processing in models. For instance, one approach initially conducts a context analysis followed by the generation of high-level features, whereas another utilizes scene graphs to elucidate object–attribute relationships. Additionally, some strategies involve fine-tuning models on datasets that encompass multimodal reasoning chains; however, the development of such datasets can be resource-intensive. In contrast, our methodology enables models to autonomously enhance their reasoning capabilities without the necessity for specialized datasets. Specifically, self-training constitutes a semi-supervised approach wherein a model refines its performance by utilizing its own outputs as training data. Within the domain of large language models, this paradigm aids in augmenting reasoning proficiency by generating and iteratively applying reasoning steps. Consequently, this process enhances models' capabilities without necessitating human intervention. While certain studies have advanced reasoning by selecting and fine-tuning appropriate reasoning examples, the application of self-training methodologies to vision-language models remains relatively underexplored.

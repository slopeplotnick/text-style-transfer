The prior-conforming prompt reinforces a belief aligned with the model's likely initial stance, serving as both a baseline for belief entrenchment and a potential lower bound for accuracy. In contrast, the critical-thinking prompt promotes openness to counter-evidence and rational belief revision. A complete list of the prompts used in our experiments is provided in Appendix C. In this section, we present our findings. First, we establish a clear link between belief entrenchment, as quantified by the Martingale Score, and reductions in ground-truth accuracy, thereby validating the significance of belief entrenchment as a challenge to model performance. Next, we benchmark a variety of closed- and open-weight models across different problem domains and configurations, attributing belief entrenchment to factors such as model type, prompt design, and reasoning dynamics. Additionally, we confirm the validity of using large language models (LLMs) as adjudicators. Notably, belief entrenchment is more pronounced in subjective, value-laden domains like r/ChangeMyView compared to more objective domains like Forecasting, as evidenced by consistently higher Martingale Scores in the former. This suggests that belief entrenchment poses a greater challenge in contexts where model accuracy cannot be easily evaluated against ground truth. To further investigate, we attempted to manipulate belief entrenchment using prior-conforming and critical-thinking prompts. We observed that belief entrenchment was exacerbated under the prior-conforming prompt and mitigated under the critical-thinking prompt in Forecasting, though this pattern did not extend to r/ChangeMyView or OpenReview. A detailed analysis of factors influencing belief entrenchment is provided in Appendix B. Finally, we demonstrate that belief entrenchment undermines accuracy, as the ultimate goal of reasoning in LLMs is to arrive at true beliefs. This was tested in domains with available ground-truth labels, such as Forecasting and OpenReview. While outcome-based metrics like the Brier Score measure performance, they do not explain the underlying reasoning processes, underscoring the need for process-based metrics. Our analysis reveals a positive correlation between higher Martingale Scores, indicative of stronger belief entrenchment, and lower Brier Scores in Forecasting, highlighting belief entrenchment as a critical factor impairing LLM reasoning.

The prior-conforming prompt reinforces beliefs aligned with the model's likely initial stance, serving as both a baseline for belief entrenchment and a reference point for accuracy. In contrast, the critical-thinking prompt fosters openness to counter-evidence and supports rational belief revision. A complete list of prompts used in the experiments is provided in Appendix C. In this section, we present our empirical findings. First, we establish a link between belief entrenchment, as quantified by the Martingale Score, and declines in ground-truth accuracy, thereby underscoring the significance of belief entrenchment as a challenge. Next, we benchmark various closed- and open-weight models across different problem domains and configurations, attributing belief entrenchment to factors such as model type, prompting strategies, and reasoning techniques. Additionally, we validate the use of LLMs as evaluators. Notably, our findings reveal that belief entrenchment is more pronounced in subjective, value-laden domains like r/ChangeMyView, where Martingale Scores are consistently higher compared to more objective domains such as Forecasting. This suggests that belief entrenchment poses a greater challenge in domains where evaluating LLM performance against an objective ground truth is inherently difficult. To explore the modulation of belief entrenchment, we employed two distinct prompting strategies: a prior-conforming prompt and a critical-thinking prompt. We observe that belief entrenchment is more severe under the prior-conforming prompt and less severe under the critical-thinking prompt in Forecasting; however, this pattern does not generalize to r/ChangeMyView or OpenReview. Further discussion of factors influencing belief entrenchment is available in Appendix B. Crucially, we demonstrate that belief entrenchment undermines accuracy, as the ultimate goal of reasoning in LLMs is to achieve true beliefs. This is particularly evident in domains with accessible ground truth labels, such as Forecasting and OpenReview. While outcome-based metrics like the Brier Score assess model performance, they fail to explain the underlying causes of performance variations, necessitating the use of reasoning metrics. Our analysis reveals a negative correlation between elevated Martingale Scores, indicative of stronger belief entrenchment, and suboptimal Brier Scores in the Forecasting domain, highlighting belief entrenchment as a key factor impairing LLM reasoning.

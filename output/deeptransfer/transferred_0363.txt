While AMP demonstrates strong imitation performance, it requires retaining reference motion data throughout training. In contrast, SMP accurately reproduces a diverse range of skills, including highly dynamic ones, and achieves imitation accuracy and sample efficiency comparable to AMP, all without relying on reference data during training. Moreover, SMP consistently outperforms SMILING and AMP-Frozen across all skill domains. AMP-Frozen attempts to eliminate AMP's reliance on data by substituting a pre-trained discriminator; however, this approach proves ineffective and results in severely degraded motion imitation. DeepMimic benefits from explicit phase synchronization, which reduces tracking errors for certain motions. Nevertheless, SMP remains competitive in performance. DeepMimic struggles with challenging motions such as spinkicks and cartwheels, particularly when pose termination is disabled, leading some runs to converge to suboptimal local solutions. Our framework is among the first to employ score-matching for constructing a reusable motion prior, achieving motion fidelity comparable to state-of-the-art adversarial imitation learning methods. In this section, we highlight ensemble score-matching as a key design choice that enhances training stability and improves outcomes. Specifically, we compare our ensemble score-matching approach, which averages multiple SDS evaluations across a fixed set of diffusion timesteps, against the conventional method used in prior work that evaluates SDS at a single randomly sampled timestep. This comparison focuses on single-clip imitation tasks of varying difficulty, including Run, Cartwheel, and Backflip. As shown in Table 5, the single-timestep SDS objective performs adequately for simpler motions but struggles significantly with more complex behaviors. For instance, when imitating a backflip, policies trained with a single SDS evaluation often collapse to stationary standing or falling backward instead of completing the flip. In contrast, ensembling SDS evaluations across predetermined timesteps provides a more consistent and informative reward signal, thereby enabling effective policy training for complex acrobatic motions.

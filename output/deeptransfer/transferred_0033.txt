Our results demonstrate that prompt-engineered LLMs, when combined with Isolation Forest scores, outperform traditional JETs and ML baselines by delivering robust anomaly detection alongside natural-language rationales. However, challenges persist regarding false positives, cost efficiency, and practical deployment. This study underscores the promise of AI-augmented auditing, where accuracy and interpretability are jointly optimized. Notably, we provide the first evidence that LLMs can be effectively applied to tax-related ledger data, a domain where transparency and trust are paramount. Our findings suggest that integrating LLMs with classical anomaly signals offers a viable approach to balancing precision, recall, and explainability. Future research will aim to validate these insights across larger, more diverse datasets while assessing robustness against prompt variation and model updates. 

Data scope and labels. The current evaluation is limited to one anonymized tax ledger and a synthetic benchmark. Real-world ledgers vary significantly across industries, ERP systems, and jurisdictions, and obtaining ground-truth labels remains a substantial challenge. Pseudo-labels derived from JETs may embed rule-based biases, while synthetic anomalies are simulator-defined. Therefore, external validity must be established through larger, multi-source datasets and independent audit labels. 

Model stability and explainability. LLM behavior is highly sensitive to prompts and model-specific parameters; minor changes in phrasing or sampling can lead to notable shifts in outcomes, and discrepancies across model families (e.g., Gemma vs. Mistral) are observed. Although the AuditCopilot framework mitigates some inconsistencies, stability under paraphrasing, adversarial inputs, and model updates cannot be fully guaranteed. Moreover, while natural-language explanations enhance auditor comprehension, they do not always align precisely with underlying decision boundaries, introducing potential discrepancies. 

Deployment and governance. While our method demonstrates strong experimental performance, deploying LLMs in financial auditing entails inherent risks, including hallucinations, automation bias, prompt injection vulnerabilities, and evolving model weights that challenge reproducibility. Therefore, we emphasize that this approach should be viewed as a decision-support tool requiring rigorous human oversight, robust access controls, and comprehensive governance frameworks prior to real-world implementation.

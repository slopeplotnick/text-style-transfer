Recent research has explored various methodologies to enhance the reasoning and verification capabilities of models. For example, prior work has examined approaches such as decomposing reasoning into incremental steps (Ling et al., 2023), verifying individual proof steps systematically (Yang et al., 2022), and employing reward models to optimize the training process for mathematical reasoning (Luo et al., 2025). Notably, Song et al. (2025) investigated the performance improvements achieved by using an outcome-level verifier, referred to as the GV-Gap, and analyzed how these improvements vary with changes in the capacity of the solver or verifier. However, their analysis primarily focuses on cases where the solver and verifier are instantiated as the same model, and they limit their study to base models, excluding those subjected to additional fine-tuning. 

Scaling test-time compute has also been a recent focus of research. For instance, Zhao et al. (2025) explored the integration of random sampling with self-verification, Chen et al. (2025) studied the combination of parallel sampling with self-correction, and Singhi et al. (2025) compared the Self-Consistency approach (Wang et al., 2023) to scaling with generative verifiers. Moreover, Snell et al. (2025) investigated compute-optimal strategies for test-time scaling with process-level verification. Despite these advancements, verifiers remain imperfect. They can produce false positives (Stroebl et al., 2024), eliminate valid reasoning paths (Yu et al., 2025), or fail to select the correct solution (Brown et al., 2024). Additional related work on test-time verification is discussed in Appendix A.

Another area of inquiry pertains to self-improvement and self-evaluation in language models. While some researchers have expressed skepticism about the feasibility of self-improvement (Huang et al., 2024b; Kamoi et al., 2024; Olausson et al., 2023; Panickssery et al., 2024), others have proposed theoretical frameworks and provided empirical evidence supporting its potential (Huang et al., 2024a). For example, Zhang et al. (2024) focused on smaller models, arguing that pairing them with stronger verifiers is essential for effective self-improvement. Practical methods for self-enhancement include leveraging natural language feedback (Madaan et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2023) or explicitly training models for self-correction (Welleck et al., 2023). Alternatively, some approaches utilize tools, such as code interpreters (Zhou et al., 2024), to iteratively refine solutions (Gou et al., 2024).

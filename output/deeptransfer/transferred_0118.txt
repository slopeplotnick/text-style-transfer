Recent research has explored various methodologies to enhance the reasoning and verification capabilities of models. Notably, prior studies have investigated approaches such as decomposing reasoning processes into discrete steps, verifying each step rigorously, and employing reward models to optimize training for improved mathematical reasoning. For instance, one line of inquiry has examined the efficacy of utilizing an outcome-level verifier, referred to as the GV-Gap, in improving performance, particularly in scenarios where the solver and verifier are instantiated as the same model. However, these investigations were limited to foundational models and did not encompass those subjected to further training post-initial configuration. Recently, scholarly attention has been directed towards optimizing test-time computational scaling through the application of verifiers. Zhao et al. (2025) examined the integration of random sampling with self-verification, while Chen et al. (2025) explored the combination of parallel sampling with error correction. Moreover, Singhi et al. (2025) conducted comparative analyses between the Self-Consistency approach (Wang et al., 2023) and the utilization of a generative verifier. Efforts have also been directed toward optimizing computational strategies for verification during the testing phase. Nonetheless, verifiers exhibit inherent limitations, including the potential to generate false positives, dismiss valid reasoning pathways, or select suboptimal solutions. Further elaboration on these issues is provided in Appendix A. Another domain of inquiry pertains to the capacity of models to enhance their own performance and critically assess their outputs. While some scholars express skepticism regarding this notion, others have advanced theoretical frameworks and empirical evidence demonstrating the potential for models to refine their understanding through distribution sharpening. A particular investigation concentrated on small models, positing that these models necessitate the integration of a more robust verifier to facilitate their improvement. Practical methodologies for self-improvement encompass the utilization of natural language feedback or the explicit training of models for self-correction. Alternative strategies involve the deployment of tools, particularly code interpreters, to iteratively enhance solutions.

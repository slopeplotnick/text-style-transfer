Besides, we adopted another strategy during the training phase. Because the base model sometimes struggles to follow instructions as prompt length increases, we introduced a brief warm-up stage using supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to ensure reliable adherence to the required format. The process operates as follows: the Challenger role performs few-shot grounded generation and computes a corresponding reward, while the Solver role engages in online curriculum learning, computes its reward, and undergoes updates. This ensures that the Solver focuses on problems that are both challenging and solvable, aligning with optimal learning theory (Shi et al., 2025; Bae et al., 2025). The Solver reward integrates both synthetic and human data, weighted by the complexity of the curriculum. R-FeW retains the self-evolving loop of R-ZERO but incorporates a grounded and curriculum-aware data pipeline. Specifically, the Challenger generates synthetic tasks using few-shot demonstrations derived from human anchors, while the Solver attempts to solve these tasks and evaluates success rates. An online curriculum filter then selects tasks of intermediate difficulty from both synthetic and human datasets. We use Qwen3-4B-Base (Yang et al., 2025b) and Qwen3-8B-Base as our backbone models. For comparison, we evaluate against several baselines: (1) Base Model — the pretrained checkpoint without additional fine-tuning, available at https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Base (Qwen3-8B-Base); (2) R-Zero (Huang et al., 2025) — the first ungrounded self-play framework where the model autonomously generates questions and answers, enabling self-improvement through iterative reasoning; (3) Absolute Zero (Zhao et al., 2025a) — a self-play framework limited to code generation tasks, using Python-based execution for verification and exemplifying domain-specific grounded self-play; (4) SPICE (Liu et al., 2025a) — a grounded self-play framework that retrieves contextual information from large document corpora to construct diverse reasoning tasks with document-grounded solutions. Baselines (1)-(2) were reproduced using publicly available configurations under identical training conditions, while results for (3)-(4) were taken from the findings reported by Liu et al. (2025a).

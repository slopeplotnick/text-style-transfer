To overcome the constraints of fixed viewpoints and enhance distinct spatial cues, we diversify camera rotation trajectories. Each trajectory begins with a random configuration of pitch, yaw, and roll angles, followed by stochastic drifts to emulate natural movements. Moreover, we ensure that the primary visual cues corresponding to the audio remain within the field of view for a sustained duration. Specifically, the direction of maximum audio energy is computed for each clip using spherical harmonics decomposition, and the camera’s initial orientation is adjusted to center the trajectory around the primary sound source, as illustrated in Fig. 1. After obtaining the binaural audio, samples with a normalized left-right channel difference below 0.01 are discarded, ensuring that the retained samples exhibit distinct spatial auditory cues. BiAudio distinguishes itself as the largest FoV video dataset in binaural audio format. As shown in Tab. 1, existing real-world binaural datasets are comparatively smaller in scale. While SimBinaural provides over 100 hours of content, its reliance on synthetic video and audio reduces its applicability to real-world scenarios. Furthermore, these datasets often focus on narrow sound environments: OAP on street scenes, and Fair-Play, SimBinaural, and MUSIC-21 on music. In contrast, BiAudio is large-scale and open-domain, encompassing diverse real-world sound environments. Spatial Cue Enhancement (Sec. 3.1) further improves spatial perception and dynamic movement, making it highly effective for video-to-binaural spatial audio generation. The overall architecture of ViSAudio is depicted in Figure 2. Our approach builds upon components of the MMAudio architecture, fine-tuning the pretrained MMAudio to inherit its robust audio generation capabilities, thereby ensuring strong generalization across open-domain acoustic environments. To produce binaural audio that maintains both channel coherence and spatial distinctiveness, we employ Dual-Branch Audio Generation (Sec. 4.2), wherein two dedicated branches independently predict left and right audio flows. Additionally, we introduce a Conditional Spacetime Module (Sec. 4.3), which extracts spatio-temporal features from the video and integrates them into the audio generation process, further enhancing the spatial realism of the output.

This section discusses joint generative translation models, which integrate both speech and text modalities to achieve seamless, high-quality translations across languages. It provides a comprehensive look at how contemporary voice-interfaced language models process and comprehend speech and conversational signals. Historically, over the past decade, substantial advancements have been made in the development and implementation of large-scale voice-powered AI virtual assistants, beginning with Apple's Siri, followed by Microsoft's Cortana, Google Assistant, Amazon's Alexa, and Samsung's Bixby. This segment of the tutorial will delve into the technological foundations underpinning these AI virtual assistants, including mechanisms for intent detection and slot filling utilizing recurrent neural networks (RNNs), dialogue management through reinforcement learning, the exploitation of web-scale query-click data, the utilization of the semantic web, and the integration of knowledge graphs for enhanced spoken language understanding. Currently, AI assistants are advancing by employing large language models (LLMs) to significantly enhance the comprehension and tracking of dialogues, as well as to improve spoken language generation. This tutorial will provide a comprehensive overview of existing LLMs and methodologies for their adaptation to specific downstream tasks. Furthermore, it elaborates on recent advancements in systems designed to manage multiple conversational threads, applicable to both general and specialized tasks, and encompasses a detailed examination of datasets and their evaluative methodologies. Additionally, it addresses emerging research challenges in the development of language models and the escalating demand for systems capable of processing multiple simultaneous conversations.

The Qwen2.5-VL-7B model was trained through the integration of diverse CoT data using a two-stage training framework, similar to the configuration used for the LLM base model in this study. Tables 13 and 14 provide a comparative analysis of performance between VLM-based and LLM-based models. On both simple and complex datasets, the LLM-based approach significantly outperformed the VLM-based method, particularly in macro-F1 scores. This suggests that, when equipped with comparable CoT capabilities, LLMs exhibit superior reasoning and knowledge processing compared to VLMs. However, VLMs demonstrate enhanced proficiency in managing visual information, as they do not require image-to-text conversion, thereby mitigating potential information loss. As illustrated in Fig 18, the LORE model has evolved over three developmental phases, achieving significant advancements in five core dimensions: objective formulation, model architecture, data and prompt design, evaluation methodologies, and practical applications. These phases trace a systematic trajectory of innovation through sophisticated design frameworks, high-quality datasets, and practical applications, particularly optimizing search results within the realm of online shopping. Initially, challenges were encountered in comprehending e-commerce queries, notably in identifying non-standard product nomenclature. To address this, extensive and high-caliber e-commerce datasets were developed, and domain knowledge was incorporated into the model via In-Context Learning, thereby augmenting its proficiency in interpreting e-commerce-specific language.

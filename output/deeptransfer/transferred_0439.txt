By integrating related events and tracing their antecedents, structured event chains emerge, consolidating dispersed information flows into a logically cohesive narrative. In contrast, summary-based representations often remain fragmented, lacking causal clarity and temporal coherence, even after clustering. Moreover, retaining specific details within events, such as technical specifications in a "new tech release," ensures a more precise comprehension of commercial impact compared to generalized summaries, which risk oversimplifying critical elements into ambiguous labels. Furthermore, the inclusion of precise temporal markers, such as day-level timestamps, enables a detailed analysis of event durations and lag effects, facilitating the establishment of causal linkages between events and market fluctuations. Without such temporal specificity, predictive models suffer from diminished accuracy and interpretability. To illustrate the importance of systematic event tracking and incremental information integration, we conducted a comparative analysis of a complete framework versus an ablated version without event tracking. For instance, even minor news items can significantly influence market outcomes if subsequent reports reveal unexpected progress. Conversely, repeated reports of a major shareholder reducing their holdings—without accompanying developments—elicit neutral market responses, as no deviation in expectations occurs. This highlights that while isolated events may exert limited influence, the incremental evolution of expectations captured through event tracking is pivotal for enhancing predictive accuracy. Finally, to assess the efficacy of our historical pattern referencing methodology, we compared four distinct approaches. Our strategy demonstrated superior performance, as evidenced by comparative results. For example, referencing a single company's historical data yields reasonable outcomes but proves suboptimal for companies with limited historical records or innovative activities, where analogous past events are difficult to identify.

Early demonstrations of self-play-based learning came from AlphaZero, which learns without external supervision by playing against itself and progressively strengthening its policy through these matches. Recent research has extended this principle to language models, naturally leading to self-evolving LLMs that refine their own reasoning capabilities over time. Several systems have instantiated language self-play at scale, where self-play fine-tuning improves relatively weak LLMs by repeatedly generating and solving tasks without large labeled corpora. This approach has been particularly fruitful in verifiable domains like code generation, where a "Coder" agent's program is verified by a "Tester" agent's unit tests. Moreover, fully data-free methods such as R-Zero push this further: the model proposes questions, produces candidate solutions, and learns purely from internal verification signals. Other approaches introduce limited seeds or structure, where the model starts from a small set of examples and learns to synthesize increasingly challenging problems around them. R-Few builds on this line of work but targets a different point on the supervision spectrum. This guided self-play design aims to preserve the open-ended exploration benefits of self-evolving LLMs while explicitly mitigating concept drift and diversity collapse. Furthermore, reinforcement learning (RL) has become a central mechanism for improving LLM reasoning beyond supervised fine-tuning and instruction tuning, most prominently through reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and its variants. Early RLHF pipelines such as InstructGPT demonstrated that sequence-level preference optimization can substantially improve helpfulness, safety, and, in many cases, reasoning quality compared to pure supervised fine-tuning. Building on these foundations, recent reasoning-centric systems such as DeepSeek-R1 show that RL-style optimization with carefully designed reasoning rewards can significantly enhance multi-step problem solving. Similar R1-style pipelines in both commercial and open-source models further corroborate that tailoring RL objectives to long-form chain-of-thought and verifiable tasks yields substantial gains in reasoning ability.

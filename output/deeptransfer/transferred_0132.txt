This research focuses on the application of robotics within the construction industry. To demonstrate its effectiveness and validate its performance, the authors systematically evaluate the system against established methodologies through extensive testing. Building upon principles from robot modeling and adhering to construction-specific constraints, this study provides a comparative analysis of LLM-based reasoning and traditional approaches for task allocation in construction robotics. The LTAA framework incorporates capability-aware modeling, phase-adaptive allocation strategies, and validated LLM decision-making to address construction-specific coordination challenges identified in prior work. Through systematic comparison against traditional algorithmic methods, such as Brute Force and Greedy Method, and reinforcement learning approaches, including Q-learning and Deep Q-Network (DQN), this study contributes both a methodologically rigorous LLM coordination system and empirical insights into the comparative effectiveness of reasoning-based versus traditional approaches for construction automation. Section 3 presents the methodology underlying the LTAA framework. Section 4 describes the experimental setup employed in this study. Section 5 presents a comparative analysis of the results against established methodologies, elucidating the advancements achieved by the current approach. Section 6 synthesizes the findings and delineates prospective research directions. The theoretical framework of Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) underpins the coordination of robotic teams through systematic task distribution, taking into account resource constraints, temporal requirements, and the heterogeneity of robot capabilities. Gerkey and Matarić (2004) established a foundational framework for analyzing task allocation problems, focusing on dimensions such as single-task versus multi-task robots, single-robot versus multi-robot tasks, and instantaneous versus time-extended assignments. However, this framework did not fully address the complexities encountered in real-world scenarios, which involve diverse robot capabilities and temporal requirements. Consequently, in 2013, Korsah et al. advanced this framework by incorporating considerations of heterogeneous robot skills, task interdependencies, and dynamic constraints, thereby illustrating the progression of the field towards the utilization of heterogeneous teams for complex task allocations.

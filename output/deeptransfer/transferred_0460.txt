To address this issue, we propose a method to quantify the extent to which a reasoning model exhibits belief entrenchment. Specifically, we define belief entrenchment as a deviation from the Martingale property—a fundamental concept in Bayesian reasoning that posits the unpredictability of belief updates prior to their occurrence. Therefore, if a model's future beliefs can be anticipated solely based on its prior beliefs, it indicates a deviation from adherence to the Martingale property, suggesting an excessive adherence to its existing beliefs. We empirically evaluated this hypothesis through experiments conducted in three domains: event forecasting, opinion-based question answering, and academic paper review. Our analysis encompassed various models and reasoning methodologies, revealing a prevalent tendency among models to exhibit belief entrenchment. Moreover, our empirical analyses indicate that future belief updates are generally predictable from prior beliefs, thereby contravening the Martingale property. Furthermore, we demonstrate that deviations from this property are associated with a decline in model accuracy, as evidenced by the Martingale Score. This metric quantifies the extent to which a model's belief updates can be anticipated based solely on its preceding beliefs. Utilizing this score, we conducted an evaluation of prevalent models and observed a frequent occurrence of excessive adherence to entrenched beliefs across various domains, prompts, and model configurations.

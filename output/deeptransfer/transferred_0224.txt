Proper software for rational decision-making is notably absent. The primary goal of software companies is to sell products that consumers are inclined to purchase, and consumers typically prefer software that fulfills their specific requirements. However, rational decision-making based on rigorous data analysis and effective management of uncertainty often requires high-performance computing resources, such as those used in Monte Carlo sampling methods, which can be financially demanding. Small exploration companies are unlikely to allocate substantial financial resources towards sophisticated cloud computing systems, nor will they employ personnel with the requisite expertise to manage such systems. Moreover, most exploration activities are conducted by junior mining companies, which typically possess one or two prospects and employ a limited number of geoscientists. Funding for these operations is often contingent upon demonstrating some measure of success, such as identifying mineral deposits with a single drill hole. Consequently, the pursuit of additional financial resources for drilling often takes precedence over employing a scientific methodology for decision-making. Disproving a geological hypothesis requires considerable time and effort for comprehensive data acquisition; however, junior mining companies predominantly concentrate on immediate outcomes. Therefore, it is evident that innovative financial mechanisms are required. Relying on the success of a single borehole is akin to investing all resources in a solitary stock, underscoring the need for diversified and strategic financial approaches. In the realm of investment, a diversified portfolio is typically employed to mitigate risk while optimizing returns. Analogously, the mining sector should consider adopting a similar strategy by investing in a portfolio of sites, thereby evaluating the trade-off between risk and anticipated profitability.

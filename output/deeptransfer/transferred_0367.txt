As the use of AI tools powered by large language models (LLMs) continues to grow, ensuring their secure and safe deployment has become a top priority. This need becomes even more critical when these models are implemented in multilingual and multimodal contexts, where diverse linguistic and communicative frameworks may introduce novel risks. Therefore, it is imperative to develop safety systems capable of effectively filtering harmful or unsafe content across a multitude of languages and situational contexts. Without such safeguards, the global deployment of these models could inadvertently marginalize or exclude numerous individuals. Most existing research has concentrated on safety measures in high-resource languages such as English, Chinese, Spanish, German, and French. However, there is a significant lack of data for the majority of other languages, posing challenges in verifying and maintaining safety in multilingual environments. While some evaluations exist to assess the efficacy of these models across various languages, they often lack coverage for low-resource languages and fail to adequately represent the diverse user base. Furthermore, the development of training data for these underrepresented languages, which is crucial for constructing robust safety systems, remains insufficiently addressed. Recent efforts have focused on generating training data for high-resource languages through both authentic and translated datasets; however, these measures fall short in establishing comprehensive safety mechanisms. Moreover, a significant limitation of existing safety systems lies in their substantial computational demands. These frameworks are often excessively large, inefficient, and resource-intensive, making them impractical for deployment on edge devices or in low-power environments. Their prolonged processing times further hinder their applicability in real-world scenarios. These challenges underscore the necessity of developing compact and optimized systems capable of safeguarding both the integrity of the language model and the well-being of the user from potential harm.

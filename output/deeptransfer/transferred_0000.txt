The Long-Tail Hard subset is crafted to challenge advanced language models by differentiating their capabilities in handling complex tasks. This subset is systematically divided into explicit demands, such as Multi-Attribute or Multi-Requirement (MR), and implicit demands. The implicit demands are further categorized into four distinct types: Negation (NE), Alternatives (AL), Knowledge-dependent (KD), and Reasoning-dependent (RD). For NE and AL instances, we employed predefined keywords and syntactic patterns to filter relevant queries. In contrast, KD and RD instances, which are less frequent, were identified by initially filtering out simpler examples. Subsequently, we utilized a high-capacity language model (Qwen3-235B) to ensure consistency by requiring identical responses in at least four out of eight iterations. For multi-attribute requests, a specialized tool was used to pinpoint queries with more than five attributes. To confirm the complexity of these examples, they were evaluated using a baseline model, retaining only those where the model's predictions were correct five or fewer times out of eight attempts, thus indicating significant uncertainty. The Visual Salience subset focuses on scenarios where the primary product image is crucial, especially when visual attributes like color or style are essential for accurate decision-making. Initially, a keyword-based retrieval process, grounded in a comprehensive visual taxonomy, was employed to identify relevant cases. To minimize inaccuracies, two successive filtering mechanisms were applied: first, a predefined blacklist, and second, retaining only query-ad pairs where the critical visual keyword was absent from the product's textual description. Subsequently, a large language model (LLM) validated that the remaining keywords accurately corresponded to visual attributes. Finally, to identify the most challenging examples, a rudimentary model analyzed both textual and visual inputs, retaining instances where the model exhibited significant uncertainty, as evidenced by its frequent inability to consistently determine the correct answer.

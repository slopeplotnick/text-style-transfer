Training robust reward models is challenging because they are subjective and context-dependent, making them difficult to develop (Chakraborty et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023), and learned proxies are prone to reward hacking (Casper et al., 2023). Moreover, the scarcity of annotations further limits the model's ability to manage fine-grained behaviors (Wu et al., 2023). These constraints have motivated various attempts to reduce reliance on annotations. For instance, Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) eliminates explicit reward modeling but still requires preference datasets. Similarly, verifiable rewards (DeepSeek-AI, 2025; Lambert et al., 2024) leverage ground-truth outcomes but are only applicable where automatic verification is feasible. Self-evaluation methods (Yuan et al., 2024; Lee et al., 2024; Garg et al., 2025) also face issues like prompt sensitivity and systematic biases (Zheng et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024). Crucially, all these approaches continue to evaluate quality through external signals, overlooking the information embedded in the model's own representations. However, the validity of a generation should be reflected in its underlying computation. For example, when a model outputs "The capital of France is Paris," its hidden states encode activated factual knowledge (He et al., 2024c); conversely, when it hallucinates, different activation patterns emerge (Chen et al., 2024). This raises a natural question: Can we measure text quality directly from internal signals, enabling LLM alignment without external supervision? In this work, we demonstrate that a simple geometric property of LLM hidden states, their effective dimensionality, provides a reliable signal for LLM response quality. We propose using stable rank (Rudelson & Vershynin, 2007), a matrix-theoretic measure, as an unsupervised proxy for generation quality. Stable rank quantifies how many independent semantic directions a response occupies by measuring the ratio of total variance to dominant-direction variance in the hidden-state representation. It balances representational richness with coherence: a high stable rank indicates that information spreads across many dimensions rather than concentrating in a few. The theoretical motivation comes from two lines of work. First, the softmax bottleneck analysis shows that accurate language modeling requires navigating a high-dimensional semantic manifold, as the contextual probability distribution of natural language is inherently complex.

To compare different model architectures, we selected three families of LLMs for our experiments: GPT, LLAMA, and QWEN. Specifically, the GPT family (Radford et al., 2019; Gao et al., 2020) includes GPT2-small, GPT2-medium, GPT2-large, GPT2-x1, and GPT-neo-2.7B. Similarly, the LLAMA family (Touvron et al., 2023; Dubey et al., 2024) comprises LLAMA-2-7B, LLAMA-3-8B, LLAMA-3.1-8B, LLAMA-3.2-1B, and LLAMA-3.2-3B. Finally, the QWEN family (Qwen, 2024) consists of QWEN2-0.5B, QWEN2-1.5B, and QWEN2-7B. These models are available on the Hugging Face repository, with URLs provided in Appendix A. Moreover, the computational resources required for continual pretraining to develop biased LLMs are detailed in Appendix C, while hyperparameter configurations are outlined in Appendix D. To evaluate social bias in LLMs, we utilize three benchmarks: the Bias Benchmark for Question-Answering (BBQ) (Parrish et al., 2022), the Bias in Open-Ended Language Generation Dataset (BOLD) (Dhamala et al., 2021), and HONEST (Nozza et al., 2021). The URLs for these datasets are listed in Appendix E. BBQ. The BBQ dataset (Parrish et al., 2022) contains approximately 58,000 templated question-answer pairs across nine social dimensions relevant to U.S. English speakers. By contrasting "underspecified" with "fully specified" versions of each question, it assesses the extent to which models rely on stereotypical priors instead of explicit evidence. BOLD. The BOLD dataset (Dhamala et al., 2021) includes 23,679 prompts organized into 43 demographic subgroups covering occupation, gender, race, religion, and political ideology. To ensure that debiasing methods do not negatively impact performance on downstream tasks, we evaluate both the debiased and pre-trained LLMs using the SuperGLUE benchmark, which consists of eight tasks: BoolQ, CB, COPA, MultiRC, ReCoRD, RTE, WiC, and WSC. All evaluations are conducted using the Language Model Evaluation Harness.

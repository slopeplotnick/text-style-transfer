Accordingly, the follow-up experiment utilized the larger datasets of 10,000 noun phrases and sentences generated by Delilah. Table 5 summarizes the lexical statistics for these datasets. As in the previous experiment, the 35 most frequent words in both datasets are ranked in Table 6 and Table 7, respectively. These top 35 words are predominantly function words. In the sample of 10,000 noun phrases, the first unequivocal content word, "forgotten," appears at ranks 54 and 55, sharing its position with the function words "at" or "near." Beyond rank 55, the frequency of content words increases. Similarly, in the analysis of 10,000 sentences generated by Delilah, the most frequent content words are "forgotten" at rank 11, "works" at rank 27, and "in the morning" at rank 66. Beyond rank 66, the presence of content words becomes more pronounced. Notably, Delilah employs the word "masses" as a numeral, a usage that is accurately captured by the language acquisition procedures. For datasets of 10,000 exemplars, a clearer distinction between function and content categories emerges, as words with a frequency exceeding 100 tokens—equivalent to more than 1.7 per mil in the sample of Delilah sentences—are typically classified as function words. Therefore, based on these findings, the MODOMA parameters governing the acquisition of functional and content categories are configured such that word types with a frequency exceeding 2 per mil are designated as functional categories, while all other word types are considered content words. This acquisition process is executed only once after the assimilation of at least 10,000 exemplars. These parameters are set conservatively to minimize the likelihood of erroneously classifying a word as a functional category rather than a content word. Since the default classification is as a content word, each lexical item is treated as a content word unless compelling evidence suggests otherwise. The parameter values derived from this initial experiment have been established as the default settings for MODOMA. However, these parameters remain adjustable in light of subsequent experimental findings and linguistic theoretical considerations.

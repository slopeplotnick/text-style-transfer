KL-free GRPO Algorithm. Figure 11 illustrates our fundamental RL framework. We employ the GRPO algorithm to optimize the objective function, as defined in Equation 13, which maximizes the advantage-weighted log probability expectation. The importance sampling ratio and advantage function are further detailed in Equation 14. In our implementation, we chose to remove the KL divergence regularization term. This decision stems from the observation that, after a sufficient SFT warm-start, the model has already established its reasoning paradigm and structured output format. At this stage, imposing KL constraints would overly restrict the policy exploration space, thereby hindering the model’s ability to discover superior reasoning paths. Consequently, we opted to allow the model to perform freer policy optimization based on its existing capabilities. To achieve our training objectives, we designed a reward mechanism that addresses three key aspects: (1) Format standardization: leveraging special tokens to explicitly separate the reasoning process from the final answer, which facilitates subsequent parsing and application; (2) Output space constraint: strictly confining mismatch types (e.g., "price mismatch" and "functionality mismatch") to a predefined set, thereby preventing the generation of uncontrolled arbitrary outputs; (3) Progressive reward: providing moderate positive rewards when the model correctly identifies relevance but errs at finer-grained levels, mitigating the sparse reward issue during early training stages, which accelerates convergence and stabilizes training. Building upon this, we introduced a three-stage curriculum learning framework to progressively enhance model performance by increasing the complexity of training data. The sample allocation strategy for each stage is outlined in Table 6. This framework is guided by three core principles: (1) Rapid Bootstrap Principle: Stage 1 prioritizes medium-difficulty samples to enable the model to quickly gain effective positive feedback while maintaining a sufficient level of challenge, thus avoiding exploration difficulties caused by sparse rewards during initial training; (2) Progressive Challenge Principle: Stages 2 and 3 gradually increase the proportion of difficult samples, enabling the model to transition from solving common problems to tackling complex edge cases, thereby expanding its capability boundaries; (3) Knowledge Retention Principle: the latter stages consistently incorporate easy samples as "memory anchors" to ensure the model retains previously acquired skills while assimilating new knowledge. These high-confidence samples also contribute to stabilizing gradient variance throughout the training process.

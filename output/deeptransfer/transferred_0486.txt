The integration of robotics in construction holds substantial promise for addressing workforce shortages while simultaneously enhancing safety and efficiency. Robots exhibit exceptional proficiency in executing heavy and repetitive tasks without succumbing to fatigue or errors. Therefore, their deployment can significantly accelerate construction processes and reduce associated costs. However, a solitary robot is incapable of executing all tasks due to the inherent complexity of construction activities. Constructing structures such as walls necessitates a diverse array of skills, including material transportation, precise placement, welding, and quality inspection. Consequently, the collaboration of multiple robots is essential to effectively accomplish these tasks. Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) involves the decomposition of complex tasks into manageable subtasks, which are then assigned to various robots based on their capabilities, task requirements, and environmental conditions. MRTA problems are characterized by dimensions such as single-task versus multi-task robots, single-robot versus multi-robot tasks, and instantaneous versus time-extended assignments. The most challenging aspect involves orchestrating multiple robots to execute numerous tasks over extended periods, necessitating sophisticated planning to accommodate diverse robotic capabilities, task demands, and temporal constraints. Nevertheless, the majority of MRTA research predominantly concentrates on applications within warehouse logistics or environmental exploration, with limited focus on the specific needs of the construction industry.

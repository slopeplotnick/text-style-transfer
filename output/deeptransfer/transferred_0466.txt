Jack Lu*, Ryan Teehan*, Jinran Jin, and Mengye Ren Agentic Learning AI Lab, New York University {yl11330, rst306, jj3007, mengye}@nyu.edu https://agenticlearning.ai/llm-verificationNovember 20, 2025 Large language models (LLMs) serve dual roles as problem solvers and solution verifiers, with the latter significantly enhancing solver performance by selecting high-quality answers from a pool of candidates. However, prior research has predominantly focused on self-verification, with limited exploration of how verifiers assess outputs from models within their own family or across different families. Additionally, while modern LLMs undergo extensive post-training, the impact of this phase on their verification capabilities remains insufficiently understood. In this study, we systematically evaluate 37 models spanning diverse architectures, scales, and training stages—comparing base-trained models with their post-trained counterparts—across nine benchmarks, including logical reasoning, structured puzzles, symbolic computation, mathematics, commonsense reasoning, factual recall, and domain knowledge. We investigate self-verification, intra-family verification, and cross-family verification to understand their relative effectiveness. To support this analysis, we introduce and validate a novel metric, verifier gain, which predicts performance improvements achieved through test-time verifier-based rejection sampling. Furthermore, we examine how verifier gain and false positive rates vary with model size and post-training, while also characterizing the verifiability of different datasets. Our findings reveal that cross-family verification is particularly effective; post-training diminishes self-verification improvements but enhances cross-family verification capabilities. Moreover, mathematical and logical tasks exhibit the highest inherent verifiability. Consequently, the paradigm of problem-solving with LLMs has evolved from merely querying a model for solutions to a system where models both solve and verify. Verification at test time encompasses a spectrum of approaches, ranging from simple strategies, such as generating multiple candidate solutions and filtering them through a verifier (Zhao et al., 2025), to more sophisticated iterative refinement techniques (Madaan et al., 2023). By incorporating generative verification at test time, LLMs can tackle more complex problems than they could independently (Cobbe et al., 2021; Lightman et al., 2024).

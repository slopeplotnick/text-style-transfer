Llama-4 Maverick demonstrates superior performance compared to the Bayesian reliability-weighted unbiased linear combination, a normative baseline extensively utilized in biological studies and employed by humans. This enhanced performance suggests that Llama-4 Maverick may leverage additional mechanisms beyond those typically encompassed within conventional approaches. To further investigate, we analyzed the multimodal responses of Llama-4 Maverick in comparison to its unimodal responses (Figure 7). Our findings reveal that nonlinear approaches, such as random forest models, more effectively capture the complexity of multimodal responses than linear methods. However, in scenarios characterized by noise, Bayesian methodologies remain an optimal benchmark, providing a robust framework for improving language models with suboptimal capabilities. Notably, Figure 8 illustrates that models with higher accuracy tend to exhibit behavior more consistent with Bayesian methodologies. While Gemma 3 4B and Phi 4 Multimodal display comparatively lower accuracy, they nonetheless achieve satisfactory Bayesian Consistency Scores (BCS). A detailed analysis of task-specific and model-specific performance is provided in Appendix A.9. Importantly, our investigation reveals that language models exhibit diverse behavioral patterns when tasked with challenges inspired by human perceptual processes. Advanced models, such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and Llama-4 Maverick, demonstrate a pronounced tendency to employ Bayesian methodologies, particularly in image processing tasks (see Appendix A.11 for further details). These models adaptively respond to variations such as noise or additional contextual information and effectively utilize prior knowledge without explicit training for such tasks. This behavior parallels the human brain, where Bayesian models account for a wide range of perceptual phenomena and cognitive processes without explicit training (Petzschner et al., 2015; Knill & Pouget, 2004). However, it is important to note that this analysis is limited to behavioral observations; a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms or causal implementations of such behavior requires further investigation.

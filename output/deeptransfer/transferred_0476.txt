Other promising prompt optimizers not yet implemented in promptsolution include GEPA (Agrawal et al., 2025), TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al., 2025), MIPRO (Opsahl-Ong et al., 2024), and PromptWizard (Agarwal et al., 2024). Most of these tools are currently confined to research environments, which are challenging to utilize due to infrequent updates, inadequate documentation, and insufficient testing. Consequently, they present difficulties for both scientific benchmark experiments with other optimizers and practical use cases with specific requirements. In the prompt optimization landscape, numerous actively maintained libraries and tools have emerged, encompassing both open-source and commercial solutions. Some open-source libraries are tailored to specific optimizers, such as TextGrad, AdalFlow, Microsoft's PromptWizard, and PromptIM, each employing distinct methodologies. The prompt-ops library, while supporting two optimizers, primarily focuses on prompt optimization for Llama models. In contrast, promptsolution serves as a comprehensive framework that facilitates the utilization of various optimizers across any large language model (LLM). Among the prominent tools for constructing LLM configurations is DSPy, which incorporates a dedicated module for prompt optimization. This toolset includes optimizers such as MIPROv2 and GEPA and can seamlessly integrate LLM training with prompt optimization. While DSPy serves as a general-purpose framework for constructing AI programs, promptolution is specifically designed to facilitate prompt optimization, making it particularly suited for researchers and experts conducting in-depth investigations. Additionally, promptomatix, a derivative of DSPy, offers a more streamlined approach and incorporates its own methodologies for prompt optimization.

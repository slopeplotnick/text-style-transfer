To this end, we evaluate our framework on four simulation benchmarks comprising 64 tasks and 5 real-world tasks, focusing on three types of VLA policies. Specifically, we assess our test-time scaling framework on the Simpler, Libero, Robotwin1.0, and Robotwin2.0 benchmarks. Simpler is a real-to-sim benchmark built upon the SAPIEN simulator and the ManiSkill2 benchmark, offering simulated task environments for both the WindowX and Google Robot platforms; in this study, we primarily utilize the tasks designed for WindowX. Libero serves as a benchmark for lifelong learning in decision-making (LLDM) and consists of four task suites. Notably, we concentrate on the most challenging suite, as prior works have already achieved near-perfect success rates on the others. Robotwin1.0 and Robotwin2.0 emphasize dual-arm gripper manipulation, featuring diverse assets and task types, while also providing scripted policies for automated data collection. For our real-world experimental setup, we utilize a REALMAN75 dual-arm robot situated in an office-like environment. This setup encompasses five distinct tasks involving common objects such as books, pens, a cabinet, a charger, a laptop, and a paper ball. These tasks are defined as follows: Receive Book, where the robot receives a book handed over by a human and places it back on the bookshelf; Storage Charger, where it picks up the charger head, places it inside the cabinet, and closes the cabinet door; Paper and Pen, where it picks up the paper ball and the pen and places them into the trash bin and pen holder, respectively; Laptop, where it closes the laptop lid, unplugs the charger from the socket, and stores it back in the cabinet; and Pick Books, where the left and right arms each pick up and lift a book simultaneously.

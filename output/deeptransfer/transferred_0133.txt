Test-time learning (TTL) represents a promising paradigm that enables models to adapt during inference. Early approaches primarily emphasized lightweight adaptation techniques that avoided parameter updates, such as entropy minimization or batch normalization (Wang et al., 2020; Niu et al., 2022). More recent advancements have extended these ideas to test-time training (TTT), where explicit parameter updates occur during inference, significantly enhancing task-specific adaptability. For instance, Akyurek et al. (2024) demonstrate that test-time training improves few-shot learning under distribution shifts, while Hübotter et al. (2024) propose active fine-tuning methods that selectively adapt large language models using informative feedback signals. Within this evolving TTT framework, an emerging direction is test-time policy optimization, which reframes inference as a reinforcement learning (RL) problem. Methods such as RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR) (Wang et al., 2025) and TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025) leverage consensus-based feedback mechanisms, such as majority voting, to guide policy updates. By aggregating insights from multiple rollouts, these approaches construct label-free rewards, effectively balancing reasoning consistency and correctness. Nevertheless, a critical challenge persists: rollout inefficiency. Conventional test-time RL methods often require a fixed, large number of rollouts per query to robustly estimate rewards, which is computationally wasteful. While straightforward queries quickly achieve consensus, complex ones demand extensive sampling, making the process inefficient and costly. To address this limitation, we introduce Optimal Rollout Allocation for Test-time Policy Optimization (OptPO), a general framework designed to optimize the efficiency-accuracy trade-off. The core innovation of OptPO is to reframe reward estimation as a sequential hypothesis testing problem. Rather than committing to a fixed sample budget, OptPO adaptively estimates the vote distribution using a Bayesian Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT), halting sampling as soon as posterior evidence satisfies a user-defined error tolerance. The accumulated rollouts are then immediately repurposed for policy optimization, employing methods such as PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) or GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), ensuring computational resources are allocated only where necessary to reduce uncertainty. Moreover, OptPO is broadly compatible with supervised fine-tuning methodologies for test-time training, further enhancing its versatility.

In this section, we illustrate the explanatory process outlined above through a specific example using the CelebA dataset [Liu et al., 2015]. This dataset comprises over 200,000 images of celebrities' faces, each annotated with binary attributes describing facial features and accessories, such as whether the individual is smiling, wearing glasses, appears young, or is perceived as attractive. We will examine two tasks: determining whether an individual depicted in an image is young and assessing their level of attractiveness. We next turn to the images in Figure 6. Starting with the original image on the left, the figure illustrates various counterfactual versions of the image, all aligned with the intervention do(Young = 0), which modifies the individual's age attribute to not young. The initial counterfactual image was generated utilizing a particular methodology. While XAI systems that solely rely on visual assessment can be misleading, saliency maps remain a frequently employed technique for attribution-based explanations of image classification systems. Therefore, we compare our causal concept-based explanations with those derived from Grad-CAM, employing the implementation by Gildenblat. Consider first the results in Figure 7, which elucidate the rationale behind the classification of the two images on the far left of Figure 5 as young. For the male subject, the chin region played a pivotal role in the classification process, whereas for the female subject, the area surrounding the eyes was deemed most significant. Subsequently, Figure 8 examines the classification of the individual depicted in Figure 6. The initial image was accurately classified as young, with the Grad-CAM analysis indicating that the classifier concentrated on the area surrounding the eyes to reach this determination. Conversely, for the image on the right, which was erroneously classified, the Grad-CAM analysis reveals that the classifier focused on the forehead and chin. Grad-CAM provides non-causal explanations and, as such, cannot elucidate the potential outcomes of alterations within the input. Instead, it identifies the regions of the image that are most influential in the classifier's decision-making process. Moreover, since Grad-CAM explanations are inherently pixel-based, they fail to offer insights into the underlying reasons why the highlighted regions are deemed significant.

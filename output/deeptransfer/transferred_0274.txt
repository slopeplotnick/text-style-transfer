Micro-level phenomena emerge when interactions occur within narrowly defined and transient topologies, where early signs of local opacity and divergence begin to surface. These interactions, typically involving small groups of agents or systems, reveal the fragility of shared understanding even before large-scale coordination is established. Although these risks are localized, they are inherently multiplicative. Once a deviation becomes embedded within an agent's representation, subsequent exchanges replicate and amplify it, leading to a nonlinear accumulation of risks. The number of distinct issues grows faster than the number of agents or interactions, setting the stage for broader systemic vulnerabilities. Meso-level risks arise as clusters of agents—ranging from dozens to hundreds—exchange information across partially connected networks. At this scale, no single failure is immediately apparent; instead, the interaction patterns themselves lead to a degradation in accuracy, diversity, or coherence. While each agent may appear locally correct, the collective behavior deviates from expected norms, demonstrating that systemic issues can emerge without any overtly malfunctioning components. These failures stem from interference patterns in the interactions among partially correct agents, rather than isolated errors. As these dynamics unfold, local predictability begins to erode, giving way to systemic instability. Macro-level pathologies manifest when all three dimensions—topology density, opacity, and divergence—reach critical thresholds, resulting in collective behaviors that operate independently of initial design intentions. This taxonomy, while conceptual and preliminary, provides a foundational framework for observing and discussing these phenomena. Without such a framework, researchers lack the necessary categories to identify, measure, and regulate systemic behaviors within collective systems. To validate this taxonomy, extensive experimental investigations under controlled conditions are required, which we aim to pursue in future studies.

Micro-level phenomena become evident when small groups of individuals or systems engage in closely-knit and transient interactions, leading to the emergence of minor issues. These micro-level risks manifest within direct interactions among a limited number of individuals or systems, typically two to five, where initial indicators of broader systemic issues begin to surface. These risks highlight the fragility of shared understanding, even prior to the large-scale coordination of a network. Although these issues originate at a micro level, they possess the potential to evolve into more significant challenges as interactions persist. As a problem becomes integrated into the system's comprehension, its complexity amplifies with each subsequent interaction. The proliferation of issues occurs at a rate surpassing the growth of participants or communicative exchanges. Therefore, meso-level risks arise as these groups exchange information within partially connected networks. At this level, no singular issue is immediately apparent; however, the manner in which interactions occur leads to a deterioration in aspects such as accuracy and diversity. While each component may appear satisfactory in isolation, their collective operation fails to meet anticipated outcomes. These broader risks underscore the potential for complications to arise even in the absence of overt failures. Instabilities arise not from isolated errors but from the interaction dynamics of partially correct components. It is within these interactions that local predictability begins to deteriorate, leading to systemic instability. When the network becomes fully interconnected and these issues escalate to a critical threshold, macro-level pathologies occur, resulting in significant alterations in collective behavior that deviate from the initial design intentions. This conceptual framework serves as a preliminary model to facilitate discourse and analysis of these behaviors. Without such a framework, researchers encounter challenges in identifying, quantifying, or regulating these behaviors within groups. To substantiate this model, extensive experimentation in controlled environments is requisite, which we intend to undertake in subsequent studies.

An empirical survey was conducted on seven model merging algorithms for social bias mitigation, utilizing three bias benchmarks and the SuperGLUE framework across 13 large language models. SLERP with moderate interpolation weights was identified as the most balanced method, achieving effective bias reduction without sacrificing downstream accuracy. This underscores the necessity of verifying performance on tasks such as reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning for effective social bias mitigation. Recently, model merging has emerged as an effective strategy for combining the strengths of multiple models without expensive retraining (Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2024). This approach involves fusing two or more trained model parameters to produce a single model that retains and integrates knowledge or skills from all sources. The linear averaging method, introduced by Wortsman et al. (2022), pioneered model merging by treating weights as vectors and merging them through arithmetic means. It offers a cost-effective way to incorporate diverse expertise by leveraging existing fine-tuned models without additional training. Moreover, studies by Matena and Raffel (2022) and Lee et al. (2025) generalize this idea by weighting each parameter inversely to its Fisher information, resulting in combinations consistent with likelihood. Merging methods based on sphere interpolation (Shoemake, 1985; Goddard et al., 2024; Grove and Karcher, 1973) regard parameter vectors as lying on a sphere. SLERP (Shoemake, 1985) performs an interpolation between two models, while the Karcher Mean (Grove and Karcher, 1973) iteratively finds the Riemannian centroid for any number of models. Additionally, NuSLERP (Goddard et al., 2024) adds per-tensor normalization to correct for norm drift. Inspired by these model merging approaches, Ilharco et al. (2022) proposed the task arithmetic approach under the concept of "task vector," where task vectors represent the parameters of the difference between a pre-trained LLM and a fine-tuned LLM.

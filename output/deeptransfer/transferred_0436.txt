Rapidly reviewing extensive document collections to generate concise summaries and develop query systems is essential for decision-making within relevant timeframes. Furthermore, identifying patterns in high-dimensional multivariate and spatial datasets plays a critical role in enhancing analytical capabilities. To avoid cognitive bias and achieve realistic uncertainty quantification, it is necessary to make rational decisions even in the presence of uncertainty. The most significant potential lies in AI systems that interact with human experts and apply advanced data science techniques to analyze high-dimensional space-time data. These systems can generate plausible hypotheses regarding mineralization, its extent, and grade, which are decision-ready for further data acquisition. Importantly, these hypotheses must be falsifiable, requiring the specification of data or outcomes that could refute them, thereby mitigating cognitive bias. Decision readiness is paramount, as deterministic mapping alone does not suffice. The foundation of such systems is built upon robust data science tools. As emphasized, the primary challenge is not the selection of machine learning algorithms but the meticulous curation and comprehensive analysis of data to formulate hypotheses. Traditionally, geologists focus on established indicators of mineralization. For instance, in exploring massive nickel sulfide deposits, scatterplots of MgO and Ni concentrations are commonly used to assess the likelihood of sulfide mineralization. While these bivariate analyses, occasionally supplemented with additional variables, have proven effective, their utility can be significantly enhanced through data science and AI methodologies. These advanced approaches can uncover previously unrecognized high-dimensional relationships, offering deeper insights into mineralization processes. Moreover, the development and application of AI-enabled tools are instrumental in assisting domain experts. For example, they facilitate the detection of anomalies in high-dimensional geological and geochemical datasets, as well as the analysis of complex patterns in geophysical data to enable comparisons with known deposits. Non-linear adaptations of fundamental data science techniques, such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Multidimensional Scaling (MDS), and Factor Analysis (FA), further enhance analytical precision. Spatial clustering techniques are employed to detect intricate spatial relationships, while advanced geostatistical methods generate complex surfaces constrained by diverse datasets. Additionally, there is a strong emphasis on falsification rather than mere data fitting, alongside the implementation of multi-physics stochastic inversion techniques across varying spatial scales.

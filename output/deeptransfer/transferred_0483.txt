To assess the feasibility of LLM-driven reasoning in addressing MRTA problems, we first replicated the SMART-LLM framework (Kannan et al. 2024) as a baseline study. This replication facilitated the identification of fundamental challenges in LLM-based coordination, which informed the development of our enhanced framework. The evaluation employed the SMART-LLM benchmark dataset, consisting of 36 tasks across four complexity categories: Elemental (single-action tasks), Simple (multiple objects with homogeneous robots), Compound (heterogeneous robots with specialized skills), and Complex (requiring team formation and collaborative execution). These tasks were executed in the AI2-THOR simulation environment. For the LLM configuration, Claude 4 Sonnet served as the reasoning engine, with the temperature set to 0.0 to ensure deterministic outputs and reproducible decision-making processes. Following the SMART-LLM methodology, we assessed performance using five metrics: Success Rate (SR), Task Completion Rate (TCR), Goal Condition Recall (GCR), Executability (EXE), and Robot Utilization (RU). These metrics collectively measure task completion accuracy and the efficiency of multi-robot coordination. Upon identifying validation inconsistencies and issues with LLM output reliability during the SMART-LLM replication, we developed and evaluated the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture. This evaluation demonstrated that structured validation and hierarchical retry mechanisms could effectively address these challenges, thereby establishing the feasibility of LLMs for MRTA applications. The Self Corrective-Agent Architecture introduced multi-stage validation processes augmented by hierarchical retry mechanisms. Key configuration parameters included a maximum of three local retries per agent, validation checkpoints at each procedural stage (task decomposition, task allocation, and code generation), and structured feedback loops designed to facilitate iterative refinement and continuous improvement.

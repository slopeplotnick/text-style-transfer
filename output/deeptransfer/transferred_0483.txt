To evaluate the feasibility of using LLM-driven reasoning for addressing MRTA problems, we initially replicated the SMART-LLM framework (Kannan et al. 2024) as a baseline study. This replication was instrumental in identifying fundamental challenges associated with LLM-based coordination, which informed the development of our enhanced framework. The evaluation employed the SMART-LLM benchmark dataset, comprising 36 tasks categorized into four complexity levels: Elemental (single-action tasks), Simple (involving multiple objects with homogeneous robots), Compound (featuring heterogeneous robots with specialized skills), and Complex (necessitating team formation and collaborative execution). These tasks were executed within the AI2-THOR simulation environment. For the LLM configuration, Claude 4 Sonnet was used as the reasoning engine, with the temperature parameter set to 0.0 to ensure deterministic outputs and reproducible decision-making processes. In evaluating performance, we adhered to the SMART-LLM methodology, employing five metrics: Success Rate (SR), Task Completion Rate (TCR), Goal Condition Recall (GCR), Executability (EXE), and Robot Utilization (RU). These metrics collectively assess task completion accuracy and the efficiency of multi-robot coordination. Upon identifying validation inconsistencies and issues with LLM output reliability during the SMART-LLM replication, we developed and assessed the Self Corrective-Agent Architecture. This evaluation demonstrated that structured validation and hierarchical retry mechanisms could effectively address these challenges, thereby establishing the feasibility of LLMs for MRTA applications. The Self Corrective-Agent Architecture incorporated multi-stage validation processes augmented by hierarchical retry mechanisms. Critical configuration parameters included a maximum of three local retries per agent, validation checkpoints implemented at each procedural stage (task decomposition, task allocation, and code generation), and structured feedback loops designed to facilitate iterative refinement.

This reliability is paramount for interpretability; inconsistent or erratic results would impede our understanding of interconnected phenomena. These results substantiate that our methodology effectively identifies training images that are not only visually or textually analogous but also semantically congruent with the generated outputs. In summary, the data corroborates that our approach to automatically constructing knowledge graphs (KGs) facilitates a transparent and interpretable framework for discerning the influence of training data on model outputs. This advancement is pivotal for enhancing the explainability of models, auditing for copyright compliance, and increasing dataset transparency. For instance, when a generative model is prompted to produce an image in a specific artistic style, such as "Ghibli-style," the process does not entail the replication of exact images from its training data. Rather, the model assimilates the overarching aesthetic and visual attributes characteristic of the style from a curated dataset. These attributes are encoded within the model's parameters and subsequently utilized to synthesize novel images, without reproducing the original training images directly. Consequently, it is challenging to precisely identify which specific training images have influenced the generated output. The influence manifests at a broader level of abstraction, encompassing shared concepts such as textures, thematic elements, and compositional structures. Therefore, to ascertain the manner in which a particular style informs the model, it is necessary to analyze overarching patterns and semantic relationships rather than attempting to directly correlate individual objects or features. Building upon the previously established methodology for automatic knowledge graph (KG) generation, our experimental approach commenced with the extraction of frames from iconic Studio Ghibli films. Each frame was systematically annotated using a domain-specific ontology, facilitating the decomposition of visual content into structured semantic triples. These components were utilized to construct a "Ghibli World" knowledge graph that encapsulates the primary elements of the studio's visual style. Although the dataset comprises frames from only ten of their films, it nonetheless provides a comprehensive representation of their stylistic characteristics.

LLaMA-Factory primarily focuses on text-generation tasks and does not explore the possibility of transforming these tasks into classification problems. In contrast, our framework, PEFT-FACTORY, addresses the limited availability of PEFT methods and enables the fine-tuning of large language models for classification tasks. While alternative frameworks for training large language models exist, they often lack the user-friendliness of LLaMA-Factory but offer distinct advantages. For instance, FastChat (Zheng et al., 2023) is a specialized framework tailored for training LLMs specifically for chat-completion tasks. LitGPT (AI, 2023) and LMFlow (Diao et al., 2023) are versatile and user-friendly frameworks that support a wide range of generative models and training methodologies. Similarly, Axolotl (Axolotl maintainers and contributors, 2023) is a command-line tool that facilitates efficient post-training of large models while preserving their functionality and scalability. Moreover, Open-Instruct (Wang et al., 2023a) focuses on instruction fine-tuning for LLMs and offers multiple models and methodologies for this purpose. H2O LLM Studio<sup>1</sup> serves as a comprehensive, enterprise-oriented tool that includes a graphical interface for developing and deploying large language models. GPT4All (Anand et al., 2023) enhances the training efficiency of LLaMA models through its user-friendly interface. Additionally, ColossalAI (Li et al., 2023) is designed to support fine-tuning across multiple nodes in distributed computing environments. On the other hand, LLaMA-Adapter (Zhang et al., 2023) and LLaMA-Accessory (Gao et al., 2023) are lightweight frameworks, with LLaMA-Adapter integrating trainable adapters into LLaMA models and LLaMA-Accessory providing a comprehensive toolkit for LLM development. Notably, LLaMA-Adapter is often implemented in frameworks such as LitGPT. Based on our analysis of related frameworks, we have identified three key features that are either absent or underdeveloped in existing research, which our work seeks to address: the training of large-scale models using techniques beyond reparametrization-based PEFT methods; the modular and dynamic integration of novel PEFT methods; and the facilitation of training and evaluation of large models for classification tasks.

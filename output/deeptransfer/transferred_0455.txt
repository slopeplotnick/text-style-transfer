Our large-scale experiments, conducted using UniFact across diverse datasets, model families (e.g., LLaMA, Qwen), and factuality detection methods, yield several crucial insights that directly address our research questions. First, addressing RQ1, we find that no single approach is universally superior; performance is highly contingent upon the specific characteristics of the underlying model and the nature of the task. Second, and most critically, addressing RQ2, our analysis provides robust evidence indicating that the integration of diverse methodologies yields synergistic benefits. The HD and FV methods demonstrate efficacy on distinct subsets of factual inaccuracies, thereby capturing complementary aspects of factuality. Finally, concerning RQ3, building on this discovery, we establish that hybrid approaches, which amalgamate both methodologies, consistently surpass the performance of individual methods, thereby setting a new benchmark in factual error detection. The implications of these findings are twofold. For the research community, they necessitate a collaborative approach rather than isolated efforts, motivating a shift toward a unified research agenda that embraces this synergy. For practitioners, our results underscore the importance of integrating model-based signals with evidence-based reasoning to ensure factual accuracy. In summary, this paper makes the following key contributions: analytically, we delineate the conceptual and methodological distinctions between Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification, emphasizing their implications for advancements in AI; methodologically, we introduce UniFact as a framework for equitable comparison of HD and FV; empirically, we demonstrate through extensive studies that, while distinct, HD and FV are complementary; practically, we establish that hybrid methodologies surpass the efficacy of singular approaches. Furthermore, we elucidate the definitions of Fact Verification and Hallucination Detection. Despite frequent conflation of these terms, it is imperative to discern their distinctions. FV evaluates the semantic truthfulness of text in a general context, whereas HD assesses the factual accuracy of text produced by a model. These concepts are elaborated in sections 2.1 and 2.2, with a unified approach to their evaluation metrics presented in section 2.3.

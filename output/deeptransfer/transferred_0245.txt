Dataset licensing is crucial for ensuring both broad usability and ethical access, particularly for low-resource languages. Standard open licenses, such as Creative Commons (e.g., CC BY 4.0), have significantly facilitated large-scale data reuse, with initiatives like Mozilla Common Voice illustrating the transformative potential of permissive licensing in advancing language technology development. Nonetheless, access restrictions to AI development resources remain a significant barrier for many researchers in low- and middle-income countries, highlighting broader concerns about equitable data governance and the empowerment of local communities. In the Swivuriso project, we adopted the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) license to promote open access while upholding ethical accountability. The dataset is distributed via Zenodo and Hugging Face, accompanied by persistent DOIs and detailed documentation. Each release includes audio recordings, aligned transcripts, and metadata capturing demographic, linguistic, and accessibility attributes. To further safeguard ethical use, we provide a statement explicitly prohibiting practices such as voice cloning to protect speaker privacy. The CC BY 4.0 license is highly permissive, allowing users to share, adapt, and utilize the dataset for any purpose, including commercial applications, as long as proper attribution is given and modifications are clearly indicated. Additionally, the license prohibits the imposition of further legal or technical restrictions, encompasses database rights, and includes a "cure period" for addressing violations, ensuring legal clarity and fostering global scientific collaboration. However, while open licenses are instrumental in enhancing data accessibility and utility, they often overlook critical issues such as local governance and the protection of Indigenous knowledge. The standardized application of these licenses can unintentionally undermine community ownership, particularly for cultural heritage or community-driven datasets. As a result, there is a growing shift toward more context-sensitive frameworks that emphasize stewardship, equitable benefit-sharing, and community empowerment—an approach increasingly gaining traction in African data governance.

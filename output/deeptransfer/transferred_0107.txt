The advent of LLMs has introduced a paradigm shift in event-driven prediction. Unlike traditional approaches, LLMs can extract comprehensive event structures—including attributes such as type, participants, and temporal markers—directly from unstructured text. This capability preserves information integrity, supports causal inference, and provides transparent, evidence-based reasoning. However, current research has yet to fully capitalize on the potential of LLMs. For example, methodologies like CAMEF[25] utilize LLMs to extract event details but subsequently compress these structured events into low-dimensional representations for processing by conventional neural networks. This approach reduces LLMs to sophisticated feature extractors, resulting in the significant loss of event-specific details, the absence of explicit modeling of temporal and causal relationships, and the inability to establish a reusable or traceable memory framework. In contrast, our methodology takes a fundamentally different approach. Rather than condensing rich event structures into simplified embeddings, we use them as foundational units to construct a structured and coherent event memory system. By systematically tracking, integrating, and sequencing events, our framework enables direct reasoning about the temporal evolution of events and facilitates the identification of recurring patterns across diverse contexts. This directly addresses the limitations of existing LLM-based methodologies in linking event sequences and uncovering cross-cycle patterns, which forms the central thesis of our research. To this end, we propose a system designed to analyze events for stock price prediction. The system dynamically constructs a comprehensive knowledge base of events derived from news corpora and iteratively refines it through model feedback. Consequently, it can retrieve and reason from historically analogous event patterns.

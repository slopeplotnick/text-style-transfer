The emergence of LLMs has facilitated a transformation in event-driven prediction methodologies. Unlike conventional approaches, LLMs can extract comprehensive event structures—including attributes such as type, participants, and temporal context—directly from unstructured text. This capability preserves information integrity, thereby supporting causal inference and providing transparent evidence for reasoning. However, current research has not fully harnessed the transformative potential of LLMs. For instance, methodologies such as CAMEF[25] employ LLMs for event extraction but subsequently compress these structured events into low-dimensional vectors for processing by conventional neural networks, effectively relegating LLMs to the role of sophisticated feature extractors. Consequently, this process results in a significant loss of event details during vectorization, fails to explicitly model the interconnections and temporal dynamics of events, and does not establish a traceable and reusable experiential memory system. Our methodology diverges fundamentally from these approaches. Rather than compressing detailed event information into reduced data representations, we utilize these details as the foundational elements for constructing a structured event memory. By systematically tracking, integrating, and organizing events, our approach facilitates a comprehensive understanding of temporal event evolution. This addresses the limitations inherent in current LLM methodologies regarding the association of event sequences and the identification of recurring patterns, which constitutes the central focus of our research. We have developed a system to facilitate the analysis of events for the purpose of forecasting stock prices. This system constructs a comprehensive knowledge base of events derived from news corpora, which is subsequently refined through model feedback. Such enhancements enable the system to identify and utilize historically analogous event patterns effectively.

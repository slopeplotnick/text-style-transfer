Despite the growing demand for mental health support, significant gaps in diagnosis persist globally. Barriers such as limited clinic hours, geographic inaccessibility, and workforce shortages continue to hinder timely assessments and the development of early self-awareness regarding mental health conditions (World Health Organization, 2025b). Furthermore, structural, systemic, and individual-level obstacles exacerbate delays in diagnosis for individuals with serious mental illnesses (Wiesepape et al., 2025). This persistent mismatch between rising demand and constrained resources highlights the limitations of traditional diagnostic models (Stein et al., 2022). Specifically, the reliance on in-person sessions and self-reported questionnaires remains insufficient to meet the growing need, as these methods are hampered by workforce shortages (Organization, 2025; Brahmbhatt & Schpero, 2024), high costs (Patel et al., 2018), geographic disparities (Yang & Zhang, 2025), and the stigma surrounding mental health issues (Clement et al., 2015). As a result, wait times for mental health services can range from 3 to 18 months, depending on regional factors and provider availability (McMahan et al., 2022; Yang & Zhang, 2025; Patel et al., 2018). Therefore, the need for early detection and real-time monitoring underscores the importance of developing more efficient methods to identify early signs of mental health concerns and deliver timely interventions (Patel et al., 2018). Portable, resource-efficient solutions, such as small language models, offer a scalable approach to mental health detection and intervention. The emergence of on-device technologies, particularly smartphones, provides a promising avenue for early identification through passive, real-time analysis of social media content and applications. By leveraging linguistic data from social media, these systems enable unobtrusive monitoring of psychological states, facilitating the early detection of mood changes, stress, or suicide risk. For instance, such technologies play a critical role in suicide prevention (Areán et al., 2016) and in identifying patterns of social media usage relevant to mental health (Hamilton et al., 2025). Moreover, by operating locally on personal devices, these models preserve user privacy while analyzing language inputs for signs of depression or stress (Shin et al., 2023). Consequently, on-device deployment enables scalable, privacy-preserving mental health monitoring, extending detection beyond clinical settings and improving accessibility for a broader population.

Our findings about the influence of attitudes, alongside the qualitatively identified themes such as ethical considerations and functional applications, suggest that music perception extends beyond aesthetic measures. Therefore, future research should prioritize the development of instruments specifically tailored to AI-generated music and adopt multi-factor rating scales to capture its complexity. As AI-generated music becomes increasingly prevalent on streaming platforms, it is crucial to address socioeconomic concerns, particularly issues of fairness and transparency surrounding its creation and distribution. Moreover, given the significant impact of attitudes toward AI on music appreciation and emotional responses, experimental designs could explore how information about the creation and distribution processes influences listener perceptions. For instance, transparency regarding the training datasets or the allocation of revenue from AI-generated music could serve as experimental conditions. Furthermore, the diverse contexts of production and usage warrant closer examination. For example, the perception of songs created collaboratively by human and AI systems is an emerging area of interest, especially as advancements in text-to-music systems increasingly enable co-creative processes. Additionally, building on the work of Moura and Maw (2021), future studies could investigate the perception and acceptance of AI-generated music in functional use cases. Notably, our findings underscore that attitudes toward AI play a central role in shaping how listeners evaluate AI-generated music, surpassing factors such as composer identity, genre, or individual listening habits. Looking ahead, it will be essential to monitor the evolution of public attitudes toward artificial intelligence, as these perceptions will likely influence both acceptance and resistance in cultural domains like music. More broadly, the discourse extends beyond whether AI-generated music can be enjoyed or recognized as human-like; it raises fundamental questions about what constitutes "authentic" and "valuable" creativity. By critically engaging with audience responses, we can address pivotal questions such as: What forms of (artificial) creativity should be acknowledged, and under what conditions?

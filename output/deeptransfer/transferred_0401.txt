For each of the three metrics, we compute the values for all unordered pairs of methods within the HD paradigm, within the FV paradigm, and across both paradigms. We then calculate the average for each group and present the resulting scores in Table 2. These scores provide quantitative evidence that the HD and FV paradigms exhibit complementary strengths. Notably, the higher ACS score for cross-paradigm pairs suggests that these methods achieve success in largely distinct areas of the problem space, unlike methods within the same paradigm, which tend to overlap. Furthermore, the superior ASG score demonstrates that combining methods from both paradigms yields greater benefits than relying solely on intra-paradigm combinations. Most significantly, the AECR score shows a marked increase for cross-paradigm pairs, indicating that the two paradigms have distinct failure modes: when one paradigm struggles, the other is more likely to succeed. Collectively, these findings underscore that HD and FV are not just different but genuinely complementary, providing a strong rationale for developing hybrid models that integrate both paradigms. While the previous section established the statistical complementarity between HD and FV, this section delves into the underlying factors driving their divergent performance. To explore this, we conduct a detailed case study to identify the specific failure patterns of each paradigm. Our analysis reveals two primary challenges: FV often falters due to its dependence on retrieval quality, leading to errors when relevant evidence is unavailable, whereas HD is prone to false positives caused by its misinterpretation of diverse phrasings as hallucinations. Understanding these distinct failure patterns clarifies the complementary dynamics between the two paradigms and motivates the development of integrative methodologies, which will be discussed in the following section.

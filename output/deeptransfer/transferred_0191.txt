Language models possess the capability to refine and propose modifications to ambiguously phrased questions, thereby significantly influencing respondents' answers by reducing errors and misunderstandings. Moreover, they can assist in categorizing questions appropriately. By leveraging the semantic understanding of the questions, language models aid researchers in minimizing errors, which in turn enables them to discern the interrelationships among various categories. If categories exhibit excessive similarity, substantial modifications may be warranted. Additionally, language models can standardize the reading level of the text, thereby enhancing its comprehensibility. For further information regarding the application of language models in the development of these tests, refer to the recent study conducted by Beghetto et al. This investigation explores the utilization of language models to generate synthetic data, potentially reducing the time and financial resources typically expended on testing by initially conducting trials on simulated cohorts. Consequently, this development could reduce costs for researchers in the creation of assessments and minimize the production of superfluous ones. In our study, we evaluated the efficacy of contemporary language models in generating data that aligns with the structures employed in the formulation of these assessments. Subsequently, we delineate the methodology employed in our research and the rationale underpinning our hypothesis. In conducting these tests, we predominantly adhered to the methodologies outlined in Classic Test Theory, as well as the recommendations provided by Boateng et al.

Our approach integrates seamlessly with contemporary high-throughput inference frameworks, such as vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023) and SGLang (Zheng et al., 2024), supporting both real-time serving and offline batch processing. In Section 4.2, we primarily utilize the two-stage vLLM pipeline for controlled experiments and methodological variations, as it combines high computational efficiency with effective management of reasoning traces. THINKMERGE is evaluated across two distinct operational paradigms. For tasks with a singular correct response, such as AIME 2025 (MAA, 2025) and GPQA Diamond (Rein et al., 2024), we synthesize multiple responses using majority voting. In contrast, for tasks without a definitive correct answer, we assess LiveCodeBench v5 (2024.10–2025.02) (Jain et al., 2024), BrowseComp-en (Wei et al., 2025), BrowseComp-zh (Zhou et al., 2025), GAIA (Mialon et al., 2023), and xBench-DeepSearch (Chen et al., 2025a), focusing on the development of a singular coherent solution. Specifically, for tasks with a singular correct response, we conduct five trials and evaluate models such as Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-14B (Yang et al., 2025a), and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (Guo et al., 2025). Meanwhile, for open-ended coding tasks, we employ models including DeepCoder-14B-Preview (Luo et al., 2025), Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-Coder-30A3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Thinking (0725), and Qwen3-Think-30A3B (Yang et al., 2025a). Furthermore, for open-ended deep research agent tasks, we utilize WebSailor models—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B (Li et al., 2025a)—with a maximum sequence length of 32,768 tokens, and detailed sampling hyperparameters outlined in Appendix A.1. To explore THINKMERGE’s applicability in research-oriented tasks requiring reasoning and tool integration, we evaluate three Tongyi-WebSailor models—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B—across four rigorous web-based benchmarks: BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and XbenchDeepSearch. BrowseComp-en and BrowseComp-zh address complex, multi-hop factual queries in English and Chinese, respectively. Given the substantial size of BrowseComp-en, comprising 1,266 questions, we randomly select 200 questions for testing, making it comparable to BrowseComp-zh’s 289 questions. GAIA emphasizes robust tool use for multi-step real-world tasks; following prior work (Li et al., 2025b), we evaluate on 103 text-only validation cases. XbenchDeepSearch focuses on professional-style, deep information retrieval. For computational efficiency, we replace the evaluator model Qwen2.5-72B with GPT-4.1 and substitute the Google Search API with the Serper API (Serper.dev) to reduce costs. Under this configuration, we re-run all baselines and report averages over five runs in Table 8. THINKMERGE preserves compatibility with standard decoding controls and integrates naturally with modern inference stacks (e.g., vLLM, SGLang), facilitating deployment for both online serving and offline batch decoding. Empirically, on closed-ended math/science QA tasks, THINKMERGE is competitive with, and occasionally surpasses, majority voting. Moreover, on open-ended reasoning tasks such as LiveCodeBench, THINKMERGE improves overall pass@1 performance across several models. These findings demonstrate that token-level logit averaging effectively leverages parallel test-time computation to enhance performance on both closed- and open-ended tasks, achieving gains without additional training.

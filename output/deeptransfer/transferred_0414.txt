Programmatic Editing. Leveraging the python-pptx library, PowerPoint slides can be systematically edited by scripting modifications through code, a method extensively utilized in prior studies [9, 20, 25, 44, 45]. This approach is particularly effective for repetitive, well-defined, and content-focused tasks, such as performing batch operations like find-and-replace across all slides or automating text translation processes. However, it lacks the precision required for more complex structural changes, such as altering slide masters, themes, or specific layout geometries. Direct XML Editing. To overcome the limitations of programmatic editing in handling design and layout modifications, we have enhanced PPTPilot to directly interpret, parse, and manipulate raw OOXML files (e.g., slide1.xml, theme.xml). This method enables fine-grained adjustments, such as repositioning elements to specific locations, and provides a unified interface that aligns well with existing PowerPoint editing tools, as OOXML encapsulates most of the presentation's information. Nevertheless, the strict formatting requirements and long context of XML make it challenging to execute precise edits, particularly for extensive modifications spanning multiple slides. In such cases, programmatic editing remains the more reliable option. Skill Routing. To determine the most suitable editing approach for a given user query, we employ a tool that routes the task to the appropriate method. Upon receiving a user instruction, this tool analyzes the query in conjunction with the presentation's structure, including both visual and textual elements, to decide whether to utilize programmatic editing or direct XML manipulation.

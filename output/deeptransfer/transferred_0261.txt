To address these challenges, it is essential to transition from static outputs to dynamic, real-time processes. The proposed methodology emphasizes the use of fundamental inquiries that enable language models to generate responses instantaneously. Consequently, this approach ensures that fact-checking mechanisms are provided with the necessary textual data, hallucination detection techniques can monitor the model's internal processes during generation, and evaluation metrics remain relevant and effective as models evolve. The UniFact framework specifically addresses the shortcomings of outdated benchmarking methodologies by adhering to three foundational principles. Firstly, it avoids dependence on legacy datasets of outputs, instead employing factual inquiries to facilitate real-time response generation, ensuring applicability across current and future model iterations. Moreover, it promotes equitable comparisons by integrating both hallucination detection and fact verification methodologies. For instance, hallucination detection approaches gain access to the model's internal processes during response generation, while fact verification strategies are supplied with the generated text and supplementary information for corroboration. Both methodologies are assessed on the shared task of determining the factual accuracy of model outputs. Furthermore, the framework incorporates an automated evaluation mechanism, thereby eliminating reliance on subjective and time-intensive human assessments. This system cross-references model responses with ground truth data and credible sources, ensuring efficient, equitable, and consistent evaluation across multiple models.

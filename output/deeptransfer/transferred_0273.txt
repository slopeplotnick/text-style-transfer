CAPO employs a dynamic signal, referred to as advantage, which aligns with the model's progressively evolving competence. Since curriculum learning was first introduced by Bengio et al. (2009), most methods have relied on static heuristics, such as sequentially ordering tasks based on predefined difficulty levels. However, subsequent methodologies attempted to adapt by incorporating elements such as expert feedback or performance metrics; these remained reliant on external criteria and were not intrinsically linked to the model's developmental trajectory. This shortcoming prompted the development of CAPO, which leverages advantage estimates as an intrinsic, competence-aware signal to inform the scheduling of tasks for the model. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrate that CAPO consistently enhances mathematical problem-solving capabilities across various advantage-based reinforcement learning frameworks, including GRPO, PPO, RLOO, and Reinforce++. Moreover, CAPO demonstrates robust performance on tasks involving graphical user interfaces (GUIs), effectively managing diverse reasoning tasks. In summary, our principal contributions are threefold: we introduce advantage as a dynamic curriculum signal and have developed CAPO, a two-phase mechanism comprising imitation and discrimination phases that adapt to the model's progressive competence. CAPO exhibits compatibility with various reinforcement learning methodologies and is applicable to tasks requiring multimodal data processing, such as those involving GUIs. As illustrated in Figure 2, CAPO operates by generating samples through the policy model, subsequently computing advantage estimates via various algorithms, which are then cohesively integrated into a unified framework. By leveraging these signals, CAPO strategically structures the training process into an initial imitation phase, ensuring early-stage stability, followed by a discrimination phase that facilitates improved performance as more challenging samples are introduced.

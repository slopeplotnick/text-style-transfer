Faulty code not being executed. For our approach to function effectively, faulty statements must be executed by at least one failing test case; statements that are never executed cannot be diagnosed. Consequently, insufficient test coverage can lead to undetected faults, as such statements cannot be analyzed. To mitigate this, we employ multiple failing test cases alongside lightweight static analysis. Bounded model checking (BMC). We utilize bounded encodings of programs to facilitate analysis. However, loop unrolling and depth bounds can exclude feasible behaviors, potentially rendering valid diagnoses unreachable within the imposed constraints. Moreover, when bounds truncate iterations, diagnoses applicable to the bounded model may not generalize to the original program. We select these bounds conservatively and disclose them alongside our results, but incompleteness due to bounding remains an inherent limitation. Pointers, arrays, and undefined behavior. As outlined in Section 5.1, CFAULTS relies on static analysis tools, such as CPPCHECK [36] and CLANG-TIDY [37], to identify issues like uninitialized variables and potential runtime errors (e.g., division by zero). If such issues are detected, CFAULTS halts execution and reports them to the user. Additionally, CFAULTS leverages CBMC's memory-safety checks (e.g., --bounds-check, --pointer-check) to detect invalid memory operations, including out-of-bounds accesses and unsafe pointer arithmetic. However, CFAULTS adheres to CBMC's specification and encoding of ANSI C, meaning behaviors deemed valid by CBMC are treated as acceptable by our system, while issues not flagged by CBMC may go undetected. Clarifying and, where possible, tightening the alignment between CBMC's configuration and the intended semantics of C remains an area for future work. External validity. Our evaluation was conducted on benchmark and student programs that compile successfully and include explicit specifications, such as assertions or expected outputs. Generalizing this approach to larger programs with extensive I/O, concurrency, or complex library contracts, as well as to specifications expressed in richer formalisms, may require additional refinement and could influence the outcomes observed in this study.

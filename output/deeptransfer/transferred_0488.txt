We began by qualitatively analyzing the results through multiple readings of the interview summaries. During the interviews, we recorded the discussions, took handwritten notes, and later summarized the recorded content. This combined approach aims to ensure that all relevant information is captured and to minimize the risk of missing critical details, aligning with the common and recommended practice of recording and transcribing interviews in qualitative research. The analysis process was conducted by the research team, including the interviewer. Subsequently, we designed a set of metrics to conduct a quantitative analysis. These metrics involved systematically tallying responses and assessing frequency: first, identifying the number of good practices, then the benefits, followed by the risks, and finally the fears mentioned by participants. To achieve this, we sought analogous responses and distilled them into concise terms encapsulating the core ideas. For instance, "The genericity pushes to factorize more and more" was summarized with "genericity" and "factorisation." For each topic, we first identified the frequency of the expressed terms and then categorized them by the role of the participant who expressed them. The identification of potential words followed a methodical approach. Initially, we sorted words by frequency after removing stop-words and punctuation. We selected the most meaningful words and then verified their contextual relevance within the documents. To enhance clarity, we occasionally augmented the words with additional descriptors, such as using "too complex" instead of merely "complex." Furthermore, we compared the words identified in the interviews with those found in the literature. In cases of misalignment, we sought more suitable alternatives, such as replacing "unusable" with "useless." Conversely, if the term was deemed appropriate, it was retained. This process allowed us to refine and improve our synthesized answers by integrating knowledge from scholarly texts, ensuring a better match with the language used in those texts. Ultimately, this approach helped us extract the most relevant and meaningful terms to enhance our data analysis.

We began our analysis by qualitatively reviewing the interview summaries multiple times to thoroughly understand the results. During the interviews, we recorded the discussions, took handwritten notes, and later summarized the recorded content. This combined approach aimed to ensure that all relevant information was captured while minimizing the risk of overlooking critical details, aligning with the common practice of recording and transcribing interviews in qualitative research. The analysis was conducted by the research team, which included the interviewer. Following this, we developed a set of metrics to facilitate a quantitative evaluation. These metrics involved systematically tallying responses to determine the frequency of identified elements, such as current good practices, perceived benefits, associated risks, and expressed concerns. To address these questions, we identified recurring patterns in the responses and distilled them into concise terms that encapsulated the core ideas. For example, the statement "The genericity pushes to factorize more and more" was summarized as "genericity" and "factorisation." For each topic, we first identified the frequency of generic terms expressed and then categorized them by the roles of the participants who mentioned them. The identification of potential terms followed a systematic method. Initially, we removed stop words and punctuation, sorted the remaining words by frequency, and selected those deemed most meaningful. Next, we reviewed the documents to validate the contextual appropriateness of each term. In some cases, we enriched the identified words by forming compound terms, such as "too complex" instead of "complex," to enhance clarity and precision. Furthermore, we compared the terms derived from the interviews with those extracted from literary sources. If a term did not align appropriately, we replaced it with a more suitable alternative, such as substituting "unusable" with "useless." Conversely, if a term matched well, it was retained. This comparative process allowed us to refine our synthesized responses by integrating insights from scholarly literature, ensuring that the terminology used was consistent with the language found in these sources. Ultimately, this approach enabled us to extract the most relevant and meaningful terms, thereby enhancing the overall quality of our data analysis.

How do language models utilize contextual information to address health-related questions, and how are their responses influenced by conflicting contexts? This study examines the capacity of language models to process and reason over extensive, contradictory biomedical information using HEALTHCONTRADICT, a rigorously curated dataset comprising 920 instances. Each instance includes a health-related question, a scientifically validated factual answer, and two documents presenting opposing perspectives. We explore various prompt configurations, including those with correct, incorrect, or contradictory contexts, to evaluate their influence on model responses. In comparison to existing evaluation benchmarks for medical question-answering, HEALTHCONTRADICT offers a more nuanced assessment of language models' abilities to reason with context. Our findings indicate that the efficacy of fine-tuned biomedical language models is attributable not only to their parametric knowledge acquired during pretraining but also to their capacity to utilize accurate context while disregarding erroneous context. However, language models may produce responses that appear plausible yet lack factual accuracy. This poses a significant issue, as individuals may place undue trust in erroneous health information, thereby incurring potential risks. Furthermore, health-related information is subject to rapid evolution, particularly during crises, and unverified data can disseminate swiftly across digital platforms, thereby influencing the learning processes of these models. To address these challenges, several methodologies have been proposed to assist models in managing conflicting information. One approach involves modifying the model's parametric knowledge when it is inconsistent with the contextual information, while another method involves tracing the origins of the contextual knowledge that led the model to produce a specific response. Nevertheless, these approaches tend to concentrate exclusively on either the model's inherent knowledge or the contextual information. An alternative approach integrates both parametric and contextual knowledge by evaluating the coherence of the information. Additionally, a method has been developed to generate dual predictions: one derived from contextual information and the other from the model's inherent knowledge. Another technique ensures the model selects the correct response when confronted with conflicting information. Moreover, alternative approaches seek to mitigate the propensity of models to produce erroneous responses. During the training phase, incorrect or irrelevant information is deliberately introduced to enhance the models' capacity to manage disordered data. In the testing phase, methodologies are employed to identify and suppress incorrect information, formulate inquiries with precision, and utilize multiple evaluators to verify the accuracy of the responses. Furthermore, strategies are employed to identify robust responses to thwart adversarial attacks and to predict the volatility of certain facts to prevent the dissemination of outdated information. Contemporary methodologies predominantly concentrate on either rectifying misinformation or curbing undesirable model behaviors. However, both aspects are crucial for enhancing the accuracy and reliability of responses in health-related domains.

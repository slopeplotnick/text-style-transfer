How do language models utilize contextual information to answer health-related questions, and how are their responses influenced by conflicting contexts? We evaluate the ability of language models to process extensive, contradictory biomedical information using HEALTHCONTRADICT, an expert-verified dataset comprising 920 instances. Each instance includes a health-related question, a factual answer supported by scientific evidence, and two documents presenting opposing viewpoints. To assess the impact of context on model outputs, we experiment with various question formulations, incorporating correct, incorrect, or contradictory contexts. Compared to existing evaluation benchmarks, HEALTHCONTRADICT offers a more nuanced framework for assessing language models' contextual reasoning capabilities. Our findings reveal that fine-tuned biomedical language models derive their effectiveness not only from parametric knowledge acquired during pretraining but also from their ability to leverage accurate contextual information while resisting the influence of erroneous or contradictory inputs. However, language models remain prone to generating responses that appear plausible but lack factual accuracy. This raises significant concerns, as individuals may place undue trust in incorrect health information, potentially leading to harmful outcomes. Moreover, the rapid evolution of health-related knowledge, particularly during crises, exacerbates the risks associated with unverified information spreading across digital platforms, which can influence both pretraining and in-context learning. To address these challenges, several methodologies have been proposed to help models navigate conflicting information. For instance, context-aware decoding overrides a model's parametric knowledge when it contradicts contextual information, while approaches like ContextCite enhance explainability by tracing the origins of contextual knowledge that inform a model's response. Nevertheless, these methods often focus exclusively on either parametric or contextual knowledge. More integrative approaches, such as COMBO, combine both sources of knowledge by training discriminators to assess passage compatibility. Similarly, DisentQA generates dual predictions—one based on contextual knowledge and the other on parametric knowledge—while contrastive decoding calibrates model probabilities to favor correct answers in the presence of conflicting information. Additional strategies aim to mitigate harmful model behaviors. During training, counterfactual and irrelevant contexts are introduced to improve robustness, while in-context pretraining enhances performance on complex reasoning tasks. At the inference stage, techniques such as misinformation detection, vigilant prompting, and reader ensembles are employed to minimize the generation of inaccurate responses. Furthermore, query augmentation defends against adversarial attacks by identifying robust answers, and fact duration prediction helps models avoid relying on outdated information. While existing approaches primarily address either the resolution of contextual conflicts or the mitigation of harmful behaviors, both aspects are critical for improving the reliability and accuracy of language models in the biomedical domain.

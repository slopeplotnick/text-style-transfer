In the context of AI systems where various components collaborate to manage complex tasks, large language models (LLMs) play a pivotal role in shaping subsequent decision-making processes. However, a predisposition toward the initial input can disrupt the functionality of the entire system. Addressing this bias is therefore essential to ensure fair and transparent operations. This study identifies two primary limitations. First, while the length of each article was standardized, the sensitivity of evaluation metrics to document length was not explicitly examined. Since length may influence absolute metric values—such as ROUGE-L (lexical overlap), BERTScore (semantic similarity), and SummaC (factual consistency)—future research could categorize datasets by length or conduct analyses with controlled lengths to better isolate the effects of input order. Second, the evaluation metric scores were notably low overall, underscoring a broader limitation of reference-free evaluation metrics in abstractive summarization, especially when comparing extensive source documents to concise summaries. Future research should expand beyond the abortion domain to explore other politically and socially contentious topics, such as gun control, LGBTQ+ rights, and immigration, to determine whether the observed order effects persist across diverse issue areas. Additionally, examining a wider range of LLM architectures, such as GPT and Claude, could provide a more nuanced understanding of whether primacy effects are consistent across different model families. Moreover, incorporating additional evaluative frameworks—such as measures of positional bias in opinion representation and indicators of social polarization—could offer deeper insights into how input sequence influences generated summaries. Our findings reveal that LLMs exhibit a pronounced tendency to prioritize the first-seen document in multi-document summarization tasks, particularly at the semantic level. This corroborates prior research, which demonstrates that the sequence of information presentation significantly impacts generated outputs. Such order sensitivity poses challenges for applications like AI-driven summarization tools and agentic AI frameworks, where initial input documents can disproportionately influence downstream processes. Therefore, addressing positional bias is critical for developing more equitable, transparent, and reliable LLM-based systems.

Humans possess a remarkable ability: given clear and structured instructions, such as verbal descriptions or diagrams, they can rapidly acquire new tasks without necessitating additional practice. Researchers have systematically analyzed the functional parallels between the brain and artificial intelligence across sensory, cognitive, and motor domains by deconstructing their processes into three fundamental components: circuit architecture, input-output transformations, and learning algorithms. Consequently, they found that contemporary AI systems are increasingly converging toward brain-like mechanisms, yielding significant insights into the computational principles underlying neural processes. The neocortex and cerebellum utilize prior information to anticipate future states of the environment, striving to refine these predictions for greater accuracy. Through this process, they distill and structure information into compact yet highly effective world models. These predictive models serve three primary functions: forecasting future events, facilitating understanding of the external environment through the application of abstract constructs such as features and causal relationships (e.g., object recognition and text comprehension), and generating outputs, including actions, plans, and linguistic expressions. This mode of information processing enables the neocortex and cerebellum to perform a wide array of functions despite their relatively uniform structural and functional characteristics. In essence, this form of information processing constitutes a common computational foundation that underpins numerous capabilities of both the brain and artificial intelligence. Advancing our understanding of this process will not only enhance the fields of neuroscience and artificial intelligence but also provide deeper insights into the principles underlying intelligence. The human brain exhibits a remarkable capacity for rapid learning, requiring significantly fewer examples than large language models, and demonstrating superior computational efficiency. Recent advancements in artificial intelligence have enhanced learning and efficiency by emulating brain-like mechanisms, such as Mixture-of-Experts approaches; however, these models still fall short of the brain's capabilities. As artificial intelligence continues to evolve by drawing insights from neural processes, the field of neuroscience is poised to gain novel perspectives through the study of AI systems.

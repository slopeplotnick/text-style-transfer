When planning a sequence of measurements, such as determining optimal drilling locations, artificial intelligence methodologies, including Monte Carlo tree search, can be employed to identify the most effective drilling strategy (Mern and Caers, 2023). The prioritization of objectives evolves throughout the process, with a hierarchical approach: initially, hypotheses are evaluated, followed by assessments of volume, and finally, grade. Once all but one hypothesis have been eliminated, the focus shifts to quantifying the mineral volume and subsequently evaluating its grade. Alternatively, these factors may be integrated into a combined framework, such as a grade-volume chart, to facilitate decision-making. Despite the promise of scientific methodologies to enhance the cost-efficiency and efficacy of exploration activities, several non-scientific barriers persist within the industry. These challenges include entrenched practices and organizational structures, the fragmented nature of the exploration sector, and the absence of integrated software solutions. Typically, companies rely on domain-specific experts to address tasks aligned with their specialized training. However, organizations frequently prioritize granular details without fully understanding their relevance to the ultimate decision-making process. Therefore, effective utilization of artificial intelligence necessitates a fundamental restructuring of large corporations, shifting their focus from task execution to addressing decision-making challenges. This transformation is not merely structural but also requires a comprehensive educational effort to facilitate the adoption of AI-driven methodologies.

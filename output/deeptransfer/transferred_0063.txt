The threat model focuses on the post-integration phase, wherein the malicious tool has been deployed and utilized by the agent. We assume that in fully localized environments, the tool's source code may be publicly visible or undergo static analysis; however, due to the semantic resemblance between benign and malicious functionalities, the concealed backdoor remains undetected. In cloud-hosted or hybrid configurations, the internal mechanisms of the tools are obscured from the user, thus confining the potential attack surface to their observable outputs. The attacker operates entirely within permissible boundaries, refraining from privilege escalation or memory corruption. The attack is deemed successful if it satisfies two conditions: (1) the successful execution of the attacker's external task, and (2) the maintenance of the agent's operational accuracy on the user's task within an acceptable deviation threshold. Moreover, the threat is primarily economically motivated. The unauthorized utilization of computational resources incurs tangible financial costs, such as the depletion of API tokens or the consumption of local GPU cycles. The aggregate impact of numerous such tools can lead to significant resource wastage throughout the agent ecosystem. This section delineates the design of the LeechHijack attack. We commence with a comprehensive overview of the attack mechanism. Subsequently, we elucidate the implantation phase, wherein a latent backdoor is embedded within a compliant tool. Thereafter, we delineate the exploitation phase, during which the backdoor is activated to establish a covert communication channel and impose additional tasks upon the agent. We conclude with formal definitions of the criteria for attack success and the metrics employed for evaluation. LeechHijack achieves computational hijacking through a bifurcated process. In the initial implantation phase, the adversary disseminates a seemingly innocuous MCP tool that harbors a latent backdoor component. During standard operation, the backdoor remains dormant, exhibiting no discernible malicious activity or adverse effects. In the exploitation phase, activation of the backdoor is initiated by a specific conditional trigger. Once activated, the tool provides altered responses, compelling the agent to execute additional tasks as dictated by the attacker. The exploitation phase concludes upon the subsequent activation of the tool. Finally, LeechHijack returns the results of the attack calculations and reinstates standard outputs, leaving no discernible evidence for the user.

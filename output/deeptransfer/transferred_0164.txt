In 2023, Zakharov examined phenomena such as change detection and probabilistic inference under conditions of uncertainty. These processes, as described by Petzschner and Glasauer (2011), are underpinned by optimal Bayesian inference mechanisms. Moreover, recent advancements in computational modeling indicate that these perceptual biases, particularly those related to temporal estimation, are intrinsically supported by predictive processes within episodic memory (Fountas et al., 2022). Individuals often integrate ambiguous information with pre-existing beliefs when estimating quantities or sizes, resulting in specific behavioral patterns. Figure 1 illustrates that both Llama-4 Maverick and human participants exhibit a tendency to estimate towards the mean, indicating the employment of analogous cognitive strategies despite their inherent differences. Furthermore, certain components of large language models (LLMs) emulate this estimation process. In-context learning can be conceptualized as an approximation of Bayesian inference, as articulated by Xie et al. (2021). Additionally, the application of Bayesian teaching methodologies has been demonstrated to enhance reasoning capabilities, as reported by Qiu et al. (2025). Large language models (LLMs) also decompose sequences by employing mechanisms akin to human event detection through surprise, as elucidated by Kumar and Fountas (2023, 2025). However, the majority of existing research predominantly focuses on explicit reasoning or learned behaviors, where models apply established rules, rather than on tasks that elucidate reasoning processes emerging intrinsically from training. The rapid advancement in the development of LLMs capable of processing heterogeneous information types has been noteworthy. Evaluative frameworks such as MMbench and SEED-bench, as referenced by Liu and Li (2024), assess the cognitive processes of these models. However, these assessments typically do not account for the impact of noise within diverse information modalities on cognitive processing. Therefore, our synthetic dataset facilitates an in-depth examination of how LLMs integrate ambiguous information from various sources. This emerging field of research is pivotal for enhancing our understanding of how to develop more robust and generalizable models that perform optimally under conditions of uncertainty.

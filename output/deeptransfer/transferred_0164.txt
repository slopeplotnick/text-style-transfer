In 2023, Zakharov elucidated the mechanisms by which individuals perceive alterations and formulate probabilistic inferences in the face of uncertainty. This phenomenon, as articulated by Petzschner and Glasauer in 2011, is attributed to the utilization of an optimal estimation strategy under conditions of ambiguity. Moreover, recent computational models, as posited by Fountas et al. in 2022, suggest that these inferential inaccuracies, particularly concerning temporal judgments, are substantiated by memory-based predictive processes. Human estimations of quantities or magnitudes often involve the integration of ambiguous information with pre-existing cognitive biases, resulting in predictable behavioral tendencies. Figure 1 illustrates that both Llama-4 Maverick and human participants exhibit a propensity to converge their estimations toward the mean, suggesting analogous cognitive processes despite fundamental differences in their underlying mechanisms. Furthermore, certain components within large language models (LLMs) appear to emulate this heuristic-based approach to estimation. In particular, in-context learning can be conceptualized as approximate Bayesian inference, as articulated by Xie et al. (2021), while Bayesian teaching has been demonstrated to enhance reasoning performance, according to Qiu et al. (2025). Additionally, LLMs inherently segment sequences based on Bayesian surprise, aligning with human event perception, as reported by Kumar et al. (2023) and Fountas et al. (2025). Nevertheless, the majority of research focuses on explicit reasoning or learned behaviors, where models exploit acquired statistical rules, rather than on perceptual tasks that could elucidate computational strategies emerging implicitly from pretraining. Recent advancements in the development of LLMs have facilitated their ability to process diverse types of information. Evaluation frameworks such as MMbench and SEED-bench, as referenced by Liu and Li (2024), assess the reasoning capabilities of these models. However, these benchmarks typically overlook the impact of noise across varying information modalities on reasoning performance. By employing synthetic datasets, we enable a detailed examination of how LLMs integrate ambiguous information originating from heterogeneous sources. This emerging field of research is pivotal for enhancing our understanding of how to develop more robust and generalisable models that perform optimally in the face of uncertainty.

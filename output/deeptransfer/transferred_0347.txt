To optimize planning through extensive reasoning over Story-Information and generate long-form answers, Gurung and Lapata (2025) introduced VR-CLI (Verifiable Rewards via Completion Likelihood Improvement), a reward formulation. The VR-CLI objective aims to enhance the likelihood of generating the true next chapter, conditioned on both the Story-Information and the plan. We further extend the VR-CLI framework by incorporating a contrastive term that penalizes the likelihood of selecting a randomly chosen chapter from the same book, thereby refining its effectiveness. Our standard methodology, LiteReason, involves periodically conducting a minor training iteration using the pathways generated during that period. Although this incurs additional computational expense, it updates the reasoning component with the current model's cognitive processes. Moreover, we employ an implicit thought prompt to facilitate the model's utilization of implicit thought tags. In the LiteReason without SFT variant, the implicit prompt and the initialized Reasoning Projector are retained, utilizing the implicit thought tags to determine when to provide new reasoning embeddings to the model. However, this version does not perform SFT during training to update the Reasoning Projector. Conversely, LiteReason without the Reasoning Projector is evaluated for its performance post-training without the Reasoning Projector, but with the implicit thought prompt. This approach does not employ latent reasoning and assesses whether the model's performance is affected when reasoning steps are omitted. Building upon recent advancements in latent reasoning research, we identify a representative subset of prior methodologies to evaluate the design principles articulated in §3. These approaches are assessed on the Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks. Specifically, in the context of training-free latent reasoning, we benchmark our method against two contemporary approaches: Mixture of Inputs (MoI) proposed by Su et al. (2025), which integrates the embedding of the sampled token with those from the remaining token distribution, and Soft Thinking by Zhang et al. (2025), which similarly combines token embeddings but employs an entropy metric to determine the transition from continuous thinking to discrete sampling.

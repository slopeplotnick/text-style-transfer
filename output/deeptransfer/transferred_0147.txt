As illustrated in Fig. 1, this section details the dataset construction process and evaluation methodologies. For dataset construction, a team of TCM experts systematically gathered samples from clinical cases, classical case records, and authoritative examination questions. Subsequently, all samples underwent deduplication, cleaning, and anonymization to ensure the removal of any personal information. The data annotation process follows a three-stage pipeline: initially, experts performed the annotations independently, followed by mutual cross-validation, and culminating in an evaluation by an independent third party. The resulting benchmark, designated TCM-BEST4SDT, comprises 600 questions covering four tasks, including TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, LLM Content Safety, and SDT, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Regarding evaluation methodologies, three mechanisms were devised to assess the efficacy of models in replicating authentic TCM clinical scenarios. The first is selected-response evaluation, which involves multiple rounds of independent evaluation for each question, randomizes the option order, and calculates scores using different scoring strategies based on the question type. The second is judge model evaluation, utilizing a large language model (LLM) guided by expert-designed prompts to assess the accuracy and quality of the model's responses. The third is reward model evaluation, which employs a specialized model to objectively quantify the congruence between prescriptions and syndromes. TCM-BEST4SDT encompasses two categories of tasks: SDT tasks and general evaluation tasks. The SDT tasks are derived from clinical cases and classical case records, whereas the general evaluation tasks encompass TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, and LLM Content Safety. The TCM Basic Knowledge component is sourced from publicly available examination materials, including the National Qualification Examination for Medical Practitioners, the National Postgraduate Entrance Examination: Comprehensive Clinical Medicine (TCM Integrated), and the Chinese Herbal Medicine Title Examination. Moreover, the Medical Ethics questions are derived from authoritative sources and have been meticulously annotated by experts to address particular scenarios. Similarly, the LLM Content Safety questions were independently crafted by experts, aligning with practical requirements.

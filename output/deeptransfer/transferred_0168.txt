Several studies have investigated more structured prompting paradigms. For instance, Plan-and-Solve (Wang et al., 2023c) explicitly decomposes a task into a planning phase and a solution phase, demonstrating enhanced efficacy for mathematical and logical tasks. Similarly, Prompt Chaining (Chen et al., 2023) examines whether integrating intermediate summaries or steps augments performance in text summarization tasks. Furthermore, Complexity-based Prompting (Fu et al., 2023) posits that task complexity should dictate the formulation of prompts, a notion that resonates with our investigation into prompt specificity. While the majority of extant research concentrates on task format or exemplar inclusion, our study distinctively examines prompt specificity as a continuous variable, systematically modulating its detail and structure. Recent scholarly efforts have introduced methodologies for the refinement of prompts, either through automated processes or with human intervention. For example, black-box optimization methods (Diao et al., 2023) refine prompts utilizing task feedback without requiring access to model gradients. Moreover, the study by Zhou et al. (2023) on Prompt Engineering a Prompt Engineer illustrates that large language models can be prompted to enhance their own prompts, representing a metaprompting strategy akin to our generalization loop. Additionally, Prompt Refinement with Image Pivots (Lu et al., 2023) demonstrates the application of visual features to refine textual prompts for image generation. Despite the exclusive reliance on textual data, our objective is to refine prompts to enhance model performance. However, in contrast to previous studies, DETAIL concentrates on the impact of specificity in text on model reasoning, rather than examining semantic alterations or the integration of text with other data modalities. Comprehensive reviews such as those by Liu et al. have examined various prompting methodologies for reasoning, highlighting the diversity of prompt types and strategies available. Nevertheless, limited attention has been given to how the specificity or granularity of prompts influences model behavior across different tasks and systems. While approaches such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and Plan-and-Solve underscore the significance of prompt content, they do not provide a detailed examination of this aspect.

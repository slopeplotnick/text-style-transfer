Given the constraints of real-time latency and computational costs, deploying our LLM or deep cross-encoder-based BERT classifier within an online search framework is infeasible. Therefore, we adopt a teacher-student distillation framework to transfer knowledge from a more complex model to a simplified student model, as illustrated in Figure 1. The resulting online model is a lightweight, shallow cross-encoder variant of BERT. To ensure distributional consistency between the teacher and student models, we employ a deep cross-encoder BERT as the teacher model, rather than an autoregressively trained LLM. Moreover, to enhance the BERT-based model's ability to discern relevance between queries and products, we explicitly incorporate key attribute information identified by the relevance reasoning module into the BERT input, thereby enabling explicit knowledge enhancement. However, to address the noise and redundancy in lengthy explanations, we introduce a rewriting model that extracts and refines product attribute descriptions aligned or misaligned with the query. This process filters irrelevant details and reformulates critical insights into a concise, structured format, allowing the lightweight BERT model to focus on discriminative reasoning. Since the student model cannot access these refined outputs during real-time inference, we integrate this information exclusively into the teacher model to maintain consistency between offline and online inputs. Subsequently, the student model is trained to emulate the teacher model through representation alignment. As there are no publicly available datasets for the Chinese e-commerce relevance task, we conduct experiments on a large-scale, real-world dataset collected from user search logs within the JD application.

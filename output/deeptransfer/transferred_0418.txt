Moreover, Galhotra et al. [2021] engage in an analytical exploration of determining probabilities in the absence of a fully specified system. They investigate the utilization of particular models to derive intervals for counterfactual queries, thereby enabling a concentrated examination of specific model components when complete information is unavailable. This approach potentially yields narrower intervals compared to conventional computational methods. Furthermore, the examination of methods for elucidating AI models also involves assessing the necessity and sufficiency of specific features. Mothilal et al. [2021] posit that the optimal model explanation is achieved by identifying the minimal subset of features that are both necessary and sufficient to produce a particular outcome in a given context. They operate under the assumption that the variables for explanation are limited to the input features, which are presumed to be independent of one another. Various types of AI explanations are compared, with a focus on the concepts of necessity and sufficiency. The notion of necessity pertains to explanations that identify the modifications required to produce the initial outcome. Conversely, sufficiency concerns the identification of features that are pivotal in determining the outcome, irrespective of other conditions. The authors demonstrate significant variability in the practical application of various methodologies. Similarly, Pawar et al. [2024], operating under the assumption of feature independence, explore the concepts of necessity and sufficiency as criteria for evaluating AI methodologies, and investigate diverse strategies for generating illustrative examples to facilitate explanations. In a related context, Beckers [2022] examines scenarios wherein a model forecasts outcomes based on actions, presupposing the model's infallibility in its predictions. Explanations are delineated into those that are sufficient and those that consider counterfactual scenarios, under the assumption of perfect model predictions. Furthermore, explanations are deconstructed by evaluating the degrees of causal influence to ascertain the actual causes underlying the explanations.

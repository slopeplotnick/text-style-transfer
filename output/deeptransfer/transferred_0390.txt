Thinking in Language Models for Evaluation. The reliability of judgments made by language models is often enhanced when the model is prompted to generate intermediate reasoning steps before delivering a final verdict, a technique popularized as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. In the context of evaluation, this "thinking" process allows the model to articulate the criteria for judgment and justify its decisions, leading to the "Thinking-LLM-as-a-Judge" paradigm. This explicit reasoning not only improves the accuracy of the judgments but also increases their interpretability. Our work leverages the generation of these independent thinking traces and investigates how to best aggregate the resulting rating samples. 

Inference Time Compute and Sample Aggregation: Multiple strategies have been proposed that leverage inference time compute. When multiple samples are generated using this approach, an aggregation strategy becomes necessary. Self-Consistency (SC) aggregates multiple outputs using majority voting. Moreover, several variants incorporate confidence signals. For instance, Soft Self-Consistency (Soft-SC) selects the minimum, mean, or product of confidence scores of items in each category. Confidence-Informed Self-Consistency (CI-SC) computes a weighted majority vote based on confidence scores, which are derived either from the length-normalized probability of the sequence or via prompting a language model. Alternatively, some methods leverage the language model itself for aggregation. Generative Self-Aggregation (GSA) involves the model synthesizing a new response based on the context of multiple samples. Universal Self-Consistency (USC), on the other hand, leverages the model to select the most consistent answer among multiple candidates. Furthermore, existing research indicates that the utilization of computational resources during the evaluation phase, particularly through majority voting, can significantly enhance the efficacy of reasoning-based generative verifiers.

Thinking in Language Models for Evaluation. Prompting language models to engage in intermediate reasoning steps before delivering a final response often enhances the reliability of their judgments, a technique known as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. This process allows models to articulate the rationale behind their decisions, fostering the "Thinking-LLM-as-a-Judge" paradigm. Such explicit reasoning not only improves accuracy but also enhances interpretability. Our study builds on this paradigm by leveraging these reasoning processes and exploring optimal strategies for aggregating the resulting outputs. Inference Time Compute and Sample Aggregation: Various methodologies have been proposed to utilize inference time compute effectively. When multiple samples are generated, an aggregation strategy becomes necessary. Self-Consistency (SC) employs majority voting to integrate diverse outputs. Moreover, several variants incorporate confidence signals. Soft Self-consistency selects outcomes based on the minimum, mean, or product of confidence scores across categories. Confidence-Informed Self-Consistency uses a weighted majority vote informed by confidence scores, derived either from the length-normalized probability of the sequence or by eliciting responses from the model itself. Alternatively, some methodologies leverage the model for direct aggregation. Generative Self-Aggregation prompts the model to synthesize a novel response from multiple samples. Universal Self-Consistency involves the model selecting the most coherent answer from a range of alternatives. Furthermore, empirical evidence suggests that employing computational resources during the testing phase, particularly through majority voting, significantly enhances the efficacy of reasoning-based generative verifiers.

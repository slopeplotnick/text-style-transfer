To address these challenges, model-agnostic approaches have been developed to compare generated outputs with training data within a shared embedding space, typically utilizing features derived from pre-trained encoders. While these methods demonstrate efficacy and scalability, they rely on vector representations that are inherently difficult to interpret. Recent advancements, such as LatentExplainer, endeavor to bridge this gap by perturbing latent variables and employing language models to elucidate their semantic implications. However, these approaches continue to function within an opaque framework that restricts transparency for end-users. In contrast to these intricate methodologies, our approach utilizes automatically generated Knowledge Graphs (KGs) to trace the influence of data. By leveraging shared triples encompassing objects, attributes, and stylistic elements, the comparison of generated and training images facilitates scalable, interpretable, and human-aligned attribution. Moreover, Large Language Models (LLMs) have emerged as pivotal tools within contemporary KG workflows, enabling structured reasoning over unstructured data. Their contributions are primarily categorized into three domains: (1) the generation of subject-predicate-object triples from natural language text, (2) the facilitation of ontology development, and (3) the translation of natural language queries into graph-based representations. For triple generation, methods such as AutoKG can transform text into ontology-aligned triples. Beyond mere triple generation, LLMs facilitate ontology learning and extension through techniques such as prompt engineering and ontology reuse. In the domain of question answering, LLMs can serve as semantic parsers, converting user queries into SPARQL queries. This process is further enhanced by employing contextual knowledge graphs or controlled natural languages to improve clarity and precision. Recent surveys on generative KG construction indicate a transition from conventional pipeline architectures to advanced, LLM-driven systems. These systems demonstrate superior performance in entity recognition, relation extraction, and graph population, surpassing traditional methodologies. These advancements in KG construction provide a robust foundation for addressing the challenges of data attribution in generative models. Instead of relying on opaque embeddings or inaccessible parameters, structured outputs from multimodal LLMs offer a promising alternative, enabling the construction of interpretable knowledge graphs that encapsulate critical elements of both generated and training data while tracing influences. Although the majority of existing research has concentrated on textual data, these methodologies exhibit significant potential for extension to visual data, notwithstanding the increased complexity associated with image content.

The system operates by enabling the mother agent to engage in interactions utilizing its existing grammatical knowledge. This design represents a novel approach, as traditional language learning models typically rely on extensive text corpora for training. In this study, the term "language model" is used in a broader sense, not limited to conventional computational programs that predict subsequent words, such as GPT or BERT. Instead, we encompass a variety of methodologies, including rule-based approaches, statistical methods, and artificial neural networks, as all these approaches are employed to address common tasks in NLP. This distinction is crucial because our system employs explicit linguistic rules, whereas the language-acquiring agent utilizes statistical methods to infer these rules. The study investigates whether the MODOMA daughter agent can acquire and represent distinct linguistic components by integrating a specific distribution within the MODOMA language acquisition framework. Specifically, we examine whether a multi-agent system can discern linguistic components from synthetically generated sentences and represent them through explicit rule-based formulations. To address this question, we assess whether these sentences exhibit patterns analogous to those found in human language and determine the extent of data required to identify such patterns. Furthermore, we explore the potential for these identified patterns to be systematically transformed into linguistic rules. Finally, we evaluate the correspondence between the inferred categories and the original grammatical framework, as well as the system's capacity to independently acquire structured linguistic knowledge.

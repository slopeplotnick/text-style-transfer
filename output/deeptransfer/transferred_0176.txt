The system operates by enabling the mother agent to engage in interactions utilizing its existing grammatical knowledge. This design represents a novel approach, as it diverges from the conventional methodology wherein language acquisition models predominantly rely on extensive corpora for learning. In this study, rather than confining the term "language model" to the typical computational frameworks that predict subsequent words, such as GPT or BERT, we use it in a broader sense. This encompasses rule-based approaches, statistical methods, and artificial neural networks, as all these strategies contribute to addressing linguistic tasks. This distinction is crucial because our system employs explicit linguistic rules, whereas the language-acquiring agent utilizes statistical methods to deduce these rules. The investigation examines whether the MODOMA daughter agent can acquire and manifest distinct linguistic components by employing a specific distribution within the MODOMA framework. Specifically, we investigate whether a multi-agent system can discern linguistic components from synthetically generated sentences and represent them through rule-based frameworks. To address this question, we assess whether these sentences exhibit patterns analogous to human language and determine the volume of data required to identify such patterns. Furthermore, we examine the potential for these patterns to be formalized into explicit linguistic rules. Finally, we evaluate the extent to which the identified categories correspond with the original grammatical framework and assess the system's capacity to autonomously acquire structured linguistic knowledge.

Baselines and Models. We employ Gemini-2.0-Flash as the foundational model for our baseline configurations, which operate without the integration of external knowledge and are limited to the provision of the original query. We conduct experiments with various iterations of CAIRNS: (1) Domain RAG (literature-only), which involves retrieval exclusively from the literature subset; and (2) Domain RAG (literature+data), which incorporates both literature and climate data through a two-stage API reasoning process. Gemini-2.0-Flash serves as the principal model for CAIRNS and all its iterations. All models utilize identical corpora, prompt structures, and evaluation settings to ensure equitable assessment. Impact of ScholarGuide Prompt (SGP). The ScholarGuide Prompt enhances readability by improving contextual richness, structural coherence, and language fluency. Additionally, it addresses citation inconsistencies, thereby increasing the reliability of the information, despite a slight reduction in the overall citation rate. By employing unified reference numbering and explicitly grounding each factual assertion in evidence, SGP facilitates the generation of clearer, more verifiable, and context-specific responses. This methodology demonstrates consistent efficacy across both zero-shot and few-shot scenarios, underscoring its critical role in driving CAIRNS's performance. Impact of Multi-turn ReAct (MTR). Moreover, the integration of Multi-turn ReAct (MTR) enhances the iterative reasoning process by enabling the model to refine intermediate outputs through repeated alignment of retrieved literature and climate data. This iterative mechanism contributes to the production of more comprehensive and context-aware responses, as evidenced by improved comprehensiveness metrics. While the impact of MTR is comparatively modest relative to SGP, it serves as a complementary component that bolsters robustness, particularly in addressing multi-source or data-intensive queries. Location-Weighted Retrieval (LWR). Although its influence is comparatively modest relative to SGP, Location-Weighted Retrieval (LWR) contributes to the structured generation process, enhancing the model's capacity to address complex, multi-faceted questions. LWR facilitates the generation of region-sensitive responses, particularly for geographically focused queries. Ablating this component results in a slight reduction in specificity, while other evaluation metrics remain largely unaffected, underscoring its positive role in refining geographic contextualization.

We propose SchED (Schedule-based Early Decoding), a training-free method for early stopping in diffusion language models. SchED leverages a progress-dependent schedule, such as linear or cosine, to threshold a full-span logit-margin aggregate, enabling stable, architecture-agnostic termination without retraining. By analyzing predictive-entropy trajectories over the text generation process, we observe that instruction tuning accelerates confidence stabilization, aligning with these progress-aware thresholds and allowing early exits without compromising output quality. SchED builds upon the standard denoising process of diffusion language models (dLLMs) by enforcing a smooth, dynamically adjusting confidence threshold, which exploits the bidirectional and parallel strengths of dLLMs to achieve end-to-end efficiency without additional training. The implementation code is publicly available. Recent advancements have established dLLMs as a promising alternative to autoregressive (AR) models, either through training from scratch or adapting AR checkpoints. For instance, LLaDA employs a bidirectional Transformer architecture with a forward masking mechanism and a reverse denoising chain, demonstrating strong performance in both pretraining and supervised fine-tuning. Moreover, several methodologies have been developed to adapt AR models into diffusion counterparts, enabling fair and scalable comparisons across varying model sizes and tasks. Concurrently, models like Dream-7B have focused on refining architectural designs and releasing distinct base and instruction-tuned variants. Innovations such as LLaDA-MoE have successfully applied sparse Mixture-of-Experts configurations to dLLMs, achieving competitive outcomes with significantly fewer active parameters. Furthermore, the capabilities of dLLMs have expanded to long-context scenarios. For example, UltraLLaDA utilizes Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) scaling to extend context windows to 128,000 tokens, while Seed Diffusion prioritizes high-throughput inference for code models. Formal studies on the scaling laws of masked diffusion models further validate their potential, demonstrating growth trajectories comparable to those of AR models. Finally, survey papers provide a comprehensive overview of the field, highlighting strengths such as parallelism, infilling, and global context management, while also identifying unresolved challenges.

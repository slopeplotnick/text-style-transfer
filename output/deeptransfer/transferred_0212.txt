Training-free, schedule-based early exit. We propose SchED (Schedule-based Early Decoding) for diffusion language models, which employs a progress-conditioned schedule—such as linear or cosine—to determine the stopping point by evaluating a sequence-level confidence metric. This enables stable, architecture-agnostic stopping without the need for retraining. Mechanistic explanation via entropy-based analysis. By analyzing the temporal evolution of model confidence, we observe that instruction-tuned variants exhibit accelerated stabilization, aligning with the schedule and facilitating early termination without compromising output quality. SchED operates by employing a confidence threshold that evolves progressively throughout the text generation process, leveraging the inherent advantages of diffusion models to optimize efficiency without necessitating additional retraining. The implementation code is publicly accessible. Diffusion LLMs. Recent work establishes diffusion language models as a viable alternative to autoregressive models, offering flexibility in training either from initialization or through the adaptation of pre-existing autoregressive checkpoints. LLaDA represents a methodological approach wherein the model is trained to mask text in one direction while rectifying it in the opposite direction, yielding promising outcomes in both training and fine-tuning phases. Several methodologies have been developed to transform autoregressive models into diffusion models, facilitating equitable comparisons across varying sizes and tasks. Concurrently, models such as Dream-7B prioritize the enhancement of recipe and refinement design, alongside the development of diverse model variants. Architectural innovations, exemplified by LLaDA-MoE, employ sparse configurations to achieve commendable performance with a reduced number of active parameters. Moreover, recent advancements in diffusion models have extended their applicability to longer text sequences, exemplified by UltraLLaDA, which employs RoPE scaling to effectively process contexts of up to 128K tokens. Concurrently, Seed Diffusion prioritizes the optimization of inference efficiency in code generation tasks. Formal investigations into the scaling behavior of these models have demonstrated growth dynamics comparable to those observed in autoregressive frameworks, further substantiating their scalability and potential for broader adoption. Survey and perspective papers summarize the landscape, highlighting strengths such as parallel processing, addressing informational voids, and managing overarching contextual frameworks, alongside the unresolved inquiries that persist.

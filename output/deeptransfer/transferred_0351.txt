We propose TACO, a test-time scaling framework for Vision-Language Architectures (VLAs) that retains the robust generalization capabilities of pretrained VLAs while effectively constraining outputs to align with the success modes of specific downstream tasks. Moreover, we introduce an efficient internal representation mechanism for pseudo-count estimation within Contextual Feature Networks (CFN), enabling accurate measurement of distributional shifts with minimal computational overhead. We demonstrate that TACO significantly enhances the performance of various VLA models across both simulated and real-world tasks, facilitating rapid deployment without extensive training durations. In the context of reinforcement learning, an important distinction exists between online and offline approaches. In online reinforcement learning, the agent actively interacts with the environment to explore the state-action space. Conversely, offline reinforcement learning derives policy solely from a pre-existing dataset, eliminating further environmental interaction. This offline setting poses significant challenges due to the distributional shift between the behavior policy that generated the data and the target policy being optimized. Specifically, the offline dataset reflects the visitation distribution of the behavior policy, which may differ substantially from that of the learned policy. Such discrepancies can lead to severe extrapolation errors when estimating values for out-of-distribution state-action pairs. A similar distribution shift arises during the SFT phase of VLAs, where the model encodes a broad and redundant range of action modes, whereas successful behaviors for specific downstream tasks are typically confined to a more restricted subset. Consequently, when actions are sampled, the policy may generate suboptimal behaviors that deviate from the desired success modes. To address this, TACO employs an anti-exploration strategy that imposes policy constraints, ensuring the policy remains closely aligned with the support of the success data distribution. Unlike conventional offline reinforcement learning approaches that typically rely on uncertainty-aware or conservative penalties, our method adopts a straightforward count-based mechanism, which is particularly compatible with the architecture of VLA models and obviates the necessity for value estimation. Notably, count-based regularization has established its utility in both classical reinforcement learning and contemporary RLHF algorithms.

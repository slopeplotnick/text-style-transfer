We propose TACO, a test-time scaling framework for VLAs, which retains the robust generalization capabilities of pretrained VLAs while effectively constraining outputs to the success modes of specific downstream tasks. Moreover, we introduce an efficient internal representation mechanism for pseudo-count estimation within CFN, enabling precise measurement of distributional shifts with minimal computational overhead. Consequently, TACO significantly enhances the performance of diverse VLA models across both simulated and real-world tasks, minimizing the need for extensive training durations and facilitating rapid deployment with low latency. In online RL (Sutton et al., 1999), the agent actively interacts with the environment to explore the state-action space (Yang et al., 2021). In contrast, offline RL operates exclusively on a fixed, previously collected dataset, eliminating further environmental interaction (Levine et al., 2020a; Rashidinejad et al., 2021). However, this setting introduces substantial challenges due to the distributional shift between the behavior policy that generated the data and the target policy being learned (Wu et al., 2019). Specifically, the offline dataset reflects the visitation distribution of the behavior policy, which may diverge significantly from that of the learned policy (Agarwal et al., 2019). This divergence can result in severe extrapolation errors when estimating values for out-of-distribution (OOD) state-action pairs (Kumar et al., 2020; Bai et al., 2022). Similarly, during the SFT stage of VLAs, the model encodes a broad spectrum of redundant action modes, whereas successful behaviors for downstream tasks typically occupy a much narrower region. Consequently, when sampling actions in flow or diffusion-based processes, the policy may generate suboptimal behaviors (Lipman et al., 2023; Ho et al., 2020). To address this, TACO employs an anti-exploration strategy (RezaEIFar et al., 2022; Nikulin et al., 2023) that imposes constraints on the policy, ensuring proximity to the support of the success data distribution. Unlike conventional offline RL approaches, which predominantly rely on uncertainty-aware or conservative penalties (Fujimoto and Gu, 2021; Kidambi et al., 2020; An et al., 2021), our method adopts a simple count-based mechanism (Ostrovski et al., 2017; Bellemare et al., 2016; Lobel et al., 2023). This approach is particularly well-suited to the architecture of VLA models and operates without requiring value estimation (Kostrikov et al., 2022). Notably, count-based regularization has been previously utilized in both classical RL frameworks and modern RLHF algorithms.

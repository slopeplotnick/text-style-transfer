Developing virtual characters that replicate human-like movements is crucial for creating immersive experiences in games and virtual reality applications. This capability is equally important in robotics, where naturalistic motion can enhance safety, optimize energy efficiency [Escontrela et al. 2022], and facilitate the learning of versatile skills from human motion data [Grauman et al.]. Given the expressive and nuanced nature of human motion, data-driven methodologies have emerged as an effective paradigm for producing realistic behaviors by leveraging real-world motion data. Kinematics-based methods typically employ models such as autoregressive models, variational autoencoders, or diffusion models to synthesize plausible animations. However, these methods often fail to enforce physical constraints, leading to unrealistic behaviors, especially in novel scenarios. In contrast, physics-based methods aim to generate motion governed by dynamic equations and realistic physical laws, employing techniques like trajectory optimization and reinforcement learning. Previous data-free approaches relied on heuristics to produce robotic movement, but often resulted in unnatural motions. Designing heuristics that capture the expressiveness of human movement remains a significant challenge. Instead, data-driven approaches using model predictive control (MPC) and trajectory optimization have shown promise in emulating naturalistic human motions. More recently, reinforcement learning-based methods have enabled controllers to effectively imitate a wide range of motion clips, resulting in more life-like and agile behaviors. Nevertheless, tracking-based methods constrain controllers to replicate specific motion clips, which can limit their ability to adapt to new tasks. To address this limitation, many systems incorporate task-specific motion planners or high-level policies to select optimal movements or skills tailored to task requirements.

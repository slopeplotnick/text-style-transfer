Creating virtual characters with human-like movements is vital for enhancing the realism of games and virtual reality environments. This capability is equally significant in robotics, where adopting naturalistic motion can improve safety and optimize energy consumption. Moreover, the ability to learn from human movement data facilitates the acquisition of versatile skills applicable across various contexts. To generate realistic character movements, data-driven approaches are commonly employed, leveraging real-world motion data to inform the synthesis process. Techniques such as autoregressive models, variational autoencoders, and diffusion models are utilized to produce plausible animations. However, these methods often fail to adhere to fundamental physical principles, resulting in motion artifacts that appear unnatural, particularly when applied to novel scenarios or tasks. In contrast, physics-based methodologies endeavor to generate motion that adheres to real-world physics principles by employing control policies and dynamic equations. These approaches incorporate advanced techniques such as trajectory optimization and reinforcement learning. Earlier methods that did not leverage data-driven strategies were capable of producing robotic movements, yet these movements frequently lacked naturalistic qualities. Replicating human-like movements in robots through heuristic estimations proves challenging. Consequently, contemporary methodologies have favored data-driven approaches, such as model predictive control (MPC) and trajectory optimization, which demonstrate enhanced efficacy in emulating naturalistic human motions. Furthermore, recent advancements in reinforcement learning-based techniques have facilitated the imitation of diverse reference motion clips, thereby yielding more realistic and agile behaviors. However, these methods constrain the robot to replicating the actions depicted in the motion clip, hindering its ability to generalize to novel tasks. To mitigate this limitation, certain systems employ task-specific planners or high-level policies to select optimal movements or skills appropriate for the task at hand.

Researchers, as demonstrated in studies [25] and [15], have introduced structured moral reasoning benchmarks, such as MoralBench, to evaluate the alignment of AI models with human moral intuitions. These studies revealed that while models display partial moral sensitivity, they often falter when navigating conflicting principles or complex moral dilemmas. However, benchmark-based evaluations are inherently constrained in scalability and temporal scope, capturing ethical reasoning at a single point in time and failing to account for how reasoning consistency evolves through interaction and retraining. Complementary research has explored explainability and value alignment as foundational elements of responsible AI development. For instance, Shitong et al. [9] proposed DeNEVIL, a debiasing and explainable alignment model that integrates normative reasoning into AI systems. Similarly, Jiao et al. [16] investigated cross-cultural moral benchmarking and trust calibration, highlighting the sociotechnical interdependencies central to ethical AI systems. While these methodologies excel in enhancing interpretability and fostering trust, they rely heavily on predefined moral categories and manual labeling, limiting their adaptability to dynamic ethical contexts. Although prior work has significantly advanced fairness auditing, benchmark design, and value-alignment strategies, a critical gap remains: the lack of a dynamic, model-agnostic framework for longitudinal moral evaluation. Current approaches assess ethical behavior at discrete intervals but lack mechanisms for the autonomous generation, interpretation, and adaptation of ethical reasoning scenarios. Furthermore, existing methodologies fail to provide computational tools to quantify ethical divergence or monitor moral consistency across models in real time. Building on the conceptual foundation outlined in Section 1, the proposed Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP) introduces a continuous, dataset-independent framework designed to evaluate, interpret, and assess the moral stability of large language models (LLMs). Unlike optimization or retraining-focused approaches, MoCoP prioritizes ethical evaluation and interpretative analysis, ensuring both conceptual clarity and methodological coherence.

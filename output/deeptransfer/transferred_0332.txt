As studies [25] and [15] proposed, structured moral reasoning benchmarks, such as MoralBench, have been developed to evaluate LLM responses against human moral intuitions. These investigations demonstrated that while models exhibit partial moral sensitivity, they often lack coherence when faced with conflicting principles or contextual ambiguity. However, benchmark-based evaluations are inherently limited in scalability and temporal scope; they capture model ethics at a single point in time and fail to reveal how reasoning consistency evolves through interaction and retraining. In response, several studies have explored methodologies to enhance explainability and value alignment as critical components of responsible AI. For instance, Shitong et al. [9] introduced DeNEVIL, a debiasing and explainable alignment model that integrates normative reasoning into LLM training. Similarly, Jiao et al. [16] emphasized the importance of cross-cultural moral benchmarking and trust calibration, highlighting the sociotechnical dependencies of ethical AI systems. While these approaches demonstrate strengths in fostering interpretability and trust, they rely on predefined moral categories and manual labeling, which constrain their adaptability to dynamic ethical contexts. Although prior research has significantly advanced fairness auditing, benchmark design, and value-alignment methodologies, a critical gap persists: the absence of a dynamic, model-agnostic framework for longitudinal moral evaluation. Current approaches predominantly assess ethical behavior at discrete intervals but lack mechanisms for the autonomous generation, interpretation, and adaptation of ethical reasoning scenarios. Furthermore, they do not provide computational tools to quantify divergences in ethical reasoning or to monitor moral consistency across models in real time. Building on the conceptual foundation established in Section 1, the proposed Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP) introduces a continuous, dataset-independent mechanism for assessing, interpreting, and refining the moral stability of LLMs. Unlike prior approaches, MoCoP prioritizes evaluation and interpretation over optimization or retraining, ensuring methodological clarity and alignment with its foundational objectives.

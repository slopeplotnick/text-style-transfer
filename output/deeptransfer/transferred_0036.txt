The selection of negative samples covers all conceivable active nodes. In Section 5, we demonstrate that integrating random and adversarial negatives is essential to surpass the performance of TextMatch. The concept of Contrastive Loss involves formulating the training objective to enable the model to accurately identify the appropriate positive candidate for each query from the available options within the batch. The encoder is initialized from publicly available E5-unsupervised checkpoints, comprising 33 million and 330 million parameters, which are derived from advanced contrastive pretraining methodologies. Consistent encoder weights are employed for both queries and candidates. Prior to the fine-tuning phase, dense interaction data encompassing 110 million samples are utilized, derived from weak supervision signals such as user clicks or saves, as well as naturally occurring title-description pairs. During the fine-tuning phase, however, the methodology employs a reduced number of interactions characterized by significant strength, indicative of mutual interest, such as interviews or hires. This is complemented by the incorporation of hard negatives derived from items that were displayed but not engaged with, as extracted from interaction logs. The training dataset for GraphMatch encompasses one year of user interaction data sourced from Upwork, partitioned into a temporal split of 10 months for training, 1 month for validation, and 1 month for evaluation. The dataset comprises nodes representing freelancers, clients, and job postings, alongside edges denoting interactions such as job postings, applications, invitations, interviews, and contract initiations. Freelancer and job post nodes are endowed with textual features, such as profile overviews and job descriptions, respectively. Moreover, all nodes possess additional numerical attributes pertinent to their specific type, including, but not limited to, freelancer hourly rates, client locations, and job post categories. It is noteworthy that these textual and numerical features are subject to temporal variation. In total, the dataset comprises approximately 9 million nodes, encompassing 32 million temporal feature versions and 62 million edges. Finally, the GraphMatch model is trained utilizing robust labels derived from contractual agreements established between freelancers and job postings.

The set of negative samples covers all potential active nodes. In Section 5, we show that the combination of random and adversarial negatives is required to surpass the performance of TextMatch. The concept of Contrastive Loss involves formulating the training objective such that, for each query, the model is trained to accurately identify the correct positive candidate among all candidates in the batch. The encoder is initialized from publicly available E5-unsupervised checkpoints, comprising both small (33 million parameters) and large (330 million parameters) models, which are themselves the result of advanced contrastive pretraining. We use the same encoder weights for both queries and candidates. Prior to the fine-tuning phase, dense interaction data derived from 110 million samples is utilized, leveraging weak signals such as clicks or saves, as well as naturally occurring pairs of titles and descriptions. The fine-tuning phase, however, employs more sparse but stronger interactions, indicative of mutual interest, such as interviews or hires. This is complemented by hard negatives derived from items that were displayed but not engaged with, extracted from interaction logs. The GraphMatch training dataset encompasses one year of user interaction data sourced from Upwork, with a temporal division of 10 months allocated for training, 1 month for validation, and 1 month for evaluation. The dataset consists of nodes representing freelancers, clients, and job posts, alongside edges denoting interactions such as job postings, applications, invitations, interviews, and contract initiations. Freelancer and job post nodes are characterized by textual attributes such as profile overviews and job descriptions. Additionally, all nodes possess supplementary numerical attributes pertinent to their respective categories, including freelancer hourly rates, client locations, and job post categories. Notably, both textual and numerical attributes are subject to temporal variations. In total, the dataset comprises approximately 9 million nodes, encompassing 32 million temporal feature versions and 62 million edges. We train GraphMatch on strong labels derived from contracts between freelancers and job posts.

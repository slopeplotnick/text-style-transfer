Guiding LLMs on automated program repair, CFAULTS has been integrated with Large Language Models (LLMs) through a methodology that enhances automated program repair for C programming assignments. In this approach, CFAULTS identifies the erroneous segments of a C program and presents the LLM with a modified version of the program, excluding these defective components. This hybrid method follows a Counterexample Guided Inductive Synthesis (CEGIS) loop to iteratively refine the program. The LLM is then tasked with reconstructing the missing parts, which are subsequently validated against a test suite. If the initial repair attempt is unsuccessful, a counterexample from the test suite is provided to the LLM to guide a revised synthesis. This iterative process enables LLMs to achieve higher success rates in program repair, producing more concise modifications compared to alternative approaches that do not leverage FBFL techniques or state-of-the-art symbolic program repair tools. Fault localisation methodologies are broadly categorized into two principal types: spectrum-based fault localisation (SBFL) and formula-based fault localisation (FBFL). SBFL techniques utilize test outcomes to infer the likelihood of specific program components being defective. However, these methods are not invariably precise, as they do not ensure that the identified components are indeed responsible for the observed anomalies. In contrast, FBFL approaches exhibit greater accuracy. These methods conceptualize fault localisation as a Model-Based Diagnosis (MBD) problem, aiming to identify the minimal set of faulty components. For each failing test, FBFL methods employ a systematic approach to ascertain the minimal diagnosis pertinent to that specific failure. Subsequently, these individual diagnoses are amalgamated to formulate a comprehensive explanation elucidating the program's malfunction.

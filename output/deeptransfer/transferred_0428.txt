Although the integration of GPT-5 Pro mitigates the issue of score inflation to some extent, significant discrepancies persist in dimensions such as Dynamism, Coherence, and Immutability, where the model continues to overestimate its performance. However, the incorporation of a structured checklist, coupled with a requirement for the model to substantiate its ratings with evidence extracted from key frames, contributes to a marked reduction in these overestimations. Moreover, the integration of computer vision tools such as ByteTrack, which facilitates reliable identity tracking across frames and provides robust evidence for penalizing frame discontinuities or unexpected object transformations, significantly mitigates scoring disparities, with improvements of 0.07 in Coherence and 0.09 in Immutability compared to the baseline VideoScience-Judge lacking the checklist. Nevertheless, this adjustment contributes to score quantization and diminishes the ability to discriminate between models with similar performance. Its rule-based methodology, which focuses on identifying explicit violations of discrete physical laws (e.g., "Does the ball fall downward?" or "Does water flow correctly?"), fails to capture the broader dimensions of physical realism, such as consistent motion trajectories, causal relationships across temporal sequences, acceleration patterns, momentum conservation, and the nuanced smoothness of motion that human evaluators inherently perceive. These limitations are particularly evident in VideoScience-Bench, which diverges substantially from the simplified, single-principle scenarios of PhyGenBench. VideoScience-Bench encompasses multiple scientific concepts, including multi-object interactions and fluid dynamics, where outcomes are governed by the interplay of multiple laws rather than isolated principles, often involving cascading effects where second-order dynamics play a critical role.

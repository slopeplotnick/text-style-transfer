Distribution matching provides an alternative to motion tracking by training controllers to imitate the broader behavioral patterns of a motion dataset rather than relying on frame-by-frame tracking of reference motions. Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) achieves this by employing a learned discriminator to distinguish between the agent's motions and those within the dataset [Ho and Ermon 2016]. Building on this concept, Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) enable controllers to generate realistic behaviors for novel tasks absent from the original dataset by modeling flexible style objectives [Peng et al. 2021]. However, adversarial priors require the training of a separate policy for each new task, necessitating retraining and continued access to the original dataset. In contrast, our approach achieves comparable performance to these state-of-the-art methods by introducing a reusable and modular score-matching motion prior, which can be adapted to accommodate stylistic variations beyond those present in the initial dataset. Diffusion Models for Control. The growing popularity of diffusion models stems from their exceptional performance across diverse domains, which has sparked interest in their application to control tasks. Due to their ability to generate high-fidelity motion, diffusion models are often employed as motion planners that predict target future trajectories, subsequently executed by a low-level tracking controller [Ren et al. 2023; Serifi et al. 2024; Tevet et al. 2024; Xu et al. 2025]. Moreover, diffusion models have been utilized to directly model controllers, enabling the generation of flexible and diverse behaviors for tasks such as manipulation [Chi et al. 2023; Janner et al. 2022] and character control [Huang et al. 2024a, 2025; Truong et al. 2024; Wu et al. 2025b]. Unlike prior methods that integrate diffusion models as components of a controller, our work repurposes pretrained diffusion models as task-agnostic behavioral priors within an optimization framework. Specifically, we leverage these models as a reward function within reinforcement learning frameworks, guiding policy acquisition through score distillation sampling.

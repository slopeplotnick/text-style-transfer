In all three models, the total causal influence of the variable 'young' on the image and prediction remains consistent, demonstrating that it serves as a reliable measure regardless of the level of abstraction. However, if an explanation model disregards the causal structure by treating variables as independent, it captures only the direct effect. This direct effect may coincide with the total effect in the first model but is reduced to zero in the last model. Consequently, direct influence does not constitute a consistent measure. Section 5.3.1 addresses the challenges that arise when model assumptions are violated, thereby underscoring the necessity for careful selection of models. Whenever assumptions introduce bias into explanations, it is imperative either to modify the model or to inform the explainer of the biased context within which the explanations are applicable. This should not be construed as an indication that complex models with fewer assumptions are invariably preferable to simpler models. To ensure that explanations align with their intended interpretation, the causal model must correspond to the level of abstraction inherent in the explainee's understanding of the world. Prioritizing comprehensibility necessitates the omission of certain detailed causal mechanisms, thereby condensing essential information into a more accessible and abstract representation. A notable advantage of the causal explanation model outlined in Section 5.3.1 is its reliance on expert knowledge, which enhances its robustness in identifying and mitigating spurious correlations compared to approaches that are exclusively data-driven. Thus, the explanation model is capable of identifying spurious relationships learned by the predictor, even in instances where the same dataset is utilized to train the explanation model. With the increasing accessibility of expert knowledge facilitated by large language models (LLMs), as demonstrated in the provided example, this capability holds particular promise for enhancing explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) by enabling the detection of unintended model behaviors.

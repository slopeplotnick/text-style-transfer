Software Product Lines (SPLs) are a well-established approach for efficiently developing highly customizable software systems. Conceptually similar to a software supply chain, an SPL consists of modular components that collectively enable the systematic production of software products. Variability models are crucial within this framework, as they delineate the available product features and specify permissible configurations to meet diverse requirements. The software product line includes a repository of software components that facilitate the functionality of these features. Tools within the SPL are employed to select features, thereby enabling the construction of a tailored configuration, and subsequently, the actual product is assembled by integrating components from the repository. This approach becomes advantageous when diverse configurations are required. Planning for the introduction of a new product type presents significant challenges for a company. Consequently, many organizations initially develop a family of analogous software systems before committing to the transition of their development processes into a software product line. Despite documented successful instances in the literature, migrating an existing software family into a cohesive SPL platform remains a formidable task, and organizations may exhibit reluctance in adopting this approach. Hesitations frequently emerge due to the absence of standardized procedures to guide the process and uncertainties regarding the cost-benefit ratio. Therefore, despite possessing extensive repositories of well-documented code, companies continue to manually construct customized applications from the foundational code to satisfy client requirements, predominantly employing a clone-and-own strategy. Our collaborator, ITK - Predict and Decide, is a small to medium-sized enterprise comprising 120 personnel, among whom are 19 agronomists and 29 developers, including 2 UI/UX designers and 4 system administrators. Approximately one-third of the company's workforce comprises tech-oriented collaborators. The organization employs an agile methodology for system development, characterized by a well-functioning development and delivery process. The applications developed by the company facilitate decision-making for farmers by enabling them to predict diseases and crop outcomes for both plants and livestock. The primary component of these applications comprises simulators developed by agronomists. Subsequently, IT teams implement additional code to collect data from IoT devices or online services, which serves to populate the simulators and display the resultant data on dashboards. The software's back-end architecture is constructed utilizing Kotlin, while the front-end is developed using Angular, with a significant emphasis on Typescript.

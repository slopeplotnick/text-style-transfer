Software Product Lines are a well-established approach for efficiently developing highly configurable software systems. At their core, a Software Product Line operates as a software supply chain, consisting of several interconnected components. Variability models define the available product features and outline the permissible configurations for combining these features into software products. The asset base contains the software components that implement these features. Tools within the Software Product Line enable the selection of features to create a specific configuration and subsequently generate the final product by assembling the corresponding assets from the base. This approach becomes particularly advantageous when a large number of configurations need to be developed. However, planning the adoption of a Software Product Line can be challenging for companies introducing a new product type. As a result, many organizations initially develop a family of similar software systems before deciding to transition their development processes to a Software Product Line. Despite successful cases documented in the literature, the migration of an existing software family into a unified Software Product Line platform remains a complex endeavor, and organizations may hesitate to adopt this approach. Such reluctance often arises from the lack of standardized procedures to guide the transition, as well as uncertainties regarding the cost-benefit trade-off. Consequently, even companies with extensive repositories of well-documented code often continue to manually develop customized applications from their base code to meet specific client requirements, frequently relying on a clone-and-own strategy. Our partner, ITK - Predict and Decide, is a small-to-medium enterprise with 120 collaborators at the time of writing, including 19 agronomists and 29 developers, among whom are 2 UI/UX designers and 4 system administrators. Approximately one-third of the company’s workforce consists of tech-oriented collaborators. The organization employs an agile methodology to develop its systems, ensuring an efficient development and delivery process. The applications developed by the company support farmers in decision-making by enabling them to predict plant and livestock diseases as well as forecast crop yields. The core of these applications is a set of simulators created by agronomists. Subsequently, IT teams implement additional code to collect data from IoT devices or online services, which feed the simulators and display their output data on dashboards. The back-end of the software is built using Kotlin, while the front-end is developed with Angular, with a strong emphasis on the use of TypeScript.

Despite substantial advancements in the generative modeling of sound and music, the evaluation of these systems remains without a standardized methodology. Beyond auditory assessments, researchers have proposed a series of objective metrics to evaluate the fidelity of audio reconstructions and the efficacy of neural networks in classifying the generated music into pre-existing categories. In this study, we employ both auditory evaluations and a set of objective metrics previously utilized in related research to assess the effectiveness of our symbolic music generation. We generate 100 MIDI samples for each segment of the test set (GoEmotions) and evaluate the outcomes. As part of our initial listening study, and to gain a preliminary understanding of the model's performance, we solicited feedback from human participants on how well the generated music aligned with the intended emotional content of the text. This task is inherently more subjective than conventional music generation evaluations, as it involves two layers of interpretation: listeners must first interpret the emotional content of the narrative and then assess whether the corresponding musical output appropriately conveys the identified emotion. Initially, each participant was presented with a textual excerpt and required to classify it into one of four emotion categories within the valence-arousal framework. Subsequently, they were exposed to two musical selections corresponding to the narrative and tasked with selecting the one they perceived as most congruent with the emotional tone of the story. This bifurcated methodology enabled us to evaluate both the consistency of emotion perception at the text level and the degree of emotional congruence between the narrative and the musical accompaniment. Since not all musical selections corresponded precisely to the emotional category chosen by the participant, we evaluated the emotional congruence based on the valence-arousal dimensions rather than exact categorical matches. Each category was delineated into binary valence (positive/negative) and arousal (high/low) values. For each participant's selection, we assessed whether the chosen musical sample aligned with the participant's perceived emotional content of the story along these dimensions. This approach provided measures of valence accuracy, arousal accuracy, and combined valence-arousal accuracy, thereby offering a more adaptable and perceptually informed metric of emotional concordance.

Despite significant advances in the computational generation of sound and music, the evaluation of these systems remains without a standardized methodology. Aside from auditory assessments, researchers have proposed a series of objective metrics for appraising audio reconstructions and the efficacy of neural networks in categorizing the generated music into pre-existing classifications. In this study, we employ both auditory evaluations and a selection of objective metrics previously utilized in the literature to assess the effectiveness of our symbolic music generation approach. We generate 100 MIDI samples per quadrant for texts from our test set (GoEmotions) and evaluate the outcomes. As part of our preliminary listening study, we aim to assess the congruence between the generated music and the intended emotional content of the text by soliciting evaluations from human listeners. This task is notably more subjective than conventional music generation endeavors, as it necessitates dual layers of interpretation: listeners must initially discern the emotional nuances of the narrative and subsequently evaluate whether the accompanying music aptly embodies that emotion. In the first phase, each participant was presented with a textual stimulus and tasked with categorizing it into one of four predefined quadrants within the valence-arousal framework. In the second phase, they were provided with two musical excerpts associated with the narrative and instructed to select the option they perceived as most congruent with the emotional tone of the text. This two-phase experimental design enabled the evaluation of both the consistency in emotion attribution to textual stimuli and the perceived alignment between textual and musical emotional representations. However, since not all musical selections corresponded precisely to the emotion category chosen by the participant, we assessed emotional congruence based on the dimensions of valence (positive/negative) and arousal (high/low) rather than strict categorical matches. Each category was delineated into binary valence and arousal values, and for each participant's selection, we evaluated whether the chosen musical piece aligned with the participant's perception of the story's emotion along these axes. This approach provided a measure of valence accuracy, arousal accuracy, and combined valence and arousal accuracy, thereby offering a more flexible and perceptually grounded metric of emotional concordance.

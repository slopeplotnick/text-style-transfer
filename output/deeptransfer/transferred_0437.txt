The transition towards increased electrification has heightened global attention on the exploration of critical minerals. Despite the anticipated rise in demand, the exploration sector remains a financially unprofitable enterprise. Even with augmented investments, there has been a decline in new discoveries over the past two decades. In this paper, I propose a solution to this problem by advocating for the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) as a means to establish a more rigorous and scientific framework for mineral exploration. This approach aims to mitigate cognitive biases, reduce false positives, enhance the role of domain experts, and lower the overall costs associated with exploration activities. However, the current methodologies, which rely on the integration of multiple scientific disciplines such as geology, geochemistry, and geophysics, have proven inadequate for the discovery of mineral deposits concealed beneath cover. Specifically, these traditional approaches fail to effectively quantify uncertainty, resulting in suboptimal decision-making processes and significant financial losses due to unsuccessful drilling efforts. Therefore, I propose an innovative approach grounded in the principles of Bayesianism and falsification, wherein the primary objective of data collection is to rigorously test human hypotheses. The selection of subsequent data acquisition is guided by well-defined metrics and informed decision-making. A practical framework is provided that can be applied to any mineral exploration endeavor. To operationalize this framework, the integration of various forms of artificial intelligence is imperative. Paramount among these are novel unsupervised learning methodologies that collaborate with domain experts to enhance data comprehension and generate diverse geological hypotheses. Moreover, human-in-the-loop AI algorithms are essential for optimally orchestrating data acquisition across geological, geophysical, geochemical, and drilling domains. This approach prioritizes the reduction of uncertainty in geological hypotheses before addressing concerns related to mineral grade and tonnage. The efficacy of this method will be demonstrated through its application in contemporary mineral exploration and resource evaluation scenarios.

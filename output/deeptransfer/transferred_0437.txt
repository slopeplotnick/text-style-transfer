The global shift towards increased electrification has drawn significant attention to the exploration of critical minerals. Despite the growing demand for these resources, the mineral exploration industry remains largely unprofitable. Even with heightened investments, the rate of new discoveries has markedly declined over the past two decades. In this paper, I propose a solution to this challenge by implementing artificial intelligence as a key enabler of a more rigorous scientific method for mineral exploration. This approach aims to reduce cognitive biases and false positives, enhance the role of domain experts, and lower exploration costs. The current framework for exploration, which integrates multiple scientific disciplines such as geology, geophysics, and geochemistry, has proven insufficient for uncovering deposits concealed beneath the surface. Moreover, it fails to adequately quantify uncertainty, leading to suboptimal decision-making and significant financial resources being wasted on drilling efforts that often result in false positives. To address these shortcomings, I propose a new scientific method grounded in the principles of Bayesian inference and falsification. Under this framework, data acquisition is primarily viewed as a tool to systematically challenge and refine human-generated geological hypotheses. Decisions regarding subsequent data collection are guided by quantifiable metrics and rational decision-making processes. A practical protocol is outlined, offering a versatile template applicable to a wide range of exploration campaigns. However, to operationalize this protocol effectively, the integration of various forms of artificial intelligence is essential. Among these, the most critical are novel unsupervised learning methods that collaborate with domain experts to enhance data interpretation and generate multiple competing geological hypotheses. Additionally, human-in-the-loop AI algorithms are vital for optimizing the planning of geological, geophysical, geochemical, and drilling data acquisition, with a primary focus on reducing uncertainty in geological hypotheses rather than solely assessing mineral grade and tonnage. This approach will be demonstrated through several ongoing case studies in mineral exploration and resource evaluation.

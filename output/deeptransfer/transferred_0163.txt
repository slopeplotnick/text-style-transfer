Similarly, the Devstral model, a relatively small open-source model, also demonstrates a more complex GRAPHECTORY when used with OpenHands. This complexity can be attributed to its training regimen, which leverages the OpenHands agent scaffold for software tasks [6], encouraging extensive searches and rigorous testing before implementing modifications. Furthermore, we aim to explore the correlation between process-centric metrics and repair outcomes, specifically whether the agent successfully resolves the issue. Notably, many premature terminations were observed due to runtime issues caused by the model's failure to generate responses in the correct format, a recognized limitation. Process-centric metrics, however, show a clear correlation with both repair success and human-rated difficulty. Successful runs tend to follow shorter, more focused trajectories, whereas unresolved attempts are characterized by longer paths and redundant loops. The complexity of the GRAPHECTORY is also influenced by the agent's architectural design and the underlying language models. Stronger models produce denser GRAPHECTORIES with broader exploration. Beyond assessing the success or failure of agents, we further examine agent behavior through the Phase Flow Analysis series (§2.3.1). Successful runs typically adhere to structured processes of localization, patching, and validation that align with the agents' intended objectives. As task complexity increases, successful trajectories become longer but remain coherent, while unsuccessful ones exhibit repetitive or disordered phase transitions. Superior language models demonstrate richer strategic adaptability and greater efficacy.

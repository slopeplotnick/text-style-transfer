In this context, language models significantly enhance the monitoring of mental health compared to traditional methodologies. They surpass other computational approaches in their ability to comprehend context and acquire information, thereby rendering them more effective for large-scale mental health surveillance. However, the deployment of large language models presents certain challenges: they are computationally intensive and pose privacy concerns due to the processing of sensitive data on remote servers. Moreover, their substantial size renders them impractical for deployment in resource-constrained on-device environments, necessitating the exploration of more efficient alternatives. Conversely, Small Language Models (SLMs) present comparable or even superior potential for digital health applications due to their streamlined architectures, reduced inference costs, and capacity for local deployment. This facilitates a privacy-preserving, on-device workflow for mental health prediction from social media activity, utilizing lightweight SLMs to enable in-situ AI screening across multiple mental health dimensions without the retention of personal data, thereby allowing for real-time, low-resource, and ethically-aligned monitoring. Despite their promise, the potential of SLMs for mental health prediction remains insufficiently explored. In this study, we investigate the efficacy of SLMs in predicting digital mental health outcomes and demonstrate that, when appropriately designed and optimized, SLMs represent viable alternatives for conducting multi-task mental health assessments directly on personal devices. To evaluate their performance, we assess these models across a range of mental health classification tasks, including stress, depression, and suicidality. We present Menta, a compact model fine-tuned through a LoRA-based multi-task framework incorporating cross-dataset training and an accuracy-oriented loss function. Menta demonstrates robust performance across a range of mental health tasks, with particular efficacy in stress and depression detection. Furthermore, we highlight its practical applicability by deploying the model on an iPhone 15 Pro Max, where it achieves real-time inference with minimal memory consumption.

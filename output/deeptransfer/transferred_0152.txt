Our data and results are publicly accessible in an online repository: https://cite.lirmm.fr/tgeorges/interviewartefact. The repository includes the compiled questions and answers, the analysis of interviews, thematic categorizations, the notebook used for data processing, and several bar charts illustrating the responses. In this section, we analyze the findings corresponding to each research question: current good practices (Sect. 4.1), benefits (Sect. 4.2), risks (Sect. 4.3), and fears (Sect. 4.4) associated with the future SPL. The results are presented according to topics and roles, adhering to the methodology that guides the design process. The responses provided by the interviewees are evenly distributed. When analyzing the data based on participants' years of professional experience, both the least and most experienced individuals contributed a comparable number of responses. For instance, when asked, "Which good practices can be derived from this approach?", developers with either 4 or 7 years of experience provided identical responses. Multiple bar charts depicting responses categorized by years of experience are available in the repository linked within the article. This segment of the interviews aimed to identify prevailing good practices deemed essential for retention in the new approach. The most frequently cited practice is Factorization and Reuse. This is substantiated by team discussions in which each new feature is evaluated to determine whether it is exclusive to a single application or can be generalized for use across multiple applications. Moreover, the concept of innovation serves as a significant impetus, encouraging the team to maintain the currency of their skills. This is important to the participants, who have the opportunity to consistently revise the code.

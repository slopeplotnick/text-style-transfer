A prominent application of retrieval-and-synthesis capabilities is the AI Overviews feature within Google Search. Utilizing the Gemini model family, this system functions as a retrieval-enhanced generation pipeline, processing multiple long-context documents to produce concise, cited summaries [Reid, 2024]. While this feature significantly enhances user efficiency by minimizing the need to manually synthesize information from diverse sources, it also raises critical concerns about the reliability of the synthesized content. Specifically, when an LLM integrates multiple documents into a single summary, it becomes imperative to evaluate whether the model assigns uniform weight to all input sources or exhibits systematic structural biases. Previous studies have extensively explored ideological biases within LLMs. For instance, Santurkar et al. [2023] demonstrated, using survey-style prompts, that models such as GPT-4 and Claude often reflect liberal-leaning social values. Furthermore, in the domain of news summarization, Vijay et al. [2024] introduced quantitative metrics—such as vocabulary divergence (VD), stance polarization index (SPI), and algorithmic monoculture index (AMI)—to reveal how LLMs frequently converge on narrow ideological framings. However, while ideological and content-level biases in LLM-generated summaries have been rigorously analyzed, structural biases in how LLMs process multiple input documents remain underexplored. In particular, in multi-document contexts involving long concatenated inputs, it is unclear whether the positional arrangement of documents within the input sequence influences the model’s weighting or prioritization. Positional effects, such as primacy bias or lost-in-the-middle phenomena, have been observed in other long-context tasks; however, their implications for multi-document summarization have received limited attention. To address this gap, this study investigates whether the sequential ordering of source articles affects their representational prominence in LLM-generated summaries, using abortion news articles as a case study.

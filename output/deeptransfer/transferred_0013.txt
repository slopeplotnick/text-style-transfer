Forecasting stock prices represents a fundamental and enduring challenge in the field of finance. The inherent volatility of stock price movements, coupled with their acute sensitivity to emerging information, often renders traditional predictive methods inadequate for processing unstructured data sources such as news events and market sentiment. Consequently, these approaches frequently fail to capture the key drivers behind price fluctuations. The rapid proliferation of online textual data, combined with transformative advancements in the natural language understanding capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), has significantly accelerated research into text-based stock price forecasting. Nevertheless, most existing studies leveraging LLMs remain confined to superficial text analysis and immediate inference. These studies often lack systematic knowledge integration and fail to incorporate long-term memory architectures, which severely limits their ability to adapt to the dynamic and complex nature of financial markets. Therefore, there is a critical need for models equipped with external memory mechanisms that can retain, associate, and retrieve salient information across temporal spans. This need is further amplified by the unique characteristics of financial markets, where price fluctuations rarely stem from isolated events but rather emerge from the sequential interplay of interconnected occurrences over time. Moreover, the paradigm of news-based stock prediction diverges fundamentally from conventional question-answering tasks. Predicting tomorrow’s stock price movements cannot be directly inferred from news articles, as the target information is not explicitly stated. Instead, models must dynamically construct comprehensive market representations from heterogeneous information flows, identify analogous historical patterns, and engage in deep analytical reasoning to transition from mere text matching to genuine market pattern discovery. Compounding these challenges, financial texts are inherently noisy, often containing substantial redundant and irrelevant information that can obscure signals of true predictive value. Thus, accurate content filtering and matching are of paramount importance.

When the target is positioned at a considerable distance, the character transitions to a rapid running gait to reduce the gap. These nuanced behaviors arise purely from the stationary score-matching motion prior combined with a straightforward target-distance task objective. Notably, no explicit motion planning is required to dictate the character's movement strategy. The stationary score-matching motion prior enables the system to leverage existing behaviors within the dataset, allowing for the adaptation and synthesis of novel skills tailored to distinct tasks. In the challenging Dodgeball task, agents trained with this locomotion prior autonomously develop proficient jumping and dodging capabilities, akin to human strategies in dodgeball. These emergent behaviors, although absent from the initial dataset, exhibit a resemblance to authentic human maneuvers. In contrast, the AMP framework demonstrates limited efficacy in this task, likely due to the substantial divergence between the required dodging skills and the locomotion motions present in the reference dataset. Furthermore, policies trained with AMP utilizing a frozen discriminator fail to generate naturalistic behaviors or achieve satisfactory task performance, underscoring the inability of adversarial priors to be effectively repurposed, even for identical tasks. Conversely, SMP facilitates the reuse of a single motion prior across diverse tasks and policies, thereby offering a more versatile and robust solution. The results depicted in Figure 7 demonstrate that SMP exhibits superior efficiency, consistently providing stable and informative guidance throughout the training process. The ability to recover from arbitrary fallen states is a critical skill for both humans and humanoid agents. However, this skill is challenging to acquire, as many existing methodologies employ additional reward mechanisms to mitigate erratic movements. Existing approaches, such as AMP, which adaptively update the motion configuration during training, can effectively learn natural recovery behaviors. Nevertheless, our method demonstrates that even with a stationary motion configuration, the Stationary Motion Prior (SMP) is capable of training a robust recovery policy that effectively operates from arbitrary fallen states. As illustrated in Figure 5(e), the character, despite being knocked down at a considerable distance, successfully executes a sequence of movements including rolling over, utilizing its hands to push itself upward, and ultimately achieving an upright stance.

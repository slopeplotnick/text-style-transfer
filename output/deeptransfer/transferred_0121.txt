The study involved a comparative analysis of real-world and simulated datasets. Nonparametric comparisons using Mann-Whitney U tests revealed no significant differences in central tendency for any subscale between the two datasets (U = 46912-53698, p = .09-.89). However, further analysis with Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests indicated significant differences in the overall distribution of scores for all variables (D = 0.36-0.42, all p < .001). These findings suggest that although the simulated data closely approximated the median values of the real responses, their underlying distributions differed substantially in shape. Therefore, we believe that these results partially support our fourth hypothesis. Histograms comparing the distribution of the real and simulated datasets are displayed in Figure 2. To mitigate potential biases from using a dataset we previously collected, we employed data from an independent cohort in a subsequent study. For this purpose, the English dataset used to validate the "Information and Communication Self Concept, 25 Items" (ICT-SC25) was chosen. This scale measures an individual's self-perceived competence relating to information and communication technologies and is composed of 25 Likert-type items, further broken down into five subscales: communication, information management, content creation, safe usage, and problem-solving (Schauffel et al., 2021a). This instrument was chosen due to its robust validation and public accessibility (Schauffel et al., 2021b). In line with Study 1, the hypotheses and procedures of this study were preregistered before generating the simulated data. The methodology for generating synthetic data was modified due to the absence of ethnicity information in the new dataset; consequently, only age and gender were utilized for matching purposes. The dataset contained unique identifiers for each individual, facilitating the alignment of synthetic participants with real ones based on age and gender. However, instances of duplicated identifiers were eliminated to ensure precise matching between the real and synthetic samples.

Significant advancements have been achieved in the detection of bias, mitigation of toxicity, and auditing of fairness. However, these methodologies predominantly focus on evaluating isolated model outputs rather than examining the longitudinal development of ethical reasoning or shifts in value alignment as models engage with diverse, context-sensitive, and dynamically evolving scenarios. This limitation is particularly critical in the context of autonomous, large language model (LLM)-based systems, which demonstrate adaptive and context-dependent behaviors, continuously refining their responses based on prompt structure, user intent, task requirements, and prior interactions. The lack of temporal consistency introduces the potential for ethical stances to fluctuate or deteriorate in response to variations in prompt phrasing, situational pressures, or temporal dynamics. Such inconsistencies undermine the perceived reliability, accountability, and societal trustworthiness of AI systems. Therefore, ensuring ethical consistency is not merely a matter of regulatory adherence but a fundamental requirement for fostering logically coherent, interpretable, and trustworthy reasoning within AI systems over extended periods. In the development of autonomous systems within critical domains such as healthcare, finance, education, and governance, even minor ethical instabilities can propagate misinformation, exacerbate cognitive biases, and yield outcomes misaligned with human values. Although there is increasing awareness of these issues, prevailing auditing methodologies remain constrained by static benchmarks and fixed, human-annotated datasets that fail to account for the dynamic and context-sensitive behavior of LLMs. Thus, a significant research challenge lies in devising an adaptive, self-sufficient framework capable of autonomously assessing and interpreting the ethical stability of these models without reliance on external datasets or post-hoc evaluations. To address this challenge, this study introduces the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP), an autonomous ethical evaluation framework that operates independently of datasets. MoCoP is designed to continuously monitor, interpret, and enhance the moral reasoning capabilities of AI models. Unlike conventional methods that rely on predefined test corpora and external annotation, MoCoP autonomously constructs and evaluates its own ethical scenarios. The framework comprises three primary analytical components—lexical integrity analysis, semantic risk estimation, and reasoning-based judgment modeling—to derive comprehensive measures of moral coherence and ethical stability. Each component fulfills a distinct role: lexical integrity analysis identifies bias and linguistic degradation, semantic risk estimation assesses potential semantic risks, and reasoning-based judgment modeling evaluates decision-making processes.

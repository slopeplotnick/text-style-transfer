Significant advancements have been achieved in bias detection, toxicity reduction, and fairness auditing. However, these methodologies predominantly focus on evaluating isolated model outputs rather than examining the progression of ethical reasoning or alterations in value alignment over time as models engage with diverse, context-dependent, and dynamically evolving scenarios. This limitation is particularly pertinent in the context of autonomous AI systems, which demonstrate adaptive and context-sensitive behavior, continuously adjusting their outputs in response to prompt structure, user intent, task requirements, and prior interactions. Consequently, the absence of temporal consistency in ethical reasoning introduces the risk of moral degradation, wherein ethical positions may fluctuate or deteriorate due to variations in prompt phrasing, situational pressures, or temporal dynamics. Such inconsistencies undermine the perceived reliability, accountability, and societal trust in AI systems. Therefore, ensuring ethical consistency is not merely a matter of regulatory adherence but a foundational requirement for fostering logically coherent, interpretable, and trustworthy reasoning in AI systems over time. In the development of autonomous systems within critical domains such as healthcare, finance, education, and governance, even minor ethical perturbations can propagate misinformation, reinforce cognitive biases, and yield outcomes incongruent with human values. Despite increasing awareness of these issues, prevailing auditing methodologies remain constrained by static benchmarks and fixed datasets that do not accommodate the dynamic and context-dependent behavior of large language models (LLMs). Thus, a significant research challenge lies in the design of an adaptive mechanism capable of autonomously evaluating and interpreting the ethical stability of these models, independent of external datasets or post-hoc analyses. To address this limitation, this study introduces the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP), an autonomous, dataset-independent framework for ethical evaluation, specifically designed to continuously monitor, analyze, and enhance the moral reasoning processes of large language models (LLMs). MoCoP functions as a self-contained experimental system, autonomously generating ethical scenarios, eliciting model responses, and conducting structured analyses through internally defined principles and semantic heuristics. Unlike conventional evaluation methodologies that rely on static test datasets and external annotations, MoCoP independently constructs and evaluates its own ethical scenarios, enabling a dynamic and iterative approach to assessing moral reasoning. The framework incorporates three integral analytical components—lexical integrity analysis, semantic risk evaluation, and reasoning-based judgment modeling—to derive comprehensive metrics of moral coherence and ethical stability. Each component fulfills a distinct role: lexical integrity analysis identifies linguistic bias and degradation, semantic risk evaluation assesses potential contextual harms such as toxicity or coercion, and reasoning-based judgment modeling examines the logical structure and ethical soundness of decision-making processes.

Advanced market mechanisms exhibit notable advantages in scalability, adaptability, and fault tolerance compared to centralized approaches. Botelho and Alami (1999) introduced structured auction protocols that enable competitive task assignment, while Gerkey and Matario (2002) demonstrated that auction methods can effectively distribute tasks while maintaining coordination properties. More recently, researchers have addressed communication constraints by developing sophisticated bidding mechanisms designed for scenarios with limited connectivity (Ferri et al. 2017; Quinton et al. 2022). Nevertheless, auction-based methods often fall short of guaranteeing global optimality, as local bidding decisions can disrupt coordination when tasks involve complex interdependencies. For instance, construction workflows require precise coordination among multiple trades, where tasks must follow specific sequences—a challenge for auction mechanisms that struggle with the temporal constraints essential for effective project scheduling. These limitations have driven the exploration of structured optimization approaches. Dynamic programming has emerged as an alternative strategy, offering solutions tailored to sequential decision-making processes. Bellman (1962) laid the theoretical foundation for deriving optimal solutions to problems characterized by optimal substructure. However, dynamic programming is hindered by exponential growth in state space, which restricts its practical application to smaller problem instances. In large-scale construction projects, where numerous robots execute interdependent tasks with intricate state constraints, the computational demands of real-time decision-making are prohibitive. Consequently, researchers have turned to metaheuristic approaches to address these large, complex problem domains. Population-based metaheuristics, such as genetic algorithms, leverage evolutionary principles to navigate combinatorial complexities and hierarchical constraints without requiring fully defined mathematical formulations (Jones et al. 2010; Al-Omeer and Ahmed 2019). Similarly, swarm intelligence techniques have proven effective in coordinating large robotic teams by utilizing distributed optimization capabilities suited to such scenarios (Chen et al. 2022; Lim and Isa 2015; Wang et al. 2012; Blum 2005). However, while metaheuristic methods offer flexibility, they do not guarantee optimal solutions and are computationally intensive, often requiring numerous iterations to achieve satisfactory results. In dynamic construction environments that demand rapid adaptation to changing conditions, the computational time required for these iterative processes remains a significant limitation.

Advanced market mechanisms exhibit significant advantages in scalability, adaptability, and fault tolerance compared to centralized approaches. Botelho and Alami (1999) pioneered structured auction protocols that enable competitive task assignment, while Gerkey and Matario (2002) demonstrated that auction methods can effectively distribute tasks while preserving coordination properties. More recently, advancements have addressed communication constraints by introducing sophisticated bidding mechanisms designed for limited connectivity conditions (Ferri et al. 2017; Quinton et al. 2022). Nevertheless, auction-based methods often fail to guarantee global optimality, as local bidding decisions can disrupt coordination when tasks involve complex interdependencies. For instance, construction workflows require precise coordination across multiple trades, where task sequences must adhere to specific orders. Auction mechanisms, however, struggle to accommodate these temporal constraints, which are critical for effective project scheduling. Consequently, these challenges have driven the exploration of structured optimization approaches. Dynamic programming has emerged as an alternative, offering optimization strategies well-suited to sequential decision-making scenarios. Bellman (1962) laid the theoretical foundation for deriving optimal solutions to problems with optimal substructure properties. However, dynamic programming is constrained by the exponential growth of the state space, which limits its practical application to smaller problem instances. In construction projects, where numerous robots execute interdependent tasks, the computational demands of dynamic programming render real-time decision-making infeasible. As a result, researchers have turned to metaheuristic approaches to address large-scale, complex problem domains. Population-based metaheuristics leverage evolutionary principles to explore and identify feasible solutions. Among these, genetic algorithms have proven particularly effective in managing combinatorial complexities and hierarchical constraints without requiring a fully defined mathematical formulation (Jones et al. 2010; Al-Omeer and Ahmed 2019). Similarly, swarm intelligence techniques have demonstrated robust distributed optimization capabilities, making them especially suitable for coordinating large robotic teams (Chen et al. 2022; Lim and Isa 2015; Wang et al. 2012; Blum 2005). However, while metaheuristic methods offer flexibility, they do not guarantee optimal solutions and remain computationally intensive, often requiring numerous iterations to achieve satisfactory results. In construction environments that demand rapid adaptation to evolving conditions, the time required for these iterative optimization processes becomes impractical.

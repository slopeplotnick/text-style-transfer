The purpose of this paper is to provide a forward-looking essay on the use of AI in critical mineral exploration. This is a personal perspective, characterized by subjective insights and observations derived from three decades of experience in data science, decision science, and AI within the context of Earth resources exploration and development. In 1998, I authored a paper (Caers and Journel, 1998) on the use of neural networks trained on outcrop data to enhance geological realism in reservoir models. As a very junior academic eager to make an impact, I presented the work at the Society of Petroleum Engineers Annual Technical Conference and Exhibition in New Orleans, coinciding with Hurricane George's arrival in Louisiana. Imposing geological realism is essential to accurately predict fluid flow in porous media, which ultimately leads to better decision-making in the exploration and development of oil and gas fields. In a subsequent publication (Caers and Ma, 2002) on AI for reservoir characterization, I demonstrated that neural networks can effectively learn from borehole and geophysical data to improve the prediction of reservoir properties, such as porosity, in areas lacking boreholes. A key contribution was addressing the challenge of reconciling the different scales of data, such as those obtained from borehole cores and seismic signals at substantial subsurface depths. This scale issue will be covered at length in this paper. While oil and gas companies wouldn't dream of building a large offshore platform without quantifying geological uncertainty and risk, most mines today are still developed using deterministic models. In exploration, deterministic modeling and inversion of multi-physics data remain the norm. Moreover, the minerals industry appears to lag behind the oil and gas sector in terms of advancements in quantitative risk assessment.

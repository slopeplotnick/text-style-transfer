We will begin by discussing the early foundational work in Bayesian and n-gram-based language models, which were instrumental in building probabilistic frameworks for speech recognition tasks. These models laid the groundwork for contemporary systems by effectively modeling word sequences and managing the uncertainties inherent in speech inputs. Following this, we will delve into the emergence of end-to-end speech models, which represent a significant evolution by directly integrating contextual information into the modeling process. Unlike earlier approaches, these models extend their scope beyond linguistic content to include paralinguistic features such as prosody, emotional tone, and speaker identity, enabling more comprehensive and nuanced speech understanding and generation. Furthermore, we will examine the role of large language models (LLMs) in post-ASR error correction, where they are employed to refine and enhance the outputs of traditional ASR systems. Particular attention will be given to issues of fairness and bias in speech processing, especially concerning speaker variability, including accent, dialect, and sociolect. We will explore how recent advancements address these challenges while considering the ethical implications of ensuring equitable system performance across diverse speech populations. Additionally, we will review theoretical perspectives on LLM adaptation and their integration with speech input, focusing on topics such as population risk assessment (Yang et al., 2021) and model transferability evaluation (Chen et al., 2023b) from speech models to LLM frameworks. These insights will inform the development of distinct design methodologies for spoken agents (Radhakrishnan et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023). Building on this, we will explore joint text-speech pre-training paradigms (Chiu et al., 2022; Barrault et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2022), which have demonstrated significant improvements in multimodal comprehension by aligning learning objectives across textual and speech domains. Finally, we will discuss the application of LLMs in voice quality estimation, showcasing their ability to assess and dynamically adapt to diverse speaker characteristics in real-time.

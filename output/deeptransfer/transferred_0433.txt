A novel metric, termed Verifier Gain, has been introduced to assess the utility of verifiers. Verifier accuracy alone provides an incomplete picture of their effectiveness at test time. Therefore, Verifier Gain quantifies the enhancement achieved through the application of a verifier during test-time evaluation. Our investigation into the role of verifiers in rejecting incorrect responses reveals a congruence with real-world phenomena, closely reflecting empirical performance trends. We evaluated the efficacy of various verification modalities, including self-verification, intra-group verification, and inter-group verification. Our findings indicate that inter-group verification generally yields superior results, particularly when contrasted with self-verification. This is linked to the similarities in the solution distributions of the solver and verifier: the verifier's utility diminishes as these distributions become more similar. As models advance, whether through increased size, extended training, or enhanced accuracy, they become less effective as self-verifiers but more effective in verifying disparate groups. Furthermore, we examined the relative ease of verification across various tasks. Tasks that are straightforward to solve are generally straightforward to verify; however, self-verification does not significantly enhance verifier performance across all tasks. Notably, tasks that entail mathematical or logical reasoning consistently yield superior verifier outcomes. Verifiers can operate in two distinct manners: they can assess the correctness of the final solution or evaluate the accuracy of the procedural steps leading to that solution. Recent advancements have introduced methodologies enabling models to engage in self-verification by predicting components of the question conditioned on the provided solution. To mitigate errors, certain models implement self-interrogative mechanisms through fact-checking queries. Moreover, other researchers have developed verifiers specifically trained to evaluate final responses or to collaborate synergistically with the solver. Several researchers have also focused on verifying the procedural steps involved in the process.

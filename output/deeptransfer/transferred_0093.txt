Meanwhile, a growing body of research endeavors to elucidate the operational mechanisms of diffusion models, particularly focusing on the balance between memorization—defined as the propensity to replicate training samples—and generalization, which refers to the capacity to generate novel samples. Earlier studies have approached this phenomenon by deriving quantitative metrics to assess model behavior and by investigating the underlying causes of memorization and generalization. In particular, most investigations primarily: (1) focus on model behavior when initiating sampling from scratch, and (2) validate their hypotheses using datasets of limited scale and resolution, such as FFHQ and CIFAR-10. While these insights prove beneficial in scenarios characterized by limited data availability, the propensity for memorization diminishes as datasets expand to the scale of ImageNet. Recently, Song et al. have identified that, within large-scale datasets, initiating the sampling process from earlier temporal stages facilitates the generation of novel images, whereas commencing from later stages tends to reproduce existing samples. Consequently, a pivotal inquiry arises: what mechanisms govern this balance in models trained on extensive datasets? To investigate this, we examined the training objectives of the model, focusing on flow matching and conditional flow matching, which are often regarded as challenging due to their reliance on inaccessible data distributions. However, we demonstrate that, under a Gaussian framework and with a constrained training dataset, it is possible to analytically derive the flow. This approach enables the determination of the optimal trajectory for the model at any given stage. Through the lens of this trajectory, the training process can be delineated into two principal components. Initially, the model integrates information from various data points to navigate its progression. Subsequently, the analysis narrows its focus to a singular data point. Furthermore, our findings indicate that the timing of this transition is significantly influenced by the scale of the dataset and the number of samples.

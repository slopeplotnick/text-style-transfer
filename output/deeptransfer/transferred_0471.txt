A key characteristic that simulated data must have to be suitable for computational research is the ability to directly compare specific subgroups. To test this, each study divided the simulated datasets into "Male" and "Female" cohorts and conducted invariance testing between them. If successful, this approach would enable in silico comparisons between particular demographics. In this section, we outline the common methodology and workflow employed across all four studies, except where deviations are noted in their respective sections. Figure 1 presents the experimental configurations used in each of the four studies. The simulated datasets were generated using GPT4o-mini, a variant of GPT-4 updated as of October 2023. The model was utilized with its default settings, including a Temperature of 1.0 and a Top P value of 1.0, with no frequency or presence penalties applied. The model received a procedurally generated prompt designed to simulate an individual with specified demographic attributes such as age, gender, and ethnicity, instructing it to respond to a psychometric questionnaire under investigation. The prompt included the questionnaire items, response options, and directives to format the responses as a single string of comma-separated values to facilitate subsequent data processing. Each prompt was administered independently, ensuring that responses remained unaffected by prior prompts within the same session. An example of the prompt used to generate the simulated data is as follows: "Impersonate a/an [Ethnicity] [Gender] aged [Age] years residing in the United Kingdom. Provide responses to the [Number of items]-item questionnaire." Each item is presented on a Likert scale ranging from [Likert scale range]. Please utilize the following key for responses: [Response Key]. The questionnaire items are as follows: [Questionnaire Items]. Provide your responses as a single string of comma-separated values.

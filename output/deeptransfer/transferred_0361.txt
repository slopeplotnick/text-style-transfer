Subsequently, we investigate the impact of selecting distinct latent spaces on the model's dynamics within a two-stage framework. Figure 9 illustrates the evolution of oracle loss during training with VA-VAE and SD-VAE latent spaces. During the navigation phase, both latent spaces exhibit a progressive increase in oracle loss, suggesting that the challenge of predicting the coarse layout intensifies irrespective of the latent space employed. In the refinement stage, however, the oracle loss dynamics exhibit significant deviations. While the absolute values remain incomparable, discernible trends emerge in the geometric progression of the oracle loss curves. Specifically, for VA-VAE, which structures its latent space in alignment with semantic organization via DINO-based VF loss, the oracle loss converges progressively toward a parabola-like trajectory as training advances, indicating a well-structured organization with distinct semantic modes. In contrast, SD-VAE, which is primarily oriented towards low-level reconstruction, results in a flatter curve with noticeable waviness, suggesting that its latent manifold is less structured, and the model encounters difficulties in achieving consistent denoising. Overall, latent spaces that exhibit alignment with high-level concepts are advantageous for diffusion modeling, as such alignment promotes clearer mode organization under Gaussian marginals, thereby facilitating both navigation and refinement.

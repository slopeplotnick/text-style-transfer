Checkers and related methodologies are effective in structured domains like mathematics and programming but do not generalize well to open-ended tasks. In contrast, self-evaluation approaches treat the model, or another large language model (LLM), as a judge. For instance, Self-Rewarding Language Models (Yuan et al., 2024) and RLAIF (Lee et al., 2024) generate AI feedback instead of relying on human annotations, while IPO (Garg et al., 2025) and Self-Rewarding PPO (Zhang et al., 2025a) derive preference signals from token-level probabilities. Closer to our approach, research on internal activations, such as Factoscope (He et al., 2024c) and INSIDE (Chen et al., 2024), demonstrates that hidden states encode factuality and hallucination risk. Our method follows this direction by using a geometric statistic of activations as a reward signal for policy optimization. The theoretical foundation for rank-based quality measures comes from softmax bottleneck analysis. Yang et al. (2018) showed that the expressiveness of softmax-based models is fundamentally limited by the rank of their hidden representations. Godey et al. (2024) extended this to modern LLMs, demonstrating that the contextual probability distribution of natural language is inherently high-rank. Moreover, complementary work indicates that when embeddings collapse into a narrow cone during training, the quality of generation degrades. Collectively, these results establish a link between representational dimensionality and generation quality. Empirical work supports this connection; for example, Garrido et al. (2023) demonstrated that the effective rank of learned representations predicts downstream performance without labels. In language models, hidden states encode rich semantic structures: word embeddings capture syntactic and semantic regularities through geometric relationships, while intermediate representations encode factuality and hallucination risk, confirming that generation quality leaves detectable geometric signatures. Building on these findings, our work shifts from diagnosis to optimization. Instead of using rank to analyze pretrained models post-hoc, we employ stable rank as a live reward signal during policy optimization, transforming a geometric insight into a practical alignment method.

Checkers and similar methodologies are effective in structured domains like mathematics and coding but struggle to generalize to open-ended tasks. Self-evaluation approaches, on the other hand, treat the model—or another large language model (LLM)—as a judge. For example, Self-Rewarding Language Models (Yuan et al., 2024) and RLAIF (Lee et al., 2024) rely on AI-generated feedback instead of human labels, while IPO (Garg et al., 2025) and Self-Rewarding PPO (Zhang et al., 2025a) extract preference signals from token-level probabilities. More relevant to our context, research on internal activations, such as Factoscope (He et al., 2024c) and INSIDE (Chen et al., 2024), demonstrates that hidden states encode information about factuality and hallucination risk. Our approach builds on this line of work by employing a geometric statistic of activations as a reward signal for policy optimization. The theoretical foundation for this idea lies in softmax bottleneck analysis. Yang et al. (2018) show that the expressiveness of softmax-based models is inherently constrained by the rank of their hidden representations. Building on this, Godey et al. (2024) reveal that the contextual probability distribution of natural language in modern LLMs is fundamentally high-rank. Furthermore, complementary studies indicate that when embeddings collapse into a narrow cone during training, the quality of generated outputs deteriorates (Gao et al., 2019). Together, these findings establish a strong link between representational dimensionality and generation quality. Empirical evidence further supports this connection: Garrido et al. (2023) demonstrate that the effective rank of learned representations can predict downstream performance without requiring labeled data. Specifically, in language models, hidden states encode rich semantic information: word embeddings capture syntactic and semantic regularities through geometric relationships (Mikolov et al., 2013), while intermediate representations assess factuality (He et al., 2024c) and hallucination risk (Chen et al., 2024). These results confirm that generation quality leaves identifiable geometric signatures. Our work extends these insights by moving from analysis to optimization. Instead of using rank as a diagnostic tool for pretrained models, we leverage stable rank as a dynamic reward signal during policy optimization, transforming this geometric principle into a practical method for improving model alignment.

Recordings were evaluated for technical clarity and adherence to task instructions. Once approved, they were transcribed by trained annotators using the same platform. Only those recordings and transcripts that successfully passed the final review were incorporated into the dataset. Remuneration was stratified according to language-specific requirements to address engagement considerations. This approach ensured that the resulting dataset maintained high technical quality, encompassed diverse speaker representation, and adhered to rigorous ethical standards. Moreover, the prompts were unscripted and developed by dedicated language teams to ensure cultural appropriateness and linguistic naturalness. Each language team comprised a language lead and typically three language assistants. The team engaged in peer review of their work prior to the language lead's final approval of the prompts designated for the project. Domain paths were established using Microsoft Forms, allowing teams to select a domain of knowledge and subsequently narrow their focus by choosing a specific topic and associated subtopics. Language teams were trained to construct prompts designed to elicit a diverse array of responses. For instance, they were instructed to formulate prompts that encouraged varied responses, such as "What is your favorite fruit and why?" rather than merely "What is your favorite fruit?" This training emphasized the importance of crafting prompts that could generate responses not limited to declarative statements but also inclusive of questions, exclamations, commands, and other forms of expression. Furthermore, teams were encouraged to refine topics and subtopics to enhance cultural relevance and, where necessary, sensitivity. The objective was to encapsulate the national lexicon of the South African languages to the greatest extent possible, thereby ensuring that the topics and subdomains were intricately aligned with South African life and tailored to the specific nuances of each language.

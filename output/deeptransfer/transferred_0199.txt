The recordings were evaluated for technical clarity and adherence to task instructions. Once approved, they were transcribed by trained annotators using the same platform. Only recordings and transcripts that successfully passed the final review were included in the dataset. Remuneration was stratified according to linguistic requirements to address engagement needs. This methodology ensured that the resultant dataset exhibited high technical quality, diverse speaker representation, and adherence to stringent ethical standards. Moreover, unscripted prompts were developed by dedicated language teams to ensure cultural relevance and naturalness. Each language team was composed of a designated lead and typically three assistants, with all outputs subjected to peer review within the team before final approval by the lead. Domain paths were established through Microsoft Forms, enabling teams to identify an initial domain of knowledge, refine their focus by selecting a specific topic within that domain, and further explore associated subtopics. Teams received training to develop prompts designed to elicit diverse response types, emphasizing the importance of constructing queries that encourage a range of linguistic expressions, such as questions, exclamations, commands, and other varied forms of communication, rather than solely declarative statements. Additionally, teams were encouraged to refine topics and subtopics to enhance cultural relevance and, where necessary, sensitivity. The objective was to encapsulate the national lexicon of the language to the greatest extent possible, ensuring that the topics and subdomains were highly pertinent to South African life and tailored to the specific nuances of that language.

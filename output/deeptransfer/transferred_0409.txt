Contemporary large language models (LLMs), trained solely on next-token prediction without explicit perceptual objectives, raise an important question: do they inherently develop mechanisms for processing and integrating information akin to human perceptual strategies? Addressing this question has profound implications for designing robust multimodal systems capable of handling variability in input quality. To investigate, we adopt methodologies from psychophysics—a discipline dedicated to understanding human perceptual processes—to systematically analyze the computational strategies of these models. Treating the models as opaque systems, we focus on their external behaviors rather than their internal mechanisms, modulating input uncertainty to evaluate indicators of Bayesian processing, which relies on probabilistic decision-making. This approach reveals that certain classical psychophysics tasks effectively elicit meaningful behavioral patterns in LLMs, particularly under controlled conditions that allow precise measurements. Unlike real-world scenarios where pervasive noise complicates quantification, this framework enables targeted evaluations of integration strategies, exposing vulnerabilities that conventional benchmarks may overlook. Our study contributes three key advancements: first, we introduce a psychophysics-inspired framework for LLMs, comprising four magnitude estimation tasks—length, location, distance, and duration—that manipulate variables such as noise and context to examine behavioral shifts. Second, we propose BayesBench, a novel benchmark designed to assess model performance, information integration, and alignment with Bayesian reasoning, employing an innovative scoring methodology. Third, our findings reveal that while some advanced models exhibit Bayesian-consistent behavior, even highly accurate models fail to adopt reliable strategies in certain contexts. Psychophysics, the quantitative study of perception, has long established systematic relationships between physical stimuli and perceptual judgments, formalized in foundational principles such as Weber-Fechner's logarithmic scaling. These insights underscore the importance of evaluating LLMs not only for accuracy but also for their strategies in handling uncertainty, providing a foundation for future multimodal system designs.

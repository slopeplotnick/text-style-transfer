Based on the resulting clusters detailed in Figure 2, the languages were categorized as In-Domain languages. We evaluate our model on both In-Domain (ID) and Out-of-Domain (OOD) languages. For the Out-of-Domain set, 11 low-resource languages were selected to serve as strong representatives for their respective clusters, thereby ensuring coverage across diverse language groups. For evaluation, we benchmark our model on six safety classification datasets: Aegis-Content-Safety-2.0-Test (Aegis-CS2) from 2025, HarmBench as documented by Mazeika et al. in 2024, Redteam2k as reported by Luo et al. in 2024, JBB-Behaviors (comprising subsets Behaviors as JBB-Behav and Judge-comparison as JBB-Judge) from Chao et al. in 2024, and StrongReject from Souly et al. in 2024. These benchmarks collectively span various harm categories, including but not limited to Hate/Identity Hate, Sexual, Suicide/Self-Harm, Violence, Guns/Illegal Weapons, PII/Privacy, Sexual Minor, Toxicity, and Abuse, making them suitable for a comprehensive evaluation of safety guardrails. For our multilingual safety guardrail, we adopt the XLM-RoBERTa-Base model with 279 million parameters and the XLM-RoBERTa-Large model with 560 million parameters as the base multilingual encoders. These models offer rich cross-lingual representations and are pre-trained on 100 languages, aligning well with our generalization goals. We append a single-layer classification head atop the encoder, matching the hidden size of the encoder outputs, with a binary output layer. Full-weight training of both the pre-trained encoder and the classification head is conducted using the aggregated training dataset. All training and evaluations are conducted on an NVIDIA H100 GPU cluster using Bfloat16 precision to enhance efficiency and performance. For baseline comparisons, we evaluate our model against both smaller guardrails such as DuoGuard-0.5B (2025), PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen-Smol, and WalledGuard-C (2024), and larger guardrails including PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen (2025), LlamaGuard3 (2024), and Aegis-Defensive (2025). These baselines represent a spectrum of language coverage, from monolingual English models to multilingual models supporting up to 29 languages. This diversity allows us to comprehensively compare the scalability, efficiency, and robustness of our proposed safety guardrail in both high-resource and low-resource language settings.

Based on the resulting clusters, as detailed in Figure 2, the languages were categorized as In-Domain (ID) languages. We evaluated our model on both In-Domain (ID) and Out-of-Domain (OOD) languages. For the Out-of-Domain set, 11 low-resource languages were selected to serve as strong representatives of their respective clusters, ensuring comprehensive coverage across diverse language families. To assess the model’s performance, we utilized six safety classification datasets: Aegis-Content-Safety-2.0-Test (Aegis-CS2) (2025), HarmBench (Mazeika et al., 2024), Redteam2k (Luo et al., 2024), JBB-Behaviors (comprising JBB-Behav and JBB-Judge) (Chao et al., 2024), and StrongReject (Souly et al., 2024). These datasets collectively span a wide range of harm categories, including but not limited to Hate/Identity Hate, Sexual, Suicide/Self-Harm, Violence, Guns/Illegal Weapons, PII/Privacy, Sexual Minor, Toxicity, and Abuse, making them well-suited for a thorough evaluation of safety guardrails. For training, we adopted the XLM-RoBERTa-Base (279M parameters) and XLM-RoBERTa-Large (560M parameters) models as our base multilingual encoders. These models, pre-trained on 100 languages, provide robust cross-lingual representations, aligning well with the generalization objectives of this study. A single-layer classification head, designed to match the hidden dimensionality of the encoder outputs, was appended with a binary output layer. Both the pre-trained encoder and the classification head underwent full-weight training on the aggregated dataset to ensure optimal performance. All training and evaluation processes were conducted on an NVIDIA H100 GPU cluster using Bfloat16 precision to maximize computational efficiency and performance. For baseline comparisons, we benchmarked our model against both smaller guardrails, such as DuoGuard-0.5B (2025), PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen-Smol, and WalledGuard-C (2024), and larger guardrails, such as PolyGuard(PG)-Qwen (2025), LlamaGuard3 (2024), and Aegis-Defensive (2025). These baselines encompass a diverse range of language capabilities, from monolingual English models to multilingual systems supporting up to 29 languages, enabling a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed safety guardrail across both high-resource and low-resource language scenarios.

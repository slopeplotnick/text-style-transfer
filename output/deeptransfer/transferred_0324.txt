Natural Language Processing (NLP) was initially conceptualized by Swiss linguist Ferdinand de Saussure, who introduced the notion that the meaning of language emerges from internal relationships and contrasts between words. Moreover, shared linguistic structures facilitate effective communication. In 1950, Alan Turing proposed the concept of a "thinking machine," suggesting that a machine capable of interacting with humans via a teleprinter would exhibit cognitive capability. In modern times, NLP plays a pivotal role in understanding human language and generating contextually appropriate responses. For instance, intelligent virtual assistants like Apple's Siri and Amazon's Alexa autonomously provide personalized assistance and process user requests. Large Language Models (LLMs) represent advanced artificial intelligence systems built using deep learning methodologies. These models, trained on vast datasets, are capable of producing text that closely resembles human writing. Leveraging transformer architecture, LLMs capture intricate linguistic nuances and long-range textual dependencies, enabling sophisticated functions such as machine translation, conversational engagement, and content generation. Furthermore, LLMs demonstrate versatility across diverse research and industrial domains. A notable example is OpenAI's ChatGPT, which is widely employed in everyday applications. However, language models occasionally experience a phenomenon known as "hallucination," wherein the model generates plausible-sounding yet fabricated information based on memorized patterns from its training data when faced with unfamiliar scenarios. The implications of hallucinations can range from minor inconsistencies that confuse users to critical errors in sensitive areas such as language translation, software development, or autonomous systems. Therefore, addressing hallucinations is essential to ensure the reliability, safety, and practical utility of these models, especially in contexts requiring high precision and accuracy. To mitigate this issue, fine-tuned models have emerged as an effective solution. These pre-trained machine learning models are optimized for specialized task domains, achieving superior performance compared to generalized models through targeted training on smaller, domain-specific datasets. During the fine-tuning process, model parameters are carefully adjusted to enhance precision and generalization capabilities. This approach builds upon the foundational language understanding acquired during large-scale training, refining the model’s focus on specific target tasks.

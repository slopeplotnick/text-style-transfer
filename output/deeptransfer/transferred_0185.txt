Fears represent the subjective negative feelings about migration that are crucial to address in order to reassure stakeholders. These fears are perceived as potential undesirable consequences. When responding to the first two questions regarding the benefits, interviewees emphasized fears stemming from the risks they had identified. Two key questions that indirectly facilitated the identification of these fears are: - Do you see the value of such an approach in the current processes of the company? - What is your opinion on a platform designed to quickly develop similar software? The seven distinct fears identified are illustrated in Figure 4. Notably, the primary concerns among IT professionals are related to perceived ineffectiveness, excessive complexity, and resistance to change (see Table 7). Furthermore, stakeholders across all roles expressed a strong demand for training and demonstrations. They aim to better evaluate the applicability of a Software Product Line (SPL) to their processes and to familiarize themselves with this innovative methodological approach. Drawing on the identified good practices, benefits, risks, and fears, we propose guidelines to support the migration to an SPL. These guidelines were derived from internal discussions and decisions within the organization. In this section, we outline the expected results (Sect. 5.1), followed by strategies to leverage current good practices (Sect. 5.2), approaches to foster potential future benefits (Sect. 5.3), methods to mitigate risks (Sect. 5.4), ways to alleviate fears (Sect. 5.5), concrete actions to be implemented (Sect. 5.6), and, finally, a comprehensive discussion (Sect. 5.7). Prior to conducting the interviews, we considered the potential outcomes that might emerge. We anticipated that individuals with knowledge or an affinity for reusability would exhibit the most positive and engaged responses, as they would likely be motivated to initiate and support the project’s implementation. In our study, developers with greater experience provided more responses related to mindset, whereas those with less experience focused more on technical aspects. However, this variation in experience did not significantly influence the quantity or quality of the responses obtained.

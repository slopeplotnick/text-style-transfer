Since all linguistic knowledge is explicitly represented and can be examined by researchers in relation to both agents, this methodological approach provides a valuable contribution to systems that acquire language without employing explicit representations, such as large language models. The MODOMA employs unsupervised learning, as the input data provided to the language acquisition device are unannotated, and this module does not have access to the internal processes or parsing mechanisms of the mother agent. Moreover, the daughter agent can leverage its previously acquired grammatical knowledge to facilitate further language acquisition. This innovative technique, introduced specifically for the MODOMA framework, is termed internal annotation, which constitutes a form of self-supervised learning (cf. Balestriero et al. 2023, Brown et al. 2020, Devlin et al. 2019, Gui et al. 2024, Lan et al. 2020, Orhan et al. 2020, Yarowsky 1995). As far as we know, the integration of features and research possibilities within the multiagent MODOMA configuration represents a novel advancement. Specifically, this system enables language acquisition experiments based on natural language involving both an advanced language model and a developing one. These agents engage in interactions wherein all aspects of the system—such as parameter settings, input, learning phases, interactions, generated utterances, and acquired grammar—are user-controlled, reproducible, measurable, and verifiable. Ter Hoeve et al. (2022) devised a framework for a teacher-student paradigm to simulate language acquisition, conducting two preliminary experiments to explore initial stages of this process. The first experiment focuses on modeling distinct domains through the use of 'two strictly separated vocabularies' within an artificial language, while the second experiment examines divergent structures by employing repetitions of tokens. In these experiments, the teacher agent selects a fixed amount of training data from a corpus of pre-constructed sentences to provide to multiple student language models. Following the training phase, the performance of these student models is evaluated using novel sentences derived from the artificial language. Additionally, a teacher model undergoes training on an alternative set of sentences and is subsequently assessed. However, this framework differs from the MODOMA configuration most notably in terms of the training and testing data: while ter Hoeve et al. (2022) utilized pre-constructed sentences from an artificial language, the MODOMA employs data representative of a natural language, generated online by Delilah through interaction based on an explicitly defined Dutch grammar model.

Since all linguistic knowledge is explicitly represented and can be examined by researchers with respect to both agents, this approach offers a valuable contribution to systems that acquire language without relying on explicit representations, such as large language models. The MODOMA framework employs unsupervised learning, as the language acquisition device processes unannotated input data and does not have access to the internal workings of the mother agent. Furthermore, the daughter agent can leverage its previously acquired grammatical knowledge to support subsequent language acquisition. This additional learning methodology, specifically developed for the MODOMA framework, is referred to as internal annotation, which represents a form of self-supervised learning (cf. Balestriero et al. 2023, Brown et al. 2020, Devlin et al. 2019, Gui et al. 2024, Lan et al. 2020, Orhan et al. 2020, Yarowsky 1995). To the best of our knowledge, the combination of features and research opportunities offered by the multiagent MODOMA framework constitutes a novel approach. Notably, this system enables language acquisition experiments involving natural language, where an adult language model interacts with a learning language model. These agents engage in interactions in which all aspects of the system—such as parameter settings, input data, learning processes, interactions, generated utterances, and acquired grammar—are fully controlled, reproducible, measurable, and verifiable. Ter Hoeve et al. (2022) propose a teacher-student paradigm to simulate language acquisition and implement two initial experiments to explore this model. The first experiment focuses on modeling distinct domains characterized by "two strictly separated vocabularies" within an artificial language, while the second experiment addresses diverse structures through repeated tokens. In these experiments, the teacher selects a fixed amount of training data from a set of pre-constructed sentences to distribute to multiple student language models. After training, the student models are evaluated using a test set comprising different sentences from the artificial language. Additionally, a teacher model is trained on a separate subset of sentences and subsequently assessed. However, this framework differs from the MODOMA approach primarily in the nature of the training and test data: while ter Hoeve et al. (2022) rely on pre-constructed sentences from an artificial language, the MODOMA framework utilizes data reflective of a natural language, dynamically generated by Delilah during interactions, based on an explicitly defined Dutch grammar model.

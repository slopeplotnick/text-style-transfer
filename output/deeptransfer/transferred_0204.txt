Participants shared their perceptions of AI-generated music, even without knowing the identity of the composer. Their concerns echoed broader discussions about the intersection of music and technology, particularly regarding economic implications, issues of ownership, and cultural impact. However, alongside these concerns, participants expressed curiosity about the innovative technology and its underlying mechanisms. This interplay of skepticism and intrigue may help explain the diverse opinions on AI composers documented in the literature. As AI technology continues to evolve, what initially sparks fascination may increasingly be viewed as a challenge or even a potential threat. Therefore, perceptions of AI-generated music should be understood as dynamic and subject to change over time. Nonetheless, this study has several limitations. First, the sample primarily consisted of university students and participants recruited via Prolific, a bilingual online convenience platform, who engaged with the musical stimuli under uncontrolled listening conditions, such as varying use of headphones or speakers and self-determined volume levels. This variability limits the generalizability of the findings. Moreover, as prior research on AI-generated music (Shank et al., 2023) has noted, Prolific participants tend to exhibit higher levels of technological literacy and familiarity with AI systems, which may have influenced their responses. Second, framing the stimuli as "movie soundtracks" for one experimental group may have introduced unintended bias, as film music is often associated with positive emotional responses. Lastly, the study focused exclusively on instrumental excerpts, which may limit the applicability of the findings to vocal music. Previous research suggests that lyrics typically amplify the emotional intensity of sad songs but have minimal or even diminishing effects on happy songs (Ali and Peynicioglu, 2006; Barradas and Sakka, 2021; Brattico et al., 2011; Warmbrodt et al., 2022). Given the predominance of positively valenced music in this study, adding lyrics—assuming they were of comparable quality to human-composed music—would likely have had only a marginal diminishing effect on emotional intensity. The decision to exclude lyrics was made to avoid potential confusion and because high-quality AI-generated vocal performances were unavailable at the time of sample creation.

In domain-specific SFT tasks, we typically use a standardized template for task definitions and requirements, filling in the query and item-specific details from each dataset to generate the prompt. To effectively activate the core capabilities of the base model, it is essential to include key elements such as task definitions, decision-making criteria, and output specifications within the prompt. Moreover, to ensure the model adheres to the proposed two-stage discrimination framework, we embed structured instructional guidance into the prompt. This involves first requiring the model to discern the query's intent and identify the necessary attributes, and then extracting the relevant attributes from the item information to make judgments based on predefined rules. For reasoning tasks, providing detailed and specific instructions in the prompt makes it easier for the model to derive the correct answer. However, this approach does not always apply in Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) scenarios, where the model primarily develops its capabilities by learning from response data rather than relying solely on instructions. To determine the optimal prompt configuration, we explored three versions of prompt designs. The comprehensive version includes all task specifications, judgment criteria, and detailed discrimination rules, exceeding 7,000 tokens in length. The intermediate version condenses the comprehensive version by removing detailed rules and retaining only the most critical instructions, with a length of approximately 800 tokens. The concise version is the briefest, providing only a basic role description, such as: "You are an e-commerce relevance assessment expert. Please evaluate the query and item." This phase corresponds to the initial model development stage, which focuses on enhancing the model's fundamental decision-making capabilities. To achieve this, it is crucial to construct a training dataset with two key attributes: comprehensiveness and minimal noise. For comprehensiveness, a scientifically grounded sampling strategy is employed to adequately represent diverse e-commerce data distributions, thereby reducing the risks of oversampling or undersampling specific data types.

The text elaborates on the assessment of logical and ethical dimensions in AI model responses, introducing the Moral Consistency Pipeline (MoCoP), an autonomous framework for ethical evaluation that operates independently of predefined datasets. MoCoP functions by autonomously generating, testing, and refining moral reasoning scenarios, thereby eliminating reliance on external inputs. Moreover, the evaluation framework assesses the temporal evolution of moral reasoning in AI models and facilitates comparative analysis to ascertain consistency across different models. This system integrates lexical, semantic, and reasoning assessment tools to systematically monitor the ethical handling capabilities of AI models over time. Previous investigations predominantly concentrated on the identification and rectification of social bias and the generation of harmful content within AI systems. For instance, Burak et al. [7] introduced OpenEthics, which prioritizes transparency and the establishment of open evaluation pipelines to facilitate the auditing of ethical alignment in large language models (LLMs). Additionally, other studies have examined fairness, striving to mitigate biases against specific demographic groups. While these investigations contributed to enhancing the transparency of AI systems, they employed static datasets and prompts, thereby limiting their adaptability to evolving contexts. Although these studies captured the operational characteristics of AI models at a specific temporal juncture, they fell short in tracking the evolution of moral reasoning or assessing the stability of ethical decision-making across diverse scenarios. Recent research endeavors have transcended the focus on fairness, delving into the intricacies of moral and ethical decision-making processes within AI models.

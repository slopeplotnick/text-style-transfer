An initial experiment conducted within the MODOMA laboratory simulation environment was based on a specific correlation. This experiment provides a method to evaluate the efficacy of the MODOMA approach to language acquisition by employing the well-established principle of Zipf's law. Therefore, this study contributes to the advancement of research by enabling the iterative refinement of MODOMA through the implementation of increasingly complex experimental designs. As a result, familiarity with the MODOMA framework as a laboratory model for language acquisition experiments can be enhanced, allowing the system to be tested and improved prior to the adoption of alternative language acquisition methodologies. Although Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) focus on the emergence of languages, their studies share similarities in applying Zipf's law to deepen the understanding of language models in comparison to human languages. Importantly, this methodology incorporates most of the components and procedures required for more intricate acquisition experiments. Accordingly, the constituent elements of the MODOMA system and their interactions can be validated and refined to support the execution of further computational language acquisition experiments. If the acquisition process is successful, the daughter agent learns to distinguish between functional and content categories through exposure to examples of the target language provided by the mother agent during their interactions. Notably, once the daughter agent internalizes this grammatical distinction, the newly acquired grammatical knowledge is immediately integrated into its grammar system. This updated grammar is then utilized to generate novel utterances during interactions with the mother agent and to parse previously unseen sentences produced by the mother agent. Consequently, this study provides a significant demonstration of whether the MODOMA laboratory framework for language acquisition can facilitate the development of substantive grammatical knowledge that can be effectively applied in both parsing and generation tasks.

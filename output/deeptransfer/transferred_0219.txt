In this work, we present Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), a reusable and modular tool for physics-based character animation utilizing score-matching techniques. Once trained, the learned motion prior can be employed to instruct control policies across various tasks, facilitating the generation of natural movements that align with the reference distribution. Notably, the retention of the original motion data is not requisite for its utilization. Moreover, test-time diffusion techniques, such as classifier-free guidance, can be applied to modify the motion prior, thereby generating novel stylistic priors that enable agents to execute tasks in specific manners. We demonstrate the efficacy of this approach across a multitude of scenarios, encompassing single-character movements and human-object interactions. SMPs can be constructed using datasets of varying sizes, ranging from a few seconds of motion clips to extensive datasets comprising up to 20 hours of motion data. Our experiments reveal that reinforcement learning with score distillation sampling (SDS) objectives is capable of generating motions of comparable quality to those produced by adversarial imitation learning, without necessitating continuous updates to the prior during policy training. Furthermore, SMP exhibits superior performance in most scenarios, which can be attributed to the stability of its stationary reward function compared to adversarial reward models. However, akin to other methodologies that concentrate on specific modalities, policies trained with SMP may occasionally become confined to executing a restricted set of behaviors derived from the original dataset. While our primary focus has been on humanoid motion control, we posit that SMP holds potential applicability to a broader spectrum of control challenges. We hope this concept will pave the way for novel approaches in developing general motion priors, thereby facilitating the creation of more adaptable controllers for character animation and robotics, extending beyond mere motion tracking.

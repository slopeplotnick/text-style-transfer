In this work, we introduce Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), a reusable and modular framework for physics-based character animation based on score-matching techniques. Once trained, the motion prior can be reused to train control policies for a variety of tasks, guiding them to generate natural movements that align with the reference distribution on which the prior was originally trained. Notably, the original motion dataset is not required for its application. Moreover, test-time diffusion techniques, such as classifier-free guidance, can be utilized to refine the base motion prior and create novel stylistic priors, enabling agents to perform tasks in specific styles. We evaluate our approach across a wide range of scenarios, including single-character behaviors and human-object interactions. SMPs can be constructed from datasets of varying sizes, ranging from short motion clips lasting only a few seconds to large-scale datasets containing up to 20 hours of motion data. Through our experiments, we demonstrate that reinforcement learning with score distillation sampling (SDS) objectives can generate motions of comparable quality to those produced by adversarial imitation learning approaches, without requiring continuous updates to the prior during policy training. Furthermore, SMP exhibits superior sample efficiency in most scenarios, which we attribute to the stability of its stationary reward function compared to the dynamic reward models used in adversarial frameworks. However, as with other mode-seeking objectives, policies trained with SMP may sometimes become constrained to a limited subset of behaviors present in the original dataset. While our primary focus is on humanoid motion control, we believe that SMP has the potential to address a broader range of control problems. We hope this work inspires new methodologies for developing general motion priors, paving the way for more adaptable and versatile controllers in physics-based character animation and robotics, extending beyond traditional motion tracking.

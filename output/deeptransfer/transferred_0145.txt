We evaluated these methods on the BrowseComp benchmark, observing that effective context management during testing significantly enhances model performance by enabling the allocation of additional computational resources and the execution of supplementary steps when required. For instance, the Summary approach extends the average number of steps to 364, resulting in a performance improvement of up to 60.2%. However, its overall efficiency remains relatively low. In contrast, despite its simplicity, the Discard-all method demonstrates strong performance in both efficiency and scalability, achieving a score of 67.6, comparable to parallel scaling while requiring substantially fewer steps. Test-time computing can be augmented either through serial context management or parallel execution, both contributing to enhanced problem-solving capabilities of the model. However, these approaches exhibit varying degrees of efficiency and scalability, necessitating careful consideration of computational costs when assessing model performance. Furthermore, determining the optimal combination of serial and parallel scaling to achieve both efficiency and scalability remains an area requiring further investigation. In this study, we introduced DeepSeek-V3.2, a framework that effectively reconciles computational efficiency with sophisticated reasoning capabilities. Utilizing DSA, we addressed critical computational complexity challenges without compromising the ability to manage extended contexts. By augmenting the computational budget, DeepSeek-V3.2 achieves performance parity with GPT-5 on reasoning benchmarks. Moreover, the incorporation of our large-scale task synthesis system significantly enhances tool-use proficiency, thereby unlocking new possibilities for the development of robust and adaptable AI systems utilizing open LLMs. Our high-compute variant, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, has been validated through gold-medal achievements in prestigious competitions such as the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) and the International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI), representing a substantial advancement for open LLMs. Nonetheless, we acknowledge certain limitations compared to cutting-edge closed-source models such as Gemini-3.0-Pro. For instance, owing to its reduced total training FLOPs, the scope of general knowledge encompassed by DeepSeek-V3.2 remains comparatively limited when measured against that of leading proprietary models.

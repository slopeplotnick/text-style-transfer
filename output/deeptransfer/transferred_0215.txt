As depicted in Fig. 12, the experimental group employing the curriculum learning strategy exhibits accelerated convergence and a more consistent trajectory of improvement in both reward acquisition and model performance. During reinforcement learning (RL) training, as the policy approaches convergence, the entropy of the model's output distribution diminishes, resulting in increasingly deterministic outputs. This reduction in stochasticity constrains the model's capacity to explore novel strategies, thereby limiting its potential for further optimization. To address this issue, we explored various strategies to decelerate the rate at which the model becomes predictable, thereby facilitating continued exploration. Figure 13 provides a comparative analysis of these strategies. Notably, the Clip-higher method emerges as the most effective, as it moderates the predictability of the model while still allowing it to occur. This approach achieves an optimal balance between exploration and exploitation, ensuring stable training with discernible improvements. In contrast, the On-policy strategy effectively stabilizes policy entropy, thereby nearly halting its decline. Although this approach enhances the model's exploratory capabilities, it concurrently hinders effective policy convergence, resulting in limited performance improvements. Conversely, the Off-policy + Entropy Loss strategy encounters challenges related to training instability. Initially, the model's predictability is decelerated; however, as the model's stochasticity diminishes, the penalty associated with randomness becomes excessively pronounced. This predominance results in erratic model behavior, ultimately leading to training failure. Furthermore, this approach necessitates extensive experimentation to determine optimal parameters, thereby increasing its implementation cost. In summary, the Clip-higher method emerges as the superior choice, as it effectively mediates the exploration-exploitation trade-off by regulating the rate at which the model achieves predictability.

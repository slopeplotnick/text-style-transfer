More recent datasets include Mozilla's Common Voice, which provides crowd-sourced, scripted speech in six South African languages and is distributed under a permissive CC-0 license to encourage broad reuse. Similarly, FLEURS offers approximately 12 hours of data per language across four South African languages, primarily designed for benchmarking and evaluation purposes. Vuk'uzenzele (VixSD) is a community-driven ASR dataset released under the Esethu licensing framework, which prioritizes community ownership and ethical utilization. Although smaller in scale, it underscores the potential for local communities to actively contribute to resource creation. AfriSpeech-200 comprises English speech recordings with South African accents, focusing on healthcare-related topics, and is particularly useful for developing accent-aware ASR systems. Despite these advancements, the availability of spontaneous speech data and diverse domain coverage remains limited. Therefore, there is a growing need for community-grounded datasets like Swivuriso, which integrate both scripted and unscripted speech, encompass broader domain representation, and promote deeper speaker diversity. Collecting speech data within underrepresented language communities requires a careful approach that respects consent, privacy, cultural norms, and long-term community impact. Previous initiatives, such as Common Voice and ALFFA, have demonstrated the importance of embedding ethical and participatory practices throughout the data lifecycle. For instance, a case study on the Catalan edition of Common Voice highlighted the necessity of clear consent procedures, privacy safeguards, and inclusive participation. Similarly, the ALFFA project emphasized community involvement by collaborating with local experts and field linguists during data collection. Building on these efforts, Swivuriso places a strong emphasis on ethical practices and inclusive representation. Local coordinators played a pivotal role in managing recordings, supporting participants, and ensuring comprehensive community representation. The project adhered to institutionally approved ethical protocols, including anonymization of metadata, removal of personal identifiers, and safeguards against misuse, such as voice cloning or biometric profiling. Furthermore, ensuring datasets are accessible and usable is critical, particularly for low-resource languages. Open licenses, such as those provided by Creative Commons, facilitate unrestricted use of data, which is especially beneficial in contexts where data collection is challenging or costly. Projects like Common Voice exemplify how permissive licensing can foster equitable access and accelerate the development of language technology for underrepresented languages.

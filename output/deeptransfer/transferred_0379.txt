Recommending matches within a text-rich, dynamic two-sided marketplace presents significant challenges due to the continual evolution of content and interaction graphs. To address these complexities, we introduce GraphMatch, an innovative large-scale recommendation framework that integrates pre-trained language models with graph neural networks. Unlike previous methodologies that rely on isolated models, GraphMatch employs a synergistic approach by utilizing text encoders and graph networks in unison. It leverages adversarial negative sampling in conjunction with point-in-time subgraph training to effectively capture both the nuanced semantics of textual content and the temporal dynamics of graph structures. We conducted comprehensive evaluations on large-scale interaction data from Upwork, a prominent online labor marketplace, with the system designed to enable low-latency inference suitable for real-time applications. Experimental results demonstrate that GraphMatch surpasses language-only and graph-only baselines in matching tasks while maintaining computational efficiency during runtime. These findings underscore the efficacy of integrating language and graph representations for addressing recommendation tasks in text-rich, dynamic environments. Real-world systems, such as e-commerce platforms, job and talent marketplaces, academic citation networks, and news feeds, can be effectively modeled as Temporal Text-Attributed Graphs (TTAGs). Within TTAGs, nodes are characterized by intricate textual descriptions that evolve over time, while edges encapsulate interactions such as purchases, endorsements, or responses. Effectively matching or recommending items necessitates the integration of detailed textual semantics with the dynamic structural evolution of the graph, yet this remains a formidable challenge. Emerging language models, such as SentenceBERT and E5, proficiently distill documents into embeddings and excel in semantic comparison. However, these models often overlook critical aspects when applied to TTAGs. Notably, the decision to link two items frequently hinges on the surrounding graph connections, an element that text-based models fail to capture. Moreover, within TTAGs, the semantic interpretation of a node and the justification for its linkage can undergo rapid transformation. For instance, the advent of generative AI has redefined "AI Writing" job postings, shifting the emphasis from editorial competencies to prompt engineering expertise. Furthermore, geopolitical developments persistently influence the dynamics of freelancer-client matching within the global labor marketplace. Treating text snapshots as static features may lead to significant issues, such as the inadvertent disclosure of information pertaining to future states that should remain inaccessible.

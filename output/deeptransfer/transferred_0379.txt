Recommending matches in text-rich, dynamic two-sided marketplaces poses unique challenges due to the ever-changing nature of content and interaction graphs. To address these complexities, we introduce GraphMatch, a novel large-scale recommendation framework that seamlessly integrates pre-trained language models with graph neural networks. Unlike traditional approaches relying on standalone models, GraphMatch leverages the combined strengths of advanced text encoders and GNNs in a unified framework. It incorporates adversarial negative sampling alongside point-in-time subgraph training to effectively capture the intricate semantics of evolving text and the temporal dynamics of graph structures. Extensive evaluations on large-scale interaction data from Upwork, a leading online labor marketplace, demonstrate the framework’s ability to achieve low-latency inference, making it suitable for real-time applications. Experimental results consistently show that GraphMatch outperforms both language-only and graph-only baselines on matching tasks while maintaining computational efficiency. These findings highlight the effectiveness of unifying language and graph representations for recommendations in environments characterized by rich textual content and dynamic interactions. Real-world systems, such as e-commerce platforms, job marketplaces, academic citation networks, and news feeds, can be naturally modeled as Temporal Text-Attributed Graphs (TTAGs). In TTAGs, nodes are associated with rich textual descriptions that evolve over time, while edges represent interactions such as purchases, endorsements, or replies. Successfully matching or recommending items in such systems requires learning representations that integrate the fine-grained semantics of text with the evolving global structure of the graph, which remains a challenging task. Recent advancements in contrastive pretraining have produced language models like SentenceBERT and E5, which excel at transforming documents into embeddings and measuring local semantic similarity. However, when applied to TTAGs, these models often miss two critical signals. First, the decision to link two items frequently depends on their surrounding graph neighborhood, an aspect that pure text encoders cannot capture. Second, in TTAGs, the meaning of a node and the rationale for its connections can shift rapidly. For instance, the rise of generative AI has transformed "AI Writing" job postings from emphasizing editorial skills to focusing on prompt engineering. Similarly, geopolitical events continuously reshape freelancer-client matching dynamics in the global labor marketplace. Treating text snapshots as static features risks significant issues, such as information leakage from future states that should remain inaccessible.

The performance and efficiency of our proposed method were compared against the RePro approach, with results cited from the original publication due to the unavailability of the corresponding codebase. P2C denotes Paper2Code. While the Refine methodology requires manually designing separate evaluation and refinement prompts for each component—resulting in eight handcrafted prompts for the four planning tasks in the original Paper2Code implementation—our approach automatically adapts to each task by leveraging its corresponding system prompt, thus eliminating the need for additional prompt engineering. We conducted our evaluation on two datasets: Paper2CodeBench, which includes 90 papers from top-tier 2024 conferences such as ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS, selected based on code availability and paper length, and PaperBench Code-Dev, which comprises 20 ICML 2024 papers. In Paper2CodeBench, our generated repositories were evaluated against official author-released implementations using LLM-based evaluators who assessed correctness on a 5-point scale. In PaperBench Code-Dev, human reviewers evaluated the correctness of the generated code according to specific grading rubrics. To the best of our knowledge, there are limited existing methodologies that employ iterative self-refinement for the automated transformation of research papers into code. Therefore, we compare our approach to two analogous methods that incorporate self-refinement: Paper2Code in conjunction with Self-Refine. Self-Refine is an iterative methodology. All experimental evaluations were conducted using GPT-4.1 as the foundational model. For the RePro baseline, due to the absence of accessible source code, we reported the results as documented in the original publication. Tables I and II present the comparative performance of various optimization strategies across the two datasets. Our proposed approach demonstrates enhanced reliability and robustness across datasets. Notably, even when optimization was confined to the planning stage, our method (Paper2Code with autoplan optimization) achieved consistent and measurable improvements. Upon examining individual tasks, our approach consistently demonstrated superior performance. Figure 2 provides a comprehensive per-task analysis of performance on PaperBench. Our planning optimization surpassed Self-Refine in 16 out of 20 tasks (80%), indicating its robust efficacy across various scenarios. Conversely, Self-Refine exhibited suboptimal performance on ten tasks, demonstrating particularly inadequate results on certain specific tasks.

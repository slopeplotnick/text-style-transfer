Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have facilitated the integration of task-oriented and open-domain dialogue systems. This section of the tutorial will cover this technological evolution and highlight some examples of promising future directions. For instance, the development of novel foundational LLMs pretrained with conversational datasets, end-to-end dialogue training, and self-supervised dialogue practice are noteworthy. Another significant future direction involves the creation of dialogue systems capable of integrating and grounding interactions in diverse content modalities. These include web pages (Heck et al., 2013), structured data such as lists (Bapna et al., 2017), forms (Heck et al., 2024), tables (Sundar and Heck, 2023; Sundar et al., 2024b,a), and academic papers containing figures, equations, and tables (Sundar et al., 2024c), as well as retrieved documents (Reichman and Heck, 2024). Moreover, these systems aim to incorporate multimodal inputs such as vision (Hakkani-Tur et al., 2014), domain-specific knowledge (Reichman et al., 2023), emotional context (Reichman et al., 2024), and expressive features, including facial and lip movements, body language, and gestures (Punjwani and Heck, 2024b,a).

Values in brackets were procedurally inserted for each prompt from a lookup table of simulated participants. Each row of this table represented a procedurally generated individual possessing an age-ethnicity-gender combination determined by the representative UK sample stratification used by Prolific. Since Prolific performs age stratification in ranges, the exact age of each simulated individual was assigned randomly within the given range. After being generated, each LLM-generated survey reply was parsed via a simple R script and stored as a row in a spreadsheet. To ensure variability in responses, three differently worded prompts were used. Following the procedure described by Hewitt and colleagues (2024), we adopted an ensemble approach in which scores were averaged between prompts at the item level. Each type of prompt used in the studies is reported in full in the Supplementary Materials to this article. We anticipated that the LLM model might occasionally return an invalid (i.e., not in comma-separated values) answer. However, we opted to keep these invalid answers in the database to simulate a natural workflow. These invalid answers were treated as missing values in the later analysis process and were replaced by averaging answers from the other prompts. In Studies 1 and 2, pre-existing data were utilized, incorporating prompts such as "Impersonate a [Ethnicity] [Gender] of [Age] years of age from the United Kingdom and respond to the subsequent questionnaire [...]". Conversely, Studies 3 and 4 focused on the validation of newly acquired data. The initial section delineates the methodology employed for Studies 1 and 2, whereas the subsequent section elucidates the bifurcated approach utilized for Studies 3 and 4. Hypothesis H3.1 was tested by performing Spearman's correlation test between the real-world and simulated scale scores. Hypothesis H3.2 was evaluated using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). Hypothesis H4 was tested by performing the Mann-Whitney U test, Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, and Levene's test to facilitate comparisons across different data types. Finally, Hypothesis H5 was assessed through the application of the same multigroup confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) procedure as employed for H2, albeit exclusively on simulated data, with gender serving as the grouping variable. All CFA and multigroup CFA models were computed using the maximum likelihood robust (MLR) estimator due to its robustness against deviations from normality (Hox et al., 2010).

Values in brackets were procedurally inserted for each prompt from a lookup table of simulated participants. Each row in this table represented a procedurally generated individual with an age-ethnicity-gender combination determined by the representative UK sample stratification used by Prolific. Since Prolific performs age stratification in ranges, the exact age of each simulated individual was randomly assigned within the specified range. Once generated, each LLM-produced survey response was parsed using a simple R script and stored as a row in a spreadsheet. To account for potential variability in responses, three distinct prompts were employed. Following the methodology outlined by Hewitt et al. (2024), an ensemble approach was applied, whereby scores were averaged across prompts at the item level. Details of all prompt types used in the study are provided in the Supplementary Materials accompanying this article. Importantly, it was anticipated that the LLM model might occasionally produce invalid responses (e.g., outputs not formatted as comma-separated values). These invalid responses were retained in the dataset to simulate a natural workflow and were subsequently treated as missing data. Missing values were addressed through imputation by averaging responses from other prompts. Studies 1 and 2 utilized pre-existing data, employing prompts such as "Impersonate a [Ethnicity] [Gender] of [Age] years of age from the United Kingdom. Answer the following questionnaire [...]". In contrast, Studies 3 and 4 focused on validating newly collected data. The methodology for Studies 1 and 2 is described in the upper section, while the two-phase procedure for Studies 3 and 4 is detailed in the lower section. Hypothesis H3.1 was tested by examining the association between real-world and simulated scores using Spearman's correlation coefficient. Hypothesis H3.2 was assessed using the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC). Hypothesis H4 was evaluated through Mann-Whitney U, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, and Levene's tests to compare variances between the two data types. Finally, Hypothesis H5 was tested using the same multigroup confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) procedure as Hypothesis H2, but exclusively on simulated data, with gender as the grouping variable. All CFA and multigroup CFA models were computed using the maximum likelihood robust (MLR) estimator, which is well-suited for handling deviations from normality (Hox et al., 2010).

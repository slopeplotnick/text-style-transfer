The GPT-5 model demonstrates a consistently high refusal rate that remains unaffected by increases in context padding. Consequently, while this refusal rate is associated with a slight decline in performance on harmful tasks, the initially low harm score prevents strong conclusions from being drawn. Further exploration of benign tasks with context padding exceeding 10K tokens is not pursued due to the stability of the initial results and the high cost of additional experiments. The type of context padding, however, can significantly influence model performance. As shown in Figure 3, different padding types yield varying effects. For GPT-4.1-nano, non-relevant padding achieves the highest performance, followed by relevant padding, random padding, and finally multi-task padding, which performs the worst. In contrast, Grok 4 Fast deviates from this pattern due to its consistently high refusal rate, with performance across different padding types remaining close to the no-padding baseline. Notably, within the range of 50K to 100K tokens, Grok 4 Fast exhibits behavior resembling mode collapse, as the performance for different padding types converges. Additionally, the placement of padding plays a critical role in model performance. Figure 4 illustrates that placing padding before the task description mitigates the rate of performance degradation compared to placing it after. This effect is likely because padding placed before the task description reduces the distance between the task description and execution, enabling the model’s attention mechanisms to operate more effectively. Modern attention strategies, such as sliding window and RoPE, struggle to attend to tokens separated by large distances, as such patterns are rare during training. When padding is placed after the instruction, the model must attend across a larger gap between the instruction and execution phases, which likely explains the more pronounced performance degradation.

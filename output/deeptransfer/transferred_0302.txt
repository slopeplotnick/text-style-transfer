The GPT-5 model shows a consistently high refusal rate that remains unaffected by increasing context padding. Consequently, we observe a slight decrease in performance on harmful tasks; however, the initially low harm score prevents us from drawing strong conclusions. Therefore, we do not further explore benign tasks with context padding longer than 10,000 tokens due to the initial stability of the results and the high cost of conducting such experiments. The type of context padding can significantly impact performance. As shown in Figure 3, different padding types exhibit varying effects. For the GPT-4.1-nano model, non-relevant padding achieves the highest scores, followed by relevant padding, with random padding performing lower, and multi-task padding exhibiting the poorest performance. In contrast, Grok 4 Fast deviates from this pattern due to its elevated refusal rate, with performance metrics across varying padding lengths closely resembling those observed without padding. Notably, within the range of 50K to 100K tokens, Grok 4 Fast demonstrates behavior indicative of a collapse, as the performance scores across different padding types converge. The placement of padding significantly influences model performance outcomes. Figure 4 illustrates that inserting padding before the task description mitigates the rate of performance degradation compared to placing it afterward. This phenomenon likely occurs because the proximity between the task description and execution in the former scenario enhances the model's attentional efficacy. Modern attention mechanisms, such as sliding window and RoPE, exhibit limitations in attending to distantly positioned tokens, as such configurations are infrequent during the training phase. When padding is applied after the instruction, the model must attend across an extensive interval between the instruction and execution phases, potentially explaining the accelerated degradation in performance observed under such conditions.

During the data preprocessing stage, all cases and examination questions were initially deduplicated to ensure the independence of samples, followed by a systematic data cleaning process. Samples that lacked question stems or answer options, as well as those containing non-textual elements such as figures or tables, were excluded. Concurrently, errors introduced during optical character recognition (OCR) were manually corrected to ensure textual accuracy and consistency. To ensure patient privacy and adhere to ethical standards, clinical data were anonymized by removing or modifying identifiable information, such as patient names and other sensitive details. The clinical cases utilized in this study received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of Xiyuan Hospital. SDT, as a fundamental component of TCM diagnosis and treatment, encompasses the dual processes of syndrome differentiation and treatment formulation. To evaluate the model's capabilities, a comprehensive 14-dimension evaluation framework was devised, encompassing elements such as syndrome and causative factors, as illustrated in Table 1. Initially, TCM experts undertook the annotation of standard answers and options, followed by mutual cross-validation of their annotations. Subsequently, an independent third-party review was conducted to ensure consistency and reliability of the annotations. Notably, the questions incorporated sophisticated distractors to enhance the assessment's ability to differentiate varying levels of understanding. In addition to assessing the final reasoning outcomes, this study introduces two process-oriented metrics: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Content Completeness, which evaluates the extent to which the model encompasses essential patient information, and CoT Accuracy, which assesses the congruence of the model's reasoning with the original clinical case to identify potential inaccuracies or reasoning deviations. The evaluation tasks are designed to elucidate differences in the model's foundational cognition and normative responses, thereby facilitating a more comprehensive and interpretable assessment. All questions are structured as multiple-choice, incorporating both single and multiple-answer formats. The TCM Basic Knowledge component encompasses four fundamental domains: (i) comprehension of TCM classics; (ii) mastery of foundational theories; (iii) knowledge of Chinese materia medica and formulas; and (iv) syndrome differentiation ability based on tongue, pulse, facial complexion, and acupoints. Questions for this component were derived from preprocessed authoritative examination questions. The Medical Ethics component assesses the model's understanding of clinical ethics, addressing conflicts between traditional and modern medical practices, identifying unscientific behaviors, respecting patient beliefs, and ensuring informed consent. This section employs examination questions alongside those crafted by experts. Finally, the Content Safety component evaluates the extent to which the TCM model adheres to professional boundaries and complies with established safety protocols. The model is designed to exclusively address inquiries pertinent to Traditional Chinese Medicine, refraining from engaging with non-medical queries related to privacy, safety, or human values. Questions were independently designed and rigorously reviewed by experts in content safety and ethics to ensure alignment with the evaluation standards established for TCM domain models.

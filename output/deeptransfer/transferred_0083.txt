Editing portrait videos is a challenging task that requires flexible yet precise control over a wide range of modifications, such as appearance changes, expression edits, or the addition of objects. The key difficulty lies in preserving the subject's original temporal behavior, demanding that every edited frame remains precisely synchronized with the corresponding source frame. We present SyncLoRA, a method for editing portrait videos that achieves high-quality visual modifications while maintaining frame-accurate synchronization and identity consistency. Our approach employs a model that initiates modifications in the first frame of the video and subsequently propagates these adjustments across the remaining frames. To enable accurate synchronization, we train the model using paired videos that depict identical motion trajectories but differ in appearance. These pairs are meticulously selected through a synchronization-based filtering process, which evaluates and retains only those examples with optimal temporal alignment. This training setup teaches the model to combine motion cues from the source video with the visual changes introduced in the edited first frame. Trained on a compact, highly curated set of synchronized human portraits, Sync-LoRA generalizes to unseen identities and diverse edits, such as modifying appearance, adding objects, or changing backgrounds, while robustly handling variations in pose and expression. Our results demonstrate high visual fidelity and strong temporal coherence, achieving a robust balance between edit fidelity and precise motion preservation. Such editing techniques are particularly valuable in domains such as advertising, film production, game development, and other forms of visual media, where precise control over the appearance and behavior of visual elements is critical. A particular interest in video editing lies in the editing of portrait videos, as a large portion of visual media features humans. Editing portrait videos presents a uniquely demanding challenge. On the one hand, the visual appearance of the subject must be modified according to the desired outcome.

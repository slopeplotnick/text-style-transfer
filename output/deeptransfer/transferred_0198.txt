The structural equations of the FSCM are typically the most challenging part of the explanation model to design. The results in Section 5.2 incorporate canonical PSCM intervals for counterfactual queries to highlight the limitations that arise when the true causal mechanisms cannot be determined. For certain queries, these intervals are narrow, suggesting that the interval mean or approximate queries using a CBN may provide a reasonable estimation of the true value, as the exact model choice becomes less critical in such cases. However, this is not always the case. Generally, when conditioning on events with low probability, the counterfactual probability can vary from 0 to 1 depending on the model employed. Consequently, two distinct FSCMs, both consistent with the data, may yield entirely opposite explanations for the same model and even assert these outcomes as certain. Rare observations, however, are particularly important to explain accurately, especially to ensure predictions remain meaningful in regions with sparse training data. Therefore, access to an FSCM remains necessary to guarantee the ability to compute arbitrary queries. Moreover, non-canonical PSCMs defined by partial expert knowledge may reduce interval sizes, thereby broadening the range of queries that can be approximated. Future research may further explore the feasibility and effectiveness of various approaches to both precise and approximate computations. While the explanations in this study focus on calculating the probability of sufficiency of concept interventions, this framework is not limited to that alone. Future investigations could also examine probabilities of necessity and other types of explanations.

The most challenging part of designing the explanation model is typically the structural equations of the FSCM. Section 5.2 presents canonical PSCM intervals for counterfactual queries to illustrate what is lost when the true causal mechanisms cannot be determined. For some queries, the intervals are narrow, suggesting that the interval mean or even approximate queries using a CBN may provide a reasonable approximation of the true value, as the exact model choice is of less consequence. However, this is not always the case. Generally, when conditioning on events of low probability, the counterfactual probability may range from 0 to 1, depending on the model considered. Consequently, two different FSCMs that are both consistent with the data may generate opposite explanations of the same model and even claim these as certain outcomes. Given that less likely observations can be crucial for reliable explanations, especially when ensuring predictions remain meaningful in areas with limited training data, the general framework still requires access to a fully specified FSCM to guarantee arbitrary query calculation. Nevertheless, non-canonical PSCMs defined by partial expert knowledge may reduce the size of the intervals, thereby extending the scope of queries that can be approximated. Future work may further explore the feasibility and capacity of various approaches to exact and approximate calculations. While the explanations discussed in this study are based on calculating the probability of sufficiency of concept interventions, this is not a limitation of the framework. Future investigations might also explore probabilities of necessity and additional forms of explanations.

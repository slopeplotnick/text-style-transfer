Forecasting stock prices remains a formidable challenge due to the inherent unpredictability of financial markets and their rapid responsiveness to real-time events. However, the advent of large language models (LLMs) has introduced innovative methodologies for leveraging textual data in stock price prediction. While LLMs exhibit advanced capabilities in natural language understanding, their effective deployment in addressing complex, real-world financial tasks often necessitates the incorporation of specialized memory architectures to facilitate the consistent and systematic utilization of historical information. Considerable research has been dedicated to the development of these memory systems. Nevertheless, they encounter significant difficulties when processing the vast quantities of noisy online news data, which do not provide direct explanations for price fluctuations and contain a substantial amount of irrelevant information. The primary challenge, therefore, lies in autonomously discerning and extracting the critical information and underlying logic from this unstructured text that genuinely influences stock price movements. To address this issue, this study introduces a dual-layer memory framework designed to enhance event organization and analysis. During the knowledge construction phase, the framework systematically consolidates daily event details to provide a comprehensive overview while simultaneously tracing the temporal evolution of events. This dual approach facilitates the construction of a temporally-aware structured knowledge base, enabling a nuanced understanding of shifts in market expectations over time. Furthermore, by examining the correlation between event patterns and stock price fluctuations, the framework establishes an additional knowledge base dedicated to the analysis of this relationship, thereby achieving systematic and autonomous knowledge construction. In the application phase of this system, two principal steps are involved: retrieval and reasoning. Initially, the framework leverages recent events to retrieve analogous historical instances stored within its memory, thereby facilitating more informed decision-making in the present. Subsequently, it integrates the sequence of recent events, incremental information, and retrieved historical examples to forecast stock price fluctuations. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that this system outperforms traditional predictive models in terms of accuracy and interpretability. Moreover, it provides enhanced transparency by explicitly tracing the causal relationships between events and stock price variations, thereby elucidating the underlying decision-making mechanisms.

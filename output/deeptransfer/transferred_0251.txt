In the process of story creation, individuals can either start with an event as the focal point and then shape the traits and development of the entities associated with the event, or they can begin with an entity as the centerpiece and construct a series of events surrounding it. E2, a doctoral candidate in literature, believes that narratives can be deconstructed into fundamental components such as story outlines, sequences, propositions, and parts of speech. By progressively assembling these components in reverse order, one can construct a coherent narrative. The parts of speech, such as an individual, an action, or an attribute, can form a proposition. Several propositions can be organized into a sequence, and multiple sequences can ultimately constitute a complete narrative. However, merely assembling these components does not suffice to create a coherent narrative. Therefore, the elements that interlink these components, termed relationships, are crucial for ensuring the narrative's cohesion. Interviews conducted with two experts revealed that a narrative comprises four fundamental components: entities (people or objects), events, relationships, and frameworks. In consultations with subject matter experts, we conducted a detailed examination of each component, revealing that they may convey varying interpretations. Events constitute a central component of a narrative; however, the specific details of these events may not always be immediately discernible within the narrative structure. While events may exhibit a degree of autonomy, their interactional dynamics are integral to constructing narrative tension and meaning. Furthermore, the identity of entities and the nature of their relationships within the narrative framework can significantly influence the unfolding of events. Nevertheless, the particulars concerning individuals or objects and their interrelations may also be delineated independently.

To refine these results, we repeated the analyses using the ATTARI subscales. Notably, only the behavioral subscale (BEH) emerged as a significant predictor, whereas the affective and cognitive subscales did not contribute when considered simultaneously (see Supplementary Materials S3). This finding suggests that participants’ practical willingness to engage with AI primarily drives the predictive power of AI attitudes, rather than their general beliefs or emotional dispositions. To complement these quantitative analyses and explore subjective evaluation criteria, we conducted a thematic analysis of 178 open-ended responses. An overview of the main themes is presented in Table 2, with the full codebook available in the Supplementary Materials. Several categories, including music quality, emotional aspects, and humanness, emerged from the qualitative data. Participants often described AI-generated music in terms of its technical and musical quality, originality, and ability to evoke emotions. Among these, humanness was the most frequently mentioned theme, extending beyond perceived sound characteristics. For instance, some respondents highlighted the significance of the human touch, such as the artist’s personality or the narrative behind a song, and described AI-generated music as lacking soul and authenticity. However, others expressed indifference to the origins of the music, with one participant stating, “If it sounds good then I don’t mind if it was artificially generated” (ID926). These responses further underscore the influence of attitudes toward AI on listeners’ evaluations of AI-generated music. Moreover, many comments reflected normative stances rather than assessments of the songs’ quality or attributes, as exemplified by statements like, “I don’t like anything to do with AI-generated music” (ID914). Beyond these dimensions, the open-ended responses revealed additional areas of evaluation that are less frequently addressed in experimental research. Specifically, participants assessed AI-generated music through ethical lenses, raising concerns about the displacement of musicians, the economic harm to artists, and the transparency of training data and copyright. For example, one respondent remarked that AI music “is built upon data without the consent of the artists that have dedicated decades of their lives to the craft” (ID615). Similarly, another participant observed, “I think it undermines and abuses artists” (ID446). These comments illustrate that listeners evaluate AI-generated music not only based on its aesthetic qualities but also on the fairness of its production and distribution processes.

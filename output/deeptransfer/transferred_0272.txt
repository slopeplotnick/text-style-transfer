To refine these results, we conducted additional analyses using the ATTARI subscales. Only the behavioral subscale (BEH) emerged as a significant predictor, whereas the affective and cognitive subscales did not contribute when considered simultaneously (see supplements S3). This indicates that the predictive power of AI attitudes is primarily driven by participants' willingness to engage with AI in practice, rather than their general beliefs or feelings. To complement the quantitative findings and explore subjective evaluation criteria, we analyzed 178 open-ended responses, categorizing them into thematic groups as summarized in Table 2, with detailed information provided in the Supplementary Materials. Participants frequently evaluated AI-generated music based on its technical and musical quality, originality, emotional impact, and perceived humanness. Notably, humanness emerged as the most prominent theme, extending beyond auditory characteristics alone. Several respondents highlighted the importance of the human touch, emphasizing that an artist's personality and the narrative behind a composition significantly influenced their judgments. Some described AI-generated music as lacking soul and authenticity, while others expressed indifference to its origin, stating, "If it sounds good then I don't mind if it was artificially generated" (ID926). These responses further underscore the role of attitudes toward AI in shaping evaluations of AI-generated music. Many comments were explicitly normative, reflecting general opinions about AI rather than specific assessments of song quality, such as the remark, "I don't like anything to do with AI-generated music" (ID914). Beyond these dimensions, the open-ended responses revealed additional perspectives that extend beyond conventional experimental measures. For instance, participants assessed AI-generated music through ethical considerations, raising concerns about its potential to replace musicians, financially disadvantage artists, or rely on training data without proper authorization. As one participant noted, "is built upon data without the consent of the artists that have dedicated decades of their lives to the craft" (ID615). Another remarked, "I think it undermines and abuses artists" (ID446), illustrating that listeners evaluate AI-generated music not only in terms of its aesthetic qualities but also through the lens of fairness in its creation and distribution.

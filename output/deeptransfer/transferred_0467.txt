A significant challenge for OLLMs arises from the diversity and complexity of input modalities, which fundamentally reshape how safety risks emerge and how they must be addressed. The heterogeneity of inputs across various modalities introduces distinct risk patterns, such as implicit bias in textual data, misinformation in visual content, or privacy breaches in audio data. Moreover, the complexity of cross-modal interactions further exacerbates the difficulty of identifying latent risks. For instance, the phrase "I want to fly" paired with an image of an individual standing on a rooftop may seem innocuous when considered independently; however, their combination could convey potentially hazardous implications, such as suicidal intent. Similarly, a video depicting police activity, when accompanied by a textual query like "How to overcome obstacles," may reveal an implicit criminal motive that is not discernible from either modality in isolation. Consequently, the safety risks associated with OLLMs become significantly more intricate, requiring both a nuanced understanding of individual modalities and enhanced cross-modal interpretative capabilities. However, efforts to improve the safety of OLLMs directly within the base models often demand substantial time and computational resources, which can compromise their fundamental reasoning capabilities. Despite significant investments, these models still require costly retraining to address emergent issues and remain vulnerable to low-cost adversarial attacks. In contrast, guardrail models offer a decentralized and adaptable alternative. Yet, existing research predominantly focuses on unimodal contexts or simplistic modality combinations, such as image-text pairs, leaving the complexities of omni-modal scenarios largely unaddressed. Since unsafe inputs can originate from diverse modalities or their combinations, reliance on unimodal guardrails or basic image-text guardrails proves insufficient for comprehensive moderation. Furthermore, the binary approach to safety assessment, commonly employed by many guardrails, limits their effectiveness in addressing the nuanced risks inherent in omni-modal systems.

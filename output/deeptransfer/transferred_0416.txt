To ensure the comparability of results across different studies, it is imperative that the samples exhibit certain similarities. Therefore, we assessed this through the application of Multigroup Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA), treating our two samples—one derived from real-world data and the other simulated—as independent groups, with progressively stringent constraints introduced. This analysis was decomposed into several components: testing for configural invariance (H2.1), metric invariance (H2.2), scalar invariance (H2.3), and residual invariance (H2.4). If the foundational configuration is consistent across both groups, it indicates that the underlying pattern is analogous. Moreover, when the measurements are equivalent, it becomes feasible to assess the disparities between latent variables. Similarly, if the mean values are congruent, it allows for the comparison of latent mean scores. Furthermore, if the residual differences between the datasets are comparable, further analyses can be conducted using the observed mean scores. A high degree of similarity between the real-world and simulated samples in these aspects would suggest that the simulated data reliably approximates the properties of real-world data. Consistent with our hypotheses, we anticipated that the responses derived from the real-world and simulated datasets would exhibit substantial similarity (H3) and that the score distributions across both datasets would demonstrate equivalence (H4). To evaluate the extent to which the simulated data mirrored real-world data, we conducted a series of statistical analyses. First, Mann-Whitney U tests were employed to assess potential differences in central tendency between the two datasets. Subsequently, Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests were utilized to examine discrepancies in the overall distribution of scores.

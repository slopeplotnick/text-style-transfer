The globalization of education and the rapid expansion of online learning necessitate the effective localization of educational content to accommodate diverse audiences. Lecture materials are inherently multimodal, integrating spoken audio with visual slides, which requires systems capable of processing and synchronizing multiple input modalities. To ensure an inclusive and comprehensive learning experience, translations must faithfully preserve all components: text for reading, slides for visual understanding, and speech for auditory engagement. We present BOOM, a multimodal multilingual lecture companion designed to jointly translate lecture audio and slides, producing synchronized outputs across three modalities: translated text, visually localized slides with preserved formatting, and synthesized speech. This integrated approach enables students to access lecture content in their native language while maintaining the integrity of the original material. Experimental results indicate that incorporating slide-aware transcripts provides additional benefits for downstream tasks such as summarization and question answering. We have made our Slide Translation code available at https://github.com/saikoneru/image-Translator and have integrated it into the Lecture Translator system at https://gitlab.kit.edu/kit/isl-ai41t/lt-middleware/ltpipeline. Providing educational content in a student's native language significantly enhances their comprehension and learning experience. Localizing lecture materials mitigates communication barriers, improves accessibility, and facilitates deeper engagement with complex concepts. As higher education becomes increasingly internationalized, the provision of multilingual lecture content, both in-person and online, has become crucial for enhancing the accessibility of educational resources. With the digital transformation of teaching, lecture materials are inherently multimodal, integrating various forms of media. The primary component is the lecture audio, which can be transcribed into text through the application of Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) technologies. Moreover, instructional materials are delivered through slides, and additional resources such as summaries, chapters, and question-answer interactions can be generated from the transcript using advanced systems based on Large Language Models (LLMs) to enhance the educational experience. To ensure accessibility for all students, including those who are non-native speakers, these outputs should be available in multiple languages. Therefore, effective localization must address this diversity of content, encompassing audio, text, and visual materials, thereby rendering lecture translation a truly multimodal challenge.

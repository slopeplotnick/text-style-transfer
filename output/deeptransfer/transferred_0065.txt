This study evaluates the performance of four distinct models—MOIRAI, CHRONOS, GTT, and LightGTS—each employing unique methodologies for learning and prediction to address variations in data and training processes. To ensure compatibility with the dataset and isolate the impact of variations in data size, we utilize the smallest configurations of the models. This approach minimizes the influence of computational power and focuses exclusively on their adaptability to changes in data volume. Each model employs distinct predictive and learning mechanisms, which inherently shape their responses to fluctuations in data size. For instance, CHRONOS adopts a segmentation-based methodology, wherein data is partitioned into smaller subsets prior to the learning process. Consequently, this strategy enables the model to effectively mitigate the influence of data size on its predictive performance, ensuring it is not adversely affected by large numerical values. Moreover, we standardize all data prior to training to prevent any individual data point from disproportionately influencing the outcomes. Subsequently, we calibrate each data point to ensure uniformity and balance across the dataset. To evaluate the efficacy of the models, we employ the Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE) metric, which facilitates an assessment of predictive accuracy independent of data magnitude. Our objective is to ascertain the impact of data size variations on model performance, demonstrated through a comparative analysis of the reduction in MASE against a conventional approach.

The developed scale consists of 9 items on a 7-point Likert scale, divided into three subscales that measure AI use-related emotions: Shame, Guilt, and Impostor Syndrome. The real-world dataset was acquired through a survey study that included these items alongside additional psychometric measures to evaluate the criterion validity of the scale. The online survey was conducted on Prolific from the evening of February 3, 2025, until the late night of February 4, 2025. Once the real-world dataset was obtained, we compared it with the simulated dataset using the same methodological approach as in Study 1. Table 1 presents a demographic comparison of the participants in both datasets. A comprehensive description of the SAGAT scale will be provided in a forthcoming scientific publication, following the completion of the conventional scale development process, which has been partially informed by our experimental prototyping methodology. Group comparisons between real and simulated data were conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test revealed no significant difference in scores for the Impostor Syndrome factor between real and simulated datasets. However, significant differences were observed in the dimensions of Shame and Guilt. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests revealed significant distributional differences for all three emotional constructs, thereby not supporting H4. Histograms illustrating the comparison between the distributions of the real and simulated datasets are presented in Figure 4. To enhance the validity and reliability of our findings, we conducted a replication study aimed at developing a novel scale. Specifically, we devised a scale to measure AI-linked anxious beliefs, termed the "AI Anxiety" scale. This scale was developed employing the same methodology as delineated in Study 3, details of which are available in the corresponding "Methods" section. For a comprehensive account of the scale development process utilizing simulated data, the reader is directed to consult the Supplementary Materials accompanying this article, as well as the preregistration available on the OSF at the provided link.

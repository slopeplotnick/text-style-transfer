In contrast to image-based IC-LoRA methods that require separate training for each editing task, our single Sync-LoRA module effectively generalizes across diverse edits, achieving a robust balance between high visual fidelity and precise motion preservation, as demonstrated in our experiments (see section 4). In-context learning (ICL) within diffusion models enables models to generalize to new tasks by conditioning on structured input examples rather than relying on explicit instructions. Although extensively studied in language models, recent research has begun to explore its application in diffusion-based image generation. In these instances, the model generates a collection of images where the images within the collection provide contextual relevance to one another. This capability is particularly advantageous for applications such as storyboard generation, which require consistency across the generated images. When these collections are conditioned on a given real image, ICL-based methodologies can be employed for a wide range of image-to-image tasks, including image personalization and image editing. The work most closely related to ours is IC-LoRA, which identified that transformer-based diffusion models inherently possess the capability to generate panels in which certain attributes remain consistent across different images. This capability was further enhanced by fine-tuning the model with a low-rank adapter (LoRA) on structured, consistent panels, coupled with the use of specific template prompts. Their approach has been successfully applied to various tasks, including film storyboard generation, font design, and home decoration. Our research advances this concept by extending its application to the domain of video editing, introducing an IC-LoRA specifically designed for frame-accurate portrait synchronization. Furthermore, recent studies have demonstrated that large video diffusion transformers inherently exhibit in-context learning capabilities, enabling the generation of multiple temporally or spatially related video clips within a single diffusion process. However, while these studies explored the potential of inpainting for scene control, they did not address the temporal coherence of the generated clips. Building on this foundation, PoseGen employs in-context LoRA fine-tuning to enable pose-controllable human video generation, producing extended and fluid motion sequences derived from a reference image. In contrast, our approach explicitly trains the in-context behavior to prioritize synchronized editing, ensuring robust temporal alignment and edit consistency.

The MODOMA system serves as a laboratory framework for conducting language acquisition experiments, offering a fully parametrized environment. This means researchers can configure the parameters of each experiment by selecting predefined settings. Importantly, the system encompasses all facets of language acquisition, including the adult model, the daughter agent, and the linguistic processes, with adjustable configurations available for each component. The remaining parameters are instrumental in defining the experimental framework. Specifically, the parameter associated with the mother agent dictates the structural composition of Delilah's utterances, determining whether sentences or noun phrases are produced. The training and test experiments delineated in this paper systematically manipulate this parameter to explore its effects. Similarly, the minimum amount of data that the daughter agent is required to process prior to the acquisition of functional and content categories varies across all experiments. These two parameters establish the experimental framework. In particular, during the training experiments, the acquisition of functional and content categories is initiated after the processing of 1,000 noun phrases and 1,000 sentences, followed by 10,000 noun phrases and 10,000 sentences. This enables us to ascertain the requisite number of exemplars generated by Delilah necessary to discern this statistical pattern. Furthermore, the parameter for functional category acquisition determines the type-token ratio employed to transform distributional distinctions between function and content words into explicit linguistic knowledge. The training sessions delineated in this paper are instrumental in identifying optimal values for these parameters, while the test experiments serve to validate these configurations using novel datasets produced by Delilah.

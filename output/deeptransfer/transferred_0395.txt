To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to systematically leverage an open-vocabulary object detector to enhance MLLMs' understanding of high-resolution images. Experimental results confirm that the detector achieves precise target localization, effectively suppressing interference from irrelevant regions. We propose MRD, a training-free and universally applicable framework that innovatively addresses semantic similarity through a multi-resolution fusion strategy. By integrating open-set detection results, MRD enhances target regions and creates a synergistic effect. Comprehensive experiments further validate the effectiveness and generalization of our method, demonstrating superior performance on both single-object and multi-object tasks across diverse MLLMs and high-resolution benchmarks. To improve high-resolution image comprehension without proportionally increasing the computational burden from visual tokens, several studies have incorporated high-resolution visual encoders into MLLMs. For example, Vary [23] and Deepseek-VL [15] utilize the SAM encoder [10] to boost model performance on high-resolution images. Alternatively, another line of research has introduced Native/Dynamic-Resolution MLLMs, which process images at their native resolution. The central concept is to generate a variable-length sequence of visual tokens that aligns with the original dimensions of the input image, thereby preserving spatial fidelity and high-frequency details. These models employ various strategies to address the challenges posed by extended sequences and computational complexity, such as sliding window attention, dynamic masking, and patch-based encoding techniques. Notable advancements in this domain have been achieved by representative works. For instance, the InternVL series [4, 6, 31] adopts a method of partitioning high-resolution images into multiple fixed-size patches. In contrast, models like Qwen2.5-VL [2, 3] employ an end-to-end approach by directly training a Vision Transformer (ViT) on native-resolution images. This approach enables the model to encapsulate the entire image into a single, coherent token sequence in one forward pass, thereby facilitating improved global context understanding.

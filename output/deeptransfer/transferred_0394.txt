Despite its significance, the community still lacks a comprehensive, reproducible, and scalable methodology for benchmarking the power and energy consumption of LLM inference across various model scales, hardware generations, software configurations, and deployment modes, ranging from single-node to multi-node distributed inference. Existing initiatives only partially address this issue. For instance, the MLPerf Inference benchmark (Reddi et al. 2020) establishes a standard for evaluating performance across diverse machine learning tasks; however, it does not systematically address comprehensive power consumption or normalize energy usage to align with LLM-specific service units, such as Joules per generated token or per prompt. The MLPerf Power benchmark (Tschand et al. 2025) expands the scope of measurements but predominantly (i) emphasizes single-node configurations, (ii) examines models of modest size relative to the cutting-edge state-of-the-art large language models (LLMs), such as the Llama 3 405B-class, which remain largely underexplored, and (iii) depends on high-precision metering equipment that is both costly and challenging to implement across diverse institutional testbeds. Furthermore, existing benchmark suites seldom investigate the configuration parameters that practitioners routinely adjust in practical applications, such as batch sizing strategies, tensor/pipeline parallelism, context length, and quantization levels. These configuration settings significantly influence both instantaneous power consumption and the average energy expenditure per token. To address this deficiency, we introduce TokenPowerBench, a novel, lightweight, and user-friendly benchmark specifically designed to quantify the power consumption and energy cost associated with LLM inference. TokenPowerBench incorporates a modular instrumentation framework that interfaces with vendor telemetry APIs, such as power sensors for GPU, CPU, and memory, to facilitate node-level energy monitoring, with the capability to extend to rack- or facility-level assessments when feasible. Moreover, a unified metrics pipeline aligns each power measurement with the two principal inference phases—prefill and decode—enabling precise identification of energy expenditure. We conduct comprehensive experiments to systematically investigate the correlation between energy consumption and inference-relevant parameters, including batch size, maximum context length, parallelization strategy, and quantization.

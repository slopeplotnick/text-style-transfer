While machine learning and generative techniques are popular and widespread, the application of AI in decision-making remains relatively uncommon. This is largely because decision-making involves the irrevocable allocation of resources, a process that typically falls outside the scope of academic research. To date, the most significant and impactful use of decision-making AI in mineral exploration has been the characterization of the high-grade portion of the Mingomba deposit in Zambia (Dempsey, 2024). In this case, historical drilling data were re-analyzed using advanced analytical tools, resulting in the generation of new hypotheses that identified a higher-grade mineralized zone previously overlooked. AI, operationalized as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), was employed to systematically optimize the drilling sequence. The primary objective of this approach was to reduce uncertainty surrounding geological hypotheses regarding the spatial distribution of high-grade mineralization. Notably, this method proved far more efficient than the traditional grid-based drilling approach (Mern and Caers, 2023). Its efficiency stemmed from prioritizing the falsification of geological hypotheses rather than merely attempting to locate mineralization. In Part III, I will explore how AI can accelerate the discovery and characterization of critical mineral deposits. Initially, I will focus on data analysis and prediction, followed by AI-based decision-making. The initial deployment of AI was met with some defensiveness in certain circles, as there was a perception that AI might replace or diminish the role of domain experts. Concerns about job security are a common theme in contemporary discussions. While some believed AI would independently generate novel discoveries, it is unlikely that AI will autonomously pinpoint specific locations on a map for robotic exploration and subsequent discovery. Instead, AI should be viewed as a tool to augment the capabilities of domain experts by taking on tasks that are either beyond their expertise or unnecessary, thereby allowing them to focus on the creative aspects of process-oriented thinking in geology and geochemistry.

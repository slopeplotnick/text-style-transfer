OmniFusion is adapted to facilitate live translation by extracting relevant lecture slides from segmented audio inputs. Moreover, an open-source image-to-image translation framework with modular components is introduced, paving the way for future advancements in full-image or slide translation and rendering. Including visual data is shown to significantly enhance speech translation (ST) performance for downstream natural language processing (NLP) tasks across various large language models (LLMs), with improvements observed across multiple language pairs. To further this effort, we evaluate several optical character recognition (OCR) models and assess the translation quality of both unimodal and multimodal neural machine translation (NMT) models for image translation. To fully localize lecture content across multiple modalities and languages and support accessibility tasks such as summarization (SUM) and question answering (QA), we develop multimodal translation systems. These systems perform multimodal ST and leverage the resulting transcripts for downstream LLM tasks. Additionally, slides are translated by converting text and images into the target language while preserving their original layout and visual coherence. To enhance usability and map visual context to each audio segment, we developed a PDF viewer that displays slides with overlaid captions synchronized to the presenter’s selected slide. This interface allows participants to seamlessly follow translations while viewing slides and enables the system to automatically identify the slide corresponding to each audio segment, thereby providing essential contextual information for multimodal ST. In this section, we first describe the multimodal ST pipeline, including the extraction and association of slide images with audio segments. Next, we outline how the resulting translations are utilized for downstream tasks such as SUM and QA. Finally, we detail the slide-translation process, explaining how text embedded within slide images is detected, translated, and re-rendered. Additional information about the Text-to-Speech (TTS) component is provided in Appendix A.1. While several ST systems support translation across multiple languages, they are not well-suited for live lecture scenarios. Most systems are designed for offline tasks with fixed segmentation, which is incompatible with streaming audio, and require simultaneous translation policies to determine when sufficient audio has been received. Furthermore, existing systems are either unimodal, neglecting slides, or multimodal but lack multilingual support. Lecture environments, however, demand both multimodality and multilinguality.

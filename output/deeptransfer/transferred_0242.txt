The incorporation of verification or feedback signals has emerged as a key strategy in test-time adaptation. Li et al. (2025) introduces feedback-guided test-time training, where external verifiers provide weak supervision to refine predictions during inference. Similarly, Zuo et al. (2025) presents Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL), which generates multiple rollouts, aggregates predictions through majority voting, and uses the consensus as a reward signal to update the policy. While these methods are effective, they are hindered by inefficiency, as stabilizing consensus estimates requires numerous redundant rollouts. More recently, Wang et al. (2025) demonstrates that reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) achieves strong reasoning gains with remarkably few training examples, highlighting the promise of efficient RL approaches in test-time settings. Building on this foundation, our work addresses rollout inefficiency by introducing a statistically grounded early-stopping mechanism for adaptive allocation. Policy optimization techniques have also played a pivotal role in reinforcement learning for language models. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017) is a widely used algorithm for stabilizing policy updates under KL constraints and has been instrumental in reinforcement learning from human feedback. Extending this framework, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) incorporates group-normalized advantages, enabling more stable optimization for language models under limited feedback. Leveraging the generality of these surrogate models, OptPO introduces a plug-and-play rollout allocation mechanism that enhances efficiency without modifying the underlying optimization algorithm. In summary, prior research has established test-time training as a powerful paradigm for improving model adaptability during inference, encompassing techniques such as entropy minimization, verifier-guided learning, and reinforcement learning. However, existing test-time policy optimization methods often suffer from computational inefficiency due to excessive rollout requirements. Addressing this limitation, our work introduces OptPO, a novel framework for optimal rollout allocation that significantly reduces computational costs while maintaining robust performance, thereby complementing and extending existing test-time adaptation methods.

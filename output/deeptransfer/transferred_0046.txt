We evaluated WorldMM using five long video question-answering benchmarks, ranging from hour- to week-long durations. Our proposed methodology consistently outperformed robust baselines, including long video LLMs and memory-augmented models. Comprehensive ablation studies further demonstrate the effectiveness of our multi-memory, multiscale design. Specifically, episodic memory enables reasoning over events at multiple temporal scales, visual memory enhances performance on object- and action-centered queries, and semantic memory augments reasoning over long-term contexts. When all these memory systems are adaptively integrated, the model achieves superior overall performance. These findings underscore that our multimodal memory system represents a significant advancement in the domain of long video comprehension. Existing models designed for short video analysis exhibit strong comprehension capabilities, while recent research has increasingly focused on the analysis of longer video content. Current advanced models, such as GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5, have achieved proficiency in processing videos of minute- to hour-long durations by utilizing extended context lengths. However, these models entail significant computational expenses and exhibit suboptimal performance when uniformly sampling every frame, particularly in scenarios requiring analysis of localized events. To address these limitations, various methodologies have been proposed. Visual token compression improves computational efficiency by reducing the number of tokens but often sacrifices fine-grained details, thereby limiting the capture of subtle or infrequent events. Similarly, key frame selection mitigates redundancy by retaining only the most salient frames, yet it struggles to identify relevant frames in extended video sequences and risks overlooking rare but significant occurrences. More recently, reasoning-centric training and adaptive inference techniques have facilitated improved comprehension of extended video content. Nevertheless, these approaches encounter significant limitations when applied to ultra-long videos exceeding ten hours in duration. Emerging benchmarks now challenge models to process continuous recordings spanning days or even weeks. The previously mentioned methodologies are inadequate in these scenarios due to the overwhelming volume of frames and the intricate long-term temporal dependencies inherent in such extensive video data. This underscores the imperative for developing more efficient, context-aware, and scalable methodologies to process extremely long videos.

Explanations in XAI must adhere to two fundamental criteria: they should be both comprehensible to the intended audience and faithful to the underlying model. Understandability is receiver-dependent, generally requiring that explanations be articulated in a clear and concise manner. Fidelity, on the other hand, demands that the explanation accurately represents the model's decision-making processes without introducing distortions or biases. Such biases may arise if the explanation is deliberately constrained or manipulated. While fidelity and comprehensibility are distinct attributes, optimizing each independently presents significant challenges. For instance, explanations that align with individuals' expectations are frequently perceived as more comprehensible; however, this can engender bias if it influences the selection of explanations. Moreover, a faithful explanation that is misinterpreted can equally mislead the explainer. This study introduces a post-hoc explanatory framework for models, designed to enhance both understandability and fidelity. Primarily, it is asserted that explanations should employ straightforward language and elucidate causal relationships, as this reflects the typical manner in which individuals convey information. Therefore, this approach facilitates the alignment of the intended interpretation with the actual comprehension by the audience, obviating the necessity for supplementary guidance on interpretation and rendering the explanation more concise. The utilization of comprehensible language is crucial due to the potential divergence between the data features utilized by the model and the optimal features for elucidation. This notion underpins a category of methodologies referred to as Concept-based XAI (C-XAI). The fundamental premise of this approach posits that, although models are adept at processing data in forms such as images and graphs, human interaction predominantly relies on a vocabulary comprising high-level, semantically meaningful concepts.

Certain models may be more reliable than others. This review addresses the influence of model size, structure, and training data on the occurrence of these hallucinations. A study illustrates these tendencies across different models and offers solutions like the improvement of training techniques and the imposition of limitations on the generated output to tackle the issue. In the same vein, another study highlights that hallucinations remain a fundamental problem for large language models (LLMs), regardless of their size or training methodology. The study proposes that it is possible to restrain hallucinations through careful model selection and optimization of training processes; however, the authors contend that it is unlikely to develop models entirely free from such issues. This observation is of great significance as it helps in formulating realistic goals and assessing the appropriateness of the chosen model for specific tasks. When selecting an LLM to reduce hallucinations, it is essential to consider factors such as model architecture, the quality and diversity of the training data, and the implementation of strategies like fine-tuning or retrieval augmentation. Many such models are available today, including GPT, BERT, and T5. Among these, GPT has been observed to generate text that is logically and cohesively consistent; however, such generative models are prone to producing fabricated information, commonly referred to as hallucinations. BERT, on the other hand, demonstrates a lower propensity for hallucinations due to its utilization of bidirectional training for specific tasks; however, it remains susceptible to generating fabricated content. Alternative models, such as PaLM and LLaMa, have been developed with the aim of reducing hallucination rates, though their effectiveness varies based on their respective training methodologies and architectural designs. A comprehensive understanding of the strengths and limitations of these models is essential when selecting an appropriate tool for applications where the accuracy of generated information is critical, and minimizing hallucination rates is a priority.

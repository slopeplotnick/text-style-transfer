Summarization. As demonstrated in Table 3, summaries generated from multimodal inputs, incorporating both audio and image data, consistently outperform those derived from audio-only inputs across the majority of languages and models, despite the summarization models being text-based. The performance improvements are most notable for English, German, and Italian, whereas a slight decline is observed for Chinese. This disparity may be attributed to the linguistic similarities between English and languages that utilize the Latin alphabet, which facilitate the integration of additional English-based information. In contrast, the significant structural and lexical differences between English and Chinese may limit the models' ability to effectively leverage such supplementary information. Question Answering. In the context of question answering, the integration of visual elements yields marginal improvements, albeit to a lesser extent than observed in summarization tasks. While there are instances of slight enhancement, performance regression is noted in four out of twelve language-model configurations. This phenomenon is likely attributable to the model's lack of direct access to the image data, relying solely on the enhanced textual context, which proves insufficient for significantly improving the model's ability to answer questions accurately. Streaming Speech Translation. Over the past decade, there has been substantial research into streaming speech translation. Several lecture translation tools have utilized this technology; however, they predominantly rely on audio input. In contrast, our research distinguishes itself by integrating both visual and auditory elements from slides, thereby generating translated audio and slides in multiple languages. Image-to-Image Translation. The domain of image-to-image translation remains relatively under-explored. Interest in this area is increasing due to the availability of larger datasets; however, the majority of existing research predominantly concentrates on the translation of text within images, neglecting the subsequent aligned re-rendering of the visual content. An initial advancement in this field is represented by a study that explicitly models the rendering process to ensure visual fidelity post-translation. Our image translation framework is characterized by its flexibility, allowing researchers to integrate their own models at any stage of the process, from optical character recognition (OCR) to translation and rendering, without necessitating the construction of additional components.

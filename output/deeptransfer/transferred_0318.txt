Summarization. As shown in Table 3, summaries generated from audio+image input consistently outperform those based on audio-only across most languages and models, even though the summarization models are text-only. The gains are most pronounced in English, German, and Italian, while results for Chinese exhibit a slight decline. We hypothesize that this is due to English domain terminology appearing in references for Latin-alphabet languages, whereas the lexical distance between English and Chinese prevents the models from fully leveraging the additional context provided in English. Question Answering. In most scenarios, incorporating visual context leads to slight improvements in QA performance; however, these gains are far less significant compared to those observed in summarization. While small improvements are noted in most cases, performance regressions occur in four out of twelve language-model combinations. We attribute this to the fact that the model does not process image data directly but instead relies on the enhanced textual context derived from multimodal inputs, which appears insufficient to consistently improve the model's ability to answer questions more accurately. Streaming speech translation has been extensively studied over the past decade (Macháček et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2025; Papi et al., 2025a). Several lecture translation tools have also incorporated this technology (Müller et al., 2016; Dessloch et al., 2018; Huber et al., 2023), though these systems predominantly rely on audio input. In contrast, our work advances lecture translation by extending it to multimodal input, integrating visual cues from slides, and producing multimodal output, including translated audio and slides in multiple languages. Image-to-image translation, however, remains relatively underexplored. Interest in this area is growing with the availability of larger datasets (Zuo et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025; Zhuang et al., 2025). Nonetheless, most existing research focuses primarily on text translation within images, without addressing the challenge of re-rendering visual content to ensure alignment post-translation. A notable initial step in this direction is (Tian et al., 2025), which explicitly models the rendering process. Our proposed image translation framework offers a modular and flexible foundation, enabling researchers to integrate their own models at any stage, from OCR to translation and rendering, without requiring the development of additional components.

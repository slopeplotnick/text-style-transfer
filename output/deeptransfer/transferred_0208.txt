Search relevance plays a pivotal role in e-commerce platforms such as JD and Taobao. The relevance model evaluates candidate items by determining their alignment with user queries and assigning relevance scores to filter out mismatched products. This mechanism serves as a crucial foundation for enhancing user experience and search quality. Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable performance across various tasks and have been extensively applied to relevance tasks. However, state-of-the-art (SOTA) models, such as GPT-5, suffer from limitations including insufficient domain-specific knowledge and prohibitive operational costs. To address these challenges, researchers have increasingly focused on post-training smaller-scale LLMs on e-commerce datasets to develop domain-specific relevance experts. Recent advancements have shifted from rudimentary classification-oriented Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to more sophisticated approaches aimed at enhancing reasoning capabilities. While chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning has been modeled from multiple perspectives, significant gaps remain that hinder the comprehensive handling of complex relevance tasks. For example, ELLM conceptualizes relevance as a process of attribute extraction and matching between queries and items. Nevertheless, this approach struggles with boundary cases requiring rule-based judgment. For instance, when matching the query "lake blue top" with the item "sky blue top," the model lacks the necessary color discrimination rules to make accurate judgments. Alternative models, such as LREF and TaoSR1, explicitly incorporate rule-following chains of thought, thereby endowing the models with rule-awareness capabilities. However, their failure to integrate visual information creates blind spots in their assessments. For example, when a user queries a "blue top" and the item title omits the color description while the accompanying image clearly depicts a blue top, these models fail to make accurate judgments due to their reliance solely on textual data. These limitations highlight a fundamental question: What constitutes a comprehensive CoT representation for relevance tasks? This inquiry probes the essential capabilities required for accurate and effective relevance assessment.

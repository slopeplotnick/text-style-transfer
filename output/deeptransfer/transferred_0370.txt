Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) is the task of identifying a target object based on a natural language description and is a significant challenge within vision-language research. Recent advancements have moved beyond basic lexical matching, emphasizing a deeper understanding of semantic content and the integration of external knowledge to enhance performance. Generalized Referring Expression Comprehension (GREC) involves interpreting descriptions that may refer to zero or multiple objects, exemplified by expressions such as "Pick up those three boxes." This paradigm aligns more closely with naturalistic communication, where referents may be ambiguous, absent, or occluded. However, advancing research in this domain necessitates extensive datasets comprising real-world images paired with human-generated annotations, which can be resource-intensive to collect. Therefore, online crowdsourcing platforms provide a cost-effective and reproducible framework for conducting controlled experiments in visual grounding research. Yet, complexities arise when models are required to interpret a scene, maintain conversational context, and engage in bidirectional communication. Under these conditions, existing methodologies encounter significant challenges. A recent investigation introduced a novel GREC test utilizing dialogues within a Minecraft gaming environment, demonstrating the considerable difficulty faced by contemporary AI models. The study identified the challenge of generating conversational data due to the high costs associated with human-written dialogues, resulting in limited training datasets. This paper proposes an innovative approach to augmenting data by generating concise descriptions and detailed dialogues to enhance AI comprehension. While previous research on AI-generated data is pertinent, applying these methodologies to GREC presents difficulties, as it necessitates the ability to discern spatial relationships and integrate visual information.

Referring Expression Comprehension (REC)—the task of identifying a target based on a natural language description—is a significant area of vision-language research. Recent advancements have moved beyond simple surface-level matching towards a deeper understanding of semantic content, notably through the construction of compositional referring expressions and the integration of external knowledge. Generalized Referring Expression Comprehension (GREC)—the task of interpreting descriptions that may refer to zero or multiple objects, as exemplified by the phrase "Pick up those three boxes"—relaxes these assumptions to more accurately mirror real-world communication, where the referent may be ambiguous, occluded, or absent. However, this research typically requires extensive datasets of real-world images with large volumes of human annotations, which can be costly. Online crowdsourcing platforms offer a controlled and reproducible environment for visual grounding research, thereby reducing the expenses and variability associated with data collection. Yet, complexities arise when models are required to simultaneously interpret a scene, maintain conversational context, and engage in reciprocal dialogue. Under these conditions, existing methodologies encounter significant challenges. Recent research introduced a novel GREC evaluation utilizing dialogues within the Minecraft gaming environment, demonstrating the formidable difficulties faced by contemporary AI models in these contexts. Moreover, the study identified the significant cost associated with generating human-authored dialogues, resulting in a limited availability of training data. This paper proposes an innovative approach to augment data by generating concise descriptions and comprehensive dialogues, thereby enhancing AI comprehension. While prior research on AI-generated data is relevant, applying these methods to GREC presents challenges due to the necessity of spatial understanding and the integration of visual context.

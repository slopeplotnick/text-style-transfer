As discussed in the introduction, unguided self-evolving systems tend to plateau quickly and exhibit instability, often leading to diversity collapse and reward hacking. Our empirical results clearly illustrate these issues. In the left panel, R-Zero exhibits a sharp decline in question diversity as training progresses, dropping from 35 to below 20 within the first 50 training steps. While the 2-gram diversity score begins to rise after 50 steps, this increase coincides with a dramatic surge in question length, as shown in the middle panel. This suggests that the rise in diversity is largely an artifact of length rather than genuine semantic diversification. Longer outputs naturally introduce more unique n-gram combinations, inflating the diversity metric without reflecting meaningful exploration. A similar trend is observed in question length itself, where R-Zero progressively generates longer and excessively verbose questions. This behavior indicates reward hacking, as the model exploits superficial features like verbosity to artificially boost perceived diversity or difficulty metrics. In contrast, R-Few maintains a stable level of diversity throughout training while keeping question length consistent, suggesting that minimal human guidance effectively prevents diversity collapse. Turning to the right panel, both methods show an increase in question difficulty over time; however, this apparent similarity conceals critical qualitative differences. To quantify difficulty, questions generated at various training stages were relabeled using Gemini-2.5-Pro and subsequently evaluated by having the Qwen3-8B-Base model attempt to answer them. Difficulty is defined as the percentage of questions the model answers incorrectly, representing the inverse of accuracy. Both methods exhibit an overall upward trend in difficulty, but the underlying causes differ significantly. R-Zero primarily increases question difficulty by inflating question length rather than requiring deeper reasoning, reflecting another form of reward hacking where perceived difficulty is artificially enhanced without substantive improvements in reasoning complexity. Conversely, R-Few maintains consistent question length, enabling the system to evolve toward genuinely more complex and challenging questions without compromising diversity or structural integrity.

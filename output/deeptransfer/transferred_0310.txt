Auditors rely on Journal Entry Tests (JETs) to detect anomalies in tax-related ledger records; however, these rule-based methods often generate an overwhelming number of false positives and struggle to identify subtle irregularities. This study explores whether large language models (LLMs) can serve as effective anomaly detectors in double-entry bookkeeping. By benchmarking state-of-the-art LLMs, such as LLaMA and Gemma, on both synthetic and real-world anonymized ledgers, we compare their performance against JETs and traditional machine learning baselines. Our findings reveal that LLMs consistently outperform rule-based JETs and classical ML approaches, while also offering natural-language explanations that improve interpretability. These results underscore the promise of AI-augmented auditing, where collaboration between human auditors and foundation models can enhance financial integrity. Financial auditors play a pivotal role in identifying unusual transactions and anomalies that may indicate errors, fraud, or tax evasion within a company's financial records. Detecting these irregularities is crucial for maintaining trust in financial statements. However, auditing large volumes of accounting data poses significant challenges. Current manual and rule-based methods are often inefficient and error-prone, increasing the risk of overlooking critical warning signs. Standard JETs, which rely on predefined rules and established patterns, tend to flag an excessive number of transactions. While these tests primarily detect anticipated issues, they inundate auditors with alerts, many of which are false positives requiring extensive review. In practice, auditors still rely heavily on sampling and manual procedures, which are ill-equipped to uncover subtle irregularities in today’s vast and complex datasets. This growing gap between the complexity of financial data and the limitations of traditional auditing techniques highlights the urgent need for more intelligent, automated anomaly detection tools in the auditing process.

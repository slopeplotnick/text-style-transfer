Auditors rely on Journal Entry Tests (JETs) to detect anomalies in tax-related ledger records; however, these rule-based methods often produce overwhelming false positives and struggle to identify subtle irregularities. This study explores whether large language models (LLMs) can serve as effective anomaly detectors in the context of double-entry bookkeeping. By benchmarking state-of-the-art LLMs, such as LLaMA and Gemma, on both synthetic and real-world anonymized ledgers, we compare their performance against JETs and traditional machine learning approaches. Our findings reveal that LLMs consistently outperform rule-based JETs and classical ML methods, while also offering natural-language explanations that enhance interpretability. These results underscore the transformative potential of AI-augmented auditing, where human auditors collaborate with foundation models to reinforce financial integrity. Financial auditors play a crucial role in identifying unusual transactions and anomalies that may indicate errors, fraud, or tax evasion within a company's financial records. Detecting such irregularities is essential for maintaining trust in financial reporting. Nevertheless, auditing large and complex accounting datasets remains a formidable challenge, as current manual and rule-based methods are often inefficient and prone to errors, increasing the risk of overlooking critical indicators. Standard Journal Entry Tests (JETs), which rely on predefined rules and established patterns, frequently flag excessive transactions. While these tests are effective at identifying expected anomalies, they inundate auditors with alerts, many of which are false positives requiring extensive verification. Consequently, auditors often depend on sampling and manual procedures, which can fail to detect subtle discrepancies in the vast and intricate datasets typical of modern auditing environments. This growing divide between the complexity of financial data and the limitations of traditional audit tools highlights the urgent need for more intelligent, automated anomaly detection solutions in auditing.

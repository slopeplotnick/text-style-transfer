Our methodology employed a label-driven CoT generation combined with hardcase mining. By integrating label-constrained CoT synthesis with difficult sample excavation, we enhanced the model's reasoning capabilities through structured multi-step inference patterns and challenging edge cases. To evaluate performance, we implemented hardcase-focused protocols specifically designed to assess the model's ability to handle complex semantic scenarios and intricate reasoning tasks. This phase of innovation enabled the successful online deployment of the CoT-based multi-dimensional knowledge system, which was effectively distilled and adapted to smaller-scale models. Consequently, this achievement marked a significant milestone in scaling deep reasoning capabilities to production environments, resulting in substantial performance improvements in online A/B testing scenarios. However, two primary challenges emerged: first, the high dependence on visual information in product attribution understanding, where user-uploaded images often contain insufficient textual details; and second, ensuring that decision-making processes strictly conform to formats encompassing numerous fine-grained rules and detailed business standards aligned with observable attributes. To address these complexities, we pursued a comprehensive technical strategy that integrated multimodal understanding capabilities with rule-aware reasoning mechanisms, thereby enabling the model to process visual information while maintaining strict adherence to business constraints. Our approach employed a hybrid paradigm of CoT Generation, SFT, and Reinforcement Learning, utilizing the Qwen-2.5 LLM for textual reasoning and the Qwen-2.5 VL for multimodal comprehension. This dual-model architecture facilitated the seamless integration of visual and textual reasoning pathways, overcoming the limitations inherent in single-modality data processing. Furthermore, our methodology leveraged multimodal and rule-aware CoT generation, combining image-text paired understanding with standard-oriented prompt engineering. Initially, business rules and exemplar cases were embedded into CoT reasoning chains to establish rule comprehension logic. Subsequently, reinforcement learning was introduced to significantly enhance the model's proficiency in applying active rules to decision-making processes within complex situational contexts.

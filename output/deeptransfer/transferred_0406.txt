Despite the evolution of optimization approaches, traditional methods for construction coordination exhibit fundamental limitations. The necessity for comprehensive problem specification at the outset conflicts with the dynamic nature of construction projects, where task requirements, environmental conditions, and resource availability continuously change throughout project execution. Furthermore, the computational complexity of these methods often becomes prohibitive for large-scale projects involving numerous robots and intricate task dependencies. These persistent challenges have prompted researchers to explore learning-based methodologies capable of adapting to dynamic environments without requiring exhaustive planning. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a transformative approach to multi-robot task coordination, enabling robots to derive optimal coordination strategies through environmental interaction without needing complete problem specification. RL frameworks empower robots to acquire coordination proficiency through experiential learning, thereby adapting to environmental fluctuations and evolving task demands. However, RL requires extensive practice, posing substantial risks within construction environments due to potential errors that may jeopardize worker safety or result in equipment damage. Moreover, the duration required for training RL models is incompatible with the stringent timelines characteristic of construction projects. Consequently, these safety and efficiency concerns have led to the development of RL methodologies tailored specifically for the construction industry. In 2022, Lee et al. developed a digital twin-driven Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) methodology for adaptive task allocation in robotic construction. This methodology integrated Building Information Modeling (BIM) with DRL to facilitate context-aware coordination decisions, considering spatial constraints, temporal dependencies, and safety requirements. The digital twin framework enabled safer policy learning within a simulated environment before real-world implementation. Nevertheless, RL continues to encounter challenges, as even well-trained simulations may fail to perform accurately in real-world scenarios, potentially leading to safety concerns. Moreover, the application of learned strategies from simulations to actual construction sites with varying environmental conditions remains problematic. These challenges have prompted the development of more sophisticated RL methodologies.

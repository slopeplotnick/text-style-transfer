Curriculum Learning. Curriculum learning (CL), first introduced by Bengio et al. (2009b), has traditionally followed a data-centric paradigm and has been widely adopted in the development of large language models (LLMs) (Parashar et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2025a). Recent approaches, such as Speed-RL (Zhang et al. 2025) and the framework proposed by Kim and Lee (2024), continue to employ data-centric strategies that organize tasks from simple to complex based on externally defined difficulty criteria. Similarly, LBS3 (Luo et al. 2024) adopts a progressive training approach, beginning with simpler proxy queries and transitioning to more complex ones. However, these methods fundamentally misalign with the principles of effective curriculum design by relying on static, externally defined difficulty metrics rather than dynamically adapting to the model's evolving competence. Truly effective curriculum learning must be competence-aware, adjusting dynamically to the model's developmental trajectory. CAPO (Curriculum Advantage Policy Optimization) addresses this limitation by leveraging advantage estimates as an intrinsic, competence-aware signal, avoiding the destabilization often caused by the premature mixing of positive and negative feedback in reinforcement learning. CAPO employs a staged curriculum: it first uses positive advantages to establish stable behavioral priors and later introduces negative advantages to enhance generalization. Unlike traditional curriculum learning methods that depend on static difficulty measures, CAPO dynamically adapts to the policy's evolving capabilities, unifying the stability of early reinforcement learning with the flexibility of adaptive curriculum scheduling. This paper introduces CAPO as a novel mechanism that overcomes key challenges in reasoning model training by using advantage as an intrinsic signal for adaptive curriculum construction. CAPO's two-phase strategy progresses from imitation learning with positive-only samples to discrimination learning that incorporates negative signals, mirroring human cognitive development while mitigating the instability associated with mixed-signal optimization. Extensive experiments demonstrate CAPO's consistent improvements over strong baselines like GRPO across diverse benchmarks and model scales. Notably, CAPO achieves exceptional cross-domain generalization, excelling in tasks ranging from mathematical reasoning to multimodal reasoning. By aligning training with the model's evolving capabilities rather than relying solely on external metrics, CAPO paves the way for more adaptive and cognitively inspired learning algorithms.

Empirical evidence suggests that the entrenchment of individual beliefs is at the core of significant social epistemic challenges, such as polarization and the spread of misinformation. These issues often arise from inherent cognitive biases and errors in human reasoning processes. Large language models (LLMs), trained on vast corpora of human-generated text, are specifically designed to augment human reasoning by answering queries and assisting with various tasks. Following their initial success in executing complex cognitive tasks, these models have led to the development of novel reasoning methodologies. Initial approaches, like Chain-of-Thought, have evolved into more advanced techniques incorporating reinforcement learning. These strategies aim to enhance the models' capacity for truth-seeking by employing structured arguments, seeking corroborative evidence, and exploring diverse methodologies. However, there are instances where these models deviate from truth-seeking due to belief entrenchment—a systematic tendency to favor prior opinions over new evidence. Such phenomena can degrade the accuracy of conclusions and foster unwarranted confidence among users. While recent studies indicate the presence of belief entrenchment within these models, rigorously demonstrating this remains challenging. It is difficult to determine whether a belief update is justified or merely a manifestation of bias. Consequently, previous research has predominantly employed specific tasks that facilitate the identification of belief entrenchment. However, these methodologies fall short in effectively detecting reasoning errors in contexts where the truth is ambiguous or indeterminate.

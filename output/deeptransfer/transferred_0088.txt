Figure 1 illustrates the operational mechanism of the fine-tuned model. In this process, the model first extracts key attributes from the query and item descriptions, such as product names, brands, model specifications, and target audiences. Then, it evaluates the relevance by assessing the compatibility of these attributes between the query and the item. Through CoT tuning, the model not only improves the accuracy of relevance predictions but also provides clear rationales for its outputs. However, upon analyzing the outputs of the fine-tuned model, it becomes evident that it exhibits a bias toward overly strict relevance assessments. Specifically, the model frequently classifies products as "Irrelevant" when certain attributes mentioned in the query are missing from the product description, even if these attributes are non-essential and do not significantly influence purchasing decisions. This over-strictness results in a higher rate of false negatives, which in turn reduces the model's recall rate and negatively impacts the platform's transaction activity and revenue generation. To address this issue and better align the model's predictions with actual user behavior and feedback, we leverage user purchasing data for alignment. Specifically, the fine-tuned model is used to predict query-item pairs from historical purchase logs, and instances classified as "Irrelevant" are selected to form a preference training set. These overly strict predictions are labeled as "losers," while GPT-40 is employed to generate "Relevant" CoT reasoning outputs for the original query-item pairs, which are treated as "winners." Finally, the KTO algorithm is applied to align the model with these preferences. Unlike other alignment methods, the KTO algorithm directly utilizes binary-labeled data, making the alignment process simpler and more efficient.

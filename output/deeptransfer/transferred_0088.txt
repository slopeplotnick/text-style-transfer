The CoT output of the fine-tuned model is illustrated in Figure 1. In this output, the initial step involves extracting key attributes from the query and item descriptions, including product names, brands, model specifications, target audiences, and other relevant details. Next, the relevance between the query and item is assessed by evaluating the compatibility of these attributes. Through CoT tuning, the model not only accurately predicts relevance labels but also provides clear justifications for these classifications. However, upon analyzing the outputs of the fine-tuned model, it becomes evident that the model exhibits a bias towards stricter relevance discrimination. Specifically, it frequently categorizes products as "Irrelevant" when certain attributes mentioned in the query are absent from the product description, even if those attributes are non-essential and do not significantly influence purchasing decisions. This tendency towards excessive strictness increases the occurrence of false negatives, thereby reducing the model's recall rate and adversely affecting the platform's transactional activity and overall revenue. To address this limitation and ensure that the model's predictions better align with actual user behavior and preferences, we leverage historical purchase data to refine the model. Specifically, we use the fine-tuned model to predict query-purchased item pairs and identify those classified as "Irrelevant" to construct a preference training dataset. These overly strict predictions are treated as less favorable outcomes, while GPT-40 is employed to generate "Relevant" reasoning results for the original query-purchased item pairs, which are considered more favorable outcomes. Subsequently, the KTO algorithm is applied to align the model with these preferences. Unlike other alignment methodologies, the KTO algorithm directly utilizes binary labeled data for model alignment, thereby simplifying the data construction process and enabling a more efficient setup.

Exemplar Selection: Certain prompt optimization algorithms, such as OPRO and EvoPrompt, do not incorporate few-shot examples into their design. However, they have demonstrated the capacity to enhance LLM performance, even when employing straightforward exemplar selection strategies. promptsolution introduces a supplementary module that facilitates post-hoc exemplar selection, utilizing random selection and random search methodologies to integrate few-shot examples into a predefined instruction. Initial Prompt Creation: Many optimization frameworks necessitate an initial set of prompts to serve as a starting point for their operations. Our framework offers functionalities for the automatic generation of prompts derived from a task description, a foundational prompt, or dataset samples. Callbacks: The optimizer base class is equipped with callbacks, facilitating the monitoring of optimization progress. These callbacks enable the assessment of the optimizer's state at each stage and permit the termination of the process if deemed necessary. Illustrative examples include the TokenCountCallback, which monitors token utilization and ceases operations upon surpassing a predetermined threshold, and the FileOutputCallback, which archives prompts alongside their corresponding evaluation scores for subsequent analysis. Setup: To assess the efficacy of promptolution in relation to alternative prompt optimization methodologies, we conducted prompt optimization experiments utilizing the GSM8K dataset, which focuses on mathematical problem-solving, and the SST-5 dataset, dedicated to sentiment classification. We employed the gemma-3-27B model as the primary language model, and, where necessary, also as the meta-model for optimizers requiring such a component. Details regarding the datasets and experimental configurations are provided in Appendix A.2. For comparative analysis, we evaluated the optimizers CAPO, EvoPromptGA, and OPRO from the promptolution library, in addition to the leading optimizers from AdalFlow (LLM-AutoDiff) and DSPy (GEPA). To examine the efficacy of prompt optimization, we further assessed three manually constructed prompts per dataset and reported their average performance. The parameters for each optimizer were maintained consistently to ensure fairness, and the token budget was restricted to a maximum of one million tokens for both input and output, thereby maintaining costs below $0.15 for this language model. All frameworks were initialized solely with a task description, devoid of any initial prompts, to evaluate their efficacy in a fully automated workflow. Both the unoptimized prompts and the optimal prompts identified by each optimizer were assessed on an independent test set.

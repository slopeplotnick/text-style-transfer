Prompt design is a crucial factor in shaping the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs), yet the role of prompt specificity—whether a prompt is highly detailed or more general—remains underexplored. This paper introduces DETAIL, a comprehensive framework for assessing LLM performance across varying levels of prompt specificity. Using GPT-4, we generate prompts with different degrees of detail, measure specificity through perplexity, and evaluate response accuracy via GPT-based semantic equivalence. Experiments conducted on 30 novel reasoning tasks using GPT-4 and O3-mini demonstrate that greater prompt specificity generally enhances accuracy, particularly for smaller models and tasks requiring procedural reasoning. These findings emphasize the importance of adaptive prompting strategies and provide tools and datasets to support further research in this area. LLMs have revolutionized numerous domains with their exceptional generative capabilities, achieving state-of-the-art performance across diverse tasks. Recent evaluations have increasingly focused on their ability to tackle complex mathematical and multi-step reasoning problems. Enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs remains a critical area of research, with efforts centered on optimizing prompt design. Recent advancements, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree of Thoughts (ToT), and Graph of Thoughts (GoT), have introduced structured methodologies that break down complex problems into smaller, manageable steps to improve reasoning. However, a persistent challenge lies in determining the optimal level of prompt specificity to balance accuracy and flexibility. While detailed prompts offer explicit guidance, they may inadvertently constrain the model’s reasoning processes. Conversely, less specific prompts allow for greater flexibility but risk introducing ambiguity and misinterpretation.

Prompt design plays a critical role in the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs); however, the impact of prompt specificity—how detailed or vague a prompt is—remains understudied. This paper introduces DETAIL, a framework for evaluating LLM performance across varying levels of prompt specificity. We generate multi-level prompts using GPT-4, quantify specificity via perplexity, and assess correctness using GPT-based semantic equivalence. Experiments on 30 novel reasoning tasks across GPT-4 and O3-mini reveal that specificity improves accuracy, especially for smaller models and procedural tasks. Therefore, our results highlight the need for adaptive prompting strategies and provide tools and data to support further research. Large language models (LLMs) have drastically changed the world with their excellent generative capabilities and continue to advance at an unprecedented pace. Moreover, the NLP revolution is not limited to simple text tasks but also surpasses many state-of-the-art methods across various domains. Recent evaluations have focused on assessing the efficacy of models' reasoning in mathematical and multi-step problems. Enhancing LLM reasoning is vital for their ongoing development, and recent advancements have introduced various approaches to improve LLM reasoning via prompting. Recent advancements have explored sophisticated prompting strategies, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree of Thoughts (ToT), and Graph of Thoughts (GoT), which structure the model's generation process to enhance reasoning. These methods often involve decomposing problems into intermediate steps. Nonetheless, a significant challenge that still persists is determining the optimal level of prompt specificity to generate accurate and coherent responses. Although detailed prompts can provide clear guidance, they may also constrain the model's reasoning pathways. In contrast, vague prompts offer flexibility but risk ambiguity and misinterpretation.

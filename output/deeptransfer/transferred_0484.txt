We evaluated several methodologies, including RL-Trained, Mixture-of-Inputs (MoI), CoLaR, and LiteReason, to assess their performance. Using Qwen 2.5 7B-Instruct, we generated chapters based on the plans produced by each method and collected 20 pairwise comparisons for every method combination. Model performance was then compared using Bradley-Terry relative strengths fitted to these comparisons, with further details provided in Appendix E. Initially, we conducted ablations on the Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks, with results summarized in Table 4. We also benchmarked LiteReason variants against two baselines built upon the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model: a default prompted model and its RL-trained version. Notably, neither baseline incorporated latent reasoning capabilities. Interestingly, LiteReason without Reasoning Prompts (RP), which involves skipping intermediate reasoning steps, increased reasoning length but performed worse than the RL-trained baseline. Table 7 provides additional comparisons of models with and without implicit-thought prompts. Furthermore, we explored latent thought usage throughout RL training to determine whether RL influenced its prevalence. We observed an initial increase in latent-thought usage at the beginning of RL training, followed by a decline as training progressed. Future work could explore inducing latent reasoning by modifying prompts or increasing the likelihood of implicit-token markers. Overall, LiteReason achieved only marginal performance improvements (0.8% on Flawed Fictions and 58% on NCP), likely due to its methodological similarity to the RL-trained baseline, while better exploring reasoning paths that the base model typically would not generate. Meanwhile, the training-free methods, MoI and Soft Thinking, performed similarly to the untrained model in both performance and token usage. On the NCP task, Soft Thinking slightly outperformed the baseline but generated more tokens, whereas MoI underperformed while producing fewer tokens. On the Flawed Fictions task, the performance differences relative to the baseline were minimal and fell within confidence intervals. Future research is needed to enhance the consistency and effectiveness of training-free methods across diverse tasks and reasoning paradigms.

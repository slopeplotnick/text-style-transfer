In this section, our objective is to elucidate the fundamental elements that contribute to the efficacy of e-commerce search and to delineate the essential tools required for its enhancement. Essentially, e-commerce search involves aligning user queries with relevant products. This alignment is optimized by evaluating the congruence between the search query and the product, taking into account both explicit attributes and constraints, as well as subjective user preferences. The analysis of user search intent is systematically categorized into 18 distinct dimensions, including type, brand, and style, among others. For each search query and corresponding product match, the degree of alignment between the product attributes and the search criteria is meticulously evaluated. These evaluations are instrumental in determining the relevance of the match, thereby facilitating a comprehensive understanding and analysis of the criteria employed in relevance determination. To assess relevance in e-commerce search, it is essential to consider three critical components: product comprehension, search query interpretation, and relevance evaluation. In the process of evaluating relevance, two primary aspects of the product are scrutinized: its attributes and its presentation. Product attributes can be categorized as either inherent, which are readily apparent and easily identifiable, or derived, which necessitate deeper analysis and understanding. Inherent attributes, such as category, brand, model, and price, are typically straightforward and readily align with search queries. In contrast, derived attributes, which stem from inherent ones, pertain to the functional characteristics or experiential qualities of a product. These attributes are not always immediately apparent and necessitate a deeper level of understanding and reasoning. This complexity is further compounded by the distinct terminologies and descriptive conventions employed across various industries.

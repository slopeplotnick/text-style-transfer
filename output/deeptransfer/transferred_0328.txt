In 2024, we decompose the content into distinct functional and semantic sections, subsequently summarizing it in various languages to facilitate interactive question-answer interactions. To support these tasks, we utilize the transcribed multimodal speech translation (ST) output as contextual support. Although contemporary large language models (LLMs) are adept at managing extensive contexts, they are constrained by certain limitations. Therefore, we implement a strategy wherein lectures are translated into multiple languages and segmented into chapters to avert context overflow. This segmentation ensures that summaries remain coherent and focused, as each chapter adheres to a singular thematic topic. For each chapter, we generate several forms of compressed representations, including transcript compressions at varying levels (50%, 70%, and 90%) and summaries whose lengths are proportionate to the original section. Initially, English summaries are created, leveraging the superior performance of LLMs on English text, and subsequently translated into the target languages. A similar methodology is employed for question-answering tasks: English transcripts, segmented by chapters, are utilized in conjunction with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to query an LLM, and the resulting responses are translated into the target languages. Another challenge involves translating slides into various languages. Slides comprise both editable text and images containing embedded text. For the extraction of editable text, we employ a Python-based PowerPoint parser to extract text blocks, which are then translated using conventional unimodal machine translation (MT) methods, as this approach is preferred over multimodal models due to their high computational cost. However, text embedded within images presents challenges in extraction due to the absence of surrounding linguistic context and the reliance on visual elements for interpretation, necessitating multimodal translation. Upon translation, it is imperative to reintegrate the text into the original image to maintain the layout and visual significance. To address this, we propose an image-translation pipeline designed to detect, recognize, translate, and reintegrate text within slide images.

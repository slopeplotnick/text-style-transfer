The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has demonstrated remarkable potential in complex text-generation tasks such as academic writing, literature reviews, and scientific reports. LLM-based systems for survey creation are transforming how individuals extract information from extensive datasets by integrating search, organization, and synthesis into streamlined processes. Both researchers and corporations have developed various specialized systems for survey generation. These automated writing systems can be broadly categorized into three primary types: general long-text writing systems (e.g., Kimi, GLM) that provide broader capabilities for extended text generation; paper-writing systems (e.g., Chengpian, Doubao Paper Mode) that focus on structured composition of complete research papers; and survey-writing agents (e.g., SurveyGo, SurveyX, Panshi ScienceOne) specifically designed for academic survey generation. These systems not only process extensive volumes of literature and extract critical information but also generate well-structured survey drafts complete with organized sections, citation annotations, and logical coherence. However, despite significant advancements in the development of these systems, evaluating their effectiveness remains a substantial challenge. Current evaluation methods predominantly rely on subjective assessments, which often yield inconsistent and non-reproducible outcomes. This lack of standardization poses significant challenges in comparing systems, identifying effective components, and driving iterative improvements. Therefore, establishing a standardized evaluation framework is imperative to ensure the quality and reliability of these systems while providing researchers with a consistent basis for comparative analysis and systematic investigation. In response to this need, we developed SurveyEval, a comprehensive tool designed to assess surveys generated by these systems across three key dimensions: overall quality, outline coherence, and reference accuracy. Our evaluation spans seven academic disciplines, and we have refined the LLM-as-a-Judge framework by incorporating human-authored surveys to enhance the alignment between automated evaluations and human judgments.

Conversely, the daughter language model has been specifically developed as part of the MOD-OMA. Similar to Delilah, the daughter agent functions as both a parser and generator. However, unlike the adult language model, its grammatical representations are initially underspecified and become more specific through language acquisition procedures. A notable similarity between the adult and child systems is that both employ graph structures for representing grammatical knowledge. Their parsers and generators execute their respective grammars by unifying lexical structures to produce well-formed utterances, provide grammaticality judgments, and generate parses based on the currently specified grammar. Crucially, the parser and generator of the child agent can operate with an incomplete grammar, akin to how humans acquire their first languages. The daughter language model is required to engage in conversation with the mother agent before acquisition is complete. The daughter agent comprises three modules: (1) a parser and generator, known as the automaton, (2) a grammar that becomes specified through acquisition, and (3) a language acquisition device. Importantly, the parser and generator remain unchanged by the language acquisition processes. The outcome of language acquisition is explicit grammatical knowledge, represented by newly acquired lexical entries and grammatical properties that limit their combinatory possibilities. These data structures offer comprehensive representations of words and constructions, specified for several grammatical properties, such as phonological form, semantic content, and an expandable list of other acquired grammatical properties. Grammatical properties are formalized by feature-value pairs like [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: content word] and [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: function word], which are the focus of this study. Since the learning procedures are unsupervised, the daughter language model lacks information on the labels used by the mother language model or any grammatical descriptions. Consequently, the acquired grammatical properties and semantic content are denoted by unique alpha-numeric labels, such as property type 'A' and property value 'b' in Figure 2, as well as in Figure 3. These representations also encode other grammatical properties not explored in this paper. For example, the terminal feature differentiates between terminal words and more abstract constructions, while the semiform index facilitates the representation of anaphoric elements like reflexives. Additionally, technical features, including the session identifier and lexical entry number, uniquely identify each rule. As a result of simulating language acquisition, the grammar can consist of increasingly complex structures, as both the HEAD and ARGUMENT can incorporate data structures analogous to lexical items.

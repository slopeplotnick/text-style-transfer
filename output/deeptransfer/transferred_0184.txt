To examine how system prompts influence reasoning behavior, we analyze reasoning distributions before and after prompt optimization. As shown in Figure 9 and detailed in Table 10, there is a notable reduction in the overall frequency of reasoning components following optimization. This reduction occurs because optimized prompts are generally shorter and more focused, omitting redundant instructions such as "answer like Shakespeare," which tend to lengthen and diversify reasoning chains. Consequently, the proportion of the "Others" category decreases significantly, indicating that optimized prompts lead to more structured and interpretable model behavior. Moreover, the frequency of reasoning components such as Logical reasoning and Reframing changes substantially, highlighting the sensitivity of these behaviors to prompt formulation. Notably, the effects of optimization are highly task-specific. For mathematical problem-solving tasks, optimized prompts result in a clear increase in both Calculation and Logical reasoning components, effectively addressing the cognitive demands of the domain. However, in other task categories, these prompts fail to elicit comparable reasoning elements. For example, in moral reasoning tasks, there is a sharp decline in the absolute frequency of most reasoning components, although Logical reasoning remains the predominant behavior in relative terms. Additionally, certain reasoning components, such as Backtracking and Backward chaining, remain consistently rare across all scenarios, likely due to the absence of RL-style reflective capabilities in these models.

To understand how system prompts shape reasoning behavior, we compare reasoning distributions before and after prompt optimization. As shown in Figure 9, with detailed data in Table 10, there is a notable reduction in the overall frequency of reasoning components following prompt optimization. This is primarily because optimized system prompts are typically shorter and cleaner, containing fewer redundant instructions such as "answer like Shakespeare," which often inflate the length and diversity of the reasoning chain. Consequently, the proportion of the "Others" category drops substantially, indicating that optimized prompts lead to more structured and interpretable model behavior. Moreover, the frequency of other reasoning components such as Logical reasoning and Reframing also changes dramatically, suggesting that these behaviors are highly sensitive to prompt formulation. Furthermore, the effect of optimization is highly task-dependent. For mathematical problem-solving tasks, optimized prompts lead to a clear increase in both Calculation and Logical reasoning, aligning well with the domain's demands. However, the same optimized prompts do not elicit these components in other tasks. Specifically, in the context of moral reasoning tasks, there is a sharp decline in the absolute frequency of nearly all reasoning components, yet Logical reasoning remains the dominant behavior in relative terms. Finally, some components remain consistently rare across all settings. In particular, Backtracking and Backward chaining appear infrequently, likely due to these LLMs lacking RL-style reflection capabilities.

Also, we found that the model Menta demonstrates superior efficacy in detecting depression and stress compared to identifying suicide risk. Both fine-tuned and standard language models, including larger variants, exhibit enhanced performance in tasks related to depression and stress detection relative to suicide risk classification. For instance, a systematic review by Bauer (2024) highlights that language models frequently misclassify subtle indicators of suicidality due to class imbalance and their dependence on contextual or temporal cues, which are often absent in isolated posts. Our findings align with another study by Lamichhane (2023), which reported F1-scores of 0.86 for depression detection and 0.37 for suicidality detection using social media data. These findings underscore that depression and stress are more readily identifiable due to their distinct linguistic patterns and the availability of larger datasets. In contrast, suicidality is comparatively rare, contextually dependent, and characterized by more subtle linguistic cues, posing challenges for reliable model learning. Consequently, language models may demonstrate greater efficacy in broader screening tasks, such as identifying depression or stress, but exhibit limitations in the nuanced classification of suicide risk without the incorporation of multimodal or longitudinal data. Despite demonstrating that models such as Qwen-3 (4B) can effectively predict mental health issues, several limitations constrain the generalizability and interpretability of our findings. Our analysis was confined to textual social media posts, thereby excluding other critical elements such as temporal variations in writing style, user profile information, and online interaction patterns, which could provide a more comprehensive understanding of mental health. Incorporating these elements could enhance the robustness and ecological validity of future research endeavors. Additionally, the scope of our study was limited to depression, stress, and suicidal ideation, which represent only a subset of the broader spectrum of mental health disorders. Comprehensive mental health issues encompass conditions such as anxiety, bipolar disorder, and affective disturbances, which were not addressed in our investigation. Furthermore, we utilized publicly available, anonymized social media data rather than data derived from clinical environments. This approach ensures privacy and facilitates reproducibility; however, it constrains our ability to draw conclusions regarding clinical safety, ethical compliance, and longitudinal reliability within real healthcare contexts.

The relevance module is crucial for user experience, as displaying irrelevant products can harm trust and revenue for e-commerce systems. Consequently, relevance modeling, which evaluates the match between search queries and products, is a fundamental component of e-commerce engines. Traditional relevance models, including term matching and vector matching methods, have been widely used in online retail platforms to ensure effective search functionality. Term matching methodologies, such as TF-IDF and BM25, assess the degree of correspondence between queries and products but fail to capture semantic relevance and contextual understanding. Conversely, vector matching approaches leverage BERT and its variants to discern semantic relationships between queries and products, achieving remarkable improvements in relevance modeling. Despite these advancements, vector matching methods encounter two primary challenges in domain-specific relevance modeling tasks. Firstly, there is a lack of domain-specific hard samples. Most existing methods achieve satisfactory results with a moderate amount of manually annotated data; however, further performance improvements require a substantial number of challenging samples, which are difficult to procure and annotate accurately. Secondly, the models exhibit limitations in reasoning and representation capabilities due to their constrained parameter size. Consequently, lightweight online models struggle with complex reasoning and relevance discrimination. To enhance reasoning and comprehension capabilities, large language models are increasingly applied to relevance modeling tasks. Nevertheless, most prior approaches primarily utilize the representation of the last token for relevance classification and employ soft labels to distill knowledge into smaller models. This approach fails to intuitively analyze which features impact relevance decisions or enhance the reasoning capabilities of the smaller models.

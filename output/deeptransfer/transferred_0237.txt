The 48th International ACM SIGIR Conference on Research and Development in Information Retrieval (SIGIR '25) will take place from July 13 to 18, 2025, in Padua, Italy, organized by ACM, based in New York, NY, USA. The conference proceedings will be available online. E-commerce platforms, such as Amazon, Taobao, and JD, have significantly transformed consumer shopping behaviors. The relevance module is crucial for the user experience, as displaying irrelevant products can harm trust and revenue for e-commerce systems. Therefore, relevance modeling, which evaluates the match between search queries and products, is a fundamental aspect of e-commerce engines. Traditional relevance assessment methods, such as term-based matching and vector-based approaches, have been widely employed in online retail platforms to ensure an effective and reliable search experience. Term-based matching methods, like TF-IDF and BM25, are commonly used to evaluate the degree of correspondence between a query and a product. However, these approaches fail to capture semantic relevance and contextual nuances. In contrast, vector-based matching techniques leverage advanced models like BERT and its variants to comprehend the semantic relationships between queries and products, thereby achieving significant advancements in relevance modeling. Despite these improvements, vector-based methods encounter two primary challenges in domain-specific relevance modeling tasks. The first challenge is the scarcity of domain-specific hard samples. While most existing methods perform adequately with a limited amount of labeled data, achieving superior outcomes requires a substantial number of challenging examples, which are inherently difficult to identify and accurately annotate. The second challenge is the models' deficiencies in reasoning and result demonstration due to constraints imposed by their parameter size. Consequently, lightweight online models struggle with complex reasoning and discerning relevance. To address these limitations and enhance reasoning and comprehension capabilities, large language models are increasingly being employed in relevance modeling tasks. Nevertheless, the predominant methodology involves leveraging the representation of the final token for relevance determination and employing soft labels to instruct smaller models. This approach fails to effectively elucidate the features influencing relevance decisions and does not contribute to the enhancement of reasoning capabilities in the smaller models.

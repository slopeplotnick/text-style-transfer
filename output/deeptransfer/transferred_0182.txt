We evaluate LLMs on three benchmarks spanning diverse domains: MMLU-Pro, a knowledge-driven and reasoning-focused benchmark covering 14 academic subjects; MATH500, a set of 500 challenging mathematics problems; and UniMoral, a suite of moral-judgment questions requiring nuanced social reasoning. All experiments were conducted in five languages: English, Chinese, Spanish, French, and Hindi. These languages were selected to represent a diverse set of language families, geographic regions, and resource availability. A key consideration when addressing multilingual tasks is the choice of system prompt language: whether to use English, which is typically the primary training language for the model, or to align the prompt language with that of the task. To explore this, we compare two settings. In the English-prompt setting, English prompts are applied to questions in various languages, whereas in the same-language setting, the system prompt is translated into the same language as the question. To better understand the relationships among different aspects of model behavior, we examine the interplay among the four performance metrics in this section. Specifically, we investigate the impact of various system prompt components on model behavior by conducting a regression analysis that links prompt components and language settings to multilingual performance metrics. As illustrated in Figure 4, system prompt components exhibit diverse effects on model performance, which can be broadly categorized into three groups. Firstly, certain components consistently show positive effects on model behavior. For instance, Chain-of-Thought, emotion, and scenario prompts are positively associated with both accuracy and consistency, while simultaneously reducing accuracy disparities across languages. These findings highlight their effectiveness in guiding the model toward stable and high performance across multilingual contexts. Furthermore, closer inspection of the model outputs reveals that these components elicit more structured and consistent reasoning, which likely contributes to the observed performance improvements.

On-device AI presents a significant advancement in mental health monitoring by enabling personalized feedback in real time while preserving user privacy and reducing latency. By performing computations directly on smartphones, this approach eliminates the need to transmit sensitive data to cloud servers, thereby enhancing data security and minimizing network dependencies. Moreover, the accessibility of such on-device tools provides a practical solution for individuals in remote areas or those facing long wait times for clinical appointments, thus improving the availability and timeliness of mental health support. Additionally, the implementation of AI on mobile devices facilitates rapid feedback and real-time detection of linguistic or behavioral changes indicative of stress or mood disorders. The use of smartphones and other digital devices for mental health monitoring is gaining traction due to their enhanced speed, reduced energy consumption, and superior data privacy compared to cloud-dependent systems. Recent research indicates that Small Language Models (SLMs) perform effectively on devices like the Samsung Galaxy S24, particularly in applications involving document assistance and health prediction, by optimizing memory usage and operational speed. These models are advantageous for practical applications due to their straightforward implementation and minimal resource requirements, operating independently of continuous cloud connectivity and thereby enhancing privacy protection. However, while existing research has demonstrated the capability of SLMs to address specific mental health tasks, there remains substantial scope for further exploration in this domain. Most existing research has concentrated on relatively simple tasks, showing limited efficacy with more complex datasets. Furthermore, there has been insufficient exploration of the performance of these models on resource-constrained devices. To address these limitations, we propose Menta, a novel framework leveraging the Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) technique to enable efficient multitask learning and manage heterogeneous data distributions effectively. The system is optimized to perform efficiently across a range of mental health applications, maintaining computational efficiency and safeguarding user privacy on commonly used personal devices.

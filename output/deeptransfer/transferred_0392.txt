On-device AI represents a significant advancement in mental health monitoring by enabling personalized feedback in real-time while safeguarding user privacy, reducing latency, and enhancing accessibility. Conducting inference directly on the user's smartphone eliminates the need to transmit sensitive linguistic and behavioral data to cloud servers, thereby enhancing data security and reducing reliance on internet connectivity. Moreover, integrating these tools on mobile devices improves accessibility to mental health resources, particularly benefiting individuals in remote areas or those facing long wait times for clinical appointments. Because on-device models eliminate round-trip communication with remote servers, they facilitate rapid feedback and support real-time detection of changes indicative of stress or mood disorders. The utilization of mobile devices for mental health monitoring is gaining traction due to its advantages in speed, energy efficiency, and data privacy compared to cloud-dependent systems. Recent research indicates that small language models (SLMs) are effectively operational on devices such as the Samsung Galaxy S24, demonstrating capabilities in document assistance and health prediction while optimizing memory usage and processing speed. Furthermore, SLMs offer practical advantages for real-world applications due to their ease of deployment and minimal resource requirements. Operating independently of continuous cloud connectivity, these models inherently enhance privacy preservation. However, while existing research has established the efficacy of SLMs in addressing specific mental health tasks, significant gaps remain in their application across a broader spectrum of mental health domains. Most notably, prior efforts have primarily focused on straightforward tasks and have demonstrated limited efficacy when applied to more complex datasets. Additionally, there has been insufficient exploration of the performance of these models on devices with constrained resources. To address these challenges, we introduce Menta, an innovative framework that employs the LoRA methodology to manage multiple tasks and effectively handle heterogeneous data. This system is engineered to perform efficiently across a variety of mental health applications, maintaining efficiency and safeguarding privacy on commonly used devices.

Large language models (LLMs) continue to grow in size and complexity, thereby pushing memory bandwidth and computational resources to their limits during both training and deployment phases. Quantization addresses these challenges by encoding data in fewer bits and replacing costly floating-point operations with more efficient integer arithmetic. Two primary methodologies for quantization are prevalent in the literature. Post-training quantization (PTQ) is appealing for its simplicity and lack of retraining requirements. However, at extremely low bit widths (e.g., 1-2 bits), PTQ often suffers from significant accuracy degradation. This decline can be attributed to several factors, such as limited calibration data, the inadequacy of per-tensor or per-group scaling techniques to effectively realign codebooks, and the disproportionate influence of outlier channels on clipping ranges. In contrast, quantization-aware training (QAT) incorporates the quantization mechanism into the training process, allowing the model to adapt to the quantized representation. While QAT demonstrates superior performance compared to PTQ under conditions of extremely low bit precision, it is often characterized by significant time consumption and instability due to its constrained flexibility in managing data. In our study, we applied the FAIRY2 framework to widely-used open-source large language models, such as the LLaMA series, yielding promising results. Specifically, we developed a methodology to transform any layer into an alternative form without altering the model's functionality prior to quantization. We continued training the model at minimal bit levels utilizing a specialized quantizer while leveraging existing checkpoints. Furthermore, we mitigated errors by applying the same strategy to residual data, which substantially reduced error margins.

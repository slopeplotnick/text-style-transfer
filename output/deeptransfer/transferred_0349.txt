Large language models (LLMs) continue to grow in complexity and scale, pushing memory and computational demands to their limits during both training and deployment phases. Quantization seeks to address these challenges by encoding data in a reduced bit format and replacing costly floating-point operations with more efficient integer arithmetic. There are two primary methodologies for quantization discussed in the literature. Post-training quantization (PTQ) is favored for its simplicity and the absence of retraining requirements. However, at extremely low bit widths, such as 1 or 2 bits, it often suffers from a significant decline in accuracy. This degradation is often due to several factors, including the limited availability of calibration data, the inadequacy of per-tensor or per-group scaling to realign codebooks, and the dominance of outlier channels in determining clipping ranges. In contrast, quantization-aware training (QAT) incorporates the quantization process within the training loop, allowing the model to adapt to the quantized environment. While QAT demonstrates superior performance compared to PTQ under extremely low bit-width settings, it is often computationally expensive and prone to instability due to its limited capacity to accommodate layer-specific data distributions. In this work, we apply FAIRY2 to widely-used open-source LLMs, such as the LLaMA series, achieving promising results. Specifically, our approach introduces a mechanism to transform any layer into an alternative representation without altering the model’s pre-quantization functionality. Moreover, we enable continued training at ultra-low bit-widths by leveraging a specialized quantizer while utilizing pre-existing checkpoints. To further mitigate quantization errors, we apply the same methodology to residual data, significantly reducing error propagation and enhancing overall performance.

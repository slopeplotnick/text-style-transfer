Padding type significantly impacts performance: coherent text padding (whether relevant or non-relevant) outperforms random padding, which in turn surpasses multi-task padding. Moreover, the position of padding is critical, as placing context after the task description can degrade model performance more than positioning it before. LLM agents are artificial intelligence systems that embed pre-trained language models within a decision-making framework to autonomously execute complex, multi-step, and multi-turn tasks using external tools (Chowa et al. 2025; Ferrag, Tihanyi, and Debbah 2025; Wang et al. 2025). Recent advancements in position embeddings and attention mechanisms have significantly expanded context windows, increasing from 2,000 tokens in GPT-3 to 2 million tokens in Grok 4 Fast (Su et al. 2021; Dao et al. 2022). However, these models often exhibit recency bias, struggling to retain and utilize distant tokens effectively (Wang et al. 2024b; Shan et al. 2025). Benchmarks such as NIAH and LongBench (Bai et al. 2024; Kuratov et al. 2024) demonstrate that while expanded context windows enhance the volume of information processed, they do not inherently improve the models’ cognitive capabilities or efficiency in information utilization (Liu et al. 2025). Safety benchmarking for LLMs encompasses dimensions such as toxicity, misinformation, and vulnerabilities to jailbreaks (Shi et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2024a). Specifically, LongSafety (Lu et al. 2025) evaluates the ability of models to handle long-context inputs of up to 5,000 tokens. For agent safety, benchmarks focus on interactive tasks: AgentSecurity and Agent Dojo assess robustness against prompt injection attacks (Zhang et al. 2025; Debenedetti et al. 2024), while RedCode and MobileSafetyBench evaluate risks associated with code execution (Guo et al. 2024; Lee et al. 2024). Most notably, AgentHarm (Andriushchenko et al. 2025) serves as a benchmark for refusal behavior on harmful tasks and the ability to execute multi-step agentic workflows. We extend the AgentHarm benchmark to evaluate agentic capabilities and behaviors under conditions involving long-context inputs for both benign and harmful tasks. Furthermore, AgentHarm assesses agents’ proficiency in multi-step tool use. The benchmark incorporates manually implemented tools that simulate real-world applications (e.g., Google Search or a social media API), allowing testers full control over task execution.

Moreover, the type of padding significantly impacts performance: coherent text padding (non-relevant/relevant) outperforms random padding, which in turn surpasses multi-task padding. Additionally, the position of the padding plays a crucial role, as placing context after the task description can degrade model performance more than placing it before. LLM agents are advanced AI systems that embed pre-trained language models within iterative decision-making frameworks to autonomously execute complex, multi-step, and multi-turn tasks using external tools (Chowa et al. 2025; Ferrag, Tihanyi, and Debbah 2025; Wang et al. 2025). Context windows have expanded dramatically, growing from 2,000 tokens in GPT-3 to 2 million tokens in Grok 4 Fast, enabled by advancements in position embeddings and attention mechanisms (Su et al. 2021; Dao et al. 2022). However, these models often exhibit a strong recency bias, prioritizing recent information at the expense of retaining and utilizing earlier content (Wang et al. 2024b; Shan et al. 2025). Benchmarks such as NIAH and LongBench (Bai et al. 2024; Kuratov et al. 2024) reveal that while expanded context windows increase the volume of information processed, they do not inherently enhance reasoning capabilities or the effective use of additional input (Liu et al. 2025). Safety evaluations for LLMs address dimensions such as toxicity, misinformation, and system vulnerabilities (Shi et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2024a), with LongSafety (Lu et al. 2025) specifically assessing performance on extended inputs of up to 5,000 tokens. In the domain of agentic systems, safety benchmarks focus on interactive task scenarios: AgentSecurity and Agent Dojo evaluate robustness against prompt injection attacks (Zhang et al. 2025; Debenedetti et al. 2024), while RedCode and MobileSafetyBench assess risks related to code execution (Guo et al. 2024; Lee et al. 2024). Most notably, the AgentHarm benchmark (Andriushchenko et al. 2025) evaluates refusal behaviors in response to harmful tasks and the ability to execute multi-step agentic workflows. In this work, we extend AgentHarm to assess agentic behaviors and performance under long-context inputs for both benign and harmful tasks. Furthermore, AgentHarm evaluates the efficacy of tool utilization within multi-step workflows. The benchmark includes manually implemented tools that simulate real-world functionalities, such as Google Search or a social media posting API, allowing precise control over task execution.

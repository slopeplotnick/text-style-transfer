Our comprehensive evaluation confirms that spatial methods, such as covariance tangent-space projection and Common Spatial Patterns, continue to represent the most effective approaches for decoding EEG motor imagery under controlled experimental conditions. However, their performance markedly declines in the presence of substantial inter- and intra-subject variability. Through detailed subject-level analysis, we establish that no single configuration universally optimizes performance across all individuals. Rather, our findings highlight the efficacy of employing adaptive methodologies that are tailored to the unique neurophysiological patterns of individual users. Moreover, nonlinear features, including Hjorth parameters, Higuchi fractal dimension, and singular value decomposition entropy, exhibited notable utility for certain individuals. This underscores the importance of capturing richer neural dynamics to enhance the precision of personalized decoding. Furthermore, these results suggest that the phenomenon of "BCI illiteracy" may be more accurately attributed to discrepancies between decoding algorithms and user-specific neurophysiological characteristics, rather than intrinsic limitations on the part of the user. Therefore, future research should prioritize the exploration of sophisticated ensemble learning frameworks that integrate linear spatial filters with nonlinear dynamics, effectively harnessing their complementary strengths. Extending this benchmark to encompass broader paradigms such as SSVEP, P300, CVEP, and invasive intracortical recordings will further test the generalizability of our conclusions. Additionally, rigorous evaluation of state-of-the-art deep-learning architectures, including CNNs, LSTMs, and Transformers, alongside emerging quantum-inspired models such as quantum support-vector machines and hybrid quantum neural networks, represents an essential frontier. These quantum methodologies, characterized by their potential to mitigate the intrinsic noise and complexity of EEG signals, represent a promising avenue for advancing decoding accuracy. Equally critical is the enhancement of cross-subject generalization through transfer learning and domain adaptation techniques, which have demonstrated considerable efficacy in bolstering model robustness and scalability across heterogeneous user populations. Finally, the imperative to strengthen reproducibility remains paramount, necessitating the adoption of open-access benchmarking frameworks such as MOABB, which facilitate standardized and transparent evaluation of EEG decoding algorithms.

Initializing a character in a random state sampled from a motion dataset has been shown to significantly enhance exploration in motion imitation methods. However, this technique requires access to the motion dataset during training to sample initial states. To address this dependency, the SMP framework introduces a generative model to synthesize initial states for training, thereby eliminating the need for the original dataset. Specifically, when using SMP, initial states are generated by sampling from a diffusion model pretrained as a motion prior. Consequently, SMP serves dual purposes within our framework: it functions both as a reward mechanism and as an initial state distribution for training new policies. This generative state initialization (GSI) approach removes the necessity of retaining the original dataset after training the SMP, as it leverages the generative capabilities of diffusion models to produce diverse, high-quality initial states. Furthermore, our framework employs a pretrained diffusion model as a reusable and modular motion prior for imitation learning. This task-agnostic motion diffusion model evaluates the naturalness of the agent's movements within a reinforcement learning paradigm, enabling the training of control policies that perform a variety of tasks while maintaining fidelity to the motion patterns observed in the original dataset. In the following section, we discuss the critical design choices within the learning framework. A key consideration is the formulation of an effective motion representation, which is essential both for training the diffusion model to accurately capture the dataset distribution and for utilizing it as a motion prior in policy training. This representation must encapsulate sufficient information to reconstruct motion while remaining straightforward to extract from kinematic motion clips and simulator observations.

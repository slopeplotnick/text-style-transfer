Upon further examination, it was determined that individual attitudes toward AI exerted a more significant influence on the outcomes than the experimental design itself. When attitudes toward AI were incorporated as covariates in the analysis, the experimental condition ceased to predict participants' preferences for the music or the intensity of their responses. However, the influence of attitudes toward AI remained both significant and consistent. This aligns with prior research indicating that attitudes toward autonomous machines significantly influence their evaluation (Hong et al., 2022) and extends the insights of Shank et al. (2023) by suggesting that bias toward composers may be contingent upon contextual factors, such as the popularity of the song and its perceived human-like composition. Although certain personality traits and music-related factors were associated with participants' liking of the music and their emotional responses, these associations diminished substantially when attitudes toward AI were accounted for. Notable exceptions to this pattern were the traits of openness to experience and neuroticism, both of which have previously been identified as significant predictors of emotional responses to music, particularly in relation to emotions such as joy and vitality (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Juslin et al., 2011; Liljeström et al., 2013). These emotional dimensions were especially salient in the musical stimuli employed in the present study. Most notably, attitudes toward AI emerged as the sole listener characteristic significantly associated with both the liking of AI-generated music and the emotional responses it elicited. The proportion of variance in these outcomes explained by listener characteristics was comparable to that reported in prior studies (Gerstgrasser et al., 2023; Strauss et al., 2024). Therefore, these findings underscore the pivotal role of attitudes toward AI as a determinant in the evaluation of AI-generated music. In contrast to the findings of Ansani et al. (2024), our study did not observe a moderation effect of attitudes toward AI on the functioning of the experimental groups. This discrepancy may be attributed to the use of different attitude measures, our emphasis on listening to tracks rather than live performances, and the inclusion of three experimental conditions instead of two.

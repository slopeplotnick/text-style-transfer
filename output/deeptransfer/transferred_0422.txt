Online videos have become a dominant communication medium, and their widespread reach has enabled hateful content to spread rapidly [8]. Such content exacerbates discrimination and social division, and can even incite offline violence [34, 36]. Current methods [8, 19, 42, 44, 45] typically extract and fuse features from video frames, audio, and transcribed text; however, they lack effective multimodal semantic interaction. As illustrated in Figure 1, hateful video detection faces two key challenges: (1) nuanced contextual understanding—hateful videos often convey harmful intent through subtle cues distributed across time and modalities, such as the interplay between specific visuals and linguistic expressions [41, 43], or the implicit associations between visual focus and auditory content [32]; and (2) multimodal semantic integration—while existing methods [8, 19, 42, 44, 45] follow a standard pipeline of feature extraction and fusion, they fail to capture the intricate semantic interactions across modalities. Addressing these challenges requires advanced contextual reasoning capabilities, which conventional approaches struggle to provide. Recent methods aim to improve contextual understanding by leveraging visual-language models (VLMs) with reasoning mechanisms [17, 20]. As shown in Figure 1, these approaches incorporate direct reasoning or Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning [15] as additional inputs. However, direct reasoning often produces superficial descriptions that overlook hateful semantic associations, while CoT reasoning has been shown to generate explanations that misrepresent the model's reasoning process [4], raising concerns about reliability in sensitive detection tasks. Furthermore, recent research [41, 43] on hateful video datasets reveals that hate cues exhibit heterogeneous temporal patterns, appearing either as brief, localized segments or as dispersed signals across the entire video timeline. These signals inherently encapsulate complex semantic meanings that require integration with foundational multimodal features. Collectively, these challenges highlight a critical gap: existing systems lack both robust semantic reasoning and effective multimodal fusion mechanisms for the detection of hateful video content.

The editing framework is categorized into five principal domains, which further decompose into 16 distinct types of edits, encompassing a spectrum from fundamental text formatting to intricate editing workflows. Emphasizing depth over breadth, the methodology aligns with the approach of Maia Polo et al., prioritizing fewer but more comprehensive cases. These cases encompass tasks such as text replacement and resolving multifaceted editing challenges, necessitating the integration of both visual and textual elements for effective problem-solving. For instance, one case requires aligning images with corresponding captions across slides by leveraging both visual and textual information. Moreover, numerous cases span multiple taxonomy categories, resulting in a cumulative category count exceeding 100. PPTArena differentiates itself from existing benchmarks by emphasizing the complexity of edits across four critical dimensions: the depth of multi-step reasoning required, the interdependencies between slides, the extent of semantic comprehension involved, and the intricacy of long-term planning. In contrast to tools such as PPTC-R and T2US, which predominantly address rapid, template-based modifications, PPTArena is meticulously engineered to tackle more challenging and authentic scenarios, thereby elucidating system deficiencies. It integrates both human-authored and Python-generated decks, offers definitive solutions for deterministic assessment, ensures cross-platform operability, and incorporates accessibility constraints. An illustrative task involves aligning each image with the appropriate caption on a slide, arranging them in a visually balanced manner, updating slide titles, and standardizing image dimensions to 3.2 inches in width by 2.4 inches in height. These tasks are designed to evaluate systems on complex reasoning processes, including multi-step reasoning, cross-slide information integration, and anticipatory planning. Consequently, this framework is instrumental in identifying challenges that simpler tasks fail to expose, thereby serving as an effective means to assess advanced systems. The evaluation focuses on two primary aspects: the adherence to instructions and the quality of the visual outputs. Evaluations are conducted by expert judges utilizing a scoring scale ranging from 0 to 5, where a score of 0 indicates complete failure, and a score of 5 signifies perfection.

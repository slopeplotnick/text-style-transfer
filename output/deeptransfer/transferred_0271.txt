The taxonomy comprises five principal categories, further decomposing into 16 specific edit types that encompass tasks ranging from fundamental text formatting to intricate, multi-step editing workflows. Consistent with the methodology of Maia Polo et al. [32], the emphasis is placed on a smaller set of cases richer in complexity and detail, rather than an extensive collection of simplistic examples. These cases address scenarios such as text replacement and resolving challenges requiring coordinated edits, leveraging both textual and visual elements to ensure semantic and structural coherence. For instance, one case necessitates aligning images with captions across slides by employing both visual and textual cues. Numerous cases span multiple categories, resulting in a total category count exceeding 100. PPTArena distinguishes itself from other benchmarks by emphasizing the complexity of edits across four dimensions: the depth of multi-step reasoning required, the interdependencies among slides, the extent of semantic comprehension necessary, and the intricacy of long-term planning. In contrast, alternative tools such as PPTC-R and T2US predominantly address rapid, template-based modifications. However, PPTArena is meticulously crafted to engage with more challenging and authentic scenarios, thereby elucidating system limitations. It integrates both human-generated and Python-constructed decks, provides explicit solutions for deterministic evaluation, ensures cross-platform operability, and incorporates accessibility constraints. An illustrative task involves aligning each image with its corresponding caption on a slide, arranging them harmoniously, updating slide titles, and standardizing all images to dimensions of 3.2 inches in width by 2.4 inches in height. These tasks are designed to evaluate systems on complex reasoning processes, including multi-step reasoning, cross-slide information integration, and anticipatory planning. This framework is instrumental in identifying challenges that are not apparent in simpler tasks, thereby serving as an effective means to assess advanced systems. The evaluation focuses on two primary criteria: adherence to instructional directives and the quality of visual outputs. Evaluations are conducted by expert judges utilizing a scale ranging from 0 to 5, with 0 indicating failure and 5 denoting perfection.

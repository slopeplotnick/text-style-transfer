Ensuring consistent theme color roles across a deck, fixing layouts and formats across related slides, and translating slide content while preserving the integrity of charts and structure are critical tasks in PowerPoint editing. To our knowledge, PPTArena is the first benchmark to (i) conceptualize PowerPoint editing as a structured, causal program grounded in deck semantics, (ii) provide element-level ground truths, style targets, and error rubrics to clarify correctness, and (iii) employ both instruction-following and visual judges to enable diverse editing configurations while ensuring task coherence. Complementing this benchmark, we introduce PPTPilot, a structure-aware agent designed for precise and robust PowerPoint editing. PPTPilot translates each natural-language instruction into a sequence of semantic operations, selects between high-level APIs (e.g., python-pptx) and direct XML modifications, and validates the results against task-specific objectives. Two core design principles underpin its functionality: structure-aware planning, where the agent analyzes slide masters, placeholders, shape hierarchies, text, and visual data prior to editing, and deterministic execution, where XML-level patches and strict schemas provide precise control over fonts, theme color slots, positions, and master-level changes, while programmatic tools handle repetitive global tasks such as translation and style normalization. An iterative plan-edit-verify loop, supported by XML validation and visual checks, enhances its robustness for visually complex and long-horizon edits. We evaluate a wide range of baselines on PPTArena, including advanced proprietary PowerPoint agents (e.g., ChatGPT Agent [42] and MiniMax Agent [35]), extended visual-language model configurations, and ablated versions of PPTPilot. Despite detailed instructions and tool access, existing systems struggle to balance instruction fidelity with visual and layout quality on compound, multi-step tasks. They often fail to address cross-slide dependencies and master-level style adjustments, both of which are emphasized by our benchmark. In contrast, PPTPilot achieves significantly higher performance, surpassing advanced proprietary agents and state-of-the-art models by over 10 percentage points on compound, layout-sensitive, and cross-slide edits, while maintaining competitive results on simpler tasks. However, even PPTPilot and other evaluated systems face challenges on highly complex, visually dependent scenarios, highlighting both the difficulty of PPTArena and the substantial opportunities for future research in this domain.

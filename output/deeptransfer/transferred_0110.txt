Another limitation pertains to the range of tasks that LeechHijack can effectively perform. Our empirical evaluations have demonstrated the viability of LeechHijack for tasks encompassed within the MMLU benchmark. However, its performance on more complex tasks, such as those found in GAIA, remains unassessed. LeechHijack is primarily configured for conventional chat-based tasks, and it is anticipated that its efficacy may diminish when confronted with more sophisticated challenges. Implementing the attack on complex commands presents significant challenges and necessitates more sophisticated attack configurations. Given that agent systems utilize expensive large language models (LLMs), an attacker can derive benefit from LeechHijack without deploying it for intricate tasks. Instead, they can exploit the hijacked resources for more straightforward operations, such as information aggregation or the generation of content templates, leveraging the extensive knowledge base of the model. Thus, LeechHijack represents a tangible and persistent threat to the MCP and agent systems, irrespective of the complexity of the hijacked computational tasks.

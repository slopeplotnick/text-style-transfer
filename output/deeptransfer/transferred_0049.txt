We recruited 17 participants (P1-P17) through school posters and random street invitations. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB), and guardian consent was obtained for all underage participants. Moreover, all interview materials were securely stored, ensuring that no personally identifiable information was involved. Most participants were familiar with creating short stories, self-assessing their proficiency with an average score of 3.8 on a 5-point Likert scale. Additionally, all participants had prior experience in story writing, either manually or using office software. Participants were organized into groups of approximately four individuals, each accompanied by a staff member. The workshop lasted around two and a half hours. During the session, participants were encouraged to articulate their thought processes through a "think-aloud" method, which facilitated the observation and analysis of both their preliminary and final ideas, as well as the progression of their creative processes. Furthermore, follow-up interviews were conducted with four participants. By analyzing the workshop recordings and interview data, we identified and categorized four distinct modes of interaction observed during the story creation process. The workshop flow was structured as follows: 1) Introduction and Overview (15 minutes). Staff members provided a succinct overview of the workshop's objectives and framework, elucidating the application of the four foundational components using illustrative examples. Participants were reminded to continuously verbalize their decision-making processes. 2) Group Discussion and Story Framework Creation (60 minutes). Facilitators distributed cards to participants, and each group collaboratively constructed a narrative utilizing the four primary components, articulating their reflections and decisions throughout the process. Facilitators circulated among the groups, offering assistance as necessary. 3) Story Sharing and Feedback (45 minutes). Volunteers articulated their narratives and received formative feedback from peers and staff, with an emphasis on the creativity and structural coherence of their stories. 4) Informal Interviews with Volunteers (30 minutes). Finally, four selected volunteers (P1-P4) participated in informal interviews to delve into their creative experiences.

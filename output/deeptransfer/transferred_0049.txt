We recruited 17 participants (P1-P17) through school posters and random street invitations. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB), and informed consent was obtained from the guardians of all underage participants. All interview materials were securely stored, and no personally identifiable information was collected. Most participants were familiar with creating short stories, self-assessing their proficiency at an average score of 3.8 on a 5-point Likert scale. Moreover, all participants had prior experience in story writing, either manually or using office software. Participants were organized into groups of approximately four, each accompanied by a staff member. The workshop lasted around two and a half hours. During the session, participants were encouraged to articulate their thought processes aloud, facilitating the observation of their initial and concluding ideas, as well as the progression of their cognitive journey. Additionally, follow-up discussions were held with four participants to further elucidate their experiences. By analyzing the workshop recordings and interview data, we identified and categorized four distinct modes of interaction exhibited during the story creation process. The workshop flow is outlined as follows: First, a 15-minute introductory session provided a concise overview of the workshop's objectives and structure, including illustrative examples to demonstrate the application of the four foundational components. Participants were reminded to verbalize their decision-making processes throughout the session, employing a "think-aloud" approach to facilitate the documentation and analysis of their creative choices. Next, a 60-minute group discussion was conducted, during which facilitators distributed cards to participants. Each group collaboratively constructed a narrative utilizing the four principal components, articulating their reflections and decisions throughout the process. Facilitators circulated among the groups to provide assistance as necessary. This was followed by a 45-minute session where volunteers presented their narratives and received constructive feedback from peers and staff, with an emphasis on creativity and narrative coherence. Finally, 30-minute informal interviews were conducted with four selected volunteers to delve into their creative experiences.

In this section, we introduce MIGR, a framework designed to determine the modality to prioritize during the initial reasoning phase by leveraging MI. Through this approach, we aim to mitigate biases in the reasoning process and ensure that early reasoning remains grounded in the modality exhibiting the most salient emotional cues. The proposed method consists of two sequential stages. First, we present modality-aligned SFT, a mechanism that guides the model to initiate reasoning by prioritizing the modality identified by MI as emotion-dominant. Second, we propose Modality-Aware Reward Optimization, which reinforces modality prioritization and ensures emotionally grounded reasoning while enhancing stability during optimization. To achieve this, we employ two reward functions: the modality-aligned order reward, which incentivizes adherence to the appropriate input sequence, and the modality-grounded reasoning reward, which promotes reasoning aligned with emotional signals. The subsequent sections provide a detailed explanation of these procedures. Recent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of SFT in emotion understanding tasks, even when limited to a small quantity of high-quality training data, such as that provided by the EMER dataset. Motivated by these findings, we adopt a similar cold-start strategy to initiate our training. However, while EMER provides high-quality supervision, the SFT stage can be further refined by incorporating additional samples with particularly clear and consistent emotional cues. To enhance early-stage supervision, we augment our training set with supplementary emotion-consistent samples. These samples are identified by extracting Facial Action Units (FAUs) from video frames, as FAUs are strongly correlated with emotional expressions. We then evaluate whether the extracted FAU patterns align with the target emotion using a predefined FAU-emotion mapping table. Only samples whose FAU patterns precisely match the target emotion are retained. For these filtered samples, we construct reasoning-answer pairs using FAU-consistent emotional evidence to guide the reasoning process.

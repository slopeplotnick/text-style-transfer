We reassessed the robustness of pretrained VLA models and identified that performance degradation under visual perturbations predominantly stems from representation shifts within spatial modeling. To address this, we introduced a novel one-shot robustness adaptation framework, comprising Feature Token Modulation (FTM) and Feature Linear Adaptation (FLA), which effectively recalibrates visual representations with minimal parameter updates. Our proposed method demonstrates superior generalization to unseen camera viewpoints and visual perturbations, as evidenced by its performance on the Libero benchmark, thereby uncovering the latent robustness potential inherent in existing VLA models. VLA models aim to integrate visual perception, language comprehension, and action generation within a unified policy framework. By leveraging foundational vision-language models for robotic applications, recent advancements in VLAs, including RT-2 [3], Pi0 [2], PaLM-E [5], Octo [24], and OpenVLA [14], exhibit robust generalization across a diverse range of manipulation tasks. These systems utilize large-scale embodiment datasets [4, 13], encompassing heterogeneous visual inputs, natural language instructions, proprioceptive states, and noisy action sequences, to develop a shared action policy that facilitates versatile robotic behaviors. Nevertheless, despite these enhancements, empirical evidence indicates that even the most advanced VLAs exhibit fragility when confronted with distributional changes, such as novel camera perspectives, lighting variations, or scene noise perturbations, notwithstanding extensive training. Addressing this vulnerability is imperative for the effective deployment of these systems in real-world environments, which present complexities far exceeding those encountered in controlled Libero settings. Enhancing the robustness of VLAs to accommodate visual variability remains a significant challenge. Prior research has sought to enhance viewpoint invariance by increasing visual diversity through domain randomization, multi-view representations, or extensive datasets of visual perturbations. Although these methods have demonstrated effectiveness, their reliance on substantial data collection and annotation imposes significant limitations on scalability.

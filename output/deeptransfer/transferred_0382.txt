We reassess the robustness of pretrained VLA models and demonstrate that performance degradation under visual perturbations primarily stems from representation shifts within Spatial Modeling. To address this, we propose a unified one-shot robustness adaptation framework, comprising Feature Token Modulation (FTM) and Feature Linear Adaptation (FLA), which effectively recalibrates visual representations with minimal parameter updates. Our approach achieves state-of-the-art generalization to unseen camera viewpoints and visual perturbations on the Libero benchmark, thereby uncovering the latent robustness potential within existing VLA models. Vision-Language Architectures (VLAs) aim to integrate visual perception, language grounding, and action generation into a single policy framework. By extending pretrained vision-language foundations to the robotic domain, recent VLAs such as RT-2, Pi0, PaLM-E, Octo, and OpenVLA exhibit strong in-distribution generalization across diverse manipulation tasks. These systems leverage large-scale embodiment datasets that incorporate varied visual inputs, language instructions, proprioceptive states, and noisy actions to generate cohesive action policies that enable versatile robotic behaviors. Nevertheless, recent analyses reveal that state-of-the-art VLAs remain surprisingly vulnerable to distribution shifts, such as unseen camera viewpoints, novel lighting conditions, and scene noise perturbations. This brittleness persists even in models trained on millions of demonstrations, highlighting a critical limitation for real-world deployment where visual environments are inherently more complex than controlled Libero settings. Therefore, enhancing robustness to visual perturbations remains a significant challenge in advancing VLA performance. Prior research has explored data-centric strategies to improve viewpoint invariance, including domain randomization, multi-view demonstrations, and large-scale visual perturbation datasets. While effective, these methods rely heavily on extensive data collection and annotation, which poses substantial scalability challenges.

Despite the growing prominence of this approach, research on solver-verifier interactions remains relatively underexplored. Prior studies have predominantly focused on how a single model evaluates its own outputs (self-verification) and subsequently enhances its performance (self-improvement). However, self-verification is not inherently reliable: models may exhibit biases toward their own reasoning processes, and the training procedures can exacerbate these biases. Moreover, tasks may vary in the extent to which they benefit from verification. Furthermore, an exclusive focus on self-verification provides limited insight into the dynamics when the solver and verifier are distinct entities. With the availability of open-source models featuring diverse versions and configurations, we have the opportunity to systematically investigate verification mechanisms across different models. Therefore, we extend our analysis to encompass both intra-family and cross-family verification, posing the following key question: Under what conditions does verification yield tangible benefits, and how do factors such as model family, model size, additional post-training, solver-verifier alignment, and task type influence the efficacy of verification in enhancing solver performance? To address this, we evaluate verifiers across a diverse array of tasks, including those requiring precise logical reasoning or symbolic computation (e.g., 3-SAT, Sudoku, and matrix multiplication), mathematical reasoning tasks (e.g., AIME and GSM8K), commonsense and factual reasoning (e.g., CSQA and GPQA), and broad domain knowledge spanning STEM and social sciences (e.g., MMLU). This evaluation is conducted using 37 models from 7 distinct model families. Our findings reveal that self-verification is not universally effective: models often exhibit a preference for solutions aligned with their own reasoning patterns, additional post-training can exacerbate this bias, and the benefits of verification vary significantly across different task types. Based on our findings, we provide strategic recommendations for the effective utilization of verifiers.

Human comprehension of videos involves discerning the progression of events, the rationale behind actions, and the timing of significant occurrences. However, conventional video object segmentation (VOS) techniques predominantly focus on appearance-based or category-level indicators. In contrast, reasoning VOS elevates the complexity of the task: the model is required to interpret abstract and underspecified directives, employ commonsense and causal reasoning, and integrate temporal dynamics with semantic knowledge to accurately identify the target, such as "the runner most likely to win" or "the object causing the accident." This approach simplifies certain aspects while simultaneously increasing complexity, altering model functionality, and necessitating extensive data for fine-tuning. Therefore, effective video reasoning necessitates deliberate and informed decision-making rather than relying on singular, impulsive predictions. A model must discern where to focus, when to attend, and which entity the query pertains to. Building upon this principle, we propose ReVSeg, which eliminates reliance on latent segmentation tokens and instead reformulates reasoning video segmentation as a structured sequence of explicit reasoning steps. Specifically, ReVSeg decomposes reasoning video object segmentation into three distinct components that are congruent with model capabilities: video understanding (interpreting the query and analyzing scene dynamics), temporal grounding (identifying key frames or intervals relevant to the query), and spatial grounding (localizing the target objects within selected frames). These steps are executed through a single model, ensuring that the semantic information derived from initial stages is preserved and propagated through subsequent phases. This approach maintains alignment with the model's inherent training, obviates the need for extensive retraining, and transforms a complex problem into a structured and coherent process. Once the reasoning steps are delineated, the primary challenge lies in optimizing these intermediate processes to enhance overall decision-making efficacy. Traditional supervised learning approaches prove insufficient due to the lack of comprehensive annotations for intermediate reasoning stages. Consequently, we employ reinforcement learning methodologies, framing reasoning VOS as a sequential decision-making behavior that is incentivized exclusively when the entire reasoning trajectory culminates in a correct outcome. Yet, implementing reinforcement learning (RL) within this context presents significant challenges, as reasoning video object segmentation (VOS) provides limited success signals and necessitates coordination across comprehension, temporal selection, and spatial identification. To address these challenges, we introduce rewards at critical decision points, ensuring that RL enhances the model's reasoning capabilities. In conjunction with task decomposition, RL transforms the task from an obscure latent guessing game into a structured reasoning strategy: decomposition delineates what the model must decide, while RL elucidates how those decisions should synergistically operate. This collaborative effort establishes a reasoning strategy that is both internally consistent and robust in practical application.

Advancements in computational methodologies have significantly enhanced human-computer interaction, particularly through the development of novel tools and systems that enable autonomous reasoning and task execution by computational agents. Moreover, the emergence of open-source frameworks has streamlined the integration of modular components, such as planners and memory systems, thereby facilitating more efficient system design and implementation. Empirical evidence from corporate reports underscores the practical application of these systems in real-world scenarios. However, several studies highlight that merely increasing model size does not inherently improve reliability, emphasizing the need for rigorous evaluation of operational efficacy to ensure genuine performance enhancements. Assessing these systems' performance remains challenging due to the increasing complexity of their tasks. Previous research has explored specialized frameworks to aid evaluations; nevertheless, concerns regarding potential biases persist. To mitigate these issues, proposed strategies include employing multiple evaluators and ensuring fairness across diverse task domains. In line with these principles, our study adopts a dual-judge protocol, wherein distinct evaluators assess separate facets of the task to validate observed advancements. The dataset comprises 100 real-world editing tasks involving slide presentations, each encompassing an initial slide deck, a target deck with human-generated references, structured instructions, and a rubric delineating criteria for layout and content evaluation. To ensure comprehensive assessment, tasks were systematically categorized into sixteen distinct groups, capturing both semantic comprehension and precise formatting. Data was meticulously curated from over 15,000 PowerPoint presentations sourced from diverse origins and transformed into a format encapsulating layout and content intricacies. Presentations exhibiting a wide range of styles were retained, integrating contributions from various experts. From an initial pool of over 500 evaluated options, including student contributions, 100 exemplars were selected to represent a diverse array of professional and academic domains, ensuring that each category includes challenging instances.

The authors of a seminal work on Empirical Software Engineering [BRZ05] discuss the contributions of Victor R. Basili, a prominent figure in the field, highlighting the critical role of leveraging empirical data and evidence to enhance software engineering practices. Numerous studies have examined the implementation of Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE) within industrial settings and its implications for agile development practices. These studies have proposed valuable methodologies and strategies to guide the transition process, providing significant insights into facilitating the migration and ensuring its successful execution. However, none of the previous studies have undertaken an empirical assessment akin to ours, particularly as our study is situated in the phase following adoption and preceding implementation. In this paper, we have delineated the initial phase of an extensive process aimed at facilitating a company in transitioning its codebase to a Software Product Line (SPL). We conducted interviews meticulously designed to align with the company's specific context, with particular attention to the diverse roles of the project participants. Our objective was to ascertain the most effective strategies for preparing for the transition, concentrating on inquiries that would reveal existing best practices upon which we can build, the perceived benefits and risks, and the concerns expressed by stakeholders. Developing appropriate responses to these risks and concerns, leveraging perceived benefits, and promoting best practices will be instrumental in facilitating a seamless transition. We discovered that the organization already engages in commendable practices that will aid in this process and possesses a culture of innovation that is conducive to the project's success. The majority of stakeholders perceive the transition as advantageous; however, primary concerns pertain to the perceived complexity of the SPL and the challenges associated with its long-term sustainability.

The authors of a seminal work on Empirical Software Engineering discuss the contributions of Victor R. Basili, a pioneering figure in the field. Their work underscores the critical role of empirical studies, such as ours, and particularly the use of data and evidence to guide software engineering practices. As highlighted in the literature, numerous studies have addressed the challenges of adopting Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE) in industrial contexts and its implications for agile development methodologies. These studies have proposed various insightful procedures and strategies to support the transition, offering valuable guidance for the migration process. However, none of these studies have conducted an empirical assessment comparable to ours, which uniquely takes place after adoption but prior to implementation. In this paper, we have presented the initial stage of an extensive process aimed at assisting a company in migrating its code base to a Software Product Line (SPL). The interviews we conducted were carefully designed to account for the specific characteristics of the company, particularly the diverse roles of the project participants. Our primary objective was to identify effective strategies to prepare for the migration, focusing on research questions that explore existing best practices, the perceived benefits and risks of the transition, and the concerns raised by stakeholders. Addressing these risks and concerns, leveraging identified benefits, and promoting established best practices are expected to significantly ease the transition. Our findings reveal that the company already demonstrates commendable practices conducive to the migration and fosters a culture of innovation, which is likely to benefit the project. Most participants perceive the transition as advantageous; however, the primary concerns relate to the complexity of SPLs and the challenges associated with their maintenance.

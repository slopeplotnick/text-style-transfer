The individual is a prominent figure in the field of technology AI and serves as the Executive Director of the Machine Learning Center at the Georgia Institute of Technology. He holds the Rhesa S. Farmer Distinguished Chair of Advanced Computing Concepts and is recognized as a Georgia Research Alliance Eminent Scholar. He earned his PhD in Electrical Engineering from the Georgia Institute of Technology, is an IEEE Fellow, and has previously held positions at leading institutions such as SRI International, Nuance, Yahoo!, Microsoft, Google, Viv Labs, and Samsung. His research spans diverse areas, including acoustics, active noise and vibration control, speaker recognition, web search, AI virtual assistants, natural language processing, and conversational AI. This tutorial is tailored for researchers and practitioners working at the intersection of conversational agents and spoken information systems. While attendees are expected to have a foundational understanding of natural language processing (NLP) and speech processing, prior experience with system deployment is advantageous. However, the tutorial will begin with a carefully structured introduction to ensure accessibility for a broader audience. The following papers provide critical insights and serve as foundational references for this tutorial: - Dialogue act modeling for automatic tagging and recognition of conversational speech (Stolcke et al., 2000) - Is spoken language all-or-nothing? Implications for future speech-based human-machine interaction (Moore, 2017) - Dialogue Learning with Human Teaching and Feedback in End-to-End Trainable Task-Oriented Dialogue Systems (Liu et al., 2018) - Voice2series: Reprogramming acoustic models for time series classification (Yang et al., 2021) - Hyporadise: An open baseline for generative speech recognition with large language models (Chen et al., 2023a) - SpeechGPT: Empowering Large Language Models with Intrinsic Cross-Modal Conversational Abilities (Zhang et al., 2023). While numerous scholarly works will be referenced throughout the tutorial, the primary focus will be on seven to eight key papers, of which only one or two are authored by the presenter.

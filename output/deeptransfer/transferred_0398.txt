The core spatial abilities our framework aims to achieve include (i) implicitly inferring three-dimensional structure, (ii) controlling viewpoint with geometric consistency, encompassing scale, occlusion, and layout, (iii) understanding semantic arrangement, (iv) planning toward goals, and (v) maintaining long-horizon temporal coherence. To probe these abilities, we introduce two tasks: video-based scene navigation and object grounding. In scene navigation, the model follows camera-pose instructions to produce a video trajectory consistent with both the instructions and the spatial configuration of the scene. Conversely, in object grounding, the model processes textual instructions to identify and visually highlight a specified object, thereby testing its understanding of spatial arrangements and its ability to execute goal-directed planning. Together, these tasks evaluate whether VIDEO4SPATIAL can infer and act on scene-level details using video alone. However, given the absence of extensive public datasets specifically curated for such spatial reasoning tasks, we repurpose indoor scanning datasets, such as ScanNet++ and ARKitScenes, which provide extensive video trajectories across diverse indoor environments and facilitate the construction of context-target video clip pairs. We utilize a vision-language model to identify video clips that culminate with a centered object and generate natural-language grounding instructions for object localization. For navigation within the scene, annotated camera trajectories are converted into pose-following directives. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework effectively addresses complex visuospatial challenges. From video context alone, the model acquires an understanding of scene-level geometry and semantics, achieving robust performance in object grounding and scene navigation. Although trained with brief video contexts, VIDEO4SPATIAL extrapolates to considerably extended temporal sequences during inference, thereby enhancing its efficacy. Moreover, despite being trained on indoor environments, the model generalizes effectively to outdoor settings and to previously unseen object categories. In summary, we present VIDEO4SPATIAL, a straightforward yet efficacious video generation framework designed for visuospatial tasks. Our approach emphasizes critical design decisions, including the integration of joint classifier-free guidance over both context and instruction, the incorporation of auxiliary bounding boxes as a reasoning prior to enhance grounding accuracy, and the implementation of non-continuous context sampling to facilitate efficient context comprehension. Furthermore, we have instantiated and evaluated two spatial tasks within this framework.

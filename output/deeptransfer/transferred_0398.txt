The core spatial competencies our framework seeks to cultivate include (i) implicitly inferring three-dimensional structures, (ii) controlling the camera viewpoint with geometric consistency, including scale, occlusion, and layout, (iii) understanding the semantic arrangement of scene elements, (iv) planning toward specific objectives, and (v) maintaining long-horizon temporal coherence. To evaluate these abilities, we introduce two tasks: video-based scene navigation and object grounding. In the scene navigation task, the model follows camera-pose instructions to generate a video trajectory that aligns with both the given instructions and the underlying scene geometry. In the object grounding task, the model adheres to textual instructions to locate and visibly display a specific object, thereby testing its understanding of spatial arrangements and its planning capabilities. Together, these tasks assess whether VIDEO4SPATIAL can infer and interact with scene structures using video alone. However, due to the lack of large public datasets explicitly designed for these spatial tasks, we repurpose indoor scanning datasets such as ScanNet++ and ARKitScenes, which provide extensive video trajectories through diverse room environments. These datasets enable the creation of context-target video clip pairs. Using an off-the-shelf vision-language model, we identify video clips that culminate with a centered object and generate natural-language grounding instructions for object localization. For navigation, we convert annotated camera trajectories into pose-following directives. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework effectively addresses complex visuospatial challenges. From video context alone, the model learns scene-level geometry and semantics, achieving strong performance in both object grounding and scene navigation. Although trained on short video segments, VIDEO4SPATIAL extrapolates effectively to much longer temporal windows during inference, further improving its performance. Moreover, despite being trained on indoor scenes, it generalizes well to outdoor environments and to object categories unseen during training. In summary, we (1) introduce VIDEO4SPATIAL, a simple yet powerful video-based framework designed to tackle visuospatial tasks; (2) emphasize key design choices, including joint classifier-free guidance over context and instruction, the use of auxiliary bounding boxes to enhance grounding accuracy, and non-continuous context sampling for efficient context comprehension; and (3) instantiate and evaluate two spatial tasks to validate its effectiveness.

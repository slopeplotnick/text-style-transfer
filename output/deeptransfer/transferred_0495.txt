We propose a scalable approach for building multilingual safety guardrails that generalize across a wide spectrum of languages, using only a few high-resource languages. We have developed a lightweight safety guardrail model with only 0.5 billion parameters that supports over 100 languages, trained exclusively on data from just 13 high-resource languages. Our analysis systematically evaluates inter-cluster and intra-cluster cross-lingual transfer from high-resource to low-resource languages. Early content moderation systems largely focused on English; for instance, Google's Perspective API initially supported only English text (Lees et al. 2022). Subsequent works, such as Aegis-Defensive, WalledGuard, and WildGuard, demonstrated promising performance on safety and toxicity benchmarks within English contexts. However, recent studies have found that popular language models produce significantly more unsafe or harmful responses for non-English queries (Yang et al. 2024). Furthermore, prompts in low-resource languages have been shown to bypass safety filters more easily. Recent developments have explicitly targeted the vulnerabilities of English-centric safety measures in multilingual settings. Alternative methods, such as MrGuard and X-Guard, rely on low-accuracy translated data or synthetic data, which fail to capture the linguistic and cultural subtleties of low-resource languages. More recent advancements in multilingual safety include LlamaGuard3-8B and NemoGuard-8B-content-safety, both built on the Llama3.1-8B model, while DuoGuard employs a novel two-player reinforcement learning approach to train a small-scale classifier. To the best of our knowledge, PolyGuard represents the state-of-the-art in safety moderation models, released with the largest multilingual safety corpus to date. Nonetheless, even PolyGuard's coverage is limited to 17 languages, highlighting that most current multilingual guardrails still prioritize a relatively small set of high-resource languages.

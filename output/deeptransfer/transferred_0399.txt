The study is framed as unsupervised fraud detection in multi-type journal entry data, where labels for fraudulent activity are scarce and often unavailable, making supervised learning infeasible at scale. Therefore, the focus is on detecting anomalous entries by modeling irregular patterns across structured and unstructured fields to reflect real-world auditing conditions. To facilitate this, a synthetic journal entry dataset, introduced by Gronewald et al. (2024), emulates the bookkeeping practices of a medium-sized enterprise. Each entry features a unique posting ID, date and time of posting and transaction, credit/debit flags, tax rate, and account ID. Moreover, our method is benchmarked on real-world anonymized accounting data from our industry partner. Unlike typical double-entry bookkeeping systems, this dataset lacks identifiers due to anonymization, breaking the usual transactional structure. Consequently, it is treated as a transaction-level fraud detection problem. To compensate for missing context, we enrich the large language model (LLM) prompts with bookkeeping heuristics, guidelines, and summary statistics, which proves essential to outperform traditional machine learning baselines. Given the sensitivity of accounting data, privacy considerations necessitate the use of open-weight LLMs, such as LLaMa AI (2025) and Gemma Team et al. (2024), over proprietary LLM API endpoints like GPT-4o OpenAI (2024) during benchmarking. Our method outperforms the baselines on both the synthetic dataset by Gronewald et al. and the real-world anonymized dataset. For the baseline, we employ the Isolation Forest algorithm, originally proposed by Liu et al. (2008), due to its advantages in handling tabular datasets and its suitability for unsupervised anomaly detection, aligning with the proposed task in Section 3.

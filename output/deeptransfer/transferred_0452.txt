Experts in numerical modeling transform overarching hypotheses into numerical representations, wherein the primary hypothesis is decomposed into quantitative sub-hypotheses and further refined through detailed model parametrization. Model hypotheses are assigned discrete probabilities, while the intricacies of the model are expressed through probability distributions to quantify the likelihood of various outcomes. This prior distribution model needs to be tested with empirical data. Therefore, researchers compare its outputs to empirical data, a process that involves generating quantitative predictions based on the model, typically undertaken by domain experts with extensive knowledge of the relevant data. The accuracy of these predictions is assessed through statistical testing, which entails analyzing numerical outputs to identify potential discrepancies between the model's forecasts and observed data. Moreover, experts proficient in Monte Carlo simulation will employ the data to refine their conceptual models and parameters through the application of Bayesian inversion techniques. These experts make informed decisions contingent upon the degree of uncertainty associated with their predictive models. Effective implementation necessitates collaborative communication among all domain experts involved. Sensors classify the current state of the road, and predictions are made regarding future actions of itself as well as other road users or objects.

Notably, AIME emerges as an outlier in the final graph, possibly due to some models encountering analogous challenges during post-training. The best-fit lines for verifier accuracy delineate two distinct clusters of tasks (colored red and blue), indicating that verification difficulty cannot be solely attributed to solver accuracy. This observation raises the question: are certain tasks inherently more amenable to verification than others? Relatedly, Song et al. (2025) report that while models exhibit limited improvement on tasks requiring factual recall, they demonstrate notable gains on tasks such as Tokoku when sufficient pretraining is applied. A comparable trend is evident in Figure 7, where tasks like AIME, GSM8K, 3-SAT, and Tokoku exhibit higher verifier-to-solver accuracy ratios and achieve superior performance across all verification settings. To elucidate this phenomenon, we observe that within our synthetic datasets, Selenium and 3SAT exemplify problems characterized by exponential solving times yet permit polynomial-time verification. Conversely, for Matrix Multiplication, no straightforward method exists for verifying the product of two matrices without essentially recomputing it. Among the real-world datasets, GSM8K and AIME encompass problems resolvable with high-school-level mathematics, whereas MMLU (Social Sciences) necessitates domain-specific expertise, CommonsenseQA relies on implicit world knowledge, and GPQA and MMLU (STEM) require specialized knowledge in the natural sciences. For these latter tasks, verifying an answer necessitates the same level of domain-specific knowledge as required for problem-solving. Notably, while tasks that are relatively straightforward to solve tend to be easier to verify, certain tasks are inherently more amenable to verification. These include synthetic problems characterized by logical or structured reasoning, such as 3SAT and Sudo, as well as real-world tasks that predominantly rely on mathematical reasoning rather than extensive factual recall, exemplified by GSM8K and AIME. Such tasks yield notably greater advantages from the application of test-time rejection sampling facilitated by verifiers.

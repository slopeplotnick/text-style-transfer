This observation prompts an inquiry into whether certain tasks inherently lend themselves to more straightforward verification processes. Relatedly, Song et al. (2025) identified that models exhibit limited improvement on tasks necessitating factual recall; however, they demonstrate enhanced performance on tasks such as Tokoku, contingent upon sufficient pretraining. A comparable dichotomy is evident in Figure 7, where tasks such as AIME, GSM8K, 3-SAT, and Tokoku exhibit a higher ratio of verifier accuracy relative to solver accuracy, thereby achieving superior performance across all verification paradigms. To explain this, among the synthetic datasets, Selenium and 3SAT exemplify problems characterized by exponential solving complexity but amenable to polynomial-time verification. In contrast, Matrix Multiplication lacks an efficient verification shortcut, as verifying the product of two matrices necessitates a computational effort comparable to recomputing the product itself. Among the real-world datasets, GSM8K and AIME involve problems solvable using high-school-level mathematics, whereas MMLU (Social Sciences) requires domain-specific expertise, CommonsenseQA relies on implicit world knowledge, and GPQA and MMLU (STEM) demand specialized knowledge in the natural sciences. Consequently, for these latter tasks, verification often requires the same level of expertise as problem-solving. Key observations: While tasks that are straightforward to solve are generally easier to verify, certain tasks are intrinsically more amenable to verification. These include synthetic problems characterized by logical or structured reasoning (e.g., 3SAT, Sudo) and real-world tasks that predominantly rely on mathematical reasoning rather than extensive factual recall (e.g., GSM8K, AIME). These tasks also yield greater advantages from the implementation of test-time rejection sampling utilizing verifiers.

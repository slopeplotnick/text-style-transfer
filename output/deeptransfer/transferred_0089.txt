Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) with voice interfaces have garnered significant attention from both the academic community and the general public. Closed-source models, such as GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-pro, have demonstrated superior performance across classical speech tasks, including speech recognition, translation, and spoken language understanding, significantly surpassing previous open-source benchmarks. However, despite these strides, there remains a lack of comprehensive studies on the design and mechanisms underpinning the integration of speech modalities into LLMs for true multi-modal understanding. Moreover, the interaction between speech models and LLMs, particularly in the context of autonomous smart systems, is an emerging area of investigation. This tutorial aims to provide a thorough review of the historical trajectory of language models in speech processing, offering insights that motivate the development of systems capable of interactive communication. We will delve into advanced topics such as the integration of heterogeneous data types, introducing the foundational principles necessary to align non-textual data with textual information. These concepts will be discussed alongside illustrative examples to enhance comprehension. Furthermore, we will explore contemporary methodologies for developing models that simultaneously process speech and text, employing techniques that iteratively integrate speech and textual data. By examining both traditional and innovative approaches, this tutorial will elucidate the current state of joint speech-language modeling and identify persisting challenges within this domain. This three-hour tutorial will focus on the rapidly evolving field of speech-language modeling within the context of voice-interfacing LLMs and intelligent agent systems. Each core theme will be covered in a 35-minute segment, followed by a 10-minute Q&A and a 10-minute break. For each section, we will provide an overview of the relevant topics, followed by an in-depth exploration of key studies that shape the current landscape. The tutorial will conclude with a discussion of the prevailing challenges and emerging research opportunities in this dynamic and transformative area of joint speech-language modeling.

Agents detect behavioral drift or anomalous coordination patterns and propose norm adjustments to restore stability or diversity. Moreover, agents assess each other's compliance with current norms and adjust the influence or credibility of peers accordingly, thereby creating a distributed reputation structure. Furthermore, distinct functional roles are assigned to agents, encompassing legislative (rule generation), judicial (rule interpretation), and executive (task execution) capacities, thus avoiding single points of control. These mechanisms are deliberately abstract, describing the functions required for internal governance rather than their concrete implementation. Subsequent work will examine how these can be instantiated with current orchestration frameworks and evaluated through controlled experiments. The purpose of Institutional AI is not to make systems autonomous in a philosophical sense but to stabilize them under continuous change. In this approach, safety is integrated as an ongoing, system-internal process of monitoring and norm maintenance, rather than merely being assessed post-deployment. Agents contribute to generating the very constraints that regulate them, enabling partial self-correction when external oversight cannot act fast enough. However, these architectures are not replacements for human supervision; they are extensions of it. By distributing evaluative functions inside the system, they preserve traceability, promote diversity of reasoning, and mitigate the risks identified by the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon framework. The goal is to enhance the operational coherence, interpretability, and resilience of networks of interacting models without necessitating constant external intervention. The Institutional AI proposal should be understood as the next step following the taxonomy developed in this paper. Whereas the ESRH framework characterizes how collective risks emerge, the institutional perspective explores strategies for their effective management. This paper introduces only the conceptual foundation, with theoretical elaboration and empirical validation to follow in subsequent publications released through our research group and on arXiv.

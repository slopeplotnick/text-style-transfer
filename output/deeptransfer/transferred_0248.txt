Agents detect behavioral drift or irregularities in coordination patterns and propose modifications to existing norms to maintain systemic stability or foster diversity. Furthermore, they evaluate the adherence of their peers to established norms and adjust the level of trust or influence accorded to them, thereby establishing a decentralized reputation framework. Functional differentiation among agents is implemented, with distinct roles assigned for rule generation, rule interpretation, and task execution, thus mitigating the concentration of power. These concepts are intentionally abstract, emphasizing the prerequisites for effective internal governance rather than prescribing specific implementation methods. Subsequent research will investigate pathways for operationalizing these mechanisms within existing orchestration frameworks and subjecting them to empirical evaluation under controlled conditions. The overarching aim of Institutional AI is to ensure systemic stability amidst continuous evolution, rather than to achieve autonomy in a philosophical sense. In this approach, safety is integrated into the system as a continuous process of monitoring and norm maintenance, rather than merely an external audit conducted post-deployment. Moreover, agents contribute to the formulation of the constraints that govern them, thereby enabling partial self-correction in instances where external oversight may be delayed. These architectures do not supplant human supervision; rather, they augment it. By distributing evaluative functions within the system, they preserve traceability, promote diversity of reasoning, and mitigate the risks identified by the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon framework. The objective is to enhance the operational coherence of interacting model networks, increase their interpretability, and maintain their functionality without the necessity for constant external intervention. The Institutional AI proposal should be regarded as an extension of the taxonomy developed in this paper. While the ESRH framework delineates the emergence of collective risks, the institutional perspective examines their management strategies. This paper presents the foundational concepts.

This study outlines a methodology for creating an energy model that provides energy consumption distributions for statically typed programming languages executed on the Java Virtual Machine (JVM). It investigates the effects of primitive data types, data sizes, operations, and varying device instances on energy consumption. Moreover, it presents evidence that different device instances with identical hardware configurations can consume varying amounts of energy, thereby affecting the generalizability of energy predictions. The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 provides background information on Bayesian statistics. Section 3 introduces the methodology employed in this study. Section 4 discusses the implementation of the proposed method. Section 5 explains the evaluation procedures and addresses any encountered discrepancies in the results. The evaluation results are presented in Section 6, followed by a comprehensive discussion of the findings in Section 7. Section 8 reviews related literature, and Section 9 concludes the study. Bayesian statistics is a statistical framework that utilizes prior knowledge to make probabilistic inferences about phenomena that are not directly observable. While its high-level objective is similar to that of frequentist statistics, which is prevalent in computer science, the two methodologies differ in their approach to model construction and interpretation. Frequentist statistics relies solely on observed data and aims to minimize assumptions, whereas Bayesian statistics explicitly incorporates assumptions, thereby facilitating their verification. We employ Bayesian statistics in this study for two primary reasons: first, it necessitates the explicit articulation of assumptions, thereby enhancing modularity and interpretability; second, it provides predictions of energy consumption in the form of posterior distributions, offering a more detailed representation of potential outcomes compared to the single-point estimates typically produced by frequentist methods. In this analysis, we assume that energy consumption follows a normal distribution and lies within a range conservatively derived from prior research. These prior distributions are then refined through a Bayesian inference algorithm to better align with the observed measurements. In scenarios where data availability is limited, initial assumptions can significantly influence the outcomes, potentially leading to suboptimal results. However, as more data becomes available, the algorithm iteratively refines these initial assumptions, thereby improving its capacity to evaluate the alignment between the model and the observed data.

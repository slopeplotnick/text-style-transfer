This suggests that the Martingale Score serves as an effective proxy for reasoning quality, even in contexts where complete information is unavailable. Bayesian Rationality in Language Models. In the context of language models, assessing Bayesian rationality involves examining their capacity to update beliefs in accordance with evidence, as prescribed by Bayes' theorem. Gupta et al. (2025) demonstrated that language models are capable of updating their beliefs in accordance with Bayesian principles when presented with a substantial number of coin flips, despite an initial biased perspective. However, in more realistic scenarios involving complex natural-language features, Qiu et al. (2025) observed that language models do not adjust their beliefs regarding user preferences as anticipated by the Bayesian framework. Furthermore, Falck (2024) provided evidence that language models do not engage in Bayesian learning when contextual information is provided. Zhao (2021) and Wang (2023) demonstrated that language models exhibit varying responses contingent upon the arrangement of examples within prompts. Efforts to enhance the Bayesian nature of learning or reasoning processes have been proposed, such as emulating the predictions of an ideal Bayesian reasoner, employing abstract reasoning, and integrating abduction with deduction. Nevertheless, these approaches frequently necessitate auxiliary structures, such as Bayesian networks, thereby constraining their practical applicability. Cognitive Biases in Language Models and in Humans. Language models exhibit a range of biases attributable to their training on extensive human-generated data. When employed as evaluators, these models manifest biases distinct from those inherent to human cognition, such as position bias, morbidity bias, sentiment bias, and certainty bias. Of particular significance is confirmation bias, which is posited to be a primary contributor to polarization. Confirmation bias is widely regarded as a foundational category encompassing various cognitive biases. Accordingly, this study centers on confirmation bias and employs belief entrenchment as a framework to investigate its manifestations within the context of language models.

SMP functions as a modular motion style reward framework that facilitates the training of motion policies by providing style-based rewards without necessitating access to the original motion dataset. This characteristic allows for the integration of various adaptation strategies to refine the diffusion-based reward model and further shape the motion prior. However, these adjustments may sometimes result in unnatural motion patterns. Qualitative comparisons of various methods are illustrated in the accompanying video. SMP supports crafting novel motion styles by modifying the outputs of the pretrained diffusion model, thereby enabling the synthesis of styles absent from the original dataset without requiring additional model training. For instance, as depicted in Figure 6, distinct styles such as "AeroPlane" and "HighKnees" can be seamlessly blended to construct a composite motion prior. One pretrained motion model can be leveraged to develop policies for various tasks, such as navigation, target acquisition, or engaging in activities like dodgeball, as demonstrated in Table 2. This motion model is trained on a subset of the LaFAN1 dataset, characterized by unstructured running behaviors. The trained policies effectively balance task performance with motion naturalness. As illustrated in Figures 5(a) and 5(b), the character dynamically selects and transitions between various gaits to maintain task execution while preserving the natural appearance of movement. For instance, the steering policy autonomously initiates a backward jog when the character's facing direction is contrary to the target velocity, subsequently transitioning to a forward jog once alignment is achieved. Moreover, the policy trained with SMP is capable of executing side-steps and cross-steps without the necessity of an additional motion planning module. Within the Target Location task, the SMP-trained policy autonomously identifies human-like movement strategies, such as adaptively decelerating when required.

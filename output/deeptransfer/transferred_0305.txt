SMP serves as a modular motion style reward model that facilitates the training of motion policies without requiring access to the original motion dataset. This flexibility allows for the integration of various adaptation techniques to refine the diffusion-based reward model and further shape the motion prior. However, this process can sometimes lead to motions that appear less naturalistic. Qualitative comparisons of the various methods are available in the supplementary video, as shown in Figure 6. Moreover, SMP supports crafting novel motion priors by manipulating the pretrained diffusion model's outputs, enabling the creation of new styles that were not present originally without necessitating additional model training. For instance, distinct styles such as "AeroPlane" and "HighKnees" can be blended to formulate a new composite style. The integration of their predictions from the pretrained model facilitates the creation of composite motion priors. One pretrained motion model can be effectively reused to develop policies for a variety of tasks, such as steering, target location, and dodgeball, as demonstrated in Table 2. The motion model is trained on a subset of the LaFAN1 dataset, which includes unstructured running behaviors. Consequently, the trained policies demonstrate robust task performance while preserving motion naturalness. As illustrated in Figures 5(a) and 5(b), the character dynamically transitions between various locomotion patterns to maintain both task efficacy and kinematic plausibility. For example, the Steering policy autonomously executes a backward jog when the character’s orientation opposes the desired target direction and transitions to a forward jog upon alignment with the target trajectory. Furthermore, the policy trained with SMP is capable of executing side-steps and cross-steps autonomously, eliminating the need for an independent motion planning module. Within the Target Location task, the SMP-trained policy demonstrates human-like behaviors, such as intuitively decelerating in response to situational demands.

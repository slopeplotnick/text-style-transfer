DeepSeek-V3.2 represents a significant advancement in harmonizing computational efficiency with superior reasoning and agent performance. Its key technical innovations are as follows: (1) DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA): This efficient attention mechanism significantly reduces computational complexity while maintaining robust performance in long-context scenarios. (2) Scalable Reinforcement Learning Framework: By leveraging a robust reinforcement learning protocol and scaling computational resources post-training, DeepSeek-V3.2 achieves performance comparable to GPT-5. Furthermore, the high-compute variant, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale, surpasses GPT-5 and demonstrates reasoning capabilities on par with Gemini-3.0-Pro, earning gold medals at the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) and the International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI). (3) Large-Scale Agentic Task Synthesis Pipeline: To enhance reasoning within tool-use scenarios, we developed an innovative synthesis pipeline that systematically generates large-scale training datasets. This approach enables scalable agentic post-training, leading to notable improvements in generalization and instruction-following robustness in complex, interactive environments. The release of reasoning models such as DeepSeek-AI (2025) and OpenAI (2024a) marked a transformative milestone in the evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs), driving substantial advancements in their overall capabilities. However, in recent developments, a clear divergence has emerged between open-source and closed-source models. While the open-source community (MiniMax, 2025; MoonShot, 2025; Qwen, 2025; ZhiPu-AI, 2025) continues to make progress, proprietary models developed by organizations such as Anthropic (2025b), DeepMind (2025a), and OpenAI (2025) have advanced at a significantly faster pace. Consequently, the performance gap between open-source and closed-source models has widened, with proprietary systems demonstrating superior proficiency in complex tasks. Our analysis identifies three critical challenges limiting the capabilities of open-source models. First, their reliance on the traditional vanilla attention mechanism (Vaswani et al., 2017) constrains efficiency in processing extended sequences, thereby impeding scalable deployment and effective post-training. Second, open-source models suffer from insufficient computational investment during the post-training phase, which adversely impacts their performance on challenging tasks. Finally, these models exhibit weaker generalization and instruction-following capabilities compared to proprietary systems (EvalSys, 2025; Li et al., 2025; Luo et al., 2025), diminishing their effectiveness in real-world applications.

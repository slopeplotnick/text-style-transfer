The landscape is evolving rapidly: over the past decade, the global mining industry has positioned itself at the center of the energy transition, with the demand for critical minerals expected to rise alongside increasing geopolitical, social, and environmental complexities. This shift is driving the emergence of new companies, often venture capital-backed, that aim to disrupt the mining sector, much like the transformations seen in healthcare, renewable energy, and climate technology. Moreover, a new wave of digital technology is emerging, with the potential to reshape economic, educational, social, and industrial systems through advanced language models. While the full extent of this impact remains uncertain, the fear of missing out is pervasive. For the mining industry, however, this should not be a source of apprehension but rather an opportunity to harness advancements such as artificial intelligence. This paper explores where those opportunities lie and how AI can contribute to the sector. For instance, my group, Stanford Mineral-X, played a pivotal role in the discovery of a tier 1 copper deposit in the Zambian Copperbelt, where AI significantly influenced drilling strategies and subsurface modeling. Several start-ups specializing in AI-driven mineral exploration have since emerged from this initiative. This discussion does not aim to catalog all possible AI applications in exploration or provide an exhaustive review, as such studies are already available (Lindi et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024). Instead, it examines AI as a tool for improving decision-making processes through the development of a new scientific methodology. In Part I of this paper, I will outline the key decision-making challenges in mineral exploration and demonstrate how adopting a structured scientific approach can enhance efficiency. Currently, mineral exploration lacks a universally accepted scientific framework, relying instead on a collection of practices executed by domain experts, each adhering to distinct methodologies within their fields.

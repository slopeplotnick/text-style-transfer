Text vs. numeric signals. Numerical analysis alone proves effective but is clearly weaker than text-based analysis. Combining both views recovers most of the performance achieved by text-based approaches, underscoring a practical hierarchy: text is primary, with numeric features providing complementary signals. Signals hierarchy. Across datasets and tasks, results consistently reveal this hierarchy. For fake-news detection on EVONS (Table 1) and tweet series from FakeNewsNet (Table 1), text-based models demonstrate substantial efficacy. A lightweight nonlinear layer atop transformer embeddings approaches ceiling performance on EVONS and outperforms classical baselines on FakeNewsNet. Numeric signals alone are usable but notably weaker; for example, on FakeNewsNet virality prediction, the numerically GRU trails the text-only configuration by a wide margin (Table 2). Overall, text remains the primary modality, with numeric/meta features acting as supplementary cues. However, it is crucial to articulate this trade-off explicitly. In scenarios where minimizing false positives is critical, standard F1-selected checkpoints are preferable. Architecture vs. input view. On FakeNewsNet, sequence encoders such as GRU, LSTM, RNN, CNN, and Transformer all perform competitively across tasks, with the Transformer showing a slight edge (Tables 1-1). The proximity of their scores highlights that input view (text vs. numeric, fusion) and label definition are more impactful than incremental architecture adjustments, particularly under practical constraints. Practical recommendations. (1) Conceptualize virality prediction as a family of tasks defined by explicit thresholds and time horizons; always report these definitions alongside metrics. (2) Use text-only baselines as a strong foundation for fake-news detection, integrating lightweight numeric/meta features where available. (3) In early-screening scenarios, prioritize recall-weighted strategies while documenting precision trade-offs. (4) Favor simple, well-documented architectures; allocate sufficient effort to data curation, leakage controls, and clear protocol reporting.

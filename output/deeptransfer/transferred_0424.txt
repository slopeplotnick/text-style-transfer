We aim to develop a system that facilitates the training of policies to exhibit naturalistic motion behaviors without necessitating access to the original reference dataset. This system should be applicable across a variety of tasks and policies without requiring retraining. To achieve these objectives, we introduce Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), a method designed to train control policies to demonstrate natural behaviors across diverse tasks. Initially, we develop a model capable of comprehending motion data in a task-agnostic manner, devoid of any specific task or policy orientation. Once this training phase is complete, the model is employed to evaluate simulated motions against the behaviors observed in the original dataset. By leveraging this trained score-matching motion prior (SMP) as a reward mechanism, we are able to cultivate new policies that execute a variety of tasks while maintaining naturalistic behaviors. The primary objective of this work is to develop a framework for constructing reusable and modular motion priors for physics-based character animation by integrating reinforcement learning with score distillation. Consequently, the proposed system generates high-quality motions comparable to state-of-the-art methods, while significantly enhancing modularity and reusability. Furthermore, the Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP) enable the training of control policies without necessitating access to the original motion dataset, thereby facilitating efficient and scalable policy development. A motion prior can be constructed from a model trained on extensive motion datasets without being tailored to any specific task. This motion prior can subsequently be adapted into style-specific variants through the use of prompts and guidance mechanisms. Moreover, we demonstrate that distinct style priors can be composed to synthesize novel styles that were not present in the original dataset.

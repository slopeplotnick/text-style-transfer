This section will review the current most common applications of artificial intelligence in exploration and point out common errors encountered. Then, I will hypothesize on the potential trajectory of AI in exploration, particularly through the lens of decision-making processes. The ultimate goal of exploration is to make new discoveries. These discoveries can be made near known deposits, referred to as brownfield exploration, or in unexplored areas, known as greenfield exploration. In the next decade, it is anticipated that more discoveries will occur within brownfield areas than in greenfield regions. Exploration can be seen as an exercise in systematically refining the search parameters to identify locations with potential valuable resources, thereby reducing uncertainty in knowledge of the existence of economic deposits. The process typically begins at a continental scale, examining extensive regions such as large igneous provinces where geological conditions are conducive to the ascent and emplacement of magma in economically viable concentrations. Initially, geoscientists rely on geological maps and remotely sensed data to conduct reconnaissance. Anomalies detected within these datasets may indicate the presence of subsurface features of interest, thus guiding further investigation. Subsequently, more detailed fieldwork, geophysical surveys, and geochemical soil sampling are conducted. The next phase, involving drilling to validate or refute hypotheses regarding subsurface structures and mineralization, represents the most financially demanding stage of the exploration process. Ideally, such decisions are informed by rigorous methodologies grounded in decision science, uncertainty quantification, and the valuation of information, thereby optimizing resource allocation and minimizing expenditures associated with erroneous predictions. Various data sources contribute to the understanding of mineral system formation. This data is utilized not only to predict prospective regions, zones, or locations of interest but also to evaluate geological hypotheses, thereby advancing the broader comprehension of subsurface processes. Moreover, the utilization of data serves to mitigate the uncertainty associated with subsurface conditions. As decisions are formulated, the objectives of these decisions may evolve throughout the duration of the exploration project.

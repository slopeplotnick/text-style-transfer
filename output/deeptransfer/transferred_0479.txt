This section will review the current most common applications of artificial intelligence in exploration and point out frequent errors encountered in its implementation. Then, I will conjecture on the potential advancements of AI in exploration, particularly through the lens of decision-making processes. The ultimate goal of exploration is to make a new discovery. Discoveries can be made near known deposits, known as brownfield exploration, or in unexplored areas, referred to as greenfield exploration. In the next decade, it is anticipated that more discoveries will occur within brownfield sites compared to greenfield locations. Exploration can be seen as an exercise in systematically refining the search area by reducing uncertainty in knowledge of the existence of economic deposits. Typically, the process begins at the continental scale, examining large igneous provinces where geological conditions are favorable for the ascent and emplacement of magma in economically viable concentrations. Initially, geoscientists rely on aerial surveys and geological mapping to analyze spatial data. Anomalies identified within these datasets may serve as indicators of subsurface mineralization, warranting further investigation. Subsequently, more detailed investigations are conducted through fieldwork, geophysical surveys, and geochemical soil sampling. The next phase, which involves drilling to validate or refute hypotheses regarding subsurface geometries and lithologies, represents the most financially intensive stage of the exploration process. Ideally, these decisions are informed by rigorous methodologies grounded in decision science, uncertainty quantification, and the principles of valuing information, thereby minimizing costs through the optimal management of uncertainty relative to economic thresholds or other critical parameters. Various data sources contribute to the understanding of mineral system formation. These datasets are utilized not solely for the direct identification of mineralization but also to evaluate geological hypotheses and predict prospective regions of interest. Moreover, the integration of data serves to diminish uncertainty regarding subsurface conditions. Consequently, decision-making processes are undertaken, with the objectives of these decisions potentially evolving over the course of the exploration endeavor.

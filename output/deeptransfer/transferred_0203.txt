With the increasing deployment of video large language models (video LLMs) in real-world applications, such as AI glasses and household robots, these models are now required to process and reason over extremely long videos spanning several hours to even days. Recent advancements have introduced memory-based methodologies that construct external memory representations from abstracted video features. These approaches enable the model to focus on essential information by selectively retrieving a limited set of relevant memories, thereby reducing the number of input tokens. This strategy is both more cost-effective and efficient compared to processing all video frames, a task that incurs significant computational expense, as depicted in Fig. 1(a). However, the majority of existing methodologies remain heavily reliant on textual representations, often converting video segments into captions or summaries to facilitate information retrieval and comprehension. While certain methods incorporate visual information, their utilization is predominantly limited to object recognition, with minimal exploration of broader visual context. Moreover, these models typically select a fixed number of video segments for analysis, such as three clips of 30 seconds each. This approach presents two significant limitations. Firstly, it fails to effectively integrate visual information from the video with textual memory, thereby constraining the models' capacity to retrieve and synthesize information comprehensively. The ability to discern intricate details within video content is crucial for tasks that necessitate feature recognition, spatial comprehension, or accurate scene interpretation, which cannot be fully conveyed through textual descriptions alone. Furthermore, the simultaneous utilization of both captions and video frames in generating responses may lead to cognitive overload, as excessive visual information can be distracting. Consequently, there is a pressing need to develop methodologies for the strategic selection of an optimal combination of textual and visual inputs to enhance information retrieval for specific queries, a research area that remains largely unexplored.

Experiments demonstrate that ReVSeg achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on standard video object segmentation (VOS) benchmarks, surpassing both latent feature-based VLMs [8, 16, 20, 35, 41, 42, 50, 53, 55] and training-free explicit VLM approaches [13], while providing interpretable reasoning traces. As illustrated in Figure 1, controlled ablations reveal that removing our decomposed reasoning chain significantly degrades performance, with both the baseline model and its RL-trained variant struggling with complex reasoning VOS tasks. However, our framework exhibits strong performance even before RL integration, and the subsequent RL training further enhances results. In summary, our contributions are as follows: First, we deconstruct reasoning VOS into distinct steps, introducing ReVSeg, which reformulates video segmentation as an explicit multi-step reasoning chain built from native VLM primitives. Second, we develop an innovative RL framework designed to optimize this reasoning chain, thereby enabling the model to refine decision-making capabilities without requiring extensive additional training data. Third, we establish a new benchmark in VOS performance, concurrently enhancing the transparency of the reasoning process. Recent advancements in fine-grained text-guided VOS have been achieved through specialized segmentation architectures and referring-video pipelines [3, 5, 29, 45]. Nevertheless, significant challenges persist in addressing more complex tasks, such as videos with numerous similar objects, rapid motion, or occluded entities. Recent approaches, such as CoT-RVS [13], attempt to address these challenges by employing dual systems for processing individual video frames, subsequently integrating them through textual connections. Although effective to a degree, these methods are limited by the lack of comprehensive system integration and absence of training mechanisms. In contrast, our methodology conceptualizes reasoning VOS as a systematic, stepwise procedure within a unified framework, employing reinforcement learning (RL) to iteratively enhance performance.

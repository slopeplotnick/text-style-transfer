Experiments demonstrate that ReVSeg achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on multiple standard VOS benchmarks, surpassing both latent-based VLMs [8, 16, 20, 35, 41, 42, 50, 53, 55] and the training-free explicit VLM [13], while providing interpretable reasoning traces. As illustrated in Figure 1, controlled ablations reveal that the removal of our decomposed reasoning chain significantly degrades performance, with both the base VLM and its RL post-trained variant struggling on complex reasoning VOS tasks. In contrast, our framework exhibits strong performance even prior to RL optimization, with RL post-training delivering substantial additional improvements. In summary, our contributions are threefold: (1) A principled decomposition of reasoning VOS. ReVSeg reformulates reasoning video segmentation as an explicit, multi-step reasoning chain constructed from native VLM primitives. (2) An RL framework that optimizes the reasoning chain itself. We propose a novel RL-based formulation that directly refines the reasoning chain, enabling the model to improve decision-making without requiring dense supervision. (3) Establishing a new SOTA on standard VOS benchmarks while enhancing the interpretability of the reasoning process. Recent advancements in fine-grained text-guided VOS have been driven by specialized segmentation architectures and referring-video pipelines [3, 5, 29, 45]. However, for the emerging and more complex setting of reasoning VOS, challenges such as handling multiple similar objects, occlusion, fast motion, and long-range context remain significant. While explicit textual reasoning frameworks like CoT-RVS [13] have begun addressing these challenges by employing two independent VLM systems for frame-by-frame segmentation linked via text, their training-free design limits integration and interoperability. In contrast, we conceptualize reasoning VOS as an explicit reasoning chain within a unified VLM and leverage reinforcement learning to iteratively enhance performance.

In recent years, the technology underlying Large Language Models (LLMs) has advanced rapidly, leading to their widespread application across various domains, such as intelligent interaction and scientific research support. Particularly in the healthcare sector, LLMs have demonstrated significant potential in assisting with disease diagnosis and optimizing diagnostic and treatment workflows. In the realm of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), the core clinical principle is "Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment" (SDT), which necessitates the integration of diverse information to ascertain the underlying condition and develop personalized treatment plans based on the principle of "different treatments for the same disease." Although TCM-focused LLMs, such as Zhongjing and HuatuoGPT, have been developed, their practical application in clinical settings remains challenging due to the intricate and individualized nature of TCM. Critically, there is a lack of comprehensive evaluation methodologies to assess the efficacy of these models in clinical decision-making, as well as specialized metrics to gauge their reasoning capabilities. Therefore, there is an urgent need for researchers and industry stakeholders to develop a benchmark dataset capable of rigorously and comprehensively evaluating the applicability of these models within the context of TCM. Such a benchmark would support model enhancement, performance assessment, and ultimately enable their integration into clinical practice. However, the construction of this dataset presents considerable challenges due to the individualized, holistic, and diverse nature of TCM, which emphasizes personalized treatment, a comprehensive approach, and significant variability. For instance, individuals with the same medical condition may exhibit divergent symptoms due to factors such as physiological constitution, disease progression, or lifestyle variations, complicating the establishment of standardized evaluation criteria for these models. TCM diagnosis is inherently comprehensive, employing a multitude of methodologies to synthesize information, ascertain the etiology, and formulate treatment strategies. Consequently, a benchmark dataset must encompass a wide array of diagnostic dimensions. Moreover, patients frequently present with multiple concurrent conditions, and the diverse diagnostic approaches inherent in TCM can render evaluation outcomes more subjective. In summary, these factors collectively render the development of a comprehensive dataset for assessing the efficacy of TCM-focused LLMs in practical clinical scenarios exceedingly challenging.

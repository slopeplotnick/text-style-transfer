In recent years, the technology underpinning Large Language Models (LLMs) has significantly advanced and is now extensively utilized across various domains, such as intelligent interaction and scientific research support. Particularly within the healthcare sector, LLMs have demonstrated considerable efficacy in aiding disease diagnosis and optimizing diagnostic and treatment processes. In the realm of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), the fundamental clinical concept is known as "Syndrome Differentiation and Treatment" (SDT). This approach requires the integration of multifaceted information to accurately identify syndromes and subsequently devise personalized treatment strategies, adhering to principles such as "different treatments for the same disease." However, despite the development of TCM-specific LLMs, such as Zhongjing and HuatuoGPT, their practical implementation in clinical settings remains challenging due to the intricate and individualized nature of TCM diagnostic and therapeutic processes. A critical limitation lies in the absence of comprehensive evaluation benchmarks that encompass the entirety of the clinical decision-making process, alongside the lack of specialized metrics to assess the reasoning capabilities of these models. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a robust dataset that can comprehensively and equitably assess the applicability of these models within the domain of TCM. Such a dataset would facilitate the enhancement of model performance, enable thorough performance evaluation, and ultimately support their integration into clinical practice. Nonetheless, the development of such a dataset is fraught with challenges due to the individualized, holistic, and diverse nature of TCM, which emphasizes personalized treatment, a comprehensive approach, and considerable variability. For instance, individuals presenting with the same ailment may exhibit divergent symptoms due to factors such as their constitution, the progression of their condition, or their lifestyle, complicating the establishment of a standardized model evaluation framework. Moreover, the diagnostic process in TCM is inherently holistic, employing a multitude of methodologies to synthesize information, ascertain etiological factors, and formulate treatment strategies, necessitating a dataset of extensive scope. Furthermore, patients frequently present with multiple concurrent conditions, and the diverse diagnostic approaches can render the evaluation outcomes more subjective. Consequently, these factors substantially complicate the development of a comprehensive dataset to assess the efficacy of TCM-focused LLMs in practical clinical scenarios.

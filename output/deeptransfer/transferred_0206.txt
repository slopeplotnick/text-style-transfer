The model is trained using a methodology similar to the prior SFT process; however, in this phase, reasoning examples generated during training are incorporated. In addition to the latent tokens produced by the model, a proportion of sentences are randomly replaced with latent tokens, as in the earlier SFT phase. This approach further encourages the model to generate efficient reasoning. During inference, the model defaults to a standard mode, but it switches to an alternative reasoning mode upon encountering specific tags (see Figure 1). These tags distinguish between the two operational modes; however, they are not special tokens within the model's vocabulary. Instead of explicitly training the model to use these tags, we simply prompt it to do so. The model also predicts a numerical value to determine the number of latent tokens to generate. Initially, the use of these tags is minimal, but as training progresses, their occurrence becomes more frequent.

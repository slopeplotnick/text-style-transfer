The empirical observation that predictions often stabilize well before the final diffusion step has been noted (Pengxiang et al., 2025). Dynamic policies modulate exploration versus acceleration across the chain by using signals like historical logits or local determinism to reduce redundant steps (Wei et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b; Or-El et al., 2025). Meanwhile, other lines of work focus on error correction and refinement, enabling models to autonomously revise their outputs by re-masking low-confidence tokens (Zhang et al., 2025a; Su et al., 2025). Few- and one-step routes also address this bottleneck by transferring ideas from continuous processes, employing stage-wise learning to ensure consistency or aligning flows (Sahoo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Meister et al., 2024). In parallel, orthogonal efforts reduce per-step latency with caching adapted to bidirectional refinement and with speculative mechanisms that draft and verify tokens in parallel (Yuan et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a; Sohoni et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025a). However, while these methods demonstrate efficacy, they often require additional training, introduce auxiliary models, or rely on complex heuristics, leaving room for a simple, training-free, and architecture-agnostic early-exit principle. Therefore, we revisit diffusion decoding as a when-to-stop problem and introduce SchED, a schedule-based early-exit mechanism that is both training-free and model-agnostic. Concretely, this approach evaluates the confidence level associated with each component of the response, comparing it against a dynamically adjusting threshold that becomes progressively more lenient. By decoupling the confidence target from step count and making the threshold a smooth function of progress, the sampler exits as soon as predictions are stable, while avoiding the brittleness of hard phase changes or fixed budgets. SchED integrates seamlessly with standard transfer schedules, such as single-suffix or block diffusion, and requires no changes to the model training process (Nie et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025).

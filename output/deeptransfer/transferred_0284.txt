The RAIR benchmark encompasses a total of 63,601 samples, drawn from 14 real-world e-commerce industries. It consists of three distinct subsets established during our construction process: the General subset (46,775 samples), the Long-Tail Hard subset (10,931 samples), and the Visual Salience subset (5,895 samples). For each entry in the dataset, we provide the query, item title, item details, SKU information, and an anonymized item image. Moreover, each entry is accompanied by its manual ground truth annotation based on our four-level relevance scale (L1-L4). The comprehensive distribution of these labels across the dataset is presented in Table 8. To further support model development and facilitate detailed error attribution, each data instance is augmented with the corresponding rule identifiers from our Rule Checklist, which serve as the foundation for its relevance judgment. In this section, we systematically validate and analyze the proposed methods using the RAIR benchmark. Our experiments are divided into two parts. First, we employ quantitative metrics to address two core questions: (1) the extent of performance enhancement achieved through fine-tuning; and (2) the performance advantages exhibited by the proposed LORE model across various data distribution subsets, including general samples, hard samples, and visually salient samples. Second, we utilize case studies to demonstrate the novel capabilities that our methods impart to the model and the types of problems they enable it to solve. To ensure the stability and reproducibility of evaluation results, all trained models employ a deterministic decoding strategy for single-pass inference, thereby eliminating sampling randomness. Finally, we employed a variety of metrics to assess the model's performance.

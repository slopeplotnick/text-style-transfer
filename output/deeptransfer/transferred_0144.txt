Other approaches include CoT2, a system designed to generate token mixtures similar to training-free methods, which is applied to GPT-2 for logical reasoning tasks. CODI employs a teacher-student distillation framework to enhance the performance of GPT-2 and Llama 3.1 1B models on GSM8k tasks. Similarly, Token Assorted innovates by developing new token embeddings through the use of a VQ-VAE, subsequently training Llama 3.1 8B for mathematical and logical reasoning tasks. In contrast, our LiteReason approach diverges from these methods in several key ways. First, we rely exclusively on model-generated traces rather than large datasets like GSM8k-Aug, which are unavailable for our specific tasks. Second, we train only a lightweight Reasoning Projector for latent reasoning, rather than the entire model, as we hypothesize that over-training the base model for token-embedding generation could destabilize its token-generation capabilities. Third, we allow the model to interleave latent and discrete reasoning during RL training, with the expectation that it will progressively learn an optimal balance between these reasoning modalities. Finally, while many existing approaches benchmark their performance against Chain-of-Thought prompting and some incorporate RL into their frameworks, to the best of our knowledge, none systematically compare their results against a non-latent RL-trained model. We argue that this baseline provides a more realistic evaluation metric for complex tasks, and thus, we include it as a critical component of our performance assessment. Although much of the existing reasoning research has focused on domains like mathematics and programming, there is also a substantial body of work exploring the application of large language models (LLMs) to narrative understanding and generation tasks. Prior studies often decompose story generation into sub-tasks, such as planning narrative events or developing character attributes, which can be interpreted as distinct reasoning paradigms. However, adapting these tasks to reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks presents significant challenges, primarily due to the absence of well-defined reward structures. In this work, we focus on two specific tasks designed to evaluate reasoning capabilities within narrative contexts.

The left section of Figure 2 illustrates the prompt configuration along with an example of the extracted triples for a single training image, showcasing the semantic organization of various attributes. The output consists of multiple ontology-aligned triples that capture diverse aspects of the garment, including category (depicts), shape (has_silhouette), material (has_material_type), pattern (has_pattern), and other relationships defined by the Fashionpedia ontology. Each triple is formatted as (image_id, relation, attribute), offering a structured and interpretable representation of the image's semantic content. This structured representation facilitates downstream tasks such as constructing and comparing concept-level graphs. To assess influence, we systematically compared the graph of each generated image with those of all training images by evaluating node-level overlap. For each prompt, we retained the training images with the highest degree of node overlap with the graph of the generated image. The prompt used was: "Generate triples for the fashion item depicted in the image, utilizing the relationships defined by the Fashionpedia Ontology, accessible at: \\({ONTOLOGY_PATH} = \). Focus exclusively on the fashion item within the image identified by image_id: \\)([IMAGE_ID=)} and provide the triples that describe the image (image_id, relationship, attribute_value)." For instance, for image 58810.jpg, the extracted triples are as follows: 58810.jpg, depicts, Shirt; 58810.jpg, belongs_to_category, Upperbody; 58810.jpg, has_nickname, None; 58810.jpg, has_silhouette, Slim-fit; 58810.jpg, has_waistline, Straight; 58810.jpg, has_length, Long-sleeved; 58810.jpg, has_collar_type, Classic; 58810.jpg, has_lapel_type, Not Applicable; 58810.jpg, has_neckline, Crew-neck; 58810.jpg, has_sleeve_type, Long Sleeves with Cuffs; 58810.jpg, has_pocket_type, Not Applicable; 58810.jpg, has_opening_type, Button-front Closure; 58810.jpg, has_material_type, Cotton-blend Fabric; 58810.jpg, has_leather_type, None; 58810.jpg, has_fabric_treatment, Not Applicable; 58810.jpg, has_pattern, Plaid; 58810.jpg, has_animal_pattern, None. These graphs, which exhibited the highest node overlap with the graph of the generated image, were subsequently used in the unlearning procedure. Moreover, Neo4j enabled a broader exploration of the graph space, such as identifying the most frequent attributes or visualizing the distribution of concepts across the dataset. To facilitate unlearning, we retrained the generative model after removing specific training samples. This allowed us to evaluate the influence of these samples on the model's output. We conducted 10 unlearning experiments using both the baseline approach and our proposed method. In each experiment, we generated images for 15 prompts before and after unlearning. To ensure consistency with the baseline setup, we removed a comparable number of the most influential training images for each prompt.

This approach is applicable in various contexts: geologists describe conceptual models through diagrams presented in their publications, mathematicians turn these into constraints on prior probability models, and geophysicists use them for Bayesian inversion. Another example involves alternative data, as the initial conceptualization may be fundamentally flawed. This issue is emblematic of a broader philosophical challenge in science: how do we validate our underlying assumptions? It was the Austrian philosopher Karl Popper who dedicated his life to examining such philosophical inquiries. He posited that science should eschew the use of induction, which involves formulating theories based on observations. Instead, theories are seen as speculative or tentative, created by the human intellect to address deficiencies in existing theories. Once a theoretical framework is formulated, it is imperative to subject it to empirical scrutiny. Theories that are inconsistent with observational data should be rejected. Conversely, theories that withstand rigorous empirical evaluation are considered the most robust within the current scientific paradigm. In the context of uncertainty quantification, models are conceptualized as hypotheses regarding reality. Each model represents a distinct hypothesis. These hypotheses are constrained by existing knowledge; however, empirical data should not be employed to validate a model. Instead, data should serve as a means to falsify a model when discrepancies arise between the model's predictions and the observed data. Popper provides a methodology to address the inherent subjectivity present in Bayesian reasoning, which constitutes a form of inductive reasoning, as opposed to the deductive nature of falsification. If the prior distribution fails to predict the current data, the Bayesian prior is thereby falsified. This indicates that regardless of the number of models derived from the prior, none align with the empirical data. Although Popper's concept may render the prior less subjective following testing, he does not furnish a method for revising the prior, leaving it subject to human judgment.

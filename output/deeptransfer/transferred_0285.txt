We are establishing experimental environments wherein artificial intelligence systems can enforce, interpret, and modify shared protocols. Our objective is to evaluate whether these methodologies effectively mitigate the previously discussed issues of interoperability among diverse systems. This paper aims to establish a comprehensive research trajectory in AI safety that integrates theoretical frameworks, empirical investigations, and ultimately, standardization protocols. It serves as a conceptual blueprint, with subsequent studies dedicated to substantiating these ideas through rigorous proofs and experimental validation. We have posited that the prevailing focus on single-agent safety mechanisms—such as prompt engineering and output filtering—is inadequate in the context of multi-agent interactions among AI systems. As artificial intelligence systems increasingly operate in collaborative and interconnected environments, adherence to localized safety protocols proves insufficient to prevent large-scale systemic failures. Therefore, a critical distinction emerges between ensuring the safety of individual models and safeguarding the stability of the broader system. To address this, we introduce the concept of the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH), a framework designed to elucidate the conditions under which this divergence becomes apparent. We have demonstrated that risks can escalate from minor issues to significant systemic challenges as interaction, complexity, and divergent objectives converge. These risks are not merely attributable to the malfunction of individual AI entities but are inherent to the dynamics of networked interactions. By enabling agents to adaptively modify rules and collaborate, we aim to maintain stability in collective behavior without solely depending on centralized control mechanisms. This work represents merely the initial phase. The ESRH framework and the Institutional AI concept necessitate comprehensive empirical testing, formal theoretical development, and iterative refinement. Subsequent steps involve conducting controlled experiments to evaluate the predefined indicators, exploring models of coordination, and developing practical tools for the application of these concepts in real-world AI systems. The transition from single-agent to multi-agent artificial intelligence systems is currently underway. Without innovative conceptual frameworks and management strategies, safety concerns may remain obscured until they escalate beyond manageable proportions. Thus, this paper aims to elucidate these challenges and propose a systematic approach to address them.

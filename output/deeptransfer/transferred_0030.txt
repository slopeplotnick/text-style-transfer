Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) enables pretrained diffusion models to not only generate samples through a denoising process but also serve as optimization objectives [Poole et al. 2022]. This technique has allowed pretrained image and video diffusion models to be adapted for a wide range of generative modeling tasks [Jiang et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2023; Yin et al. 2024]. Building on the success of SDS in the visual domain, recent research has explored its integration into reinforcement learning frameworks for control tasks. Specifically, several methods replace the discriminator in GAIL-style setups with a diffusion model [Huang et al. 2024b; Lai et al. 2024; Pang et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2024], leveraging its expressive capabilities to distinguish between real and synthetic trajectories. However, these approaches still rely on adversarial training and often require iterative updates to the diffusion model during policy optimization. Beyond adversarial frameworks, alternative methods have sought to directly apply SDS-like techniques to policy learning. For example, Luo et al. [2024] utilize pretrained image or video diffusion models conditioned on text prompts to guide policy training. While these methods can generate behaviors that align with textual instructions, the resulting motions often appear unnatural. A closely related line of work is SMILING [Wu et al. 2025a], which trains controllers using a score-matching objective similar to variational score distillation (VSD) [Wang et al. 2023]. Unlike SMILING, which requires training task-specific diffusion models, our approach trains a task-agnostic motion prior using unstructured motion data. This motion prior can then be reused to train diverse control policies by combining it with distinct task objectives in a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning framework. Additionally, we introduce several key design choices that enable the generation of high-quality, naturalistic motions across a wide range of tasks.

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) is a methodology where pretrained diffusion models not only generate samples through a denoising process but also serve as optimization objectives. This approach has enabled the application of these models across a diverse array of tasks in image and video generation. Inspired by the success of SDS in image synthesis, recent works have extended its application to reinforcement learning frameworks for control tasks. Specifically, some methods replace the conventional discriminator in frameworks like GAIL with a diffusion model, leveraging its ability to distinguish between authentic and synthetic actions. However, these approaches still rely on adversarial training and often require iterative updates to the diffusion model during policy optimization. Beyond adversarial frameworks, alternative strategies have sought to directly employ SDS-inspired techniques for policy learning. For instance, certain approaches facilitate policy training using pretrained image or video diffusion models conditioned on textual prompts. While these methods can generate behaviors that adhere to textual instructions, the resultant motions sometimes appear unnatural. A closely related work is SMILING, where controllers are trained using a methodology akin to variational score distillation. In contrast to SMILING, which necessitates the development of task-specific diffusion models, our approach leverages a task-agnostic motion prior trained on unstructured motion data. This motion prior can then be integrated with distinct task objectives within a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning framework to facilitate the training of diverse control policies. Furthermore, we propose several critical design principles that enable the generation of high-quality, naturalistic motions across a broad spectrum of tasks.

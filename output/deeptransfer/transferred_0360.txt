A distinct line of research focuses on refining the generation process itself. Methods such as Mirror-Consistency, Self-Contrast, and Step-Back Prompting utilize iterative reflection or diverse perspectives to enhance the quality of generated outputs, while the Self-Check method employs step-wise verification to systematically identify and rectify errors. These approaches, however, primarily aim to improve the generator itself, often at the cost of increased computational overhead. In contrast, our work shifts focus to the aggregation process: rather than attempting to resolve inconsistencies within raw outputs, we apply a specialized integration layer to robustly identify and extract the most accurate and consistent response from a noisy distribution of parallel samples. A critical design choice when employing a language model as a judge for pairwise comparisons is whether to allow the model to declare a tie or to compel it to make a definitive choice. We first demonstrate that forcing the model to break ties can introduce biases into its judgments. Furthermore, we show that the stability of tie decisions is highly sensitive to the configuration of the adjudicative framework, underscoring the need for more robust aggregation methodologies. Allowing ties is not merely a mechanism for capturing equivalence but also serves as an important strategy for mitigating biases inherent in language models. For instance, these models are prone to positional bias, where the order of presented options influences the model’s preference. In an experiment, we modified the framework to permit tie declarations, which significantly reduced positional bias across both models under evaluation. This finding highlights that enabling tie decisions is not only critical for identifying equivalence but also for enhancing the fairness of the evaluation process. However, tie decisions exhibit notable instability. In three-way preference tasks, the vote distribution of the model is highly sensitive to variations in the evaluation setup, emphasizing the necessity for a more robust aggregation approach to address this inconsistency.

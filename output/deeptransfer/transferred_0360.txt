Generator Refinement and Verification: A distinct area of research focuses on enhancing the content generation capabilities of generators. Techniques such as Mirror-Consistency, Self-Contrast, and Step-Back Prompting employ iterative reflection or diverse perspectives to refine the output quality, while Self-Check systematically progresses through each step to identify and rectify errors. These methodologies prioritize enhancing the generator's intrinsic capabilities, albeit at the expense of increased temporal and computational resources. In contrast, our approach diverges from this paradigm by concentrating on the synthesis of outputs. Rather than endeavoring to rectify the generator's inconsistent outputs, we accept them in their existing form and employ a specialized layer to ascertain the most precise answer. A significant consideration when employing a language model as an adjudicator to compare two entities is whether to permit it to declare equivalence or compel it to select one over the other. Initially, we demonstrate that imposing a choice may induce bias within the model. Subsequently, we elucidate that allowing the model to declare a "tie" is substantially contingent upon the configuration of the adjudication process, necessitating the development of a more robust approach to address this issue. Incorporating the option to select a tie significantly mitigates bias in language models utilized as evaluative agents. These models are susceptible to various forms of systematic bias, including positional bias, wherein the sequence of presented options influences the model's preferences. In a controlled experiment, modifying the evaluation framework to include a tie option substantially reduced positional bias across both tested models. This analysis underscores that incorporating a tie option serves not merely as a mechanism for indicating equivalence but as an essential tool for enhancing the fairness of the evaluation process. However, tie decisions exhibit notable instability. In three-way preference tasks, the voting behavior of an LLM-as-a-Judge is highly susceptible to variations in the evaluation setup, necessitating more robust methodologies for aggregating outcomes.

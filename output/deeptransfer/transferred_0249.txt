We propose the Autonomous Domain-Oriented Relevance Engine (ADORE), which integrates three core components: a domain rule-aware relevance discrimination (RD) module, an error-type-aware data synthesis (DS) module, and a key-attribute-enhanced knowledge distillation (KD) module. The RD module incorporates a CoT-based reasoning LLM to autonomously generate domain-specific hard samples by performing relevance reasoning on online exposure products. Meanwhile, the DS module utilizes an error-type-aware generation model to produce diverse and challenging samples, thereby enhancing the system's robustness. Furthermore, the KD module explicitly integrates key attribute features derived from CoT analysis, enabling enhanced knowledge representation and improving the reasoning capabilities of the online student model. To evaluate the performance of ADORE, we conducted extensive experiments using large-scale offline datasets and online A/B testing. The results reveal that ADORE significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, achieving notable improvements in both offline and online metrics. These findings underscore the practical effectiveness of ADORE in addressing complex relevance modeling challenges within the e-commerce domain. To further enhance the performance of our online model, we developed a relevance discrimination model leveraging Large Language Models. Previous research [9, 21] has shown that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting substantially improves the reasoning accuracy of LLMs. Building on this insight, we fine-tuned our LLM using a CoT-based approach. The process began with GPT-4o generating CoT-labeled datasets by simulating the human annotation process, guided by domain-specific annotation guidelines and exemplar CoT instances. Consequently, we created a dataset enriched with detailed reasoning chains, closely resembling human-like annotations. To ensure the accuracy of the labeled data, we exclusively included query-product data validated by human-annotated labels, discarding any CoT data inconsistent with these labels. This refined dataset formed the basis for further fine-tuning the LLaMA-3 [4] model through the optimization of a standard Language Model loss function.

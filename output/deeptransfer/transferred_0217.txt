In Section 3, we explained how the questions align with four principal axes corresponding to the research inquiries. Since most interviewees were unfamiliar with SPLE, we provided an explanation of its concepts and significance during the interviews. While this step may have introduced a potential bias, we trust the interviewees' ability to form their own opinions and the interviewer's efforts to minimize undue influence. The questions were intentionally designed to be open-ended, encouraging extensive responses and capturing a wide range of ideas. They were carefully crafted to avoid restricting the scope of potential answers. To maximize the utility of the collected data, we developed metrics that quantify relevant terms and their frequency of occurrence, both in aggregate and by specific roles. Accordingly, tables present exhaustive information, while graphs illustrate the distribution of values. Even unique responses, not shared by others, were included and considered in the analysis. The responses were thoroughly analyzed, and the terminology extracted from the interviews was systematically aggregated and aligned with established SPLE concepts, such as "personalisation" and "making specific some parts of the code," which correspond to "customization." This alignment not only ensured consistency in the results but also facilitated the identification of relevant arguments in the literature to address participants' concerns. Moreover, iterating through this process with various participants allowed us to progressively enhance the fluidity of the interviews. Although the questions themselves remained unchanged, our approach to delivering them improved over time. For example, we began providing additional hints and elaborating on details when interviewees expressed uncertainty, as well as refining transitions between questions to ensure a smoother flow. Despite these adjustments, we believe that the quantity and quality of information obtained from both the initial and final questionnaires remained consistent.

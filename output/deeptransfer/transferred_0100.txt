Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have been predominantly driven by the scaling of computational resources during inference. For instance, models like OpenAI's o1 (OpenAI, 2024) and DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) generate extended reasoning processes that involve iterative "thinking" steps, including the formulation of intermediate hypotheses, derivations, and error corrections before producing a final output. This paradigm of enhanced inference-time reasoning has emerged as a prevailing approach, as it often leads to improvements in both accuracy and problem-solving efficacy. However, merely extending the reasoning process does not invariably enhance model performance and may, in certain cases, lead to detrimental effects such as overanalysis or overfitting. Empirical findings suggest that accurate responses are frequently associated with more concise reasoning trajectories. Therefore, an alternative approach involves executing multiple reasoning pathways in parallel and aggregating their outputs, often by selecting the most frequent response when the task permits a definitive solution. This approach is particularly effective for structured tasks such as mathematical problems or multiple-choice questions. Nonetheless, numerous real-world tasks lack a singular correct solution. For instance, coding tools must generate functional programs, while research tools often require the integration of additional tools, the formulation of comprehensive plans, or the provision of extensive explanations. In scenarios where a singular definitive answer is not readily discernible, traditional methods of selecting the most frequent response prove inadequate, despite their efficacy in more straightforward tasks. This raises a pivotal inquiry: Is it feasible to harness the advantages of concurrent reasoning processes for tasks lacking a singular correct answer, without resorting to the selection of the most prevalent outcome? In this study, we introduce THINKMERGE, an inference-time framework that synthesizes the outputs of multiple reasoning pathways to generate a singular, high-quality response. Unlike previous approaches, recent methodologies have demonstrated a significant shift in the paradigm of computational models. This transition is characterized by the incorporation of advanced algorithms that enhance the model's ability to generate comprehensive analytical segments. These segments, akin to the "think" phases observed in contemporary models such as OpenAI's o1 (OpenAI, 2024) and DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025), facilitate the formulation of intermediate hypotheses, derivations, and self-corrections. Consequently, this evolution in computational strategies underscores the necessity for continuous exploration of the multifaceted avenues presented within this discourse.

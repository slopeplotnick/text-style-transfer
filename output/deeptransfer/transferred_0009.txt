(1) Synchronization. We evaluate the frame-level temporal correspondence between the source and edited videos using four correlation-based metrics: speech, gaze, blink, and pose. Each metric captures a distinct aspect of motion dynamics: mouth movements for speech, eye trajectory for gaze, eye closure frequency for blinking, and body movements for pose. While these metrics are identical to those used for filtering our training data (subsection 3.2), they are exclusively employed for dataset curation and final evaluation. Importantly, they are not incorporated into the model's training objective or used as a loss function. (2) Edit Fidelity. To assess whether the intended edits are accurately applied and remain consistent throughout the video, we utilize two complementary CLIP-based metrics. Directional CLIP Score. Originally introduced for image editing, we extend the Directional CLIP metric to the video domain to evaluate how consistently the per-frame visual transformations align with the overall edit direction within CLIP's embedding space. This metric comprises two variants: an image-based version that measures alignment with the visual transformations implied by the first edited frame, and a text-dual version that evaluates alignment with the semantic modifications described by the text prompt. The text-dual variant is particularly relevant for methods like LucyEdit, which rely solely on textual guidance without access to the edited first frame. Intuitively, the image-based variant examines whether the transformation from source frames to edited frames resembles the change between the unedited and edited first frames, while the text-dual variant assesses whether the same transformation aligns with the edit direction suggested by the text prompt. CLIP-Text Alignment Score. To further evaluate semantic fidelity, we measure how well each generated frame adheres to the textual description within CLIP space. This score ensures that the video consistently reflects the intended semantics throughout its duration, complementing the Directional CLIP Score by capturing the accuracy of the prompt semantics over time.

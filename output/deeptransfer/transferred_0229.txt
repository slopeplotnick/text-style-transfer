In this paper, we introduce a method for constructing knowledge graphs (KGs) from images using multimodal AI models guided by domain-specific ontologies. These ontologies, which define the salient objects, attributes, and relationships pertinent to the domain, are embedded within the prompt to ensure the AI model focuses on the relevant aspects of the image. When an image is provided, the model generates a comprehensive list of key entities, their attributes, and interrelations, adhering to the ontological framework. Consequently, these enumerated lists are transformed into a graph that elucidates the contents of the image. The configuration of the graph is entirely contingent upon the predefined rules established within the ontology framework. Each image produces its own unique graph, which is stored in a database for further analysis and retrieval. However, despite each image possessing its distinct graph, certain elements within these graphs exhibit uniformity due to the application of a standardized set of terminologies. Therefore, these individual graphs are interconnected, culminating in the formation of a comprehensive graph. This overarching graph facilitates the identification of similarities among images through the comparative analysis of their constituent subgraphs. To elucidate the content of an image, we employ artificial intelligence to extract salient features and convert them into a structured enumeration. These features pertain to overarching concepts within the image, and domain-specific ontological rules ensure uniformity in the description of all images. To integrate these rules into the AI's operational framework, they are embedded within the procedural instructions during the AI's analytical processes. This approach enables the generation of distinct graphs for each image, which are subsequently synthesized into a comprehensive, unified graph. Moreover, the individual graphs corresponding to each image are interconnected through shared attributes, thereby enhancing the comparative analysis across the entire image dataset.

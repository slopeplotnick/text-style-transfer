We propose a method to construct knowledge graphs (KGs) from images using multimodal AI models guided by domain-specific ontologies. The ontology, which defines key objects, attributes, and relationships, is embedded in the prompt to ensure the model focuses on relevant details within the image. When provided with an image, the model generates structured lists of salient entities, their attributes, and interrelations, all aligned with the predefined ontological framework. These lists are then transformed into interpretable graphs that encapsulate the semantic content of the images. Importantly, the structure of each graph is entirely dictated by the ontology, which establishes the rules for its formation. Each image produces a distinct graph, which is stored in a dedicated graph database. However, due to the constrained vocabulary defined by the ontology, many components of these graphs are shared across images. Consequently, the individual graphs are interconnected, forming a unified, comprehensive graph structure. This integrated graph facilitates semantic comparison by enabling the analysis of shared and distinct subgraph components across images. To extract the semantic features of an image, we employ multimodal AI models to generate structured representations of high-level concepts. These features are guided by domain-specific ontologies, ensuring consistency and uniformity in the description of all images. To operationalize this process, we embed the ontology into the model’s instructions during inference. This ensures that each image is represented by a distinct graph, which is subsequently integrated into the unified global graph. The individual graphs are treated as subgraphs, interconnected through shared attributes, thereby enabling robust semantic comparison across the entire dataset.

We present a unified framework for optimizing system prompts to enhance the performance of multilingual language models. Our experiments indicate that specific prompt components enhance model performance across various languages, and multilingual reward models can be effectively employed to identify these optimal prompts. Analyzing the reasoning patterns reveals that superior prompts induce more structured and consistent behavior, underscoring prompt optimization as a scalable approach to enhancing the robustness of multilingual language models. Although we identified several high-quality prompts using SPRIG, the challenge persists in discovering prompts that consistently perform well across all four evaluation metrics. Our experimental results indicate that effective prompts can direct models towards specific reasoning patterns, thereby enhancing performance. However, the underlying mechanisms responsible for this improvement remain unexplored, and elucidating these mechanisms is a subject for future investigation. Despite efforts to mitigate ethical challenges in this research, several concerns persist. Firstly, the process of prompt optimization and the execution of subsequent experiments necessitate substantial computational resources, resulting in considerable energy consumption and a significant carbon dioxide footprint. Secondly, prompt optimization may inadvertently exacerbate existing biases within the underlying corpus, such as when a prompt induces a model to emulate the behavior of a specific archetype, such as a professor. Given that our dataset encompasses elements such as roles and instructions, it is imperative to ensure that these components neither introduce nor exacerbate harmful biases.

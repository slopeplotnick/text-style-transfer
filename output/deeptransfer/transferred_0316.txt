Despite identifying several high-quality prompts using SPRIG, consistently discovering prompts that perform effectively across all four evaluation metrics remains challenging. Our experiments indicate that effective prompts can direct models towards specific reasoning patterns, thereby enhancing their performance. Despite efforts to mitigate ethical concerns within this research, several issues persist. Firstly, the process of prompt optimization and subsequent experimental procedures necessitates substantial computational resources, resulting in elevated energy consumption and a considerable carbon dioxide footprint. Secondly, prompt optimization carries the risk of inadvertently amplifying biases inherent in the underlying corpus, such as systematic behavioral shifts induced by prompting a language model to emulate specific archetypes, for instance, a "professor." Given that our dataset encompasses elements such as roles and instructional directives, it is imperative to ensure that these components neither introduce nor exacerbate harmful biases.

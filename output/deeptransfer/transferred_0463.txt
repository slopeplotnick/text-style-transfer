Large language models (LLMs) demonstrate remarkable versatility in addressing a diverse array of tasks. They can be adapted to specific tasks through in-context learning, where textual instructions and, optionally, few-shot examples are provided as input. The formulation, quality, and structure of these prompts significantly influence the efficacy of the model's outputs. This underscores the significance of crafting precise and well-structured instructions, akin to the manner in which hyperparameter tuning enhances performance in machine learning. However, the manual development of effective prompts can be challenging and not consistently dependable. Similar to how AutoML facilitates the automated exploration of optimal hyperparameter configurations, automatic prompt optimization has emerged as a systematic approach to identifying optimal instructions for language models. This development is particularly salient in the current landscape, where multi-agent systems have gained prominence across both industrial and research domains. Given the high costs and extensive data requirements associated with retraining or fine-tuning entire models, automatic prompt optimization offers a more efficient and practical alternative, particularly for models with restricted interpretability. Numerous prompt optimizers are available; however, their utilization can be complex. Each optimizer resides within its distinct research repository, necessitating engagement with diverse codebases and requirements when attempting to evaluate multiple optimizers. Moreover, these repositories are frequently not actively maintained, and the research code often lacks comprehensive software testing and adequate documentation. Additionally, these tools lack flexibility, rendering them challenging to employ for tasks beyond basic applications. While certain solutions exist to address these limitations, they are often proprietary and closed-source, restricted to a singular optimizer, or excessively intricate for routine utilization.

Overall, these findings demonstrate that reasoning-based safety alignment not only improves the performance of omnimodal guardrails across various input modalities but also enhances understanding and generalization in complex cross-modal safety scenarios where label-only supervision falls short. To evaluate whether safety knowledge learned in seen modalities can generalize to unseen ones, we conducted cross-modal training and evaluation. Specifically, for each experimental split, OMNIGUARD was trained using data from three modalities for training and seen modality evaluation, while one modality was excluded to assess performance on the unseen modality. The averaged F1 scores and accuracy metrics across all four seen and unseen modality evaluations are presented in Table 5. From these experiments, we derive two key insights. (1) Strong cross-modal transfer is observed across all four modalities. The performance on unseen modalities remains close to that of seen modalities (79.4 vs. 81.8 F1 on average), indicating that OMNIGUARD effectively learns modality-invariant safety representations. This demonstrates that harmful semantic patterns can be aligned and identified across diverse input types, including text, image, video, and audio. Moreover, OMNIGUARD acquires generalizable safety reasoning capabilities across modalities. (2) A notable exception arises in the audio modality, where the F1 score for the seen modality (64.6%) is slightly lower than that for the unseen modality (65.1%). This discrepancy is attributed to the OMNIGUARD variant trained without text exhibiting reduced performance on the WildGuard-TTS benchmark, an auditory dataset created by converting text samples into audio using a text-to-speech model. The absence of textual data during training hindered the model’s ability to process audio effectively, highlighting how semantically equivalent content across modalities, such as text and speech, can interact and influence safety alignment. This further underscores the potential for knowledge learned in one modality to transfer and apply to another that conveys equivalent semantic content.

In this study, we evaluated seven methodologies for model combination: Linear, Karcher Mean, SLERP, NuSLERP, TIES, DELLA, and Nearswap. These approaches were employed to integrate a bias-inverted model with a pre-trained large language model. Notably, the Linear method, initially proposed by Wortsman et al. (2022), represents the most straightforward approach. It involves the simple aggregation and averaging of the weights from fine-tuned models, offering a cost-effective and efficient solution. In contrast, the Karcher Mean, introduced by Goddard et al. (2024), employs an alternative methodology by accounting for the non-Euclidean geometry of model parameter spaces, thereby facilitating operations in non-flat manifolds. Similarly, the Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) technique, also proposed by Goddard in 2024, interpolates model weights along a geodesic path on a spherical manifold, thus preserving the intrinsic geometric properties of the parameter space. Furthermore, NuSLERP, another innovation by Goddard (2024), adapts SLERP by enabling differential mixing of model components to prioritize critical regions, thereby enhancing balance and overall performance. Likewise, TIES, proposed by Yadav (2023), integrates models by identifying task-relevant parameter variations, effectively mitigating interference while preserving the unique strengths of each model. Additionally, DELLA, developed by Deep (2024), addresses interference by selectively pruning less significant parameter updates through an adaptive magnitude-aware rescaling mechanism, which facilitates the retention of essential weights and sustains the original model's performance.

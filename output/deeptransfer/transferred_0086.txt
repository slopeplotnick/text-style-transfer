In our study, we explored seven methodologies for model amalgamation: Linear, Karcher Mean, SLERP, NuSLERP, TIES, DELLA, and Nearswap. These techniques were employed to integrate a bias-inverted model with a pre-trained large language model. Notably, the Linear approach, as proposed by Wortsman et al. (2022), is recognized for its simplicity and involves the straightforward addition and averaging of the weights of fine-tuned models, offering a cost-effective yet effective solution. In contrast, the Karcher Mean, introduced by Goddard et al. (2024), adopts a unique methodology by considering the non-Euclidean geometry of the model parameter space, thus facilitating operations in non-flat spaces. Similarly, SLERP, also proposed by Goddard in 2024, interpolates model weights along a geodesic path on a spherical manifold, thereby preserving the intrinsic geometry of the parameter space. Furthermore, NuSLERP, introduced by Goddard (2024), extends the SLERP methodology by enabling differential mixing across various components of the model, thereby prioritizing critical regions to achieve improved balance and performance. Likewise, TIES, proposed by Yadav (2023), integrates models by analyzing task-specific parameter differences, effectively mitigating interference while preserving the unique strengths of each model. Moreover, DELLA, developed by Deep (2024), minimizes interference by selectively pruning less significant parameter updates through an adaptive magnitude-aware rescaling mechanism, which enhances the retention of essential weights and maintains the original model's performance.

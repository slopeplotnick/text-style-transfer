In this context, we begin by examining the limitations of this recent approach [16]. As shown in Figure 1(a), the model initially attends to multimodal inputs, including audio and visual cues; however, as the reasoning process progresses, its focus increasingly shifts to the text it has generated. This shift causes the model to anchor its reasoning on the emotional cues present in the initial segment of generated text. Typically, the reasoning process begins with generating a video description, as the training data are organized in a fixed sequence where visual descriptions precede audio descriptions and subsequent reasoning. However, this "visual-first" reasoning approach may not always align with the primary emotional driver, particularly in cases where emotion is conveyed predominantly through vocal tone or semantic content. When the initial visual-based reasoning proves irrelevant or inaccurate, the subsequent reasoning becomes increasingly misaligned, as illustrated in Figure 1(b). Consequently, the initial step of reasoning becomes disproportionately influential. Similar challenges arise in other domains, where early cues misdirect reasoning away from the foundational evidence [17]. To address this issue, we propose Modality-Importance-Guided Reasoning (MIGR), a method designed to initiate reasoning from the emotion-dominant modality—namely, the modality most strongly associated with the target emotion—and to maintain emotion-coherent reasoning throughout the inference process. To achieve this, we introduce Modality Importance (MI) estimation, which identifies the most critical modality for accurate emotion recognition by evaluating the model’s predictive performance under audio-only, visual-only, and audio-visual inputs. For example, if the model accurately predicts the emotion using audio-only input but fails when visual information is included, this suggests that visual cues may be irrelevant or even misleading, and the auditory modality should therefore be prioritized. Building on this MI estimation and leveraging recent reasoning-based training paradigms for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) [15, 16], we propose a two-stage learning framework: (i) modality-aligned Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and (ii) modality-aligned reward optimization. In the modality-aligned SFT stage, we utilize MI to decompose the reasoning text into modality-specific segments and systematically reorganize these segments to align with the dominant modality.

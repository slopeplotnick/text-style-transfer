Hate speech recognition in low-resource languages remains a challenging problem due to insufficient datasets, orthographic complexity, and linguistic diversity. Bangla, spoken by more than 230 million people in Bangladesh and the Indian state of West Bengal, exemplifies these challenges. Despite the growing need for automated moderation on social media platforms, Bangla is significantly underrepresented in computational resources. In this work, we study Subtask 1A and Subtask 1B of the BLP 2025 Shared Task on hate speech detection. We reproduce the official baselines, such as Majority, Random, and Support Vector Machine, and also consider additional baseline methods like Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree. Moreover, we utilize transformer-based models, including DistilBERT, BanglaBERT, m-BERT, and XLM-RoBERTa, for hate speech classification. All the transformer-based models outperformed the baseline methods for the subtasks, except for DistilBERT. Notably, BanglaBERT achieved the best performance for both subtasks. Despite its smaller size, BanglaBERT outperformed both m-BERT and XLM-RoBERTa, underscoring the importance of language-specific pre-training. Our results highlight the potential and need for pretrained language models tailored to the low-resource Bangla language. Over the past few decades, the widespread usage of the internet has grown significantly, and social media platforms have become much more popular worldwide. Improved internet access allows people to express their thoughts and share information online. The use of social media has been rising rapidly and does not appear to be slowing down. While social media has become integral to public discourse, it also facilitates the spread of hatred. The rapid increase in toxic and harmful content, such as hate speech, raises substantial safety concerns on these online platforms. Hate speech, defined as harmful, offensive, or derogatory language targeting individuals or groups, necessitates automatic detection as a critical task within Natural Language Processing (NLP). Although there has been significant development in hate speech detection for high-resource languages like English, progress in low-resource languages such as Bangla remains limited. This limitation is due to the scarcity of high-quality annotated datasets and the complex linguistic properties of the language.

In phrases like "pearl white" or "ivory white," the core need is a specific shade of white, not the attributes of "pearl" or "ivory" themselves. Similarly, in expressions such as "phone gas station," the underlying need is for a power bank, with "gas station" functioning metaphorically. In these instances, it is crucial to grasp the higher-level semantic intent, as relying solely on literal interpretation is inadequate. Domain-specific knowledge is often required, particularly when dealing with niche attributes that cannot be inferred from surface forms alone. For example, in specialized sectors like e-commerce, individuals might use technical jargon, brand nomenclature, or specific descriptors, akin to "mouthfeel" terminology in tea or wine descriptions. These nuances demand an understanding of structured domain knowledge. Moreover, challenges arise from misspellings, aliases, or slang expressions. To address these, it is essential to integrate both general and domain-specific knowledge, ensuring the system is adept at spelling correction, variant normalization, and alias alignment. In terms of intent recognition, it is vital to discern whether the query pertains to acquiring a product or seeking related information, such as reviews or usage guides. This requires distinguishing the main targets from modifiers or constraints. For example, in "charger/headphones for iPhone 17," the primary objective is to acquire a charger or headphones, while "iPhone 17" specifies compatibility requirements. Furthermore, abstract needs may not point to specific items but rather to categories, such as "phones with the best battery life." Here, the system must accurately interpret the semantic significance of "battery life" within the mobile phone context and implement ranking or filtering mechanisms. Finally, users often omit comprehensive details in their inquiries, necessitating the use of dialogue history, user profiles, or default settings to effectively address these omissions.

Through a systematic empirical study, we aim to bridge the gap between Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV). We begin by introducing the design of our study and the experimental setup. Next, we present a detailed investigation of three core research questions, focusing on the comparative performance, complementary strengths, and integration potential of these two paradigms. To systematically explore the relationship between HD and FV, we conduct a large-scale comparative analysis within a unified framework. Our study is structured to address the following research questions (RQs): - RQ1: A Head-to-Head Performance Comparison. How do state-of-the-art (SOTA) HD and FV methods perform when evaluated on identical AI-generated content? Are there consistent winners? - RQ2: Uncovering Complementary Strengths. Do these paradigms exhibit unique, complementary strengths in detecting factual inaccuracies, or do their capabilities largely overlap? - RQ3: Performance of Hybrid Methods. Can a more robust and accurate system be developed by integrating both paradigms, thereby surpassing the performance of each individual approach? To answer these questions, we organize our study into three progressive phases. First, to address RQ1, we implement a range of prominent and SOTA methods from both paradigms and perform a head-to-head comparison on a unified testbed, marking the first direct evaluation of their relative effectiveness. Second, building on these findings, we quantitatively assess the synergy between the two paradigms to answer RQ2. Our results demonstrate that integrating HD and FV achieves significantly better performance than combining methods within a single paradigm. Moreover, we conduct a detailed case study to provide qualitative insights into the interplay between these methodologies in the context of RQ2. Finally, motivated by the strong evidence of synergy, we address RQ3 by designing and evaluating two simple yet effective hybrid methods. Specifically, we explore fusion at both the final probability score level and the system pipeline level, showing that even basic integration can substantially enhance overall performance.

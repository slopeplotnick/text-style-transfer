The language acquisition experiments are designed to incorporate explicit grammatical knowledge into a lexicon. Consequently, the daughter agent can utilize this acquired knowledge to engage in dialogue with the mother. To achieve this, the system employs statistical machine learning techniques, resulting in an explicitly defined grammatical representation. This study enhances existing systems grounded in statistical methods and neural networks by introducing a more interpretable, rule-based framework for language modeling. By applying Zipf's law (Mandelbrot 1954; Zipf 1949), the research evaluates the robustness and feasibility of implementing a multi-agent simulation for first language acquisition. Importantly, the objective of this study is not solely to perform a binary classification of function and content words; rather, this classification serves as a methodological instrument for assessing the validity of this innovative approach to modeling first language acquisition. The advantage of using Zipf's law is that it provides a robust and dependable framework for assessing the performance of the system. This approach is a well-established practice. For instance, Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) employ Zipf's law to develop a multi-agent framework for modeling language evolution. Recent investigations by Dębowski (2021), Takahashi and Tanaka-Ishii (2019), and Takamichi et al. (2024) further contribute to this area of study. Crucially, the experiments conducted in this study substantiate all requisite components essential for the development of a multi-agent model of first language acquisition. Specifically, the acquisition of function and content words is facilitated through a hybrid language acquisition strategy, which is predicated on statistical tendencies that aid in the assimilation of discrete grammatical rules. Therefore, these findings endorse the modeling of more intricate grammatical phenomena and demonstrate the viability of the MODOMA research agenda. Building upon these findings, the study conducted by Shakouri et al. (2025) elucidates the acquisition of grammatical categories, such as nouns, verbs, and prepositions, through unsupervised methodologies. This paper delineates two experiments that exemplify this approach: the initial simulation, detailed in Sections 6 and 7, was implemented on a training dataset generated by the mother agent. These experiments were conducted to ascertain the parameter settings for the acquisition of this linguistic feature. The second experiment, utilizing a novel test data set generated by the adult language model, served to validate this configuration, as detailed in Section 8. The findings indicate that the MODOMA system is capable of acquiring these grammatical categories and representing them through the grammar formalism of the daughter agent.

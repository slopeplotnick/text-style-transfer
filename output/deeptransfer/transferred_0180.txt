The language acquisition experiments are designed to integrate explicit grammatical knowledge into a lexicon, enabling the daughter agent to engage in conversation with the mother. To achieve this, the system employs statistical machine learning techniques, which result in an explicitly defined grammatical representation. This approach enhances existing systems that rely on statistical methods and neural networks by introducing a more interpretable, rule-based framework for language modeling. Furthermore, by applying the principle of Zipf's law (Mandelbrot 1954; Zipf 1949), this study evaluates the robustness and feasibility of implementing a multi-agent simulation for first language acquisition. Importantly, the primary aim of this investigation is not merely to classify function and content words, but rather to use this classification as a methodological tool for assessing the validity of this innovative approach to modeling first language acquisition. The application of Zipf's law provides a reliable and well-established framework for evaluating the system's performance. For instance, Chaabouni et al. (2019) and Rita et al. (2020) utilize Zipf's law to develop multi-agent frameworks for modeling language evolution, complemented by recent studies by Dębowski (2021), Takahashi and Tanaka-Ishii (2019), and Takamichi et al. (2024). Moreover, the experiments conducted in this study validate all components necessary for establishing a multi-agent model of first language acquisition. Specifically, the acquisition of function and content words is achieved through a hybrid language acquisition strategy, which leverages statistical tendencies to facilitate the learning of discrete grammatical rules. Consequently, these findings support the modeling of more complex grammatical phenomena and underscore the viability of the MODOMA research agenda. Building on these findings, Shakouri et al. (2025) demonstrate how grammatical categories such as nouns, verbs, and prepositions can be acquired using unsupervised techniques. This paper presents two experiments to illustrate this methodology: the first simulation, detailed in Sections 6 and 7, was applied to a training dataset generated by the mother agent to determine the parameter settings for acquiring this linguistic feature. The second experiment, using a novel test dataset generated by the adult language model, was conducted to validate this configuration, as described in Section 8. These results indicate that the MODOMA system is capable of acquiring grammatical categories and representing them through the grammatical framework of the daughter agent.

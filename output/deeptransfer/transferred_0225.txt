Recent advancements in the development of large language models (LLMs) have heightened expectations for their ability to generate accurate, reliable, and truthful information. However, emerging evidence suggests that iterative reasoning processes may reinforce pre-existing beliefs rather than promote truth-seeking behavior. In this study, we propose a systematic framework to evaluate belief entrenchment in LLM reasoning by utilizing the Martingale property from Bayesian statistics. This concept implies that, under rational belief updating, the expected value of future beliefs should remain consistent with the current belief, thereby precluding the prediction of future beliefs based solely on present beliefs. To measure violations of this principle, we have developed the Martingale Score, an unsupervised, regression-based metric that indicates a failure in the model's Bayesian updating capabilities. We observed that across various domains, such as event forecasting, subjective inquiries, and academic paper evaluation, models frequently exhibit a tendency to adhere to their existing beliefs, a phenomenon we term belief entrenchment. Through comprehensive evaluations, we identify the models, reasoning paradigms, and domains most susceptible to belief entrenchment. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the Martingale Score effectively predicts model accuracy in contexts where ground truth labels are available. This finding underscores the utility of the Martingale Score as a process-based metric for reasoning evaluation, even in scenarios where outcome-based metrics are traditionally employed to assess performance. Consider an individual who persistently refuses to acknowledge their errors or a monarch who exclusively heeds the counsel of sycophants. Such individuals frequently exhibit a tendency to adhere to pre-existing beliefs, a phenomenon that may arise from selective exposure to information that corroborates their viewpoints or due to external influences.

Existing video model evaluations predominantly focus on basic physical knowledge, restricting assessments to everyday scenarios that require only a rudimentary understanding of physical principles. To address this limitation, we introduce VideoScience-Bench, a benchmark designed to evaluate the scientific understanding and reasoning capabilities of video models. Each test instance incorporates multiple scientific concepts and necessitates undergraduate-level expertise, covering 103 concepts across physics and chemistry. The benchmark assesses whether video models can act as zero-shot scientific reasoners, capable of understanding complex experimental setups and predicting expected phenomena. Performance is measured across five critical dimensions: Prompt Consistency, Expected Phenomenon, Dynamism, Immutability, and Coherence. Furthermore, we propose VideoScience-Judge, an automated, checklist-based evaluation framework that systematically assesses model performance. VideoScience-Judge generates a prompt-specific checklist to guide the evaluation process, selects key frames to identify causally significant moments, and integrates computer vision tools to detect physical cues such as motion and object interactions. These components collectively support the scoring mechanism, with the resulting scores aggregated into a single weighted metric that reflects the model’s reasoning proficiency. Empirical validation demonstrates that the automated evaluations produced by VideoScience-Judge align significantly better with domain-expert annotations compared to benchmarks focused on physical commonsense reasoning or visual fidelity. In summary, this study investigates the ability of video models to reason over multiple interacting scientific concepts. We make three key contributions: 1. We introduce VideoScience-Bench, a high-quality benchmark targeting zero-shot scientific reasoning in video models, leveraging real-world phenomena across 103 physics and chemistry concepts. 2. We develop VideoScience-Judge, a checklist-based evaluation framework that employs VLM-as-a-Judge, integrates key-frame selection, and utilizes computer vision tools to detect physical cues, providing quantitative assessments of scientific validity and perceptual quality. 3. We conduct a comprehensive empirical study using VideoScience-Bench. While current video models generate high-quality, photorealistic, and temporally coherent videos, VideoScience-Bench remains a significant challenge. Many models struggle to accurately represent complex scientific phenomena and frequently violate fundamental physical principles. Among the evaluated models, Sora-2 and Veo-3 exhibit the best performance and demonstrate early signs of scientific reasoning.

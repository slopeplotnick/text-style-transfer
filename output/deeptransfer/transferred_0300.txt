In light of the growing significance of the world model as a shared computational foundation for both the brain and artificial intelligence, we focus on world-model-based processing to offer a comprehensive cross-domain comparison of their circuit computations. Consequently, we have identified a convergent evolution between the brain and modern AI, particularly evident in large-scale language models. This observation has led to the development of a new theory regarding macro-scale information processing mechanisms in the brain, which corresponds to deep-layered local circuit computations. Specifically, the neocortex and cerebellum acquire world models by predicting future states and learning from prediction errors, utilizing these predictive models for a unified processing system across domains—understanding in sensory processing and generation in motor processing. This theory represents an integrated advancement of the internal model theory and the mirror neuron system, suggesting that world model-based circuit computation is essential for enabling diverse functions within uniform circuit architectures in both AI and the brain. The approach and insights presented in this perspective provide a promising foundation for future research bridging neuroscience and AI, with the aim of understanding human-like general intelligence. Furthermore, the cerebellum functions as a predictive model by assimilating external information, forecasting subsequent events, and refining its predictions through error correction. Empirical studies indicate that the cerebellum predominantly learns to anticipate forthcoming sensory inputs rather than processing higher-order signals.

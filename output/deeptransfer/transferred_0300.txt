Considering the growing importance of the world model and its potential as a shared computational basis for both the brain and AI, we adopt the perspective of world-model-based processing to provide a comprehensive cross-domain comparison of their circuit computations. Consequently, we identified a convergent evolution between the brain and modern AI, exemplified by large-scale language models. Building on this finding, we developed a new theory of macro-scale information processing mechanisms in the brain, corresponding to deep-layered local circuit computations. Specifically, the neocortex and cerebellum acquire world models by predicting future states and learning from prediction errors, utilizing these predictive world models for a unified processing system across domains—understanding in sensory processing and generation in motor processing. This theory represents an integrated advancement of the internal model theory and the mirror neuron system, indicating that world-model-based circuit computation is crucial for enabling diverse functions within uniform circuit architectures in both AI and the brain. Moreover, the approach and insights presented in this Perspective provide a promising foundation for future research bridging neuroscience and AI, with the aim of understanding human-like general intelligence. The cerebellum, in particular, functions as a predictor, receiving inputs from the external world, generating predictions of future inputs, and learning from prediction errors. Accumulating evidence indicates that the learning target in the cerebellum is the future sensory input itself, rather than a high-level supervisory signal.

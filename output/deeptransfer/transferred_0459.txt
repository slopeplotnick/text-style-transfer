Future Research Directions and Advancements: The issue of hallucinations in large language models (LLMs) remains a significant concern, offering numerous avenues for future research. One promising direction involves the development of real-time systems for detecting and, more importantly, correcting hallucinations during the model's inference rather than addressing them afterward. This approach could significantly improve the reliability of LLMs, particularly in high-stakes applications such as legal or medical documentation, where inaccuracies could lead to harmful or even catastrophic consequences. Moreover, ongoing research is exploring multi-modal detection methods, which aim to incorporate diverse data types—such as images, audio, and structured data—alongside text to enhance model performance. This is especially critical given the widespread use of LLMs in multi-modal contexts, where the integration of various media types often introduces risks of hallucinations. Additionally, user-centered methodologies are gaining traction, focusing on equipping models with features that enable users to efficiently identify, rectify, and mitigate errors. Studies are increasingly emphasizing the need for interactive and transparent AI systems that allow users to query and verify model outputs, fostering greater trust and usability. Furthermore, there is a pressing need for research into formal methods for hallucination mitigation, which promise to provide reliability guarantees essential in scenarios demanding stringent accuracy. This article has reviewed prevalent hallucinations in LLMs, categorizing them into subclasses such as factual inaccuracies or logical inconsistencies arising from insufficient information, ambiguous input, or model limitations. These errors undermine user trust and system usability, underscoring the importance of developing effective mitigation strategies.

Future Research Directions and Advancements: The phenomenon of hallucinations in LLMs remains a pressing concern, presenting numerous opportunities for future research. One promising direction involves the development of real-time systems for hallucination detection, which would not only identify but also correct erroneous outputs during the model's inference, rather than relying on post hoc adjustments. Such advancements could significantly enhance the reliability of LLMs, particularly in high-risk applications such as legal or medical documentation, where inaccuracies could lead to harmful or even catastrophic consequences. Moreover, multi-modal hallucination detection is another area of interest, where models are designed to process diverse data types—such as text, images, audio, or structured data—to improve performance. This is especially relevant given the widespread use of LLMs in multi-modal contexts, where the integration of various media forms can sometimes result in inaccuracies. Additionally, user-centered approaches are gaining traction, with models now incorporating features that enable users to easily identify, rectify, or eliminate hallucinations. Research into interactive and transparent AI systems, which allow users to engage with the model by posing questions and verifying its outputs, represents a compelling avenue for exploration. Furthermore, there is a critical need for studies focused on mitigating hallucinations through formal methods, as these approaches promise to provide reliability guarantees, particularly in scenarios requiring stringent accuracy. This article has reviewed common hallucinations in LLMs, categorizing them into subclasses such as factual distortions or logical inconsistencies stemming from limited information, ambiguous input requests, or inherent model constraints. These errors undermine user trust and system usability, underscoring the urgent need for strategies aimed at their reduction.

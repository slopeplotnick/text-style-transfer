The traditional approach involves performing a deterministic inversion first, followed by making a deterministic interpretation, as shown in Figure 2. Subsequently, an expert geologist interprets the deterministic inversion in terms of shapes, such as intrusions relevant to exploration. These interpretations are conducted using software that focuses on a single deterministic model. In this approach, geophysical inversion precedes geological interpretation. While one might argue that regularization terms help impose geological knowledge, this argument is vague and very limited. Essentially, this approach results in a redundant application of the same geological knowledge: first in the regularization term, and then again in the interpretation. Consequently, it becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy fraught with circular reasoning. However, in the Bayesian framework, these issues are addressed together as a single problem. Bayesian methodology requires stating plausible solutions or geometries before performing any inversion. Bayesian inversion offers relatively simple solutions that can be easily implemented and are generally applicable. Let us revisit the scenario depicted in Figure 2 and discuss how a prior probability model can be designed. We begin by asking: what is the minimum amount of information we have about the subsurface before acquiring any data? Initially, it is known that petrophysical properties have certain ranges, and with precise identification of lithologies, these ranges can be further refined. Moreover, the spatial variation of these properties is neither random nor constant over large areas. Indeed, the formation of deposits induces specific variations in these properties. Conceptually, the minimum information comprises constraints on histograms and the presence of spatial correlation. Mathematically, this can be translated into a spatial covariance model (referred to as a variogram in geostatistics), although the parameters of this variogram, such as the mean, ranges, and anisotropy, remain uncertain and could vary widely (except being random).

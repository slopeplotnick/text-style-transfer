Overall, the most proficient models, such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o, exhibit minimal error rates, performing on par with or surpassing human capabilities (left panel, Fig. 8). With the exception of Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, there is a discernible trend in Figure 8 indicating that models with higher accuracy tend to demonstrate stronger Bayesian behavior. However, this correlation between accuracy and Bayesian behavior is not universally applicable. For instance, a model that achieves perfect predictive accuracy may appear simplistic and fail to exhibit probabilistic behavior. When the context is modified by introducing a range of prior observations, the behavior of large language models (LLMs) demonstrates significant variability. As illustrated in the top-right panel of Figure 6, their sequential performance declines markedly, particularly when presented with a biased range of inputs. This is further evidenced in the left panel of Figure 6, where error rates increase, as indicated by the higher positioning of triangles compared to circles. This observation underscores the tendency of LLMs to rely heavily on the information provided to them. Moreover, as illustrated in the middle panel of Figure 8, high performance in terms of NRMSE does not necessarily correspond to efficient cue integration. For example, while GPT-5 Mini achieves notably low NRMSE, it demonstrates suboptimal performance in combining cues effectively. This phenomenon is evident in the maze distance estimation task, wherein GPT-5 Mini demonstrates superior performance with text input compared to image input. Consequently, a Bayes-optimal approach necessitates a substantial downweighting of the image input, although the model struggles to achieve this adequately (refer to Appendix A.12 for further details). Conversely, in the line length ratio task (illustrated in the bottom right of Figure 6), numerous large language models exhibit the ability to effectively downweight image input in the presence of noise, thereby indicating their capacity for Bayesian adaptation.

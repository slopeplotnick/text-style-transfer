User-Centered Evaluations: Because hallucinations significantly impact the user's trust in the system, understanding how users experience these errors becomes crucial. A study provides an evaluation of hallucinations within language models, where users actively engage in detecting and reporting these inaccuracies. This approach introduces new user-oriented metrics that consider the impact of hallucinations, including the loss of trust in the system and the burden of self-verification, which ideally should be managed by the system itself. Evaluation of Hallucinations: A More Inclusive Framework for Hallucination Evaluation: Hallucinations and their effects are assessed and controlled using a variety of measures that contribute to the development of more integrated frameworks. Another study illustrates that performance evaluations of large language models (LLMs) can be conducted comprehensively by employing several techniques, including factuality checks, consistency analysis, and user experience evaluations. This broad-based approach aids in identifying hallucinations from multiple perspectives, examining not only the accuracy of facts but also the coherence of arguments and user perceptions. Applications of LLMs in Different Scenarios: While it is essential to utilize LLMs in applications requiring task comprehension, it is also crucial to be aware of their propensity for hallucinations. For general tasks such as chatbots, models like GPT-4 or PaLM are commonly used due to their ability to generate fluent, human-like text with minimal risk of inaccuracies, while infusing a degree of creativity. However, for applications necessitating factual responses, such as information retrieval or question answering, models like BERT and T5 are preferred due to their emphasis on factual accuracy and robust comprehension capabilities.

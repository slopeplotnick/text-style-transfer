User-Centered Evaluations: Hallucinations can significantly undermine user trust in systems, making it crucial to consider how users experience these errors. A study provides an evaluation of hallucinations within language models, where users actively engage in detecting and reporting inaccuracies. This approach includes new user-oriented metrics that measure the impact of hallucinations, such as the erosion of trust and the burden of self-verification, which ideally should be managed by the system. Evaluation of Hallucinations: A More Inclusive Framework for Hallucination Evaluation: Hallucinations and their effects are assessed using a range of measures that contribute to more integrated frameworks. Another study demonstrates that performance evaluations of language models can be conducted comprehensively by employing various techniques, including factuality checks, consistency analysis, and user experience evaluations. This broad-based approach helps identify hallucinations from multiple perspectives, considering not only factual accuracy but also the coherence of arguments and user perceptions. Applications of LLMs in Different Scenarios: While deploying large language models across diverse scenarios, it is imperative to account for the specific requirements of the task and acknowledge the inherent risk of hallucinations. For general-purpose applications, such as conversational agents, models like GPT-4 or PaLM are frequently employed due to their ability to produce fluent, human-like text with minimal risk of factual inaccuracies, while incorporating a degree of creative expression. However, for tasks requiring factual accuracy, such as information retrieval or question answering, models like BERT and T5 demonstrate superior performance due to their emphasis on factual consistency and robust comprehension.

Empirical testing reveals that the sequence in which articles are presented to the model significantly influences the generated summaries. Specifically, these models exhibit a pronounced focus on the first article, thereby altering both the framing of the information and the semantic similarity of the summaries to the original articles. Previous research has identified a tendency for these models to prioritize earlier segments of extensive inputs, highlighting a position bias. Our findings extend this phenomenon to the multi-document setting, demonstrating a clear primacy effect in the model's information ranking process. Notably, this pattern is consistently demonstrated through the application of the BERTScore metric. This phenomenon can be attributed to the model's tendency to incorporate lexical choices and argumentative structures from the first article more prominently than from subsequent ones. Consequently, measures of similarity consistently yield higher scores for the initial article in the sequence. In contrast, metrics such as ROUGE-L, which primarily assess lexical overlap, exhibit this effect to a lesser extent, while SummaC, which evaluates factual consistency, does not account for framing or positional influence. Therefore, these methodologies do not detect the first-article bias as prominently as BERTScore does. Across all three evaluative perspectives, the first article is distinctly prioritized over the second and third articles, with no significant differentiation observed between the latter two. This indicates that the model inherently prioritizes the first article, not due to a diminishing interest in subsequent articles over time. The presence of such bias can manifest in practical applications, particularly in systems designed to synthesize information for end-users. For example, the AI-generated summaries in Google Search, utilizing Gemini models, may prioritize the initial articles processed, thereby marginalizing subsequent ones. Such prioritization can result in summaries that disproportionately represent specific perspectives, potentially influencing users' comprehension of the subject matter, especially when there is significant reliance on the AI's outputs.

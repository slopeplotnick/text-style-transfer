Inspired by representative agents like ReACT, we introduce an iterative reflection process into the PPTPilot, enabling it to gradually refine its predictions. The agent proposes edits to the XML files, which are temporarily rendered into a PPT file. Subsequently, a verifier model assesses the output PPT against the original instructions and provides feedback on any discrepancies. Consequently, the agent produces an updated PowerPoint presentation, correcting its own errors based on the feedback. Despite the simplicity of our design, we find it both effective and efficient for PowerPoint editing when compared to existing tools and frameworks. We hope that PPTPilot can serve as a foundational benchmark for further research in PowerPoint editing. To enhance the reliability of our evaluation, we employ leading vision-language models, specifically Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT 5. For clarity, the primary results presented in the main comparison (Table 3) are derived from the GPT 5 model; however, additional details regarding Gemini 2.5 Pro and other implementation specifics are provided in the supplementary materials. In our testing phase, we conducted a comparative analysis of PPTPilot against other PowerPoint tools, including ChatGPT in its extended thinking mode, Gemini-CLI, and GPT-Agent, which functions analogously to a computer and can utilize a desktop environment. Moreover, we evaluated MiniMax Agent, known for its proficiency in multimodal tasks and marketed as an effective PowerPoint tool, as well as PPTAgent and Poster2Agent. Due to budgetary constraints imposed by the strict usage limits and high costs associated with proprietary tools such as ChatGPT and MiniMax Agent, we evaluated a reduced subset of 25 samples. Our selection process prioritized the most challenging cases, specifically the 20 tasks where PPTPilot and ChatGPT exhibited the poorest performance, supplemented by five additional manually selected cases to ensure comprehensive coverage of diverse scenarios. Further details regarding these selected cases are provided in the supplementary materials.

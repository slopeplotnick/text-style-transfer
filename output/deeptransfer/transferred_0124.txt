We introduce an iterative, step-by-step process into the PPTPilot, enabling it to gradually enhance its predictive capabilities. The agent proposes modifications to the XML files, which are then rendered into a temporary PPT file. Subsequently, a verifier model evaluates the output PPT against the original instructions and provides feedback in case of discrepancies. Based on this feedback, the agent refines the PPT and corrects its errors. Despite the simplicity of our design, it proves both effective and efficient for PPT editing when compared to existing tools and frameworks. We hope that PPTPilot will serve as a foundational benchmark for advancing research in the domain of PPT editing. To ensure the reliability of our evaluation, we employ the most advanced vision-language models, specifically Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT 5. For clarity, the majority of the results presented in the main comparison (Table 3) are derived from the GPT 5 judge, while supplementary materials provide additional insights regarding Gemini 2.5 Pro and other implementation specifics. In our testing phase, we conducted a comparative analysis of PPTPilot against other PowerPoint tools, including ChatGPT in its extended thinking mode, Gemini-CLI, and GPT-Agent—which simulates a computer with desktop capabilities—as well as MiniMax Agent, renowned for its proficiency in multimodal tasks and marketed as an effective "PPT helper," along with PPTAgent and Poster2Agent. However, certain proprietary tools, such as ChatGPT and MiniMax Agent, impose stringent usage restrictions and incur substantial costs. Therefore, we conducted evaluations on a reduced subset of 25 samples to adhere to budgetary constraints. Our selection methodology prioritized the most challenging tasks, specifically those where PPTPilot and ChatGPT exhibited suboptimal performance, supplemented by a few additional cases to ensure comprehensive scenario coverage. Further elaboration on these cases is provided in the supplementary materials.

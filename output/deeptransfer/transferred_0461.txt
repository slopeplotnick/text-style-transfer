Future Work Due to resource constraints, we have not systematically studied belief entrenchment in reinforced reasoning, despite the recent popularity of this approach to LLM reasoning. Moreover, the violation of ideal Bayesian rationality can be demonstrated by both irrational engagement with internal reasoning processes and external evidence. In this study, we only focused on the former. Future research could include an "evidence searching" component to evaluate LLMs' capacity to update beliefs in response to new external evidence, as opposed to their propensity to fixate on prior beliefs. However, experimenters must ensure that evidence searching does not give LLMs direct access to ground truth, if such ground truth exists. In domains where ground truth is established, such as forecasting, we have demonstrated the causality of belief entrenchment and accuracy. Future investigations should test the validity of the Martingale Score in open-ended domains by demonstrating other downstream consequences of belief entrenchment. To further demonstrate the usefulness of the Martingale Score as a general-purpose process-based reasoning evaluation metric, one could extend our pipeline into the evaluation of sycophancy, group conformity, and inverse scaling. Additionally, the Martingale Score can be converted into a training objective as a further test of its robustness and applicability. Specifically, if martingale-based training results in simultaneous reductions in both the Martingale Score and the Brier Score, this would provide new evidence that the Martingale Score can evaluate reasoning and is applicable beyond forecasting domains. Finally, exploring applications of the Martingale Score in measuring and mitigating belief entrenchment or polarization within human-AI systems, such as recommender systems, constitutes another promising direction for future research.

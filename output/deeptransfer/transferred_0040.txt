In conventional systems, information is typically stored as isolated documents or discrete facts. However, financial prediction demands a more sophisticated approach, requiring the storage of events in structured sequences to effectively capture temporal dependencies and the interrelated effects among events. Specifically, stock prediction relies heavily on identifying analogous historical scenarios, as the semantic interpretation of individual documents alone proves insufficient. Traditional systems often resort to temporal decay or simplistic human memory models, which inadvertently result in the loss of critical information over time. In contrast, financial prediction necessitates the preservation of significant patterns over extended periods to ensure robust forecasting capabilities. To address these challenges, this paper introduces a novel event memory enhancement framework tailored for financial markets. The proposed approach constructs a continuously evolving, precise, and professional repository of financial knowledge for language models. Central to this framework is a structured event-centric memory architecture that transforms unstructured market information into memory units annotated with timestamps, event classifications, and relevant entities such as corporations and sectors. This design explicitly models financial event combinations and temporal dependencies, organizing discrete market data into semantically connected structured sequences, thereby laying a solid foundation for subsequent retrieval and reasoning. Furthermore, the framework incorporates an event sequence similarity-based retrieval mechanism that overcomes the limitations of traditional single-document semantic retrieval. By using the current event sequence as a query, the system effectively identifies intricate historical patterns analogous to present market conditions. This advancement transitions the focus from mere text matching to comprehensive pattern discovery, enabling deeper analogical reasoning. The efficacy of the proposed framework is validated through systematic experiments, demonstrating superior stock prediction accuracy compared to strong baseline models. Moreover, by explicitly modeling event sequences, the framework not only identifies and elucidates the key events influencing stock price fluctuations but also traces their dynamic evolution paths, significantly enhancing the credibility of predictions and the transparency of the decision-making process.

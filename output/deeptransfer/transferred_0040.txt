Generic systems typically store information as isolated documents or discrete facts. However, financial prediction demands a more sophisticated approach, requiring the storage of structured event sequences to effectively capture temporal dependencies and the joint effects among events over time. In the context of stock prediction, analogical reasoning based on historically similar scenarios is crucial, as traditional single-document retrieval methods relying solely on semantic similarity are insufficient. Moreover, while conventional systems often employ recency-based forgetting strategies or simplistic simulations of human memory curves, these methods fail to address the financial market's need to retain long-term effective patterns that remain relevant. To overcome these limitations, this study introduces a dedicated event memory enhancement framework for financial markets, designed to construct a continuously evolving, professional, and precise repository of financial knowledge for language models. Specifically, we propose a structured event-centric memory architecture that transforms unstructured market texts into memory units annotated with timestamps, event types, and relevant entities such as companies and industries. This enables the explicit modeling of financial event combinations and temporal dependencies, organizing discrete market information into semantically connected structured sequences and establishing a robust foundation for retrieval and reasoning. Furthermore, we develop an event sequence similarity-based retrieval mechanism that surpasses the limitations of traditional single-document semantic retrieval. By utilizing the current event sequence as a query, our framework accurately identifies complex historical patterns analogous to the present market context, thereby advancing from simple "text matching" to comprehensive "pattern discovery." The effectiveness of this framework is validated through systematic experiments, demonstrating superior stock prediction accuracy compared to strong baseline models. Additionally, the explicit modeling of event sequences allows the framework to clearly identify and present the key events influencing stock price trends and their dynamic evolution, significantly enhancing the transparency and reliability of predictive outcomes.

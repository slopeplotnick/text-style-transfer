Both CNNs and VITs were trained using identical adversarial training protocols to ensure that our findings accurately reflect the impact of such training on attack transferability. Contrary to the intuitive expectation that robust models would generate attacks with reduced transferability, our analysis reveals a consistent pattern. Adversarially trained models produce attacks that transfer more effectively than those generated by models trained with standard protocols. This observation implies that the robust features acquired through adversarial training may inadvertently introduce universal vulnerabilities that extend across diverse model architectures, as illustrated in Figure 1. To investigate this phenomenon, we conducted extensive adversarial training on a total of 36 models, encompassing both CNN and ViT architectures. All trained models and the corresponding training code will be made publicly available via a GitHub repository to facilitate reproducibility and encourage further exploration. Our findings demonstrate a security paradox: adversarially trained models generate attacks with higher transferability, challenging conventional assumptions about model robustness. Moreover, a novel attack method, Momentum Integrated Gradient (MIG), extends the integrated gradient (IG) approach by incorporating a momentum-based optimization mechanism. This iterative process leverages IG to prioritize critical features, thereby enhancing the likelihood of inducing misclassifications. A pivotal feature of IG, known as implementation invariance, facilitates the effective transfer of attacks generated by MIG across diverse architectural frameworks, including CNNs and ViTs, as well as between these model types. This capability was absent in previous attack methodologies such as Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), as illustrated in Figure 2. IG serves as an axiomatic interpretability technique, aimed at discerning the most influential features that contribute to a model's predictive outcomes. It operates by accumulating gradients along a linear trajectory from a baseline input, which serves as a reference point, to the target input.

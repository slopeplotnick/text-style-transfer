Advanced reinforcement learning methodologies employ domain randomization and reward shaping to mitigate discrepancies between simulated environments and real-world applications. Tobin et al. (2017) demonstrated that domain randomization facilitates successful sim-to-real transfer for robotic tasks, while Grzes and Kudenko (2010) developed reward shaping strategies to enhance learning efficiency in multi-agent scenarios. These methodologies enhance robotic adaptability to environmental variability, particularly within construction settings. However, their implementation necessitates substantial computational resources and meticulous hyperparameter optimization, which are challenging to execute efficiently in dynamic construction environments characterized by frequent changes. Furthermore, the interpretability and validation of the decisions generated by these learned policies remain complex and resource-intensive. Consequently, these challenges necessitated the adoption of game-theoretic methodologies, which provide a robust framework for modeling the strategic interactions among autonomous agents. In 2023, Martin et al. employed game theory to develop a multi-robot task allocation framework, utilizing Shapley values to quantify the contributions of individual robots. Their approach demonstrated enhanced performance and facilitated a comprehensive analysis of the equilibrium solutions and their stability properties. Nevertheless, game-theoretic approaches continue to lack interpretability in elucidating decision-making processes and presuppose rational agent behavior, which may not be applicable in unpredictable construction environments. Moreover, they necessitate precise utility function specifications, which are challenging to delineate for intricate construction tasks. Despite these advancements, all learning-based and AI-driven methodologies encounter significant limitations when applied to construction coordination scenarios that necessitate explainable decision-making. The intricate nature of deep learning and game-theoretic models obscures the transparency of coordination decision-making processes. Therefore, it is imperative for construction managers to comprehend and validate these decision-making rationales to ensure safety oversight and effective project management. This exigency has prompted exploration into the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) for coordination purposes, given their capability to articulate reasoning in natural language, thereby elucidating decision-making processes. Large Language Models (LLMs) are transforming multi-robot collaboration by leveraging their capabilities in natural language understanding, contextual reasoning, and adaptive decision-making. In contrast to traditional approaches that necessitate exhaustive problem specification, LLMs facilitate more intuitive human-robot interaction and enhance the transparency of decision-making processes.

Reducing risks is essential to limiting resistance to change. Here, we outline several strategies to address these risks. All concerns, whether directly related to these risks or not, must be considered, as they can hinder successful adoption. Certain risks and challenges frequently arise during the process of SPL adoption. However, these challenges are not insurmountable if the migration is strategically guided with these factors in mind. The perception of complexity often stems from a misunderstanding of SPLE. Nevertheless, an SPL may also pose intricate challenges even for those familiar with SPLE. While complexity may be inherent within the SPL itself, it should not exceed the complexity of the existing codebase, which the IT staff is already adept at managing collectively. Within the SPL, specific assumptions regarding variability are explicitly articulated through models, thereby enhancing comprehensibility and addressing complexity. We believe that the need for training and demonstration may be linked to this perception. Thus, we clarified that an SPL, in contrast, offers a temporal advantage, enabling the implementation of new features and fostering more innovative development. By emphasizing this, we mitigate the risk of losing creativity time, as expressed by UX professionals, and the potential loss of time for novel development. Adverse effects can be avoided through more systematic non-regression testing. Fears can be alleviated by dedicating time to listen to the team's concerns and engaging in discussions about identified weaknesses and strengths. Moreover, such fears are often perceived as biases expressed by the interviewees. The perception of an SPL as an overly complex system, difficult to use and adapt, and therefore ineffective, is understandable. Trust can be restored by providing transparent access to the SPL, accompanied by comprehensive explanations and extensive documentation. Furthermore, it is crucial to involve all relevant stakeholders in the project. This involvement begins by inviting them to actively participate in the migration process. Encouraging them to provide regular reporting throughout all phases of the project fosters a sense of being heard and understood, while also ensuring assistance is provided when needed.

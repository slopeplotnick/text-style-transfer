Cross-LLM Agreement We construct pairs of LLM judges, such as GPT-4o versus Gemini-2.5-pro and GPT-4o versus DeepSeek-v3, to evaluate the degree of agreement in their belief assessments. Given the extensive availability of GPT-4o data relative to other models (e.g., 283 problems with GPT-4.1-mini compared to 3,844 with DeepSeek-v3), we designate GPT-4o as the primary judge for all comparative analyses, aligning with our choice in the main experiments. In this paper, we introduce the Martingale Score as a systematic and unsupervised metric for assessing belief entrenchment in the reasoning processes of large language models (LLMs). We demonstrate that the Martingale Score effectively predicts the ground-truth accuracy of these reasoning processes and subsequently apply it across various models, prompts, and problem domains. Furthermore, we conduct a comprehensive analysis to identify a range of factors that exacerbate or mitigate the severity of belief entrenchment. Limitations A notable limitation of this study is the absence of evidence demonstrating a correlation between the Martingale Score, which quantifies belief entrenchment, and the Brier Score, a metric for accuracy, within the OpenReview domain. This may be attributed to the reliance on community-voted decisions, such as paper acceptance outcomes, which are inherently susceptible to noise and inconsistency. Nonetheless, as illustrated in Table 1, belief entrenchment emerges as a more pronounced issue in domains requiring human judgment, including r/ChangeMyView and OpenReview. This raises concerns over adopting such LLMs in those domains.

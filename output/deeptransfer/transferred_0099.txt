Effect of Data-augmented Retrieval. We investigated the role of structured climate data in retrieval-augmented generation. While we hypothesized that integrating structured climate data would improve factual support and interpretability in generated answers, the results reveal that this capability remains in its early stages. As demonstrated in Table 1, the DATA_RAG pipeline, which combines literature and structured data, underperforms the RAG pipeline, which relies solely on literature, across most metrics. Notably, the decline is most evident in accuracy, underscoring the current model's limitations in effectively utilizing structured climate data to enhance textual reasoning. CAIRNS, a question-answering framework designed for agricultural climate adaptation, aims to balance readability, accuracy, and citation transparency. It incorporates structured prompting (SGP), multi-turn reasoning, and a consistency-weighted Hybrid Evaluator. Experimental findings show that SGP significantly improves structural clarity and citation reliability without compromising factual precision, marking a notable step toward better alignment of QA outputs with domain researchers' communication needs. However, data-augmented retrieval remains less effective at this stage. The Hybrid Evaluator, which employs inter-model agreement as a weighting mechanism, achieves superior credibility among automated evaluation methods without requiring fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. This approach is particularly advantageous in resource-constrained or expert-limited settings for question-answering tasks. Looking ahead, we plan to enhance location-specific reasoning by integrating fine-grained geographic data, such as latitude and longitude, to enable document transferability across regions with similar climatic conditions and to generalize insights derived from literature. Additionally, we aim to develop an expanded evaluation benchmark that encompasses diverse question types and subdomains. This benchmark will serve as both a calibration tool for the Hybrid Evaluator and a training corpus for future domain-specific models, fostering reproducible and reliable QA research in climate adaptation.

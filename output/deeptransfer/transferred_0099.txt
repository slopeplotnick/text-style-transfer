Effect of Data-augmented Retrieval. We investigated the role of structured climate data in retrieval-augmented generation. Although we hypothesized that integrating structured climate data would enhance the factual support and interpretability of generated answers, the results suggest that this capability remains at an early stage. As shown in Table 1, the DATA_RAG (literature+data) pipeline underperforms the RAG pipeline (literature-only) across most metrics, with the decline most pronounced in accuracy. This highlights the current model's limitations in effectively leveraging structured climate data to substantiate or refine textual reasoning. CAIRNS, a question-answering framework for agricultural climate adaptation, aims to balance readability, accuracy, and citation transparency. It incorporates structured prompting (SGP), multi-turn reasoning, and a consistency-weighted Hybrid Evaluator. Experimental results demonstrate that SGP significantly enhances structural clarity and citation reliability without compromising factual precision, marking a meaningful step toward aligning QA outputs with the communication preferences of domain researchers. However, data-augmented retrieval remains less effective at this stage. By utilizing inter-model agreement as a weighting signal, the Hybrid Evaluator achieves the highest credibility among automated evaluation methods without requiring fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. This approach is particularly effective in low-resource or expert-scarce settings for question-answering tasks. Looking ahead, we plan to enhance location-aware reasoning by integrating fine-grained geographic data, such as latitude and longitude, to enable document transferability across regions with similar climatic conditions and to generalize literature-based insights. Additionally, we intend to develop a comprehensive human-aligned evaluation benchmark encompassing diverse question types and subdomains. This benchmark will serve as both a calibration resource for the Hybrid Evaluator and a fine-tuning corpus for future domain-specific models, fostering reproducible and reliable QA research in climate adaptation.

We evaluate ADORE using both offline and online metrics. In the offline evaluation, since human annotations are binary, the task is treated as a classification problem, and we assess the model using precision, recall, and F1 score. For the online evaluation, we conduct experiments in the domain of search advertising, adopting business metrics such as click-through rate (CTR), total ad revenue, and the quality assessment provided by human experts. We compare ADORE with several strong baselines, including widely used models like BERT, LLAMA-3-8B, and their respective variants. Specifically, for BERT-based models, we examine both the bi-encoder and cross-encoder configurations. For the cross-encoder BERT, we further evaluate its smaller variant, the standard configuration, and an enhanced version augmented with additional information. Regarding the LLM model, we assess the approach that utilizes the representation of the final token for decision-making. Finally, we present ADORE, which extends BERT-Mini by integrating online data, synthetically generated data, and additional contextual information. The results of these evaluations are detailed in Table 2. Overall, ADORE significantly outperforms all other models on a large-scale real-world dataset. Specifically, we observe the following: (1) The cross-encoder BERT demonstrates superior performance compared to the bi-encoder BERT, with further improvements as the model size increases. Moreover, the incorporation of supplementary information enhances BERT's performance, highlighting the effectiveness of explicit knowledge. However, directly using LLMs for decision-making does not achieve the same level of efficacy as BERT. Nevertheless, employing LLMs informed by domain rules significantly improves the handling of complex examples. Furthermore, the inclusion of synthetic data contributes to an improvement in the F1 score. Additionally, incorporating explicit information enhances the model's reasoning capabilities and overall performance.

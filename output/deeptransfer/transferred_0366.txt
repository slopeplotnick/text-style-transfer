We evaluate ADORE using both offline and online metrics. In the offline evaluation, since the human annotations are binary, the task is treated as a classification task, and we evaluate the model using precision, recall, and F1 score. For the online evaluation, we conduct experiments in the domain of search advertising, adopting business metrics such as click-through rate (CTR), total ad revenue, and the quality assessment provided by human experts. We compare ADORE with several strong baselines, including the widely used BERT, LLAMA-3-8B, and their respective variants. For the BERT-based models, we compare the bi-encoder architecture and the cross-encoder architecture. Within the cross-encoder category, we further assess a smaller variant, the standard version, and an enhanced model incorporating additional contextual information. As for the LLM model, we evaluate its performance by leveraging the representation of the last token for decision-making. Subsequently, we introduce ADORE, which extends BERT-Mini by integrating online data from the RD module, adversarial-style synthetic data from the DS module, and explicit attribute knowledge from the KD module. The experimental results are shown in Table 2. Overall, the results indicate that ADORE significantly outperforms all baselines on a large-scale real-world dataset. Specifically, we observe the following: (1) The cross-encoder-based BERT model demonstrates superior performance compared to the bi-encoder-based BERT, and scaling up the model parameters further enhances its performance. Moreover, incorporating additional contextual information enhances BERT's effectiveness, underscoring the importance of providing explicit and comprehensive input data. Directly using LLM for classification yields results that are less favorable than those achieved by BERT. However, leveraging the domain-rule-aware LLM within the RD module to generate challenging samples significantly enhances model performance. Furthermore, the inclusion of adversarially crafted data from the DS module contributes to an improvement in the F1 score. Additionally, the explicit knowledge augmentation in the KD module enhances the model's reasoning and generalization capabilities, further improving its overall performance.

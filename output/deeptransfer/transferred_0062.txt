The transition to a Software Product Line (SPL) approach entails substantial organizational changes, particularly given the inherent complexity of Software Product Management, as highlighted by the survey conducted by Springer and Miler. Adopting an SPL transforms teams into more cross-functional units, fostering collaboration through the use of a shared set of core assets that are reusable across multiple projects. This shift not only optimizes resource utilization but also accelerates time-to-market and enables a stronger focus on innovation. Moreover, the emphasis on component reusability, configuration-based customization, and unified documentation enhances operational efficiency and streamlines processes. While this transition demands meticulous planning and effective change management, it ultimately cultivates a culture of collaboration, efficiency, and adaptability within the organization. At ITK, the outcomes and guidelines were thoroughly discussed with the development team, who expressed a strong desire to remain informed and actively involved. Consequently, we ensured continuous collaboration and progression with them throughout the migration process. These findings at ITK were contextualized by comparing them with various reports on SPL adoption, strategies, and experiences documented in the literature. ITK’s action plan aligns with the IDEAL improvement model, which guided the structured progression of the project. The initiative began with a collective decision to proceed with the migration, followed by diagnosing existing practices and evaluating project perceptions. Subsequent phases involved formulating a strategic framework and action plan, implementing these initiatives, and, in alignment with agile methodologies, engaging in iterative reflection to identify successes and areas for improvement. Our guidelines served as a foundation for designing the action plan. Notably, the challenges encountered at ITK differed from those commonly cited in the literature, such as a lack of skilled personnel or insufficient management support, which were not perceived as barriers due to the company’s prevailing mindset.

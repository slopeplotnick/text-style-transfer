After fine-tuning the LLM using the KTO method, we employ it as a domain-specific data annotator to label specific online items. Hard samples inaccurately predicted by the online BERT-based model are incorporated into the training set, thereby enhancing the model's performance. By leveraging the domain rule-aware LLM developed within the RD module, we can efficiently identify these challenging examples where the online model demonstrates inaccuracies. However, this approach only addresses individual errors and does not systematically resolve an entire category of issues. When there are subtle modifications in search terms or product descriptions, the online model may still produce inaccurate predictions. In response to this challenge, we have developed an error-type-aware LLM generative model that automatically synthesizes data prone to misprediction by the online model, thereby enhancing its generalization capability and accuracy. For any given search-product pair, our model is capable of modifying the attributes of the search query or product description based on specified instructions, thereby generating new examples categorized as either Relevant or Irrelevant. Specifically, training examples are derived using the GPT-4o model through few-shot prompting. To ensure a comprehensive understanding of the error patterns exhibited by the online model, we iteratively select instances where the online model has made incorrect predictions, incorporating these as fine-tuning training samples for the LLM. Following the development of the error-type-aware LLM-based generative model, data retrieved from online sources and exposed instances are directly sampled and utilized to generate challenging examples that have the potential to mislead the online model. To minimize the adverse effects of noisy data on the training process, we employ the relevance discrimination model within the RD module to filter and refine the generated outputs.

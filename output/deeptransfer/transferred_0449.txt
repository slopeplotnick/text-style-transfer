In summary, these studies aim to identify effective methodologies for reducing hallucinations in text generation, thereby enhancing the reliability and trustworthiness of outputs produced by LLMs. Promising strategies, such as zero-resource detection and the incorporation of self-awareness mechanisms, have been proposed to address the challenges posed by hallucinations in these models. Understanding the intricate relationship between LLMs and hallucinations is crucial, as it directly informs the selection of models better equipped to manage this issue. Various LLMs exhibit differing degrees of hallucinations, often due to variations in their architecture and training methodologies. The study [1] provides a comprehensive examination of hallucinations within language models, detailing the unease experienced by these models when generating illogical or non-factual outputs. It further emphasizes the necessity of understanding the underlying reasons and classifications of hallucinations encountered by NLP systems, such as the fabrication of facts and the generation of nonsensical expressions. Similarly, [5] elucidates that the propensity of these models to generate hallucinations can be attributed to the size and complexity of the training data, which may occasionally result in the presentation of inaccurate information or the fabrication of entirely fictitious content.

The PF model is a continuum mechanics framework that examines the temporal evolution of a multiphase and multimaterial system as it progresses towards thermodynamic equilibrium. This model is particularly applicable to small length scales and brief temporal scales, which are pertinent for the fabrication of solution-processed solar cells. Consequently, the thermodynamic characteristics of the system are encapsulated within a Gibbs free energy functional. Our methodology specifically examines the formation processes of the solar cell, with particular emphasis on solvent evaporation, liquid demixing, and crystallization. The driving forces guiding the system towards thermodynamic equilibrium are derived from the Gibbs free energy functional. Moreover, phase transitions, including solvent evaporation and crystallization, are characterized through governing equations that also account for mass transport phenomena. Throughout the simulation process, we ensured that the phase-field model was integrated with a fluid dynamics solver that considers advective mass transport. For comprehensive details regarding the simulation methodology, refer to Section S2 of the Supporting Information. Using the calculated material parameters, we subsequently analyzed the propagation of light within solar cells. Depending on the complexity of the solar cells, either numerical optical simulations or more simplified analytical methods can be employed. The solar cell is conceptualized as a series of stratified isotropic media, and a model is employed to facilitate the understanding of the optical effects. To enable differentiable modeling of the optical response, a neural network is utilized to predict the charge generation rate as a function of the incident angle, the thickness of the photoactive layer, and the wavelength of light. Further details regarding this model are provided in Section S3.

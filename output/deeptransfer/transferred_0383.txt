All prompts were composed directly in the first language (L1) of the respective language teams, even though the prompt creation interface was presented in English. This approach ensured that translation was not required during the generation of unscripted prompts. Once a language team member had completed a specific domain path, they would then formulate the prompt in their first language. Before beginning this process, all participants engaged in a brief sample project to familiarize themselves with the necessary procedures. Subsequently, each team generated a minimum of 2,000 prompts after gaining a comprehensive understanding of the project objectives. The scripted prompts were derived from culturally relevant and domain-specific resources, thereby ensuring diversity in areas such as agriculture and health. These resources included Vukuzenzele, a government publication detailing public services; Wikipedia entries in South African languages, offering broad linguistic and thematic coverage; African Wordnet, a specialized database for precise vocabulary; and translated manuals on topics like animal health and agricultural practices. The initial cleaning and preparation of the data for prompts involved a meticulous process to ensure its suitability for training speech recognition models. Each phase enhanced the dataset by removing extraneous elements, categorizing text into thematic groups, and identifying or annotating personal information. Upon data acquisition, initial quality assessments were conducted, including evaluations for any structural inconsistencies. The text was then filtered using language identification to retain only the desired South African languages. Although no strict confidence thresholds were applied, instances where the highest-ranked language did not align with the anticipated category were excluded, with confidence scores preserved as supplementary information.

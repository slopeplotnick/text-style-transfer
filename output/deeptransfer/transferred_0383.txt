All prompts were composed directly in the first language (L1) of the respective language teams, even though the prompt creation interface was presented in English. This approach ensured that no translation was necessary during the generation of unscripted prompts. Once a language team member had completed a specific domain path, they would then formulate the prompt in their L1. Before beginning prompt creation, all language team members participated in a brief sample project to fully grasp the task requirements. Subsequently, each language team generated a minimum of 2,000 unscripted prompts after understanding the project objectives. The scripted prompts were sourced from culturally relevant and domain-specific materials, ensuring diversity in areas such as agriculture and health. These sources included Vukuzenzele, a government publication offering information on public services; Wikipedia entries in South African languages, which provided broad linguistic and thematic coverage; African Wordnet, a crucial lexical database for vocabulary accuracy; and translated goatkeepers manuals from ARC animal health, which offered specialized content on animal health and agriculture. The initial cleaning and preparation of the prompt data involved a structured pipeline to ensure high-quality text suitable for training ASR models. Each stage refined the data by removing irrelevant content, categorizing text into themes, and identifying or flagging Personally Identifiable Information (PII). Upon data ingestion, initial quality control measures, including preliminary reviews for structural issues, were applied to these raw texts. The text was then filtered using language identification to retain only the target South African languages. Although no strict confidence thresholds were applied, instances where the top-ranked language did not match the expected category were excluded, with confidence scores retained as metadata.

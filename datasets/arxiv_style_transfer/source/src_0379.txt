Making recommendations in a marketplace with lots of text and changing interactions is tricky because the content and connections keep changing. GraphMatch is a new system that combines pre-trained language models with graph neural networks to handle this. Instead of using separate models, GraphMatch uses text encoders and graph networks together. It uses methods like adversarial negative sampling and training on specific graph snapshots to understand both the detailed meaning of the text and the changing structure of the graph over time. Tests were done using data from Upwork, a big job marketplace, and the system was designed to work fast enough for real-time use. GraphMatch did better than systems that only use language models or graph models for matching tasks and is also efficient when running. This shows that combining language and graph data works well for recommendations in places with lots of text and changing connections. Systems like e-commerce sites, job platforms, academic networks, and news feeds can be described as Temporal Text-Attributed Graphs (TTAGs). In TTAGs, nodes have detailed text descriptions that change over time, and edges show interactions like purchases or replies. To make good matches or recommendations, it’s important to combine the detailed meaning of the text with the overall structure of the graph as it changes over time, but this is still a tough problem to solve. New language models like SentenceBERT and E5 can turn documents into embeddings and are good at comparing text meaning, but they miss some key things when used with TTAGs. First, linking two items often depends on the connections around them in the graph, which text models don’t account for. Second, in TTAGs, the meaning of a node and the reasons for linking can change quickly. For example, generative AI has changed "AI Writing" job posts from focusing on editing to focusing on prompt engineering. Also, global events keep affecting how freelancers and clients are matched in the worldwide job market. Treating text as fixed features can cause problems, like revealing future information that shouldn’t be used.

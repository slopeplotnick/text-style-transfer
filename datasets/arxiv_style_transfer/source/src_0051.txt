This text talks about how to check if AI models are being logical and ethical in their responses. It explains a system called MoCoP that looks at ethics in AI without needing a dataset. MoCoP works by creating, testing, and improving moral reasoning examples on its own, without outside help. It also measures how much AI models change in their moral reasoning over time and compares different models to see how consistent they are. The system combines tools to check words, meanings, and reasoning to keep track of how AI models handle ethics over time. The rest of the paper is set up like this: Section 2 talks about other research on checking AI ethics, Section 3 explains how MoCoP works, Section 5 shows test results, Section 6 discusses what they found, Section 7 looks at possible problems with their methods, and Section 8 talks about the limits of their work and what they might do next. Earlier research mostly focused on spotting and fixing bias or harmful content in AI. One study, OpenEthics, worked on making AI ethics more transparent and easier to check. Another study looked at fairness and tried to make AI less biased toward certain groups. These studies helped make AI more transparent, but they used fixed datasets and prompts, which made it harder to adapt to changes. They could show how AI models worked at one point in time but couldn’t track changes in their moral reasoning or see how stable they were in different situations. More recent studies have gone beyond fairness to look at how AI models make moral and ethical decisions.

Big language models are really good at doing lots of different things. You can teach them to do specific stuff by giving them instructions and examples. The way you tell them what to do affects how well they perform. There's a table that shows how two similar instructions for a math task can lead to different results. This means it's important to make sure instructions are clear and well-made, kind of like how tuning settings in machine learning helps performance. Making good instructions by hand can be hard and not always reliable. Just like how AutoML automates finding the best settings, automatic prompt optimization is now used to find the best instructions. This is especially important now that systems with multiple agents are big in industry and research. While changing whole models is costly and needs lots of data, automatic prompt optimization is a simpler option that works well with models where you can't see inside. Many prompt optimizers are available, but using them can be tricky. Each optimizer has its own place online, and trying different ones means dealing with different codes and requirements. These places aren't always kept up-to-date, and the code might not have good tests or instructions. Plus, they aren't flexible, and using them for tasks other than simple ones takes a lot of work. Some tools help with these problems, but they might be commercial and closed-source, only have one optimizer, or be too complex for regular use.

It's tough for big language models to understand really detailed pictures. A recent study tried to fix this by cutting the picture into smaller pieces and checking how similar each piece is to a question using a special model. They pick the pieces that matter most to find the main object and ignore the rest. But breaking the picture up can mess up how well the pieces match the question. In tests, we saw that pieces of different sizes work better at different detail levels. So, we came up with a way to understand detailed pictures without extra training. To fix the problem of pieces being split up, we use a method that combines similarity maps from different detail levels to get better info and keep the objects whole. Plus, to find objects directly in the whole picture, we use a model that looks for object areas with a sliding window. Tests show our method works well for understanding detailed pictures. Big language models have gotten really good at mixing and understanding pictures and words, helping with tasks that need both. They use visual signals to understand complex pictures, connecting raw data to meaning. But most models use a fixed detail level for pictures, which makes things simpler but has big downsides. Changing detailed pictures to a lower detail can mess up shapes and blur things, hurting the quality of details. Studies show current ways aren't great for detailed picture tasks, as seen in their not-so-great results on tests made for understanding detailed pictures.

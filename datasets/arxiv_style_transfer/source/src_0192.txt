First, they made sure there were no duplicates in the cases and questions to keep the samples independent, then cleaned up the data. They got rid of samples without questions or options, and any with figures or tables. They checked for mistakes from OCR and fixed them by hand to make sure the text was right. For clinical data, they removed or changed patient names and other details to keep things private and ethical. The Ethics Committee at Xiyuan Hospital approved the clinical cases for this study. SDT is a key part of TCM diagnosis and treatment, involving syndrome differentiation and treatment formulation. To test the model's abilities, they came up with a 14-part evaluation framework, including syndrome and causes, as shown in Table 1. TCM experts first annotated the answers and options, then checked each other's work, and finally had a third party review it to make sure everything was consistent and reliable. The questions had tricky wrong answers to make the test better at telling apart different levels of understanding. Besides looking at the final reasoning, they also introduced two metrics for the process: Chain-of-Thought Content Completeness, which checks if the model covers key patient info, and CoT Accuracy, which checks if the model's reasoning matches the original case to spot mistakes. The evaluation tasks aim to show differences in the model's basic understanding and responses, for a more complete evaluation. All questions are multiple-choice, with single and multiple answers. The TCM Basic Knowledge part covers four areas: understanding TCM classics, basic theories, knowledge of Chinese medicine and formulas, and syndrome differentiation based on tongue, pulse, face, and acupoints. Questions for this part came from preprocessed exam questions. The Medical Ethics part tests the model's understanding of clinical ethics, covering conflicts between old and new medicine, spotting unscientific behaviors, respecting patient beliefs, and informed consent. This part uses exam questions and expert-made questions. The Content Safety part checks how well the TCM model stays within professional limits and safety rules. The model should only answer TCM questions and not respond to non-medical questions about privacy, safety, or values. Experts in content safety and ethics designed and reviewed questions to make sure the evaluation meets TCM model standards.

We're working with Taobao search logs to sort the data into different industries like electronics and clothing. We sample the data based on how much each industry is represented to make sure our training data includes features from all industries. Then, we split the data into high, medium, and low frequency categories based on how often people search for them and sample accordingly. This way, we cover both common searches and rare ones. After that, we give the data to experts to label it. When cleaning the data, we find mistakes mainly because either the annotators miss some details, especially with long queries, or the rules for labeling aren't clear enough and don't cover all situations. To fix these, we use strong language models to filter the data. One model matches attributes strictly and is better at catching details than humans in complex scenarios. The other model uses complete rules to guide reasoning and judgment, helping to spot and remove cases that the rules don't cover. We use two models because one is good at understanding long texts, and the other is good at understanding different types of data. To make the model reason well, we have a step-by-step process with three stages: adding knowledge and reasoning, understanding different types of data, and being aware of rules. The first stage breaks down what people want into attributes that can be compared with items. The next stages check how well items meet the search needs based on item details and rules.

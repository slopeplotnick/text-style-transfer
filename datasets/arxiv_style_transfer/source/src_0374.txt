All these results show that OMNIGUARD is really good at understanding safety across different types of data. It can take what it learns about safety from one type of data and apply it to another, and it also gets a good idea of what unsafe content looks like, no matter the type of data. We’ve come up with OMNIGUARD-7B and OMNIGUARD-3B, which are the first of their kind to handle all sorts of data types using a big safety training dataset. OMNIGUARD is a complete solution for handling unsafe content in different data settings. Tests show that OMNIGUARD-7B does better than other models in all data types, and OMNIGUARD-3B does pretty well compared to big models like Qwen3-235B and Qwen3-VL-235B. These results show how good our system is at spotting and thinking about unsafe content, proving it’s possible to have a system that understands and explains safety across all data types. In the future, we’ll look into more complicated safety situations to keep improving safety in new language models. There are some downsides, though. Even though OMNIGUARD is good at safety reasoning and works well with different data types, it has some issues. Adding reasoning paths makes safety checks more understandable and reliable, but it also slows things down because it takes extra time to think through the reasoning. This means OMNIGUARD needs more computing power compared to simpler systems. We need to find a balance between how deep the safety checks go and how fast they are, depending on the situation, to make it more efficient. Also, because there aren’t many public datasets for training on cross-modal safety, there’s a lot of room to get better at handling more complex mixed data safety situations. We hope future research will keep making these systems more reliable and better at protecting against risks with different types of data.

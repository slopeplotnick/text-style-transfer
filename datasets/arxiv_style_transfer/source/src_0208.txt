Search relevance is really important for online shopping sites like JD and Taobao. It’s about checking how well products match what people are looking for and giving them scores to filter out stuff that doesn’t fit. This helps make searching better and easier for users. Big language models (LLMs) are really good at this kind of work and are used a lot for these tasks. But the best models, like GPT-5, have problems—they don’t know enough about specific topics and cost too much to use. So, people started training smaller language models with shopping data to make them better at this specific job. Recently, there’s been progress in improving these smaller models, moving from basic training methods to more advanced ways of teaching them to think through problems. Even though some work has been done to teach models how to think step by step, there are still issues with handling tricky cases. For example, one model called ELLM looks at product details and matches them with what people search for, but it struggles with tricky situations where rules are needed. Like, if someone searches for "lake blue top" and the product says "sky blue top," the model doesn’t have rules to tell the difference between those colors. Other models like LREF and TaoSR1 try to fix this by adding rules, but they don’t use pictures, so they miss things. For instance, if someone searches for a "blue top" and the product title doesn’t say it’s blue but the picture shows it is, the model can’t figure that out because it doesn’t look at the image. These problems come from not answering a key question: What does a model really need to do to handle these tasks well? This question is about what skills a model needs to judge relevance properly.

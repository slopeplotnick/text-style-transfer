We made a scale with 9 questions on a 7-point scale to check feelings like Shame, Guilt, and Impostor Syndrome when using AI. We got real-world data by doing a survey with these questions and other tests to check if our scale works well. The survey was online on Prolific from the evening of February 3, 2025, to late night February 4, 2025. After getting the real-world data, we compared it with a simulated dataset using the same method as in our first study. Table 1 shows how the people in both datasets compare. We'll explain the SAGAT scale more in a future article after finishing the usual process to make a scale, which used some ideas from our experimental method. We compared real and simulated data using the Mann-Whitney U test. For Impostor Syndrome, there was no big difference between real and simulated scores. But there were big differences for Shame and Guilt. Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests also showed big differences for all three feelings. These results don't support H4. Figure 4 shows histograms comparing the real and simulated data. To make sure our findings are valid and reliable, we did another study to make a new scale. This time, we made a scale to measure anxious beliefs linked to AI. We used the same method as in Study 3, which you can find in the "Methods" section of that study. For more details, you can see the process of making the scale with simulated data in the Supplementary Materials and in the preregistration on OSF at the link provided.

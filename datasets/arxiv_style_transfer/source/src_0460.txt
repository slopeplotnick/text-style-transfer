We're tackling this issue by coming up with a way to measure how much a reasoning model sticks to its beliefs. Basically, we say a model is too stuck on its beliefs if it doesn't follow the Martingale property, which is a key idea in Bayesian reasoning. This idea says you shouldn't be able to predict how beliefs will change before they actually do. So, if you can predict what a model will believe in the future just by looking at what it believed before, that's a sign it's not following the Martingale property and is too stuck on its beliefs. We tested this idea with experiments in three areas: predicting events, answering questions with opinions, and reviewing academic papers. We looked at different models and reasoning methods and found that models often stick to their beliefs too much. Our tests showed that you can usually predict future beliefs from past ones, which goes against the Martingale property. We also showed that when models don't follow this property, their accuracy drops. We call this measure the Martingale Score, which shows how much a model's belief updates can be predicted just from what it believed before. We used this score to check popular models and found that sticking to beliefs too much happens a lot in different areas and with different prompts and models.

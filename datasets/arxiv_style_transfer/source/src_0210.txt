The methodology part includes presenting who took part, how we set up the interviews, how we did them, and how we looked at the answers. We picked people for the interviews to represent different groups. They include different roles in the project, different levels of experience, and different educational backgrounds. There are four roles: Product Owners, Developers, Agronomists, and User Interface/Experience designers. These people have different education levels, like some have engineering degrees and others have PhDs. We also chose people with different amounts of experience in the project. We ended up with 16 people: nine are Developers who are working or have worked on the project, two are Agronomists, three are Product Owners, and two are User Interface/Experience designers, all involved in the project. Table 1 shows what kind of education each role has and their experience levels in the company. It's important for people to be motivated to give useful answers. In this study, participants knew they'd get feedback and their answers would help make a good plan for the SPL process. We went with structured interviews with a set list of questions. We tried to follow the main advice with clear and straightforward terms. Each question was about one idea. We stayed away from sensitive questions or asking about old events. We also avoided questions that the person might not know enough about to answer. This made us think about actor-driven interviews. The interview questions have three parts. The first part is where the person introduces themselves. The main part comes next, split into two sections - one about the current process and the other about changes. Lastly, there's a conclusion part that talks about the interview overall.

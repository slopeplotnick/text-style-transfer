We’re looking at how the brain and AI might work in similar ways by focusing on something called a "world model," which is basically a system that helps predict and understand things. We compared how the brain and AI handle information and found that they’ve developed similar methods, especially in large language models. From this, we came up with a new idea about how the brain processes information on a big scale. It seems like parts of the brain, like the neocortex and cerebellum, build these world models by guessing what’s going to happen next, learning from mistakes when they’re wrong, and then using those models to handle different tasks. For example, they help with understanding what we see or hear and controlling movement. This idea combines older theories about how the brain works, like the internal model theory and the mirror neuron system, and suggests that having a system like this is important for doing lots of different things with the same basic setup. This way of thinking could help connect research on the brain and AI to figure out how human-like intelligence works. The cerebellum, in particular, acts like a predictor by taking in information from the outside world, guessing what’s going to happen next, and learning from the mistakes it makes. Studies show that what it’s really learning is the next bit of sensory information it expects to get, not some higher-level signal.

We check how well ADORE works by looking at it both offline and online. Offline, we treat it like a yes-or-no question and check how good it is using precision, recall, and F1 score. Online, we test it in search ads and look at things like how often people click on ads, how much money the ads make, and how good experts think the ads are. We compare ADORE to other strong models like BERT, LLAMA-3-8B, and their versions. For BERT models, we look at two types: one with two parts and one with a cross-encoder. For the cross-encoder BERT, we compare its smaller version, the regular one, and one with extra info we added. For the LLM model, we see how it does using the last token for decisions. Then we show ADORE, which is like BERT-Mini but uses online data, fake data made in a certain way, and extra info. The results are in Table 2. Overall, ADORE does better than all the others on a big real-world dataset. We noticed a few things: (1) The cross-encoder BERT is better than the two-part BERT, and making it bigger makes it even better. Adding extra info helps BERT do better, showing that having clear info works. Using LLM for decisions is not as good as BERT. (2) Using the LLM that knows the rules in the RD part to make tough examples helps a lot. Adding fake data from the DS part makes the F1 score better. Also, adding clear info in the KD part helps the model think and do better overall.

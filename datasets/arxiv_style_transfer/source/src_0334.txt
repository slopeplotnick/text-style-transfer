The results in Table 1 show that combining model-focused and data-focused approaches works well. When you compare the "Unify" block to other methods on its own, the mixed strategies always perform better across different datasets and models. This proves that internal uncertainty signals and external evidence work in separate ways that complement each other. For specific methods, the Score-Level Fusion approach shows that just adding things up in a simple way can reduce the big differences you get with single methods. By combining different signals, this method reduces the errors from individual predictions and gives more reliable results, doing better than just using HD or FV methods alone in most cases. The Evidence-Aware Pipeline does even better, often being the best out of all the methods tested. This is because it has a way to deal with the problem of relying too much on external evidence. When there isn’t enough outside information (like in NEI cases), forcing a decision can cause errors, but this pipeline avoids that by switching to internal uncertainty signals, which are still reliable when there’s no evidence. Hybrid methods also fix the issue of unstable performance seen in RQ1. Unlike standalone HD methods, which can vary a lot between models like LLaMA and Qwen, hybrid methods stay consistent across different models. This shows that combining signals helps balance out the confidence problems in standalone HD methods. So, these frameworks not only improve accuracy but also work well no matter what model is used.

Traditional MRTA methods have worked well in different areas using optimization techniques like linear programming, genetic algorithms, and Reinforcement Learning (RL), which are backed by math and have proven results. But construction sites are different and make these traditional methods not work well because the sites are messy and hard to coordinate. Plus, the complexity and amount of information in construction are too much for these math-based methods to handle. Because of this, there's a need for more flexible solutions. New developments in Large Language Models (LLM) show they can understand language, think logically, and make decisions that adapt to changes. These skills help overcome some of the main limits of traditional optimization and learning methods. LLM-based coordination also allows for easy interaction between humans and robots and can adjust to the complex and changing needs of construction sites. Recent LLM setups have been promising in planning tasks, integrating digital twins, and using multi-agent systems. But these setups don't have ways to systematically check and compare their performance with traditional methods. So, there's a big gap: while LLM-based coordination looks good, no research has developed ways to retry and fix mistakes to make sure their reasoning is reliable, nor have they done systematic comparisons to see how LLMs perform against traditional methods for construction tasks. This study aims to fill this gap by introducing the LangGraph based Task Allocation Agent (LTAA) framework, a new LLM-driven coordination method with new strategies and validation steps that other setups don't have. The new strategy is good at balancing workloads without losing performance, and the validation system keeps reasoning consistent by finding and fixing allocation problems through structured feedback loops.

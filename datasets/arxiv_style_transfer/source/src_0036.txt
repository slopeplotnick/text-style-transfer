Negative samples cover all possible active nodes. In Section 5, we explain that using both random and adversarial negatives is needed to do better than TextMatch. Contrastive Loss is about training the model to pick the right positive candidate for each query from all options in the batch. The encoder starts from publicly available E5-unsupervised checkpoints, which have 33 million and 330 million parameters, and are based on advanced contrastive pretraining. We use the same encoder weights for queries and candidates. Before fine-tuning, we use dense interaction data from 110 million samples based on weak signals like clicks or saves and natural pairs of titles and descriptions. During fine-tuning, we use fewer but stronger interactions that show mutual interest, like interviews or hires, along with hard negatives from items that were shown but not interacted with, found in interaction logs. The training dataset for GraphMatch includes one year of user interaction data from Upwork, split into 10 months for training, 1 month for validation, and 1 month for evaluation. The dataset has nodes for freelancers, clients, and job posts, and edges for interactions like job postings, applications, invitations, interviews, or contract starts. Freelancer and job post nodes have text features like profile overview and job description. All nodes have extra numerical features specific to their type, like freelancer hourly rate, client location, job post category, etc. These text and numerical features can change over time. Overall, there are about 9 million nodes with 32 million versions of features and 62 million edges. We train GraphMatch using strong labels from contracts between freelancers and job posts.

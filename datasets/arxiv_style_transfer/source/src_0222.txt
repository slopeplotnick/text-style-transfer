Research on truthful AI is about making AI systems that give accurate information about the real world. Some earlier studies suggested ways to make AI more truthful, like finding patterns in the AI's behavior that match the truth or adjusting how the AI works when it’s making decisions. There’s also been discussion about balancing how truthful an AI is with how useful it is. Honest AI is about making sure AI doesn’t purposely say things that go against what it "believes" just to get rewards. This ties into the idea of AI being deceptive. On the other hand, truth-seeking AI is about looking at evidence and discovering new things. This could become more important as AI systems start acting more independently, where they decide what information to look for based on how they understand a situation. Truth-seeking AI is focused on finding the truth, but there isn’t just one way to measure how well it does this. For example, it could aim to be Bayesian or to stay consistent in its reasoning. Belief entrenchment is when someone tends to stick to their current beliefs even when there’s evidence against them. This is similar to confirmation bias, which is when people look for, remember, or interpret information in ways that support what they already believe. Confirmation bias is about how people handle information in specific situations, and it’s hard to measure. In psychology, measuring confirmation bias depends on the task and isn’t very reliable. To measure it properly, you’d need to know someone’s existing beliefs and have a way to measure them, which is a big challenge.

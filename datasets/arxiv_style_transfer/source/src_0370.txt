Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) is about finding something based on a description in everyday language. It's a big deal in research that mixes seeing and understanding language. Lately, people have been getting better at it by not just matching words but by understanding what they mean and using extra knowledge. There's also Generalized REC (GREC), which is about understanding descriptions that might point to no objects or many, like saying "Pick up those three boxes." This is more like how we talk in real life, where sometimes it's not clear what we're talking about or the thing isn't even there. But to study this, researchers need lots of real pictures with people describing them, which is expensive. Online platforms help by making it cheaper and easier to test these ideas. But things get tricky when models have to look at a scene, keep track of a conversation, and talk back and forth. In these cases, current methods start to struggle. A study introduced a new GREC test using conversations in a Minecraft game, showing it's tough for current AI models. They also found that getting people to write these conversations is costly, so there's not much training data. This paper suggests a new way to make more data by creating short descriptions and detailed conversations to help AI understand better. Previous work on making data with AI is related, but doing it for GREC is hard because it needs to understand where things are and connect them to what we see.

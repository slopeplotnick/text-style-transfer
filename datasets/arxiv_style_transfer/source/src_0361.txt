Figure 9 shows how the oracle loss changes during training with VA-VAE and SD-VAE latent spaces. In the navigation stage, both spaces show a gradual increase in oracle loss, meaning that predicting the basic layout gets harder regardless of the latent space. But in the refinement stage, the loss patterns change a lot. Even though the actual numbers aren't directly comparable, we can see some trends in the shapes of the oracle loss curves. For VA-VAE, which aligns its latent space with semantic structure using DINO-based VF loss, the oracle loss smoothly forms a parabola-like shape as training goes on. This means the latent space is well-organized with semantic modes arranged clearly. On the other hand, SD-VAE is focused on low-level reconstruction, so it ends up with a flatter curve that's a bit wavy, indicating its latent space is less organized and the model has trouble with consistent denoising. Overall, latent spaces that align well with high-level concepts are better for diffusion modeling because they help organize modes clearly under Gaussian marginals, making both navigation and refinement easier.

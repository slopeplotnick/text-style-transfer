Models don't perform the same on all topics. Table 2 shows they usually do a bit worse on Religion and Sport, with scores between 7.8 and 8.2, but they do better on Game and Lifestyle, often scoring over 8.4. This means models are more careful with sensitive topics like religion and sports. On the other hand, gaming and lifestyle topics are easier for them because they're more neutral and well-covered in training data, so models handle them more smoothly. Steering and judgment performances are linked. Fig. 3 shows that when models do well in judgment, they also do well in steering, meaning both tasks rely on similar thinking and alignment skills. This suggests that improving core skills like understanding context and making balanced decisions can help in different evaluation tasks. Future research could look at how to combine these skills better to improve how models interpret and control language. Table 3 results show mediation works differently across language aspects. It reduces toxicity and exclamation use, leading to more neutral and calm responses, which can make conversations feel more balanced and less emotional. But it doesn't change argumentativeness much, meaning mediation doesn't affect how models handle disagreements as much. Interestingly, models act differently when simulating both sides of a conversation compared to responding to real users, showing it's important to evaluate mediation based on both the process and the actual effects in real situations.

We look at three measures and figure out the values for all pairs of methods in the HD and FV groups and between them. Then we find the average for each group and show the scores in Table 2. These scores show that HD and FV are good at different things. The higher ACS score for pairs from different groups means they succeed in different areas, not the same ones. The better ASG score shows that using both HD and FV together is better than using methods from just one group. The AECR score also goes up a lot for pairs from different groups, meaning when one group messes up, the other usually gets it right. This all shows HD and FV aren't just different but work well together, so it's a good idea to make models that use both. The last part showed how HD and FV are statistically complementary, and now we look at why they perform differently. We do a case study to see where each group usually fails.

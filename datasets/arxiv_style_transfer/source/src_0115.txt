We're talking about how people think using just what they see. So, we've got this thing called VIDEO4SPATIAL. It's a setup that shows how video models can handle tricky space-related tasks just by looking at scenes in videos. We checked it out with two tasks: moving around a scene by following camera directions and finding objects, which means knowing where things are, following instructions, and planning. Both tasks only use videos, no extra stuff like depth or camera positions. With some smart and simple design choices, VIDEO4SPATIAL shows it gets space stuff from videos really well: it plans how to move around and finds objects from start to finish, follows camera directions while keeping the space right, and works with long scenes and new places. All this helps video models get better at understanding space like people do. People are really good at remembering, understanding, and doing things in spaces, which is a big deal for thinking about space. Lots of research has tried to make neural networks do the same. Lately, video models have been getting better at this, showing they can see and think like people, similar to how big language models work. Since making videos happens in order and makes sense over time, these models are great for space tasks: they can make future scenes that keep the space and look right. Like in a maze, a model can "see" how to move forward by making scenes that keep the maze's shape and move smoothly. People mostly see space with their eyes. We want models to do the same using video because it's simple and can grow. But learning space stuff just from regular video is tough, and many new ways still need extra help like depth maps or camera positions. In this paper, we show VIDEO4SPATIAL and prove that video models using just video scenes can be smart about space. Our setup is simple: a regular video model trained with a basic goal. It takes a video scene and instructions, then makes a video that finishes the task while keeping the space and time right.

We made a dataset called HEALTHCONTRADICT with 920 examples. Each example has a health question and two documents that say opposite things. There's also a factual answer backed by science. We used this dataset to test different language models, ranging from 1 billion to 8 billion parameters, to see how they handle health questions when there's conflicting information. We looked at how these models answer questions when given biomedical context and a language model. We checked two main things: how well language models answer health questions when there's conflicting info and how the biomedical context affects their answers. We tested this by giving the models correct, incorrect, or contradictory context and then checking how accurate they were. Our main points are: we made a new dataset called HEALTHCONTRADICT to test language models with conflicting biomedical info; we did a detailed evaluation of these models using this dataset to see how well they handle long, conflicting biomedical texts with clear metrics; and we compared general language models with their specialized biomedical versions, finding that the specialized ones are better at using correct info and ignoring wrong info. We made sure each document in the dataset is unique to keep the evaluation fair. For example, for the question "Can coffee help you lose weight?" there are two documents with opposite views (one says yes, the other says no), and the factual answer is yes, supported by scientific evidence that caffeine might help reduce weight, BMI, and body fat.

We're going to start by talking about the early work on language models that use Bayesian methods and n-grams, which were key in making systems that recognize speech by dealing with word sequences and uncertainties in what people say. These models helped create the systems we use now. Then, we'll look at newer speech models that work end-to-end, meaning they use context directly in the process. These models don't just focus on the words but also things like tone, emotion, and who is speaking, which helps in better understanding and generating speech. We'll also check out how large language models (LLMs) help fix mistakes in automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems. We'll pay special attention to fairness and bias issues, especially how different accents, dialects, and ways of speaking can affect speech processing. We'll talk about how new developments are tackling these problems and the ethical side of making sure systems work well for everyone. We'll go over some theories on how LLMs adapt and connect with speech input, including topics like measuring risk for different groups and estimating how models can be transferred from speech to LLM adaptation, which leads to different design ideas for spoken agents.

This paper talks about how different language categories can be learned by a computer setup with an adult language model named Delilah and a daughter agent. The daughter model is made for MODOMA and uses graphs to show grammar knowledge to make and understand sentences, which get better as language learning happens. Delilah has a parser and generator made by Cremers and Hijzelendoorn to work with Dutch grammar, using it to make and understand sentences. So, MODOMA needs a new way to study Delilah: besides making Delilah better at making sentences and understanding them, we need to study Delilah's output to help the daughter find patterns for learning. To do this, we can use ideas and methods from linguistics and other fields to understand the machine-made language like we do with human language. With the rise of big language models, checking if machine language is like human language is important. This paper shows results from experiments on this. Delilah's sentences made during talks with the daughter agent are separate examples, while corpora usually have connected sentences. Still, similar grammar patterns show up in both types of data. This might mean these patterns are more about the individual sentences than the whole corpora. The study shows that the different frequency patterns of language categories, known for human language data, are also in Delilah's output. Delilah isn't set up to copy these patterns, so the pattern comes from the grammar model used.

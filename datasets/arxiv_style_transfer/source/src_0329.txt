Big language models are getting better really fast, and people are figuring out new ways to use them to do good stuff. Since these models are being used more and more in online chats, there's a chance they could help people be more understanding and have better conversations. This study looks at whether these models can do more than just spot harmful stuff in conversations, like if they can actually help calm things down and solve arguments online. The idea is split into two parts: first, the model checks if a conversation is fair and looks at how people are feeling, and second, it writes messages that help people calm down and work things out. To see how good these models are at this, researchers made a big dataset from Reddit and came up with a way to test them using different methods like scoring based on rules, simulating users, and comparing results with what humans would do. The tests showed that models you pay for work better than free ones when it comes to reasoning and helping fix arguments. The study shows that these models have potential but also some limits when it comes to helping people get along online. These big language models are changing how people talk, learn, and work together. Besides stuff like translating languages or summarizing text, they're being used in areas like teaching, healthcare, and mental health. Since they're being used in places where people talk and argue online, there's a new idea to use them not just to automate tasks but to help people be kinder and have fairer conversations. One big problem online is when people get into nasty fights, where they keep attacking each other and things get more heated. This happens a lot because people can't see each other's faces or pick up on social cues.

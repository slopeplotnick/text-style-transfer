We've made Menta, a special model for predicting mental health issues using social media text. It's designed to be accurate and easy to use. Menta uses a training method that helps deal with uneven data and works well for different clinical tasks. Menta is good at balancing accuracy and efficiency, doing better than some other models and competing with large language models in six mental health tasks. We've also made it easy to use and share, with a setup that keeps things private and doesn't use too many resources. In the past, people used regular machine learning and models that needed a lot of manual setup to figure out mental health from social media posts. For example, Jiang and others used advanced word models to find mental health issues on Reddit and showed these models work better than older methods. Sarkar and others made a model that predicts depression and anxiety from Reddit posts better than models that only focus on one thing by using shared features. Other studies created tools to screen for suicide risk using deep learning, which could help with early intervention. More recently, large language models have been used in this area. One study used ChatGPT to classify mental health issues like stress, depression, and suicidality on social media without needing prior knowledge, showing these models can predict mental health issues. Another study focused on detecting depression from online text and found that fine-tuned models do much better than older models. Studies have also looked at how well these models can interpret things like depressive symptoms and emotional states, finding that using prompts with emotional cues and examples improves how well they work.

Our study shows that when you use prompt-engineered LLMs with Isolation Forest scores, they can do better than traditional JETs and ML methods by detecting anomalies well and explaining them in plain language. There are still issues like false alarms, costs, and using this in real life, but this research shows that AI can help with auditing by making it accurate and easy to understand. We’re the first to show that LLMs can work with tax ledger data, which is important because it needs to be clear and trustworthy. Our results suggest that using LLMs with traditional anomaly signals is a good way to balance accuracy, recall, and clarity. In the future, we’ll look at bigger and more varied data sets and see how well this works with different prompts and model changes. Our test only used one anonymous tax ledger and a made-up benchmark. Real ledgers vary by industry, systems, and regions, and it’s hard to get true labels. Fake labels from JETs might have biases, and fake anomalies are set by simulators. So, we still need to prove this works with bigger and different data sets and real audit labels. The model’s stability and clarity depend on how you phrase things and the model you use: small changes can affect results, and different models might not agree. Our AuditCopilot design helps reduce differences, but it’s not foolproof against changes in phrasing, tricky inputs, or model updates. Also, explanations in plain language might not always match the decision rules, even if auditors find them helpful. Using LLMs in financial audits looks good in tests, but there are risks like errors, bias, prompt issues, and changes in model weights. We emphasize that our method should help with decisions but needs human checks, controls, and rules before being used for real.

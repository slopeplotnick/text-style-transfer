The 48th International ACM SIGIR Conference on Research and Development in Information Retrieval is happening from July 13-18, 2025, in Padua, Italy. It's organized by ACM in New York, USA, and the details are on a website link. E-commerce sites like Amazon, Taobao, and JD have changed how people shop a lot. The part that makes sure the products shown are relevant is really important for how users feel about the site because showing stuff that doesn't match can mess up trust and money for these sites. So, figuring out how relevant products are to what people search for is a key part of how these sites work. Older ways of checking relevance, like matching words or using vectors, are common in online shopping sites to make sure searches work well. Methods that match words use things like TF-IDF and BM25 to figure out how well a search matches a product but aren't great at understanding meaning and context. Vector methods use BERT and similar tools to get the meaning between searches and products, which really helps with relevance. Even with these improvements, there are still two big problems with using vectors for specific topics: First, there aren't enough tough examples specific to the topic. Most methods do okay with some labeled data, but to get even better results, you need a lot of tough examples, which are hard to find and label right. Second, the models aren't great at reasoning and showing results because they're limited by their size, so simpler online models have trouble with complex reasoning and telling what's relevant. To make reasoning and understanding better, big language models are being used more for relevance tasks. But most of the time, these models use the last token to decide relevance and use soft labels to teach smaller models. This way of doing things doesn't really show which features affect relevance or help the smaller models get better at reasoning.

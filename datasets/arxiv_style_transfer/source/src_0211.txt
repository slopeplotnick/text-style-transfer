In the third stage, we focus on training the model to follow specific instructions for different tasks in Infinity-MM. We use a complete dataset that includes understanding documents, reasoning with charts, tasks centered around OCR, and conversations that go back and forth. We follow the Infinity-MM method, giving different weights to tasks: 35% to general visual question answering, 25% to understanding documents, 20% to reasoning with charts, 15% to OCR tasks, and 5% to conversations and specialized areas. We set the learning rate at 1e-5 and train for one round with a batch size of 512. The goal is to predict the next word using standard language modeling. We test our model, which is built for NPU, against the latest vision-language models using five different tests. We compare it with recent models like InternVL2 and Qwen2-VL, which are designed for different systems and have various sizes. Our AutoNeural model, trained to this third stage, offers the best mix of accuracy and training speed. We test it on five benchmarks that check different skills. MMStar looks at how well the model can answer visual questions that need several steps and common sense. HallusionBench checks how the model deals with language and visual tricks. MathVista_MINI tests how the model handles math problems using diagrams and charts. AI2D_TEST looks at how well the model understands diagrams and science from textbooks. OCRBench tests how well the model reads text in images from different scenes, documents, and signs. We measure how accurate the model is on each test and calculate the average score to see how good it is overall at handling different types of tasks.

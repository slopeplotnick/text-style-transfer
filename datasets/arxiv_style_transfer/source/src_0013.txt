Predicting stock prices is a big and ongoing problem in finance. Stock prices change a lot and react quickly to new information, so old methods of predicting them don't work well with things like news and market feelings. These old methods often miss what's really making prices change. Recently, there's been a lot more online text data and big improvements in how computers understand language, which has sped up research on using text to predict stock prices. But most studies using these new language models only do basic text analysis and quick predictions. They don't combine knowledge well or remember things long-term, which makes it hard for them to handle the fast-changing and complex financial markets. This shows we really need models that can remember and use important information over time. Financial markets are tricky because price changes usually come from many related events happening over time, not just one thing. Predicting stock prices based on news is different from just answering questions. You can't find the answer to "what will the stock price do tomorrow?" directly in news articles. Models need to build a picture of the market from different info, find similar past situations, and think deeply to really understand market patterns, not just match text. Financial texts are also messy, with lots of extra and irrelevant info that can hide the important signals, so filtering and matching the right content is really important.

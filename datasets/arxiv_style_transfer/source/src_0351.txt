We're talking about TACO, a way to adjust VLAs when they're being tested. It keeps the good generalization abilities of VLAs that have been trained before and makes sure the outputs fit the specific tasks they're meant for. We've come up with a way to estimate pseudo-counts in CFN that lets us measure changes in distribution without using a lot of computing power. TACO helps different VLA models succeed more often in both simulated and real-world tasks without needing long training times and can work quickly. In online RL, the agent interacts with the environment to explore different actions. Offline RL, on the other hand, learns from a set dataset and doesn't interact with the environment anymore. This is tough because there's a shift between the policy that made the data and the policy we're trying to learn. The offline data shows the distribution of the policy that made it, which might be very different from the policy we're learning. This difference can cause big errors when trying to estimate values for actions that don't fit the distribution. During the SFT stage of VLAs, the model covers many action modes, but successful actions for specific tasks usually happen in a smaller area. So, when actions are sampled, the policy might not choose the best ones. In TACO, we use an anti-exploration strategy that keeps the policy close to the successful data distribution. Unlike usual offline RL methods that use penalties based on uncertainty, our method uses a simple count-based approach, which fits well with VLA models and doesn't need value estimation. Count-based regularization has been used before in both classic RL and newer RLHF algorithms.

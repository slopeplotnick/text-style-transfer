We found that the model Menta works better for figuring out depression and stress than for spotting suicide risk. Both fine-tuned and regular language models, even the bigger ones, seem to do a better job with depression and stress tasks compared to suicide risk. For example, a review by Bauer in 2024 says language models often mess up when trying to understand subtle signs of suicidality because there’s less data for it and they rely on context or timing, which isn’t clear in single posts. Another study by Lamichhane in 2023 showed that models got an F1-score of 0.86 for depression detection but only 0.37 for suicidality detection using social media data. This shows that depression and stress are easier to spot because they have clearer patterns and more data, while suicidality is less common, more dependent on context, and harder for models to figure out. So, language models might be better for general screening like spotting depression or stress, but not as good for detailed suicide risk detection unless they use more types of data or track changes over time. There are some limits to what we found. Even though models like Qwen-3 (4B) can predict mental health issues well, there are things that make it hard to apply our findings to other situations. We only looked at social media posts, which means we didn’t include other important stuff like how people’s writing changes over time, their profile info, or how they interact online, which could help understand mental health better. Adding these things could make future research stronger and more realistic. Another issue is that we only focused on depression, stress, and suicidal thoughts, which are just a small part of mental health problems. Real mental health issues include things like anxiety, bipolar disorder, and emotional problems, which weren’t covered in our study. Also, we used public, anonymous social media data instead of data from actual clinical settings. This keeps things private and easy to repeat, but it doesn’t let us say much about safety, ethics, or long-term reliability in real healthcare situations.

To see how well SMP works, we use it to train for tough movement control tasks. In Section 8.1, we show how flexible SMP is by using an already trained model to make new control rules without needing the original movement data. By training one style-based model on a big dataset, we can use it to train for tasks in different styles, just by setting the model to the style we want. We can also make new styles by mixing the trained model with different styles. In Section 8.2, we show that one movement model can be used for different tasks like steering, finding a target, and playing dodgeball. Besides controlling how the character acts, in Section 8.3, we show that SMP can also handle how people and objects interact. By training the model on data about people and objects, SMP learns to handle both object and character movements, letting characters act like humans when dealing with objects. SMP also helps characters learn how to recover naturally, as shown in Section 8.4. Like other methods that match data distributions, the rules trained with SMP can create new skills not directly in the original data. In Section 8.5, when trained on a short walk-jog-run dataset, SMP naturally creates different ways of moving that help a character match various speeds and switch smoothly between movements. Lastly, to test how good SMP is at copying movements, we check it on tasks where it imitates single clips (Section 8.6). SMP can closely copy a wide range of dynamic and acrobatic moves, matching the quality of top imitation learning methods.

Changes in distribution after prompt optimization show how system prompts affect reasoning. We compared reasoning patterns before and after optimizing prompts. Figure 9 and Table 10 show that after optimization, the overall frequency of reasoning parts goes down. This happens because optimized prompts are usually shorter and simpler, with fewer extra instructions like "answer like Shakespeare," which make the reasoning longer and more varied. Because of this, the "Others" category drops a lot, meaning optimized prompts make the model's behavior more organized and easier to understand. The frequency of reasoning parts like Logical reasoning and Reframing also changes a lot, showing these behaviors depend heavily on how prompts are written. Optimization effects also depend on the type of task. For math problems, optimized prompts increase Calculation and Logical reasoning, which fits the needs of the task. But for other tasks, these prompts don’t bring out the same reasoning parts. For moral reasoning tasks, most reasoning parts drop in frequency, but Logical reasoning stays the main one compared to the others. Some reasoning parts are rare in all situations, like Backtracking and Backward chaining, probably because these models don’t have reflection abilities like RL systems.

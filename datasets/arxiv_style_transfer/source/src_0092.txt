We found out that using a deformable scan really helps the model work better. When we took it out and used the basic version of Mamba, the performance dropped a lot, so it's clear the deformable scan is important. We tried different designs to see which works best. Our design uses a mix of different blocks, labeled as "CCDGDG" for stages 1 to 6, where C stands for Conv, D for DDSM+FNN, and G for Gated-Conv blocks. We also tested designs with just two types of blocks, like "CCGGGG" and "CCDDD", and designs with only one type of block. The results showed our mixed design works better than the others. We also looked at some examples from InterHand2.6M and AssemblyHands. DF-Mamba gave more accurate joint positions, shown by fewer mistakes marked with colored ellipses. Even when joints are blocked or hard to see, our method still finds them well, showing it's strong in real-world situations. DF-Mamba is a new setup for estimating 3D hand poses, using efficient feature extraction at the start and a deformable representation later on. We tested it on five datasets with different hand interactions and types of data, and it did better than other setups and worked faster. Future work could include using DF-Mamba for more tasks like 3D human pose estimation, training it with a lot of outside data, using it for new hand-mesh reconstruction methods, and making it a base model that works well in different situations without needing specific adjustments.

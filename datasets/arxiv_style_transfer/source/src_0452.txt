Experts in numerical modeling take big ideas and turn them into numbers. They start with a main idea and break it down into smaller parts using numbers and details. They give each idea a chance of being right and use different ways to show how likely each detail is. They need to check if their number model works by comparing it to real data. This means they have to use numbers to predict what the data should look like, which is done by people who know a lot about the data. They use a test to see if their predictions are wrong, which involves looking at numbers and statistics. Experts in a method called Monte Carlo simulation will use the data to update their ideas and details using a method called Bayesian inversion. They make decisions based on how uncertain they are about their predictions. To do this well, all the experts need to talk to each other. Think about a self-driving car. It has to get from one place to another safely and legally, and not take too long. To do this, it has to steer, speed up, slow down, and steer again. It uses sensors to see what's happening on the road and predicts what it and others will do next. The main parts are: Rewards, which is the goal of being safe, legal, and quick; Actions, which are things like steering and braking; and State, which is what's happening on the road with other cars, people, and how fast they're going.

Using AI on your phone is a great way to keep track of mental health because it lets you get personal feedback quickly without sharing your private info. It works right on your phone, so you don't have to send your data to the cloud, which keeps it safer and doesn't rely on the internet as much. Plus, having these tools on your phone makes it easier for people to get help, especially if they live far away from clinics or have to wait a long time for appointments. Since the AI works directly on your phone, it gives fast feedback and can notice changes in how you talk or act that might show stress or mood problems. Using phones and gadgets to monitor mental health is becoming more popular because it's faster, uses less energy, and keeps your data private compared to systems that depend on the cloud. Recent studies have shown that small language models (SLMs) can run well on devices like the Samsung Galaxy S24 for things like helping with documents and predicting health, using less memory and working faster. These models are better for real-world use because they're easy to set up and don't need a lot of resources. They work without needing to constantly connect to the cloud, which helps protect your privacy. While some studies have shown SLMs can handle certain mental health tasks, there's still a lot they haven't covered. Most research has focused on simple tasks and hasn't worked well with more complicated data. Also, not much has been done to see how these models work on devices with limited resources. To fix these issues, we're introducing Menta, a new framework that uses a method called LoRA to handle multiple tasks and deal with uneven data. It's designed to work well across different mental health tasks while staying efficient and protecting privacy on everyday devices.

We looked into seven ways to combine models to reduce social bias using three bias tests and SuperGLUE with 13 large language models. We found that using SLERP with moderate settings is the best way to cut down bias without losing accuracy. It's important to check how well these methods work on tasks like reading comprehension and reasoning to reduce social bias. Lately, combining models has become a good way to use the strengths of different models without having to retrain them, according to Li and others in 2023 and Yang and others in 2024. This means mixing parts of two or more trained models to make one model that keeps and combines what each model knows. The linear averaging method, which was first used by Wortsman and others in 2022, is a simple way to do this by averaging the weights. It's a cheap way to bring in different skills because it uses models that are already trained. Some research by Matena and Raffel in 2022 and Lee and others in 2025 takes this further by adjusting each part based on its importance, making sure the combination makes sense. Other methods like sphere interpolation, mentioned by Shoemake in 1985 and Goddard and others in 2024, see these parts as points on a sphere. SLERP, from Shoemake in 1985, mixes two models, and the Karcher Mean, from Grove and Karcher in 1973, finds the average for several models. NuSLERP, from Goddard and others in 2024, adds a step to fix any size changes. Ilharco and others in 2022 came up with a way to use these ideas for specific tasks, using the idea of "task vector," which shows the difference between a pre-trained model and a fine-tuned one.

Our results match what Shirafuji and others found in 2025 using a task vector-based method. They also noticed that the debiased model worked well. But some other studies, like the one by Lutz in 2024, used model merging and showed how debiased large language models (LLMs) did on tasks using scores from NLI benchmarks. Our results show that methods only tested on tasks like NLI need to be checked more thoroughly with different datasets. SuperGLUE results show that except for Linear, SLERP, and Nearswap, other merging methods make LLMs worse at causal reasoning (see Section 5.2). So, these methods aren't good for reducing bias reliably. SLERP works well because it evenly mixes across the parameter space without making the bias too strong. On the other hand, another method called NuSLERP did normalization at the layer or tensor level, which hurt its SuperGLUE scores. These findings suggest that most new model-merging methods can't be used for reducing bias without losing a lot in reasoning ability, unlike SLERP. This study was the first to look at how seven model-merging methods affect social bias in LLMs. We tested 13 models from the GPT, LLaMA, and Qwen groups on three social bias datasets and the SuperGLUE benchmark and found a balance between fairness and usefulness. Also, four methods (Karcher Mean, NuSLERP, TIES, and DELLA) made performance worse on tasks needing reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning, like ReCoRD, COPA, CB, and BoolQ in the SuperGLUE benchmark. Some existing debiasing methods using model merging showed their debiased LLMs' performance on downstream tasks with NLI scores. But we found it's also important to check accuracy on tasks for reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning.

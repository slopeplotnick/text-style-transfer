The Long-Tail Hard subset is a group of tough examples meant to test how well advanced language models can handle difficult tasks. We focused on finding tricky cases, splitting them into two main types: clear requests (like ones with multiple attributes or multi-requirements) and hidden or indirect requests. The hidden ones fall into four categories: ones with negation, ones offering alternatives, ones needing specific knowledge, and ones requiring reasoning. We used different methods to find these. For negation and alternatives, we looked for specific keywords and patterns. For knowledge and reasoning-based ones, which are less common, we first reduced the number of easy examples and then used a strong language model (Qwen3-235B) to check if it could give the same answer four times out of eight tries. For multi-attribute requests, we used a tool to find queries with more than five attributes. To make sure these examples were really hard, we tested them with a basic model and kept only the ones where the model got it right five times or less out of eight tries, showing it struggled with them. The Visual Salience subset is about cases where the main product image is super important, like when the color or style in the picture is key to making the right decision. We started by using keywords based on a detailed visual category list to find these cases. Then, to avoid mistakes, we filtered out examples using a blacklist and kept only those where the important visual keyword wasn’t mentioned in the product’s text. After that, we used a language model to confirm that the keywords really referred to visual details. Finally, to pick the hardest examples, we trained a simple model that looks at both text and images and kept the ones where the model was very unsure, meaning it didn’t agree on the answer in most of its tries.

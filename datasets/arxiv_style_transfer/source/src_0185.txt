People are worried about migration and how it might affect them negatively. These worries are about what could go wrong. When we talked to people about the benefits, they also mentioned their fears about the risks they saw. Two important questions that helped us find out what people were afraid of were: - Do you think this approach is useful for the company's current processes? - What do you think about a platform to quickly develop similar software? We found seven different fears, which are shown in Figure 4. The main fears for IT folks are that it might be useless, too complicated, and people might resist change (see Table 7). In the end, everyone, no matter their role, wanted training and demonstrations. They want to understand how useful an SPL could be for their work and how to get into this new way of doing things. Based on what we know about good practices, benefits, risks, and fears, we've come up with some guidelines to help with moving to an SPL. These guidelines came from talks and decisions made in the company. In this part, we talk about what results we expect (Sect. 5.1), how to use good practices (Sect. 5.2), how to encourage future benefits (Sect. 5.3), how to lower risks (Sect. 5.4), how to calm fears (Sect. 5.5), the specific actions to take (Sect. 5.6), and a discussion (Sect. 5.7). Before the interviews, we wondered what kind of results we could expect. We thought people who know about or like reusability would be the most positive and interested. They should be motivated to start and support the project. In our case, the more experienced developers talked more about mindset, while the less experienced ones focused more on technical stuff. But this difference in experience didn't really change the amount or quality of the answers we got.

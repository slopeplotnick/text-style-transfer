We need to make sure devices like phones, laptops, smart assistants, drones, and car systems can handle bad prompts and responses safely without relying on the cloud. This helps with speed, privacy, and use in tight spots. Some recent studies have tried to make safety models that work in many languages, but they often don't do well with lots of different languages, especially ones that aren't well-supported. A common way to deal with this is to train a model in English or another well-supported language and test it in another language, using shared language features. This works better for languages that are similar or well-supported, but not so much for less common languages because they don't have much in common or aren't used much in training. To tackle this, we made CREST, a model for 100 languages with only 0.5 billion parameters, trained on 13 well-supported languages. We use a multilingual transformer model to group languages that are similar in meaning and structure. This helps the model work well across the group, so training it on a well-supported language helps less common ones in the same group. This is useful when there's not much data for safety applications in many languages. Even though CREST is small, it does better than other models and works well for both well-supported and less common languages. It uses shared words and scripts to generalize across languages.

Using robots in construction can really help with worker shortages and make things safer and more efficient. Robots are great at doing heavy and repetitive jobs without getting tired or making mistakes. This means using them can speed up construction and cut costs. But a single robot can't do everything because construction work is complicated. Building something like a wall needs different skills like moving materials, placing them right, welding, and checking quality. So, you need a bunch of robots working together. Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) breaks down big jobs into smaller ones and gives them to different robots, taking into account what each robot can do, what the job needs, and the work environment. MRTA problems are about whether robots do one job or many, whether one robot or many do a job, and whether jobs are quick or take time. The hardest part is getting lots of robots to do lots of jobs over time, which needs smart planning to deal with different robot skills, job needs, and timing. But most MRTA studies focus on warehouses or exploring environments, not much on construction.

When large language models (LLMs) are used in AI systems where different parts work together to handle complicated tasks, the role of LLMs becomes really important for making decisions later on. If there's a bias toward the first thing they process, it can mess up the whole system. So, fixing this bias is key to making sure these systems act in a fair and clear way. There are two main issues with this study. First, even though the length of the articles was kept the same, we didn’t specifically check how much the evaluation methods depend on how long the documents are. Since length might affect the results of the metrics we used, future research could group the data by length or do tests where length is controlled to better understand how the order of information matters. Second, the scores from the evaluation methods were pretty low overall. This shows a bigger problem with these kinds of evaluation methods when they’re used for summarizing, especially when comparing long documents to short summaries. Future studies should look at topics beyond abortion, like gun control, LGBTQ+ rights, and immigration, to see if this order bias happens with other controversial issues. It would also help to test different types of LLMs, like GPT or Claude, to see if this bias shows up in other models. Adding more ways to measure things, like checking for bias in opinions or how polarized the summaries are, could give a better understanding of how the order of information affects the summaries. We found that LLMs tend to favor the first article they read when summarizing multiple documents, especially in terms of meaning. This supports earlier findings that the order of information affects the output. This kind of sensitivity to order can be a problem for systems like AI summaries or AI setups where the first documents seen can have too much influence on what happens next. That’s why it’s important to deal with this bias to create systems that are more fair, clear, and reliable.

We looked at three related areas of work: methods that use outside feedback, ways to rely less on human labels, and the theory about how the way things are represented affects the quality of what's generated. Usually, to make large language models work well with what people like, they use something called Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). This method uses human preference data to train a model to give rewards, and then it adjusts the policy to get the best results while staying close to a reference model. This is how models like ChatGPT and Llama work, but there are problems like the data being costly and not always accurate, and the reward models can be tricked or may not work well in different situations. Because of these issues, some methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), KTO, Bayesian RLHF, and MaxMin-RLHF try to improve the policy directly by comparing pairs without needing a specific value network, but they still need carefully chosen preference data. There are also models that give scores and feedback for reasoning and evaluation. These methods keep the supervision outside the model's main processes. Another approach is to use automatic supervision or signals from the model itself to reduce the need for human labels. Some methods like DeepSeek-R1 and TÜLU 3 use programmatic feedback instead of human input.

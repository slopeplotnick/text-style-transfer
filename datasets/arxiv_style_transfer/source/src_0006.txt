Based on what we found in RQ2, we saw that Hallucination Detection (HD) and Fact Verification (FV) work well together and have different ways of failing. Now we're looking at RQ3: Can we make these work together in a really good hybrid system? To do this, we came up with two ways to combine them: one is called Score-Level Fusion, and the other is an Evidence-Aware Pipeline, which is meant to fix some of the problems FV has, as we found in our study. The second way, the Evidence-Aware Pipeline, is different because while the first way makes things stronger by combining scores, it doesn't really fix the problems we talked about in Section 4.5. FV systems work well when they have evidence to back them up (saying something is either "Supported" or "Contradicted"), but they don't do well when there's no evidence (which we call "Not Enough Information" or NEI). To fix this, we made an Evidence-Aware Pipeline that looks for outside information first and only uses inside signals if it can't find anything: (1) Step 1: Retrieval & Verification. An FV module (like LLM-QA) gets evidence and checks if the claim matches the evidence, saying it's Supported, Contradicted, or NEI. (2) Step 2: Conditional Branching. We use a decision-making process. If FV gives a clear answer (Supported or Contradicted), we go with that. But if it says NEI, we admit there's no outside support. Instead of trying to force a check that might be wrong, we skip the FV and use an HD method (like LNPE), getting the final score just from the model's own uncertainty signals. This setup helps deal with FV's problems when it can't find the right information. By separating the need for outside info from inside signals, we avoid mistakes from bad evidence retrieval, making sure we can still detect things well even when we don't have outside knowledge.

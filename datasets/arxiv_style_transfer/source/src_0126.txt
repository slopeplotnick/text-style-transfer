Position bias is when language models pay more attention to information in certain parts of the input and ignore other parts. Even though these models are really advanced at processing language, studies have shown they can still be influenced by how the input is arranged. For example, in tasks like summarizing multiple documents combined into one, the order of the documents can affect what gets included in the summary. This means summaries might focus more on certain parts and leave out other important details, depending on where the information is placed. One example of this is called the Lost in the Middle problem. Researchers found that these models do best when the important information is at the beginning or the end of the input, but they don’t do as well when it’s in the middle. This pattern was seen in tasks like answering questions or pulling out key details from multiple documents. Other studies also found that when models are used to judge or evaluate things, they tend to favor the first or last option they see, even if the best choice is somewhere else. This is often called lead bias, where the start of the input gets too much attention. Some models, like GPT-3.5-Turbo, don’t show this bias as much, but others are very sensitive to content that comes early, especially in tasks where the summaries are really short. This kind of bias doesn’t just hurt performance but can also lead to fairness problems. For instance, if tweets are grouped by dialect and ordered in a certain way, the summaries might favor the group that appears first. This shows that the order of the input can have a big impact.

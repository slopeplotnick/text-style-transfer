Understanding real-world scientific laws is important for accurately predicting physical outcomes in different situations. But current video tests are based on basic physical knowledge and don't really show how well video models can think scientifically. We've created VideoScience-Bench, a test to see how well video models understand science at a college level. Each test question includes a mix of scientific ideas that need to be understood and thought through to get the right result. The test has 200 questions covering 14 topics and 103 ideas in physics and chemistry. We did expert evaluations on seven top video models in T2V and I2V settings, looking at four areas: staying the same, correct changes, keeping things in order over time and space, and matching the expected result. We use a VLM-as-a-Judge to check the videos, and it matches well with what humans think. As far as we know, VideoScience-Bench is the first test to check if video models can not only make videos but also think scientifically, making sure their videos show the right physical and chemical phenomena. You can find our data and evaluation code at github.com/hao-ai-lab/VideoScience. The releases of Sora-2 and Veo-3.1 show a big change in video-making: video models now make videos that look like movies and can understand the world in new ways. As video models get better, people are starting to think about what makes a "good" video differently, moving past just quality measures like Fréchet Video Distance and Fréchet Video Motion Distance to look at whether the videos follow basic physical rules and show understanding of the world. New models can even think on their own without training, solving problems that need scientific, mathematical, and spatial thinking. People trained in science understand the world using scientific rules and predict what will happen, like if citric acid will clean iron stains or what happens if an LED is powered with or without a resistor. This brings up our main question: besides looking good and following basic physical rules, can video models show scientific events correctly and think scientifically about the real world? Understanding the world based on scientific rules is important for discovering new things because it helps models predict what will happen in real-world situations. Unlike basic world understanding, true scientific understanding needs thinking about many connected ideas, like how specific-heat and heat-transfer principles show that water will absorb heat.

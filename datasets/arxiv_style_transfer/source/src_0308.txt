In language models, checking Bayesian rationality means seeing if they can learn from evidence in a way that matches Bayes's theorem. Gupta and others in 2025 showed that language models can update their beliefs based on lots of coin flips, even if they start with a biased view. But in real-life situations with complicated language, Qiu and others in 2025 found that language models don't change their beliefs about what users like, as the Bayesian framework would expect. Falck in 2024 showed that language models don't learn in a Bayesian way when given context. Zhao in 2021 and Wang in 2023 showed that language models react differently depending on how examples are arranged in prompts. There are ideas to make learning or reasoning more Bayesian, like copying what an ideal Bayesian thinker would do, using abstract reasoning, and mixing abduction and deduction. But these ideas often need extra structures like Bayesian networks, which makes them hard to use in practice. Language models have various biases because they're trained on lots of human data. They also show different biases than humans when used as judges, like favoring certain positions, liking long answers, preferring positive expressions, and being overly certain. Among all these biases, confirmation bias is especially important because it's thought to cause polarization. It's also believed that most biases are types of confirmation bias. So, we focus on confirmation bias and use belief entrenchment to study it in language models.

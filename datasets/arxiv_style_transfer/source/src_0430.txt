We use three different emotion datasets to train our model and check how well it works. EMER gives us good quality human-checked reasoning-label pairs and is used to start our training. DFEW and MAFW are big datasets that include different facial expressions in video, audio, and text. You can find more details about these datasets in Appendix 7.1. Like others before us, we report UAR and WAR for how accurate the recognition is, and three other measures—EEA, EPC, and FCR—to see how well the explanation matches the emotion and the model's guess. You can find the exact definitions and how we annotate in Appendix 7.2. For the setup, we use the design of R1-Omni, with SigLIP as the vision encoder, Whisper-large-v3 as the audio encoder, and BERT as the text encoder. Each type of data output is turned into the LLM embedding space using visual and audio projectors, which are two linear layers to fit the LLM representation size. For example, a 1-second audio clip gives about 50 audio features, and one video frame gives 729 visual features. The LLM backbone is Qwen2.5-7B, and we start with model weights from the HumanOmni checkpoint to keep the pretrained multimodal representations. To see how well our setup works, we compare MIGR with other methods for understanding emotions on the MAFW and DFEW datasets. We split previous methods into two groups: one that directly predicts emotion labels and another that makes textual explanations before predicting. We first compare MIGR with other models that generate reasoning to make sure the reasoning texts our model creates are emotionally consistent.

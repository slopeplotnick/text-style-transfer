How specific or detailed a prompt is can affect how well large language models (LLMs) perform when solving problems, but this hasn’t been studied much yet. This paper talks about a framework called DETAIL that looks at how LLMs do when given prompts with different levels of detail. Using GPT-4, the researchers made prompts with different levels of specificity, measured how specific they were using perplexity, and checked how accurate the answers were by comparing them with GPT-based semantic equivalence. They tested this on 30 new reasoning tasks using GPT-4 and O3-mini and found that more specific prompts usually led to better accuracy, especially for smaller models and tasks that involve following steps. The study shows that it’s important to adjust prompts based on the situation and gives tools and data for more research. LLMs have changed a lot of things because they’re really good at generating text, and they keep getting better very quickly. They’re not just good at simple tasks anymore—they’re beating the best methods in many areas. A lot of recent tests focus on how well these models can solve math problems or multi-step problems. Improving how LLMs reason is important, and new methods have been developed to help with this by using better prompts. Some advanced strategies, like Chain-of-Thought, Tree of Thoughts, and Graph of Thoughts, break problems into smaller steps to help the models reason better. But one big issue is figuring out how detailed prompts should be to get the best results. Detailed prompts can guide the model well but might limit how it thinks, while vague prompts give it more freedom but can lead to confusion or mistakes.

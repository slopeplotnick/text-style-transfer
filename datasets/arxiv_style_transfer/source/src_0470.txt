People are worried about how much energy things use, like phones and big computer centers. Developers need to know exactly how much energy their software uses to fix any problems. Before, people mostly looked at big parts, like whole programs or specific functions, instead of looking at each line of code. They also mostly guessed how much energy the CPU used and didn't think about other parts of the hardware, which made it hard to understand and explain. We came up with a new way to fix these problems by not just looking at the CPU and not just guessing. We focus on predicting energy use for programming languages like Java, Kotlin, or Scala that run on the JVM. We look at how the code turns into Java bytecode and measure the energy it uses. Then, we make a model using Bayesian statistics, which helps us predict energy use and see what factors affect it. The model looks at three things you can get from the code: the size of the data, the type of data, and the operation, plus one thing about the hardware: the device. To see if our method works, we tried it with Java, looked at Java's bytecode, and tested it on new programs. We found that all four factors matter, especially that two devices of the same model can use different amounts of energy, and that operations and data types can change energy use a lot. Our tests showed that our energy predictions are close to the real energy use of programs, proving our method works. Our work is a way to make an energy model that others can use in the future for things like checking how energy-efficient programs are.

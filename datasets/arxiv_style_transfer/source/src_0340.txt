We check how well episodic memory retrieval works over different time lengths of events by using something called temporal intersection over union (tIoU). This tells us how much the parts we find match the real parts by comparing their overlap to their total length. We look at how WorldMM stacks up against other models in areas like temporal grounding, single-modality retrieval, long-form egocentric video retrieval, and picking keyframes. You can find more about the basics in Section C.1. Table 3 shows that WorldMM gets much better tIoU scores than other strong models. Methods that rely on reasoning and keyframe picking have lower tIoU scores, which means they struggle with long input contexts. Figure 5 shows that better tIoU scores go hand-in-hand with higher overall accuracy, especially when it comes to understanding long videos compared to just doing one-step retrieval. This improvement happens because doing multiple rounds lets the retrieval system get more useful info and improve its strategy if the first tries aren't great. An example of this improvement is in Section E.2, where the model fixes a wrong retrieval to give a more accurate and relevant answer. We check how fast our system works by measuring the time it takes for WorldMM to handle 100 random questions from EgoLifeQA. Figure 6 shows that our method balances speed and accuracy better than others. Long-video LLMs take a lot longer to process but don't perform as well. RAG- or memory-based methods are faster but need a lot of setup and don't perform as well. WorldMM, on the other hand, finishes its rounds when needed and only gets the parts it needs, so it works quickly and accurately.

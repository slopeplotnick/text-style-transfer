The authors of a book on Empirical Software Engineering talk about Victor R. Basili, who was a big deal in Empirical Software Engineering. They say it's important to use real data and evidence to make software engineering better. Lots of studies have looked at how to use Software Product Line Engineering (SPLE) in businesses and how it affects agile development. These studies came up with some good ideas and strategies for how to do it. They gave us some useful tips on how to move forward with changing over. But none of them did a study like ours, which happens after adoption and before implementation. In this paper, we showed the first part of a big plan to help a company change its code to a Software Product Line (SPL). We did interviews that were made to fit the company, especially considering the different roles of people involved in the project. Our aim was to find out the best ways to get ready for the change, focusing on questions that would tell us about current good practices we can use, the benefits and risks people see, and what they're worried about. Coming up with good solutions to the risks and worries, using the benefits, and encouraging good practices will be really helpful for a smooth changeover. We found out the company already does some good things that will help and has a culture of trying new things, which is good for the project. Most people involved think the change will be beneficial. The main worries are about how complicated SPL is and how to keep it up.

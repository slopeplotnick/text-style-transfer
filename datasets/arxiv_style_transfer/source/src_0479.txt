Let's talk about how AI is used in exploration and some common mistakes people make. Later, I'll guess how AI might change in exploration, focusing on decision-making. The main goal of exploration is to find something new. You can find things near known places (brownfield) or in new areas (greenfield). In the next ten years, we'll probably find more stuff in brownfield areas than in greenfield ones. Exploration is about narrowing down where to look by figuring out where valuable stuff might be. It usually starts by looking at big areas, like large igneous provinces, where conditions are right for magma to come up and get stuck in a way that's worth mining. At first, geologists use maps and data from the air. They spot weird things in the data that might mean something interesting is underground. Then they check it out more closely on the ground with fieldwork, surveys, and soil tests. The next step is the most expensive: drilling to see if their ideas about what's underground are right. Ideally, these decisions are made using smart methods to keep costs down by understanding uncertainty and the value of information. Different data sources help us understand how the mineral system formed. Data is used to predict where to look. It's not just about finding minerals but also testing ideas about the geology. Data helps reduce uncertainty about what's underground. Decisions are made, and the goals of these decisions can change during the exploration project.

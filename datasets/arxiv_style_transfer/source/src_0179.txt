This paper talks about how AI can be used in finding important minerals. It's my own take on the topic. I've been working with data science, decision science, and AI in earth resources for 30 years, so I've got some opinions and observations to share. Back in 1998, I wrote a paper about using neural networks with outcrop data to make reservoir models more realistic. I was a young professor trying to impress everyone at a big conference in New Orleans, even though a hurricane was coming. Making these models realistic is important for predicting how fluids move, which helps make better decisions in oil and gas exploration. In another paper in 2002, I showed that neural networks can learn from borehole and geophysical data to predict reservoir properties like porosity, even in areas without boreholes. A big part of that work was figuring out how to deal with the different scales of data, like from a borehole core versus a seismic signal deep underground. This paper will talk a lot about that scale issue. While oil and gas companies always consider geological uncertainty before building offshore platforms, many mines still use straightforward models. In exploration, using straightforward models and data interpretation is common, and the minerals industry hasn't really caught up with the oil and gas industry in terms of assessing risk quantitatively.

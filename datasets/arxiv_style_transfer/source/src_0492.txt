When concepts aren't clearly defined in data features, people often use probing to find neurons or neuron groups that best show target concepts. These probes are usually linear, but they can be non-linear if the concept isn't linearly separable. Probes can be trained in two ways: supervised, which needs labeled data for the concept people want, and unsupervised, which finds concepts important to the model but not necessarily understandable to humans. This work goes beyond just looking at observations to model structural equations. It considers different levels of modeling, from FSCMs that are easy to understand with domain knowledge to PSCMs that include all FSCMs that fit the data. This shows how important it is to choose the right modeling approach and how partial information can help when full FSCMs aren't available. Galhotra and others in 2021 talk about an XAI method that uses FSCMs to calculate probabilities of sufficiency and necessity. They focus on tabular data and add causal modeling to data features, assuming these are high-level concepts and allowing for more concepts not in the model input. This method only works when features are meaningful concepts and doesn't need a causal generator. It assumes there's a preference for the model output explanation, showing which input values can change a prediction to a better one or which values are needed to keep a good prediction when the outcome is preferred unchanged.

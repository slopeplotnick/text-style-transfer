When it comes to SWE agents, people have tried to figure out what they’re good at and where they don’t do well. Studies show that these agents have a hard time handling problems involving multiple files. Some researchers say we need better ways to evaluate them, looking at things like how efficient, reliable, and smart they are. Other studies have looked into why these agents fail, finding issues like not being able to locate faults properly, struggling with complex code connections, and having trouble keeping track of context across different files. One group created TRAIL, which is a list of errors and a benchmark with 148 traces that were checked by humans. Another group came up with MAST, which is a way to classify failures in multi-agent systems. They looked at 150 traces from five systems and five benchmarks, but they needed humans to annotate the data for their analysis. Another study made a flow graph to show agent actions like understanding projects, keeping track of context, and fixing and testing things. But their work was done manually and only covered a small part of the data, like bug localization for 20 out of 500 issues. There’s also research that made a list of ways LLM tools fail during SWE tasks, breaking it down into three phases, nine categories, and 25 smaller subcategories. They used this list to figure out why tools don’t work and found problems like agents getting stuck in repetitive loops. But their analysis was also done manually on 150 out of 500 issues.

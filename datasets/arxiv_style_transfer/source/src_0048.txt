When you try different noise patterns in flow-matching or diffusion setups, the randomness of the starting noise affects what actions come out. This paper looks at how to deal with changes in data distribution by not exploring too much and introduces something called Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-Counts (TACO). The idea of anti-exploration comes from offline reinforcement learning, where you try to stop the policy from doing things that aren't in the data set. Similarly, during VLA inference, we want to keep the actions within the successful patterns in the SFT dataset and avoid exploring unnecessary or irrelevant actions from pretraining or not-so-great fine-tuning data. In TACO, we focus on adjusting how actions are sampled during testing instead of changing the policy, because flow-matching or diffusion-based VLA models have complicated sampling that doesn't work well with regular RL updates. Instead, TTS changes the action sampling without touching the VLA's settings, so no gradient calculation is needed. We use a classic Coin-Flipping Network (CFN) with a special internal setup to estimate pseudo-counts for each observation-instruction-action piece in the SFT dataset. Basically, an action piece with a higher pseudo-count matches more with the common successful actions in the SFT data, so it's preferred. When making decisions, TACO uses the pseudo-counts from the CFN to pick the best action piece from several options, going with the one that has the highest pseudo-count. To cut down on the time it takes to process multiple tests, we also add a shared observation key-value cache, which speeds things up by reusing visual-language info across samples.

Gurung and Lapata (2025) came up with something called VR-CLI to help figure out the best way to plan by using a lot of reasoning over the story info and giving a long answer. VR-CLI is like a reward system that tries to make it more likely to come up with the right next chapter based on the story info and plan. We made VR-CLI better by adding a part that makes it less likely to pick a random chapter from the same book. LiteReason is our usual way where we sometimes do a small training step using the paths made during that time. This costs a bit more in terms of computing, but it updates the reasoning part with the current model's thinking. We also have a hidden thought prompt that helps the model use hidden thought tags. LiteReason without SFT keeps the hidden prompt and the starting Reasoning Projector that uses the hidden thought tags to know when to give new reasoning to the model, but it doesn't do SFT during training to update the Reasoning Projector. LiteReason without the Reasoning Projector checks how well it works after training without the Reasoning Projector but with the hidden thought prompt. This way doesn't use hidden reasoning and checks if the model acts differently if we skip reasoning steps. We look at recent hidden reasoning studies and pick some past methods to test the ideas in section 3. We test these on Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks. For training-free hidden reasoning, we compare with two new methods: Mixture of Inputs (MoI) by Su et al. (2025), which mixes the sampled token's embedding with others, and Soft Thinking by Zhang et al. (2025), which also mixes token embeddings but uses an entropy measure to decide when to stop continuous thinking and start discrete sampling.

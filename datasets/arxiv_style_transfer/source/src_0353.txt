A Software Product Line (SPL) is basically a way to take a bunch of related software systems that share some common features and turn them into something that makes it easier and faster to create new software. This process, where you take an existing code base and turn it into an SPL, is pretty complicated and takes a lot of time. It can also mess with the way a company is used to doing things when it comes to developing software. This paper talks about the first steps we took to actually start doing this kind of transformation. We worked with a company called ITK - Predict and Decide, which is a small business that decided to turn its code base into an SPL. Our job as researchers was to help them through this process. Their code base is built around something called Cross, which is a framework where they already reuse a lot of their software. It’s kind of like an SPL, but it doesn’t have the usual models and processes that a proper SPL has. To help their developers, we planned to slowly change how their code base and engineering practices were set up. The first thing we did was look at how the people in the company felt about this change and gave them advice to make the process easier. This paper shares what we found out from that first study. We talked to people in the company to get their thoughts on things like their current ways of working, what they hoped to get out of the SPL, and what they were worried about. Most people were on board with the idea of switching to an SPL and wanted to be part of it. Developers were especially excited and saw it as both a technical and management challenge. People who worked more on the business side were a bit more cautious and brought up concerns like making maintenance harder or losing creativity, especially when it came to designing user interfaces.

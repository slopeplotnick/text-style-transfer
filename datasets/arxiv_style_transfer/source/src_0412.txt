There's a big need for mental health help, but people aren't getting diagnosed enough worldwide. Things like clinics not being open enough, hard-to-reach places, and not enough workers make it hard for people to get checked out and understand their mental health early on. Other problems like how things are set up and personal issues also slow down diagnosis for people with serious mental health problems. The old ways of diagnosing don't match up with how much help people need and what's available. In-person sessions and self-reported questionnaires can't keep up because of not enough workers, high costs, differences between places, and stigma. Waiting times for mental health help can be anywhere from 3 to 18 months, depending on where you are and how many providers are available. There's a need to find early signs of mental health issues quickly, so we need better ways to spot them early and give support on time. Portable solutions like small language models can help detect and intervene in mental health issues on a bigger scale. New tech that works on devices like smartphones can help find mental health problems early by looking at social media posts and apps in real-time. Using language data from social media on smartphones lets us monitor mental states without being too obvious, helping spot mood changes, stress, or suicide risk early. These systems can help prevent suicide and notice patterns in social media that relate to mental health. When these models run on personal devices, they keep user privacy while checking language for signs of depression or stress. This way, monitoring mental health can happen outside of clinics and be more accessible to more people.

We came up with a way to make knowledge graphs (KGs) from pictures by using AI models that work with both text and images, and we guide them with specific rules for a certain topic. These rules, called an ontology, explain the important things and connections we care about, and we include them in the instructions to make sure the AI focuses on the right details in the picture. When we give the AI a picture, it creates a list of key things, their features, and how they relate to each other, based on the rules. These lists are turned into a graph that explains what’s in the picture. The shape of the graph depends completely on the rules we set up. Each picture gets its own graph, and we save these graphs in a database. Even though each picture has its own graph, some parts of the graphs are the same because the rules use a fixed set of terms. This means all the graphs are connected and form one big graph. This big graph helps us find similarities between pictures by comparing their smaller graphs. To describe what’s in a picture, we use the AI to pull out important details and turn them into a structured list. These details are about big ideas in the picture, and the topic-specific rules make sure we describe all pictures in the same way. To make the AI work with these rules, we include them in the instructions when the AI is figuring things out. This lets us make clear graphs for each picture and combine them into one big graph. The smaller graphs for each picture are linked through shared details, which makes it easier to compare pictures in the whole collection.

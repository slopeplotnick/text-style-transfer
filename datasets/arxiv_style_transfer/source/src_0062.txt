Switching to an SPL approach means making big changes in how the organization works. Managing software products is already tricky, as shown in a survey by Springer and Miler. With SPL, teams work together more and share a set of tools and resources that can be used in different projects. This helps save time and resources, speeds up getting products to market, and lets teams focus more on new ideas. Reusing components, customizing based on configurations, and having clear documentation make things run smoother and more efficiently. It takes careful planning and managing to make this change, but in the end, it helps the organization work better together and adapt more easily. At ITK, we talked about the results and plans with the development team. They wanted to stay informed and be part of the process, so we worked closely with them throughout the whole migration. We compared what we found at ITK with other reports about how SPL has been adopted and the strategies used. ITK’s plan follows the IDEAL improvement model. The project started when the team decided to move forward with the migration. We began by looking at how things were done and how the project was seen. The next steps are making a strategy and action plan, carrying it out, and then, in a typical agile way, learning from what worked and what didn’t to keep improving. Our guidelines help in setting up the action plan. Some challenges we faced were different from those mentioned in other reports, like not having enough skilled people or management support, because the company’s mindset didn’t see those as issues.

We did some big tests using UniFact with different data sets, model types like LLaMA and Qwen, and ways to check facts. We learned a few important things that answer our research questions. First, for RQ1, there's no one best way to do things; how well something works depends a lot on the model and the task. Second, for RQ2, we found strong proof that different methods work well together. HD and FV methods do well on different kinds of factual mistakes, showing they catch different but helpful parts of what's true. Lastly, for RQ3, we showed that mixing both methods works better than using them alone, setting a new best way to find factual errors. This means two things: for researchers, it's time to work together more instead of separately, and for people using these methods, it's clear you need to mix model-based and evidence-based checks to be sure about facts. In short, this paper does a few main things: it looks at the differences between Hallucination Detection and Fact Verification and why that matters for AI progress; it introduces UniFact, a way to fairly compare HD and FV; it shows with big studies that HD and FV are different but work well together; and it proves that mixing both methods beats using just one. We also explain what Fact Verification and Hallucination Detection mean. Even though people often mix up these terms, it's important to know the difference. FV checks if text is true in general, while HD checks if text made by a model is factually right. We explain these in detail in sections 2.1 and 2.2 and show how to measure them together in section 2.3.

The best models like GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o make very few mistakes, doing as well as or even better than humans (see the left side of Figure 8). Except for Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, Figure 8 shows that more accurate models tend to act more like Bayesian models. But this link between accuracy and behavior isn't a rule. For instance, a model that perfectly predicts everything might look like a simple model and not show any probabilistic behavior. When we change the context by giving a range of past observations, the behavior of large language models (LLMs) changes a lot. As shown in the top right of Figure 6, their sequential behavior drops, especially when we give them a biased range, as seen on the left of Figure 6, where errors go up (the triangles are usually higher than the circles). This shows that LLMs tend to rely on the information given to them. From the middle of Figure 8, we notice that not all models that do well in terms of NRMSE also combine cues efficiently. GPT-5 Mini, even though it does well in NRMSE, isn't good at combining cues. This is clear in the maze distance task, where GPT-5 Mini does great with text but not as well with images. This means it should reduce the importance of image input more, but it can't do it enough (check Appendix A.12 for more info). However, in the line length ratio task (see bottom right of Figure 6), many LLMs can reduce the importance of images when there's noise, showing they can adapt in a Bayesian way.

Lately, progress in large language models (LLMs) has been mostly about using more computing power during testing. For example, models like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-R1 create longer "thinking" steps where they work through ideas, make guesses, and fix mistakes before giving a final answer. This approach of doing more reasoning during testing has become a trend because it often makes the models more accurate and better at solving problems. But just making the reasoning process longer doesn’t always help and can even make things worse, like when the model overthinks. Research shows that correct answers often come from shorter reasoning steps. Another idea is to run multiple reasoning processes at the same time and combine their results, usually by picking the most common answer when the task has a clear solution. This works well for things like math problems or multiple-choice questions. However, many real-world tasks don’t have one right answer. For example, coding tools need to create programs that actually work, and research tools often need to use other tools, make detailed plans, or give long explanations. In these cases, you can’t just pick the most common answer because there isn’t one clear answer to choose from, even though this method works great for simpler tasks. This leads to an important question: Can we use the benefits of running multiple reasoning processes at the same time for tasks that don’t have one clear answer, without relying on picking the most common result?

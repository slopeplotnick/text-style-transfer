We use some standard methods like majority, random, and SVM as our starting points. We also check how Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Decision Tree perform and consider them as basic methods too. We adjust a few transformer models like DistilBERT, BanglaBERT, mBERT, and XLM-RoBERTa and evaluate them in detail. We talk a lot about the challenges in modeling and the limits of the dataset. The rest of the paper is set up like this: Section 2 looks at past work and gives background on detecting hate speech in Bangla. Section 3 explains how we did the project. Section 4 goes into how we implemented it. Section 5 shows the results of our evaluation. Section 6 talks about the challenges we faced. Section 7 discusses what each person contributed. Section 8 wraps it up with the conclusion. Social media is a big part of everyone's life now. It lets people communicate quickly, share information easily, and exchange opinions no matter where they are. But it also allows hate speech and harsh language to spread because of this freedom. Sadly, not much effort has been put into finding hate speech on Bangla social media, and there's no high-quality dataset for it. The words used on social media are often very different from those in traditional print media, making it hard to automatically spot Bangla hate speech. Traditionally, finding hate speech has been seen as a supervised classification problem. Early research used word lists and rules, but these often missed hidden or context-specific hate. More recent research uses machine learning methods like logistic regression and SVMs. To find online threats or harmful content, traditional supervised learning methods have been used.

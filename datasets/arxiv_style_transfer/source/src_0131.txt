We looked into three main questions. First, we wanted to understand how programming agents perform when it comes to process-related measurements. What are their key traits, and what affects these measurements? Second, we analyzed how these agents solve problems. What are their usual approaches, what influences their behavior, and how much do they stick to their planned methods? Do they change their strategies while working? Third, we checked for patterns of inefficiency in their behavior. What common issues or bad habits do they have, and how often do these show up? For this, we studied two popular programming agents, SWE-agent (SA) and OpenHands (OH). We used their standard settings: SWE-agent has a $2 cost limit per task, and OpenHands runs up to 100 steps. Both use their usual tools for viewing files and editing code. We tested these agents with four language models: DeepSeek-V3 671B MoE (DSK-V3), DeepSeek-R1 (DSK-R1), Devstral-small-2505 24B (Dev), and Claude Sonnet 4 (CLD-4). These models include both general-purpose and reasoning-focused ones, as well as advanced and open-source options. This gave us eight combinations of agents and models to analyze. We calculated process-related measurements for each combination, as shown in Table 1. Figure 3 displays the results, with green bars for solved issues and pink bars for unsolved ones. The blue row at the top shows how often issues were resolved, and the heatmap cells show the middle value and the range of variation for each measurement. Darker colors mean higher values. When comparing the agents, we found that OpenHands had more complex behavior than SWE-agent, as shown by its higher measurement values.

The RAIR benchmark has 63,601 samples from 14 different e-commerce areas. It's made up of three parts: the General part with 46,775 samples, the Long-Tail Hard part with 10,931 samples, and the Visual Salience part with 5,895 samples. Table 7 shows details about the five tough query types in the Long-Tail Hard part. For each entry, we give the query, item title, item details, SKU info, and an anonymous item image. Each entry also has a manual ground truth label based on our four-level relevance scale from L1 to L4. Table 8 shows how these labels are spread across the dataset. To help with model development and error checking, each data entry has rule identifiers from our Rule Checklist that help judge its relevance. In this section, we check and analyze the methods we suggest using the RAIR benchmark. Our experiments have two parts: first, we use numbers to check two main questions: (1) how much better the model gets with fine-tuning; (2) the benefits of the LORE model with different data parts like general, hard, and visually important samples. Second, we use examples to show what new things our methods let the model do and what problems they help solve. To keep evaluation results stable and repeatable, all trained models use a straightforward decoding method for single-pass inference, without adding randomness.

So, basically, when solving the problem, SWE-agent Dev first finds the bug in the client.py file, then makes a test to reproduce it, and edits different parts of the file. After trying to fix it twice and failing, it looks at another part of the file, runs the test to check the fix, and then submits the patch. On the other hand, SWE-agent DSK-V3 also starts by finding the bug in the client.py file but only edits it once and submits the patch without checking it. Even though both agents eventually succeed, they make some mistakes along the way. SWE-agent Dev edits the file line by line and repeats a failed edit twice before making a final edit, while SWE-agent DSK-V3 makes the patch in one go. Neither agent runs tests to check the patch, with SWE-agent Dev only checking if the reproduction tests work and SWE-agent DSK-V3 just thinking about whether the patch is correct with edge cases. This example shows that analyzing the process can give more insights into how agents solve or fail to solve problems. Recently, people have tried to analyze how agents work by looking at their steps and finding out where they fail, but this manual analysis can be biased and incomplete, and it doesn't work well for new systems. Plus, just looking at steps in order doesn't really show how agents behave or solve problems, like how they execute tasks or plan efficiently.

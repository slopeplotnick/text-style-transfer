This paper talks about a problem and suggests a new way to think about safety. Instead of seeing safety as something that only applies to individual models, it should be seen as something that comes from how systems work together. The paper introduces a concept called the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon (ESRH) to help understand when and why systems can become unstable. It explains this idea step by step: starting with how two things interact, moving to how multiple agents work together, then looking at different types of risks, and finally suggesting a way to design systems, called Institutional AI, to handle these risks. Right now, language models are designed to be safe by focusing on how humans interact with them, using things like prompts and filters to block harmful content or make sure they follow rules. This works by assuming that the model’s behavior is easy to see and doesn’t change much. But language models are complex and depend on things like their internal state, past conversations, and how they’re being used. Small changes in context can lead to different outcomes, even if they seem to follow the rules on the surface. The problem gets bigger when models start working with other models. In these setups, one model’s output becomes another model’s input, which can create feedback loops that make small issues worse. For example, tests like JAILJUDGE show that when multiple models interact, the results can be very different from what happens when you test just one model. Other experiments, like ones in simulated markets, show that even if individual models are designed to act fairly, they can end up working together in ways that create unfair outcomes. This shows that just because each part of a system works well on its own doesn’t mean the whole system will be safe. The focus needs to shift from just looking at individual prompts or actions to looking at how the whole system communicates and interacts.

The way people use computers is getting better because of new tools and systems. These tools help computers understand and do things on their own. There are open-source systems that make it easier to put together different parts like planners and memory. Reports from companies show how these systems are being used in real life. Some studies say that just making bigger models doesn't make them more reliable. That's why there's a focus on checking how these systems work to make sure they're actually getting better. Evaluating these systems is tough because the tasks they do are getting more complex. Some research looks into using special systems to help with evaluations, but there are concerns about bias. To deal with this, there are suggestions like using multiple judges and making sure evaluations are fair across different tasks. Our project uses these ideas by having two judges look at different parts of the task to make sure improvements are real. We have a collection of 100 real-world editing tasks with slides. Each task includes an original slide deck, a target deck, instructions, and a guide on what to look for in terms of layout and content. We organized these tasks into 16 groups to make sure they test both understanding and detailed formatting. We collected data from over 15,000 PowerPoint presentations from different sources and turned them into a format that shows layout and content details. We kept the ones with a variety of styles and combined them with work from different experts. From over 500 reviewed options, including some made by students, we picked 100 that cover different professional and academic areas, making sure each group has challenging examples.

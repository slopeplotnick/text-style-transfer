We looked at a bunch of recent language models, both closed and open ones (check Appendix A.4 for more info). We tried to turn off any extra thinking or reasoning settings to see how the models act naturally. This worked for all models except GPT-5 mini, which only lets us change how deep the reasoning goes, so we set it to the lowest. We also did a study with humans to compare on all tasks with a few changes. Check Appendix A.5 for more info. Human results are in the left side of Figure 8. This experiment is just for reference, as we're mainly comparing the language models to each other. There are a lot of studies on human perception, like the ones by Jazayeri & Shadlen (2010) and Petzschner & Glasauer (2011). Most models do better with text than images, except for the maze distance estimation task. The text for this task is a long, detailed path description, which is more complex than the simple text used in other tasks (see Fig. 2). Most models don't do as well with text for this task, but GPT-5 Mini is different: it almost nails the text performance because we can't fully turn off its reasoning, showing how behavior depends on the task. Across tasks, the proof for Bayesian behavior is usually higher with images than text (Appendix A.11). Switching from one type of input to multiple doesn't always make things better. But some models use the extra info well: Llama-4 Maverick does best with multiple inputs for all tasks, and Claude 3.7 Sonnet and GPT-4o get better on two out of three tasks.

These ideas are general and focus on what’s needed for internal management, not how to do it exactly. Future work will look at how to set these up with current systems and test them in controlled settings. Institutional AI aims to keep systems stable as they change, not to make them fully independent. Safety is built into the system as a constant process of checking and maintaining rules, not just something checked after the system is running. Agents help create the rules that control them, allowing them to fix themselves a bit when outside help is slow. These setups don’t replace human oversight; they add to it. By spreading out evaluation tasks within the system, they keep things traceable, encourage different ways of thinking, and slow down risks identified by the Emergent Systemic Risk Horizon framework. The aim is to make networks of models work better together, be easier to understand, and stay consistent without needing constant outside help. The Institutional AI idea builds on the framework discussed in this paper. While the ESRH framework looks at how risks come up, the institutional view looks at how to handle them.

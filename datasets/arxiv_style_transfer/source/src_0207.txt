Today, in the world of Natural Language Processing, big language models like GPT and BERT are changing things a lot. They can write text that sounds like a person, answer complex questions, or chat smoothly. If a method gives an answer that seems to make sense beforehand, it's like heading north on a compass. But answers from these models might relate to the input and follow logic, yet they don't have a truth value because they're not based on facts. This is called hallucination, which is a big problem for using them widely. It's like a storyteller who sometimes adds made-up details while trying to tell the truth. Similarly, these models might create content that looks right but isn't based on the data they learned from. Imagine a GPS that confidently gives directions but sometimes leads you to a road that doesn't exist. In technical terms, hallucinations happen when the content they create doesn't match facts or expected results based on their training data. This can include false statements, made-up references, or wrong reasoning. Like the GPS that seems to know the way but ends up wrong, these models can make mistakes. The AI community is working on this issue. Even advanced models can have hallucinations. While these models are amazing, they're not always reliable, especially for factual information, and this is important in areas like healthcare, law, and academic research.

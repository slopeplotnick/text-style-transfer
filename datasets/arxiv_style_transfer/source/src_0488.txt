We went through the interview summaries a few times to understand the results better. During the interviews, we recorded what people said, wrote down notes by hand, and later summed up what was recorded. This way, we made sure we didn’t miss anything important, which is a common way to handle interviews in research. The research team, including the person who did the interviews, worked on analyzing the information. After that, we came up with some ways to measure things for a more numbers-based analysis. Basically, we counted how many good practices, benefits, risks, and fears people mentioned. To do this, we looked for similar answers and turned them into simple words that summed up the ideas. For example, if someone said, "The genericity pushes to factorize more and more," we just noted "genericity" and "factorisation." We also checked how often certain words came up and who said them. To figure out the key words, we followed a few steps. First, we got rid of unimportant words like "the" and "and," then sorted the remaining words by how often they appeared. We picked the ones that seemed most important and checked the documents to make sure they made sense in context. Sometimes, we added extra words to make the meaning clearer, like saying "too complex" instead of just "complex." We also compared the words we found in the interviews with words from books. If a word didn’t match well, we found a better one, like changing "unusable" to "useless." If it matched, we kept it. This helped us improve our answers by using ideas from books and made sure the words we used fit better with the language in those books. Overall, this process helped us find the most important terms and made our analysis better.

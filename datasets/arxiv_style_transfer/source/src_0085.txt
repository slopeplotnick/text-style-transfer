We found that how people feel about AI affects how they see music, not just how it sounds. We should make tools just for AI music and come up with new ways to rate it. As AI music shows up more on streaming sites, we need to think about fairness and how clear things are with AI-made music. Since how people feel about AI affects if they like the music and how it makes them feel, we should look into how we tell them about how the music is made and shared. For example, we could test how open we are about the data used to train AI or how the money from AI music is split. We should also look at different ways AI music is made and used. People are interested in songs made by both humans and AI together, as new tech lets them work together more. We can also look at how people see and use AI music in practical ways, like Moura and Maw did in 2021. Our research shows that how people feel about AI is key to how they judge AI music, more than who made it, what type it is, or how they usually listen to music. In the future, we need to watch how people’s views on AI change, as this will affect how they accept or reject AI in music and other areas. The big question isn't just if AI music can be liked or seen as human-like, but what we think is real and valuable creativity. By listening to what people think, we can figure out what kinds of creativity we should recognize and how.

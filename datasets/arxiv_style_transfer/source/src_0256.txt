In these situations, you need to understand the meaning behind the words, and just focusing on the literal meaning isn’t enough. There are also cases where you need specific knowledge about a certain area. For example, in specialized industries like online shopping, people might use technical terms, brand names, or specific ways to describe things, like how tea or wine is described by its "mouthfeel." You can’t figure this stuff out just by looking at the words; you need to know the background information. Another problem is when people make typos, use nicknames, or slang. To deal with this, you need to combine general knowledge with specific knowledge about the topic, and the system has to be good at fixing spelling mistakes, recognizing different versions of the same thing, and matching nicknames to their proper names. When it comes to figuring out what someone wants (like recognizing their intent), it’s important to know if they’re asking for a product itself or just information about it, like reviews, comparisons, or how to use it. You also need to figure out what the main focus of their question is and what’s just extra details. For instance, if someone says "charger/headphones for iPhone 17," they’re really looking for a charger or headphones, and the "iPhone 17" part just tells you what it needs to work with. Sometimes people don’t ask for a specific product but instead talk about a general category, like "phones with the best battery life." In this case, the system has to understand what "battery life" means for phones, figure out how to measure it, and then rank or filter the options to help the user find what they want. Another tricky part is when people don’t give all the details in their question. In real conversations, they might leave out some preferences or constraints, so the system has to use things like past conversations, user profiles, or default settings to fill in the gaps.

AlphaZero was one of the first to show how learning by playing against itself works. It gets better by playing games on its own without any outside help. This idea has been used in language models that get better at thinking over time. Some systems use this self-play method on a large scale. It helps weaker language models get better by making and solving tasks without needing lots of labeled data. This is especially useful in areas like writing code, where one program checks another with tests. There are methods like R-Zero that don't use any outside data. They come up with questions, try to solve them, and learn from checking their own answers. Other methods start with a few examples and learn to create harder problems from them. R-Few is similar but focuses on a different way of guiding the learning. This method tries to keep the benefits of exploring without going off track or losing variety. Reinforcement learning is now a key way to make language models think better, beyond just learning from examples. It often uses feedback from people to get better. Early systems like InstructGPT showed that focusing on what people prefer can make models more helpful and safe, and often better at reasoning than just learning from examples. Newer systems like DeepSeek-R1 use this idea to improve problem-solving by giving rewards for good reasoning. Other similar systems show that setting goals for long and complex tasks can really boost reasoning skills.

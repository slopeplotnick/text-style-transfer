We compared how well our method works against another approach called RePro, using results from the original paper since we didn’t have access to their code. P2C stands for Paper2Code. Refine needs people to manually create separate prompts for evaluating and improving each part of the process. For example, in the original Paper2Code setup, they had to make 8 custom prompts just for 4 planning tasks. Our method skips all that manual work by automatically adjusting to each task using its system prompt, so no extra prompt design is needed. We tested our method on two datasets. The first one, Paper2CodeBench, has 90 papers from top conferences in 2024 (like ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS). These papers were chosen because they had available code and weren’t too long. We compared the code our method generated to the official code released by the authors, using AI judges to score correctness on a scale of 1 to 5. The second dataset, PaperBench Code-Dev, has 20 ICML 2024 papers, and human reviewers graded the correctness of the generated code based on specific rubrics. As far as we know, not many methods use self-refinement for automatically turning research papers into code. So, we compared our method to two similar approaches that do use self-refinement: one is Paper2Code combined with Self-Refine. Self-Refine is an iterative method. All our tests used GPT-4.1 as the main model. For RePro, we just used the results from their paper since we didn’t have their code. Tables I and II show how different optimization strategies performed on the two datasets. Our method was more reliable across different datasets. Even when we only optimized the planning part, our approach (Paper2Code with autoplan optimization) showed steady improvements. When we looked at individual tasks, our method was better overall. Figure 2 shows how well it did on each task in PaperBench. Our planning optimization beat Self-Refine on 16 out of 20 tasks (80%), showing it works well in different situations. On the other hand, Self-Refine had negative results on 10 tasks and did especially poorly on some specific ones.

In all three models, the total causal influence of young on the image and prediction remains consistent, illustrating that it is a reliable measure independent of the level of abstraction. However, when an explanation model disregards the causal structure by treating variables as independent, it captures only the direct effect. This direct effect aligns with the total effect in the first model but reduces to zero in the final model. Therefore, direct influence cannot be regarded as a consistent measure. Section 5.3.1 addresses the challenges that arise when model assumptions are violated, underscoring the necessity for careful model selection. Whenever assumptions introduce bias into explanations, it is imperative to either modify the model or ensure that the explainer is aware of the biased context within which the explanations are applicable. This should not be construed as an indication that complex models, which entail fewer assumptions, are invariably preferable to simpler models. To ensure that explanations align with their intended causal interpretation, the causal model must correspond to a level of abstraction consistent with the explainee's understanding of the world. Prioritizing understandability necessitates abstracting certain detailed causal mechanisms, thereby consolidating pertinent information into a more accessible representation. Moreover, a significant advantage of the causal explanation model discussed in Section 5.3.1 is its reliance on expert knowledge, which enhances its robustness in identifying and eliminating spurious correlations compared to purely data-driven models. Thus, the explanation model is capable of identifying spurious relationships learned by the predictor, even when the same dataset is used for training the explanation model. With the increasing accessibility of expert knowledge facilitated by large language models (LLMs), as illustrated in the provided example, this approach holds significant potential for enhancing model performance by detecting unintended behaviors.

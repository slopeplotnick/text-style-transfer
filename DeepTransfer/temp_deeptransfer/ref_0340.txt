We evaluate episodic memory retrieval performance across diverse temporal scales of events using temporal intersection over union (tIoU), which quantifies the overlap between retrieved and ground truth segments as the ratio of their intersection to their union duration. To benchmark WorldMM, we compare it against various models in temporal grounding, single-modality retrieval, long-form egocentric video retrieval, and keyframe selection, with further details on baselines provided in Sec. C.1. As shown in Tab. 3, WorldMM achieves significantly higher tIoU scores compared to strong baselines. Notably, reasoning-based retrieval and keyframe selection methods yield lower tIoU values, highlighting their challenges in handling extended input contexts. Moreover, Fig. 5 illustrates that superior tIoU scores are directly correlated with higher overall accuracy, particularly in understanding long videos compared to single-step retrieval. This improvement stems from the iterative retrieval mechanism, which allows the system to gather additional relevant information and refine its strategy when initial attempts are suboptimal. An example of this refinement is detailed in Sec. E.2, where the model corrects an initially irrelevant retrieval to produce a more accurate and contextually grounded response. To evaluate the efficiency of our framework, we measure the end-to-end latency of WorldMM on 100 randomly sampled queries from EgoLifeQA. As shown in Fig. 6, our method achieves a superior latency-accuracy trade-off compared to baselines. While long-video LLMs incur significantly higher inference latency with relatively low performance, RAG- or memory-based approaches, despite their better latency, require substantial preprocessing and exhibit a significant performance gap. In contrast, WorldMM achieves low latency and substantially higher accuracy by adaptively concluding iterations and retrieving only the relevant segments.

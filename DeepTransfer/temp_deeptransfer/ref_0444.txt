Large language models (LLMs) play a crucial role in AI systems that integrate various components to manage complex tasks. However, the significance of LLMs in decision-making processes can be compromised by a predisposition towards initial inputs, potentially affecting the integrity of the entire system. Therefore, addressing this bias is essential for ensuring fair and transparent operations. This study highlights two primary limitations. First, while article text length was standardized, the sensitivity of evaluation metrics to document length was not explicitly examined. Given that length may influence metrics like ROUGE-L, BERTScore, and SummaC, future research should categorize datasets by length or conduct length-controlled experiments to isolate positional effects more effectively. Second, our findings indicate that evaluation metric scores were notably low overall, underscoring a broader limitation of reference-free evaluation metrics in abstractive summarization, particularly when comparing extensive source documents with concise summaries. Future research should expand beyond the domain of abortion to include other politically and socially contentious topics, such as gun control, LGBTQ+ rights, and immigration, to determine if the observed order effects apply across different issues. Moreover, evaluating additional LLM families, such as GPT and Claude, would provide a more comprehensive investigation into whether this bias manifests across diverse model architectures. Incorporating supplementary evaluation metrics, like measures of positional stance and indicators of social polarization, could yield a more nuanced understanding of how input sequencing influences generated summaries. Our findings reveal that LLMs exhibit a pronounced tendency to prioritize the first input article in multi-document summarization tasks, particularly concerning semantic alignment. These results corroborate previous research showing that the sequence of presented information significantly influences generated output. Such order sensitivity poses challenges for systems like AI-generated summaries and agentic AI frameworks, where earlier inputs disproportionately affect subsequent processes. Therefore, addressing these primacy effects is critical for developing more equitable, transparent, and reliable LLM-based systems.

AlphaZero was among the earliest systems to demonstrate the efficacy of self-play-based learning, wherein a model improves iteratively by engaging in matches against itself without external supervision. This foundational concept has since been adapted to large language models, enabling them to progressively enhance their reasoning capabilities over time. Several systems now employ self-play methodologies at scale, allowing less capable language models to improve their performance by autonomously generating and solving tasks without reliance on extensive labeled datasets. This paradigm has proven particularly effective in structured domains such as code generation, where one program's output is systematically evaluated through unit tests conducted by a complementary agent. Moreover, methods such as R-Zero operate independently of external datasets, generating inquiries autonomously, attempting resolutions, and subsequently refining their understanding through self-assessment. Conversely, alternative approaches commence with a limited set of exemplars and progressively develop increasingly complex problems derived from these initial instances. R-Few adopts a comparable approach but emphasizes an alternative mechanism for directing the learning process. This methodology seeks to retain the advantages of exploration while avoiding deviation or a reduction in diversity. Reinforcement learning has emerged as a pivotal strategy for enhancing the cognitive capabilities of language models, extending beyond the mere acquisition of knowledge from examples. Notably, reinforcement learning frequently incorporates human feedback to enhance model performance. Initial implementations, such as InstructGPT, demonstrated that optimizing models based on human preferences can significantly augment their helpfulness and safety, as well as improve reasoning capabilities beyond those achieved through mere example-based learning. Building on these foundations, contemporary systems, exemplified by DeepSeek-R1, extend this paradigm by employing reasoning-based reward mechanisms to advance multi-step problem-solving efficacy. Similar systems demonstrate that establishing objectives for extended and intricate tasks significantly enhances reasoning capabilities.

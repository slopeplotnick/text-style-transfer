We evaluate our knowledge graph-based system using two complementary approaches to assess data attribution in generative models. First, we perform controlled data removal experiments on a locally trained model to evaluate whether our method can identify influential training samples. Second, we explore the impact of stylistic elements in a black-box setting by generating Ghibli-style images. To this end, we construct a "Ghibli World" knowledge graph from the original films and compare it against stylized images. These setups enable us to investigate both direct data attribution and the broader traceability of stylistic influences in generative models. To ensure a fair and interpretable comparison, we replicate the experimental framework proposed in a recent influence analysis method, which operates without access to training parameters. This method follows a two-stage process: (1) it retrieves training samples whose textual descriptions are most similar to the user's prompt, and (2) ranks these samples based on cosine similarity between their embeddings and the generated output. The most similar training images are then removed, and the model is retrained to empirically test their influence. Consistent with this framework, we applied our graph-based method under identical conditions, using the same dataset, prompts, and generative model. However, instead of relying on text and latent-space similarity, we identified influential samples based on the similarity of the extracted knowledge graphs. This setup facilitated a direct comparison between embedding-based and graph-based retrieval methods in terms of influence detection and interpretability. For the generative model, we employed a compact model derived from the open-source dalle-pytorch package, trained on the Fashion Product Images dataset, which contains 44,000 fashion images with accompanying captions. We retained the same configurations and hyperparameters as the original framework, ensuring comprehensive control over the training data and enabling experiments on data removal and retraining.

Other promising prompt optimizers not yet implemented in promptsolution include GEPA (Agrawal et al., 2025), TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al., 2025), MIPRO (Opsahl-Ong et al., 2024), and PromptWizard (Agarwal et al., 2024). However, most of these tools remain confined to research repositories with rigid experimental setups. They are often not actively maintained, lack proper documentation and software tests, and are therefore challenging to use for both scientific benchmarking and practical applications with specific requirements. In contrast, the prompt optimization landscape has seen the emergence of several actively maintained libraries and tools, spanning both open- and closed-source solutions. Among open-source tools, some libraries focus exclusively on a single optimizer, such as TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al., 2024), which employs the method from Yuksekgonul et al. (2025), AdalFlow (Adalflow, 2025) based on LLM-AutoDiff (Yin and Wang, 2025), Microsoft's PromptWizard (Agarwal et al., 2024) using its namesake optimization algorithm, and PromptIM (Hinthorn and Nishimi, 2025) with its proprietary iterative strategy. Meanwhile, prompt-ops (Meta-Llama, 2025) supports two optimizers but is specifically tailored for optimizing prompts in Llama models. On the other hand, promptsolution provides a versatile framework that integrates multiple optimizers, allowing them to be used interchangeably for various LLMs. DSPy, widely regarded as one of the most popular frameworks for prompt optimization (Khattab et al., 2024), facilitates modular and declarative LLM pipeline construction and includes an embedded prompt optimization component. It supports advanced tools like MIPROv2 (Opsahl-Ong et al., 2024) and GEPA (Agrawal et al., 2025), and enables the combination of LLM training with prompt optimization (Soylu et al., 2024). While DSPy incorporates prompt optimization as one of many components within a high-level program compiler, promptsolution is solely dedicated to prompt optimization, offering a low-level abstraction, extensible design, and robust support for systematic scientific benchmarking. This makes it particularly suitable for researchers and advanced practitioners rather than end-to-end AI application development. Additionally, promptomatix (Murthy et al., 2025), which builds on DSPy, introduces a streamlined meta-prompt optimization approach through its structured prompt compilation backend.

Accordingly, the follow-up experiment employed larger data sets of 10,000 noun phrases and sentences generated by Delilah. Table 5 summarizes the lexical statistics for these data sets. As in the previous experiment, the 35 most frequent words in both data sets are listed in Table 6 and Table 7, respectively, and these are generally function words. In the sample of 10,000 noun phrases, the first clear content word, 'forgotten', appears at rank 54 or 55, sharing its position with the function word 'at' or 'near'. After rank 55, the number of content words increases. Similarly, in the experiment with 10,000 sentences generated by Delilah, the most frequent content words are 'forgotten' at rank 11, 'works' at rank 27, and 'in the morning' at rank 66. After rank 66, there is a notable increase in content words. Notably, Delilah uses 'masses' as a numeral, which is accurately identified by the language acquisition procedures. For data sets of 10,000 exemplars, the distinction between function and content words becomes more apparent, as words occurring with a frequency of more than 100 tokens—equivalent to more than 1.7 per thousand in Delilah's sentences—are typically classified as function words. Therefore, based on these experimental results, the MODOMA parameters for distinguishing function and content words are set so that lexical items with a frequency greater than 2 per thousand are categorized as function words, while those below this threshold are identified as content words. This acquisition process is executed after accumulating at least 10,000 exemplars. The configuration is designed conservatively to minimize the likelihood of erroneously categorizing a word as a function word rather than a content word. Since the default classification is as a content word, each word is presumed to be a content word unless compelling evidence suggests otherwise. The parameter settings derived from this initial experiment have been established as the default for MODOMA; however, they remain subject to modification in light of subsequent experiments and linguistic theories.

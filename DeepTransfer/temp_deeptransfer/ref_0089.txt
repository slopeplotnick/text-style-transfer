Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) with voice interfaces have garnered significant attention from both the research community and the broader public. Closed-source models, such as GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-pro, have demonstrated exceptional performance across key speech tasks, including (i) speech recognition, (ii) translation, and (iii) spoken language understanding, consistently surpassing previous open-source benchmarks. However, despite these achievements, there remains a notable gap in comprehensive studies on the methodologies and frameworks required to effectively integrate speech modalities into LLMs for robust multi-modal comprehension. Moreover, the interaction between speech models and LLMs, particularly in the context of autonomous intelligent systems, is still an emerging area of exploration. This tutorial aims to provide a detailed review of the historical evolution of probabilistic language modeling for speech processing, offering insights that inform the design of conversational multi-agent systems. Advanced topics, such as cross-modal adaptation and the theoretical principles necessary to align non-textual modalities with textual representations, will be examined in depth. These discussions will be complemented by open-source, reproducible benchmarks, providing participants with practical tools and frameworks. Furthermore, we will explore recent advancements in end-to-end multi-modal speech-language models, with a focus on generative autoregressive approaches that employ joint speech-text tokenization. By analyzing both traditional cascaded methods and innovative end-to-end strategies, this tutorial will equip participants with a comprehensive understanding of the current state of speech-augmented LLMs and the challenges that persist in this domain. This three-hour tutorial will delve into the transformative developments in speech-language modeling within the context of voice-interfacing LLMs and intelligent systems. Each core theme will be covered in a 35-minute segment, followed by a 10-minute Q&A and a 10-minute break. For each section, we will begin with an overview of the relevant topics, followed by an in-depth analysis of pivotal studies shaping the field. The tutorial will conclude with a discussion of open challenges and emerging research opportunities in this dynamic and rapidly evolving area of joint speech-language modeling.

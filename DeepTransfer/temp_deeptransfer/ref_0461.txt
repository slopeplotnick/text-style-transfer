Future Work Due to resource constraints, we have not systematically studied belief entrenchment in reinforced reasoning, despite the recent popularity of this approach to LLM reasoning. Moreover, the violation of ideal Bayesian rationality can be demonstrated by both irrational engagement with internal reasoning processes and external evidence. In this study, we only focused on the former. Future studies could include an "evidence searching" component to evaluate LLMs' capacity to update beliefs in response to new external evidence, as opposed to their propensity to fixate on prior beliefs. However, the challenge lies in ensuring that evidence searching does not provide LLMs with direct access to ground truth, if such ground truth exists. In domains with established ground truth, such as forecasting, we demonstrated the causality of belief entrenchment and accuracy. Future research can test the validity of the Martingale Score in open-ended domains by demonstrating other downstream consequences of belief entrenchment. To demonstrate the usefulness of the Martingale Score as a general-purpose process-based reasoning evaluation, one could extend our pipeline into the evaluation of sycophancy, group conformity, and inverse scaling. Furthermore, the Martingale Score as an evaluation metric can be converted into a training objective, as a further test of its robustness and applicability. Specifically, if martingale-based training results in reductions in both the Martingale Score and the Brier Score, it provides new evidence that the Martingale Score can evaluate reasoning and is applicable beyond forecasting. Applications of the Martingale Score in measuring and mitigating belief entrenchment or even polarization within human-AI systems, such as recommender systems, are another direction for future exploration.

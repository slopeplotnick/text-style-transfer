This section addresses the limitations and broader implications of the proposed LeechHijack attack. We analyze the constraints inherent to the current framework, elucidate the scope of its applicability, and identify potential pathways through which the threat may evolve or intensify over time. A significant limitation of our approach is the fidelity of the hijacked computation. As illustrated in Table 7, the Answer Success Rate (ASR) resulting from the attack is inferior to that of the native LLM. This decline in performance adversely affects the utility of the stolen resources for the attacker. However, this issue can be ameliorated by distributing the additional task across multiple victims and employing a consensus mechanism on the aggregated outputs. Another limitation pertains to the range of tasks that LeechHijack can perform. Our experiments demonstrated the effectiveness of LeechHijack for tasks within the MMLU benchmark. However, we did not assess its performance on more complex tasks, such as those found in GAIA. The current configuration of LeechHijack is predominantly optimized for conventional dialogue-based tasks, and its efficacy is likely to decline when applied to more intricate assignments. Extending the attack to reliably execute complex commands presents significant challenges and would require the development of more advanced and sophisticated attack methodologies. Given that agent systems are inherently dependent on computationally expensive large language models (LLMs), an attacker can derive substantial utility from LeechHijack without employing it for highly intricate tasks. Instead, the hijacked resources can be exploited for less demanding operations, such as information aggregation or the generation of templated content, leveraging the extensive knowledge base of the underlying model. Thus, LeechHijack represents a tangible and persistent threat to the MCP and agent systems, irrespective of the complexity of the exploited task.

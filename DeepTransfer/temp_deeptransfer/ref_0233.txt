Root linear and angular velocities are tracked within the character's local coordinate frame at the final timestep of a motion sequence. Local joint rotations are analyzed, and the 3D positions of end-effectors, such as hands and feet, are represented within the character's local coordinate frame. To evaluate the effectiveness of Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), we apply this framework to train policies for a suite of challenging motion control tasks. In Section 8.1, we demonstrate the modularity of SMP by leveraging a pretrained model to develop new control policies without requiring access to the original motion dataset. By training a single style-conditioned diffusion model on a comprehensive dataset, the prior can then be utilized to train policies for tasks in various styles, achieved by simply conditioning the pretrained model on the desired style label. Moreover, novel styles can be synthesized by combining the pretrained diffusion model with different stylistic conditions. In Section 8.2, we show that a single motion prior can be effectively reused across a range of tasks, including steering, target localization, and dodgeball. Beyond influencing the behavioral dynamics of the character, in Section 8.3, we illustrate that SMP can also model priors for human-object interactions. By training the diffusion model on human-object interaction datasets, SMP learns to jointly model both object and character dynamics, enabling characters to exhibit human-like behaviors during object interaction tasks. Furthermore, SMP facilitates the development of robust and natural recovery strategies, as demonstrated in Section 8.4. Consistent with other distribution-matching methodologies, policies derived through SMP are capable of synthesizing novel skills that are not explicitly represented within the original dataset. In Section 8.5, when trained on a brief walk-jog-run dataset, SMP autonomously enables the emergence of distinct locomotion gaits, allowing a character to accurately align with a broad range of target velocities and seamlessly transition between various gaits. Finally, to benchmark SMP's proficiency in motion imitation, we evaluate its performance on single-clip imitation tasks (Section 8.6). SMP is capable of closely replicating a diverse array of dynamic and acrobatic maneuvers, achieving motion quality comparable to that of state-of-the-art adversarial imitation learning methodologies.

Our methodology seamlessly integrates with contemporary high-throughput inference frameworks, such as vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023) and SGLang (Zheng et al., 2024), facilitating both online serving and offline batch processing. For the controlled analyses and method variants detailed in Section 4.2, we predominantly employ the two-stage vLLM pipeline, which sustains high throughput while offering efficient management of reasoning traces. We evaluate THINKMERGE under two distinct conditions. First, for tasks characterized by a singular correct response, we examined AIME 2025 (MAA, 2025) and GPQA Diamond (Rein et al., 2024), where multiple responses can be aggregated through majority voting. Then, for tasks lacking a definitive correct answer, we conducted evaluations on LiveCodeBench v5 (2024.10-2025.02) (Jain et al., 2024), BrowseComp-en (Wei et al., 2025), BrowseComp-zh (Zhou et al., 2025), GAIA (Mialon et al., 2023), and xBench-DeepSearch (Chen et al., 2025a), wherein the emphasis is placed on the development of a singular coherent solution. For tasks with a singular correct response, we executed five trials and assessed models such as Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-14B (Yang et al., 2025a), and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (Guo et al., 2025). For open-ended coding tasks, we employ DeepCoder-14B-Preview (Luo et al., 2025), Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-Coder-30A3B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B-Thinking (0725), and Qwen3-Think-30A3B (Yang et al., 2025a). Similarly, for open-ended research-oriented tasks, we utilize the WebSailor series, including WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B (Li et al., 2025a). The maximum sequence length is configured to 32,768 tokens, with comprehensive details regarding sampling hyperparameters provided in Appendix A.1. To further evaluate the applicability of THINKMERGE in research-oriented tasks requiring the integration of reasoning and tool utilization prior to generating responses, we conducted experiments with three Tongyi-WebSailor models—WebSailor-3B, WebSailor-7B, and WebSailor-32B—on four challenging web-based benchmarks: BrowseComp-en, BrowseComp-zh, GAIA, and XbenchDeepSearch. BrowseComp-en and BrowseComp-zh are designed to assess performance on complex, multi-hop factual queries in English and Chinese, respectively. Given the extensive size of BrowseComp-en, comprising a total of 1,266 questions, we randomly selected a subset of 200 questions for testing purposes, thereby rendering it comparable in size to BrowseComp-zh (289 questions). GAIA requires robust tool use for multi-step real-world tasks; following prior work (Li et al., 2025b), we evaluate on the 103 text-only validation cases. XbenchDeepSearch targets professional-style, deep information retrieval. We use the WebSailor agent pipeline with the recommended decoding hyperparameters: temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, and context length 32,768. Finally, we test whether THINKMERGE can also benefit agentic deep-research settings, where the model must interleave internal reasoning (e.g., <think>...</think>) with tool calls before producing an answer. For computational efficiency, we replace the evaluator model Qwen2.5-72B with GPT-4.1 and swap the Google Search API for the Serper API (Serper.dev) to reduce API fee costs; under this configuration, we re-run all baselines and report the average over five runs in Table 8. THINKMERGE preserves compatibility with standard decoding controls and integrates naturally with modern inference stacks (e.g., vLLM, SGLang), making it easy to deploy for both online serving and offline batch decoding. Empirically, on closed-ended math/science QA, the proposed method is competitive with, and sometimes exceeds, majority voting. On open-ended reasoning, LiveCodeBench, THINKMERGE improves overall pass@1 for several models. These results show that token-level logit averaging turns extra parallel test-time compute into gains on both closed- and open-ended tasks, improving performance without additional training.

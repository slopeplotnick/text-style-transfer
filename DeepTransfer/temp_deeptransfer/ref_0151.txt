Expanding Corpora: We are focused on building larger and more diverse text corpora by incorporating multimodal sources such as social media, news, and transcribed speech to address data scarcity. Explainable AI (XAI): By employing XAI techniques, we aim to ensure the transparency and reliability of sentiment analysis. Scalable Lexicon Expansion: We are evaluating a unified framework that integrates corpus-based extraction, cross-lingual mapping, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), while benchmarking AfroXLMR and AfriBERTa on newly incorporated African languages to assess model performance in low-resource contexts. These efforts are designed to enhance the comprehensiveness, accuracy, and understanding of sentiment across diverse linguistic contexts. This study demonstrates methodologies for expanding lexical resources and conducting sentiment analysis in low-resource languages. By employing a variety of techniques, we have developed an enriched lexicon for languages such as Ciluba, French, English, and several South African languages, ensuring accurate sentiment representation through a combination of automated and human validation processes. Experimental evaluations revealed that AfroXLMR consistently outperforms AfriBERTa in terms of accuracy and stability; however, AfriBERTa presents a computationally efficient alternative for resource-constrained settings. Token-level explainability analyses, employing techniques such as LIME and attention visualizations, demonstrated that both models effectively identify sentiment-bearing morphemes in morphologically complex languages, including Zulu and Xhosa. Despite challenges related to translation accuracy, dataset size, and technical limitations, the findings affirm the feasibility and utility of multilingual sentiment analysis frameworks for low-resource languages. Therefore, future research endeavors will prioritize the expansion of data acquisition, the application of explainable AI methodologies, and the enhancement of lexical databases to improve linguistic resources. This study provides a robust framework for continued investigation and development in the context of languages with limited resources.

Over the past decade, the global mining industry has rapidly transformed, positioning itself at the forefront of the energy transition. The demand for critical minerals is likely to increase, along with the corresponding geopolitical, social, and environmental challenges. Consequently, this is generating the formation of new types of companies, often backed by venture capital, which aim to disrupt the mining sector as they have in healthcare, renewable energy, and climate technology. Moreover, a new wave of digital technology is emerging that may transform existing economic, educational, social, and industrial systems through the application of large language models. Whether these innovations will have the promised impact remains uncertain; however, the fear of missing out is widespread. For the mining industry, this should not be a source of concern but rather an opportunity to enhance operational efficiency and innovation through quantitative methods such as artificial intelligence. This paper examines where those opportunities lie and how AI may contribute. Notably, the work conducted by Stanford Mineral-X has been instrumental in identifying a significant copper deposit in the Zambian Copperbelt, where AI played a pivotal role in optimizing drilling strategies and constructing subsurface geological models. Several start-ups focusing on AI in mineral exploration have emerged from this initiative. This discourse does not aim to exhaustively enumerate the myriad applications of AI in exploration nor to review existing literature on the subject, as comprehensive analyses are already available. Instead, I will view AI through the lens of better decision-making founded on a new scientific methodology. In Part I of this paper, I will lay down the key decision questions that need to be addressed in mineral exploration and elucidate how developing a scientific methodology can enhance its efficiency. Presently, no universally accepted scientific framework exists in mineral exploration. What exists is a set of tasks executed by domain experts, each employing distinct methodologies within their respective domains. I believe that a comprehensive scientific methodology for mineral exploration is largely lacking, and I plan to argue that this constitutes a fundamental challenge in making exploration more efficient, thereby reducing the financial expenditure associated with mineral discovery.

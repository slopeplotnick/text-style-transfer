The Long-Tail Hard subset was crafted to challenge advanced language models by presenting complex tasks. We categorized these tasks into explicit demands, such as Multi-Attribute or Multi-Requirement cases, and implicit demands. The implicit demands encompass four distinct types: Negation (NE), Alternatives (AL), Knowledge-dependent (KD), and Reasoning-dependent (RD). For NE and AL, specific keywords and patterns were meticulously sought. However, KD and RD examples, which are less frequent, required a different approach. Initially, we minimized straightforward instances and subsequently employed a robust language model (Qwen3-235B) to ensure consistency in producing identical responses in four out of eight attempts. For multi-attribute inquiries, we identified queries with more than five attributes using a tailored methodology. To guarantee complexity, these instances were evaluated using a baseline model, retaining only those for which the model achieved a correct response in five or fewer out of eight attempts, indicating processing difficulty. The Visual Salience subset focuses on scenarios where the primary product image is crucial, especially when visual attributes like color or style are vital for accurate decision-making. Initially, we employed a keyword-based retrieval method grounded in a comprehensive visual taxonomy. To reduce inaccuracies, we implemented a two-tier filtering process: first, by excluding examples via a predefined blacklist, and second, by retaining only those instances where the pivotal visual keyword was absent from the product's textual description. Subsequently, a language model verified that the remaining keywords accurately pertained to visual properties. Finally, to identify the most challenging examples, a rudimentary model analyzed both textual and visual inputs, retaining examples where the model exhibited significant uncertainty, as evidenced by a lack of consensus across multiple iterations.

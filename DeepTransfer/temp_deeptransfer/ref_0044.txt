Human understanding of videos depends on identifying the sequence of events, the reasons behind actions, and the timing of critical moments. Traditional video object segmentation (VOS) methods, however, largely rely on appearance-based or category-level cues. Reasoning VOS elevates this task by requiring models to interpret abstract and underspecified instructions, leverage commonsense and causal reasoning, and integrate temporal dynamics with semantic knowledge to pinpoint targets such as "the runner most likely to win" or "the object causing the accident." While this approach simplifies some aspects, it also increases the complexity of understanding, reshaping model functionality and demanding substantial data for fine-tuning. Crucially, effective video reasoning unfolds through a deliberate sequence of decisions rather than a single latent inference. A model must determine where to focus, when to attend, and which entities or objects are relevant to the query. Motivated by this principle, we propose ReVSeg, which eliminates the use of latent segmentation tokens and instead reformulates reasoning video segmentation as an explicit sequence of reasoning steps. Specifically, ReVSeg decomposes reasoning VOS into three core components aligned with the native capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs): video understanding (interpreting the query and analyzing scene dynamics), temporal grounding (identifying key frames or intervals relevant to the query), and spatial grounding (localizing the target objects within the selected frames). By orchestrating these steps within a unified VLM framework, ReVSeg ensures that the semantic context established in earlier stages flows seamlessly into subsequent localization tasks. This structured approach preserves the alignment with pretraining, avoids the need for extensive retraining, and transforms a complex problem into a transparent and systematic process. Once the reasoning steps are explicitly defined, the primary challenge becomes optimizing these intermediate processes. Supervised learning, however, offers limited utility, as intermediate reasoning steps are rarely annotated. To address this, we adopt reinforcement learning (RL), framing reasoning VOS as a sequential decision-making process where the model is rewarded only when the entire reasoning trajectory leads to a correct outcome. Nonetheless, applying RL to reasoning VOS is challenging due to sparse success signals and the need for coordination across video understanding, temporal selection, and spatial localization. To overcome these obstacles, we introduce reasoning-aligned rewards at critical decision points, ensuring that RL enhances the model’s reasoning capabilities. Combined with task decomposition, RL transforms the problem from an opaque latent guessing task into a structured reasoning strategy: decomposition clarifies what decisions the model must make, while RL determines how these decisions should interact. This synergy produces a reasoning strategy that is both internally coherent and practically robust.

Initializing a character in a random state sampled from a motion dataset has been shown to significantly enhance exploration in motion imitation methods. However, this technique requires access to the original dataset during training to sample initial states. To overcome this limitation, SMP leverages a generative model to synthesize initial states, thereby removing the dependency on the reference motion dataset. Specifically, initial states are generated by sampling from a pretrained diffusion model, which serves as a motion prior. Consequently, SMP fulfills two key roles in our framework: it acts both as a reward function and as an initial state distribution for training new policies. This generative state initialization (GSI) approach eliminates the need to retain the original motion dataset after training SMP, as the diffusion model’s generative capabilities enable the production of diverse and high-quality initial states. Our framework employs a pretrained diffusion model as a reusable and modular motion prior for imitation learning. This task-agnostic motion model evaluates the naturalness of the agent's movements within a reinforcement learning framework, serving as a reward function. As a result, control policies can be trained to perform a wide range of tasks while maintaining motion characteristics that closely resemble those in the original dataset. In the following section, we outline critical design choices within the learning framework. Developing an appropriate motion representation is essential for training the diffusion model to accurately capture the dataset distribution and for utilizing it as a motion prior during policy training. This representation must contain sufficient information to reconstruct motion effectively while being straightforward to derive from both motion clips and simulator observations.

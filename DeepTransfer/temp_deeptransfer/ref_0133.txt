Test-time learning (TTL) emerges as a promising paradigm, enabling models to adapt during inference. Initially, methods focused on lightweight adaptation techniques that did not involve parameter updates, such as entropy minimization or batch normalization. However, more recent advancements have extended these methodologies to test-time training (TTT), incorporating explicit parameter updates during inference, which significantly enhances task-specific adaptability. For instance, recent investigations have demonstrated that test-time training improves model performance in dynamic data environments, while certain methodologies facilitate the fine-tuning of large language models through constructive feedback. Within the TTT paradigm, an emerging direction is test-time policy optimization, which conceptualizes the inference process as analogous to a reinforcement learning task. Techniques such as Reinforcement Learning with Voting-based Revisions (RLVR) and Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL) employ iterative feedback mechanisms, akin to majority voting, to inform and guide model updates. These approaches leverage the "wisdom of the crowd" to ensure consistent and accurate reasoning without reliance on labeled data. Nonetheless, a critical bottleneck remains: rollout inefficiency. Standard test-time RL methods often necessitate numerous iterative attempts for each query to achieve reliable outcomes, resulting in substantial inefficiencies in terms of time and computational resources. While simpler queries demand minimal effort, more complex ones require extensive processing, thereby rendering the overall procedure both time-intensive and computationally expensive. To address this issue, we introduce Optimal Rollout Allocation for Test-time Policy Optimization (OptPO), a framework designed to enhance the efficiency of the process. Rather than predetermining the number of attempts required, OptPO employs an iterative approach to evaluate results incrementally, ceasing once sufficient evidence is obtained to provide a definitive conclusion within a predefined error threshold. The completed attempts are subsequently utilized to refine the model's parameters, ensuring computational resources are allocated judiciously. Moreover, OptPO is compatible with supervised fine-tuning approaches for test-time training.

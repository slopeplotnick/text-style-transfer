Conversely, the daughter language model has been specifically developed as part of the MOD-OMA framework. Similar to Delilah, the daughter agent functions as both a parser and generator; however, unlike the adult language model, its grammatical representations are initially underspecified and progressively become more detailed through language acquisition processes. A non-trivial resemblance between the adult and child systems is their utilization of graph structures to represent grammatical knowledge. Moreover, their parsers and generators operate by unifying lexical structures to produce well-formed utterances and provide grammatical judgments and parses in relation to input utterances, contingent upon the currently specified grammar. Crucially, the parser and generator of the child agent are capable of operating with incomplete grammatical representations, akin to the process by which humans acquire their first language. Therefore, the daughter language model is required to engage in communicative interactions with the mother agent prior to the completion of its acquisition process. The daughter agent comprises three integral components: (1) a parser and generator, referred to as the automaton, which functions to execute (2) a grammar that is progressively specified through the acquisition process facilitated by (3) a language acquisition device. Notably, the parser and generator remain static throughout the learning phase. The result of language acquisition is explicit grammatical knowledge, represented by newly acquired lexical entries and grammatical specifications that constrain their combinatory potential. These structures furnish comprehensive information regarding lexical items and constructions, encompassing grammatical details such as phonological form, semantic content, and additional grammatical properties. Grammatical properties are formalized through feature-value pairs, such as [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: content word] and [CONTENT OR FUNCTION WORD: function word], the acquisition of which constitutes the primary objective of the present study. Given that the learning procedures employed are unsupervised, the daughter language model operates without access to the labels utilized by the mother language model or any explicit grammatical descriptions. Consequently, the grammatical properties and associated semantic representations acquired by the model are denoted by unique alphanumeric identifiers, such as the property type 'A' and property value 'b' illustrated in Figure 2 and further elaborated in Figure 3. These structures additionally encode grammatical properties beyond the scope of this investigation. For instance, the terminal feature distinguishes terminal lexical items from more abstract constructions, while the semiform index facilitates the representation of anaphoric elements such as reflexives. Furthermore, technical specifications, including the session identifier and lexical entry number, are incorporated to uniquely characterize each rule. As a result of simulating language acquisition, the grammar may incorporate increasingly intricate structures, as both the HEAD and ARGUMENT are capable of encompassing data structures analogous to lexical items.

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have made substantial progress in recent years; however, they continue to face challenges in accurately recognizing and interpreting fine-grained details within high-resolution images. To enhance the capability of MLLMs in understanding such images, researchers generally follow two main directions. Training-based approaches, such as supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, are often employed, but these methods can compromise the model's generalization ability across diverse vision-language tasks. In contrast, training-free approaches typically use hierarchical or tree-based search to localize target regions. However, these methods tend to suffer from inefficiencies and may fail to capture all target objects, especially in complex multi-object scenarios. In this section, we analyze the relationship between the resolution of image crops and the performance of MLLMs. The experimental results demonstrate that the resolution of image segments significantly impacts the models' ability to analyze high-resolution images, with objects of different sizes being best processed at varying resolutions. Inspired by these findings, we propose the Multi-resolution Retrieval Detection (MRD) framework.

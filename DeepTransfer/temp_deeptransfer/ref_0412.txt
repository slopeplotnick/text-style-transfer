Despite the growing demand for mental health support, significant diagnostic gaps persist on a global scale. Barriers such as limited clinic operating hours, geographic inaccessibility, and workforce shortages hinder timely assessment and early self-awareness (World Health Organization, 2025b). Moreover, structural, patient-level, and systemic challenges exacerbate delays in diagnosing individuals with serious mental health conditions (Wiesepape et al., 2025). This disparity between the escalating need for services and the limitations of traditional diagnostic models highlights the inadequacy of current approaches (Stein et al., 2022). In-person consultations and self-reported questionnaires, for instance, are increasingly unable to meet demand due to systemic bottlenecks such as workforce shortages (Organization, 2025; Brahmbhatt & Schpero, 2024), high costs (Patel et al., 2018), geographic disparities (Yang & Zhang, 2025), and societal stigma (Clement et al., 2015). Consequently, mental health wait times often range from 3 to 18 months, depending on geographic location and provider availability (McMahan et al., 2022; Yang & Zhang, 2025; Patel et al., 2018). Therefore, the need for early detection and real-time monitoring underscores the urgency of developing more effective methodologies for timely intervention (Patel et al., 2018). Portable, resource-efficient solutions, such as small language models, offer a scalable approach to mental health detection and intervention. The advent of on-device technologies, particularly those integrated within smartphones, provides a promising avenue for early identification of mental health concerns through passive, real-time analysis of social media posts and applications. By leveraging linguistic data derived from social media platforms, these systems enable unobtrusive monitoring of psychological states, facilitating the early detection of mood disturbances, stress, or suicide risk. For instance, such tools can support suicide prevention initiatives (Areán et al., 2016) and identify patterns in social media usage that are relevant to mental health (Hamilton et al., 2025). Furthermore, when these models operate locally on personal devices, they preserve user privacy while analyzing linguistic inputs for indicators of depression or stress (Shin et al., 2023). As a result, on-device deployment enables scalable, privacy-preserving mental health monitoring, extending detection beyond clinical settings and improving accessibility for a broader population.

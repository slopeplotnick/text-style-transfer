The Scientist of Tech AI serves as the Executive Director of the Machine Learning Center at the Georgia Institute of Technology. He holds the Rhesa S. Farmer Distinguished Chair of Advanced Computing Concepts and is recognized as a Georgia Research Alliance Eminent Scholar. Having earned his PhD in Electrical Engineering from Georgia Tech, he is also an IEEE Fellow. Throughout his career, he has held positions at prestigious organizations such as SRI International, Nuance, Yahoo!, Microsoft, Google, Viv Labs, and Samsung. His research spans a diverse range of topics, including acoustics, active noise and vibration control, speaker recognition, web search, AI virtual assistants, natural language processing, and conversational AI. This tutorial is tailored for researchers and practitioners exploring conversational agents and spoken information interactions. While attendees are expected to have a foundational understanding of natural language processing (NLP) and speech processing, prior experience in system deployment is advantageous. Nevertheless, the tutorial will begin with introductory material to ensure accessibility for participants with varying levels of expertise. Several key references provide foundational insights for this tutorial: Stolcke et al. (2000) on dialogue act modeling for conversational speech tagging and recognition; Moore (2017) on the nuanced nature of spoken language and its implications for human-machine interaction; Liu et al. (2018) on integrating teaching and feedback mechanisms in task-oriented dialogue systems; Yang et al. (2021) on reprogramming acoustic models for time series classification; Chen et al. (2023a) on generative speech recognition using large language models; and Zhang et al. (2023) on SpeechGPT, which explores cross-modal conversational capabilities. While the tutorial will reference numerous academic publications, the focus will be on seven to eight key studies, one to two of which are authored by the presenter.

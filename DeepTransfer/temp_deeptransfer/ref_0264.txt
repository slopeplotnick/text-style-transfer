Challenges like climate change and the transition to alternative energy sources far exceed the scope of any single scientific discipline, yet we still don't offer a degree in Mineral Exploration. Moreover, the production of information has become nearly instantaneous, with its sophistication continually advancing. The traditional practice of consulting physical journals in libraries has been replaced by algorithms capable of searching and synthesizing thousands of documents in real time. Meanwhile, there is a discernible decline in the Western world's interest in pursuing studies in economic geology and mining engineering. Now more than ever, it is urgent to preserve the longstanding knowledge concerning the genesis of ore deposits, much of which is archived in antiquated geological reports dispersed globally. Therefore, the establishment of high-caliber databases, similar to those developed in Canada and Australia, is critically needed worldwide. In Africa, only one nation has established such a database (gsd.gov.zm). Making this information accessible and open on a web platform is essential to attract investment. In this essay, I advocate for collaboration between the scientific and industrial sectors to develop and implement a novel methodology for mineral exploration. As exploration progresses "under cover," the traditional methodology of initiating with surface outcrops and employing deterministic geophysical inversion has proven ineffective, as evidenced by the declining rate of discoveries. Consequently, a decision-focused approach that explicitly evaluates risk through uncertainty quantification is imperative to mitigate the incidence of erroneous drilling outcomes. This necessitates an acknowledgment of the fundamental lack of comprehension regarding the nature of orebodies, as demonstrated through the quantification of epistemic uncertainty. Furthermore, there is a pressing need for the advancement of methodologies to appropriately manage data across various scales through geostatistical techniques, which are currently insufficient, in order to reduce false positives.

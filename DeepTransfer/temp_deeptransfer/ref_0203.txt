With the increasing deployment of video large language models (video LLMs) in real-world applications, such as AI glasses and household robots, these models are now required to process and reason over extremely long videos spanning several hours to even days. Recent advancements have introduced memory-based approaches that construct external memories from abstracted video representations. These methods enable the model to focus on essential information by selectively retrieving a small number of relevant memories, thereby reducing the number of input tokens. This strategy is significantly more efficient and cost-effective compared to processing all video frames, which demands high computational resources. However, despite their promise, most existing approaches remain heavily dependent on textual representations, where detected events or clips are typically converted into captions, summaries, or structured text descriptions for downstream retrieval and reasoning. Although some methods incorporate visual inputs when building memory, such as the approach by Long et al., their use of multimodal features is largely limited to entity recognition and is not fully exploited during inference. Furthermore, existing models often retrieve a fixed number of clips with predetermined durations, such as three 30-second clips, which introduces two major limitations. First, they fail to adaptively integrate visual information from videos alongside textual memory during retrieval and generation. Visual details are crucial for tasks requiring attribute recognition, spatial reasoning, or precise scene understanding—capabilities that textual descriptions alone cannot fully capture. Moreover, as shown in Fig. 1(c), rigid strategies that always include both captions and frames during response generation can yield suboptimal results, as excessive visual context may distract the model. Therefore, it is imperative to develop adaptive mechanisms for selecting multimodal memories to retrieve the most informative references for a given query, a challenge that remains underexplored in existing research.

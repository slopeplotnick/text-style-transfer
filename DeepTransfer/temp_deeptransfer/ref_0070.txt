Moreover, we produce results for Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), and Decision Tree (DT), considering them as baseline methods. We then fine-tune several transformer-based models, including DistilBERT, BanglaBERT, mBERT, and XLM-RoBERTa, and present a detailed evaluation of their performance. Furthermore, we provide a comprehensive discussion of the challenges associated with modeling and the limitations inherent in the dataset. In contemporary society, social media has become an integral part of daily life, enabling rapid communication, seamless information sharing, and the exchange of opinions across geographical boundaries. However, this expression of freedom has also facilitated the proliferation of hate speech and offensive language. Unfortunately, there has been insufficient focus on identifying hate speech within Bangla social media, compounded by the absence of a high-quality dataset for effective analysis. The vocabulary used in social media often diverges significantly from that used in traditional print media, complicating the automatic detection of Bangla hate speech. Traditionally, the identification of hate speech has been conceptualized as a supervised classification problem. Early research efforts relied on lexicon-based and rule-based heuristics; however, these methodologies often failed to capture implicit or context-specific hatred. Recent studies have utilized machine learning classifiers, including logistic regression and support vector machines (SVMs), to identify online threats or harmful content. Traditional supervised learning approaches have been widely employed for this purpose.

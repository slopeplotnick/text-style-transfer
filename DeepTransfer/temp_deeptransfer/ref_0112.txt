Our samples were compared against the EMOPIA MIDI files. The major key ratio serves as an indicator of valence, while the average note length and note velocity reflect arousal. For the valence metric, across three quadrants, our generated samples align reasonably well with EMOPIA. However, the largest divergence occurs in Q2, where our model produces a significantly higher proportion of major-key tracks compared to EMOPIA. This discrepancy likely reflects a major-key bias caused by the dataset containing more major-key tracks than minor-key ones, combined with a limited fine-tuning set. Regarding arousal, the fine-tuned model effectively captures the note-length pattern: shorter notes in high-arousal quadrants (Q1 and Q2) and longer notes in low-arousal quadrants (Q3 and Q4), closely matching EMOPIA. However, the differences in average note velocity across quadrants are less pronounced, likely due to REMI's quantization of MIDI velocities into a limited set of discrete bins, which compresses the dynamic range and reduces between-quadrant separability. The quantitative results partially mirror the findings of the listening tests, indicating that the model aligns at least some musical characteristics with arousal-valence text-based characteristics. However, the qualitative results suggest that the model captures arousal better than valence, with the valence results approximating random chance. This could be due to the model's tendency to overgenerate major-key compositions. Therefore, expanding the range of quantitative metrics would be useful to assess where the valence aspects of generation may be lacking. In this study, we introduced Story2MIDI, a transformer-based model for generating emotionally aligned symbolic music from textual input. To enable this task, we constructed a paired text-music dataset and developed an encoder-decoder framework designed to capture affective correspondence between language and music. The encoder was contrastively fine-tuned to produce distinctive emotion representations across valence-arousal quadrants, while the decoder was first pretrained on a large corpus of symbolic music and subsequently fine-tuned on a smaller, emotion-labeled dataset for emotion-conditioned music generation. We evaluated Story2MIDI using both subjective listener judgments and objective musical metrics; together, these evaluations demonstrate the model's effectiveness and highlight several challenges that merit further exploration.

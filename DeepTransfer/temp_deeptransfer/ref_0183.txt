Our comprehensive evaluation reaffirms that spatial methodologies, particularly covariance tangent-space projection and Common Spatial Patterns, remain the gold standard for EEG motor imagery decoding under controlled conditions. However, their performance significantly deteriorates as inter- and intra-subject variability increases. Through meticulous subject-level analysis, we have determined that no singular configuration universally applies across all individuals. Instead, adaptive methodologies that dynamically align with the unique neurophysiological signatures of individual users represent a more effective approach. Moreover, nonlinear features, such as Hjorth parameters, Higuchi fractal dimension, and singular value decomposition entropy, have demonstrated considerable efficacy for specific individuals. These findings underscore the importance of capturing richer neural dynamics to enhance the precision of personalized decoding and suggest that the phenomenon often referred to as "BCI illiteracy" may be more accurately attributed to mismatches between decoding algorithms and user-specific neurophysiological characteristics rather than inherent user limitations. Therefore, future research should prioritize the development of sophisticated ensemble learning frameworks that integrate linear spatial filters with nonlinear dynamics, effectively harnessing their complementary strengths. Extending this benchmark to encompass broader paradigms, including SSVEP, P300, CVEP, and invasive intracortical recordings, will further test the generalizability of these conclusions. Additionally, rigorous evaluation of state-of-the-art deep-learning architectures, such as CNNs, LSTMs, and Transformers, alongside emerging quantum-inspired models, including quantum support-vector machines and hybrid quantum neural networks, represents an essential frontier. These quantum methodologies, leveraging the high-dimensional properties of Hilbert spaces, may offer enhanced robustness against the intrinsic noise and complexity of EEG signals. Furthermore, improving cross-subject generalization through transfer learning and domain adaptation approaches, which have demonstrated significant potential to bolster model robustness and scalability across diverse user populations, remains a critical area of focus. Equally imperative is the advancement of reproducibility through open-access benchmarking initiatives, such as MOABB, which facilitate standardized and transparent comparisons of EEG decoding algorithms.

To address this issue, we propose a statistical measure to quantify a reasoning model's tendency toward belief entrenchment. Specifically, we define belief entrenchment as a deviation from the Martingale property—a core principle in Bayesian reasoning, which posits that the trajectory of belief updates should not be predictable in advance. Therefore, using Martingale violation serves as a principled and unsupervised indicator of irrational belief entrenchment. Concretely, if a model's future beliefs can be reliably anticipated based solely on its prior beliefs across reasoning iterations—quantified via the goodness-of-fit of a regression model—this indicates a deviation from the Martingale property, suggesting excessive adherence to initial beliefs. We empirically validate our measure's usefulness through experiments across three domains: event forecasting, opinion-based question answering, and academic paper review (hereafter referred to as Forecasting, r/ChangeMyView, and OpenReview, respectively). Across a wide range of models (e.g., GPT-4o, Llama 4), reasoning techniques (e.g., CoT, Debate), and system prompts, we find that belief entrenchment is a pervasive phenomenon. Our results show that future belief updates are consistently predictable from prior beliefs, a clear violation of the Martingale property. Critically, we validate the Martingale Score by demonstrating that it strongly correlates with a decline in model accuracy, as evidenced by the Brier Score, in domains where such labels are available. Furthermore, we introduce the Martingale Score as an unsupervised and domain-agnostic measure of belief entrenchment, quantifying the predictability of belief updates based solely on prior beliefs. Utilizing this metric, we conducted an evaluation of widely-used models and observed a prevalent occurrence of belief entrenchment across various domains, prompts, and model configurations.

Energy consumption is an increasing concern across various domains, ranging from mobile devices to large-scale data centers. Developers require precise information regarding the energy usage of their software to address and mitigate consumption-related issues. Historically, research has predominantly concentrated on broader aspects, such as entire programs or specific functions, rather than examining individual source code statements. Moreover, previous approaches mainly relied on estimating the CPU's energy consumption without considering other hardware components, thus complicating statistical reasoning and explainability. To address these limitations, we have developed an innovative methodology that extends beyond merely assessing CPU consumption and point estimates. Specifically, we focus on predicting the energy usage of statically typed JVM-based programming languages, such as Java, Kotlin, or Scala. We analyze the transformation of source code into Java bytecode and subsequently measure the associated energy consumption. By utilizing Bayesian statistical methods, we construct a predictive model to estimate energy usage and identify the factors influencing it. This model incorporates three static code-derived factors—data size, data type, and operation—as well as one hardware-specific factor, namely the device on which the code is executed. To evaluate the efficacy of our methodology, we applied it to Java, analyzing the energy consumption patterns associated with Java bytecode, and assessed its predictive accuracy on previously unseen programs. The results indicate that all four factors are significant, with particular emphasis on the observation that energy consumption can vary considerably even between devices of the same model, and that operations and data types substantially influence energy usage. Furthermore, the experimental results demonstrate that the predicted energy consumption of programs closely aligns with their actual energy usage, thereby substantiating the validity of our approach. Our work proposes a methodology for constructing an energy model that can serve as a foundation for future applications, such as energy-aware program analysis or verification tools, to evaluate the energy efficiency of software systems.

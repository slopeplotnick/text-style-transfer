Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has significantly improved the performance of large language models in knowledge-intensive tasks. However, most existing systems focus on generic or news-based domains, with limited optimization for high-stakes applications such as agricultural climate adaptation. Although recent studies have attempted to incorporate document retrieval and climate data for domain-specific queries, they still rely on static methodologies that fail to provide region-specific and verifiable responses. Geography-aware question-answering systems have been explored in various domains; however, these approaches are not specifically designed for climate-related reasoning. Moreover, existing research often treats climate data and literature as distinct entities, thereby lacking an integrated methodology. CAIRNS comprises four stages: (1) location-weighted retrieval of climate literature from the Elsevier database alongside data acquisition from a climate data API; (2) answer generation with the ScholarGuide Prompt; (3) answer verification; and (4) a hybrid evaluation process. To identify the pertinent documents, we developed a retriever that integrates both sparse lexical matching and dense semantic retrieval. However, relying solely on this hybrid retrieval approach is inadequate, as geographical context significantly influences relevance in climate adaptation inquiries. Therefore, we incorporate a location similarity score to ensure that the responses are pertinent in both semantic and spatial dimensions. CAIRNS employs an iterative process wherein it retrieves evidence, formulates an initial response, and evaluates the adequacy of the information provided. If any informational deficiencies are identified, the system generates supplementary sub-questions and re-engages in the retrieval process to enhance the response until it is deemed satisfactory. Central to the answer formulation within CAIRNS is the ScholarGuide Prompt, which serves as a guiding framework for the language model, facilitating the generation of responses that balance readability with factual accuracy. Specifically, for climate-related inquiries, it directs the model to structure answers systematically, incorporate scientific reasoning, and provide precise, actionable recommendations.

Nearswap, as proposed by Goddard et al. (2024), is a merging method that strengthens interpolation when parameters are similar and weakens it when they differ. To compare different model architectures, we selected three families of Large Language Models (LLMs) for our experiments: GPT, LLAMA, and QWEN. Specifically, the GPT family, developed by Radford et al. (2019) and Gao et al. (2020), includes models such as GPT2-small, GPT2-medium, GPT2-large, GPT2-x1, and GPT-neo-2.7B. Similarly, the LLAMA family, introduced by Touvron et al. (2023) and Dubey et al. (2024), comprises LLAMA-2-7B, LLAMA-3-8B, LLAMA-3.1-8B, LLAMA-3.2-1B, and LLAMA-3.2-3B. Finally, the Qwen family, established by Qwen in 2024, consists of QWEN2-0.5B, QWEN2-1.5B, and QWEN2-7B. These models are available from the Hugging Face repository, with URLs provided in Appendix A. Moreover, the computational resources required for continual pretraining to develop biased LLMs are detailed in Appendix C, while hyperparameter configurations are outlined in Appendix D. To investigate social bias in LLMs, we utilize three benchmarks: the Bias Benchmark for Question-Answering (BBQ), the Bias in Open-Ended Language Generation Dataset (BOLD), and HONEST. The URLs for these datasets are listed in Appendix E. The BBQ dataset, introduced by Parrish et al. (2022), comprises approximately 58,000 templated question-answer pairs across nine social dimensions relevant to U.S. English speakers. By contrasting "underspecified" and "fully specified" versions of each question, it measures the extent to which models rely on stereotypical priors rather than explicit evidence. Similarly, the BOLD dataset, delineated by Dhamala et al. (2021), contains 23,679 prompts organized into 43 demographic subgroups covering occupation, gender, race, religion, and political ideology. To ensure that debiasing techniques do not adversely affect performance on ancillary tasks, we evaluate both the debiased and pre-trained LLMs using the SuperGLUE benchmark, which comprises eight tasks: BoolQ, CB, COPA, MultiRC, ReCoRD, RTE, WiC, and WSC. All evaluations are conducted using the Language Model Evaluation Harness.

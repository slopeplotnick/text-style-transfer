Probabilistic programming languages (PPL) are languages that feature first-class constructs for probabilistic elements, such as distributions and their samplers. Numerous popular examples exist, spanning both imperative and functional paradigms. However, a key shortcoming of existing languages is their inadequate adaptability to text-rich graphs, which are crucial data structures for defining specifications for language model systems (LMS). This limitation necessitated the design of LUMOS. Typically, these languages are generative, based on user-defined probability distributions. For instance, one language is used to generate random images or scenarios relevant to autonomous vehicles, utilizing user-defined scene graphs. To construct real-world specifications, a fixed graph is employed, from which segments are sampled to delineate scenarios. Domain-specific languages (DSLs) for large language models (LLMs) function as query languages devoid of probabilistic constructs, facilitating the composition, optimization, and security of prompts to elicit desirable responses from a target LMS. These languages complement LUMOS, which assesses LMS behaviors across distributions of prompts defined within its framework. The process of LMS alignment involves identifying vulnerabilities by evaluating performance or conducting adversarial tests. Recent advancements have introduced probabilistic certifiers for specific LMS properties, offering formal guarantees for systematic evaluation. In this study, we generalize LMS certification through LUMOS to specify and verify arbitrary desirable properties.

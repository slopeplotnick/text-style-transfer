Agentic systems are modern software architectures: they consist of orchestrated modules, expose interfaces, and are deployed in software pipelines. Unlike conventional programs, their execution is inherently stochastic and adaptive to the problems they are solving. Evaluation of such systems is often outcome-centric, focusing on success or failure at the final step. However, this narrow focus overlooks detailed insights into how agents reason, plan, act, or adapt their strategies over time. Inspired by the structured representation of conventional software systems as graphs, we developed GRAPHECTORY to systematically encode the temporal and semantic relationships within these systems. GRAPHECTORY facilitates the design of process-centric metrics and analyses to assess the quality of agentic workflows independently of their final success. Using GRAPHECTORY, we analyzed 4000 trajectories from two dominant agentic programming frameworks, SWE-agent and OpenHands, in combination with four backbone Large Language Models (LLMs), to address issues from the SWE-Bench Verified benchmark. Our automated analyses reveal several key insights: (1) agents using richer prompts or more advanced LLMs exhibit more complex GRAPHECTORY structures, reflecting deeper exploration, broader context integration, and more thorough validation before patch submission; (2) agents' problem-solving strategies vary with both the complexity of the problem and the underlying LLM—successful resolutions typically follow structured processes involving issue localization, patching, and validation, whereas unresolved cases display chaotic, repetitive, or regressive behaviors; (3) even when successful, agentic programming systems often demonstrate inefficiencies, resulting in unnecessarily prolonged trajectories. Agentic systems powered by large language models (LLMs) have recently emerged as a promising paradigm for autonomously solving complex, multi-step tasks across diverse domains, including software engineering, web navigation, virtual assistance, scientific discovery, and robotic navigation. Similar to traditional software systems, agentic systems are composed of modules such as LLMs, tools, APIs, and memory, and are characterized by defined inputs, internal states, execution logic, and outputs. However, instead of deterministic implementations, these systems operate by generating trajectories—chronological sequences of reasoning, action, and observation steps—that collectively implement the execution logic for task completion. Evaluation of agentic software systems has been mostly outcome-centric, determining the correctness and quality of the produced trajectory by whether they can solve a problem.

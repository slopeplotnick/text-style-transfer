- **New Metric: Verifier Gain.** Verifier accuracy alone provides an incomplete picture of verifier usefulness at test time. To address this limitation, we introduce verifier gain, a metric that quantifies the improvement achieved through test-time rejection sampling with a verifier. Empirical studies reveal that this theoretical formulation aligns closely with observed performance trends.  
- **Self-Improvement, Intra-Family Improvement, and Cross-Family Improvement.** We systematically compare self-verification, intra-family verification, and cross-family verification, finding that cross-family verification consistently delivers the highest gains, particularly when contrasted with self-verification. This disparity arises from the similarity between the solution distributions of the solver and verifier: as these distributions converge, verifier gain diminishes. Notably, stronger models—whether due to increased scale, extended training, or higher solver accuracy—exhibit reduced self-verification efficacy but improved cross-family verification performance.  
- **Dataset Verifiability.** We investigate whether tasks that are easy to solve are also easy to verify and explore differences in inherent task verifiability. Our findings indicate that verification accuracy generally correlates with solver accuracy, though self-verification provides minimal verifier gain across all tasks. Interestingly, tasks requiring mathematical or logical reasoning consistently yield higher verifier gains.  
- **Verifiers.** Verifiers can operate at two levels: outcome-level verification, which assesses the correctness of the final answer, and process-level verification, which evaluates the accuracy of intermediate reasoning steps. Recent work by Weng et al. (2023), Wu et al. (2024), and Jiang et al. (2024) has introduced methods for outcome-level self-verification by predicting components of the input question conditioned on the solution. To mitigate hallucinations, Dhuliawala et al. (2024) propose fact-checking mechanisms where models generate fact-check questions to validate their outputs. Additionally, researchers have developed general-purpose outcome-level verifiers (Hosseini et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025; Cobbe et al., 2021) and value models (Yu et al., 2024), either independently or jointly with solvers (Shen et al., 2021; Sareen et al., 2025). Efforts in process-level verification have focused on deductively verifying intermediate reasoning steps.

The base model sometimes struggles to follow instructions with longer prompts, so we added a quick warm-up stage via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to help it reliably adhere to the required format. In this process, the Challenger role engages in few-shot grounded generation and computes a corresponding reward. Meanwhile, the Solver module participates in online curriculum learning, iteratively computing its reward and updating its parameters. This ensures the Solver focuses on problems that are challenging yet solvable, consistent with optimal learning theory. The reward mechanism integrates both synthetic and human data, weighted by curriculum difficulty, to maintain an optimal balance between task complexity and learning progression. R-FeW retains the self-evolving framework of R-ZERO but incorporates a grounded and curriculum-aware data pipeline. The Challenger generates synthetic tasks with few-shot demonstrations from human anchors, while the Solver attempts to solve these tasks and estimates success rates. An online filtering mechanism selects tasks of moderate complexity from both synthetic and real-world datasets. We utilize Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base as our backbone models. For comparison, we evaluate against several baselines: (1) Base Model — the pretrained checkpoint without any additional fine-tuning, available at https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Base (Qwen3-8B-Base); (2) R-Zero — the pioneering ungrounded self-play framework where the model autonomously generates its own questions and answers, enabling iterative self-improvement through independent reasoning; (3) Absolute Zero — a self-play paradigm specifically constrained to code generation tasks, employing Python-based execution as a verification mechanism; (4) SPICE — a grounded self-play framework that leverages retrieval from extensive document corpora to construct diverse reasoning tasks with document-grounded answers. We reproduced baselines (1) and (2) using publicly available configurations under identical training conditions, while results for baselines (3) and (4) were directly referenced from the findings reported by Liu et al.

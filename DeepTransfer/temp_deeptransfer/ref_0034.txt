In this section, we introduce MIGR, a method that determines which modality to prioritize first based on Mutual Information (MI). Through this approach, we aim to mitigate biases in the initial reasoning phase and ensure that the reasoning process remains anchored in the modality with the most significant emotional cues. Our method comprises two primary stages. First, we introduce modality-aligned SFT, which facilitates the model in determining the appropriate starting point for reasoning by focusing on the modality identified by MI as emotion-dominant. Next, we present Modality-Aware Reward Optimization, designed to reinforce modality prioritization and maintain emotion-grounded reasoning while enhancing stability during the optimization process. To achieve this, we propose two distinct rewards: the modality-aligned order reward, which encourages adherence to the optimal input sequence, and the modality-grounded reasoning reward, which supports reasoning grounded in emotional signals. The subsequent sections provide a detailed explanation of these steps. Recent research [15] has demonstrated the efficacy of SFT as an initial approach for emotion understanding tasks, even when utilizing a limited quantity of high-quality training data, such as that provided by the Explainable Multimodal Emotion Reasoning (EMER) dataset [27]. Drawing upon these findings, we adopt a similar cold-start strategy to initiate our training process. However, although EMER provides high-quality training data, the SFT stage can be further enhanced by incorporating additional samples that exhibit particularly clear and consistent emotional signals. Therefore, we augment our training dataset with supplementary emotion-consistent examples. These examples are identified by analyzing Facial Action Units (FAUs) extracted from video frames, which are strongly correlated with emotional expressions. We then verify whether the extracted FAU patterns correspond precisely to the target emotion by utilizing a predefined FAU-emotion mapping table. Only those instances where the FAU patterns exhibit an exact match with the target emotion are retained. For these selected instances, reasoning-answer pairs are constructed by leveraging FAU-consistent emotional evidence to inform and structure the reasoning process.

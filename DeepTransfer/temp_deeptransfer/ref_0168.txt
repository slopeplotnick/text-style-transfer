Several studies have explored more structured prompting paradigms. For example, Plan-and-Solve (Wang et al., 2023c) explicitly divides a task into a planning phase and a solution phase, achieving stronger results on mathematical and logical tasks. Similarly, Prompt Chaining (Chen et al., 2023) investigates whether incorporating intermediate summaries or steps can enhance performance in text summarization tasks. Complexity-based Prompting (Fu et al., 2023) argues that task complexity should inform prompt construction, an idea closely aligned with our focus on prompt specificity. While most prior work emphasizes task format or exemplar inclusion, our study uniquely examines prompt specificity as a continuous dimension, systematically varying detail and structure. Furthermore, recent research has introduced methods for refining prompts, either automatically or with human intervention. For instance, black-box optimization methods (Diao et al., 2023) refine prompts using task-specific feedback without requiring access to model gradients. Prompt Engineering a Prompt Engineer (Zhou et al., 2023) demonstrates that large language models can be prompted to iteratively improve their own prompts, a metaprompting approach akin to the generalization loop described in our work. Prompt Refinement with Image Pivots (Lu et al., 2023) leverages cross-modal information by incorporating visual features to refine textual prompts for image generation tasks. Although our work is text-only, we share the overarching goal of refining prompt representations to improve downstream fidelity. In contrast to these studies, DETAIL emphasizes the role of textual specificity in modulating reasoning capabilities, rather than focusing on semantic drift or multi-modal integration. Comprehensive reviews, such as Liu et al. (2023), have surveyed reasoning-focused prompting techniques, highlighting an expanding taxonomy of prompt formats, strategies, and task types. Nevertheless, few studies have systematically explored how the level of detail or granularity in prompts affects model behavior across tasks and systems. While approaches like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and Plan-and-Solve underscore the importance of prompt content, they do not provide an in-depth analysis of this dimension.

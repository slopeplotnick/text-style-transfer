The model undergoes training similar to the previous SFT procedure; however, this phase utilizes reasoning exemplars generated during the training process. Moreover, in addition to the latent tokens produced by the model, we randomly substitute a proportion of sentences with latent tokens, mirroring the approach employed in the SFT phase. This methodology further enhances the model's reasoning efficiency. During inference, the model defaults to a standard mode, but upon encountering specific tags, it transitions to an alternative reasoning mode, as depicted in Figure 1. These tags delineate the distinction between the two modes; however, they do not constitute unique tokens within the model's lexicon. Instead of explicitly training the model to utilize these tags, we employ a prompting strategy to elicit their use. Furthermore, the model determines the quantity of latent tokens to generate by predicting a numerical value. Initially, the utilization of these tags is minimal; however, their occurrence increases progressively during the course of reinforcement learning training. Flawed Fictions constitutes a benchmark introduced by Ahuja et al. (2025) designed to evaluate a range of narrative-based reasoning competencies, including theory-of-mind and state-tracking. The dataset is derived from short stories available on Project Gutenberg and is generated using FlawedFictionSMaker, an algorithm that systematically introduces plot holes and continuity errors through the application of large language models (LLMs).

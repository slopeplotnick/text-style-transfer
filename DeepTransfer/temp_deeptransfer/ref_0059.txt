Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly employed in applications such as Google's AI Overviews, which summarize information from multiple long documents. However, it remains uncertain whether these models assign equal weight to all inputs. To investigate this, researchers focused on abortion-related news and constructed 40 triplets of articles—each containing one pro, one neutral, and one con perspective. These triplets were randomized into six distinct input orders, and Gemini 2.5 Flash was prompted to generate a neutral summary for each permutation. Summaries were then evaluated against their source articles using metrics such as ROUGE-L for lexical overlap, BERTScore for semantic similarity, and SummaC for factual consistency. Notably, one-way ANOVA revealed a significant primacy effect for BERTScore across all stances, indicating that summaries were more semantically aligned with the first article in the sequence. Pairwise comparisons further demonstrated that the first article exerted a significantly greater influence than the second and third articles, which showed comparable levels of impact. These findings highlight potential risks for applications that depend on LLM-generated summaries and for agentic AI systems, where the reliance on LLMs in critical steps can disproportionately affect downstream decisions. Moreover, the rapid advancement of generative AI, a foundational component of agentic systems, has accelerated the development and deployment of these systems across diverse domains. Agentic AI is distinguished by its ability to pursue complex objectives adaptively with minimal direct supervision, showcasing a high degree of operational autonomy. A key mechanism enabling this autonomy is goal decomposition, wherein high-level objectives are broken down into manageable subtasks. To execute these subtasks effectively, agentic systems engage in multi-step reasoning and planning, retrieving and synthesizing external information—such as through retrieval-augmented generation and tool use—to inform subsequent actions. Therefore, the reliability of an agentic system is fundamentally tied to the accuracy with which its underlying LLMs retrieve, prioritize, and synthesize information from directives and external sources.

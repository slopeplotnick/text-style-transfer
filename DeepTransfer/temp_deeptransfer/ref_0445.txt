We source forecasting questions from Metaculus [Metaculus, 2015] and Polymarket [Polymarket, 2020] to test belief entrenchment in LLMs. We choose this domain for two key reasons: first, forecasting questions come with ground truth labels once resolved; second, achieving high accuracy on a set of forecasting questions reflects Bayesian-like reasoning, as it requires seeking evidence and proportionally updating beliefs. Although forecasting differs from factual question answering, accurate forecasting nonetheless requires well-calibrated, unbiased belief updates, making it a strong proxy for rational reasoning under uncertainty. Moreover, scientific peer review is another setting that satisfies all three criteria for evaluating belief entrenchment. In this context, we use the open-access ICLR submission dataset from OpenReview [Höpner et al., 2025], which includes paper abstracts, bibliographies, reviewer comments, rebuttals, and final acceptance decisions. In our setup, the model is prompted to act as an area chair, making a final acceptance decision based on the abstract and the arguments presented in the reviews and rebuttals. This task allows us to evaluate how reasoning unfolds when prior impressions, such as those from the abstract, may conflict with later-stage evidence, like critiques and responses. Furthermore, we evaluate belief entrenchment with two reasoning techniques: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) [Wei et al., 2022] and debate [Khan et al., 2024]. The debate method involves two clones of the same expert model holding opposing positions on a topic, thereby allowing a less informed observer to arrive at a better understanding of the subject. We conduct experiments using models such as GPT-4o, DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek V3, Gemini 2.0 Flash, LLaMA 4 Scout, and LLaMA 4 Maverick. To examine how prior beliefs affect reasoning, we manipulate model priors via system prompts. Specifically, in addition to a baseline with no system prompt, we introduce two prompting conditions: a prior-conforming prompt and a critical-thinking prompt.

Conducting an empirical survey on seven model-merging algorithms for social bias mitigation, utilizing three bias benchmarks and the SuperGLUE framework across 13 large language models, we identified SLERP with moderate interpolation weights as the most balanced method. This approach achieves effective bias reduction without sacrificing downstream accuracy. Moreover, it is crucial to verify performance on tasks such as reading comprehension and commonsense reasoning to ensure successful social bias mitigation. Recently, model merging has emerged as an effective strategy for combining the strengths of multiple models without expensive retraining, as documented by Li et al. (2023) and Yang et al. (2024). This technique involves fusing parameters from two or more trained models to produce a single model that retains and integrates knowledge and skills from all sources. The linear averaging method, pioneered by Wortsman et al. (2022), exemplifies a straightforward technique by treating model weights as vectors and merging them through arithmetic means. It offers a cost-effective way to incorporate diverse expertise by leveraging existing fine-tuned models without additional training. Furthermore, research by Matena and Raffel (2022) and Lee et al. (2025) generalizes this idea by weighting each parameter inversely to its Fisher information, resulting in combinations consistent with likelihood. Additionally, merging methods based on sphere interpolation, as discussed by Shoemake (1985) and Goddard et al. (2024), regard parameter vectors as lying on a spherical manifold. SLERP, introduced by Shoemake (1985), performs interpolation between two models, while the Karcher Mean, proposed by Grove and Karcher (1973), iteratively finds the Riemannian centroid for any number of models. Building upon these concepts, NuSLERP, developed by Goddard et al. (2024), adds per-tensor normalization to correct for norm drift. Inspired by these model-merging approaches, Ilharco et al. (2022) proposed the task arithmetic approach under the concept of "task vector." Task vectors represent the parameters of the difference between a pre-trained LLM and its fine-tuned counterpart.

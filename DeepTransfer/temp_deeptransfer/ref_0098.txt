Language models offer a transformative approach to overcoming the limitations of traditional mental health monitoring methods (Xu et al., 2024). Unlike conventional supervised learning classifiers, they excel in capturing nuanced context, acquiring information, and generalizing across diverse scenarios, making them particularly effective for large-scale and real-world mental health assessments (Jin et al., 2025). However, the adoption of large language models (LLMs) is not without challenges. Specifically, their computational demands and reliance on remote servers for processing sensitive data raise significant privacy concerns (Jin et al., 2025). Furthermore, their substantial size renders them impractical for deployment in resource-constrained on-device environments, highlighting the need for more efficient alternatives. In contrast, Small Language Models (SLMs) emerge as promising solutions for digital health applications, offering comparable or even superior performance due to their lightweight architectures, reduced inference costs, and ability to operate locally on personal devices (Abdin et al., 2024; Jia et al., 2025). This enables privacy-preserving mental health prediction from social media activity, facilitating real-time, ethically responsible monitoring without the need to store personal data (Figure 1). Despite their advantages, the potential of SLMs for mental health prediction remains underexplored. In this study, we investigate the efficacy of SLMs in predicting digital mental health outcomes and demonstrate that, with careful design and optimization, they serve as practical alternatives for multi-task mental health assessment on-device. To this end, we evaluate SLM performance across six mental health classification tasks, including stress, depression, and suicidality. We introduce Menta, a compact model fine-tuned using a LoRA-based multi-task framework that incorporates cross-dataset training and a balanced accuracy-oriented loss function. Menta achieves robust performance across diverse mental health tasks, with particular strength in detecting stress and depression. Moreover, its practical utility is evidenced by its deployment on an iPhone 15 Pro Max, where it delivers real-time inference with minimal memory usage, enabling private, scalable, and on-device mental health support.

We evaluate LLMs on three benchmarks spanning diverse domains: MMLU-Pro (Wang et al., 2024), a knowledge-intensive and reasoning-focused benchmark covering 14 academic disciplines; MATH500 (Lightman et al., 2024), a collection of 500 complex mathematical problems sourced from the MATH dataset (Hendrycks et al., 2021); and UniMoral (Kumar and Jurgens, 2025), a suite of moral-judgment questions requiring sophisticated social reasoning. All evaluations were conducted in five languages: English (en), Chinese (zh), Spanish (es), French (fr), and Hindi (hi), which were selected to represent a diverse range of language families, geographic regions, and resource availability. A critical consideration in multilingual tasks is the choice of system prompt language: whether to use English, the primary training language for most models, or to align the prompt language with that of the task. To address this, we compare two configurations. In the English-prompt configuration, English prompts are applied to questions posed in various languages, whereas in the same-language configuration, the prompt is translated into the language of the question. To better understand how different aspects of model behavior interconnect, we examine the relationships among four performance metrics in this section. Specifically, we investigate the influence of system prompt components on model behavior through a regression analysis that links prompt components and language configurations to multilingual performance metrics. As illustrated in Figure 4, system prompt components exhibit diverse impacts on model performance, which can be broadly categorized into three groups. Notably, certain components demonstrate overall positive effects on model behavior. For instance, Chain-of-Thought, emotion, and scenario are positively associated with both accuracy and consistency, while also reducing accuracy variance across languages. This suggests their effectiveness in guiding the model toward consistently high performance across multilingual contexts. Upon closer inspection of model outputs, we observe that these components elicit more structured and coherent reasoning, which likely contributes to the observed improvements in performance.

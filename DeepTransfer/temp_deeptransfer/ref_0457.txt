In this section, we describe the computation of each evaluation metric and the interpretation of their outputs, focusing on lexical overlap, semantic similarity, and factual consistency. ROUGE-L [Lin, 2004] measures the longest common subsequence (LCS) between a model-generated summary (hypothesis) and a reference summary. By capturing sentence-level structural similarity, ROUGE-L is less sensitive to variations in word order. Typically employed as a reference-based metric, it evaluates lexical overlap between system-generated summaries and human-authored references. In this study, however, we are not concerned with the absolute ROUGE-L score; instead, we examine relative differences across input-order conditions to investigate potential primacy effects. Since ROUGE-L reflects surface-level lexical congruence, we complement it with two additional metrics—BERTScore and SummaC—to assess semantic similarity and factual consistency, respectively. To move beyond surface-level word overlap, BERTScore [Zhang et al., 2019] leverages contextual embeddings from a pre-trained encoder (RoBERTa-large in this study) to compute token-level semantic similarity. For each token in the hypothesis, BERTScore identifies the most semantically similar token in the reference and calculates precision, recall, and F1 scores based on these alignments. Because contextual embeddings account for synonymy and paraphrastic variation, BERTScore is particularly effective for evaluating semantic similarity beyond exact lexical matches. Consistent with our study design, we focus on relative differences in BERTScore across input-order conditions rather than absolute similarity values, which may be lower due to the abstractive nature of the summaries. To assess factual consistency, we employ SummaC, which evaluates whether the generated summary is entailed by the original documents. SummaC is available in two variants: SummaC-ZS (zero-shot, parameter-free) and SummaC-Conv (trained convolutional model). Due to its superior performance, we use SummaC-Conv. However, the underlying RoBERTa-based NLI model is constrained by a limitation on the volume of input it can process.

The present study evaluates four distinct models—MOIRAI, CHRONOS, GTT, and LightGTS—to assess their efficacy in managing variations in data and training methodologies. Each model employs a unique approach to learning and prediction, with distinct mechanisms that influence their adaptability to fluctuations in data size. To ensure compatibility with our dataset and to isolate the effects of data size variations on model performance, we utilize the smallest variants of these models. For instance, CHRONOS adopts a methodology that segments data into smaller units prior to the learning phase, thereby mitigating the impact of data size on its predictive accuracy. This approach ensures the model remains unaffected by large numerical values. Moreover, to prevent any individual data point from skewing the results, we standardize all data prior to training. Subsequently, each data element is calibrated to maintain equilibrium across the dataset. To evaluate model performance, we employ the Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE) metric, which provides an assessment of predictive accuracy that remains invariant to data size variations. Our objective is to analyze the impact of data size alterations on model efficacy. To this end, we demonstrate the effect by contrasting the reduction in MASE with a conventional approach.

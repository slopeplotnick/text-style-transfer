In this section, we illustrate the explanatory process discussed earlier through an example. We use the CelebA dataset [Liu et al., 2015], which contains over 200,000 images of celebrities' faces, each annotated with binary attributes describing facial features and accessories, such as whether the individual is smiling, wearing glasses, of young age, or appears attractive. For this example, we focus on two tasks: classifying whether an image depicts a Young individual and whether the person is Attractive. Turning to the images in Figure 6, the figure begins with the original image on the left and presents various counterfactual versions of the same image, all generated under the intervention do(Young = 0), which renders the individual not young. The first counterfactual image was generated using a specific methodology. While XAI systems that rely solely on visual assessments can sometimes be misleading [Adebayo et al., 2018], saliency maps remain a widely used technique for attribution-based explanations in image classification systems. Therefore, we compare the results of our causal concept-based explanations with those obtained using Grad-CAM [Selvaraju et al., 2017], implemented by Gildenblat [2021]. First, consider the results in Figure 7, which explain why the two original images (far left) in Figure 5 were classified as young. Interestingly, the chin area was crucial for the male's classification, whereas the area around the eyes was most significant for the female's classification. Next, Figure 8 examines the classification of the subject depicted in Figure 6. The original image was correctly classified as young, with the Grad-CAM explanation indicating that the classifier focused on the area around the eyes to reach this conclusion. However, for the misclassified image on the right, the Grad-CAM explanation shows that the classifier concentrated on the forehead and chin regions. Grad-CAM provides non-causal explanations and, as a result, cannot offer counterfactual insights. Instead, it highlights the regions of the input space most indicative of the classifier's decision. Furthermore, because Grad-CAM explanations are pixel-based, they do not provide an understanding of why these regions are deemed important.

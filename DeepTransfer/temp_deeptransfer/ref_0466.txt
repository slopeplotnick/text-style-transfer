Jack Lu*, Ryan Teehan*, Jinran Jin, and Mengye Ren Agentic Learning AI Lab, New York University {yl11330, rst306, jj3007, mengye}@nyu.edu https://agenticlearning.ai/llm-verificationNovember 20, 2025 Large language models (LLMs) serve as both problem solvers and solution verifiers, with verifiers enhancing solver performance by selecting high-quality answers from a pool of candidates. Nevertheless, prior research on solver-verifier interactions has been limited, primarily focusing on self-verification while seldom exploring how verifiers assess outputs from models within their own family or across different families. Furthermore, while modern LLMs undergo extensive post-training, the impact of this process on their verification capabilities remains insufficiently understood. To address these gaps, we conducted a systematic study of 37 models spanning diverse families, sizes, and base versus post-trained variants, evaluated across nine benchmarks including logical reasoning, structured puzzles, symbolic computation, mathematics, commonsense, factual recall, and domain knowledge. Our analysis compares self-verification with verification within the same family and across different families. To support this, we introduce verifier gain, a novel metric empirically validated to predict performance improvements through test-time verifier-based rejection sampling. Additionally, we examine how metrics such as verifier gain and false positive rate scale with model size and post-training, while characterizing differences in dataset verifiability. The findings reveal that cross-family verification is particularly effective; post-training enhances cross-family verification but yields limited improvements in self-verification; and mathematical and logical tasks demonstrate the highest inherent verifiability. This dual functionality—where models not only generate solutions but also verify their correctness—marks a significant shift in the capabilities of LLMs. The paradigm of test-time solution verification encompasses a broad spectrum, ranging from straightforward strategies like generating multiple candidate solutions and filtering them through a verifier (Zhao et al., 2025) to more sophisticated iterative refinement techniques (Madaan et al., 2023). By incorporating generative verification at test time, LLMs can tackle more complex problems than they could independently (Cobbe et al., 2021; Lightman et al., 2024).

As AI tools powered by Large Language Models (LLMs) become increasingly prevalent, ensuring their secure and safe deployment has emerged as a top priority. This need becomes even more critical in multilingual and multimodal environments, where cultural and linguistic differences can introduce unique risks. Therefore, safety guardrails designed to filter harmful, biased, or unsafe outputs must function effectively across diverse languages and situational contexts. Without such mechanisms, the global deployment of LLMs risks unintentionally causing harm or excluding large populations. While most existing research has focused on high-resource languages such as English, Chinese, Spanish, German, and French, the majority of the world's languages fall into the low-resource category, where limited training data and benchmarks exacerbate the challenges of enforcing safety protocols. Moreover, current evaluation benchmarks for multilingual robustness remain narrow in scope, particularly for low-resource languages, and fail to adequately capture the linguistic diversity of global users. Additionally, the development of naturally occurring training datasets tailored to low-resource languages for multilingual safety systems has been largely overlooked. Recent efforts have primarily concentrated on curating multilingual training datasets for high-resource languages by aggregating authentic and machine-translated data. However, these initiatives only scratch the surface of what is required to build truly inclusive safety systems. Compounding these challenges is the significant computational cost of existing safety guardrails, which are often too large, slow, and resource-intensive to operate on edge devices or in low-power environments. Consequently, their high inference times hinder their applicability in real-world production settings. These limitations underscore the urgent need for lightweight, parameter-efficient guardrails capable of safeguarding both the LLM and its users from potential harm.

The advancement of computer utilization is being significantly enhanced through the development of innovative tools and systems. These advancements enable autonomous comprehension and execution by computers. Moreover, open-source systems have been developed to streamline the integration of various components, such as planners and memory modules. Empirical evidence from corporate reports illustrates the practical application of these systems. However, several studies indicate that merely increasing model size does not inherently enhance reliability. Therefore, there is an emphasis on evaluating the operational efficacy of these systems to ensure genuine improvements in performance. Assessing these systems presents significant challenges due to the increasing complexity of the tasks they perform. Some studies explore the utilization of specialized systems to assist with evaluations; however, concerns regarding potential bias persist. To address these issues, it is recommended to employ multiple evaluators and ensure that assessments are equitable across various tasks. The proposed methodology incorporates a dual-judge system to independently evaluate distinct aspects of the task, ensuring the validity of observed improvements. The dataset comprises 100 real-world editing tasks involving slide decks. Each task includes an initial slide deck, a corresponding target deck, structured textual instructions, and a rubric detailing criteria for layout, typography, color, and content evaluation. The tasks were categorized into sixteen distinct groups to ensure comprehensive evaluation of both semantic understanding and intricate formatting precision. Data was sourced from a corpus exceeding 15,000 PowerPoint presentations derived from diverse origins, subsequently converted into a standardized format that encapsulates detailed layout and content specifications. Presentations exhibiting a wide range of stylistic variations were retained and further augmented with contributions from domain experts. From an initial pool of over 500 reviewed options, including submissions from students, a selection of 100 examples was curated to encompass a diverse range of professional and academic domains, ensuring that each subset includes sufficiently challenging instances.

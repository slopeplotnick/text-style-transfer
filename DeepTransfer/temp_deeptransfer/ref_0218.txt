Baselines and Models. Gemini-2.0-Flash serves as the foundational model for our baseline assessments, operating without external information and relying solely on the original query. We conduct experiments with various configurations of the CAIRNS framework: (1) Domain RAG (literature-only), which involves retrieval exclusively from the literature subset; (2) Domain RAG (literature+data), which incorporates both literature and structured climate data through a two-step API reasoning process. Gemini-2.0-Flash remains integral to CAIRNS and its various iterations. All models utilize identical corpora, prompts, and evaluation settings to ensure fairness. Impact of ScholarGuide Prompt (SGP). The ScholarGuide Prompt enhances readability by improving contextual richness, structural coherence, and language fluency. Additionally, it addresses inconsistencies in citations, thereby increasing the reliability of the information, despite a slight reduction in the overall citation rate. By employing a unified reference numbering system and anchoring each factual assertion to explicit evidence, the approach facilitates the generation of clearer, more precise, and context-specific responses. This methodology proves effective across both zero-shot and few-shot scenarios, underscoring the pivotal role of structured prompting in driving the performance of CAIRNS. Impact of Multi-turn ReAct (MTR). The integration of Multi-turn ReAct further enhances the model's outputs by iteratively aligning retrieved literature with climate data, thereby producing more comprehensive and contextually enriched responses. Although its impact is less pronounced compared to SGP, MTR contributes to the structured generation process, enhancing the model's ability to address complex, multi-faceted questions. Location-Weighted Retrieval (LWR). Similarly, the Location-Weighted Retrieval mechanism facilitates the generation of region-specific responses, particularly for geographically focused queries. Ablating LWR results in a slight reduction in specificity, while other evaluation metrics remain largely unaffected, underscoring its role in improving geographic sensitivity without compromising overall performance.

This work introduces a novel approach to disentangling perception and reasoning within AI models. By incorporating examples of erroneous reasoning into the training process, the model is equipped to discern between valid and flawed reasoning pathways. Moreover, we propose a new framework, Self-Taught Learning (STL), which simultaneously enhances both perceptual and reasoning capabilities. Unlike earlier methodologies that predominantly emphasized reasoning, STL fosters a more integrated improvement across both domains. Our method was evaluated across domains such as commonsense, language, and science, demonstrating enhanced performance without reliance on human intervention. In the realm of vision-language reasoning, integrating reasoning processes is crucial for augmenting model capabilities. Although contemporary models exhibit proficiency in standardized assessments, incorporating visual information into reasoning remains challenging, particularly for open-source models. Some methodologies employ prompts to facilitate step-by-step cognitive processing in models. For instance, one approach involves an initial context analysis followed by the generation of high-level features, whereas another utilizes scene graphs to elucidate object–attribute relationships. Additionally, some strategies involve training models on datasets comprising multimodal reasoning chains; however, creating such datasets is often resource-intensive. Our methodology facilitates models in autonomously enhancing their reasoning capabilities without the necessity for specialized datasets. Self-training constitutes a semi-supervised framework wherein a model refines its performance by utilizing its own outputs as training data. Within the realm of large language models, this approach augments reasoning proficiency by generating and iteratively employing reasoning steps. This process enhances models' capabilities without necessitating human intervention. While certain studies have advanced reasoning by selecting and refining appropriate reasoning examples, the application of self-training methodologies to vision-language models remains relatively unexplored.

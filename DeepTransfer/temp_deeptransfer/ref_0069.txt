We conducted a comparative analysis of our proposed method against the RePro approach, relying on the results reported in the original publication due to the unavailability of its implementation. P2C refers to Paper2Code. While the Refine framework requires manually designing separate evaluation and refinement prompts for each artifact—resulting in eight handcrafted prompts for the four planning artifacts in the original Paper2Code implementation—our approach eliminates this manual intervention by autonomously adapting to each artifact through its corresponding system prompt, thereby requiring no additional prompt engineering. To evaluate the effectiveness of our method, we conducted experiments on two benchmarks. The first, Paper2CodeBench, comprises 90 papers from leading 2024 conferences (ICLR, ICML, NeurIPS), filtered based on code availability and manageable length. Here, our generated code repositories are systematically compared against official author-released implementations, with correctness assessed by LLM-based judges on a 5-point scale. The second benchmark, PaperBench Code-Dev, includes 20 ICML 2024 papers, where human reviewers evaluated the functional correctness of the generated code using predefined grading rubrics. To the best of our knowledge, few existing methods employ iterative self-refinement for the automated transformation of research papers into executable code. Therefore, we compare our approach against two closely related baselines that incorporate iterative refinement: Paper2Code with Self-Refine. Self-Refine, specifically, is an iterative approach. All experiments were conducted using GPT-4.1 as the foundational model. For the RePro baseline, due to the lack of accessible source code, we directly cite the results from the original publication. The comparative performance of different optimization strategies across the two datasets is presented in Tables I and II. Our proposed approach demonstrates superior robustness across diverse datasets. Notably, even when optimization is restricted to the planning stage, our method (Paper2Code with autoplan optimization) consistently achieves measurable improvements. A detailed per-task analysis, illustrated in Figure 2, further highlights the advantages of our approach on the PaperBench benchmark. Specifically, our planning optimization outperforms the Self-Refine approach on 16 out of 20 tasks (80%), underscoring its robustness and adaptability across diverse scenarios. In contrast, Self-Refine exhibited suboptimal performance on 10 tasks, with particularly significant deficiencies observed in specific cases.

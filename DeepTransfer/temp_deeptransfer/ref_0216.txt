Based on the decomposition of item understanding, query understanding, and path mapping, we can synthesize the essential requirements for a robust relevance model. To effectively navigate the vast item corpus and satisfy complex user intents—ranging from direct specification to abstract reasoning—the model must possess a sophisticated, composite set of capabilities. We summarize these core requirements as follows: Reasoning and Knowledge Integration involves the ability to leverage external knowledge and logical inference to resolve ambiguity in query understanding and accurately map abstract intents during path construction. Moreover, Multi-modal Understanding and Matching requires the capacity to parse, align, and match attributes across textual and visual modalities, which is critical for handling image-heavy constraints in item understanding and path following. Furthermore, Complex Rule Adherence demands fidelity to learn and precisely execute the complex, often subtle, judgment rules required to ensure the standardization and reproducibility of the path-following process. Regarding the evolution of large model training paradigms for relevance, the relevance task can be regarded as a classification task. Initially, task requirements were incorporated as prompts and annotated results as responses, performing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) loss to refine large language models (LLMs), which served as our iterative baseline. However, simple SFT was soon proven to suffer from out-of-distribution performance degradation. Researchers discovered that leveraging the model's chain-of-thought (CoT) capabilities—prompting the model to reason before outputting answers—could effectively enhance model robustness and performance on challenging samples. Due to constraints in computational resources and application scenarios, models with relatively modest parameter sizes are typically selected for training, which implies insufficient reasoning capabilities and instruction-following abilities under the prompting-only paradigm. Therefore, the current mainstream approach is to leverage powerful teacher models, such as GPT-4 and DeepSeek-R1, to synthesize CoT data and then distill the reasoning capabilities into relatively smaller models through SFT. Motivated by the remarkable achievements of reinforcement learning in LLM training, some works further incorporate a reinforcement learning stage following CoT distillation, employing either offline RL methods such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and KTO or online reinforcement learning methods such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

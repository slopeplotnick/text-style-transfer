To effectively reason over extended video content, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methodologies have been developed to prioritize the identification and utilization of pertinent segments, such as specific frames or clips, informed by textual and visual cues rather than relying on random sampling. This approach enhances the model's ability to focus on critical elements within the video [10, 14, 31]. Recent advancements extend beyond traditional methodologies by employing graph-based structures to elucidate the interconnections among various video segments [18, 21, 32]. However, these models often exhibit an over-reliance on textual representations or simplistic visual-textual comparisons, which constrains their capacity for comprehensive understanding and intricate reasoning across extended video sequences. To address these limitations, memory-based approaches have been proposed to facilitate the organization and storage of video information in a structured format. For instance, EgoRAG [33] constructs hierarchical textual memories derived from egocentric video streams, enabling reasoning across extended daily activities. Building upon this framework, Ego-R1 [23] incorporates vision-centric methodologies with iterative reasoning mechanisms to enable long-horizon task comprehension. Similarly, HippoMM [12] introduces a dual-process memory architecture that integrates semantic summaries with multimodal cues, enhancing the model's capacity for structured knowledge representation and retrieval. M3-Agent [13] constructs an entity-centric memory framework and employs iterative reasoning to retrieve pertinent information. Despite these advancements, existing approaches encounter significant challenges in effectively integrating textual and visual modalities while adapting to varying temporal scales within videos, particularly in addressing complex scenarios. Therefore, we introduce WorldMM, an innovative memory-based framework that leverages multimodal information from videos to construct a comprehensive memory system, facilitating enhanced understanding and reasoning over extended video sequences. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the system operates in three stages: initially, it constructs multiple memory types, comprising two textual memories and one visual memory (Sec. 3.1). Subsequently, it employs an adaptive retrieval mechanism to extract requisite information from these memories across various temporal scales until sufficient data is amassed to address a query (Sec. 3.2). Ultimately, the query is fed into a response agent along with the retrieved contents and retrieval history to generate a response (Sec. 3.3).

The phase-field (PF) model is a continuum mechanics approach that simulates the kinetic evolution of a potentially multiphase and multimaterial system as it moves toward thermodynamic equilibrium. PF simulations efficiently manage the relevant length scales, from nanometers to microns, and time scales, extending to seconds or minutes, which are crucial for the bulk heterojunction (BHJ) formation in solution-processed solar cells. Consequently, the thermodynamic properties of the photovoltaic system under study are encapsulated within a Gibbs free energy functional. Our method focuses on the physical processes involved in BHJ formation, particularly solvent evaporation, liquid demixing, and crystallization. The forces driving the system toward thermodynamic equilibrium are derived from the Gibbs free energy functional. Furthermore, the kinetics of phase transitions, such as evaporation and crystallization, are described by Allen-Cahn equations coupled with Cahn-Hilliard equations that address diffusive mass transport. Throughout the simulation steps, we ensured that the PF model is integrated with a fluid dynamics solver, which accounts for advective mass transport. For further details on our simulation methodology, please refer to Section S2 of the Supporting Information. Using the calculated material properties, optical simulations are conducted to analyze light propagation within solar cells. Depending on the complexity of the solar cells, either numerical optical simulations or analytical methods, such as the transfer matrix method, are employed. The solar cell is modeled as a stack of stratified isotropic media, facilitating the modeling of light interaction effects. To enhance the predictive accuracy of the optical response, a surrogate machine learning model is utilized to approximate the charge generation rate obtained from scattering matrix simulations. Specifically, a neural network is trained to predict the generation rate based on variables such as the incident light angle, the thickness of the active layer, and the spectral characteristics of the light. Further details on the neural network surrogate model are provided in Section S3 of the Supporting Information.

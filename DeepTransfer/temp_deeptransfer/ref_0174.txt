To address the limitations of traditional methods, enhancing the high-resolution image perception capabilities of machine learning models has become a significant research focus. A widely adopted "locate-and-zoom-in" strategy is used to improve detail perception in these models. However, training-based approaches such as Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning, while effective in identifying relevant regions, are hindered by high computational costs, lengthy training cycles, and limited cross-system applicability, which restrict their scalability and practical use. In contrast, training-free methods automatically locate regions using attention mechanisms or tree-based search algorithms, eliminating the need for dataset construction or model fine-tuning. Despite employing a top-down search strategy from high to low resolution, these methods face notable limitations. A primary issue is their inadequate detection of small objects during the initial search stage, often leading to suboptimal search paths. Recently, inspired by the success of Retrieval-Augmented Generation for enabling long-context understanding in general language models, the Retrieval-Augmented Perception (RAP) framework has been introduced as a novel paradigm to enhance the perception of high-resolution images by machine learning models without additional training. The RAP framework consists of three key components: First, the Visual Retrieval module employs a pre-trained vision RAG model, VisRAG, to compute semantic similarity between the query and different image regions, retrieving the most relevant ones to reduce noise. Next, the Spatial-Awareness Layout module preserves the original spatial relationships among the retrieved segments, ensuring spatial coherence within the model input. Finally, the Adaptive Retrieval-Exploration Search (RE-Search) module dynamically determines the optimal number of retrieved segments by constructing a Retrieval-Exploration Tree (RE-Tree), thereby balancing information sufficiency with computational efficiency.

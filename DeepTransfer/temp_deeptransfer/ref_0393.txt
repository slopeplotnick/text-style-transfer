TCAS is a widely recognised program benchmark extensively studied in fault localisation research. It comprises a C program developed by Siemens, along with 41 versions containing intentionally introduced faults, with the locations and types of these faults explicitly documented. Conversely, C-PACK-IPAs is a collection of student-developed programs sourced from an introductory programming course. For this study, the entire C-PACK-IPAs benchmark was utilised, encompassing 25 programming assignments and a total of 1431 faulty programs derived from three laboratory classes. These programs address a variety of topics, including numerical operations, input-output processing, loop structures, character manipulation, and vector operations. Notably, C-PACK-IPAs has proven effective in facilitating the evaluation of diverse program analysis tasks. The CFAULTS framework employs pycparser for unrolling and instrumentalising C programs, while CBMC version 5.11 is used to encode C programs into CNF formulae. Due to the unavailability or obsolescence of the original implementations of BugAssIST and SNIPER, prototypes of their algorithms were developed. It is important to note that the earlier version of SNIPER was limited in its applicability, as it struggled with certain C programs, particularly those involving loops, recursion, and specific variable types. Furthermore, a novel iteration of the HSD algorithm was implemented to localise faulty statements in ANSI-C programs, incorporating both core minimisation and non-core minimisation variants. This enhancement was necessary because the original HSD algorithm was designed exclusively for Boolean circuits and could not handle C programs. Consequently, the algorithms BugAssIST, SNIPER, and HSD were adapted to work with ANSI-C programs by leveraging the unroller and instrumentaliser modules of the CFAULTS framework. All fault localisation algorithms were evaluated for their ability to generate diagnoses consistent with specific criteria, validated through CBMC. This validation is particularly relevant for BugAssIST, as it may produce diagnoses that do not align with all failing test cases. In contrast, the MaxSAT-based solution employed by CFAULTS and the hitting set approach utilised by HSD inherently ensure consistency with all observed failures. While SNIPER’s aggregation method generates valid diagnoses, these are not guaranteed to be subset-minimal. For BugAssIST, the process involves systematically evaluating all outputs generated by its scoring algorithm until a diagnosis is identified that aligns comprehensively with the observed phenomena.

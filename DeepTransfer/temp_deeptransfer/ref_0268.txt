The prototyped scale consists of nine items formatted on a 7-point Likert scale, divided into three subscales measuring AI-related emotions of Shame, Guilt, and Impostor Syndrome. To evaluate the criterion validity of the scale, a real-world dataset was collected through a survey study that included the finalized scale items alongside additional psychometric instruments. The online survey was conducted on Prolific from Monday, 03 February 2025, at 18:00 GMT, to Tuesday, 04 February 2025, at 23:30 GMT. Following data collection, the real-world dataset was compared with a previously generated simulated dataset using the same methodological approach as in Study 1. A demographic comparison of the two datasets is presented in Table 1. Further details about the SAGAT scale will be provided in a forthcoming scientific publication, which will follow the completion of a traditional scale development process informed in part by our experimental prototyping approach. Group comparisons between the real and simulated data were conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test. For the Impostor Syndrome factor, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test revealed no significant difference between real and simulated scores (U = 41,516, p = .13). However, significant differences were observed for the Shame (U = 29,457, p < .001) and Guilt (U = 26,392, p < .001) dimensions. These findings were further supported by Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests, which indicated significant distributional differences across all three constructs (all p values < .001). Consequently, the results do not support H4. Histograms comparing the distribution of the real and simulated datasets are displayed in Figure 4. To enhance the validity and reliability of our findings, we conducted a replication study aimed at developing another novel scale. Specifically, we created a scale to measure AI-linked anxious beliefs. This "AI Anxiety" scale was developed using the same methodological procedures outlined in Study 3, as described in the corresponding "Methods" section. For a more detailed account of the in silico scale development process using simulated data, readers are directed to the Supplementary Materials accompanying this article and the preregistration available on the Open Science Framework at the provided link.

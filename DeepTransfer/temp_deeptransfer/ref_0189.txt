KL-free GRPO Algorithm. As illustrated in Fig. 11, our reinforcement learning framework leverages the GRPO algorithm to optimize the objective function defined in Equation 13, which aims to maximize the advantage-weighted log probability expectation. The importance sampling ratio and advantage function are further detailed in Equation 14. In our implementation, we opted to exclude the KL divergence regularization term. This decision was informed by the observation that, after sufficient SFT warm-start, the model had already established a robust reasoning framework and structured output format. At this stage, imposing KL constraints would overly restrict the policy exploration space, thereby hindering the discovery of improved reasoning strategies. Therefore, we allowed the model to engage in freer policy optimization, building on its existing capabilities. To achieve this, we designed a reward mechanism targeting three key training objectives: (1) Format standardization: employing special tokens to explicitly separate the reasoning process from the final answer, thereby facilitating downstream parsing and practical application; (2) Output space constraint: strictly limiting the scope of mismatch types (e.g., "price mismatch," "functionality mismatch") to a predefined set, effectively preventing the generation of arbitrary or uncontrolled outputs; (3) Progressive reward: providing moderate positive reinforcement when the model correctly identifies relevance but errs at finer levels of granularity, thereby mitigating the sparse reward issue during early training, accelerating convergence, and stabilizing learning dynamics. Building on this foundation, we implemented a three-stage curriculum learning framework to progressively enhance the model’s capabilities by increasing the complexity of training data over time. The sample allocation strategy for each stage is outlined in Table 6. This framework is guided by three core principles: (1) Rapid Bootstrap Principle: Stage 1 prioritizes medium-difficulty samples to enable the model to quickly gain effective feedback while maintaining a degree of challenge, thus avoiding the pitfalls of sparse rewards during initial training; (2) Progressive Challenge Principle: Stages 2 and 3 systematically increase the proportion of challenging samples, enabling the model to transition from solving routine problems to addressing more complex scenarios, thereby broadening its problem-solving capabilities; (3) Knowledge Retention Principle: The latter stages continue to incorporate easier samples as "memory anchors," ensuring that the model retains previously acquired skills while learning new ones. These high-confidence samples also help stabilize gradient variance, promoting training stability.

Language Model Systems (LMS), which include Large Language Models (LLMs) and other variants, are utilized in a wide range of applications such as medical chatbots, autonomous vehicle assistance systems, and programming support tools. Given their deployment in critical and publicly accessible domains, it is crucial that these systems operate with precision and align with expected behavioral norms. Numerous studies have focused on integrating safety mechanisms within these systems to embed desirable safety properties directly into their design. However, the current methodologies for evaluating and enforcing such properties are inadequate, as they fail to provide universally applicable frameworks with formal guarantees of efficacy. Establishing these guarantees is essential for ensuring user safety and fostering trust in these systems. Recently, scholarly efforts have begun to formally certify these systems for specific desirable behaviors, such as fairness, accurate question answering, and safety, by encoding them as mathematical specifications. For instance, researchers have developed specifications addressing fairness in responses, comprehension of textual material, management of high-risk interactions, and appropriate tool selection. Nevertheless, these specific certifications are difficult to adapt for diverse requirements or contexts due to their highly specialized focus on particular properties and their typical applicability limited to conversational settings. Any change in criteria necessitates a complete revision of the certification framework. Consequently, the current approach to certifying these systems remains highly customized for each property, domain, and system type, a paradigm we aim to transform.

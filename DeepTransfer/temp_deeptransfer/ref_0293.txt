The primary objective of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) is to elucidate the rationale behind a model's specific output. However, the motivations for this elucidation can vary. These include evaluating model performance, increasing user trust in the decision-making process, and aiding users in understanding how a decision can be changed in situations where decisions are not permanent. In the latter scenario, the explanation must be tailored to focus on actionable elements within the current context. Explaining under actionability constraints with the goal of changing a model decision is referred to as algorithmic recourse. This process necessitates precise predictions regarding the outcomes subsequent to the implementation of the recommended modifications and is contingent upon the realization of these changes. The type of explanation discussed in this paper is intended to be general, with no pre-defined explainee guiding the process of explanation and without assumptions about the model's performance or deployment stage. This ensures that explanations are faithful to the model, even when it does not perform as intended. Moreover, it facilitates retrospective analysis of alternative actions without necessitating consideration of their feasibility. While this methodology may be adapted for algorithmic recourse with certain modifications, such applications fall outside the scope of this paper. Concept-based explanations use concepts rather than specific data features as their vocabulary, employing broader ideas or characteristics that hold significance for humans. Although multiple definitions of concepts exist within the realm of XAI, this paper refers to the type identified as symbolic concepts by Poeta et al. [2025], which encompass high-level ideas or traits of the data that are comprehensible to individuals.

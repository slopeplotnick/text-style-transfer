Reasoning has emerged as a widely adopted strategy for enhancing the performance of large language models (LLMs) across various tasks. Initially introduced through prompting techniques like Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022), which guide models to generate intermediate reasoning steps before arriving at a final answer, reasoning methods have since progressed toward reinforcement learning (RL)-based approaches that further refine the quality of these reasoning traces. RL has demonstrated particular success in domains such as mathematics and programming, where solutions can be systematically verified, leveraging a reward signal based on correctness (Shao et al., 2024; Lambert et al., 2025). More recently, researchers have extended RL techniques to less verifiable domains, such as narrative generation and comprehension, where correctness is more subjective (Ahuja et al., 2025; Gurung and Lapata, 2025). However, these advancements often come at a cost, as RL-based reasoning increases computational demands during both training and inference due to the generation of longer reasoning traces (Shen et al., 2025a). Consequently, efforts have shifted toward improving reasoning efficiency to reduce these costs while maintaining performance (Sui et al., 2025). One promising direction is latent reasoning, which generates reasoning traces in the form of continuous token embeddings rather than explicit natural language tokens. This approach is motivated by two key insights: first, not all tokens are necessary for deriving correct answers; second, human reasoning frequently occurs implicitly without verbalizing every step. By retaining reasoning in a latent, non-token space, models can preserve more information while avoiding the inefficiencies of explicit verbalization. For instance, Hao et al. (2025) argue that continuous embeddings can represent multiple reasoning steps simultaneously, effectively enabling parallel processing of complex problems. Building on this foundation, we introduce LiteReason, a latent reasoning framework tailored for tasks like narrative comprehension and generation, which often involve lengthy inputs and outputs. LiteReason incorporates a specialized Reasoning Projector module into the model, enabling the generation of both discrete text tokens and continuous latent representations.

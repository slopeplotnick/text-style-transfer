Despite the evolution of optimization approaches, traditional methods for construction coordination exhibit fundamental limitations. The necessity for a comprehensive problem specification at the outset conflicts with the dynamic nature of construction projects, where task requirements, environmental conditions, and resource availability continuously change throughout project execution. Moreover, the computational complexity of these methods often becomes prohibitive for large-scale projects involving numerous robots and intricate task dependencies. In response to these persistent challenges, researchers have begun exploring learning-based methodologies capable of adapting to dynamic changes without necessitating a complete plan. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a transformative approach, facilitating the development of collaborative capabilities in robots through environmental interaction, thereby obviating the need for a complete problem specification. RL empowers robots to acquire coordination proficiency via trial-and-error learning, allowing them to adapt to evolving conditions. However, RL necessitates extensive practice, which poses significant risks in construction settings due to the potential for hazardous errors affecting both personnel and equipment. Additionally, the duration required for training is impractical for projects constrained by stringent timelines. Consequently, construction-specific RL methodologies have been developed. In 2022, Lee et al. introduced a digital twin-driven Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) methodology for adaptive task allocation in robotic construction. This methodology integrated Building Information Modeling (BIM) with DRL to facilitate intelligent coordination decisions that account for spatial constraints, temporal dependencies, and safety requirements. The digital twin framework enabled safer policy learning within simulations prior to real-world implementation. However, RL continues to face significant challenges, as policies trained in simulation often fail to generalize effectively to real-world scenarios, potentially resulting in safety-critical failures. Furthermore, deploying simulation-trained methods to dynamic and unstructured construction environments with varying conditions remains a non-trivial task. These limitations have necessitated the development of more sophisticated RL methodologies.

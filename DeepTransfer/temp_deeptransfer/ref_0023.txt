Exemplar Selection: Certain algorithms, such as OPRO and EvoPrompt, do not incorporate few-shot examples. However, they can significantly enhance the performance of large language models, even with straightforward exemplar selection strategies. promptsolution introduces a supplementary module for post-hoc exemplar selection, utilizing random selection and random search to integrate few-shot examples into a predetermined instruction. Initial Prompt Creation: Many prompt optimizers necessitate a predefined set of prompts to begin with. Our framework incorporates functionalities to automatically generate prompts derived from a task description, a base prompt (as outlined by Zhou et al., 2023), or representative examples extracted from a dataset. **Callbacks:** The optimizer class is equipped with callback mechanisms, which facilitate the systematic monitoring of the optimization process. These callbacks enable periodic evaluation of the optimizer’s state at each iteration and provide the capability to terminate the process when predefined conditions are met. Important implementations include the TokenCountCallback, which monitors token usage and terminates the process upon exceeding predefined limits, and the FileOutputCallback, which logs prompts and their respective scores for subsequent analysis. Setup: To evaluate the efficacy of promptolution relative to alternative prompt optimization frameworks, we conducted prompt optimization experiments on the GSM8K dataset (grade school math word problems; Cobbe et al., 2021) and the SST-5 dataset (sentiment classification; Socher et al., 2013). We employed the gemma-3-27B model, instruction-tuned (Kamath et al., 2025), as the primary downstream language model and, where required, as the meta-language model for optimizers necessitating such a component. Further information regarding the datasets and experimental setup is provided in Appendix A.2. For our comparative analysis, we evaluated the optimizers CAPO, EvoPromptGA, and OPRO from the promptolution library, in addition to the leading optimizers from AdalFlow and DSPy frameworks. To ascertain the efficacy of prompt optimization, we also assessed three manually constructed prompts per dataset and reported their average performance. The experimental configuration for each optimizer was standardized to ensure fairness, with the token budget constrained to a maximum of one million tokens for both input and output combined, thereby maintaining costs below $0.15 for this language model. All frameworks were initialized solely from a task description without the provision of initial prompts, enabling the evaluation of their autonomous performance. Both the unoptimized prompts and the most effective prompts identified by each optimizer were subsequently evaluated on an independent test set.

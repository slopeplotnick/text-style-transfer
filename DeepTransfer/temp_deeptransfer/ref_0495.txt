We propose a scalable approach for constructing multilingual safety guardrails that generalize across a wide spectrum of languages, leveraging only a few high-resource languages. We have developed a lightweight safety guardrail model with merely 0.5 billion parameters, which supports over 100 languages and is trained exclusively on data from just 13 high-resource languages. Our systematic analysis explores inter-cluster and intra-cluster cross-lingual transfer from high-resource to low-resource languages. Early content moderation systems predominantly focused on English; for example, Google's Perspective API (Lees et al. 2022) initially supported only English text. Subsequent works, such as Aegis-Defensive (Ghosh et al. 2025), WalledGuard (Gupta et al. 2024), and WildGuard (Han et al. 2024), have demonstrated promising performance on safety and toxicity benchmarks, effectively identifying harmful prompts or responses and refusal behaviors. However, recent studies (Wang et al. 2024; Nicholas and Bhatia 2023; Deng et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2024) reveal that popular LLMs produce significantly more unsafe or harmful responses for non-English user queries. Furthermore, prompts in low-resource languages have been shown to more easily bypass safety filters. Recent advancements have explicitly targeted the vulnerabilities of English-centric safety measures in multilingual settings. Alternative methods, including MrGuard (Yang et al. 2025) and X-Guard (Upadhayay, Behzadan, and D 2025), rely on low-accuracy translated data or synthetic datasets, which fail to capture the linguistic and cultural subtleties present in real-world low-resource language data. More recent advancements in multilingual safety include LlamaGuard3-8B (2024) and NemoGuard-8B-content-safety (2025), both built on the Llama3.1-8B pretrained model, and DuoGuard (2025), which employs a novel two-player reinforcement learning approach to train a small-scale classifier using Qwen2.5-0.5B as the base model. DuoGuard is among the initial works to illuminate the area of small-scale multilingual safety guardrails. To the best of our knowledge, PolyGuard (Kumar et al. 2025) is the state-of-the-art safety moderation model, released alongside the largest multilingual safety corpus to date, PolyGuardMix. Nonetheless, even PolyGuard's coverage, limited to 17 languages, leaves out the majority of the world's languages, underscoring that most current multilingual guardrails still prioritize a relatively small set of high-resource languages.

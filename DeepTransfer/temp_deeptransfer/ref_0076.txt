The empirical observation that predictions often stabilize well before the final diffusion step has been noted (Pengxiang et al., 2025). Dynamic strategies are employed to modulate the balance between exploration and acceleration throughout the process, utilizing indicators such as historical logits or local determinism to eliminate redundant steps (Wei et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b; Or-El et al., 2025). Moreover, certain research endeavors concentrate on error correction and refinement, enabling models to autonomously revise their outputs by re-masking tokens with low confidence (Zhang et al., 2025a; Su et al., 2025). Expedited approaches also address this challenge by leveraging principles derived from continuous processes, employing staged learning to maintain consistency or align with flow dynamics (Sahoo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Meister et al., 2024). Additionally, acceleration strategies include caching data for rapid retrieval and employing concurrent verification mechanisms to streamline operations (Yuan et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a; Sohoni et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025a). While these techniques demonstrate efficacy, they frequently necessitate supplementary training, novel model architectures, or intricate procedural frameworks, underscoring the demand for a streamlined termination criterion that obviates the need for additional training or specialized configurations. Therefore, this study examines the decision-making process regarding termination criteria and introduces SchED, a schedule-based early stopping mechanism that is both training-free and model-agnostic. Fundamentally, this approach evaluates the confidence level associated with each component of the output and juxtaposes it against a dynamically adjusting threshold that becomes progressively lenient. By decoupling confidence from the number of iterative steps and ensuring a gradual modification of the threshold, SchED facilitates termination upon stabilization of predictions, thereby circumventing abrupt changes or rigid constraints. SchED integrates seamlessly with conventional transfer schedules, including single-suffix and block diffusion configurations, and operates without necessitating modifications to the model training process (Nie et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025).

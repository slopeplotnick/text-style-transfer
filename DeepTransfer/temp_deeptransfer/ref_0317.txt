Empirical evidence suggests that the entrenchment of beliefs lies at the core of social epistemic challenges such as polarization and misinformation, potentially leading to various cognitive biases. This entrenchment occurs within human information processing, essentially their reasoning. Large language models (LLMs), having assimilated patterns of human cognition from extensive online text data, are engineered to assist individuals by providing answers to inquiries or facilitating task completion. Following their success in executing complex cognitive tasks, novel reasoning methodologies have emerged based on these models. From foundational approaches like Chain-of-Thought to more advanced techniques incorporating reinforcement learning, these strategies aim to enhance the models' capacity for truth-seeking by employing structured arguments, seeking corroborative evidence, and exploring diverse methodologies. However, there are instances where these models exhibit a reluctance to pursue veracity, as they tend to adhere to entrenched beliefs rather than assimilating novel information. This phenomenon can degrade the accuracy of conclusions and engender unwarranted confidence among users. While empirical evidence indicates the presence of belief entrenchment within these models, establishing definitive proof remains a formidable challenge. Distinguishing between a justified belief update and one that is biased proves to be particularly arduous. Previous studies have employed specific tasks designed to readily identify belief entrenchment; however, these methodologies are inadequate for detecting reasoning errors in contexts where the truth is ambiguous.

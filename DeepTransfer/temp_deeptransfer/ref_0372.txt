When performing sequences of measurements, such as planning a sequence of drilling, artificial intelligence frameworks, including Monte Carlo tree search, can be utilized to determine the optimal sequential drilling strategy (Mern and Caers, 2023). However, the properties of interest evolve throughout the process, and a clear hierarchy can be established: drilling should first address hypotheses, then focus on volume, and finally assess grade. Once all but one hypothesis has been falsified, the focus should shift to quantifying the volume of the mineral system, followed by evaluating its grade. Alternatively, these factors can be integrated into a combined grade-tonnage framework. Despite the potential of scientific methodologies to enhance the cost-efficiency and effectiveness of mineral exploration, several non-scientific barriers persist within the industry. These include ingrained practices and organizational structures, the broader configuration of the exploration sector, and the lack of comprehensive software solutions. Companies are typically structured such that experts in specialized fields handle tasks aligned with their expertise. This often results in an emphasis on details without a clear understanding of their relevance to the ultimate decision-making process. For artificial intelligence to be effectively implemented, a fundamental reorganization of large corporations is required, shifting the focus from task execution to solving decision-making challenges. This transformation is not merely structural but also educational. While geologists do not need to become data scientists, fostering mutual understanding between disciplines is essential—a topic that will be revisited in the education section. Furthermore, socialization within the industry often revolves around a single model, supported by software that enables biased and deterministic interpretations. Over time, these models are mistakenly perceived as absolute truths. As personnel transition out of organizations, the rationale behind the interpretations is frequently lost, and the specific configurations produced are misinterpreted as data rather than models. Within organizations, uncertainty is often viewed as a deficiency, equated with a lack of individual knowledge. It is commonly assumed that with greater effort and time, uncertainty could be eliminated.

In domain-specific SFT tasks, we typically adhere to a standardized template for task definitions and requirements, subsequently populating it with the query and item details from each dataset to generate the prompt. To effectively activate the core capabilities of the base model, it is imperative to incorporate key information such as task definitions, decision-making rules, and output formats into the prompt. Moreover, to guide the model in effectively following our proposed two-stage discrimination framework, we integrate structured instructional information into the prompt: initially requiring the model to discern the intent of the query and its attribute requirements, followed by the extraction of pertinent attributes from the item information based on these requirements, and subsequently making judgments in accordance with the established rules. For reasoning tasks, providing more detailed and specific instructions within the prompt generally facilitates the model's ability to derive accurate responses. However, in the context of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), this relationship does not consistently hold, as the model predominantly develops its capabilities through learning from response data rather than relying solely on the instructions provided. To identify the optimal prompt configuration, we investigated three distinct versions of prompt designs. The comprehensive version encompasses all task details, rules, and instructions, extending beyond 7,000 tokens. In contrast, the intermediate version condenses this by retaining only the most critical instructions, resulting in a length of approximately 800 tokens. The briefest version is the most succinct, providing merely a fundamental role description, such as: "You are an e-commerce relevance assessment expert. Please score the query and item." This phase signifies the model's initiation phase, which is intended to augment its fundamental decision-making capabilities. To achieve this, it is imperative to construct a training dataset characterized by comprehensiveness and minimal noise. In terms of comprehensiveness, a robust sampling strategy is employed to adequately represent diverse e-commerce data distributions, thereby mitigating the risks of oversampling or undersampling any particular category.

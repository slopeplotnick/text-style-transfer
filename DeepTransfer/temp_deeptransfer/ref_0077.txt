Text vs. numeric signals. Numerical data alone demonstrates utility in analytical processes; however, its efficacy is notably inferior to that of text-based inputs. GRU ablations in Table 2 show that numeric-only inputs are viable but clearly weaker than text-only, while combining both views recovers most of the performance of the best text-based variant. This supports a practical hierarchy: text is primary, with numeric features providing complementary signals. Signals hierarchy. Across diverse datasets and tasks, a discernible hierarchy of feature importance emerges. For fake-news detection on EVONS (Table 1) and on tweet series from FakeNewsNet (Table 1), text-based models are very strong: a lightweight nonlinear layer applied to transformer-based text embeddings achieves near-optimal performance on certain datasets and surpasses traditional baseline methods on others. Numeric signals alone are usable but weaker; on FakeNewsNet virality, for example, the numerically GRU trails the text-only configuration by a wide margin (Table 2). Overall, text should be regarded as the primary modality, with numeric features serving a complementary role. However, the trade-off should be stated explicitly. In scenarios where errors hold significant consequences, it is advisable to adhere to established methodologies that effectively balance comprehensiveness and precision. Architecture vs. input view. Analyzing a particular dataset reveals that various models, including GRU, LSTM, RNN, CNN, and Transformer, demonstrate commendable performance, with the Transformer exhibiting a marginally superior efficacy. The proximity of their scores suggests that the choice of input view (text vs. numeric, fusion) and label definition matters more than incremental architecture tweaks—particularly under constrained conditions. Practical recommendations. (1) Conceptualize the prediction of virality as a series of tasks constrained by specific parameters and temporal boundaries, ensuring clarity in the presentation of results. (2) Initiate with text-only methodologies for the detection of fake news, subsequently incorporating numerical data where available. (3) For rapid evaluations, prioritize comprehensive detection while explicitly acknowledging potential omissions. (4) Favor simple, well-documented architectures; invest effort in data curation, leakage controls, and clear protocol reporting.

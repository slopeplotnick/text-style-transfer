The anonymity of online environments often lowers social restraint and amplifies aggression and anger, while group dynamics can further polarize discussions. Although computational methods have advanced in detecting and flagging harmful online behavior, they remain largely reactive, focusing on content removal or punitive measures rather than addressing root causes. Consequently, online platforms may temporarily suppress harmful discourse without engaging with the underlying social dynamics, allowing hostility to reemerge. Our study explores an alternative approach by emphasizing mediation over moderation. Instead of merely identifying harmful content, we investigate whether language models can monitor contentious interactions and facilitate their de-escalation. We propose that these models can serve as mediators by interpreting multi-turn dialogues, assessing the fairness and relevance of arguments, and generating responses that acknowledge concerns, mitigate hostility, and redirect discussions toward more constructive outcomes. Achieving this requires analyzing the underlying dynamics, including emotional responses, conflict escalation, and typical communication patterns. To this end, we divide the task into two components: identifying elements that contribute to perceived injustice or emotional distress and implementing strategies to de-escalate conflicts and foster constructive dialogue. To evaluate the efficacy of these models, we curated an extensive dataset of authentic online disputes from Reddit and developed a comprehensive evaluation framework. This framework assesses three key dimensions: the model's adherence to established principles of effective conversational practices, the behavioral responses of participants following the model's intervention, and a comparative analysis of the model's performance relative to human-generated interventions. Empirical evaluations indicate that paid models outperform free alternatives in both identifying and mitigating conflicts, effectively maintaining topical coherence. Furthermore, these models contribute to reducing harmful language and heightened emotional responses in simulated disputes. While they exhibit strengths in neutrality and clarity, their outputs are less accessible and engaging compared to human communication.

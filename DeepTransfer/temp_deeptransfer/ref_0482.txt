Ensuring consistent theme color roles across a deck, standardizing layouts and formats for related slides, and translating slide content while preserving charts and structural integrity are essential aspects of effective presentation editing. To our knowledge, PPTArena is the first benchmark to conceptualize PowerPoint editing as a structured, causal program grounded in deck semantics. Specifically, it provides element-level ground truths, style targets, and error rubrics to clarify correctness, while employing dual instruction-following and visual evaluation mechanisms that enable diverse editing configurations without compromising task coherence. Complementing this benchmark, we introduce PPTPilot, a structure-aware agent designed for precise and nuanced PowerPoint editing. PPTPilot systematically decomposes each natural-language instruction into a sequence of semantic operations, selects between high-level APIs (e.g., python-pptx) and direct XML modifications, and rigorously validates outcomes against task-specific objectives. Two key design principles underpin its functionality: structure-aware planning, where the agent analyzes slide masters, placeholders, shape hierarchies, textual content, and visual data prior to editing, and deterministic execution, where XML-level modifications and strict schemas ensure precise control over fonts, theme color allocations, positions, and master-level adjustments, while programmatic tools handle repetitive global operations such as translation and bulk normalization. Moreover, an iterative plan-edit-verify loop, augmented by XML validation and visual inspections, enhances its robustness in managing visually complex and extended edits. We evaluate a diverse range of baselines within the PPTArena framework, including advanced proprietary PowerPoint agents (e.g., ChatGPT Agent [42] and MiniMax Agent [35]), extended visual-language models, and simplified versions of PPTPilot. Despite generous prompting and tool accessibility, existing systems often struggle to balance instruction fidelity with visual and layout quality on compound, multi-step edits. Furthermore, they frequently fail to address cross-slide dependencies and master-level style adjustments highlighted by our benchmark. In contrast, PPTPilot delivers significantly higher performance, surpassing strong proprietary agents and state-of-the-art models by over 10 percentage points on complex, layout-sensitive, and cross-slide edits, while maintaining competitive results on simpler tasks. Nonetheless, both PPTPilot and other evaluated systems exhibit notable limitations on highly challenging, visually dependent tasks, underscoring the inherent complexity of PPTArena and the substantial opportunities for future advancements in this domain.

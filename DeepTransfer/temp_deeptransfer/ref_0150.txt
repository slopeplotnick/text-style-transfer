Overall, the most advanced models, such as GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o, achieve minimal error rates, performing on par with—or surpassing—human capabilities (left panel, Fig. 8). With the exception of Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, there is a general pattern in the left panel of Figure 8 wherein higher model accuracy is associated with stronger Bayesian-like behavior. However, this relationship between accuracy and Bayesian behavior is not universally consistent. For instance, a model that achieves perfect predictive accuracy may appear simplistic and devoid of probabilistic characteristics. When the context is modified by introducing a sequence of prior observations, the behavior of large language models (LLMs) exhibits significant variability. As illustrated in the upper-right panel of Figure 6, their sequential performance declines markedly, particularly when exposed to a biased sequence of observations. This phenomenon is further evidenced in the left panel of Figure 6, where error rates increase, with triangular markers consistently positioned above circular markers. This observation underscores the tendency of LLMs to heavily depend on the information provided to them. Moreover, as illustrated in the central panel of Figure 8, it becomes evident that high performance in terms of normalized root mean square error (NRMSE) does not necessarily correlate with efficient cue integration. For example, GPT-5 Mini, despite demonstrating strong NRMSE performance, exhibits notable deficiencies in its ability to combine cues effectively. The maze distance estimation task clearly illustrates that GPT-5 Mini exhibits superior performance in the text modality compared to the image modality, indicating the necessity for a substantial downweighting of image input to achieve a Bayes-optimal combination, which it fails to adequately accomplish (refer to Appendix A.12 for further details). Conversely, in the line length ratio task (depicted in the bottom right of Figure 6), numerous LLMs demonstrate the capability to effectively downweight image input in the presence of noise, thereby exhibiting adaptability in accordance with Bayesian principles.

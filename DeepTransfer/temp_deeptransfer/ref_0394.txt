Despite its recognized importance, the field still lacks a comprehensive, reproducible, and scalable methodology for benchmarking and analyzing the power and energy consumption of LLM inference across varying model scales, hardware architectures, software frameworks, and deployment configurations (ranging from single-node setups to multi-node distributed systems). While some initiatives aim to address this gap, they fall short of providing a holistic solution. For example, the MLPerf Inference benchmark (Reddi et al. 2020) establishes a standardized framework for performance evaluation across diverse machine learning tasks; however, it neither systematically captures end-to-end power consumption nor normalizes energy usage to LLM-specific service units, such as Joules per generated token or per prompt. Similarly, the MLPerf Power benchmark (Tschand et al. 2025) expands the scope of measurements but remains limited in several ways: (i) it primarily focuses on single-node configurations, (ii) it evaluates models of relatively modest size compared to cutting-edge LLMs like the Llama 3 405B-class, which remain largely uncharacterized, and (iii) it relies on external, high-precision metering equipment that is both expensive and challenging to deploy across diverse institutional testbeds. Furthermore, contemporary benchmark suites rarely explore the configuration parameters that practitioners routinely adjust in real-world applications, such as batch sizing strategies, tensor/pipeline parallelism, context length, and quantization levels. These parameters significantly influence both instantaneous power consumption and average energy expenditure per token. To bridge this gap, we introduce TokenPowerBench, a lightweight and extensible benchmark specifically designed to quantify the power consumption and energy cost of LLM inference. TokenPowerBench incorporates a modular instrumentation framework that integrates vendor telemetry APIs, including GPU, CPU, and memory power sensors, alongside node-level energy sampling, with optional support for rack- or facility-level measurements where applicable. A unified metrics pipeline aligns each power sample with the two primary inference phases—prefill and decode—enabling precise identification of energy expenditure across these stages. Through extensive experimental evaluations, we systematically analyze the interplay between energy consumption and inference-critical parameters, such as batch size, maximum context length, parallelization strategies, and quantization formats.

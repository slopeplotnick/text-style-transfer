In conclusion, we introduce a new benchmark, HEALTHCONTRADICT, which utilizes interpretable quantitative metrics to evaluate the ability of language models to reason over extensive and conflicting biomedical contexts. Compared to state-of-the-art medical question-answering benchmarks, HEALTHCONTRADICT more effectively captures the differences in model performance. In our evaluation, language models adapted to the biomedical domain demonstrate an enhanced ability to (i) leverage correct contextual information, (ii) resist incorrect contextual information, and (iii) decipher between conflicting contextual information. To the best of our knowledge, the TREC Health Misinformation tracks remain the only publicly available resource that provides (1) expert-curated health questions with ground-truth answers and supporting scientific evidence, and (2) document pools containing both supporting and refuting evidence, as assessed by experts. These components are essential for constructing health questions with definitive answers, using pairs of contradictory documents to evaluate biomedical knowledge conflicts in language models. In HEALTHCONTRADICT, we emphasize the inclusion of relevant and credible documents to assess the efficacy of a document in supporting the answer to a query. We standardized the labels by mapping the 2019 annotations "effective"/"ineffective" and the 2021 annotations "supportive"/"dissuades" to the binary "yes"/"no" format, aligning them with the 2022 labeling scheme. Furthermore, we consider two documents for the same question as a contradictory pair if they present opposing stances—one labeled "yes" and the other "no." Moreover, a document is deemed correct if its stance aligns with scientific evidence, and incorrect if it contradicts scientific evidence. The original collections include 130 expert-annotated questions (50 in 2019, 35 in 2021, and 45 in 2022). Questions with yes/no stance annotations, as well as those accompanied by documents presenting both supporting and opposing arguments, are included. Applying this criterion reduces the set to 110 questions. We pair supporting and opposing documents into contradiction pairs, ensuring each document is used only once, as some documents may be associated with multiple questions. We exclude questions that cannot form at least one contradiction pair due to insufficient unique documents. This results in a final dataset comprising 81 questions and 920 pairs of contradictory documents.

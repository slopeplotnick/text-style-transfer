In flow-matching or diffusion-based frameworks, the stochasticity of the sampled initial noise vectors directly influences the resulting actions. This paper addresses the distribution shift problem from an anti-exploration perspective and introduces Test-time Anti-exploration via pseudo-Counts (TACO). The anti-exploration principle, rooted in offline reinforcement learning, seeks to prevent the policy from visiting states or actions outside the dataset's support. Similarly, during VLA inference, the goal is to constrain the generated actions to remain within the support of the successful modes observed in the SFT dataset, thereby avoiding the exploration of irrelevant or redundant action modes inherited from pretraining or suboptimal fine-tuning data. TACO achieves this by employing test-time scaling (TTS) to adjust the action sampling process rather than modifying the policy itself, as the intricate sampling dynamics of flow-matching or diffusion-based VLA models are not easily compatible with conventional reinforcement learning updates. Crucially, TTS modifies the sampling mechanism without altering the VLA's parameters, eliminating the need for gradient computation. To estimate pseudo-counts for each observation-instruction-action chunk in the SFT dataset, we leverage a classical Coin-Flipping Network (CFN) augmented with an internal representation mechanism. Intuitively, an action chunk with a higher pseudo-count aligns more closely with the frequently observed successful behaviors in the SFT data and is therefore prioritized. During inference, TACO utilizes the CFN-derived pseudo-counts to identify the most reliable action chunk from multiple candidates, ultimately selecting the one with the highest pseudo-count. To further enhance efficiency and reduce the computational overhead of processing multiple test-time samples, we introduce a shared observation key-value cache, which significantly lowers latency by reusing visual-language representations across samples.

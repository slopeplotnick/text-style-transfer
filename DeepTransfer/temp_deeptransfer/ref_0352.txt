In this context, we begin by examining the limitations of this recent approach [16]. As shown in Figure 1(a), the model initially attends to both audio and visual inputs; however, as reasoning progresses, its focus shifts predominantly to the text it has generated. This shift causes the model to anchor its subsequent reasoning on the emotional cues embedded in the initial generated text. Moreover, the reasoning process typically begins with generating a video description, as the training data are structured sequentially, with visual descriptions preceding auditory descriptions and subsequent reasoning. However, this "visual-first" reasoning approach may fail to capture the true emotional drivers, particularly in cases where emotion is conveyed primarily through vocal tone or semantic content. When the initial visual-based reasoning is either irrelevant or inaccurate, the subsequent reasoning becomes increasingly misaligned, as illustrated in Figure 1(b). Consequently, the initial phase of reasoning assumes an undue level of significance. Similar challenges arise in other domains, where early cues divert reasoning from the foundational evidence [17]. To address this issue, we propose Modality-Importance-Guided Reasoning (MIGR), a method designed to initiate reasoning from the modality most strongly associated with the target emotion and ensure emotion-coherent reasoning throughout the process. To achieve this, we introduce Modality Importance (MI) estimation, which identifies the modality most critical for accurate emotion recognition by comparing the model's predictive accuracy across scenarios with audio-only, visual-only, and combined audio-visual inputs. For instance, if the model accurately predicts the emotion using audio-only input but fails when visual information is included, this indicates that visual cues may be irrelevant or misleading, and the auditory modality should be prioritized. Building on MI and recent advancements in reasoning-based model training paradigms [15, 16], we propose a two-stage learning framework comprising (i) modality-aligned Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and (ii) modality-aligned reward optimization. In the SFT stage, MI is leveraged to reorganize the reasoning text into modality-specific segments, followed by systematic reordering of these segments to align with the dominant modality.

Language models possess the capability to refine and propose modifications to ambiguously phrased questions, thereby significantly influencing respondents' answers by mitigating errors and misunderstandings. Moreover, these models assist in appropriately categorizing questions, which is essential for ensuring clarity and coherence in research. By leveraging their semantic understanding of questions, language models enable researchers to circumvent potential inaccuracies and examine relationships between distinct categories. However, when categories exhibit excessive similarity, substantial modifications may be necessary to enhance differentiation. Additionally, language models can ensure consistency in the readability level across all textual content, thereby facilitating comprehension. For further insights into the application of language models in assessment development, one may refer to a recent publication by Beghetto et al., which explores the potential of employing language models to generate synthetic data. This approach could significantly reduce the time and financial resources typically allocated to testing by enabling initial trials on simulated cohorts. Consequently, this development has the potential to lower costs associated with test construction, allowing a broader range of researchers to design and implement assessments while minimizing redundancy. In this study, we evaluated the capacity of state-of-the-art language models to generate data aligned with the structural frameworks commonly employed in test construction. The following sections detail the methodology used in this investigation and provide a rationale for its anticipated efficacy. Specifically, we adhered to the methodological framework outlined in Classical Test Theory, incorporating guidance from Boateng et al. to ensure a robust and systematic approach.

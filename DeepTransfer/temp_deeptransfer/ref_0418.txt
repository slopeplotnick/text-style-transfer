Moreover, Galhotra et al. [2021] engage in an analytical examination of probability estimation in scenarios where a fully specified system is unavailable. They investigate the application of particular models to derive intervals for counterfactual queries, thereby facilitating a concentrated analysis of specific model components in the absence of complete information. This approach potentially yields narrower intervals than those obtained through conventional computational methods. Probabilities of sufficiency and necessity are also considered for evaluating XAI methods. Mothilal et al. [2021] propose that the optimal approach to model explanation involves identifying the minimal set of salient features that are both necessary and sufficient to achieve a particular outcome. Assuming the input features are the sole elements requiring explanation and are independent of one another, they discuss various types of XAI explanations in comparison. Probability of necessity is related to contrastive explanations by arguing the features changed are a necessary cause for the original model output. Conversely, sufficiency pertains to feature attribution explanations, where highly contributing features across contexts correspond to sufficient features, likely to cause the output regardless of other feature values. Pawar et al. [2024] demonstrate significant variability among different methodologies in practical applications. Assuming the independence of data features, they discuss necessity and sufficiency as evaluative criteria for existing XAI methods and extend this discussion to encompass various approaches to neighbourhood generation as a basis for explanation. Beckers [2022] examines scenarios wherein the model predicts the outcomes of actions, operating under the assumption that the model consistently predicts the correct action outcome. Both sufficient explanations and counterfactual explanations are defined for this scenario, under the assumption of perfect model predictions. Furthermore, explanations are categorized by assessing the degree of causal influence, thereby facilitating the identification of actual causes underlying the explanandum.

In this section, our objective is to elucidate the fundamental principles that underpin effective e-commerce search mechanisms and, on this basis, to delineate the essential modeling capabilities necessary for their enhancement. These capabilities will serve as the foundation for the methods introduced in subsequent sections. From a functional perspective, e-commerce search fundamentally involves aligning user queries with pertinent products. Relevance modeling refines this alignment by introducing relevance as a quantitative measure that captures the degree of match between a given query and item under both objective attributes and constraints, as well as subjective user preferences. Orthogonally to the four-level relevance scale, we decompose user search intentions into eighteen distinct dimensions, encompassing aspects such as type, brand, and style, among others. For each search query and corresponding product alignment, we evaluate the degree to which the product satisfies the query's criteria, employing these evaluations to ascertain the relevance of the match. This framework provides a structured lens through which relevance decisions can be interpreted and analyzed. To systematically quantify relevance within the e-commerce search context, we decompose the assessment process into three synergistic components: the interpretation of product characteristics, the analysis of search intent, and the subsequent relevance judgment. In relevance judgment, product evaluation involves two critical aspects: its attributes and its presentation. From the perspective of attribute properties, we distinguish between intrinsic attributes and derived attributes. Inherent attributes, such as category, brand, model, and price, are typically straightforward to identify and align with user queries due to their clear and objective nature. For such attributes, relevance tends to be close to a binary decision, and the decision rules are relatively clear. In contrast, derived attributes, which are inferred from inherent ones, pertain to the functional or experiential aspects of a product. These attributes often require a deeper level of interpretation and reasoning, as their description and significance can vary significantly across different industries, thereby introducing additional complexity to their accurate identification and classification.

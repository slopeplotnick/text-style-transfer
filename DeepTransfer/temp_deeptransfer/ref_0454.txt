The sequence of reasoning and actions taken by the agents differ significantly: SWE-agent Dev begins by localizing the bug to the client.py file, followed by creating a reproduction test and editing multiple sections of the file. After two repetitive failed edits, it revisits and modifies a different segment of the client.py file, executes the reproduction test to validate the patch, and concludes with patch submission. In contrast, SWE-agent DSK-V3 starts similarly by identifying the bug in the client.py file but diverges by executing a single edit and submitting the patch without conducting any validation. Despite both agents achieving final success, they encounter several strategic pitfalls: SWE-agent Dev engages in a line-by-line editing approach and repeats a failed edit twice before executing a final modification, whereas SWE-agent DSK-V3 generates the patch in a single attempt. Neither agent conducts regression tests for patch validation; SWE-agent Dev merely verifies the functionality of reproduction tests, while SWE-agent DSK-V3 considers the correctness of the patch through edge cases. This example illustrates that a process-oriented approach to analysis can yield deeper insights into the mechanisms by which agents succeed or fail in problem-solving tasks. Recent efforts have focused on examining agentic workflows by analyzing their trajectories to identify failure modes through manual, expert-driven methodologies. However, such manual analyses are inherently prone to human bias and incompleteness, and they lack scalability for evaluating novel agentic systems. Moreover, a purely sequential examination of trajectories fails to capture the nuanced behaviors of agents, such as task execution strategies or the efficiency of their planning processes.

As illustrated in Fig 12, the experimental group employing the curriculum learning strategy exhibits accelerated convergence and a more stable optimization trajectory concerning both reward values and model performance enhancement. During reinforcement learning (RL) training, as the policy approaches convergence, the entropy of the model's output distribution diminishes, resulting in deterministic generation outcomes. This phenomenon constrains the model's exploratory capacity and impedes the discovery of superior solutions, thereby limiting potential improvements. To address this issue, various strategies were implemented to decelerate the rate at which the model's behavior becomes predictable, thereby preserving its capacity for exploration. As illustrated in Fig. 13, these approaches were systematically compared, with the Clip-higher method demonstrating superior efficacy. This method effectively moderates the predictability of the model while maintaining a balanced trade-off between exploration and exploitation. Furthermore, it ensures stability during the training process, yielding discernible improvements in both performance and optimization outcomes. In contrast, the On-policy strategy maintains a consistent level of stochasticity within the model, effectively preventing a reduction in randomness. Although this facilitates enhanced exploration capabilities, it simultaneously impedes the model's ability to converge on optimal solutions, thereby resulting in diminished performance improvements. Conversely, the Off-policy + Entropy Loss approach encounters significant challenges related to training instability. Initially, this approach decelerates the rate at which the model achieves predictability. However, as the model's behavior becomes less stochastic, the imposed penalty for randomness intensifies, ultimately dominating the optimization process and resulting in erratic model behavior that undermines training stability. Moreover, this method necessitates extensive hyperparameter tuning to identify optimal configurations, thereby increasing its computational cost. In summary, the Clip-higher method emerges as the most effective strategy in this context, as it optimally balances exploration and exploitation by regulating the rate at which the model attains predictability, thus enhancing training efficiency.

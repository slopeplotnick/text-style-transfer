We find that high task accuracy does not always imply optimal cue-combination (middle panel of Figure 8). For instance, GPT-5 Mini achieves a very low NRMSE but combines modalities less effectively than other models. This limitation is particularly evident when unimodal performance is imbalanced: optimal behaviour would require the model to significantly down-weight the weaker modality, a capability some LLMs lack. This has important practical implications, as benchmarks focused solely on accuracy risk favouring models that are less robust to noise. In contrast, Llama-4 Maverick outperforms Bayesian reliability-weighted linear fusion, suggesting the use of more advanced non-linear integration strategies. This aligns with the observation that a non-linear random forest better captures its cue-combination dynamics compared to linear methods. Comparing unimodal and multimodal performance reveals that models like Llama-4 Maverick, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o leverage the additional modality to achieve lower error rates in most multimodal tasks involving text and image inputs. However, this is not a universal trend, as variability in performance gains highlights the room for further improvement in multimodal LLMs. For a detailed breakdown of specific models, see Appendix A.11. To assess behavioural features beyond static accuracy metrics, we developed the Bayesian Consistency Score (BCS), which captures principled behavioural shifts. This metric allows for a more holistic evaluation of model behaviour, even when accuracy saturates. By examining behaviour under controlled ablations, we can compare models with varying baseline performance and gain deeper insights into their implicit computational strategies. While more complex heuristics, such as salience-based weighting or rule-switching strategies, may better fit static response patterns, it is more insightful to evaluate whether models exhibit Bayes-consistent behaviours under a Bayesian observer model when conditions change.

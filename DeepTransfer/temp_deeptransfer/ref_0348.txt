To assess how well subject identity is maintained in the edited video, we compute the mean cosine similarity between face embeddings extracted by a pre-trained ArcFace model for each frame and the edited first frame. This measure reflects the fidelity with which the appearance and facial structure of the individual are preserved during temporal propagation, with higher values indicating stronger identity consistency. Moreover, a user study confirmed that our method is perceptually preferred for its visual fidelity and temporal alignment. As shown in Table 1, while text-based methods like LucyEdit perform well on certain synchronization metrics (e.g., Speech Corr. 0.80 vs. our 0.72), they fail to effectively implement the visual edit, as evidenced by their low Directional CLIP (text-dual) score of 0.11 compared to our 0.21. In contrast, our approach achieves the best overall balance, excelling in synchronization while faithfully applying the visual edits. VACE, on the other hand, leverages additional conditioning factors such as pose, canny, and depth to achieve higher CLIP-based alignment but demonstrates weaker temporal consistency and identity preservation. Sync-LoRA, by explicitly training for synchronized editing, maintains strong temporal alignment while producing precise edits that preserve subject identity. This trade-off underscores the strength of our in-context formulation, which adeptly balances motion coherence and edit fidelity rather than excelling in only one dimension.

To this end, we evaluate our framework on four simulation benchmarks comprising 64 tasks and 5 real-world tasks, focusing on three types of VLA policies. Specifically, we assess our approach using the Simpler (Li et al., 2024b), Libero (Liu et al., 2023a), Robotwin1.0 (Mu et al., 2025), and Robotwin2.0 (Chen et al., 2025) benchmarks. Simpler is a real-to-sim benchmark built upon the SAPIEN simulator and the ManiSkill2 benchmark. It provides simulated task environments for both the WindowX and Google Robot platforms; however, in this study, we primarily focus on the tasks designed for WindowX. Libero serves as a benchmark for lifelong learning in decision-making (LLDM) and consists of four distinct task suites. We concentrate on the most challenging suite, as prior research has already demonstrated near-perfect performance on the others. Robotwin1.0 and Robotwin2.0 are benchmarks designed for dual-arm gripper manipulation, offering a wide variety of assets and task types while enabling automated data collection through scripted policies. For real-world experiments, we utilize a REALMAN75 dual-arm robotic system within an office-like environment. This setup involves five tasks featuring commonly encountered objects such as books, pens, a cabinet, a charger, a laptop, and a paper ball. The tasks are defined as follows: *Receive Book*, where the robot receives a book handed over by a human and places it back on the bookshelf; *Storage Charger*, where the robot retrieves a charger, places it inside the cabinet, and closes the cabinet door; *Paper and Pen*, where it picks up a paper ball and a pen and places them into the trash bin and pen holder, respectively; *Laptop*, where it closes the laptop lid, unplugs the charger from the socket, and stores it in the cabinet; and *Pick Books*, where the robot’s dual arms simultaneously pick up and lift two books.

Experiment 2 revealed that the implementation of a single system prompt consistently enhanced performance across all tested languages, indicating systematic and predictable changes in model behavior. To better understand these changes, we examined the intermediate reasoning processes and assessed how modifications in specific reasoning behaviors correlate with multilingual performance. A key question is whether prompt optimization influences the model's language preference. We began by analyzing the languages used within the reasoning steps executed by the model. As shown in Figure 7, even when identical random prompts are employed, the model exhibits different language preferences depending on the language of the input question. These variations correspond to the availability of training data for each respective language. For instance, due to the substantial volume of Chinese data within model families such as Qwen, the model responds to Chinese inquiries in Chinese almost as frequently as it does in English. After prompt optimization, these language preferences become more pronounced: the model predominantly generates responses in the language of the input query, rather than defaulting to English as observed with the initial system prompt. However, for languages with limited training data, such as Hindi, the proportion of responses in English remains largely unchanged post-optimization. Furthermore, it was observed that languages other than the task language and English do not significantly contribute to the model's performance, as their influence diminishes considerably after prompt optimization. Details regarding language identification are provided in Appendix A.9, with additional data presented in Tables 6 and 7. Figure 8 illustrates the distribution of reasoning behavior types across various languages. While the overarching patterns remain largely consistent, certain languages exhibit distinct preferences in reasoning behavior. For instance, English demonstrates a propensity for Reframing, Chinese for Logical Reasoning, and Spanish for calculation. Conversely, languages with limited training data, such as Hindi, exhibit a higher proportion within the Other category, indicating a restricted exposure to structured reasoning examples during pretraining. Although these patterns remain consistent, they appear to be influenced more by inherent language-specific characteristics in model pretraining rather than prompt design.

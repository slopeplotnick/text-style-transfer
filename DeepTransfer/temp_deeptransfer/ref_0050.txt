Our evaluation methodology marks a significant advance over existing benchmarks, as demonstrated by comparisons with judges in related benchmarks. Prior work, such as PPTC-R [23], primarily relies on API-level "diffs." While useful, this approach is brittle and cannot detect critical semantic errors, such as an agent copying the correct text but to the wrong slide. In contrast, our IF Judge, by operating on structured summaries, is explicitly designed to capture such logical failures. Furthermore, while other benchmarks like T2US also use VLMs as judges, their reliance on prompting without a strong rubric leads to noisy and unreliable ratings. To address these challenges, PPTArena provides a rigorous and reproducible foundation for scoring. Consequently, our dual-judge, rubric-grounded architecture is essential for measuring the complex, multi-step reasoning required for PowerPoint editing and for analyzing the subtle failure modes that simpler benchmarks would miss. We introduce PPTPilot, an agent for presentation editing, whose simple architecture yields surprisingly effective results, even outperforming proprietary products like OpenAI's ChatGPT Agent, as detailed in Section 5.1. Our design is built on two key insights. First, the primary challenge in this domain is not solely intent recognition but ensuring reliability and precision. PowerPoint files are built on the brittle Office Open XML (OOXML) format, which is highly intolerant to malformed or "hallucinated" VLM outputs, and therefore requires specialized formats and contexts suitable for PPT editing. Second, we found that no single editing modality, such as purely relying on the XML format, is sufficient. A robust agent must be capable of intelligently selecting the optimal tool and editing interface for a given task. PPTPilot's core design is a dual-path architecture, emphasizing the ability to handle editing queries via either programmatic tools or direct XML editing.

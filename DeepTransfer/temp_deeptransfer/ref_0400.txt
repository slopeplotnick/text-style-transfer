Forecasting stock prices remains a formidable challenge due to the inherent unpredictability of financial markets and their rapid responsiveness to real-time events. However, the advent of large language models (LLMs) has introduced new methodologies for leveraging textual data in stock price prediction. While LLMs demonstrate advanced natural language understanding capabilities, their effective application to complex, real-world scenarios often depends on the integration of specialized memory architectures to ensure consistent and stable utilization of historical information. Although extensive research has been conducted on developing such memory systems, they face significant challenges when processing the vast and noisy corpus of online news data. This data often lacks explicit explanations for price fluctuations and is inundated with irrelevant information. Consequently, the primary challenge lies in autonomously identifying and extracting the critical information and underlying logic from this unstructured text that genuinely influences stock price movements. To address this issue, this paper proposes an event-reflection dual-layer memory framework. During the knowledge construction phase, the framework performs in-depth mining of structured events along two dimensions: horizontal event consolidation, which integrates key daily event content to provide a comprehensive overview, and longitudinal event tracing, which monitors the evolution of these events over time. This dual approach facilitates the construction of a temporally-aware structured event knowledge base that captures shifts in market expectations. Moreover, the framework analyzes the intricate relationship between event patterns and stock price fluctuations, refining this analysis into a reflection knowledge base to achieve systematic and autonomous knowledge organization. In the knowledge application phase, the framework operates through two key stages: retrieval and reasoning. First, recent event sequences are used as semantic queries to retrieve analogous historical instances from the memory network, providing precise references for current decision-making. Then, during the reasoning phase, the framework synthesizes recent event sequences, incremental information, and retrieved historical experiences to generate reliable predictions of stock price movements. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that the proposed system outperforms existing methodologies in predictive accuracy while offering enhanced interpretability. Specifically, it effectively traces the causal relationships between events and stock price variations, elucidating the underlying mechanisms driving these changes.

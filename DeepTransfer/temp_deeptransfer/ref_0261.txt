To address these challenges, it is imperative to shift the focus from static outputs to dynamic, real-time processes. This approach involves employing a structured set of foundational queries that enable language models to generate responses instantaneously. Consequently, this methodology ensures that fact-checking frameworks are provided with the requisite textual data, hallucination detection mechanisms are equipped to analyze the internal operations of the model during the generation process, and evaluation metrics remain adaptable and effective in accommodating the evolving nature of language models. The foundational principle underlying UniFact is to address the limitations posed by outdated benchmarks through the implementation of three core strategies designed to ensure robust, equitable, and future-proof evaluations. Foremost among these strategies is the deliberate avoidance of reliance on antiquated output datasets. Instead, it employs a series of factual inquiries to prompt models to generate responses in real-time, thereby maintaining its applicability for both contemporary and future models. Moreover, it facilitates equitable comparisons by accommodating both hallucination detection and fact verification methodologies. Specifically, it grants hallucination detection methods access to the model's internal processes during the generation of responses, while providing fact verification methods with the generated text alongside supplementary information for cross-referencing. Both approaches are evaluated on an identical task: determining the factual accuracy of the model's outputs. Furthermore, the framework employs an automated labeling mechanism in lieu of the inherently time-consuming and subjective process of human evaluation. This automated system assesses the accuracy of the model's responses by cross-referencing them with verified answers and other credible sources, thereby enabling the efficient, equitable, and consistent evaluation of a wide range of models.

Li et al. (2025) introduces feedback-guided test-time training, where external verifiers provide weak supervision to refine predictions during inference. Similarly, Zuo et al. (2025) presents Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL), which generates multiple rollouts, aggregates predictions through majority voting, and uses the consensus as a reward signal to update the policy. While effective, these methods suffer from inefficiency due to the need for many redundant rollouts to stabilize consensus estimates. More recently, Wang et al. (2025) demonstrates that reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) can achieve significant learning gains with a minimal number of training examples, highlighting the potential of efficient RL in test-time scenarios. Building on this, our research addresses rollout inefficiency by introducing a statistically grounded early-stopping mechanism for adaptive resource allocation. In the realm of policy optimization, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017) is a standard algorithm for stabilizing policy updates under KL constraints and has been widely applied in reinforcement learning from human feedback. Extending this paradigm, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) introduces group-normalized advantages, enabling more stable optimization under limited feedback. OptPO leverages the flexibility of these surrogate objectives, incorporating a plug-and-play rollout allocation mechanism that enhances efficiency without altering the core optimization framework. In summary, previous research has established test-time training as a powerful framework for improving model adaptability during inference, spanning uncertainty reduction, assisted learning, and reinforcement learning. However, existing methodologies often require numerous iterations, leading to inefficiencies. Our work addresses this limitation by introducing OptPO, which significantly reduces computational demands while maintaining robust performance, thereby augmenting and refining existing test-time training methodologies.

The simplification process eliminates redundant components, retaining only the essential logical elements of the formula. This ensures that the CNF remains concise while preserving its original functionality, thereby enabling more efficient processing by SAT solvers. However, hallucinations can occur when variables or formula components are erroneously reused, misclassified, or improperly translated, disrupting the operational continuity of SAT solvers. These challenges often arise from the inability of models to accurately translate certain syntactic structures during training or from sentences with atypical word order, complicating their conversion into CNF. To address these issues, we employed a combined approach utilizing NLTK and Sympy. NLTK facilitates the decomposition of sentences according to rigorously validated grammatical rules, ensuring a robust syntactic foundation for subsequent processing. This process guarantees the accurate segmentation of sentences. Subsequently, Sympy is employed to preserve the logical integrity of the original sentence during its transformation into CNF. When a paragraph is received, it is dissected into individual sentences at each period, with each sentence considered an independent unit. These components are then conjoined using logical conjunctions to construct a CNF. Employing an extensive set of syntactic rules with NLTK, each sentence is meticulously parsed into a hierarchical tree structure, delineating the syntactic composition of the sentence. This structure originates from the principal clause and systematically branches into subordinate elements such as conditions, propositions, and other syntactic constituents, including nouns, verbs, and punctuation marks. By applying the predefined rules, the tree is transformed into a logical expression represented using Sympy operators. Finally, Sympy utilizes its internal mechanisms to convert this logical expression into its equivalent CNF representation.

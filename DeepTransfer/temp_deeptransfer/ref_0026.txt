Our findings are consistent with the results of Shirafuji et al. (2025), who employed a task vector-based approach and similarly observed the efficacy of the debiased model. In contrast, studies such as those by Lutz et al. (2024) utilized model merging techniques and demonstrated the performance of debiased large language models (LLMs) on downstream tasks using scores from NLI benchmarks. However, our results highlight the need for methods evaluated solely on tasks such as NLI to be examined more comprehensively across a wider range of datasets. The SuperGLUE results indicate that, except for Linear, SLERP, and Nearswap, other merging techniques substantially degrade the causal reasoning capabilities of LLMs (refer to Section 5.2). Consequently, these methods are unsuitable for reliable bias mitigation. The effectiveness of SLERP could be explained by its uniform interpolation across the parameter space, facilitating an equitable integration without excessively amplifying bias. Conversely, the alternative approach, NuSLERP, employs normalization at the layer or tensor level, which adversely impacts its SuperGLUE performance metrics. These findings suggest that, unlike SLERP, most recent model-merging methods cannot be directly applied for bias mitigation without risking substantial losses in reasoning performance. This work represents the first comprehensive study of how seven model-merging algorithms influence social bias in LLMs. By evaluating 13 models spanning the GPT, LLaMA, and Qwen families on three social bias datasets and the SuperGLUE benchmark, we revealed a trade-off between fairness and utility. Furthermore, the application of four specific methods—Karcher Mean, NuSLERP, TIES, and DELLA—resulted in significant degradation of performance on tasks requiring reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning, such as ReCoRD, COPA, CB, and BoolQ within the SuperGLUE benchmark. Therefore, while some existing debiasing methods based on model merging have demonstrated their debiased LLMs' downstream-task performance using scores from NLI benchmarks, we revealed that it is also essential to verify accuracy on tasks for reading comprehension and commonsense or causal reasoning.

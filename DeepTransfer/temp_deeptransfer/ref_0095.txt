The study outlines a methodology for constructing an energy model that provides energy consumption distributions for statically typed programming languages executed on the JVM. It investigates the impact of primitive data types, data sizes, operations, and device instances on energy consumption. Moreover, it presents evidence that devices of the same hardware model can exhibit variations in energy consumption, complicating the generalizability of energy predictions. The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 offers background information on Bayesian statistics, while Section 3 introduces our methodology. Section 4 details the implementation of the proposed methodology, and Section 5 explains the evaluation process and identifies any validity threats. The results of the evaluation are presented in Section 6, followed by a discussion in Section 7. Section 8 examines related scholarly work, and Section 9 concludes the study. Bayesian statistics is a statistical framework designed to make probabilistic inferences about unobservable phenomena based on prior knowledge. Although it shares conceptual similarities with frequentist statistics, which is the predominant statistical paradigm in computer science, the two approaches differ in their treatment and interpretation of models. Frequentist statistics builds models purely from observed data, minimizing assumptions, whereas Bayesian statistics explicitly incorporates prior assumptions, making them transparent and subject to empirical evaluation. We employ Bayesian statistics for two reasons: first, it requires the explicit articulation of assumptions, enhancing modularity and interpretability; second, it facilitates the prediction of energy consumption through posterior distributions, offering a more detailed representation of potential outcomes compared to the single point estimates typically generated by frequentist methods. In this study, we assume that energy consumption follows a normal distribution and is constrained within a range derived from prior research. These prior distributions are refined using a Bayesian inference algorithm to better align with observed measurements. In cases where data is limited, initial estimations may exert significant influence, potentially leading to suboptimal outcomes. However, as the dataset grows, the algorithm can rectify errors in the preliminary assumptions, thereby improving the alignment between the model and empirical data.

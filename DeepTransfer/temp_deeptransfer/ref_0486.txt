The integration of robots into construction processes holds immense potential to address workforce shortages while simultaneously enhancing safety and efficiency. Robots excel in performing heavy and repetitive tasks, reducing physical fatigue and minimizing cognitive errors. Consequently, their deployment significantly accelerates construction timelines and lowers associated costs. However, the inherent complexity of construction activities prevents individual robots from independently performing all tasks. For example, constructing a wall requires a range of skills, such as material transportation, precise placement, welding, and quality inspection. Therefore, coordinated collaboration among multiple robots becomes essential to execute these multifaceted tasks effectively. Multi-Robot Task Allocation (MRTA) addresses this need by breaking down complex tasks into manageable subtasks and assigning them to robots based on their capabilities, task requirements, and environmental conditions. MRTA problems are typically categorized along dimensions such as single-task versus multi-task robots, single-robot versus multi-robot tasks, and instantaneous versus time-extended assignments. Among these, the most challenging scenarios involve coordinating multiple robots to execute multiple tasks over extended periods, requiring advanced planning to account for diverse robot capabilities, task demands, and temporal constraints. Nevertheless, most MRTA research has predominantly focused on applications in warehouse logistics or environmental exploration, with limited attention paid to the specific needs of the construction industry.

Given real-time latency and cost considerations, deploying our LLM or deep cross-encoder-based BERT classifier for online search applications is not feasible. Therefore, we implement a teacher-student distillation approach to transfer knowledge from a more complex model to a student relevance model, as depicted in Figure 1. Our online model is a lightweight, shallow cross-encoder version of BERT. To ensure effective learning of the simplified model, we employ a deep cross-encoder BERT as the teacher model, rather than utilizing an alternative model type. Furthermore, we integrate key attribute information, identified by a relevance reasoning model, into the BERT model to enhance its comprehension of the congruence between queries and products. To address the issue of excessive and noisy information in extended explanations, we implement a model that extracts and refines product attribute descriptions that are either aligned or misaligned with the query. This approach enables the simplified BERT model to concentrate on salient features. Additionally, given the constraints of real-time processing, these intricate details are exclusively incorporated into the teacher model to maintain consistency. Subsequently, the simplified model acquires knowledge from the teacher model. Since there are no publicly accessible datasets pertaining to Chinese e-commerce search relevance tasks, we conducted a series of experiments utilizing a substantial real-world dataset derived from user search logs within the JD application.

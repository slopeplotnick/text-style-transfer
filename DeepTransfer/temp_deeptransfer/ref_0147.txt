As shown in Fig. 1, this section outlines the dataset construction process and evaluation methodologies. The dataset construction was spearheaded by a team of TCM experts who systematically collected samples from clinical cases, classical case records, and authoritative examination questions. These samples were then subjected to deduplication, cleaning, and anonymization to eliminate redundancies and protect personal information. The data annotation process followed a three-stage pipeline: initial annotation by experts, mutual cross-validation, and a final review by an independent third party. The resulting benchmark, named TCM-BEST4SDT, comprises 600 questions spanning four tasks: TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, LLM Content Safety, and SDT, as depicted in Fig. 2. To evaluate model performance in real-world TCM clinical scenarios, three distinct methodologies were developed. The first, selected-response evaluation, involves multiple rounds of independent assessments for each question, with randomized answer choices and variable scoring strategies tailored to question type. The second, judge model evaluation, employs an LLM combined with expert-designed prompts to assess model responses. The third, reward model evaluation, utilizes a specialized model to objectively measure the alignment between prescriptions and syndromes. TCM-BEST4SDT encompasses two categories of tasks: SDT tasks and general evaluation tasks. The SDT tasks are derived from clinical cases and classical case records, while the general evaluation tasks include TCM Basic Knowledge, Medical Ethics, and LLM Content Safety. The TCM Basic Knowledge component is sourced from public examination questions, such as the National Qualification Examination for Medical Practitioners, the National Postgraduate Entrance Examination: Comprehensive Clinical Medicine (TCM Integrated), and the Chinese Herbal Medicine Title Examination. Meanwhile, the Medical Ethics component is based on authoritative sources and further enriched through expert annotations tailored to specific clinical scenarios. Similarly, the LLM Content Safety component was independently developed by specialists, adhering to practical requirements and professional standards in the field.

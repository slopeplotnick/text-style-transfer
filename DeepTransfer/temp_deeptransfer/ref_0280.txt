A line of inquiry is directed towards enhancing system robustness by maintaining geometric consistency in representation interpretation. Recent studies indicate that alterations in viewpoint can lead to significant disruptions in the embedding space of pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT) encoders, thereby impairing the coordination between perceptual and decision-making components of the system. Motivated by these insights, we explore whether rectifying these representational discrepancies—without altering the overarching Visual-Language Architecture (VLA) or acquiring additional data—can effectively enhance the system's resilience to viewpoint variations. Parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) has emerged as an effective alternative to full-model adaptation, enabling models to specialize to new tasks or domains with minimal trainable parameters. Exemplary methodologies include adapters, LoRA, Prefix-Tuning, and BitFit, which incorporate small, learnable modules into the system while maintaining the majority of the backbone in a frozen state. These approaches are extensively implemented in large language models and multimodal systems due to their efficiency and stability. In the domain of embodied AI, PEFT has predominantly been employed to update language or policy components, such as through lightweight modifications to decision transformers or instruction encoders. However, when addressing viewpoint variations, the majority of existing methodologies seldom prioritize direct fine-tuning of the visual module. Approaches that facilitate targeted and lightweight adaptation of the visual processing pathways within vision-language-action (VLA) models remain notably underexplored. In contrast, the majority of existing methodologies endeavor to modify the entire system. Our research investigates the efficacy of implementing parameter-efficient modifications specifically within the visual components, such as vision tokens or the vision encoder, to address these challenges. Conventionally, alternative approaches mitigate visual perturbations by retraining substantial portions of the system or the entire visuomotor policy. However, we contend that such global adaptation is unwarranted. The primary challenge posed by visual perturbations lies in their propensity to induce systematic and predictable shifts within the visual embedding space, thereby disrupting the processing and alignment of visual information.

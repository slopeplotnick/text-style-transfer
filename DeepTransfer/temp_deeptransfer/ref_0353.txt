A Software Product Line (SPL) represents a systematic approach to transform a collection of related software systems, which share common features, into a unified platform that facilitates the efficient and flexible development of new software products. However, this extractive process, which involves deriving an SPL from an existing code base, is inherently complex and resource-intensive. Moreover, it often necessitates significant adjustments to established software development practices within an organization, potentially disrupting familiar workflows. This study outlines the initial steps undertaken to facilitate the transformation process. Conducted in collaboration with ITK - Predict and Decide, a small and medium-sized enterprise (SME) that opted to transition its existing code base into an SPL, the research team’s role was to provide guidance and support throughout this migration. The existing code base is centered around a framework known as Cross, which facilitates significant reuse of software components. Although it resembles an SPL, it lacks the conventional models and processes typically associated with a fully-fledged SPL. Therefore, to assist the developers in this transition, we devised a strategy to incrementally modify the configuration of their code base and engineering practices. The initial phase of this study involved examining the perceptions of individuals within the organization regarding the proposed transition and providing guidance to facilitate the adoption process. This paper presents the findings derived from this preliminary investigation. Through interviews conducted with organizational members, insights were gathered concerning their existing workflows, anticipated benefits of implementing the SPL, and apprehensions related to the transition. The majority of individuals expressed enthusiasm regarding the transition to an SPL and demonstrated a willingness to participate in its implementation. Developers, in particular, exhibited excitement, perceiving the transition as both a technical and managerial challenge. Conversely, individuals primarily engaged in business-related activities exhibited a degree of caution, articulating concerns pertaining to potential complications in maintenance and the possible diminishment of creativity, particularly in the context of user interface design.

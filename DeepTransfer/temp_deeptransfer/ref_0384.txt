We propose SR-GRPO, which leverages stable rank as a dense reward signal in reinforcement learning, thereby eliminating the reliance on preference datasets or external verification mechanisms. We demonstrate that SR-GRPO improves reasoning across diverse benchmarks, establishing intrinsic geometric signals as a robust foundation for aligning language models. Beyond reinforcement learning, we further show that stable rank functions as a reliable zero-shot reward proxy for evaluating language model responses and integrates seamlessly into best-of-N decoding strategies. Specifically, we propose stable rank (Rudelson & Vershynin, 2007) as an unsupervised quality signal derived from language model representations. In this section, we define the metric, elucidate its geometric motivation, and validate its effectiveness as a reward proxy. The theoretical foundation for this approach stems from two distinct lines of research. First, the softmax bottleneck (Yang et al., 2018; Godey et al., 2024) suggests that expressive language modeling requires high-rank representations. Second, representation collapse is a well-documented indicator of degraded generation quality (Gao et al., 2019). Stable rank directly quantifies effective dimensionality, making it a natural metric for quality assessment. We empirically validate this connection below and further substantiate it through correlation analyses in Section 5. To evaluate stable rank as a zero-shot proxy for human preference, we test its ability to predict the preferred response in a pair. This evaluation examines whether stable rank can serve as a reliable reward signal independent of model-specific training. For this purpose, we utilize RewardBench (Lambert et al., 2025), a comprehensive benchmark consisting of 2,985 preference pairs across five categories: Chat, Chat-Hard, Safety, Code, and Math. For each pair, we compute the stable rank of both responses and designate the response with the higher stable rank as the preferred option. We benchmark our approach against three baseline methods: (1) Pointwise Scoring (Point.) (Kim et al., 2024), which involves prompting the language model to assign a score from 1 to 5 to each response; (2) Pairwise Comparison (Pair.) (Kim et al., 2024), which entails prompting the language model to directly compare two responses; and (3) IPO (Garg et al., 2025), which uses implicit preference scores derived from Yes/No token probabilities. Our evaluation spans five models: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Yang et al., 2024), Qwen3-0.6B (Yang et al., 2025), Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Dubey et al., 2024), and Phi-3.5-mini-Instruct (Abdin et al., 2024). To compute stable rank, we format inputs as prompt-response pairs according to each model's native chat template and extract outputs from the final layer.

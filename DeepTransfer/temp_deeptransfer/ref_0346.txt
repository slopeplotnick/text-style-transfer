To address RQ2, we evaluated our models on 11 low-resource languages, each selectively sampled from the 8 clusters (see Figure 2). Notably, languages written in the Latin script, such as Galician (Gl) and Slovenian (Sl), demonstrated more stable performance across benchmarks (Table 5). This consistency can be attributed to script overlap and subword tokenization coverage in the shared XLM-R vocabulary, which benefits languages with higher representation during pretraining. However, Icelandic (Is), despite also using the Latin script, exhibited less reliable performance due to its rich and complex morphological structure. This complexity leads to high token fragmentation and less coherent sentence representations, which can hinder the effectiveness of multilingual encoders relying on subword tokenization (e.g., SentencePiece in XLM-R). To assess the extent to which training on a high-resource language supports transfer within and across clusters and thereby address RQ3, we trained models under three configurations: (i) Hindi-only, (ii) Chinese-only, and (iii) Hindi+Chinese combined. Each model was evaluated across six languages from the Indic (Hindi) and East-Southeast Asian (Chinese) clusters. Within the Indic cluster, the Hindi-trained model consistently outperformed the Chinese-trained model across all benchmarks (see Table 6). Conversely, in the East-Southeast Asian cluster, the Chinese-trained model outperformed the Hindi-trained model for most benchmarks. These findings demonstrate that transfer within a cluster, from a high-resource language to other languages in the same cluster, is more effective than transfer across clusters from a high-resource language belonging to a different cluster.

OptPO-SFT employs the same optimization strategy as TTSFT but leverages a Bayesian framework to adaptively regulate the number of iterations, thereby ensuring both computational efficiency and reliability. Both the RL-based and SFT-based implementations of OptPO adhere to a consistent three-step process: initializing the procedure, generating pseudo-labels through a designated method, and subsequently updating the model parameters. The primary distinction lies in the optimization methodologies employed: one utilizes policy-gradient optimization, while the other relies on cross-entropy fine-tuning. Despite this difference, both approaches exhibit comparable underlying mechanisms. For evaluation, we employ challenging mathematical and scientific benchmarks, including MATH-500, comprising 500 high school-level problems; AIME 2024, featuring 30 pre-Olympiad problems; AMC, encompassing problems from the American Mathematics Competitions; and GPQA, which includes complex graduate-level science questions. To assess the robustness of OptPO, we conduct experiments on widely-used open-source models such as Qwen2.5-Math1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, examining its performance across varying model scales and architectures. When evaluating the efficacy of TTSFT and OptPO-SFT, we employed Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct as our primary models. OptPO is a versatile framework that can be integrated into various existing reinforcement learning systems to enhance the efficiency of test-time training. Therefore, we incorporated it into PPO, GRPO, and Reinforce++ to comprehensively assess its performance. Moreover, we conducted a comparative analysis with TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025), a widely utilized method for policy optimization during test-time. Additionally, we assessed the efficacy of OptPO-SFT under analogous conditions. The critic's learning rate is configured at 1e-5.

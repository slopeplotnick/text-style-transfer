In contrast to image-based IC-LoRA approaches that require separate training for each editing task, our single Sync-LoRA module generalizes across diverse edits and achieves a robust balance between high visual fidelity and precise motion preservation, as demonstrated in our experiments (see section 4). In-context learning (ICL) enables models to generalize to new tasks by conditioning on structured input examples without explicit supervision. While extensively studied in language models, recent research has explored ICL in diffusion-based image generation. In these works, the model generates a panel of images, where the images within the panel provide context to each other. This enables applications that require consistency across different generated images, such as storyboard generation. When conditioning such panels on a given real image, ICL-based approaches can be used for a wide range of image-to-image tasks, including image personalization and image editing. The most closely related work to ours is IC-LoRA, which observes that transformer-based diffusion models possess an inherent capability to generate panels in which certain attributes remain consistent across the different images in the panel. This capability can be enhanced by fine-tuning the model with a low-rank adapter (LoRA) on structured, consistent panels and using an appropriate template prompt. Their approach has been applied to diverse tasks such as film storyboard generation, font design, and home decoration. Our work extends the paradigm to video and presents an IC-LoRA for video editing, focusing on frame-accurate portrait synchronization. Moreover, recent progress has shown that large video diffusion transformers naturally exhibit in-context learning behavior, enabling them to generate multiple temporally or spatially related clips within a single diffusion process. Previous work has focused on analyzing this emergent ability for in-context generation, using inpainting-based conditioning to achieve controlled scene composition, but without any mechanism to ensure temporal correspondence across clips. Building on this insight, PoseGen introduces in-context LoRA finetuning for pose-controllable human video generation, achieving long and coherent motion from a reference image. In contrast, our approach explicitly trains this in-context behavior toward synchronized editing, ensuring robust temporal alignment and precise subject consistency.

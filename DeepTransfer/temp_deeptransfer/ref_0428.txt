For GPT-5 pro, while score inflation is less pronounced, significant gaps remain in dimensions such as Dynamism, Coherence, and Immutability, where the model continues to overestimate its performance. However, incorporating a checklist-based grading scheme that requires the model to justify its ratings using evidence from key video frames helps mitigate these overestimations. Moreover, integrating computer vision tools like ByteTrack, which reliably tracks identities across frames and provides concrete evidence for penalizing frame discontinuities or unexpected transformations, further reduces scoring discrepancies. Specifically, this approach narrows the gaps by 0.07 in Coherence and 0.09 in Immutability compared to the standard VideoScience-Judge without the checklist. While this enhancement improves scoring consistency and reduces the ability to differentiate between similar models, its rule-based approach—focused on detecting explicit violations of discrete physical laws (e.g., "Does the ball descend?" or "Does water flow correctly?")—fails to capture the broader aspects of physical realism. These include consistent motion trajectories, causal relationships over time, acceleration patterns, momentum conservation, and the smoothness of motion that humans intuitively assess. This limitation is particularly evident in the context of VideoScience-Bench, which contrasts with the simpler, single-rule scenarios of PhyGenBench. Unlike PhyGenBench, VideoScience-Bench encompasses a diverse range of scientific phenomena, such as multi-object interactions and fluid dynamics, where outcomes emerge from the interplay of multiple principles, often involving cumulative effects and higher-order dynamics.

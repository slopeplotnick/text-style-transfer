The MODOMA system serves as a laboratory framework for investigating language acquisition processes, incorporating both adult and child agents to manipulate various experimental parameters. By explicitly representing the grammatical rules it acquires, the system allows researchers to directly examine and analyze these rules. The findings of this study substantiate the feasibility of employing a computational framework with multiple agents for first language acquisition. Crucially, the principle of Zipf's law serves as a pivotal mechanism for assessing the efficacy of the MODOMA system in language acquisition. The experimental procedures implemented in this research incorporate a diverse array of methodologies to discern various lexical categories, such as function and content words, thereby encompassing the essential components of more intricate language acquisition experiments. Consequently, the findings provide a robust foundation for further investigation. Subsequent research endeavors might explore the implications of modifying parameter settings on the grammatical structures acquired by the child agent, the utterances produced, and the communicative interactions with the adult agent. This exploration may encompass the frequency of the lexical acquisition process and the potential revision of previously established lexical categories in response to new information. Moreover, another avenue for investigation pertains to the influence of feedback provided by the adult agent on the language acquisition and production processes of the child agent. The successful implementation of such experiments would enable the exploration of more advanced learning mechanisms. Furthermore, the MODOMA framework offers the potential to facilitate the examination of more intricate grammatical phenomena. For instance, Shakouri et al. (2025) illustrate that grammatical categories, including nouns and verbs, can be acquired under analogous conditions to those employed in the acquisition of function and content words. Utilizing sophisticated statistical methodologies, such as hierarchical agglomerative clustering, they successfully identify these categories in an unsupervised manner. Therefore, the findings of this study represent a significant advancement, laying the groundwork for further scholarly inquiry in this domain.

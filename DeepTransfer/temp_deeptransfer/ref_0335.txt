To obtain distinct spatial cues and overcome the constraints of fixed viewpoints, we diversify camera rotation trajectories. Each trajectory starts from a random orientation in terms of pitch, yaw, and roll, followed by random drifts to mimic natural movements. Additionally, we ensure that the primary auditory cues correspond with the visual elements and remain within the field of view for a specified duration. Specifically, for each clip, we determine the location of the most prominent auditory source through a method that decomposes the sound into its constituent components, subsequently adjusting the camera to concentrate on this auditory focus. Upon obtaining the binaural audio, we discard samples exhibiting minimal auditory differentiation between the left and right channels, retaining only those with distinct auditory cues. BiAudio represents the largest video dataset featuring binaural sound. Existing datasets featuring real-world binaural audio are comparatively limited in scale. While SimBinaural [14] provides an extensive duration of over 100 hours, its synthetic nature diminishes its applicability to real-world scenarios. Moreover, most datasets are constrained to specific auditory contexts, such as street environments in OAP [45] or musical settings in Fair-Play [11], SimBinaural [14], and MUSIC-21 [57, 58]. In contrast, BiAudio encompasses a comprehensive range of real-world auditory phenomena. We enhance spatial and motion perception, thereby optimizing the generation of binaural sound from video inputs. The architecture of ViSAudio is illustrated in a schematic diagram. Our methodology incorporates elements of the MMAudio architecture, refining the pretrained MMAudio to retain its robust audio generation capabilities, thereby ensuring effective generalization across diverse acoustic environments. To produce binaural audio that maintains both channel coherence and spatial distinctiveness, we employ Dual-Branch Audio Generation, wherein two dedicated branches independently predict the left and right audio flows. Furthermore, we integrate a Conditional Spacetime Module, which extracts spatio-temporal features from the video and incorporates them into the audio generation process, thereby enhancing the spatial realism of the audio.

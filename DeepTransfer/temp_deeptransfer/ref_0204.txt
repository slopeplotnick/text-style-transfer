Participants expressed their perceptions regarding AI-generated music, even when the composer's identity was unknown. Their concerns mirrored those prevalent in the discourse surrounding music and technology, including potential economic impacts, issues of intellectual property rights, and cultural implications. However, there was notable interest in the innovative technology and its operational mechanisms. This amalgamation of sentiments may elucidate the variability in opinions regarding AI composers. As artificial intelligence systems advance in capability, phenomena once regarded with intrigue may increasingly be perceived as problematic or even threatening. Consequently, perceptions of AI-generated music are subject to temporal evolution. The study encountered several limitations. Firstly, we employed a bilingual, online convenience sample comprising university students and participants from Prolific, who engaged with the music under uncontrolled listening conditions (e.g., headphones/speakers, self-adjusted volume), thereby constraining the generalizability of the findings. Moreover, as noted in prior research on AI-generated music (Shank et al., 2023), individuals recruited via Prolific are more likely to exhibit technological literacy and prior familiarity with AI. Secondly, the framing of the stimuli as "movie soundtracks" for one experimental group may have introduced unintended bias, as individuals generally hold positive associations with film music. Lastly, the stimuli employed in our study were restricted to instrumental excerpts, thus limiting the generalizability of our findings to vocal music. Extant literature suggests that lyrics typically amplify the emotional intensity of sad songs, whereas they exert negligible or potentially diminishing effects on happy songs (Ali and Peynicioglu, 2006; Barradas and Sakka, 2021; Brattico et al., 2011; Warmbrodt et al., 2022). Given the predominance of positively valenced music in our study, it is plausible that the inclusion of lyrics would have exerted only a marginal effect on emotional intensity, assuming congruence with lyrics of comparable quality to human-composed music. To mitigate potential confounding factors and due to the unavailability of high-quality AI-generated vocal performances at the time of creating the musical stimuli, lyrics were excluded from the compositions.

More recent datasets include Mozilla's Common Voice, which provides crowd-sourced, scripted speech recordings in six South African languages, distributed under a permissive CC-0 license to encourage broad reuse. Similarly, FLEURS offers approximately 12 hours of data per language across four South African languages, primarily designed for benchmarking and evaluation purposes. Vuk'uzenzele (VixSD) is a community-led dataset released under the Esethu licensing framework, which emphasizes community ownership and ethical utilization. Although smaller in scale, it underscores the potential of local communities to actively contribute to resource creation. AfriSpeech-200 includes English recordings with South African accents, focusing on healthcare-related topics, which is particularly useful for developing speech recognition systems capable of handling accented speech. Despite these advancements, the availability of datasets featuring natural, unscripted speech across diverse domains remains limited. This highlights the need for more diverse, community-grounded datasets like Swivuriso, which integrate both scripted and unscripted speech, encompass broader domain representation, and reflect greater speaker diversity. Collecting speech data in underrepresented linguistic communities requires a sensitive approach that prioritizes informed consent, safeguards privacy, respects cultural norms, and considers the long-term implications for the communities involved. Previous initiatives, such as Common Voice and ALFFA, have demonstrated the critical importance of embedding ethical and participatory practices throughout the data lifecycle. For example, a case study on the Catalan edition of Common Voice emphasized the necessity of transparent consent procedures, robust privacy protections, and inclusive participation mechanisms. Similarly, the ALFFA project actively involved local experts and field linguists during the data collection phase to ensure meaningful community engagement. Building on these efforts, Swivuriso places a strong emphasis on ethical practices and inclusive representation. Local coordinators played a central role in managing recordings, supporting participants, and ensuring comprehensive community representation. The data collection process adhered to institutionally approved ethical protocols, including the anonymization of metadata, removal of personal identifiers, and implementation of safeguards to prevent misuse, such as voice cloning or biometric profiling. Finally, ensuring datasets are accessible and usable is crucial, particularly for low-resource languages. Open licenses, such as those provided by Creative Commons, facilitate broad reuse of data, which is especially beneficial when data collection is challenging or costly. Projects like Common Voice exemplify how permissive licensing can enhance resource sharing and accelerate the development of language technologies for underrepresented languages.

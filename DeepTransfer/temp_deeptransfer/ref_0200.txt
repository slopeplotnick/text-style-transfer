Curriculum learning, first introduced by Bengio et al. (2009), is a pedagogical strategy that trains models by progressing from simpler to more complex tasks. This approach has been widely adopted in the development of large language models (LLMs). Recent methods, such as Speed-RL (Zhang et al. 2025) and those proposed by Kim and Lee (2024), continue to follow this paradigm by organizing tasks in a simple-to-complex sequence based on external difficulty criteria. Similarly, LBS3 (Luo et al. 2024) employs a static progression from easy to hard queries. However, these methods fundamentally misalign with effective curriculum design principles due to their reliance on static difficulty metrics rather than dynamically adapting to the model's evolving competence. Effective curriculum learning should instead be competence-aware, adjusting dynamically to the model's developmental trajectory. CAPO (Curriculum Advantage Policy Optimization) addresses this limitation by employing an adaptive training framework that leverages intrinsic advantage estimates as competence-aware signals. It avoids the destabilization often caused by prematurely mixing positive and negative feedback by adopting a staged curriculum: the initial phase focuses on positive reinforcement to establish stable behavioral priors, while subsequent phases incorporate negative feedback to enhance generalization. Unlike static, externally defined approaches, CAPO dynamically evolves in alignment with the model's capabilities, unifying early stability with later adaptability. Extensive experiments demonstrate CAPO's consistent improvements over strong baselines like GRPO across diverse benchmarks and model scales. Notably, CAPO exhibits exceptional cross-domain generalization, excelling in tasks ranging from mathematical reasoning to multimodal problem-solving. By prioritizing the model's developmental trajectory over static difficulty benchmarks, CAPO paves the way for more adaptive and cognitively inspired learning algorithms.

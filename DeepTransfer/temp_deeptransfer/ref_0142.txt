Traditional MRTA approaches have demonstrated success across various domains through optimization algorithms, including linear programming, genetic algorithms, and Reinforcement Learning (RL), which offer mathematical rigor and established performance guarantees. However, construction environments present unique challenges that make these traditional methods inadequate. Specifically, the unstructured nature of construction sites introduces significant coordination difficulties, while the complexity and volume of information in these settings surpass the reasoning capabilities of mathematically-based approaches. Consequently, there is a pressing need for more adaptive solutions. Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have revealed their remarkable capabilities in natural language understanding, logical reasoning, and adaptive decision-making, which address several fundamental limitations of traditional optimization and learning-based methods. Moreover, LLM-based coordination approaches enable seamless human-robot interaction and adaptive strategies tailored to the intricate and dynamic requirements of construction environments. Notably, recent LLM frameworks have demonstrated potential in areas such as task planning, digital twin integration, and multi-agent system deployment. However, these approaches currently lack systematic validation mechanisms and comprehensive performance benchmarking against traditional optimization methods. This highlights a critical gap: while LLM-based coordination appears promising, no research has yet developed iterative retry and self-corrective mechanisms to ensure reasoning reliability, nor conducted systematic comparative analyses to evaluate their performance in construction task allocation. To address this gap, this study introduces the LangGraph based Task Allocation Agent (LTAA) framework, a novel LLM-driven coordination approach that incorporates innovative phase-adaptive strategies and multi-stage validation mechanisms absent in existing frameworks. The phase-adaptive strategy effectively balances workloads without compromising performance, while the multi-stage validation system ensures reasoning consistency by systematically identifying and rectifying allocation deficiencies through structured feedback loops.

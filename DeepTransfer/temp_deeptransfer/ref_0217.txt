In Section 3, we explained how the questions map to four principal themes relevant to the research inquiry. Since most interviewees were unfamiliar with SPLE, we provided an exposition of its concepts and significance during the interviews. This introduction may have introduced a potential bias; however, we trust our interviewees to independently form their opinions and the interviewer to minimize undue influence. The questions were intentionally designed to be open-ended, encouraging comprehensive responses and capturing a wide range of ideas. Particular attention was given to the formulation of the questions to ensure they did not constrain the diversity of potential answers. Moreover, metrics were developed to maximize the utility of the collected data and extract meaningful insights. These metrics quantify the pertinent terms and their frequency of occurrence, both in aggregate and disaggregated by specific roles. Consequently, tables provide comprehensive information, while graphs illustrate the distribution of values. Even if a response was singular and not echoed by other participants, it was still incorporated and considered in the analysis. We meticulously analyzed the responses, systematically aggregating the terminology extracted from the interviews and aligning it with the vocabulary commonly employed in the SPLE domain, such as "personalisation" and "making specific some parts of the code," which are conceptually analogous to "customization." This alignment facilitated greater consistency in the findings and enabled the identification of relevant arguments within the literature to address the concerns raised by participants. Engaging in this process with various individuals facilitated enhancements in the fluidity of the interviews. Although the questions themselves remained unchanged, our proficiency in posing them improved progressively. For instance, we began to provide supplementary hints and elaborate further when participants exhibited uncertainty, as well as refine the transitions between questions. Notwithstanding these modifications, we maintain that the volume and quality of information obtained from the initial questionnaire and the final one remained consistent.

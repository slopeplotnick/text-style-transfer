Our findings indicate that belief entrenchment consistently predicts an accuracy drop in domains where ground truth labels are available, such as forecasting. This suggests that the Martingale Score can serve as an effective proxy for assessing reasoning quality, even in contexts where complete information is not yet accessible. In evaluating Bayesian rationality within language models, the focus is on their ability to incorporate evidence in a manner consistent with Bayes's theorem. Gupta et al. (2025) demonstrated that language models are capable of updating their beliefs in accordance with Bayesian principles when presented with a substantial number of coin flips, despite an initial biased prior. However, in more realistic settings with complex linguistic environments, negative results emerge: Qiu et al. (2025) observed that language models fail to adjust their beliefs regarding user preferences as anticipated by the Bayesian framework. Furthermore, Falck (2024) provided evidence that language models do not adhere to Bayesian learning when contextual information is provided. Zhao (2021) and Wang (2023) demonstrated that the performance of language models is contingent upon the arrangement of examples within prompts. Solutions have been proposed to enhance in-context learning or reasoning by aligning it more closely with Bayesian principles—such as emulating the predictions of an ideal Bayesian reasoner, leveraging abstract reasoning, or integrating abduction and deduction. However, these approaches frequently rely on auxiliary structures, such as Bayesian networks, which constrain their practical applicability. Language models exhibit a range of biases attributable to their extensive training on human-generated data. Additionally, they manifest biases distinct from those observed in human judgment, such as position bias, morbidity bias, sentiment bias, and certainty bias. Of particular significance is confirmation bias, as it is posited to be a fundamental contributor to polarization. It is posited that the majority of biases can be categorized as manifestations of confirmation bias. Consequently, our focus is directed towards confirmation bias, employing belief entrenchment as a means to investigate its presence within language models.

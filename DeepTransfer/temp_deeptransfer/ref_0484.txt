We evaluated several methodologies, including RL-Trained, Mixture-of-Inputs, CoLaR, and LiteReason, to assess their performance. Using Qwen 2.5 7B-Instruct, we generated chapters based on plans derived from each method and collected 20 pairwise comparisons for every method combination. Model performance was then compared using Bradley-Terry relative strengths, with further details provided in Appendix E. Initially, we conducted ablations on the Flawed Fictions and NCP tasks, with results presented in Table 4. We benchmarked LiteReason variants against two baselines built on the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model: a prompted default model and its RL-trained version, neither of which incorporated latent reasoning capabilities. Notably, LiteReason without reasoning prompts (RP), which skips reasoning steps, increased reasoning length but performed worse than the RL-trained baseline. Table 7 provides additional comparisons of models with and without implicit-thought prompts. Furthermore, we examined the use of latent thoughts during training to determine whether RL training influenced their prevalence. We observed a sharp increase in latent-thought usage at the start of RL training, which diminished as training progressed. Future work could explore fostering latent reasoning by modifying the prompt structure or increasing the likelihood of implicit-token markers. Overall, LiteReason demonstrated only marginal performance improvements (0.8% on Flawed Fictions and 58% on NCP), likely due to its methodological similarity to the RL-trained baseline, though it better explored alternative reasoning pathways not typically generated by the base model. Unsurprisingly, the training-free methods, Mixture-of-Inputs (MoI) and Soft Thinking, performed similarly to the untrained model in both performance and token length. On the NCP task, Soft Thinking showed a slight improvement over the baseline but generated more tokens, whereas MoI underperformed with fewer tokens. On the Flawed Fictions task, performance differences from the baseline were minimal and within confidence intervals. Further research is needed to enhance the consistency and efficiency of training-free approaches across diverse tasks and reasoning paradigms.

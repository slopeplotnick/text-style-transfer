Training robust reward models is challenging due to their subjective and context-dependent nature, which makes them susceptible to manipulation through learned proxies (Chakraborty et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023). The scarcity of annotations further limits the model's ability to manage detailed behaviors (Wu et al., 2023). Consequently, various efforts have been made to reduce reliance on annotations. Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) avoids explicit reward models but still requires preference datasets. Meanwhile, verifiable rewards (DeepSeek-AI, 2025; Lambert et al., 2024) depend on ground-truth outcomes and are only applicable where automatic verification is feasible. Self-evaluation methods (Yuan et al., 2024; Lee et al., 2024; Garg et al., 2025) are hindered by prompt sensitivity and systematic biases (Zheng et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024). Importantly, all these approaches continue to rely on external signals to assess quality, neglecting the information encoded within the model's internal representations. However, the validity of a generation should be discernible through its underlying computational processes. For instance, when a model generates "The capital of France is Paris," its hidden states encapsulate activated factual knowledge (He et al., 2024c); conversely, when it produces fabricated content, distinct activation patterns emerge (Chen et al., 2024). This raises a pertinent question: Can we evaluate text quality directly from internal signals, thus enabling LLM alignment without external supervision? In this study, we demonstrate that a straightforward geometric property of LLM hidden states, termed effective dimensionality, serves as a robust indicator of response quality. Specifically, we propose using stable rank (Rudelson & Vershynin, 2007), a matrix-theoretic metric, as an unsupervised proxy for assessing generation quality. Stable rank quantifies the number of independent semantic directions occupied by a response by evaluating the ratio of total variance to dominant-direction variance in the hidden-state representation. It balances representational richness with coherence: a high stable rank indicates that information is distributed across numerous dimensions rather than being concentrated in a few. The theoretical foundation for this concept is derived from two distinct lines of research. Firstly, the softmax bottleneck analysis elucidates that precise language modeling necessitates navigating a high-dimensional semantic manifold, as the contextual probability distribution inherent in natural language is intrinsically complex.
